**The Kheperi are a people who believe nothing counts until it is written down.** Their culture
note now sets out what that means: an unattested act did not happen, the sin is a hanging promise
rather than an unkind one, and the first question in any dispute is why a thing was never entered.

**The weighing measures purity, not size.** A farmer with twelve settled undertakings passes as
surely as a lord with four hundred, and the priests say so to frightened people because it is
true. What length buys is a long tomb and a hard account to keep honest.

**A promise closes four ways, and only one of them is keeping it.** Perform it, settle it, be
released from it, or have another take it up. A man ruined by a wreck who agrees terms with his
creditor has closed cleanly and performed nothing.

**Anyone may refuse to release a dying man**, at no cost to themselves and entirely within their
rights. It is the most frightening power an ordinary Kheperi holds.

**Widows, orphans, the stricken and the destitute are provided for by rule rather than by
kindness.** The note sets out the ladder—house, burial club, temple—and what each provides in
shelter, teaching and defence, along with what happens to a living person who can no longer
settle their own affairs.
