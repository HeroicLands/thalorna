**Scripts and records**

- Khelathi-zethu, the temple script, has about four hundred and seventy picture-signs, in right-to-left lines or top-down columns, with royal names in a corded oval.
- Qalezu, the everyday Khelathi alphabet, writes only consonants, right to left, and the settled desert courts write in it; the Dunhara and the desert tribes keep no writing.
- Several scripts are named varieties of the main ones: Tharavarkon and Thurarth of the Thuravark runes, and Okhatha-zethu and Merazan of Qalezu.
- The K'otsel cord records are described knot by knot, Kaliharans keep their knowledge by memory through the new Vaarthari skill, and the Ithari's stone marks are no script.
- Every Khelathi god has a full-colour emblem icon, its hieroglyph painted on lapis, and the Khelathi Pantheon shows each god's glyph.
- New spelling pages show how roman Vylari, Khazari, Nordmal and Khelathi are set in Semaron, Pirzath, Thuravark and Qalezu, and each script says how a scribe marks a stressed word.
- The Semaron, Pirzath, Thuravark and Qalezu pages each show Vylari, Khazari, Nordmal or Khelathi words set in the script itself.
- The Khelathu banner is a painted temple wall inscribed in Zethu.
- Khelathi inscriptions can be written sign by sign in Zethu on any page.
- The Áelendan, very conservative, write their own tongue in the Sinale hand and carve in their old runes, while the Élavendri, who adapt readily, write in Semaron; ancient texts in the region are Áelendi.
