**Peoples at home** — The culture accounts of three peoples now describe ordinary life from childhood to the deathbed, who each admires, and what each gets wrong.

- **Vylarians** keep their dead as wax faces by the door, and the account shows how that shapes wills, adoption, tombs and the dread of having a name struck out.
- **Helionites** judge a person by what is shown in public, and the account follows that into their festivals, their burials and the dead no city has spoken for.
- **Aelwythans** gain a fuller account of their ages of life, their lean years, and the crusade the island does not discuss.
- **The Empire** now agrees across its notes: Heliónis is independent in practice, the Augustar is his own Pontifex Maximus, and a citizen's wife keeps the citizenship for life while his children hold it only as dependents.
