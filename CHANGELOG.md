# thalorna

## 0.2.2

### Patch Changes

**Peoples**

- The Áelendan have their own culture page: a conservative human people of the highlands, guardians of the thin places, with a spiritual tradition of their own.

- Aelwythi has a pronunciation guide for its older speech and writes its familiar word spellings in the Semaron Kuriale hand.

- _Bjartur_ is the Nordmal god's name, spelled with a vowel in every note.
- Tharavarkon text is carved with bound runes, two staves on one stem.

**Calendars**

- Dates on a people's pages are given in that people's own count: the Khelathi, Hellád, Nordland, Vrystwald, Vedyaran, Tāntsou, Tellumi and Sovidur calendars, and the Vylarian elsewhere.

**Languages**

- The 921-concept vocabulary reference covers everyday conversation,
  questions, time, uncertainty and courtesy, with guidance on phrases and
  idioms.
- Everyday dictionaries across twenty-three human languages cover the shared
  core, with worked expressions suited to each tongue.
- Aelwythi’s everyday and literary vocabulary, speech and examples share older
  verbs, distinct singular and plural address, clear modern definitions, and
  emphasis through word choice and phrasing.
- The Helonic and Taruzeli stress examples are said the way each language's stress rule says, and the Áelendi note keeps its stress on the first syllable throughout.
- The Kaliharan stem _thari-_, "what came before", is written without an acute.
- Tarveni stress falls by the spelling guide's rule, as Vylari's does, with an acute where a word leaves it.
- Okharik's sound notes agree that it has the rasping _kh_.

**Verse**

- Aelwythi verse pairs automatic Semaron script with clearer modern English
  readings and the Roman source, numbering every fifth line.
- Linked names in parallel verse remain readable in their own language
  alongside the surrounding prose.

**Scripts**

- Words with accented y display correctly in ordinary Semaron script.
- Khazari text is set in the redrawn printed Pirzath Hand.
- Khazari says a foreign name without its opening vowel.
- Helonic, Bizarian and Bethuan agree with each other and with the pronunciation guide on vowels, consonants, the article and the Bethuan enclitic.
- Names such as Khankhor, Khaddaris, Krisamar, Timiakit, Eutalash, Plantash and Kalumion are respelled to be read as they are said.
- Bizarian and Bethuan are written in the Helónika, each with its own letters, and a merchant house's local books are kept in cursive Bizarian.
- Vedyari text is set in the Vedyakshara script font.
- Helonic and Valtari text is set in the Helónika script font.
- Vylari, Provenzal, Tarveni, Élavendri, Valtari and Varokhi text is shown in the Semaron script, Nordmal in Thuravark runes, Khazari in Pirzath, and Khelathi, Atárzadi, Dunhari and Nyáluban in Qalezu, wherever a page marks the words with their language.
- **Vedyari:** the language note and the Vedyakshara script note agree that about one noble in six can read, and the sound notes name _f_ and _ts_.
- **Vedyari register:** the six Vedyari names that opened on "The" are listed without it, and the verb for dying is _daush-_.
- Vedyari names that held a vowel _r_ are spelled with _ri_ for easier pronunciation, as in Pritimaja Kayadara and Driksharana Manrishti.
- The Vedyari language page describes only the sounds its spelling writes.
- Teng Chou and Veldikko text is set in its own typeface.
- Sinale and Áelendi text is shown in the Veldikko script, running right to left.
- Names first introduced in verse now link to their notes or carry typed name markers. The markers identify each name's language, with the verse language as the fallback when its origin is unknown.
- The Zemor Hodhis and its six schools are spelled the same way everywhere, and their pages and icons have new addresses.

**Khelathi in Zethu**

- Khelathi words, the four quarter endings and the plural can be shown in their sacred Zethu hieroglyphs.

**Languages and names**

- Khazari words and names are respelled to sound like the stone-tongue they evoke, house and hold names are written in two dashed parts (_Dalk-thumár_), and several characters, places and titles are renamed, among them _Hulabat_ and _Hudakal_.
- The Khazari pages carry a phrasebook, a gate-cry, a forge oath, a maker's mark, a toast and the words over a tomb, each with its pronunciation and meaning.
- Sinale, Tarveni and Vedyari words and names are respelled closer to the speech they evoke, and many characters, lineages, places and Janpodas are renamed in each.
- Khelathi names write _ch_ for _tj_ (_Chaq'ur_, _Chelsur_), the longest names are shorter, and Khelathi gains numerals, ten everyday verbs and the words of the sacred river hunt.
- Vylari asks, commands and exclaims with words of its own (_eza_, _areza_, _vez_, _voza_, _aia_), and reports speech with _vethor_, "says".
- The pronunciation guide covers vowel _y_, long vowels, doubled consonants, _ph_ and the vowel pairs _ie_, _uo_, _ae_, _ao_ and _ui_.
- Vylari gains some ninety words, among them the senses, the passions and four kinds of love, and its grammar page sets out its plurals, its six present forms, "of", time words and "let, must, may and can".
- Vylari is stressed by the pronunciation guide's rule, and its third-person verbs end in _-zor_: _lokazor_, _falvazor_, _yarezor_.
- Vylari has words for "perhaps", "truth" and "truly" (_zikai_, _varelza_, _varlai_), and the Morning Call and the Second Reading turn on _zikai_, the word that marks a thing as only alleged.
- The setting lists the core words every human language covers, and Vylari, Tantsou, Áelendi and Élavendri have a word for each; Provenzal and Tarveni share Vylari's.
- Áelendi borrows from Sinale alone, and its everyday words are its own; Élavendri takes some Vylari words for the city's furniture, tableware and dress, among them _ómbela_ (a chair) and _vémrai_ (clothes).
- The Grukar converse by body language and scent; their grunts and bellows only lend emphasis, they keep no names or script, and only the Grukar-Sha learn to speak human tongues.
- Vedyari's word endings are shown as they are written: _-oka_, _-ona_, _-uta_, _-ru_ and _-ija_, and the station names are spelled _Karmija_, _Danhija_, _Sentija_ and _Ritrija_.
- The language pages no longer carry sound-change step tables or the sections that listed them: Provenzal, Tarveni, Áelendi, Élavendri, Vylari, Helonic and Jurthati keep their words, sources and sound rules.
- Áelendi's own words no longer echo Vylari, among them the words for a judgment, a vow, a horse, ten and four, and Élavendri follows; Élavendri takes the Vylari _thálbezon_ for steel; Sinale gains its words for teaching and wisdom.
- Tantsou words and names drop their accent marks, and the Tantsou page sets out the tongue's twenty opening consonants and eighteen finals.
- A few Tantsou names are respelled to match their roots: chaos is _huen_ (_Huenlei_, _Huenzhuo_, _Genhuen_), the boar is _zhin_ (_Zhinho_), and the slave caste is the _Nuitsou_.
- Tantsou gains words for lightning, a hand and an account, so _Lanei_, _Tsaihuo_, _Wanduo_, _Wansou Zhuo_ and _Wantou_ read as the words they are built from.
- _Dou Chou_ is glossed as the Record of the Road.
- Some Tantsou given names are renamed, and the moon goddess is now _Youbui_.
- Tantsou and Jenchai keep records, literature and letters on handscrolls, with Teng Chou written in vertical columns read from right to left.

**Pronouncing Thalorna**

- The pronunciation guide is reorganized into a common section and a short section for each language that departs from it, with a plain substitute given for every hard sound.
- Names with no vowel or a hard ending are respelled so they can be said, among them _Svartbrandur_ and _Velgrímur_, and the pages of the renamed characters, places and titles move with them.
- Languages that stress the first syllable of every word say so.

**Scripts and records**

- Khelathi-zethu, the temple script, has about four hundred and seventy picture-signs, in right-to-left lines or top-down columns, with royal names in a corded oval.
- Qalezu, the everyday Khelathi alphabet, writes only consonants, right to left, and the settled desert courts write in it; the Dunhara and the desert tribes keep no writing.
- Several scripts are named varieties of the main ones: Tharavarkon and Thurarth of the Thuravark runes, and Okhatha-zethu and Merazan of Qalezu.
- The K'otsel cord records are described knot by knot, Kaliharans keep their knowledge by memory through the new Vaarthari skill, and the Ithari's stone marks are no script.
- Every Khelathi god has a full-colour emblem icon, its hieroglyph painted on lapis, and the Khelathi Pantheon shows each god's glyph.
- New spelling pages show how roman Vylari, Khazari, Nordmal and Khelathi are set in Semaron, Pirzath, Thuravark and Qalezu, and each script says how a scribe marks a stressed word.
- The Semaron, Pirzath, Thuravark and Qalezu pages each show Vylari, Khazari, Nordmal or Khelathi words set in the script itself.
- The Khelathu banner is a painted temple wall inscribed in Zethu.
- The Khelathi page opens with the memorial of Meqes VI and its translation.
- Khelathi inscriptions can be written sign by sign in Zethu on any page.
- The Áelendan, very conservative, write their own tongue in the Sinale hand and carve in their old runes, while the Élavendri, who adapt readily, write in Semaron; ancient texts in the region are Áelendi.
- Khelathi names such as Meqes can be written in Semaron, which writes its missing _q_ as _k_.
- The Veldikko, the Sinale hand, is written right to left.
- Thuravark and Qalezu write the letters they lack with their nearest letter, and every script writes ë as e.

**Sinale**

- The Lament of Haulonna sets its Sinale original in Veldikko beside a Roman reading and an English translation of its five remembered lives.
- The pronunciation guide records Sinale pitch and emphasis notation with Unicode values; the language note clarifies word order, wearing, relations and how to proof a passage.

- _The Seven Towers_ and _Guest-Right_ pair alliterative Sinale originals with fresh modern English and matching native-script readings.
- _The Gathering on the End_ adds four original Sinale speeches with fresh modern English and matching native-script readings.
- _The Long Watchers' Sayings_ adds the complete 210-line Sinale cycle with fresh modern English and matching native-script readings.
- _The Asking_ adds all nine original Sinale turnings with fresh modern English and matching native-script readings.
- _The Praise of the One Who Stays_ adds an original Sinale praise-song with fresh modern English and a matching native-script reading.
- Sinale verse displays its rising, falling and emphasis marks.

**Skills and lore**

- The Kharessa, the Bethuan unarmed art, and the Path of Breath, Koremdanis Barkratis's sword discipline, are combat skills, with their techniques and maneuvers described.
- The Gar-Au's regalia are described, the Ruwozi and the Kolembozi are named as clans of the Nyáluba, and the Ústaran Schism has a single entry.
- The swordmaster who challenges Koremdanis Barkratis is Weja the Unbound, of Jenchai.
- Poems that were tagged with a metre or a rhyme scheme now state it in a sentence beside the poem.
- The Walking of the Line is set in two columns, Vylari in Semaron beside the translation, with every fifth line numbered.
- The Tevran and Vylarian military pages show their tables as tables.
- The Sinale's numinous, which joins spirit, totem and the celestial to the natural world, is described, along with how little of it humans may reach.

## 0.2.1

### Patch Changes

In the PDF books, a reference to another book names it by a short tag, such as (Aelw. p307), and term lists set the term in bold at the start of the line with the description running on after it.

Chronology sections give each event's date first, in the Vylarian Calendar (such as ~10000 BF), print across the page in the PDF books, and leave out "Felt here" where nothing was felt. In the PDF books, lists are spaced and bulleted clearly, long profile boxes continue onto the next page, population figures read as 12,500 or 11 million, and the page number is set at body size.

Lists that describe the Aureldian faiths, Khelâthi country, the Khazryn tongues, Grukar hearing, Bizarian names and the Kalihara adventure branches read as full sentences, and the Varnakan traditions name their path cleanly.

## 0.2.0

### Minor Changes

Thalorna is the World of Thalorna, a fantasy setting for Song of Heroic Lands, delivered as Foundry VTT compendium packs, PDF books and a website built from the same notes. This first release contains:

- **Foundry:** the module installs in any Foundry VTT 14 world; its journals read under every system, and its creature, character and item packs appear under Song of Heroic Lands 0.8.8 or later.
- **Books:** a PDF gazetteer for each of twenty-four regions and peoples, and _The World of Thalorna_, _The Rules of Thalorna_ and _The Bestiary of Thalorna_ sourcebooks.
- **Calendars:** the setting's calendars ship as Foundry calendars and Calendaria imports; Calendaria is optional.
- **Peoples:** every culture has an account of ordinary life from childhood to the deathbed, and an Adventurer's Guide that opens on an arrival, covers power, faith and daily life, and ends with a glossary.
- **Places:** nearly nine hundred places, from cities to wild sites, each opening on what it is known for and naming the body that governs it.
- **History:** every region has a written history in ages or eras, with king-lists, dated events, blood-fields and sites that carry adventure hooks.
- **Faiths:** the Ten and their twenty-nine devotional orders, the Aurèldían, Khelâthi, Vedyaran, Itzáni and Nyáluba gods, the north's cosmology, and the Shadow behind every undead.
- **Ranks and bodies:** every character shows the standing held in each faith, house, guild or order, read from that body's own rank ladder, with offices held apart from rank.
- **Characters:** the people the setting names have pages with a description, an appearance, a home and their patrons, rivals and affiliations, and three Varokhi heroes come with playable sheets.
- **Bestiary:** creatures read as field-guide entries with signs and local customs, ninety-one ordinary animals live in Thalorna's own lands, and every creature has a sheet with its attacks and icon.
- **Adventures:** ten dread adventures that turn on a reversal, and twenty-five wild places, each with a site page and a five-scene sword-and-sorcery adventure.
- **Literature:** every culture keeps its own songs, sagas, laments and recitations, quoted in the entries they concern.
- **Languages and names:** names read easily at the table, every tongue has its own sound, and lexicons give the words, name elements and naming customs of each people.
- **For the referee:** passages meant only for the referee are secret sections in Foundry and hidden spoilers on the website, and unfinished draft pages say so above their text.
- **Reading:** the first mention of a named person, place or body links to its entry or is highlighted, and every page ends with what touches it: places held, chronology, events and appearances in song.
