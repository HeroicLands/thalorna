# thalorna

## 0.2.0

### Minor Changes

Thalorna is the World of Thalorna, a fantasy setting for Song of Heroic Lands, delivered as Foundry VTT compendium packs and a website built from the same notes. This first release contains:

**Peoples and cultures**

- Every people's culture account follows ordinary life from childhood to the deathbed: who it admires, what it gets wrong, and how it keeps its dead.
- Vylarians keep their dead as wax faces by the door, Byzarians and Tarûzelians live by the kept bargain, and Tellumi keep their record on clay.
- Every culture has an Adventurer's Guide that opens on an arrival and a local greeting, covers power, daily life, faith and places, and ends with a glossary.
- The Khazári live in cities cut into the cliff face, mine by listening to the stone, and keep the deep places for refuges, tombs, oaths and worship.
- The seven Khazári clans are named for their master crafts, and every hold is ruled by its council of elders, never by a lord.
- Jénchài, an island empire of eight great houses under a poor emperor at Léngwou, has its own culture page and gazetteer volume.
- The Biko of central Xerathia's rainforests are many small tribes following four loose traditions, each with its own way of choosing leaders and burying its dead.
- The steppe tribes of the Khazryn, the Sovidur, have their own culture, tongue and year-count, and the Tellumi hold the old oases under the Grazian Mountains.
- A Vrystwald village holds 200 to 500 people under three co-equal elders, a Shaman, a War Chief and an Other Chief, and keeps its own totem animal.
- The Varokh ransom captives whose people can buy them back, count the Nordmen least foreign of all peoples, and hold the Vylarian Empire as a nemesis.
- The Grukar are wholly alien: no trade, envoy or ransom crosses their ground, each tribe answers only to its spawner, and captives are eaten.
- The tribes of the Black Wilds, the Hénhatōutsou, have their own culture page apart from Tāntsou.
- Northern pages cover the life of a raiding crew, funerals from pyre to grave-ale, the whale and seal hunts, and the rites of ting and hall.
- The Khelâthi hold that nothing counts until it is written down, close a promise in four ways, and take strangers' obligations to the temple.

**Places and history**

- Nearly nine hundred places have entries, some still drafts, and twenty-four northern settlements read as places with holders, livelihoods and quarrels.
- Region, city and institution pages across the setting open on what each place is known for and what a newcomer should expect there.
- Every place names the body that governs it, from royal seats and provincial capitals to village councils and guild boards.
- Okháris holds ten village tribes and three temple-cities, running hot and fertile in the west to semi-arid savanna at the Bethûa border.
- Kalihara is one tropical island about four hundred miles across, a ring of cloud-wrapped mountains around a lake-filled bowl, governed by a Council at Ilovanu.
- Kalihara's four great works, left by the vanished Ithári and understood nowhere off the island, are failing, and its towns, lineages, sanctuaries and gear have pages.
- Tarûzel is an archipelago of five city-states off the coast of the Kingdom of Amradad, with its Grand Council at Besîkar.
- The Khazryn is a vast desert and steppe crossed by one camel road to Tāntsou, holding dead cities, mountain labyrinths, blood lands and a northern taiga.
- Vrystwald has a sixty-village roster, fourteen village notes, five frontier reaches and the Deepwood's rivers, with every village's ruling seat and its two rivals named.
- Aurèldía, Aelwyth, Vylaria, Heliónis, Byzaría, Élavendre, Provènzia and Tarvénia each have a written history, with places to visit and adventure hooks.
- The Nordlands date their years by the sittings on Dómsey, and Vrystwald counts in Varokh winters; both have histories of their own.
- Aû'Khelâthu's temple count runs in eight ages with a full king-list, and Vedyara's history in eight eras, each with events and sites carrying hooks.
- A dated history gives each great event of the world a page and a year, setting each people's reading side by side without choosing between them.
- Vedyara's mountain wall has six crossings, five with summit shrines, and the guide-peoples Ösket and Hvarn keep the roads above the last shrine.
- Vedyara's temple-republics, kingdoms and city-states, the Moon City of Chandrapur, its three seas, the march of Bhūmipāla and its everyday gear all have pages.
- Every land states its population, and the north is small: its halls and harbors are villages of a few hundred, with only three towns.
- A setting note explains blood-fields, the haunted grounds of old slaughter: their five kinds, how they harm the living and what ends one.
- Provènzia won its independence in the War of the Charter, no emperor has ever ruled all of Aurèldía, and Heliónis is independent in practice.
- Thalorna has a 365-day year that never drifts, four fixed quarter days, and one moon, Vaelith, on a thirty-day cycle.
- The Vylarian Calendar is the shared year-count, the Great Convergence stands 6,958 years ago, and Hellád keeps an older calendar of its own.
- Vylarian, Tarûzelian, Khelâthi and Vedyaran coins are compendium items priced so a purse can be counted, with copper as token money.

**Faiths and lore**

- The Ten are the gods who won the war against the giant-kin, and each has a senior priest's account of what the god asks and what devotion costs.
- Mótefnir the Maker, god of craft and invention, is the tenth of the Ten, and the Dreadspawn are his making.
- Hrímthur the Rime-Giant was killed and cut into the ground the world stands on, and his faith is kept in the north outside the Ten.
- The north's cosmology covers the ring of ages, the giant the world was cut from, Asguard's four regions where the dead gather, and Aldarlok.
- Twenty-nine devotional orders of the Ten are affiliations of their own, each with a northern name, its English phrase and a shared three-rank ladder.
- The northern feasts of Jel, Somarmál, Midsomar and Vetrnetr, Svartbrandr's Fire-Weeks, and the Rite of Minnir's Well have accounts of their own.
- The Shadow is chaos from outside creation that corrupts mortals and gods, and every undead in every culture is bound to it.
- A dreadspawn is made alive by a celestial hand, a construct is matter moved by a mage, and an undead is a body its spirit has left.
- Qe'âret's Weighing opens both of a Khelâthi's accounts, the scribe's and the gods', and only the unwritten one cannot be bought out.
- The Aurèldían gods carry names of their own, among them Árdavon of the gates, Sélmoros who judges the dead and Ústaron of the forge.
- Vedyara's ten gods each have a lore entry and a ritual skill, and the Itzáni, Nyáluba and Okhárins keep gods and spirit traditions of their own.

**Ranks and affiliations**

- Every character who belongs to a faith, house, guild or order shows the standing held there, read from that body's own ladder.
- Rank ladders list every rung with its level, and standings have pages saying how the law treats a holder and what it costs and wins.
- A Title Lexicon sets every people's ranks and offices side by side, keeping offices such as a marshal or an elected chair apart from rank.
- The northern kingdoms, Stormveld and the Vrystwald tribes list the standings their own law confers, named in Nordmal and Varokhi.
- Seventy-seven temples, guilds, houses and companies of Aû'Khelâthu have pages, and every Khelâthi body ranks its people from Outcast and Bondsman to Zelar and Zabu.
- The Ksêmor Hodhîs is a tradition every mage taught the six currents belongs to, and each school ranks initiate, adept and master.
- The Ashen Vow, the Sealed Word, the Iron Cinder, the Twinblade Order, the Áelendan Wardens and the Twilight House rank members by their own grades.
- A pantheon confers only the standing it grants, so someone who merely holds to the Ten stands as its adherent.
- Affiliations list the common skills their members share.

**Characters**

- The people the setting names in passing have their own character pages, with a portrait where one is drawn, a description and the notes that mention them.
- Profiles state a character's culture and home settlement, the roles they can fill in an adventure, and their patrons, enemies and affiliations as linked entries.
- Every character gives hair, eyes, height, weight, age and skin, and lists visible marks such as scars, tattoos and missing parts.
- Three Varokhi heroes, Véthar, Dómvith and Wíthrún, come with campfire tales and playable character sheets.
- The officers, priests, traders and nobles of Aû'Khelâthu, and its reigning court, each carry an appearance and a dossier.

**Bestiary**

- Ninety-one ordinary animals, from the crocodile and cobra to the lion and eagle, have creature notes placed in Thalorna's own lands.
- Every creature reads as a field-guide entry: where it lives, what it does, the signs that give it away and how local people deal with it.
- Every creature has a Foundry sheet with its attacks, attribute ranges and weight, and its own compendium icon.
- Creatures that win by grappling strike harder for each Tactical Advantage spent on impact while grappling.
- Dragons are mortal and unravel magic worked near them, and a tereb rises again wherever it is destroyed until its Hallowed Ground is desecrated.
- Dreadspawn are made and never born, the learned names for the undead are the tereb and the damut, and Njörven the Sea Wraith has an entry.
- Kalihara has its own animals and flora, and the Khazryn Desert holds eight creatures found nowhere else.

**Adventures**

- Ten dread adventures each strand the party somewhere remote and wrong, turn on a reversal partway through, and end in horror and treasure.
- Every dread adventure plants fair clues on every route and gives the referee read-aloud text, running advice and ways back for missed steps.
- Twenty-five wild places, from the Khazryn Desert to the Nordlands, each have a site page and a five-scene sword-and-sorcery adventure, marked as drafts.
- Places describe what a visitor sees and hears, and keep the truth in sections for the referee alone.
- _The Provenzian Grand Games_ includes the Lordeme festival, its competitions and its place in Provenzian politics.

**Literature**

- Each culture has its own works, from Vylarian, Provenzian, Tarvénan, Helionite and Byzarian collections to Élavendri, Aelwythan and Sinalë songs and tales.
- Northern poems, sagas and laments include the hof poem _Mótmál_, the Ship-Flyting, the Whale-Prophecy and the fort-duty saga of Stórald of Draskborg.
- Varokhi lays, household charms and the ancestor-tally stand beside _The Sturgeon Road_, which tells Véthar's spirit journey in verse.
- Khelâthi hymns, instructions, tales and the litany of the Weighing are quoted in the entries they concern.
- The Khazári keep seven works, including their origin tale, the lament for the sealed city of Khazártúrn and the sayings of twelve masters.
- Vedyara keeps the _Recitation of the Kings of Mahānadi_, _The Forty Days_ and five more temple, river and war works.
- The Tellumi epics, the steppe's calling of the dead, a caravan-master's road tales and two Nyáluba griot songs carry the Khazryn's and Nyáluba's folklore.
- _The Seven Towers_, a Sinalëan elegy for a fallen city of light, is quoted in the entries for the Sinalë and Khazártúrn.

**Languages and names**

- Names and words in every tongue are spelled to read and say easily at the table, with a mark only for unexpected stress or a separately sounded vowel.
- Every name is coined in one of the setting's own tongues, and each tongue has a sound of its own.
- Lexicons for most tongues give their words, the elements names are built from, and every name in the setting with the page where it is met.
- Word lists of several hundred words each cover Sinalë, Vedyari, Khazári and Vylari, with worked examples for coining new words and names at the table.
- The Khazári count in sixteens and the Sinalë in twenties, and both language pages set out their grammar in full.
- Sinalë sounds soft and old, rich in _l_, _r_, _n_ and _th_, and Tāntsou is written in a four-tone sound system.
- The Varokhi page offers eighty-one men's names, seventy-five women's and eighty-eight clans, with the rules for building given, clan and ground names.
- The Nordmal page sets out how every northern name is built, so a referee can coin a person, clan, place, office or god from it.
- Khelâthi long names have a near name never written, Vedyarans offer a short calling name, and the Sinalë never shorten a name at all.
- Dictionaries give a noun's gender and an irregular plural where their language uses them, and each language page lists the names its speakers carry.
- Khazári Pirzath has a carved form and an everyday hand, Khelâthi two hands without vowels, and the Áelendan borrow the Sinalë Veldirno.
- Vylari is the language of learning across the Empire, Helonic the language of culture and the arts, and Valtári the harbor pidgin.

**Foundry and website**

- The module installs as `thalorna` in any Foundry VTT 14 world; its journals read under every system, and its creature, character and item packs appear under Song of Heroic Lands 0.8.8 or later, which it recommends.
- The release carries PDF books: a gazetteer for each of twenty-four regions and peoples, and _The World of Thalorna_, _The Rules of Thalorna_ and _The Bestiary of Thalorna_.
- The setting's calendars ship with it, each as a Foundry calendar and a Calendaria import; Calendaria is optional.
- Thalorna shows its own knight-and-dragon artwork in Foundry's package list and setup screen, and the homepage opens on the same battle.
- Every icon ships inside the module with its artist and licence, so creatures, items, faiths and orders show their own art.
- The first mention of a named person, place or body links to its entry, or is highlighted where no page exists yet.
- Pages end with sections gathering what touches each entry, such as what a place holds, chronology, events and appearances in song and story.
- Draft pages carry a notice saying they are unfinished, and referee passages are secret sections in Foundry and spoilers on the website.
- Terms appear as definition lists, numbered figures and maps carry captions beneath them, and pictures draw at a size suited to their kind.
- Places, faiths, orders and lore show the culture they belong to, and a page with its own hero image shows it in place of the stock banner.
