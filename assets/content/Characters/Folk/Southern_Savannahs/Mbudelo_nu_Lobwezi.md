# Appearance {#appearance}

Mbudelo nu Lobwezi is a 58-year-old man who stands 5'8" tall and is of heavy build. He has brown skin, a shaved scalp, a short gray beard, and dark brown eyes in a wrinkled face. He leans on a staff of baobab wood. He wears sorghum-colored cloth and a necklace of baobab seeds, one for each village of the clan.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Mbudelo nu Lobwezi is the paramount of the [[affiliation-lobwezi|Lobwezi]] and sits at [[place-lombesavlg|Lombesa]]. He holds the place by descent and the consent of the elders, with the senior griots of the villages beside him, and he speaks for the clan at the [[affiliation-greatlubemo|Great Lubemo]]. He keeps the clan's villages and their griots in one order of precedence, and he makes no decision against a village tree's custodian.

He speaks last in a village council, and what he says last is remembered. He consults [[being-bonzimbelbwz|Bonzimbe of the Lobwezi]], the senior griot of the Long Pact.

## Background

**Formation.** He grew up in a village at Lombesa around its baobab, made his offering at the root as a boy, and learned from the village's senior griot to speak last in council.

**Accession.** At forty-eight he became paramount by descent and the elders' consent. In his years in the seat a village of the clan has lost its baobab and dispersed, and its households now live at Lombesa, so he knows what a dead tree costs.

**The record.** The Lobwezi griots sit at the Lubemo as its record, and the Lubemo that is gathering has a war to enter into it. He carries the clan's claim that the war has put the Pact's first term in question: the five do not lift a weapon against the five.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is deliberate, humorless in council and kind at a village fire.

### Motivation

He wants the Pact's terms recited whole before the renewal, whatever the Lubemo decides, and he wants the record exact.

### Strengths

The griots of every Lobwezi village answer him, and the other four clans accept their record as the record.

### Weaknesses

He holds by custom and not by force, and the record is trusted only while the griots do not choose sides.
