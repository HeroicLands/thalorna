# Appearance {#appearance}

The Ékunda Delegate travels light in a rawhide tunic with a staff and a water-skin, and waits to be asked before speaking, as an officer who carries other people's words does.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The Ékunda Delegate carries the elders' answer to the [[affiliation-greatlubemo|Great Lubemo]] at [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]] and speaks for the open water of the spring. The delegate binds the [[affiliation-ekunda|Ékunda Tribe]] only with the elders' agreement.

## Background

The Delegate sat among the village's householders and then among its elders, carried messages to other clans as a young kinsman and learned the order of speaking at the sitting before being named to speak for the tribe.

## Psyche

### Personality

Careful and exact about what has been agreed.

### Motivation

The Delegate wants the elders' brief kept and entered in the record of the Lubemo.

### Strengths

The Delegate knows the elders' answer and the order of business.

### Weaknesses

The Delegate cannot bind the tribe beyond what the elders have said.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

The Delegate answers to the elders and to [[being-ndowelaeknd|Ndowela nu Ékunda]].

## Plot Hooks

1. [[scenario-trnsatsprng|The Turns at the Spring]]: the Delegate needs a trusted traveler to carry the elders' brief to Heto Lubemo and speaks for the open water at the sitting.
2. [[scenario-cnvnrspkslst|The Convener Speaks Last]]: the Delegate may speak for the tribe at the sitting and binds it only with the elders' agreement.
