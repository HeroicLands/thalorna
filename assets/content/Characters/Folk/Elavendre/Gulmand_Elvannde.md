# Appearance {#appearance}

Gulmand Élvannde is a 65-year-old man who stands 5'8" tall and is of light build. He is small and stooped, with white hair thin under a circlet of woven living apple wood that he has tended for forty years. His face is weathered and deeply lined and his green eyes are bright. He wears a gray wool robe stained at the hem with orchard grass.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Gulmand Élvannde is the grove-elder of the oldest orchard at [[place-lamanis|Lámanis]], the cider town of the middle basin a day above Péravel by barge. He tries cases in the old orchard and nowhere else, and the town's court of orchard-owners sends him the quarrels that concern trees. The town's first trees, it says, were planted by hand from stock the Áelendan brought down.

At the foot of the orchard stands a stone-lipped spring in a ring of ancient trees. Every household in the town drew its first cupful of the year from it at the blossom festival. At midwinter the water sank, and by spring the basin was dry. A party of University scholars lodged in the town in the autumn, measured the well and left a day later with a long case on a mule. Gulmand has written to the court at Péravel and been answered with an invitation to submit a petition, which is to say, to wait.

## Psyche

### Personality

Gulmand is gentle, stubborn and unhurried. He speaks to the trees, to the well and to a visitor in the same voice. He does not raise it, and a refusal from him is a statement that the matter is closed.

### Motivation

He wants the lip-stone found and brought home. He wants the town to come back to the well, and he does not want the cider-pressers to be punished for being afraid.

### Strengths

The orchard and the town trust him. He knows every tree by its year and every household by its first cupful.

### Weaknesses

Half the town does not want a quarrel with a University that buys its cider. He is old, and a petition that waits is a petition that is forgotten.

## Social

### Companions

The cooperage hall's master keeps the orchard-owners' court. The sheriff's reeve comes up from Péravel when the levy is in dispute.

### Patrons

The Grove Council, whose circlet he wears, and whose elders will answer his letters.

### Enemies

The levy-clerk, who wants the whole quarrel to go away before the autumn assessment. The lord who owns the largest presses, who does not like to be reminded of the well.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-groveconcl|The Grove Council]]**: Gulmand is Grove-Elder of the Lámanis orchard.
- **[[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Kingdom of Élavendre]]**: He is a Freeman of the crown.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The stone from the well.** The elder wants the lip-stone found and brought home, and will pay in kind, in introductions to the elders of the Grove Council.
2. **The petition.** The levy-clerk wants the whole quarrel to go away before the autumn assessment, and would pay a party to persuade the elder to withdraw. The elder asks the same party to carry his petition to Péravel instead.
3. **The blossom festival.** The festival is three weeks off, and the town splits between those who want the well sung over and those who will not go to the orchard. Gulmand asks a party to stand at the orchard gate and learn who comes.

:::secret {title="The well-stone"}
The stone is in a chapterhouse at Péravel, entered in a catalog without the name of the well. The University's Keeper of the Collections recorded the origin in a private ledger that no one but she reads. The spring is not an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] and has no door or slow hour: it is sacred ground, kept for centuries by people who spoke to it, and it stopped because the stone it ran through was lifted out.

Set back in its place by someone who sings the old words over it, it runs again within a night. Gulmand knows the old words. He has not been asked to sing them.
:::
