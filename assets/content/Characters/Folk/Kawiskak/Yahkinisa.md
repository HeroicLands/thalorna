# Appearance {#appearance}

Yahkinisa is a 43-year-old woman who stands 5'2" tall with a light build. She has tawny skin, chapped complexion, black hair, and dark brown eyes.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Yahkinisa, Watches the North Wind, is the lodge-shaker of the Apowak, the Loon band of the [[lore-kawiskakclt|Spruce People]], who summer at the [Apowosipa]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Loon Lake, in the forests of the [[place-kawiskakrgn|Kawiskak Region]]. When a band needs to know something no one can see, she builds a shaking lodge (_tisoman_), a narrow tower of poles wrapped in hides, and goes in alone at dusk. The lodge sways and shakes, voices speak from inside it, and the spirits answer the band's questions through her mouth. A lodge-shaker has no authority beyond the answers, and one whose answers keep failing is not asked again. Yahkinisa is asked often.

A family of [Barren-Ground People]{.name data-type="lore/culture"} came starving to the lake last winter and is still there. The lake keeper, [[being-osipisa|Osipisa]], fed them from her own stores, and half the bands that meet at the lake have not forgiven her. Four generations ago, at the same lake, a lodge-shaker's voices named a starving party of the same people _mohkisk_, the starving spirit in human bodies, and the bands killed them on the ice. Since then no lodge-shaker's word can name a person _mohkisk_ on its own; the whole lake must hear the case in summer.

Yahkinisa has proposed a middle way: the strangers should be given the hot fat, in front of the whole lake at the summer gathering, so that everyone can see whether there is ice in them. Osipisa has refused, because the fat is for one of the people whose heart is turning, and giving it to the strangers says they are _mohkisk_ without a hearing. Yahkinisa answers that the fat never harmed anyone, and that a lake that will not look is a lake that is afraid of what it might see. The strangers have been asked nothing.

She is forty-three, thin, with a carrying voice and no patience with what she calls the lake's shame. A party at Loon Lake in summer may be asked to stand witness when the fat is given, or to take the strangers away before it is.

What she wants is the strangers tested in front of the whole lake, so that nobody need be afraid of them or of the lake's old shame.
