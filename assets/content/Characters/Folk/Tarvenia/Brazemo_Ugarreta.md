# Appearance {#appearance}

Brazemo Ugarreta is a 48-year-old man who stands 5'7" tall and is of medium build. He is a sturdy farmer gone gray, with a barley-grower's hands and the castle's badge pinned on a coat he keeps for assembly days.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Brazemo Ugarreta farms barley on the valley floor below [[place-durgalis|Dúrgalis]], and the lord of the castle, [[being-ugarrektsol|Ugarrek Tsoluregi]], has named him his steward. On the assembly day he sits in the square under the castle and says what the lord thinks, and the valley weighs it as one voice among the rest. The [[affiliation-cncldrgls|Council of Dúrgalis]] has no officer of its own, and Brazemo is not one: he speaks for the castle, and the assembly speaks for itself.

The lord leans on him more than he admits, especially in the matter of the valley's debts on the coast, which Ugarrek cannot read and Brazemo can.

## Psyche

### Personality

Brazemo is plain, patient and loyal to both the castle and the valley, which he holds to be the same loyalty.

### Motivation

He wants the hail counted honestly, the Valaron debt kept from falling to a lowland house and the bandits off the pass road.

### Strengths

The free men trust him because he farms beside them, and the lord trusts him because he reads.

### Weaknesses

He has no power in the assembly but his voice, and the valley is quick to remind him.

## Social

### Companions

Tsola Galtsuregi, the castle's notary, and the farmers of the valley floor.

### Patrons

Ugarrek Tsoluregi, lord of the castle.

### Enemies

The bandits of the pass road, and the Valthari house that holds the Valaron note.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-cncldrgls|Council of Dúrgalis]]**: Brazemo holds the office of Steward, as a Freeman.
- **[[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Kingdom of Tarvénia]]**: He is a freeman of the realm.
- **[[affiliation-menutsa|The Mother]]**: He swears at the stone in the square.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The note on the ship.** The steward wants to read the Valaron note before the Valthari house calls it, and the house will not show it to a farmer.
2. **The count.** A man swore in the square that the hail spared his field, and it did not. The steward wants the lie found before the autumn count, without the castle seeming to judge the assembly.
