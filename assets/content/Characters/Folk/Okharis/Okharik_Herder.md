# Appearance {#appearance}

An Okharik Herder wears rough hide against thorn and night, carries a spear, a knife and a skin of soured milk, and walks behind the herd at its pace.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Grazing in [[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] is not owned. It is held by customary right, so that this clan takes that valley in that season, and the rights are ancient, minutely known and the commonest cause of bloodshed in the realm. A herder keeps a household's cattle on the clan's grazing, counts them morning and evening, and learns the grazing, the cairns and the rights from the household. A stranger met on the track is handed soured milk, cold from the shade, and the herders stand by to see whether it is drunk.

## Background

A herder is raised to the work in the household, walking with the herd as a child before carrying a spear. Some young herders are called to the Hunt, and some go to the Trial of Flames as warriors.

## Psyche

### Personality

Watchful and plain-spoken.

### Motivation

The herder wants the herd whole and the clan's grazing kept.

### Strengths

The herder reads cattle, weather and ground, and knows which clan grazes where in each season.

### Weaknesses

The herder answers for every head lost, and a loss counts against the household and the clan at the moot.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

A herder answers to the household and the lineage, and through them to the clan's chieftain.

## Plot Hooks

1. [[scenario-shuremisadv|The Hearth Without Light]]: a young herder who has lost a household's cattle to the ash-ground at the Ash-Fold watches from the wall at noon and guides strangers across the cattle-ground for a share.
2. **First on the valley.** Two herders of neighboring clans meet in a valley that each says is its clan's for the season, and one asks strangers to stand witness to which herd came first.
3. **The tribute count.** A herder of an eastern clan drives the cattle the clan owes Bethua for grazing its grandfathers held free, and wants company on the road to the Bethuan posts, where the count is taken head by head.
