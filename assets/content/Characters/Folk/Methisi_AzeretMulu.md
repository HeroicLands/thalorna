# Appearance {#appearance}

Methisi Azeret'Mulu is a 49-year-old man who stands 5'9" tall and is of light build. He has brown skin, a shaven and oiled head, a thin line of kohl at each eye and dark brown eyes that are calm and attentive. He dresses in fine white linen in the Khelathi manner, with a broad collar of beads and gold and a scribe's palette at his belt, and over it, when he leaves the Strangers' Row, a heavy Vilérian cloak against the cold. He speaks excellent Vilari, slowly and a little formally, and smiles often.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Methisi Azeret'Mulu is the factor of the Khelathi temple-estates in [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]: the agent of [[place-zelgeru|the Zelgeru]], the great temple-estates of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]], and of the treasury of the Gar-Au behind them. He keeps a counting-room and a warehouse on the walled [Strangers' Row]{.name data-type="place/site"} on the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]], where the city's few registered Khelathi traders live by license, and he is the most senior Khelathi in the empire. The Watch locks the Row's gate on him at dusk like any other foreigner.

He offers the throne what its own houses cannot: grain and silver. The temple-estates have more grain than the delta can eat, and he offers the throne a fleet's worth of it each year on credit, and Khelathi silver in exchange for the throne's paper. Every coin he lends is a coin the throne need not borrow from [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]]. Orzara divides over him. The dealers in the Seldan want his grain and his silver; the hawks remember that Khelathi gold paid for the war Taruzel won, and call every loan he makes a war chest.

## Background

**The house.** He is a Zabu, of a titled house of the delta that has served the temple-estates as scribes and stewards for many generations. He was trained in the scribal schools and keeps his own accounts in both Khelathi hands. He served the temple-estates' trade in Ólvestra and in the Bizarian ports before he was sent to Orzara.

**Orzara.** He came to the Strangers' Row three years after the Taruzelian war, when no Khelathi could walk the River Circle without the Watch beside him. He has spent nine years making himself useful: lending quietly to houses that could not borrow elsewhere, buying their notes from creditors who wanted out, and sending grain at cost in two lean winters. Several senators now owe him more than they owe anyone in Viléria.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is courteous, patient and very hard to read. He never argues and never threatens, and he lets a debtor understand his position without being told it. He is genuinely interested in Vilérian law, rites and history and asks good questions about them, which some of his hosts find flattering and others find alarming.

### Motivation

He wants the throne's grain contract, a fleet a year from the delta to the River Circle on Khelathi credit, and he wants the throne's paper in Khelathi hands. He wants votes in the Seldan through the debts he holds, enough to keep the hawks from closing the Row. He says he wants friendship between two old empires, and he says it well.

### Strengths

He has more silver behind him than any house in the city. He owns debts in a dozen senatorial houses, and their votes. He knows how the Danvar's paper works better than most of its own partners, and he is never in a hurry.

### Weaknesses

He is a foreigner in a city that hates his country. He lives behind a gate the Watch locks at dusk. His grain arrives in one fleet, once a year, and his whole standing depends on it arriving. The stairs of the River Circle want him dead.

## Social

### Patrons

The temple-estates and the Gar-Au's treasury, whose silver he lends. He has no patron in Viléria and does not expect one.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The locked gate.** A senator who owes Methisi money must see him tonight, after the Row's gate is barred. The senator hires a party to get the factor out, or get himself in, without the Watch's knowledge.
2. **The lean winter.** The river is low and the Khelathi grain fleet is a week from the river mouth. Methisi hires a party to ride down the river and see the fleet's lighters past the shallows before the Grain House's friends can delay them.
3. **The hawk's son.** The son of a hawk senator has borrowed from Methisi under a false name. Methisi asks a party to deliver the note to the father in person, with the factor's compliments and no demand for payment.
4. **The burned warehouse.** Someone has thrown oil and a torch over the wall of the Strangers' Row into Methisi's warehouse. The Watch says it is investigating. Methisi wants a party to learn who did it before the Watch decides it was an accident.

:::secret {title="What the factor needs"}
Au'Khelathu spent more on Taruzel's war than its treasury admits, and the temple-estates have carried a share of it. The silver Methisi offers the throne is real, and it is lent against the next flood's harvest. His instructions are not to buy dependence cheaply, whatever the hawks believe. They are to get returns: interest in coin, paid on the day, and liens on revenues the throne cannot easily repudiate. He has found one. Through a Helionite partner he has agreed to lend [[being-ylzmsmlvrts|Yalzemas Molvarates]] the advance on a ten-year contract for the land tax of Hilen, secured on the contract.

He is also buying rooms inside the line. A citizen of the Grand Market buys tenements on Nerval from [[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]] in his own name with Methisi's silver, one stair at a time, and lets them to Khelathi sailors who have no right to sleep inside the line. It is the first Khelathi property in Orzara outside the Row, and it is unlawful.

The grain fleet is his weakness and the city's. It sails once a year from the delta in one convoy and lies a week at anchor off the river mouth while lighters carry its grain up to the River Circle. A fire at that anchorage, a bribed pilot or a blocked channel would end Orzara's outside grain for a season, and Methisi's standing with it.

He does not know that a society of veterans on a stair of the River Circle means to kill him on the night of the next dole.

**Hook: the anchorage.** A Vaskelen lighterman tells a party that a man paid him to learn which night the Khelathi fleet's grain hulls will lie closest to the river mouth's reed-beds.
:::
