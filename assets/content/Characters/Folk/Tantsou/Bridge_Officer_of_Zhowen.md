# Appearance {#appearance}

The officer stands at the foot of the bridge in a linen coat and knee boots, a shortsword at the hip and the day's list in one hand, and reads each name on a permission aloud before looking up at its bearer.

# Dossier {#dossier}

A Bridge Officer of Zhowen keeps the foot of the [Jingdou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"} or the [Bwidou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the two long bridges that run from the shores of [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]] to the island of the enclave. Permission to cross is written, and the officers at each bridge foot keep the day's list of those who hold it. The officer reads each permission against the list. A person who sets foot on a bridge without permission is shot dead by the archers on the enclave walls; no magistrate hears the case, and no plea is entered.

## Background

The officer is a [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]], posted to the bridge foot as to any other post of the throne, and has read the list there long enough to know its petitioners by their papers.

## Psyche

### Personality

Exact and unbending.

### Motivation

The officer wants the list kept, and nobody on the bridge who is not on it.

### Strengths

The officer knows the day's list and every name on it.

### Weaknesses

The officer reads the list and not the person, and a name entered wrong is a name that is not there.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

The officer answers to the [[being-gvrnrzhowen|Governor of Zhowen]], whose officers keep the bridge lists, and the archers on the walls keep the enclave's side of the water.

## Plot Hooks

1. [[scenario-flngnwrgn|A Filing in the New Reign]]: the officer reads the Dúnavarre embassy's permission against the day's list on the morning of its hearing.
2. **The servant who did not come back.** A foreign merchant wants to know whether a missing servant's name was on a day's list, and the officer keeps the lists.
3. **A name entered wrong.** A petitioner's name on the list is written otherwise than on the permission, and the officer lets nobody cross until the two agree.
