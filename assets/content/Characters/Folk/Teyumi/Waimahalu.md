# Dossier {#dossier}

Waimahalu, Sun Dance, is the rain chief (_lumamota_) of [[place-yaahosa|Grandmother House]], the great house of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] where every town meets as one at the canyon feast. He keeps the town's chief rain rites, orders its year, settles what its chambers cannot, and speaks first at the feast. His elder sister, [[being-yalutseya|Yalutseya]], is the house head, and decides who eats.

Three summers have come up short of rain. At last year's feast Waimahalu said aloud that the rain was being kept from the canyon, and that the bear doctors should be asked to search the towns for the dry hearts responsible. In the [[lore-lumaponu|Rain Road]] a Dry Search goes through a town house by house over four days, and on the fifth morning the bear doctors name the dry hearts in the plaza; each is sent to a chamber elder or put out as one of the Dry. None has been held at the canyon feast, where it would bind every town at once, since the [Year of Shut Doors]{.name data-type="lore/history"}. He has said that the [[place-hepuhosa|Door House]] is gathering every dry heart in the north into one canyon, and that the rain will not come until somebody looks at who is eating there.

This summer, on the last day of the rain dances, the clowns swept the plaza with a turkey feather looking under the pots for dry hearts, and named every dog and child they passed through a lump of quartz. Waimahalu laughed as he had to. He has not sent the clowns' chamber its corn. He is fifty-five, gaunt, devout and frightened for his people, and he believes every word he says. A party at the feast is watched by his bear doctors, and a party of strangers who eat from the canyon's corn in a dry year are the first thing they will look at.

What he wants is the dry hearts found and named at the canyon feast, so that the rain can find the canyon again.
