# Dossier {#dossier}

Kenuvawesa, Canal Walking, of the Water clan, is the canal chief (_kenuvamota_) of [[place-kenuvahosa|Canal House]] on the lowland canals of the [[place-kenuvasokorgn|Canal Plain]], a lean man of fifty who knows every head gate on the plain by its stones. Every household on the main canal and its branches waters its fields by turns, and he names the turns at the spring planting, standing on the mound with the sun watcher. A household that takes water out of turn loses its next turn, and one that does it twice loses its share of the canal. The house head keeps the storerooms, as in any great house of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]], but on the plain the canal chief's word is the one a farmer fears.

This winter a southern trader came up out of the desert by a western route to the Canal House, sold his cacao and copper bells there, and went home with turquoise without ever coming to the [[place-kwatuhosa|South House]], whose Keeper of the South Road decides what goes north and at what price. The Keeper, [[being-aalohalu|Aalohalu]], sent a runner to ask why. Kenuvawesa answered that a guest who comes to the door is fed.

He is exact, unsmiling and fair, and the plain trusts him more than it likes him. A party coming up out of the desert by the western route arrives at his door, and he feeds it, asks what it carries, and lets it trade. A party that wants water for its animals on the plain takes it in turn, like everyone else, or finds out how far his word runs.

What he wants is every turn of water on the plain kept, and no house in the canyons telling the Canal House whom it may feed.
