# Appearance {#appearance}

Zarmelas Helarkales is a 78-year-old man who stands 5'9" tall and has wasted to a scant frame. He has light skin gone sallow and deeply lined, white hair cut close under the diadem, and gray eyes that are still quick. He sits more than he stands, in the purple of the throne over layers of fine wool against a cold nobody else in the room feels, and he keeps something in his hands at all times: a stylus, a cup, the ivory rod of the Sarnaks Maldor, because his hands shake when they are empty. His voice is dry and quiet, and the court has learned to be silent before he speaks so as not to miss it.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Zarmelas Helarkales is the reigning Orzas of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], the sovereign whose name is hallowed like a god's and who rules from the [[place-palvar|Palvar]] in the Inner City of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]. He is old, he is ill, and he has adopted nobody. Every great house, the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], [[affiliation-sorvan|the Imperial Guard]] and every man who hopes to be named are counting what that will cost. Three names are spoken of on the Heights: [[being-klvnsmrdvns|Kolvenas Mardovanes]] of the Grain House, the Senate's choice; [[being-hlzrsvndrn|Halzoras Vendaren]], a legate and the Guard's favorite; and [[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]], the old houses' man.

He has no money. The throne pays the veterans' pension with silver borrowed from the great houses of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], rolls the loans over as they fall due and puts nothing by ([[affiliation-tarmex|The Tarmeks Orzan]]). He is not toothless. He can have a man put to death for treason, and the proscription board can strike a house from the rolls and forfeit everything it owns to the treasury. He can also make men: offices, Elevated standing, a Senate grant of the citizenship and the seal on a charter are his to give, and he sells them, quietly, for coin now. The new houses going up on [[place-selvin|Selvin]] are his reign's purchases.

## Background

**The adopted son.** He was born to a minor branch of a Silver house and served the reign before his own as a _felzos_ (legate) in the provinces, and then as one of the six with standing access to the privy presence. The Orzas before him had no son. He adopted Zarmelas at fifty-five, in the last winter of his own life, and Zarmelas took the name of House Helarkales with the diadem. He has reigned twenty-three years, and the court still remembers that he was a careful choice and not a loved one.

**The lost war.** The war in which Taruzel won its independence was fought and lost in his reign. He did not command it, and he signed the recognition within a year of the defeat at [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]]. The officer corps blames the Senate, the Senate blames the officers, and the veterans blame whoever is nearest. Nobody blames the Orzas aloud. He knows exactly how much that silence is worth, and that it lasts only as long as he does.

**The long decline.** He has not left Arvon since the spring before last except for the state's days, when he is carried to the [[place-pnthnrldnm|Temple of the Twelve]] to make the first offering as the cult's chief priest.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is patient, dry and courteous, and he has never in his reign been seen to lose his temper. He listens far more than he speaks and lets petitioners talk themselves into the corner he chose for them before they arrived. He likes to be told the truth and rarely is, and he rewards the few who tell it by keeping them close and powerless. He has a dry humor that the court has learned to dread, because it is usually the first sign that a man is about to lose something.

### Motivation

He wants to die on the throne with the empire's credit intact, and he wants to choose the man who follows him rather than have the choice made over his body. Beneath both is a plainer wish: he does not want to be remembered as the Orzas who lost Taruzel and then lost the money. He thinks in seasons, like everyone else in Orzara, and the next season's rollover matters more to him than the next reign's bill.

### Strengths

He holds the only powers in the empire that cannot be bought from someone else: to proscribe, to adopt and to sell what the throne grants. He knows the court better than any man in it, because he has outlived most of the men who knew it better.

### Weaknesses

He is dying, and the city knows it. His treasury is empty, and every coin he spends is borrowed from a man who wants something for it. His power to proscribe is a weapon he can use only once against any one house, and only against a house the others will not rise to defend. The longer he waits to name an heir, the more each house prepares for the night he dies.

## Social

### Patrons

He has none. Every man in the empire owes the Orzas his place, at least in law.

### Enemies

He has no declared enemy and many patient ones.

- [Conservative Faction]{.name data-type="affiliation"}: The faction in the Seldan that has never forgiven him for the Taruzel recognition.
- The old houses: Houses cheapened by his sales of titles, who resent the new houses on Selvin and the throne that made them.

### Creditors and Servants

- [[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]]: Senior partner of the House of Veldis, who holds more of the throne's paper than any other lender and presses gently at every rollover. He treats her as the most dangerous person in the empire after himself.
- [[being-krznsthlrts|Korzenas Thalurates]]: Master of House Thalurates, who buys the debts of the houses whose votes the throne needs. The Orzas uses him and watches him.
- [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]]: The Imperial _Tarkelos_ (treasury officer), who holds the Tarmeks at the Orzas's pleasure and negotiates every rollover. The Orzas trusts him as far as he trusts anyone.
- [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]]: Master of House Mardovanes, who dines the succession every week on Selvin without naming the Orzas, and whose house wants a temple for the Orzas Azénales that only the throne and the Senate can give.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|The Vilérian Empire]]**: Orzas, holding the title of Lusos, the speaker for the citizen body, for what it lets him block.
- **[[affiliation-imperialcult|Imperial Cult]]**: Sarnaks Maldor, the cult's chief priest, an office he holds in his own person.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The patent's price.** A provincial family of standing wants Elevated rank and has the coin. It hires a party to carry its petition to the palace, learn from the Orzas's secretary what the rank costs this season, and come back without being seen to have asked.
2. **The proscription board.** The board meets, and the name of a senator who lent the throne too much is entered for its next session. The senator's household hires a party to learn who put it there, and the answer is the Orzas's own secretary.
3. **The title on Selvin.** A merchant who bought Elevated standing from the throne last year finds that the patent bears a seal the Offices do not recognize. He wants a party to find out whether he bought it from the Orzas or from someone selling in the Orzas's name.
4. **The offering.** The Orzas is to be carried to the Temple of the Twelve for the state's day, and the Imperial Guard has doubled his escort without saying why. A party hired to the procession's train sees which officers are watching the crowd and which are watching the litter.

:::secret {title="How the Orzas plays the succession"}
The Orzas knows he has months. His physician told him in the spring, and the apothecary's ledger in the Gilded Quarter says the same to anyone who can read it.

He also knows what the succession is worth to him while it is open. Every house that lends to the throne lends partly on the hope that its candidate will be named and its paper honored by the next reign. The moment he adopts, the houses whose men were not chosen stop lending, call what they can, and begin to buy the Guard. The throne would default before the new heir was dressed. So the succession is his last collateral, and he spends it slowly. He lets each house's candidate believe he is favored, through the old man's courtesy, through a seat at a feast, through a sentence the court repeats, and he takes each house's loan on the strength of it.

Three adoption deeds lie drafted in his secretary's strongbox, each in a different name and none sealed. He reads them in the evenings. His secretary does not know whether he has chosen one. Until a deed bears his seal, each claimant has reason to keep bargaining.

His sales are recorded in a book his secretary keeps: every office, title and grant sold this reign, with the price and the buyer. It is the throne's best revenue after the fee, and the next Orzas can use it to unmake every house it made. Several buyers would pay heavily to have their entries cut out.

A Vilérian claimant has secretly offered Nartun's priests the proscription of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] in return for their knights and their temple coin behind his adoption. The Orzas has heard of the offer. He does not believe any Orzas could deliver it, because the Black Flame has patrons in the Seldan and preachers on every Circle in the Warrens, and he is curious to see which claimant thinks he can.

**Hook: the fourth deed.** A scrivener of the Gilded Quarter has been paid to draw a fourth adoption deed in the Orzas's hand, perfect to the letter, and leaves the city the next morning. A party is hired by one claimant's household to find the scrivener before another claimant's household does.
:::
