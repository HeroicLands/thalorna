# Appearance {#appearance}

Sathemar Ardelen is a 61-year-old man who stands 5'7" tall and is of medium build, still straight-backed and hard in the arm. He has light skin weathered by thirty years of sun, white hair cut close to the skull, gray eyes and a white sword-cut across the back of his left hand. He walks slowly to hide a limp. He wears a veteran's plain tunic and cloak and the bronze discharge token of a senior centurion on a thong at his neck, and nothing else that would mark him out from the men in the dole queue. His voice is a parade-ground voice kept low, and when he raises it a whole Circle hears.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Sathemar Ardelen is the man the veterans' stairs of the River Circle and the Spire Circle call their tribune. He holds no office. The stairs of [[place-warrensorz|the Warrens]] and the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]] are organized by legion, each with a senior veteran whose word counts on it, and over twenty years the senior veterans of some sixty stairs have come to send their quarrels with the city to Sathemar. He petitions the courts and the Seldan for them, speaks for them in the dole queue, and stands on the steps of the Spire Circle's pay-hall on dole-day where every man in the queue can see him.

He promises them the pension. It is the legion's promise, earned by twenty years and graded by the rank a man left with, and Sathemar tells the stairs at every dole that no Orzas, no Senate and no banker will ever touch it while he lives. The stairs believe him. Every claimant to the throne knows that the man who has Sathemar's word has the Circles on the night the Orzas dies, and every claimant has sent someone to ask for it.

## Background

**The legion.** He enlisted at fifteen in a legion of the western frontier and rose by the commoner's track to _hundar maldor_ (senior centurion), the summit of it, in his last four years. He fought in four campaigns, was wounded twice and decorated three times, and was discharged at thirty-five with the citizenship and a senior centurion's pension.

**The stairs.** He took a room on his legion's stair on Nerval and worked as a foreman of porters on the River Circle for twenty years. He settled quarrels on the stair, then on the stairs around it, then in the porters' guild. When a landlord let a legion's stair to strangers eleven years ago, it was Sathemar who took the old men to the courts and won, and the stairs began to call him tribune, the word a legion uses for the officer who speaks for the ranks.

**The petition.** Last winter a watchman of the River Circle was knifed by a man of [[affiliation-cleanshlds|The Clean Shields]], who was paid off and turned out the same night. The stairs of the River Circle petitioned the [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]] to have the company removed from the river, and Sathemar has taken up the petition.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is grave, plain-spoken and patient, and he listens to the oldest man on a stair before he listens to anyone else. He is honest in small things and proud of it. He has no vices the stairs can see, and he lives on his pension in one room, which the stairs see too.

### Motivation

He wants the pension untouched, for the men who earned it and the widows who hold it after them. He wants the foreign companies off the river and out of the city's gate-yards. He wants the stairs to have a voice the Seldan must hear, and he wants to be that voice.

### Strengths

The stairs trust him, and he can bring a Circle into the street or keep it at home. Every claimant needs him. He is a good speaker, an experienced officer, and the most respected man in the Warrens after the Master of the Irons, whom he outranks in the opinion of every old soldier.

### Weaknesses

His whole standing rests on a promise the throne pays with borrowed money. He holds no office, owns nothing, and can be ruined by a single dole-day that comes short. He hates the Taruzelians and the Khelathi who helped them, and says so.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The petition.** Sathemar asks a party to find the man of the Clean Shields who knifed the watchman, so that the petition has a name and a face before the court sits.
2. **The claimant's dinner.** A claimant's agent has invited Sathemar to dine. He asks a party to come as his guests and to remember everything the agent promises, because he means to hold the household to it in public.
3. **The short dole.** The Spire Circle's pay-hall has paid half a ward short and closed its doors, and the queue is still standing at noon. Sathemar asks a party to find out from the Dole desk in Arvon whether it is a clerk's error or the beginning of something, and to be back before the queue decides for itself.
4. **The widow's token.** A widow of his own stair says her dead husband's pension is still being drawn at another ward. Sathemar wants the man who is drawing it, quietly.

:::secret {title="What the tribune knows"}
Sathemar knows the pension is borrowed. Four years ago his sister's son, a scribe of the Dole desk in the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]], showed him the desk's monthly warrant and the Tarmeks's note on the back of it: the sum drawn against the house loans to meet it. He has known since that the promise he makes at every dole is a loan the throne rolls over each quarter. He cannot say it. The stairs would not believe him, or they would, and either way he would be finished, and the man who said it after him would be a worse one.

He also knows that his stairs take a share of the City Watch's night tallies, the coin the watch-houses take for letting men without the right sleep inside the line, and that the senior veterans who keep the stairs are paid for their silence. He has never taken a coin of it and has never stopped it.

A society of officers and veterans of the eastern legions, meeting on a stair of the River Circle, means to kill the Khelathi factor before the throne takes his loan, so that the loan dies with him. Sathemar has been asked to give the society his word. He has not given it and has not refused it, and he has told nobody, which the society takes as consent.

He also wants to rise, and does not say so: a Senate grant for his name, a seat on a Circle's council, an office under the next reign. A claimant who offers him one of those in private gets a hearing no public offer of patronage ever has.

**Hook: the society's knife.** A young veteran of the society comes to a party, frightened, and says the killing is set for the night of the next dole, on the quays. He wants the tribune told, because he believes the tribune would stop it, and he does not know that the tribune already knows.
:::
