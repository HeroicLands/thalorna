# Appearance {#appearance}

Volkema Satheris is 39 years old. She has a weathered face, dark gray eyes and black hair cut level with her jaw. A blue anchor within a ring of rope marks her right arm. Her fingers are thickened by rope work, and she turns a ledger’s leaves with the side of her hand to keep the writing clean. She stands to speak when the Roll House’s court is crowded.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Volkema is the Korvelos, Keeper of the Roll of the [[affiliation-seamensgld|Seamen's Guild of Elissara]]. A captain’s daughter, she sails eleven years as a hand before taking charge of the roll, which she has kept for six. She is the first woman to hold it. The old hands trust her arithmetic, and the hands ashore choose the Korvelos each year by a show of hands when the sailing year opens.

The roll gives work in turn to the hands ashore and unhired. A captain can call a particular hand by name for an extra fee, but cannot simply fill a crew outside it. Volkema wants the roll to keep earning livelihoods and paying for the guild’s obligations. The owners want more control of crews and wages, and the fleet wants copies of the names. An officer waits in [[place-aelissium|Elissara]] for a copy she insists on checking name by name.

## Background

**The voyages.** Eleven years at sea give Volkema a practical understanding of what a promised wage buys and what an unfit hull can cost. Her father’s calling does not exempt her from sailing as a hand.

**The book.** Election puts her over the hiring order rather than over the captains at sea. She learns to explain a disputed place on the roll with the recorded voyages open between herself and the complainant.

**The sixth year.** She keeps the guild’s accounts while its members face pressure from owners and the fleet. Guild money buys burials, support for the widows of men lost at sea, and food and beds between voyages. A poor season reaches those commitments before it reaches a council debate.

## Psyche

### Personality

Volkema is brisk with familiar hands and careful with strangers who ask for lists. She lets an angry member finish, then asks which entry he disputes.

### Motivation

She wants the guild to keep its promises to the hands and their households without handing their working lives to the shipowners or the navy.

### Strengths

She knows the work represented by the marks beside each name. Her accounts can turn a general accusation of favoritism into a particular hiring decision that can be examined.

### Weaknesses

She takes on decisions that properly belong to the membership when she fears an open argument leaves the hands exposed. Trust in her arithmetic does not give her their consent to every use of the roll.

## Social

The Marker works at the Roll House’s door and knows the hands by their marks. The guild’s elected representative sits on the city’s council separately from Volkema’s office. [[being-znthnlnthms|Zenothen Linthimis]] presides there over a majority held by shipowners, merchants and small crafts, despite the seamen’s numbers. [[being-arklrdsklmkns|Arkalordas Kalmakanes]] is among the senior hands whose named hiring fees help maintain the house.

### Affiliations

- **Seamen’s Guild of Elissara**: a Hand elected to hold Korvelos.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The navy’s copy.** Volkema asks a party to check a section of the copy against the men it names before the fleet’s officer collects it. A discrepancy needs an answer from a living hand, not merely a corrected spelling.
2. **The struck ship.** An owner disputes the guild’s judgment that his ship is unfit. Volkema wants an independent account of the hull and stores before the quarrel becomes a refusal to hire any guild hands.
3. **The burned bar.** The Marker recognizes a struck hand sailing under another name, his barred anchor burned out. Volkema asks a party to establish whose berth he takes and whether the captain knows, before the ship leaves the basin.

:::secret {title="The lodgings on the copy"}
The fleet wants the roll for a press, not for ordinary hiring. Its officers mean to take every marked man from his lodging when the fleet is ordered against Taruzel. Volkema has guessed the purpose and sells the copies anyway: the guild needs the money, and she believes the fleet would take the men without them. In the copy she checks for the waiting officer, every called hand’s lodging is written wrong. The Marker knows; the old hands do not. The guild’s council representative would carry the matter to the Tevran if he knew.
:::
