# Appearance {#appearance}

Brazemos Tharkis is 47 years old. He is broad-shouldered, with a reddish-brown beard, a receding hairline and pale brown eyes. He dresses carefully for the patrons’ box and becomes restless there before a race. When he talks about a horse he leans forward; when a patron talks about a nephew he settles back and listens warily.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Brazemos is Master of [[affiliation-ethloi|the Ethloi]], the Golds of [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. A man of the horse order and a Patron who pays toward the faction’s stables and teams, he holds the office by the board’s annual choice from its own number. He speaks for the faction, chooses drivers with the Master of the Stables and keeps the peace between patrons. The Golds’ resources belong to a faction sustained by its board, not to him alone.

He wants the accession games contract and the continued concession of the stalls and cookshops around [[place-rathaunamelvar|the Racecourse]]. [[being-rzmsplthn|Razemos Pelthen]], Master of the Irons, wants both. Brazemos must make the Golds’ public promises credible while preserving agreement among men who each expect their money to buy the best place beside the next Orzas.

## Background

**The horses.** Brazemos’s place on the board rests on the share he pays into its teams and stables. He leaves the stable yard’s daily work to its master, but comes to the choice of drivers ready to argue for a steady hand over a patron’s favorite.

**The board’s promise.** The Golds agree to cheer the court’s expected claimant at the accession if he is named, and to keep the Heights quiet on the night the Orzas dies. Brazemos treats that promise as work for the stand-clubs to prepare. Their captains answer to the Master, but the patrons who fill the stands also expect to be heard.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is sociable until talk turns to a favor that would cost the faction a race. He can refuse a patron’s nephew politely at dinner and bluntly in the stable yard.

### Motivation

He wants a successful season to make his board’s next choice easy. The games contract matters more to him than a fine place in one procession: it would vindicate the faction’s claim to stand beside the throne.

### Strengths

He knows when a dispute needs the board’s decision and when it needs the two offended patrons in the same room. He takes the stable master’s practical objections seriously.

### Weaknesses

He expects a shared interest in winning to settle more than it does. A patron may prefer a favor for his house to a stronger team, and Brazemos can spend too long arguing the team’s merits.

## Social

The Golds’ patrons supply money and clients; their stand-clubs supply the crowd. Brazemos needs both to keep the board’s promise of quiet on the Heights. He visits the clubs before race days to hear what their captains expect the Irons to do.

Razemos offers a larger crowd and commands the Irons through a different arrangement. Brazemos contests his claim to the Racecourse concessions without pretending that a decision inside the Golds’ board binds their rival.

### Affiliations

- **Ethloi**: Patron holding Master of the Golds, chosen annually by the board.
- **Vilérian Empire**: Gulmen, a man of the horse order.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The remount claim.** A cavalry prefect claims that a Gold patron’s new team was meant for his wing. Brazemos needs the purchase traced before race day; he hires the party to compare the breeder’s promise with the patron’s account.
2. **The empty box.** Two houses claim a dead patron’s board seat and box. Brazemos asks the party to carry their evidence to the board before the dispute spills into the stands. Neither house accepts a place in the crowd as a substitute.
3. **The concession.** A cookshop keeper seeks the party’s help carrying a petition about the Racecourse concession. Brazemos will hear terms that keep the trade operating, but the keeper wants an answer before the rivals’ contest for the throne’s favor settles the question without her.

:::secret {title="What the board does not know"}
The Golds’ last great win is bought with a Gilded senator’s payment to Razemos to lose it. Brazemos and the rest of the board do not know. The senator’s bargain is not Brazemos’s act or a decision of the board.

The patrons’ sons owe the Irons’ betting book, and several of those sons sit in the Senate. The patrons do not know the full sum. Razemos knows it and asks the sons how they mean to vote on the concession; Brazemos’s office does not give him access to that book.
:::
