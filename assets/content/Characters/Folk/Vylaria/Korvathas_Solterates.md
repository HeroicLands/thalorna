# Appearance {#appearance}

Korvathas Solterates is 43 years old. He has a broad chest, a round face, short brown hair and hazel eyes that keep returning to the doorway during an audience. He walks quickly and stops abruptly when he wants a clerk to explain a figure. His voice carries well across a crowded room.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Korvathas is Veramus of Order on [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]], commanding [[affiliation-citywatchorz|the City Watch]] through its Nokaran, [[being-kholsamelint|Kolsame Linthimis]]. He answers for public order, the watch-houses and city jails beyond the [Inner Wall]{.name data-type="place/structure"}. The [[affiliation-sorvan|Sorvan]] keeps the Inner City under its own command; Korvathas cannot send its guards to fill a gap on a Circle.

He wants enough men on the streets to make his orders credible. He has ordered the lean-tos below the [Salkalon Gate]{.name data-type="place/structure"} cleared, while the captain at [[place-rathaunamelvar|the Racecourse]] asks for more men than the city can pay for. Korvathas dislikes issuing a second order because the first has not been carried out. That impatience makes a reassuring return of numbers especially welcome.

## Background

**The council seat.** Korvathas comes from the senatorial order and holds his appointment at the Orzas’s pleasure. Command of the Watch does not put him above his four colleagues: grave council business is decided by majority, and the chair rotates.

**The work.** He deals with the Watch’s deployment and the demands made on it. The courts judge the people it arrests, and an order to keep a street clear is not a judgment against everyone found on that street.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is brisk and easily irritated by an answer that begins with an excuse. He pays close attention when someone gives him a definite obstacle, a place and a remedy he can authorize.

### Motivation

He wants the city to remain governable through the next proclamation. Keeping the streets open matters to him more than winning an argument about whose Circle deserves the largest patrol.

### Strengths

He understands that every additional post takes men from somewhere else. He can make rival petitioners state which duty they are willing to leave uncovered.

### Weaknesses

He accepts a completed count too readily as evidence of completed work. He is slower to ask who was counted, who was missed and why.

## Social

Kolsame holds her command at his pleasure, but her officers know the wards more closely than he does. Korvathas asks her for results while the Watch’s captains press their own requests through the same chain. The council’s Law portfolio and the ward magistrates determine much of what his men must enforce; none of those demands creates another paid watchman.

### Affiliations

- **Orzara Courts**: a Veramus holding the portfolio of Order, with one vote among the five.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a Kurthos of the senatorial order; his Watch command comes from his civic office.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The race-day request.** The Racecourse captain asks the party to document where the brawls begin before his next appeal for men. Korvathas will reconsider a deployment if the account identifies a post that can be moved, which makes every suggested reduction contentious.
2. **Below the gate.** A household facing the clearance of its lean-to needs help moving its people and goods before the Watch arrives. Korvathas’s office can be approached about the timing; the party must make a specific request while the clearance order remains in force.
3. **Two demands, one patrol.** Court business and a threatened market disturbance need the same watchmen. Korvathas asks the party to carry the competing accounts to Kolsame and return with a proposal before either petitioner claims he has already promised the men.

:::secret {title="The reassuring count"}
The [Spire Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} watch-house sells wooden tallies that let paying sleepers remain in [[place-warrensorz|the Warrens]] without a right of residence. Its officers direct the sweeps at stairs that have not paid. Korvathas sees the number put across the Clearance and is satisfied; he does not know how the stairs are selected and takes no share of the tally money.

His demand for a larger return can therefore punish the sleepers who cannot pay while leaving the trade intact. Proof must connect a tally, a payment and a spared stair to the report he accepts. A tally alone can be dismissed as a watchman’s misconduct before it reaches his desk.
:::
