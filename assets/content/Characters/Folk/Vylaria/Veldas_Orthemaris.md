# Appearance {#appearance}

Veldas Orthemaris is 66 years old. He is tall and narrow, with a long nose, brown eyes and iron-gray hair combed behind his ears. His formal clothes sit neatly on a stooped frame. He folds a petition along its existing creases and keeps his finger on the disputed clause while asking a question. He rarely appears hurried, even when the waiting room is full.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Veldas chairs [[affiliation-tolvan|The Fiscal Court]], the Tolvan, from its Fiscal Hall within the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]] at [[place-arvon|Arvon]]. He is an ethlen of the Gilded estate. As Talkor of the Tolvan he is appointed by the Orzas for a renewable ten-year term and can be dismissed at imperial discretion. Reformists and Conservatives alike regard his cautious moderation as less objectionable than the alternatives.

He wants tax collection that produces the promised revenue without provoking abuses that make the contracts impossible to defend. The Tolvan sets rates, awards the tax-farmers’ contracts, hears appeals and audits their conduct. It does not collect every taxpayer’s coin itself. Veldas resists reforms whose consequences he cannot bound, but also resists letting the worst collectors make their own impunity the court’s policy.

## Background

**The Gilded estate.** His standing gives him access to the families whose influence sustains the tax-farming system. It also makes their demands personal: an appeal against a collector can arrive at the same table as an invitation from the collector’s kin.

**The chair.** Appointment puts him between the sixteen-seat council, the provincial officers and the auditors. He learns to ask which part of a complaint concerns a rate, which a contract and which the collector’s conduct before promising a remedy.

**The present cases.** The chief auditor [Selmara Dorvena]{.name data-type="being/npc"} prosecutes eight great tax-farmers at once. Their families press him to slow her. At the same time a proposal to try salaried collectors in two provinces gives the reform dispute a limited test he is cautiously willing to consider.

## Psyche

### Personality

Veldas gives qualified answers and means the qualifications. He prefers a troublesome specific question to a declaration of principle that conceals what its speaker asks him to sign.

### Motivation

He wants the council to reach a defensible decision on the two-province trial without sacrificing the pending prosecutions to secure its votes.

### Strengths

He understands the points where contract terms, revenue estimates and political support depend on one another. His restraint keeps opponents at the same hearing long enough to state the actual disagreement.

### Weaknesses

Caution gives powerful families time to organize. Petitioners whose losses mount while a case waits have little use for his reputation as the least objectionable choice.

## Social

Selmara’s enforcement tests his moderation without making her his enemy. The tax-farming families approach through their court connections, while provincial taxpayers seek hearings against officials whose contracts carry imperial authority. A party with evidence can help make a case fit for hearing; Veldas does not promise a verdict in return for bringing it.

### Affiliations

- **Fiscal Court**: Talkor, holding Talkor of the Tolvan.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The proposed trial.** Veldas wants accounts from the two provinces proposed for salaried collection. He commissions independent copies of assessments and receipts so the council can compare the reformers’ estimates with what taxpayers actually pay.
2. **The auditor’s witness.** A witness in one of Selmara’s cases cannot reach the Fiscal Hall without crossing the collector’s territory. Veldas authorizes travel expenses and asks a party to escort the witness without coaching the testimony.
3. **The appeal’s figures.** A taxpayer’s appeal and the collector’s return agree on the land but disagree on the amount received. Veldas needs the original receipts brought to the hearing before either side persuades the council to rule from its copies.
