# Appearance {#appearance}

Melkanas Sarvothis is a 47-year-old man who stands 5'8" tall and is of heavy build. He has light skin with high color in the cheeks, chestnut hair kept short, and hazel eyes. He wears the grey robe of a priest of Sélmoros in fine wool, belted with a purse and a set of brass weights, and a heavy silver seal-ring on his right hand. His hands are soft except for a callus on the right thumb where a scale's beam rests. He is pleasant, quick and friendly, and he does sums in his head while he talks.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Melkanas Sarvothis is a prior of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]]: the priest who governs, for the temple of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] in the holy city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]], its largest temple-estate in the uplands. The estate takes in the valleys around the lumber settlement of [[place-kalderenna|Kalderenna]] and the forest held of the temple by lay barons, among them [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]]. Melkanas keeps the estate's court, which a knight of the [[affiliation-sacredflame|Order of the Sacred Flame]] holds for him at Kalderenna, collects its dues in coin, charcoal and days of labor, and decides which stands may be cut and by whom. He spends most of the year in the holy city and rides out to the estate at the quarter days.

He sells the cutting by the valley. A contract crew pays the temple for the right to take a whole valley at once, cuts it in a season and moves on, and the coin comes to the temple in a single sealed bag rather than in a year of charcoal. The temple's dues have never been paid so promptly.

[The Withering]{.name data-type="lore"} has reached the estate, as it has reached every estate in the kingdom. Wherever a stand of his forest goes grey in midsummer, he orders the procession with lamps around it, the fast, and the village's dues raised for the year, as the high priesthood teaches. The dead stands are then let to the contract crews, who cut them for charcoal.

## Background

**A second son of the temple.** The Sarvothis are a priest-noble family of no great age. Melkanas was raised in the temple of Sélmoros and kept its treasury's ledgers for ten years before he was given the estate, and the high priest chose him for his arithmetic.

**The good years.** In the six years he has held the estate, its dues have risen every year. The high priest has praised him before the temple's chapter, and the other priors have begun to sell their cutting the same way.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is cheerful, practical and very good with figures. He likes people, likes to be liked, and avoids an unpleasant conversation by changing the subject to the price of charcoal. He is devout in the way of a man who keeps the rites exactly and does not think about them much.

### Motivation

He wants the dues paid and the high priest pleased. He wants to be high priest of Sélmoros himself one day, and he knows that the man who fills the temple's treasury is the man the chapter remembers.

### Strengths

He controls who cuts in a great stretch of the kingdom's forest, and the contract crews and the timber buyers of the march need his seal. He is a fine accountant and knows the value of every stand on his estate.

### Weaknesses

His income depends on cutting a forest that is dying. The barons and the old charcoal-burners blame the contracts for the Withering to his face, and he has no answer but the high priesthood's teaching. He cannot lower the dues his temple asks of him, and he cannot raise them further.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The marked stand.** His forester has marked a stand near Kalderenna for a contract crew, and the old fellers say the leaves are the wrong color. Melkanas wants a party to escort the crew in, because the burners have said they will not let it pass.
2. **The rival bid.** Two contract crews want the same valley, and one has offered a better price than the other. Melkanas wants to know who is paying for the better bid before he accepts it.
3. **The procession.** The villagers of a dying valley refuse to walk the penance procession, saying they have walked it twice and the trees died anyway. Melkanas needs the rite done before the feast, and asks a party to learn who is telling them not to.

:::secret {title="What the prior has seen"}
Melkanas has seen what the barons say, and has said nothing. The valleys his contract crews have cut are the valleys where the Withering came next, more often than not, and the old burners' chosen-tree grounds have suffered least. He does not know why. He has not tried to find out, because if the contracts are the cause he is the cause, and if they are not, nothing he does will matter. The dues his temple asks of him are what the contracts pay for, and a judgment on a village is no fault of a prior's.

He also keeps a second purse. The timber house at [[place-ashenmere|Ashenmere]] that buys most of his contracts pays him a tenth over the temple's price in imperial silver, by weight, into the hands of a cousin in the lower city. The temple's ledger shows the price, and his cousin's strongbox shows the rest.

He holds [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]]'s rent book. The baron is two years behind and has borrowed from the temple's treasury besides. Melkanas has offered to forgive both if the baron lets a contract crew into the valley above [[place-dremenna|Dremenna]], the last uncut valley on the estate. Ardemos has not answered.

**Hook: the cousin's strongbox.** The prior's cousin in the lower city has been robbed, and he cannot report what was taken. Melkanas hires a party to recover it without asking the king's sergeants, and is very anxious that nobody count it.
:::
