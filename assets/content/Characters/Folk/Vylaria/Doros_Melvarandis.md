# Appearance {#appearance}

Doros Melvarandis is a 69-year-old man who stands 5'11" tall and is of medium build. He has medium skin weathered by years of riding his land, black hair gone gray at the temples and brown eyes that settle on a stranger and stay there. He dresses as a country lord: good wool, a plain belt, boots made for mud. He moves with the slow care of a man whose joints ache in the damp, and he is seldom seen without a tall archer a pace behind his shoulder.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Lord Doros Melvarandis is a lay baron of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]]. He holds land in the country where the forest gives way to fields, for rent and service, and keeps a hall, a manor court and men for the host as a baron must. His personal archer-guard for ten years has been [[being-erythnslnds|Erithon Uselindas]], the foremost marksman of the local militia, and over that time the yeoman has become his counselor as well as his guard.

## Background

**The baron's son.** Doros inherited his land and his hall as barons of Nartun do, holding them of a temple or of the crown for rent and service. A baron holds no priestly office, and Doros has never wanted one.

**The long tenure.** He has kept his courts, paid his rents and sent his men to the host for most of his life, and he has outlived most of the men who served him. His neighbors know him as a careful landlord who settles quarrels himself before they go up to the holy city.

**The archer.** When Erithon rose to be the militia's best bowman in his twenty-second year, Doros took him into his household as his own guard. He has come to rely on the younger man's judgment as much as his bow.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is unhurried, courteous and plain-spoken, a man who listens to a whole quarrel before he gives a word on it. He is fond of the people who serve him and slow to trust anyone else.

### Motivation

He wants his land kept whole and his people fed through his last years, and he wants to see the men he relies on provided for after him.

### Strengths

He knows his land and its families by name, keeps an honest court, and has the trust of his tenants. With Erithon at his shoulder he is hard to surprise.

### Weaknesses

He is old and tires quickly. He leans on Erithon more than a baron should lean on any one man, so moving Doros means moving the archer first.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The arrows at the scene.** Arrows of Erithon's distinctive make are found where a man of Doros's land was attacked. Doros does not believe his archer did it, and he hires a party to learn who is copying the arrows before the matter reaches a court in the holy city.
2. **The rent.** The temple Doros holds of sends its prior's clerk to renew the lease, and the clerk asks for more than the land yields. Doros wants a party to go up to [[place-nartum|Nartun]] with his steward and argue the old terms.
3. **The poacher.** A poacher has been taking game from the sacred stands on Doros's land and selling it south. Erithon has chased him twice and lost him twice, and Doros wants the man taken alive for his court.
4. **The call from the Sanctum.** A message comes from the Verdant Sanctum asking Erithon to come at once. Doros will not forbid it and will not be left unguarded, and he asks a party to ride with the archer or to stand in his place at the hall until he returns.

:::secret {title="The Poisoned Arrow"}
Whoever copies Erithon's arrows is not a quarrel of Doros's land. The copies turn up at crimes beyond it, and the trail runs up into the politics of the holy city, where someone with reach wants the archer ruined. Doros is the easiest road to Erithon: a baron whose guard is accused can be pressed to dismiss him, and an old baron who refuses can be accused with him. Who stands behind the copying is open, and a party that follows it finds the kingdom's quarrels of temple and crown waiting at the end of the road.
:::
