# Appearance {#appearance}

Sundrith ira Thalruvan wur Pinnhald is 61 years old. He has a broad back, blue-gray eyes and white hair tied clear of his neck. He walks stiffly on dry ground and stands more easily with a pole in his hand. His palms are ridged with old rope scars. At the hall he sits close enough to the door to hear a late arrival.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Sundrith is first among the headmen of [[place-pinnhald|Pinnhald]], the half-Varokh timber town where every householder may speak in the hall. The imperial charter calls its chosen magistrates tavemoi; the town still takes its quarrels to the headmen. He speaks for their decision rather than ruling the hall alone.

Thirty spring runs give his opinion weight with the raftsmen. He wants the town’s crews employed and its custom of leaving one tree in three respected. This season a missing raft, an expelled cutting crew and a visiting priest each demand an answer the timber-lords would prefer somebody else to give.

## Background

**The river.** He learns the spring run with men who measure a good season by returning together. Over thirty journeys he carries mast timber to the lowland yards and walks home.

**The hall.** Age brings him a place among the headmen. Choosing crews gives him influence over the timber-lords, whose cutting-rights are worth little without men to work them.

**The town’s voice.** As first headman he receives provincial clerks and southern priests. He keeps the distinction between honoring a guest and surrendering the hall’s customs.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is patient with a difficult account and sharp with an easy excuse. He expects a speaker to name who is affected before describing what the town ought to do.

### Motivation

He wants Pinnhald to keep its work and its right to choose how that work is done.

### Strengths

He knows the river’s demands and the crews’ abilities, and can bring the householders into one discussion.

### Weaknesses

He can treat a bargain made for the town as his alone to defend. He finds it harder to acknowledge a wrong decision than to endure a bad run.

## Social

The headmen decide together; the timber-lords hold provincial cutting-rights and need the crews he helps choose. The town’s kindreds give his words force, but do not owe him obedience outside their decisions.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a free resident under imperial law without the citizenship.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The missing raft.** Sundrith asks a party to trace a mast-timber raft that never reaches the yards, with the four men’s names as important to him as the timber-lord’s marks.
2. **The marked tree.** An expelled crew disputes the mark that led it to fell a protected tree. He agrees to hear evidence brought by outsiders before the hall decides whether to restore its work.
3. **The offering at the door.** A southern priest insists on making an offering inside the hall. Sundrith asks the party to negotiate a place outside it without letting a refusal become violence.

:::secret {title="The grove beyond the line"}
Two winters ago the province’s buyers threaten to refuse Pinnhald’s rafts unless they again carry mast timber. Sundrith agrees to the timber-lords’ plan to cut a protected grove beyond the border and chooses crews who will keep quiet. The headmen mark a protected tree for a crew that refuses the grove work, using the resulting offense to exclude it. The village beyond the line now demands the cutters surrendered before summer as payment. Sundrith has told neither the town nor the timber-lords of that demand.
:::
