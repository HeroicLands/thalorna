# Appearance {#appearance}

Merzanas Zelmaris is a 47-year-old man who stands 6'1" tall and is of medium build. He has fair skin, chestnut hair worn long at the neck in the Heights fashion with a short beard oiled and shaped to a point, and pale blue eyes. He is handsome and knows it. He dresses in the senator's stripe on fine dyed wool, wears a gold ring on every finger but his thumbs, and smells faintly of cedar oil. He talks easily and well, with a good laugh and a habit of resting a hand on the shoulder of the man he is persuading.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Merzanas Zelmaris is master of [House Zelmaris]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a founding _ethlen_ (Gilded) house of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]], and a _kurthos_ (senator) of the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]]. The house's villa stands on [Torvel]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the older of the twin patrician hills of the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]]. Its fortune stands on [Nerval]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the River Hill above the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]], where the house owns more of the _tengali_ (tenements) of the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]] than any other landlord in the city: something over three hundred buildings, and the rooms of perhaps forty thousand people.

He has never climbed one of his own stairs. His steward manages the buildings, his rent-men collect at the month's end, the day after the dole, and his clerks tally the rooms on the house's books by stair and by legion. The veterans who rent from him pay out of the pension, so his rent is the first claim on it, and the stairs know it.

## Background

**The house.** The Zelmaris are among the oldest houses on the Heights, and for most of their history they lived on their estates in Hilen and the offices of the throne. Merzanas's grandfather sold the estates after a run of lean harvests and bought tenements on Nerval with the money, after a fire on Nerval, when the ground was cheap and the throne was paying to rebuild. The old houses thought it beneath a Gilded house to live on rent, and the Zelmaris have been richer than most of them ever since.

**The master.** Merzanas inherited at thirty. He is a good speaker in the Seldan and a popular host, and he sits on the committee of the chamber that hears petitions on the building law. He has three sons, a wife from a Silver house who runs his household, and a passion for horses that the Golds of the Racecourse have learned to profit from.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is charming, quick and easily bored. He likes to be liked and works at it. He thinks of his tenements as a ledger, never as places, and is genuinely surprised when someone describes one to him. He keeps his promises to his equals and forgets those he makes to anyone else.

### Motivation

He wants an exemption from the seven-story limit for the house's buildings on Nerval, voted by the Seldan and sealed by the throne, so that every tenement can carry an eighth floor of timber and a fifth more rent. He wants the stairs paying on time. And he wants to come out of the succession with his rents untouched, whoever is named.

### Strengths

His tenements house a fifth of the Warrens. His rent-men know every stair, every legion's floor and every tenant behind on his rent, and a man behind on his rent listens to the house. He is liked in the Seldan, where his vote is cheap to buy and pleasant to have.

### Weaknesses

His buildings are old, tall and timber above the third floor, and fire comes to the Warrens first. The stairs hate him as they hate every landlord, and his name is the one they know. He spends more than the rents bring in and borrows the difference.

## Plot Hooks

1. **Rent day.** Word on the stairs is that tomorrow's pension will come short, and the rent-men go up the stairs of Nerval the day after. The house's steward hires a party to walk with them.
2. **The petition.** The tribune of the stairs has brought a fourth petition on rent day to the Seldan's committee. Merzanas pays a party to learn which _kurthoi_ the tribune has visited and what he has promised them.
3. **The cracked wall.** A tenant on the sixth floor of a Zelmaris building reports a crack in a party-wall that grows every rain. The steward has not answered. The tenant, a widow on a pension, hires a party to make him answer.
4. **The Gold's debt.** Merzanas has lost a horse wager to the Golds' book that he cannot pay this quarter. He wants a party to carry a sealed offer to the Golds' Keeper of the Book, and to bring back nothing in writing.

:::secret {title="The landlord's eighth stories"}
The house has already built the eighth stories. Forty Zelmaris tenements on Nerval carry a floor of timber above the seventh, framed inside the roof-line so that it cannot be seen from the lane, and the ward's _peltros_ (ward magistrate) has signed each one off as a roof-space for drying laundry. The house pays him a monthly sum through its steward. In two places on the upper slope the house's builders have also cut through a fire-wall between wards to join two buildings into one, and bricked a timber door into the gap. If fire takes an upper floor in either block, it will cross the fire-wall in an hour.

Merzanas knows his buildings are dangerous. He does not think of them as places, but he can read a steward's report. He wants the exemption passed so that the eighth stories become lawful before anyone climbs to one.

He is also selling. The worst three wards of his tenements, the oldest and most crowded, are being sold one stair at a time to a citizen of the Grand Market who pays in good silver and asks few questions. The buyer is a nominee. The silver behind him is [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]]'s, and the Khelathi factor is buying rooms inside the line through citizens' names, as the Taruzelian houses trade behind veterans' names in the Grand Market. Merzanas does not know where the silver comes from and has not asked.

**Hook: the new landlord.** A veteran has come home to find his legion's stair let to strangers by a new landlord. The new landlord is the nominee, and the strangers are Khelathi sailors with no right to sleep inside the line.
:::
