# Appearance {#appearance}

Varelos Kolsamis is 46 years old. Grain dust collects in his dark curls and in the creases beside his brown eyes. He is broad through the shoulders, with a voice trained to carry across a roofed yard. He wipes a scale pan before weighing even a sack whose owner is impatient.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Varelos calls weights on the Counting Floor at [[place-granaria|Kelborara]]. He stands between the wagons and the clerks’ tables, where a number spoken clearly becomes an entry against a household’s harvest. The sack-carriers know him from twenty years of this work.

He comes from a tenant village outside town and still receives its news at the Floor. When the houses’ grain halls are full and the villages are short, he asks the factors how long their bought grain will remain under lock. He is careful to make that a question rather than an accusation.

## Background

**The village.** His family’s harvest brings him to Kelborara before he earns wages there. Waiting with a village’s sacks teaches him to listen for the difference between the weight called and the figure repeated by a clerk.

**The Floor.** He makes his living as a measurer for twenty years. The carriers learn that he will halt a line to check a beam that does not balance.

**The lean seasons.** The workers who arrive from the tenant villages have become his neighbors. He now spends as much time hearing what their wages must cover as arguing over the sacks themselves.

## Psyche

### Personality

He speaks plainly and remembers who has had no work that week. He can wait through a slow weighing without showing irritation, then become abrupt when a factor treats an idle carrier as disposable.

### Motivation

He wants the villages that grow the grain to eat through a lean year.

### Strengths

He understands the Floor’s daily movements, has the carriers’ confidence, and knows how long it takes to empty a hall by hand.

### Weaknesses

He assumes that a shared hardship makes people agree about its remedy. He is much less patient with dissent from his own neighbors than with an employer’s refusal.

## Social

The sack-carriers’ fraternity is his principal circle. The town’s granary-keepers value his work without sharing his view of the households that send it to them.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a free resident under imperial law without the citizenship.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The count.** A factor offers a party money to count sacks in three halls without the keepers knowing. Varelos can explain their ordinary movements, but first asks whose account the count will serve.
2. **The carrier’s brother.** A carrier seeks his younger brother, missing since midsummer. Varelos supplies the names of the gangs the young man worked beside; the party must seek their answers itself.
3. **The western fire.** The cohort’s officer needs witnesses to a fire in an empty state granary. Varelos asks a party to establish which workers are being questioned before the next morning’s labor is interrupted.

:::secret {title="The grain rising"}
Varelos plans a seizure of the houses’ granaries in the next hungry year, with the fraternity’s officers behind him. Weapons lie beneath the floor of the roofless state hall that recently burned. The fire is an accident caused by two carriers moving weapons by lamplight. The missing younger brother joined the rising, argued against it at midsummer, and is buried under the same floor. Varelos expects the twenty provincial soldiers to be held for a night and the legion to need a day’s march. He has not reckoned with the houses’ separate agreement to close their halls during the succession.
:::
