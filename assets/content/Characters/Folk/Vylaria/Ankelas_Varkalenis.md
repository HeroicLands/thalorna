# Appearance {#appearance}

Ankelas Varkalenis is 73 years old. His white hair is fine and sparse, his eyes pale brown, and the skin of his long hands shows every vein. He dresses with exact attention to precedence. Each morning he walks down from [[place-antel|Antel]] on the arm of his house’s freedman; the house keeps no chair-bearers, and the slow walk is part of the city’s daily sight.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Ankelas is master of [[affiliation-varkalenis|House Varkalenis]] and has held its seat in [[affiliation-seldan|The Vilérian Senate]] for fifty years. He is also priest of the temple of his divine ancestor in [[affiliation-imperialcult|the Imperial Cult]]. The stipends from the house’s three ancestral temples sustain much of its household. Its palace on Antel has flaking frescoes, rented rooms in the second court and cabbages in the garden, while the masks of five ancestral Orzoi still stand in the cupboard by the door.

He has named no heir. Three cousins attend his morning call expecting adoption, among them his nephew [[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]], Keeper of the Masks and the old houses’ choice for the imperial succession. Ankelas wants an heir the household can afford without surrendering the name’s dignity. Courtesy costs him little, and he is generous with what little else remains.

## Background

**The cupboard by the door.** Ankelas learns his family’s precedence through the offices of its dead. Their masks remain the measure against which he judges a proposal, even when the proposal concerns a leaking roof.

**Fifty years in the chamber.** He holds the Senate seat through fifty years of argument. The house votes with the Conservatives on precedence, commissions and citizenship; he regards these as obligations of the name rather than favors for a faction.

**The unanswered adoption.** Old age makes the empty place behind his name urgent. The cousins’ attendance brings company and rivalry into the house, and every apparent kindness can be read as a bid for the will.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is courteous, slow to interrupt and exact about how people are introduced. He can make a shabby reception feel formal by giving its visitors his whole attention.

### Motivation

He wants the house’s temples, Senate voice and name to pass together to an heir capable of keeping them.

### Strengths

He knows the Senate’s families and forms through long service. His personal generosity keeps clients attending a house that has little money to distribute.

### Weaknesses

He treats the clerical origins of [[affiliation-thalurates|House Thalurates]] as a lasting disqualification and refuses to dine with them. That contempt narrows his choices while his household’s needs grow.

## Social

The three cousins compete for his adoption. The cult’s treasurer threatens to let a temple porch to a ropemaker unless the house produces the endowment that protects it. House Mardovanes seeks full consecration for its ancestral Orzas, against the Varkalenis vote. Ankelas hears all these requests as claims on what his family must keep.

### Affiliations

- **House Varkalenis**: Of the Name, holding Master of the House.
- **Vilérian Senate**: an Ethlen senator of a founding Gilded house.
- **Imperial Cult**: a Zalvos holding Zalvos of a Temple.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: Kurthos.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The temple’s copy.** Ankelas needs a copy of the burned endowment deed from a provincial Scribal Guild chapterhouse before the treasurer lets the porch. He offers a formal introduction and what travel money the household can spare.
2. **The cousins’ evidence.** A cousin presents an allegation against another and demands that Ankelas hear it before writing his will. Ankelas hires independent witnesses to establish what happened, without promising which cousin he favors.
3. **The rented court.** One of the antiquarians renting rooms asks to examine an ancestral mask for a commission. Ankelas wants the commission’s patron identified before he admits the work into the household.

:::secret {title="The stipends’ creditor"}
Ankelas borrows against the house’s stipends from a Danvar moneylender. The note is sold on, and a factor of House Thalurates now holds it. Ankelas does not know who holds the debt. The holder means to forgive it in return for the house’s heir marrying a Thalurates daughter; the offer has not settled the adoption or the marriage.
:::
