# Appearance {#appearance}

Kaldremas Zurmelates is 51 years old, a lean man with graying black hair, a long nose and deep-set brown eyes. He sits very straight, with both hands flat beside the papers before him. When an advocate repeats an argument, Kaldremas turns the relevant sheet toward the speaker and waits.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Kaldremas is Veramus of Law in [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]]. From the [Hall of the Five]{.name data-type="place/structure"} in [[place-platandstr|the Platan District]], he oversees the ward magistrates, Civil Courts and Criminal Tribunal. When the five veramusoi sit as the supreme civic court, he presides; on the council’s other grave business he has one vote among five, and the chair rotates.

He wants the courts to finish cases before patronage or a proclamation carries them elsewhere. A judgment reached after the witnesses disappear is little use to the people waiting below his hall. He takes particular care over requests to postpone a hearing, although a persuasive explanation from a fellow senator still gets more time than an equally urgent plea from a shopkeeper.

## Background

**The appointment.** Kaldremas belongs to the senatorial order and holds his portfolio at the Orzas’s pleasure. His rank gives him access to the throne’s servants, but no promise of a place on the next reign’s council.

**The limits.** His civic jurisdiction stops at matters touching the nobility, [[affiliation-seldan|the Senate]] or the crown. Those belong to the imperial tribunals, and sometimes the Senate itself. He knows the route by which a cause leaves his courts and insists that the clerks record it; he cannot summon an imperial judge back down the hill.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is restrained and attentive to wording. A precise admission interests him more than an advocate’s display of outrage, and he lets an uncomfortable silence last until someone answers.

### Motivation

He wants judgments that survive the departure of the people who obtained them. He also wants his own decisions to be remembered when a new Orzas chooses the next council.

### Strengths

He separates the disputed fact from the parties’ complaints about one another. He knows which bench can hear a case and when a proposed remedy exceeds its authority.

### Weaknesses

He gives familiar manners too much credit. His patience with the senatorial households makes his stated impatience with delay hard for a poor petitioner to believe.

## Social

[[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]] brings the [River Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} veterans’ petition against [[affiliation-cleanshlds|the Clean Shields]]. Kaldremas wants witnesses who can distinguish what happens on the grain road from what they have heard in the city. The courts’ ushers and the Criminal Tribunal’s jailers keep their own duties; [[affiliation-citywatchorz|the Watch]] enforces and investigates, and does not pronounce his judgments.

The [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]] can lift a cause from the civic basilicas at the throne’s word. Kaldremas’s influence may obtain an explanation, but he cannot promise a client that a gray stole at the basilica door will leave empty-handed.

### Affiliations

- **Orzara Courts**: a Veramus holding the portfolio of Law, equal to the other four councilors.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a Kurthos of the senatorial order.

## Plot Hooks

1. **Before the second hour.** An advocate’s chief witness disappears after entering the Platan baths. Kaldremas will hear the case tomorrow; the advocate hires the party to find the witness before the hearing rather than seek another postponement.
2. **The transferred cause.** A client vanishes into the Offices after his case leaves the civil basilica. Kaldremas supplies the family with the recorded transfer and asks the party to discover which imperial tribunal now holds the man; the paper grants no right to remove him.
3. **The river petition.** Sathemar needs testimony about the Clean Shields’ conduct at the moorings. Kaldremas asks for separate accounts from porters, barge crews and the Watch before the petition comes before the council; each account may embarrass a different patron.
