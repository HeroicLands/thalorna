# Appearance {#appearance}

Nethoren Tholmaris is 62 years old. He is tall and spare, with a bald crown, white hair at the sides and clear gray eyes. He turns his whole body toward a speaker. His careful voice carries a proclamation cleanly, but in conversation he often pauses to repeat a disputed name exactly as it appears in the roll.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Nethoren is Master of [[affiliation-imprlhrlds|the Imperial College of Heralds]] in [[place-arvon|Arvon]]. A sworn Herald himself, he sits among the usoi, the prefects of [[affiliation-imperialoffices|the Imperial Offices]], and answers to [[being-holimar|Holimar]], Keeper of the Seal. His College settles the arms, marks, liveries and precedence by which the empire’s houses appear before the throne.

He wants an accession order that he can defend from the roll. The Orzas is old and has adopted nobody; houses already petition to stand closer to the next sovereign, and every favorable place will be read as a favor from the new reign. Nethoren will hear a precedent even from a house he dislikes. He is less patient with a petitioner who treats the desired place as proof of an entitlement to it.

## Background

**The roll.** His authority rests on the College’s record of the houses, their estates and their precedence. The Keeper of the Roll of Arms enters changes from the Scribal Guild’s spring registry. Nethoren rules on petitions, but a herald who finds a reason in the roll can bring grounds for changing a ruling.

**The schools.** Pursuivants learn beside heralds in the compound and in country houses. Nethoren approves their swearing when he is satisfied that they will not guess. Knowing when to consult the roll matters to him as much as a recital made without a mistake.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is exact without being hurried. He asks a pursuivant to show where an answer comes from and allows the search to take time, even when a visiting household grows impatient.

### Motivation

He wants the College to settle disputes that would otherwise ruin ceremonies or become quarrels between houses. He also wants its accession arrangements ready before the city hears a death proclamation.

### Strengths

He distinguishes the office a house’s dead held from the standing its living members hold now. He can explain a precedence decision in terms that another herald can check.

### Weaknesses

He underrates what the early sight of a draft is worth. To him an unapproved procession order is work still to be corrected; to a claimant’s steward it is information worth buying.

## Social

Holimar receives the College’s business as Keeper of the Seal. [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]] publishes the estate changes the heralds must learn, while the Herald of the Palor handles the embassies’ precedence under the College’s authority.

[[being-lrdmlnvsbr|Lord Aimilon Visbari]] teaches pursuivants at his family’s house outside [[place-polthara|Polthara]]. His school is one of the places from which Nethoren receives men ready to be examined.

### Affiliations

- **Imperial College of Heralds**: a Herald holding Master of the College.
- **Imperial Offices**: an Usos, answerable through the College’s business to the Keeper of the Seal.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The earlier place.** A Silver house hires the party to find the precedent that would move it ahead of a rival at the accession. Nethoren will hear a documented claim, but the archive may support a narrower privilege than the house wants.
2. **The draft order.** A claimant’s steward offers money for Nethoren’s unfinished procession order. A clerk asks the party how to refuse without offending the house; reporting the approach gives Nethoren a chance to protect the draft before it leaves the compound.
3. **The disputed lesson.** A pursuivant brings two incompatible accounts of a house’s precedence to his examination. Nethoren sends him back to establish which record governs, and the young man seeks the party’s help approaching the household whose preferred account may lose.
