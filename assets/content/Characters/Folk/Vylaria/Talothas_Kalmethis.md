# Appearance {#appearance}

Talothas Kalmethis is 58 years old, with white hair above his ears, a heavy jaw and brown eyes under thick brows. He is broad through the waist and moves carefully on the wet quays. During a report he rubs his thumb along the edge of the table, stopping only when the speaker reaches the price.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Talothas is Veramus of the Grain on [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]], responsible for the state granaries, licensed markets and the capital’s daily supply. His agents bring the bread price from [[place-grandmarketorz|the Grand Market]] at every noon bell. He reads it beside the incoming barge counts, which often disagree with the counts from [[affiliation-citywatchorz|the Watch]].

He wants a reliable arrival of grain at a price the city can bear. The reported reserve is about a week, with nothing behind it, so a delay on the river cannot be treated as a quarrel between two merchants. He is willing to hear every supplier and reluctant to let any one house become the only house able to feed the city.

## Background

**The office.** Talothas is a senator appointed at the Orzas’s pleasure. He sits as an equal with the other four veramusoi, sharing the rotating chair and the council’s majority decisions. His control of a granary is not control of the treasury that must pay for its grain.

**The market.** In a lean month he can order dealers to open their stores and sell at his price. A dealer who holds stock back after the order risks forfeiture and a case before the Criminal Tribunal. Talothas wants proof of the withheld stock before he makes a public accusation that an empty warehouse can defeat.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is patient with practical detail and impatient with assurances about a house’s honor. He asks when the grain reaches the quay and what the seller expects if the river keeps it upstream.

### Motivation

He wants bread to remain within reach of the people who must buy it daily, and enough choice among sellers to negotiate the next purchase.

### Strengths

He compares price, promised arrival and the cost of delay. He can hear a rival merchant’s useful information without treating the merchant’s preferred remedy as part of the evidence.

### Weaknesses

He trusts the granaries’ tallies as the measure of the reserve. The urgency of today’s arrivals leaves little room in his attention for grain the books say is already stored.

## Social

[[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] wants a duty on foreign grain; [[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]] wants trade free and the Khelathi contract. [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]] offers grain on credit and silver for the throne’s paper. Each wants his help against the others, while he needs their barges to keep arriving.

At [[place-wharvesorz|the Wharves]], granary-keepers open and reseal the halls before witnesses. [[affiliation-cleanshlds|The Clean Shields]] escort grain and hold the moorings above the river gate; their count and the Watch’s count reach Talothas by different hands.

### Affiliations

- **Orzara Courts**: a Veramus holding the Grain portfolio.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a Kurthos of the senatorial order, appointed by the Orzas.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The missing sacks.** Talothas needs a party to follow one disputed barge count from its escort to the water-stair. He wants the point where the figures diverge before either side turns the difference into a charge against the other.
2. **The closed store.** A dealer claims his warehouse is empty when ordered to sell. Talothas asks for evidence of its recent deliveries; the porters’ testimony matters more than a rival’s estimate of what ought to be inside.
3. **Before the noon bell.** A merchant offers grain on terms that expire before the next price report. Talothas hires the party to verify that the promised cargo exists and can reach the city, while the merchant presses him to decide without it.

:::secret {title="The reserve on the page"}
Talothas does not know that the keeper of the two state granaries nearest the river gate has sold their reserve and replaced the bottom of each bin with sand under an arm’s depth of grain. The sales begin three lean winters ago. The tallies still declare both halls full, and the keeper’s copied seal conceals the openings from the ordinary witnessing procedure.

When the reserve is opened against a shortage, the porters reach sand in the first hour. Talothas’s office and the city’s bread are at risk together. Proof brought to him before the opening gives him time to seek a purchase; it supplies neither the money nor a supplier he can trust.
:::
