# Appearance {#appearance}

Doranda Brazemen is 52 years old. She has dark brown eyes, black hair pinned high and skin weathered by the reflected light from the pans. Her loose work coat is costly cloth with plain fastenings. She carries a sunshade when she walks the banks and stops beside a salt heap to judge it from more than one side.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Doranda runs the Brazemen pans at [[place-thremuzara|Thremuzara]], the largest on the coast. For twenty years she has set the spring price and the other four pan families follow it. The surrounding fishing villages need their salt; a village selling its fish elsewhere pays more in the next season.

She wants to stop a lesser village buying imported salt through a Delalara house. She presents the dispute as protection of the coast’s established trade and offers work to people who can discover the importer’s terms. Her stewards control entry to the pans, and she conducts most business from the house above them rather than at the curing sheds.

## Background

**The estate.** The family takes its name from its provincial estate, and she grew up with salt treated as both a crop and a claim on the coast. She learned the stages of the basins before learning their accounts.

**The largest pans.** Two decades in charge make her the first price other owners hear each spring. She keeps that influence through the scale of her holdings rather than an office over the other families.

**The foreign salt.** A village’s independent buyer threatens more than one season’s sales. If its bargain works, other villages can ask for the same freedom from the pan families’ price.

## Psyche

### Personality

She is composed, decisive and courteous to a useful visitor. She asks what a proposal will cost before asking whether it is popular.

### Motivation

She wants her family’s pans to remain the indispensable supplier of the coast’s curing houses.

### Strengths

She knows the salt trade, controls substantial stores and can coordinate the families around a price that benefits them all.

### Weaknesses

She treats dependence as consent. A village with another source can turn a small commercial loss into a challenge to the whole arrangement.

## Social

The other four pan families follow her price but remain owners in their own right. Provincial tax-farmers count the heaps; the families prefer the province’s soldiers to stay elsewhere.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: of the propertied order.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The imported salt.** Doranda hires a party to learn when the Delalara salt ship is due and which villages agree to buy from it. The village offers the same party work protecting the delivery.
2. **The clay heap.** A tax clerk finds a heap with a clay core. Doranda wants its ownership established before the clerk names her family in a demand for payment.
3. **The runaway claim.** Her stewards post a reward for a missing raker as a runaway slave. The man reaches the party first and insists that he is born free; Doranda demands his return under the bill of sale.

:::secret {title="The cheap rakers"}
For six years Doranda buys kidnapped people from [Devorin]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a slaver operating from a hidden dock at [[place-mercavia|Delalara]]. False debt-slavery bills disguise the trade. The Brazemen keep the bought rakers on outer pans where visitors do not go; the other families buy fewer people from the same source and cannot expose it without naming themselves. The escaped raker is a Delalara boy taken at twelve who can identify the dock. The salt boat due at Volkemara is delayed because its master sees a night unloading at the outer pans; Doranda’s stewards keep him as a guest while deciding what to do.
:::
