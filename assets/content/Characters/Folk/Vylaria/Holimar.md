# Appearance {#appearance}

Holimar is 60 years old. He is a compact man with close-cut gray hair, dark eyes and a round face that stays composed while a petitioner grows angry. His sleeves are narrow enough to keep clear of wet ink. He speaks softly and makes a visitor approach the desk to hear an answer, without raising his voice to fill the room.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Holimar keeps the imperial seal and heads the chancery of [[affiliation-imperialoffices|the Imperial Offices]] in [[place-arvon|Arvon]]. He is a freedman of the imperial household, appointed by the Orzas and answerable to him alone. He has held the seal for nineteen years. He is exact, very rich and bitterly resented in [[affiliation-seldan|The Vilérian Senate]], whose great men must wait on his bench before a victory in the chamber becomes a sealed act.

He wants the desks to keep working while every house prepares for a new reign. The reigning Orzas is seventy-eight and has adopted nobody. A new sovereign appoints a new Keeper, and houses already court the claimants for the seal on behalf of their own freedmen. Holimar receives their petitions with the same care as ordinary business; he does not mistake the care for security.

## Background

**The household.** Imperial service gives Holimar access without giving him a noble house. His freedom is a standing he keeps separate from the office that makes senators wait for him.

**The seal.** He took charge nineteen years ago. The duty is precise: an act is the throne’s act because the Keeper seals it, and a document sealed in his absence is a forgery however genuine its words.

**The desks.** His chancery keeps the census, pensions, levies and provincial correspondence. The compound also houses institutions with their own heads; Holimar’s seal does not make him the Imperial Tarkelos or the Talkor of the Fiscal Court.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is unhurried and literal. He gives the petitioner an answer to the request actually written, then leaves space for a corrected request.

### Motivation

He wants to keep control of what passes his desk through the succession and retain the means to live safely when another man holds the seal.

### Strengths

He knows which desk holds a matter and which official can move it. He can distinguish a delay caused by missing evidence from one bought by a petitioner’s opponent.

### Weaknesses

His office makes him useful to people who despise his standing. He has little reason to trust their assurances about what happens after the old Orzas dies.

## Social

[[being-krznsthlrts|Korzenas Thalurates]] wants the seal to remain in the Offices he knows. The treasury, Fiscal Court, Heralds and Scribal Guild share the compound’s business while retaining their own authority. Petitioners find Holimar through clerks and ushers; an introduction can shorten the route without deciding his answer.

### Affiliations

- **Imperial Offices**: an Usos holding Keeper of the Seal.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: a freedman of the imperial household.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The duplicate levy.** A Taruzelian envoy asks for the levy the Provinces desk still drafts for Taruzel. Holimar needs to learn who offered the copy before a clerk turns routine paperwork into a diplomatic insult.
2. **The petitioner’s number.** Two clerks offer a party’s patron the same place before the next feast day. Holimar asks for the promises in writing; each clerk now wants the party to withdraw its account.
3. **The promised seal.** A house’s freedman claims a claimant has promised him Holimar’s office. Holimar commissions an inquiry into who carries that promise and what the house gives in return, without authorizing an attack on the claimant.

:::secret {title="The index outside the compound"}
Holimar keeps a private index of every record the Offices lose on his instruction during his nineteen years with the seal. It names the requesting house and the price paid. The index is kept outside the compound, beyond a successor’s ability to seal it away, and its existence is the reason no great house tries to remove him. He does not show a hired investigator the index merely because the investigator has his commission.
:::
