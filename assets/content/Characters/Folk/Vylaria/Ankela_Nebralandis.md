# Appearance {#appearance}

Ankela Nebralandis is 59 years old. Her black hair is gray at the temples, and she wears it pinned clear of her collar. Her dark blue stole bears the embroidery of her rank and a gold pin shaped as an open book. She reads a document flat on the table, with both hands away from its seal, and asks its bearer to finish speaking before she begins.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Ankela heads [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]] from [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. She is the seventh of her line to hold senior Guild office. As High Hand she chairs its Council of Guildmasters and holds an ex officio seat on the council of the [[affiliation-tevran|Great College of Guilds]]. Her position lasts for life or until resignation; the monthly Guild council remains the body that decides standards, discipline and relations with the administration.

The throne wants its grants and appointments quickly, and Ankela wants each one to survive the question of whether it is genuine. She is willing to put more hands on a difficult commission. She resists dispensing with the examination that gives those hands their authority. Petitioners meet her through the Guild hall on the Circle of Árdavon in the [[place-platandstr|Platan District]], where the working copies of the dole roll are also kept.

## Background

**The family’s work.** Senior Guild service is the Nebralandis family’s distinction. Ankela grew up hearing particular documents discussed by the mistakes their copyists prevent, and she keeps that habit of reading a dispute through its paper.

**The examining table.** The Guild’s seven-year training makes a scribe responsible for the seal beneath a document. Ankela carries its insistence on witnesses, registration and careful copying into her own work with senior scribes.

**The chair.** As High Hand she must defend those procedures to officials who depend on them and resent their delay. Her council seat in the Great College gives her another hearing, but also binds the Guild to disputes among trades far beyond its own desks.

## Psyche

### Personality

Ankela listens without filling a silence. She asks for the missing page before asking who benefits from its absence, and treats a probationer’s accurate objection as seriously as a Guildmaster’s.

### Motivation

She wants the Guild’s seals to remain worth trusting through the imperial succession, when every disputed grant has a powerful defender.

### Strengths

She knows which question belongs to an examiner, which to the Council and which must be answered by the official demanding a signature. [[being-ailemkrmys|Ailema Keramyes]] is among the authentication experts on whose work the Guild relies.

### Weaknesses

Her confidence in proper procedure makes it difficult to admit how much damage a properly placed Guild member can do. She sometimes keeps an inquiry inside the institution after an outsider needs its answer.

## Social

Provincial chapterhouses bargain with her over the reach of Orzara’s rulings. Imperial officers press for speed, while the Guild’s own scribes expect protection from orders that make their seals dishonest. She can give a party access to an examiner or a contested archive request; access to the imperial archive still carries the Guild’s conditions.

### Affiliations

- **Imperial Scribal Guild**: High Hand, its chair.
- **Great College of Guilds**: a Kelros holding the Scribal Guild’s council seat.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The missing treaty.** Two signatories hold differing copies of a fifty-year-old treaty whose original cannot be found. Ankela commissions a party to trace the last recorded loan of the original before either side enforces its own version.
2. **The cellar key.** The Guild’s requests to search the old Senate hall’s cellars meet locked doors. Ankela seeks an introduction to someone authorized to admit its examiners, with the terms recorded before they enter.
3. **The probationer’s copies.** A probationer finds small alterations across twenty years of dispatches. Ankela asks a party to collect independent copies from their recipients before she puts an accusation before the Council.
