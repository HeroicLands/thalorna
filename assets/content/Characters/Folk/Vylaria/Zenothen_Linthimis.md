# Appearance {#appearance}

Zenothen Linthimis is 54 years old. He has a wide mouth, brown eyes and thick gray hair that refuses to lie flat above his forehead. His right thumb is flattened by an old yard injury. He wears a clean wool coat to council and carries a short measuring rule in its pocket. When shown a plan, he asks for the dimensions before admiring the drawing.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Zenothen is master of the shipwrights and Holkelros, first of the [[affiliation-tevranaelissium|Tevran of Elissara]]. The council’s nineteen guild representatives choose their presiding member each year. He is in his third year, two more than custom expects. He keeps the city’s seal, presides in the Charter Hall on market square and answers for [[place-aelissium|Elissara]] to the throne. The guilds retain their own courts, and questions between guilds come to the council.

He wants the free city to keep governing itself. Its bargain with the Empire requires the spring payment, free passage for the capital’s cargoes, and a river mouth kept lit and dredged. The surrounding province of Salkalon regards that independence as a privilege it merely tolerates. Within the walls the seamen and bargemen resent a majority held by shipowners, merchants and small crafts. Keeping a vote together does not make their grievance disappear.

## Background

**The yards.** Shipwright work gives Zenothen a practical distrust of promises without provision for materials and labor. A hull must carry its load regardless of the agreement that orders it built.

**The guild’s seat.** As the shipwrights’ representative he learns the council’s nineteen interests through work they share: wharves, watches, dredging and the passage of goods. He can speak for the yards without claiming to speak for every hand who works on the river.

**The third year.** Repeated election keeps him beside the chest that holds the charter. An advocate from Delalara now asks every guild for a copy, claiming to write a history, while the reigning Orzas is old and has adopted nobody.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is direct about practical work and evasive when a question touches the charter. He turns an argument toward the next task that must be paid for, sometimes before everyone has said what they came to say.

### Motivation

He wants Elissara’s own courts and council to survive the next accession, with its spring payment made and its river mouth working.

### Strengths

He understands what the city’s chartered obligations cost in timber, labor and money. He can negotiate work shared among guilds that distrust one another’s political aims.

### Weaknesses

He assumes that keeping the city running justifies keeping some decisions away from its representatives. His third year gives opponents time to notice how often he makes that argument.

## Social

The shipwrights are his constituency, not the whole city. [[being-vlkmsathrs|Volkema Satheris]] keeps the seamen’s roll while a separate representative holds their council seat. The Salkalon deputy of the Great College counts the city’s guilds among the province’s; the council answers over his head to the Tevraks. Bizarian clerks collect the pledged customs outside the walls, beyond the council’s own dues.

### Affiliations

- **Tevran of Elissara**: a Kelros elected to hold Holkelros.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The charter copies.** Zenothen hires a party to learn which guilds have supplied the Delalara advocate and where the copies go. The commission asks for an account of the traffic, not the seizure of every copy.
2. **The channel’s price.** Guilds dispute their shares of urgent dredging work. Zenothen wants soundings checked by people unconnected to either contractor before he asks the council to pay.
3. **The spring note.** The city’s spring payment travels as a Danvar note. A delayed messenger leaves the council without its acknowledgment, and Zenothen commissions a party to trace the delivery before a creditor can call the delay a default.

:::secret {title="The unsealed charter"}
The charter in the chest carries the last Orzas’s seal, not the present sovereign’s. The copy sent for the reigning Orzas’s seal lies in the chancery at Orzara, unsealed and unrefused. Zenothen pays a chancery clerk each spring to keep it at the bottom of the pile. He believes the imperial councilors would refuse it and sell the city to Salkalon for the price of a loan if it came before them. That belief explains his payment; it does not establish what the councilors decide if the copy is brought out.
:::
