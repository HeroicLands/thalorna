# Appearance {#appearance}

Thelmara Peleris is 58 years old. Her brown hair is threaded with silver and gathered low behind her head. She has hazel eyes, a broad face and a steady, low voice. At guild sessions she wears its great gold pectoral; at her own desk she wears the gold balance-scale pin of a great house’s senior partner. She lays a disputed note beside the account against which it is drawn before hearing its owner’s explanation.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Thelmara is senior partner of [House of Peleris]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, the largest of the great banking houses, and Danvor Maldor of [[affiliation-danvar|The Bankers' Guild]]. Its council at [[place-orzara|Orzara]] elects the chief officer for ten years, renewable once. She is in her eighth year and eligible for that renewal. Her political ability and the concessions she wins from the Tarmeks give her standing beyond the wealth of her own house.

She wants a traveler’s note to be honored without a dispute about the throne’s next loan becoming a dispute at every counter in the west. The great houses lend to the crown and seek concessions in return. The Great College’s charter review puts the guild’s own enforcement under scrutiny just as powerful houses ask to keep its auditors out of their books. Thelmara must answer for the guild without turning every case into a bargain for her renewal.

## Background

**The Peleris partnership.** The house’s place rests on nine generations of its family and three centuries of banking at Orzara. Thelmara learns its business through accounts that join distant transactions to promises made at her own counter.

**The council.** Senior partnership brings dealings with great houses whose interests do not match her own. She builds influence through agreements that each house can defend to its partners, and remembers who accepts the cost of keeping one.

**The eighth year.** Her tenure steadies the guild through the years after Taruzelian secession. The treasury concessions credited to her make renewal plausible, while the charter review and the throne’s debt make every concession more expensive to defend.

## Psyche

### Personality

She is sociable over a meal and spare with reassurances in business. She asks a petitioner what result he can afford as readily as what result he wants.

### Motivation

She wants the guild’s credit and its right to examine its own members to survive the imperial succession together.

### Strengths

She can turn a quarrel between houses into terms their partners can compare. Her knowledge of clearing makes it difficult to disguise a cost merely by moving it to another account.

### Weaknesses

Her own house’s weight makes neutrality hard to demonstrate. A decision favorable to a lesser house can look like an attempt to weaken a rival great one, even when its accounts justify it.

## Social

[[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]] presses for restrictions on the guild’s auditors as part of the throne’s borrowing. [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]], the Imperial Tarkelos, negotiates the other side of those loans. [[being-slkmrtlthts|Solkimar Talothates]] presides over the College reviewing the guild’s charter. Thelmara can introduce investigators to a chapterhouse; she cannot make another house welcome an examination.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The suspect notes.** Good forgeries of travelers’ notes reach several chapters. Thelmara hires a party to compare their first presentations and find a common source before the council decides which losses to honor.
2. **The auditor’s pattern.** An auditor’s findings repeatedly favor houses with particular court connections. Thelmara needs independent evidence of the transactions before she can distinguish corruption from a politically convenient accusation.
3. **The petitioners’ seat.** The Aureldian crowns seek representation for a great house outside imperial territory. Thelmara asks a party to bring the petitioners’ exact terms to divided partners who have heard incompatible accounts of the request.
