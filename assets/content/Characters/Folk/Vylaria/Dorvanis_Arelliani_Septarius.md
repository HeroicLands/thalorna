# Appearance {#appearance}

Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius is a 66-year-old man who stands 5'11" tall and is of medium build. He has medium skin, smooth and lined about the eyes, a bald crown fringed with short gray hair and steady brown eyes behind a pair of ground-crystal lenses in a silver frame, which he wears on a cord and lifts to read. At formal sessions he wears the imperial purple stole and the great gold collar of imperial finance; on other days, a plain gray robe that could belong to any senior clerk. He speaks slowly, chooses every word, and answers most questions with a question of his own.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius is the Imperial _Tarkelos_ (treasury officer), the chief officer of [[affiliation-tarmex|The Tarmeks Orzan]], the empire's treasury and central bank. He holds the strongroom at Orzara jointly with the _holrozimos_ (chief auditor) of the Tarmeks and the captain of the imperial guard detail, he negotiates every rollover of the throne's loans with the great houses of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], and he is one of the six officers with standing access to the Orzas's privy presence. He has held the office since 705, appointed for life on the Dukran's recommendation, and he is widely held to be the most quietly powerful man in imperial government after the Dukran himself.

The Tarmeks's books are his. The figures the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] hears, the estimates [[affiliation-tolvan|the Tolvan]] works from and the terms the great houses accept all pass across his desk.

## Background

**The clerk of the strongroom.** He was born to a family of the propertied order in the river towns below the capital, the son of a grain factor, and entered the Tarmeks at seventeen as an assayer's clerk. He spent twenty years in the strongroom and the script office and became _Orsan_ (senior deputy) of the Strongroom at forty. He has never held a post outside the treasury.

**The war.** He was made _Tarkelos_ two years before the Taruzelian war. He paid for the war, for the defeat and for the pensions of a generation of discharged men, all of it on borrowed money, and he kept the throne's credit whole through every month of it. The great houses learned in those years that he never lies to them, and the Seldan learned that he never tells it more than it asks.

**The present.** He is sixty-six and in good health. His wife is dead. His son is a _therkalen_ (provincial deputy) of the Tarmeks in Hilen, and his daughter is married to a Silver house that took her for her father's name.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is patient, exact and impossible to hurry. He never raises his voice and never repeats himself. He treats every man the same, from the Orzas to the assayer's boy, with a grave courtesy that some find reassuring and others find frightening. He has no visible vices and takes no visible pleasures except reading.

### Motivation

He wants the throne's credit to outlive him, and he believes he is the only man who can keep it alive through a succession. He also wants something for himself, the one thing a lifetime of service has not given him: his family raised, so that his son is not a clerk's son in the next reign.

### Strengths

He knows the treasury from the strongroom up, after forty-nine years in it. The great houses, the Seldan, the Tolvan and the throne all need him to keep doing what he does. He is honest in every particular anyone can check.

### Weaknesses

Every faction at court would like the _Tarkelos_ in its debt, and he can oblige none of them without offending the rest. His office is for life or until the Orzas dismisses him, and the next Orzas will owe his throne to men who may want a different _Tarkelos_. He cannot block the Reformist seat at this spring's strongroom audit.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The strongroom audit.** The annual audit falls in the third week of spring, and a Reformist panelist reputed to use methods learned in Vedyaran banking sits on it. Three different households hire a party, separately, to learn what the panelist has been asking the Tarmeks's clerks.
2. **The frozen note.** The Tarmeks has suspended a moneylender house of Ólvestra, and notes cashed against it cannot be redeemed. A Provénzian merchant holding a chest of them hires a party to carry a petition to the _Tarkelos_ in person and come back with an answer.
3. **The disbursement.** A _bandren_ (general) wants silver for a campaign the Orzas has not approved. The _Tarkelos_ asks a party to take his refusal to the general's camp, in writing, and bring back the general's reply unopened.
4. **The son in Hilen.** The _Tarkelos_'s son, the Tarmeks's deputy in Hilen, has stopped answering his father's letters. Dorvanis asks a party to ride to Hilen and find out why, and to tell nobody they went.

:::secret {title="The true accounts"}
The empire's credit is the _Tarkelos_'s silence.

The strongroom is sound. Its bullion matches its books, as it has for three centuries, and the Reformist panelist will find nothing wrong in it. What the books do not show is the debt. The throne's loans are entered in the accounts the Seldan sees as rolled, never as owed, so the figure the chamber debates is about a third of the true one. The interest alone is larger than the land tax. Dorvanis keeps the true ledger in his own hand, in a cipher of his own, and it is not in the strongroom.

He also knows three things that could each end a great house or a guild. He has reckoned the House of Veldis's notes against its clearing balances, and the house has issued about a fifth more than it holds. He has entered the Tarmeks's advance to House Mardovanes on three Olkassa harvests not yet grown. And he has paid the Steel Guild for every watered blade the throne has given a foreign crown in fourteen years, and has watched the guild deliver fewer each year at the same price, as if it were drawing on a store that is running out. None of the three knows the others are exposed. Each sends him a gift, and he keeps them all, because returning one would tell the house that he knows.

His price is his house's future. He has let it be understood, at three dinners on the Heights, that the claimant whose backers see his family raised to the Elevated estate and his son confirmed in the Tarmeks will find the throne's accounts in good order on the morning of his accession. He has not said what the others will find.

The Reformist panelist will not find the debt in the strongroom. She will find, if she reads the clearing claims, that one great house draws on the Tarmeks far beyond its deposits. Dorvanis does not mean to stop her.

**Hook: the cipher ledger.** A party is hired by a claimant's household to learn where the _Tarkelos_ keeps a ledger "that is not in the strongroom". The household does not know what is in it, only that the _Tarkelos_ carries its key on the cord with his spectacles.
:::
