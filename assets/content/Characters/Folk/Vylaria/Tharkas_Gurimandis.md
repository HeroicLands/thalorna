# Appearance {#appearance}

Tharkas Gurimandis is 60 years old. He is upright and spare, with close-cut gray hair, brown eyes and the patient expression of a man waiting for a precise answer. His naval coat is carefully maintained rather than fashionable. He stands when the names of the drowned are read and remains standing until the last.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Tharkas commands the imperial fleet from [[place-castamar|Kastamar]]. A senator from a house with a long naval line, he holds the command for five years and presses for war with the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] in his letters to the Senate and in his audiences. He loses a son at the Tamzir Shoals. His officers repeat his call for a renewed war; the shipping houses of Delalara fear it would destroy their trade.

He wants crews at sea, but fewer than twenty ships occupy a quay built for sixty and much of the fleet remains in sheds. He refuses to lend warships to merchants, while considering an offer to settle Bralvik’s centuries-old Boat-Bill in return for crews. His sailors value him because he eventually obtains their pay, even when it arrives as discounted notes.

## Background

**The naval house.** He grows up among people for whom service in the fleet is both a profession and a claim to public standing. He learns to present the yards’ needs to senators who never inspect a slipway.

**The Shoals.** The defeat of 708 takes his son and much of the empire’s eastern squadron. He carries that loss into his argument for restoring the fleet.

**The command.** Five years as admiral make him responsible for late quarters, unfinished hulls and the crews’ willingness to accept another note. His patience at the pay table does not moderate his demand for war.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is disciplined, patient in an audience and stubborn once a question touches the fleet’s honor. He listens to a shipwright’s technical objection more readily than a merchant’s political one.

### Motivation

He wants the empire’s naval power restored and the defeat at the Shoals answered.

### Strengths

He understands the fleet’s needs and has the standing to keep asking the Senate for money when a lesser officer would be dismissed.

### Weaknesses

He treats commercial compromise as surrender too readily. The fleet he wants to send east cannot reliably pay the men who would row it.

## Social

The master shipwright [Kaldremos Holimandis]{.name data-type="being/npc"} keeps the yards working beneath him. [[place-mercavia|Delalara]]’s shipping houses oppose his war policy; Bralvik remembers an older naval injury and still sends its bill.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]**: of the senatorial order.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The discounted notes.** Tharkas hires a party to learn who tells oarsmen that the next quarter will never arrive, making them sell their notes at half face.
2. **The Bralvik terms.** He sends the party to learn what Bralvik would require before he makes a formal offer on the Boat-Bill.
3. **The drowned name.** A widow asks about a husband listed among the Shoals dead but reportedly alive in Taruzel. Tharkas grants access to the muster entry and wants proof before altering the memorial.
4. **The merchant’s offer.** A shipping house seeks three laid-up ships for convoy duty. Tharkas will hear a party carrying its proposal but will not promise that payment alone changes his answer.

:::secret {title="The two unpaid hulls"}
Three years ago Tharkas permits Kaldremos to complete and sell two galleys for which the treasury does not pay, as bare hulls to a Helionite captain claiming privateer-hunting work. The price keeps the yard and crews paid. The Roll of the Fleet still lists the ships in the sheds under false names. The captain buys for the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]], which fits the ships out at [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]]. Tharkas does not know their destination. Kaldremos suspects it from the payment and requested oar pattern. Proof could ruin the admiral’s war policy; it does not dictate what he or the Senate does next.
:::
