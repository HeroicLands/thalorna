# Appearance {#appearance}

Razemos Pelthen is a 42-year-old man who stands 6'0" tall and is of heavy build. He has medium skin, black hair cropped like a soldier's, brown eyes, a nose broken flat long ago and a white scar through his upper lip that makes his smile crooked. He wears the Irons' gray: a plain gray tunic of good cloth and, on a cord at his throat, a ring of gray iron, the same ring every Wearer of the faction wears, though his is old enough to have worn smooth. He is loud, warm and quick to laugh, and he knows the name of every captain in the stands.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Razemos Pelthen is Master of the Irons, the head of the [[affiliation-zargoi|Zargoi]], the larger of the two racing factions of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]. He owns the faction's stables beyond the Clearance, its teams and horses, its betting books and the debts written in them. He chooses its drivers, and he speaks for the Irons to the throne and to anyone who wants the crowd. The captains of the stand-clubs answer to him, and he can turn the clubs out into the streets of the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]] on any day he chooses.

He sells the crowd. He has let it be known that the Irons will cheer whichever claimant is named if that claimant's household promises the accession games contract, and that they will be in the streets on the night the Orzas dies whatever happens.

## Background

**The legion.** His father was a Master of the Stables and a Brother of the Stand, and Razemos grew up in the Irons' yard beyond the Clearance, among the horses. He enlisted at fifteen, as his father had, and served his twenty years in a legion of the east. He fought in the Taruzelian war, came home with a centurion's deputy's rank, a broken nose and the citizenship, and took his legion's stair on Nerval.

**The faction.** He drove a team for the Irons in his first year home and lost, and did not drive again. He was better with men than with horses. He became captain of his legion's stand-club, then Keeper of the Book, and when the old Master died without a son five years ago, the captains put the book, the stables and the teams in his hands and the Master's heirs took a price for them that Razemos is still paying.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is loud, generous and good company, and he means most of it. He pays for the funerals of his clubs' dead himself, keeps a dozen widows in bread and remembers their children's names. He also has men beaten in the crush after the races when they owe the book, and he does not see a contradiction.

### Motivation

He wants the accession games contract: the throne's silver for a season and the city's gratitude for a reign. He wants the concession of the stalls and cookshops around the Racecourse Circle, which the Golds' patrons have held for a generation. He wants the Irons to win the next great race. And he wants the faction clear of the price it owes the old Master's heirs.

### Strengths

The Warrens follow the gray. The stand-clubs are the largest organized body of men in the city outside the legions and the Watch, and they can fill a street in an hour. His book holds the debts of senators' sons, and some of those sons now sit on their fathers' benches.

### Weaknesses

The faction owes more than it earns: the old Master's heirs, the stable licenses and the price of the teams. He cannot afford to lose the games contract, and every claimant knows it. The City Watch wants the clubs broken, and a riot that goes too far would give it the excuse.

## Social

### Patrons

He has none. Several claimants' agents have bought drinks for his captains this year, and he has received them all.

### Enemies

- [[affiliation-ethloi|The Ethloi]]: The Golds, who won the last great race at the feast of the last divine Orzas.
- [[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]]: The watchmen who arrest his men when they can reach them.
- [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]]: The tribune of the veterans' stairs, who claims to speak for the same men Razemos claims to own, and tells the stairs that the factions spend their pensions on wagers.

### Others

- [[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]]: The landlord of Nerval, who rents him the ground floors where a dozen of the Irons' clubs keep their taverns. The two men have never met.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-zargoi|Zargoi]]**: Master of the Irons, a Brother of the Stand of his legion's club.
- **[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|The Vilérian Empire]]**: a citizen by twenty years' service, drawing a deputy centurion's pension.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The teams at dawn.** The Irons' teams cross the Clearance at first light on race day, and Razemos has heard that someone means them not to reach the Racecourse. He hires a party to ride with them.
2. **The son's debt.** A senator's son owes the Irons' book more than his allowance, and his father's steward is trying to buy the page. Razemos asks a party to see that the steward is told the price in person.
3. **The captain's price.** A claimant's agent is buying the captains of the Irons' biggest stand-clubs before Razemos can sell them himself. Razemos wants the agent found and the captains reminded who keeps the book.
4. **The widow's bread.** A widow the faction keeps in bread says the club captain who brings it has been keeping half. Razemos asks a party to follow the bread one month.

:::secret {title="The senator's letter"}
Razemos fixed a race for a senator. Three seasons ago a _kurthos_ (senator) of a Gilded house asked, in a letter in his own hand, for the Golds' team to win the great race on his patron's feast day, and paid for it. Razemos told his First Driver to lose by a length, and the senator's friends won heavily on the Golds. He kept the letter. It is the most dangerous paper he owns, because it can ruin the senator and Razemos both: a race fixed on a divine Orzas's feast day cheats every man who bet on it, and the senator's friends are not the only ones who did. Each of them knows the other has reason to keep it quiet, and each has begun to wonder whether the succession changes that.

The fix paid the old Master's heirs for a year. Razemos needs the games contract to pay them for the next, and he has begun to think the letter would buy it from whichever claimant the senator backs.

**Hook: the copy.** Razemos hires a party to make a fair copy of the letter, in a hand good enough to pass, so that he can show a claimant's household the copy and keep the original.
:::
