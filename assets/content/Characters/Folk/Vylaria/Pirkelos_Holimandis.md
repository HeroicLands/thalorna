# Appearance {#appearance}

Pirkelos Holimandis is a 74-year-old man who stands 6'0" tall and is of heavy build, broad still though age has softened him. He has medium skin weathered by a lifetime on the slopes, grey hair kept short, and green eyes under heavy brows. He holds his back straight like the soldier he was, walks the village wall each evening with a stick he does not lean on, and has a voice made for calling a muster.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Pirkelos Holimandis is the elder of [[place-mylanthos|Milanthos]], a walled farming village of about four hundred in a side valley off the road to [[place-tyrellan|Tirellan]] in [[place-moktur|Moktur]]. He is a former commander of the village militia and is now in his seventies. The village has no charter of its own: its taxes go to the revenue's men from Tirellan and its roads are the _pozerus_'s, and its quarrels go to Pirkelos and the heads of the oldest families on the bench under the lime tree by the green. He keeps the militia's list, every man who can carry a spear, and it musters at the [Blockhouse]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at the top of the village when the smoke from the ridge says a war-band is out.

It was Pirkelos who first told [[being-azendsdmtr|Azendas Damator]] to take up healing, and Azendas is now Senior Priest of [[affiliation-lizelas|Lizelas]] on the Tirellan circuit. When the temple hierarchy in the capital questions the priest's methods, Azendas finds shelter in Milanthos, and Pirkelos gives him the local political cover. Asked why a village that counts every sheaf gives a tenth of its harvest to a priest, Pirkelos points at the barn and says that the priest's blessing fills it.

## Background

**The militia.** Pirkelos commanded the village's militia for years. The Blockhouse it built in the hunger raids, for the people to shelter in, still stands over the village, and he knows what a war-band does to a village whose wall is not manned.

**The priest.** When a weary former soldier came to work the farms of the circuit, Pirkelos encouraged him to take up healing in the goddess's service. Azendas has been his friend since.

**The elder.** In his old age the village made him its voice. He speaks for Milanthos to the revenue, to the temple and to the riders from Tirellan, and he does not ask the village's leave to do it.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is plain, gruff and unbending, a commander who still thinks in musters and supplies. He trusts what he has seen with his own eyes and little else.

### Motivation

He wants Milanthos fed through every winter and safe behind its wall, and he wants the priest who helps him do it left alone.

### Strengths

The village trusts him without question, he knows every family and every field, and he can turn out eighteen men with spears at an hour's notice.

### Weaknesses

He is old, and his health is the village's weak point. He will not give way to the revenue or the temple even when giving way is wiser.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The auditor's visit.** An auditor of the revenue has come to Tirellan to learn why the temple's harvest receipts from the circuit are so small, and she is riding out to Milanthos next. Pirkelos wants a party to keep her company on the road, and to keep her from the Blockhouse.
2. **The blight.** A blight has come into the barley on the upper strips that the priest's blessing has not stopped, and some farmers whisper that Lizelas has turned from Azendas. Pirkelos wants it stopped before the whispers reach the circuit.
3. **The muster.** The smoke on the ridge calls the militia out, and Pirkelos has eighteen men fit to carry a spear. A party in the village is asked to stand with them at the wall until the riders from Tirellan come.
4. **The successor.** Pirkelos has heard that the temple means to give the Tirellan circuit to a younger priest with friends in the capital. He wants to know who the man is, and what he would do with the village's harvest.

:::secret {title="The Truth of Milanthos"}
The temple's share never leaves the village. For nine harvests the sheaves the households carry to the Blockhouse have stayed there, threshed and stored in sealed jars in the tower's cellar and in two pits dug under its floor, and the receipts Azendas sends to the temple in Tirellan record a fraction of them. The rest is a famine store for the whole circuit. Pirkelos and Azendas agreed it between them in the year the imperial road's last bridge below the provincial boundary fell, and the caravans that brought southern grain in a bad year stopped coming. In the wet summer three years ago the store fed four villages through the winter.

What is in the cellar is two winters' bread for a thousand people, taken from the temple and from the emperor's tithe on the temple's grain, and enough to have Azendas stripped of his rank and Pirkelos tried for theft. Every household in Milanthos knows, and none has told. Pirkelos keeps the Blockhouse key on his belt and the auditor out of it.
:::
