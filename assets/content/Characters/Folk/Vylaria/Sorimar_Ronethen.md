# Appearance {#appearance}

Sorimar Ronethen is a 55-year-old woman who stands 5'8" tall and is of heavy build. She has medium skin browned by years of working in yards, brown hair pinned up under a plain cap, and brown eyes that settle on a piece of work and stay there. Her hands are a carver's, broad and scarred across the knuckles. She wears the guild's dark coat over working clothes and smells faintly of sawdust and beeswax. She speaks slowly and expects to be listened to.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Sorimar Ronethen is the head of [The Woodworkers' Guild of Nartun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, which keeps its hall on the market square of the lower city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]]. Its masters are carvers, turners, joiners and the charcoal-burners whose work the guild judges, and they take the temples' commissions for altar-screens, panels and choir-stalls and sell the rest down the road to [[place-hylen|Hilen]]. She presides over the judging of the guild's work, and she decides which disputes between its masters go to the guild's own hearing and which go to the courts of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom]].

She has kept [[being-brnksksrns|Branakis Kaseranis]], the charcoal-burner of [[place-kalderenna|Kalderenna]], in the guild against steady pressure from its younger and more pliable masters, who find him difficult and his prices high.

## Background

**The yards.** Sorimar was apprenticed as a girl to a carver of the lower city, and she spent her working years in the yards and workshops of Nartun, carving for the temples.

**Mastery.** She became a master of the guild and made her name on temple work, the screens and panels that the priest-noble families commission and the pilgrims come to see.

**The hall.** The masters chose her to lead the guild, and she has done it since in the way she carved: slowly, to a standard, and without much interest in what was easy.

## Psyche

### Personality

She is patient, stubborn and plain-spoken. She cares more for good work than for good manners, and she will defend a difficult master whose work is sound over an agreeable one whose work is not.

### Motivation

She wants the guild's mark to mean what it says. A charcoal, a panel or a beam judged by the guild should be the best of its kind, and she wants the temples and the smiths to keep trusting the judging.

### Strengths

She commands respect in the guild and in the temples, whose commissions are the guild's living. She knows every master's work by sight.

### Weaknesses

The younger masters want the guild run for profit, and their number grows every year. Her standard costs the guild sales, and its members feel the cost.

## Social

### Others

- [[being-volkhemarzur|Volkemar Zurmelates]]: A trader running charcoal works on a great scale, whose cheap charcoal undercuts the guild's burners and whose friends among the younger masters would like the judging eased.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The judging.** The buyers who undercut Branakis want the Order's knight at Kalderenna to find him in breach of the estate's rules. Sorimar wants a party to bring her a sample of his charcoal and a sample of theirs, carried down from the clamps sealed, so that the guild can judge them before the estate court sits.
2. **The altar-screen.** A guild master's screen for a temple has cracked before its consecration. Sorimar wants to know whether the timber was bad or the work was, before the temple asks.
3. **The vote.** The younger masters have called a meeting of the guild to strike Branakis from it, and Sorimar needs a party to bring him down from the forest to answer for himself, which he has refused to do twice.
4. **The grey stand.** A burner of the guild has brought Sorimar charcoal from a stand whose leaves turned the wrong color, and says it is not like other charcoal. She wants it taken to someone who can say why.

:::secret {title="Why she keeps him"}
Sorimar keeps Branakis in the guild because the guild's judging of charcoal rests on him. His clamps are the standard the burners are measured against, and if he is struck from the guild, there is no master left who can say what good charcoal is, and the cheap charcoal from the dead stands will pass the judging within a year. She has never said this to him, or to the younger masters, because both would take it the wrong way.
:::
