# Appearance {#appearance}

Darmun Parvandar is a 51-year-old man who stands 6'0" tall and is of heavy build. He is broad in the shoulder and square in the face, with tanned weathered skin, black hair cropped close, heavy brows and a thin mouth that seldom moves. He wears a soldier's coat of dark blue wool under the governor's gold-edged mantle, and a brass gate-key on a cord at his neck that he touches when he is thinking.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Darmun Parvandar is the Tagemader of [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]], the governor who holds the capital's garrison and revenue for the Shebarader apart from the palace. His men keep the city's gates and streets. The court guard keeps the palace and answers to the Shebarader alone, and the vazhmader's court and the market inspector's men answer to other officers of the realm, so within the walls his is one authority among four and the one that carries the keys.

His men also keep the gate of the Tellumi quarter. The four exile houses that hold their courts there petition the throne for wardens of their own on that gate, and Darmun is the officer whom the petition would replace. Each time the petition comes before the court he advises the Shebarader against it, without heat. The law of the Kingdom forbids the exile houses any military activity, and a gate watched by their own men is a gate the law does not watch. The Council of the Princes sits in the city once a year and fills it with the princes' parties, and for that month he counts every sword that comes through a gate.

His revenue is the city's dues and the gate tolls. The great houses of the court want the Tellumi courts kept in their place, and he is careful that nobody can say he has taken a house's side.

## Background

**The garrison.** Darmun is the son of a captain of the Shebarakhend garrison, and he enlisted in it at sixteen. He served on the gates and the walls and then with the southern host, and he made his name keeping a garrison's pay ledgers so exactly that no officer above or below him could take a coin from them.

**The city.** The Shebarader made him tagemader of the capital when its gates needed a man the great houses could not buy. He has held the city since as he held the ledgers: by counting, by the law of the Kingdom and by taking no house's side.

## Psyche

### Personality

Darmun is grave, methodical and dry. He keeps ledgers of everything his men do and reads them at night. He does not take a bribe and does not take offense, and both facts make the court's great houses uneasy.

### Motivation

He wants the capital's gates in the Shebarader's hands and in no one else's. He wants to be able to say, to any house, that he treated it as he treated every other.

### Strengths

He knows every gate, street and guard-post of the city by the hour, and he is trusted by the Shebarader because he has never asked him for a favor.

### Weaknesses

He cannot see a request as anything but a precedent. A petition that deserves a hearing on its merits gets the same refusal as a bribe, and a house that learns this stops bringing him the warnings he needs.

## Social

### Companions

The captain of the gate-guard and the quarter-wardens who keep the city's streets at night.

### Patrons

The Shebarader, by whose grant he holds the city.

### Enemies

The exile houses, who want his gate, and the great houses, who find his refusals as even as the Tellumi find them.

- **[[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]]**: He keeps the rites of the city's fire-temples as the governor must.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The count at the gate.** A Tellumi retinue is said to have more armed men in its quarter than the law allows. Darmun wants a party that is no house's client to count them, and to tell him what they find before the Council of the Princes sits.
2. **The charts that changed hands.** A great house's astrologer has gone over to an exile court with the charts of twenty years, and the house cannot be seen to ask for them back. Darmun wants them found without a warrant, because a warrant would name the house.
3. **The unsealed garden.** A disgraced officer's garden is to be sold through the vazhmader's court to a Tellumi court. A great house wants a flaw found in the deeds, and Darmun wants to know who in his own garrison has been paid to find it.
