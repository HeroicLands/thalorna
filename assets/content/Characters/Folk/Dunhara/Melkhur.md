# Appearance {#appearance}

Melkhur is a 55-year-old man who stands 5'11" tall and is of light build. He is thin and gentle-faced, with tawny skin, shoulder-length hair going gray at the temples, pale gray eyes and a small mouth that rests in a courteous half-smile. He wears the dyed blue of a poet's sash over plain undyed wool, and he walks under the palms with his hands behind him.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Melkhur is a poet by training and a sorcerer by choice, and he holds [[place-vzhmvd|the Weighing-Place]], the palm oasis where the judging poets of a third tribe once weighed the recitations of two tribes in dispute. He gives no clan name. The judging poets of [[affiliation-bnchvzhmvd|the Bench of Vazhmavad]] still sit on the benches in their blue sashes and still recite, but they recite other people's lines, sentence after sentence in even voices, and they do not know their own names. Melkhur decides everything else: who draws water, who eats the dates, who is received at his table and who rides out.

He is courteous to the point of tenderness. A party that comes to Vazhmavad finds the poets reciting and Melkhur walking out under the palms to meet it, inviting the guests to bread and salt at his table in the shade of the largest palm. He asks their names. The tribes say that he takes a name as other men take a coin, by asking for it and being given it, and that a tribe whose genealogy goes loses its standing in the recitation and with it its claim to wells and wives and herds. The raiders who strike the caravans of the southern circuit, the Unrecited, are men and women whose names he has taken. Every guide routes around the oasis.

The deepest well of the oasis is the one he keeps for himself. Nobody else draws from it, and those who have looked down it say the water at the bottom gives back no reflection. He does not conceal what he does. He answers a direct question exactly.

## Background

**The training.** Melkhur learned the recitations as a boy among the poets, and he names no tribe or clan as his own. He came to Vazhmavad as a judging poet of the bench while tribes still brought their disputes to be weighed, and he held more of the recitation in memory than any other poet on it.

**The weighing.** The tribes say the bench's last judgments went unenforced and that the tribes stopped bringing their cases. In the years that followed, the judging poets began to recite other people's lines and forgot their own names, and Melkhur alone kept his.

**The oasis.** He has held the oasis since the bench fell silent. He keeps the deepest well for himself, receives every guest at his table and asks every guest's name, and the guides have learned to route around him.

## Psyche

### Personality

Melkhur is mild, attentive and honest. He is never rude and never in a hurry, and he says exactly what he does and what it costs. His courtesy has no seam in it, which is why guests believe it.

### Motivation

He wants the recitations kept. He wants a hearing that no tribe will bring him, and a judgment that someone will enforce.

### Strengths

He holds more recitation in memory than any living poet and can name the line a sheikh cannot prove. He is at ease with every question and every refusal.

### Weaknesses

The tribes say the well asks and he gives, and that it has not stopped asking. He cannot refuse a guest who asks him for something he has the means to give.

## Social

### Companions

The judging poets, who walk the palms beside him and cannot say who he is, and the Unrecited, who water at the oasis between raids.

### Patrons

None among the living. The tribes say that his sorcery comes up out of the deepest well, and that nothing else answers him.

### Enemies

The tribes whose lines he has taken, who cannot prove what they lost. The Lawkeeper at Ghavulavad, who needs a bench of a third tribe for every dispute and has none to send to. [[being-trvshshrdr|Torvash Shiradar]], whose poets went to the weighing-place with a dispute and have not come back.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-bnchvzhmvd|Bench of Vazhmavad]]**: Melkhur holds the office of Holder of the Oasis, the judging poet whose word settles what the bench does.

## Plot Hooks

1. **A claim lost.** A sheikh is asked to prove his line before the common council at Ghavulavad and cannot, because his poet came back from Vazhmavad empty. He wants a party to go to the oasis and ask for the recitation back.
2. **The first case in years.** A sheikh whose line is disputed brings his poet and his rival's to Vazhmavad, because no other bench will hear a case this old. Melkhur agrees to have it weighed, and the sheikh wants the party with him on the benches when the recitations begin.
3. **Bread and salt.** A party that has eaten at Melkhur's table is his guest for the stated days. He offers help with whatever they came for, and the price is a name.

:::secret {title="What lives in the well"}
The source of his sorcery is below the deepest well of the oasis. Melkhur found a hollow in the water, a place where the dark does not reflect, and the thing that lives in it takes what is given and returns nothing. It is [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]], the chaos from outside creation, and it is attracted to what the Dunhari hold most dear. It accepts a recitation as payment, and the more he gives it, the more it gives him: sight into a man's line, speech in a dead voice, a word that unmakes an oath. He pays with other people's names. He does not know, or has chosen not to learn, what the hollow keeps of its own.
:::

:::secret {title="The Riders Who Ask Your Name"}
Melkhur is paying for something he lost, and the hollow returned it. In the poets' account of the second refusal, a tribe in the south called Bazuran was swallowed by its neighbors when [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|the Warrior's Circle]] refused to act against them. Its poets came to Vazhmavad seeking a judgment and the weighing-place weighed in their favor, and nobody enforced it. When the tribe was gone, so were the recitations that proved it had ever been wronged. He keeps them. He is the last place the tribe exists, and if its recitations are returned to the tribes that swallowed it, he loses his power and his purpose together and does not fight to keep either.
:::
