# Appearance {#appearance}

Ghezrun Mazhen is a 48-year-old man who stands 5'9" tall and is of medium build. His olive skin is smooth, his black hair is oiled and kept short, and his beard is pointed and clipped to a line. His hazel eyes are friendly and his smile arrives a moment before he does. He wears a coat of Taruzelian silk in a muted green, with a ring on three fingers of each hand, and he smells of cedar.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Ghezrun Mazhen is the Tagemader of [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]], the governor who holds the city for the Shebarader with its garrison and its revenue. His is the richest governorship in the realm and it is held on the terms of every other, by the Shebarader's grant and revocable at a word. Every caravan from the interior pays its toll under his walls, and the city's wealth depends on the road staying open. He plays the temples and the toll-house against each other for the same reason, and he plays the great powers of the region against one another as carefully as the Shebarader does.

The two quarrels he has to manage are the sealed bales and the handover. The fire-temples hold estates in the oases round the city, and a load that comes in under a temple's seal pays no toll, so a priest will lend his seal to a bale for a share of its price. Ghezrun wants the seals opened and checked at the gate, and the priests call that sacrilege. At a well a day out, tribal escorts hand the desert caravans to the city's riders, and one tribe holds a blood-debt against a family in the garrison and refuses to hand over to any troop with that family's men in it. He must choose between moving the men and moving the well.

He is courteous, generous with his table and quick to say what a guest wants to hear. He hungers for the king's signet that lies in the vault the tribes call the Dry Stair, and he pays well for it and says he does not care how it is taken. He knows that the governor of [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]] pays guides and thieves to dig at the Stair, and he means to have the signet in his own hand before anyone that governor hires brings it up.

## Background

**The house.** Ghezrun is the second son of a house of the Amradi court that has served the Shebaraders as clerks and envoys for generations. His mother's people are an old family of the wadi towns, and he grew up in [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]] between his father's chancery and his mother's garden, learning the court's manners from the one and the long memory of the wadi families from the other.

**The road.** He entered the Shebarader's service as a clerk of the Yorvabun, auditing the toll ledgers that came up from Tahrodan, and then rode for ten years as an envoy to the caravan-masters and the tribal escorts. He learned there what a delay costs a caravan and what a gift of grain buys at a well, and he learned to price a man's goodwill to the coin.

**The city.** The Shebarader gave him Tahrodan when the governor before him was recalled over a shortfall in the toll. He has held it since by keeping the toll silver flowing north, the audit clean and the temples and the garrison each in need of him. The sealed bales and the handover well are the two quarrels he has not settled, and the signet is the one ambition he keeps from the court.

## Psyche

### Personality

Ghezrun is warm, fluent and observant. He remembers a guest's wife's name and the price of her father's camels, and he uses both gently. He is generous because it is cheaper than being feared, and he knows exactly how much each kindness cost.

### Motivation

He wants the city's toll to keep flowing, the audit to pass without a question, and the temples and the garrison to keep needing him more than they need each other.

### Strengths

He reads a room in a breath and has an answer for each person in it. He keeps the toll-house ledgers honest enough that the audit sent to Shebarakhend never gives the Yorvabun a reason to look closer.

### Weaknesses

He believes he can arrange anything with the right payment, and so he hires strangers for work he does not understand and does not ask what it costs anyone but him.

## Social

### Companions

The clerks of the toll-house, the captain of the mounted garrison, and the caravan-masters who have learned that his table is where a delay is forgiven.

### Patrons

The Shebarader, by whose grant he holds Tahrodan, and the great houses of the court who value the toll silver he sends north.

### Enemies

The priests of the fire-temples who lend their seals, and the tribe that holds the blood-debt against the garrison.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]]**: Ghezrun holds the office of Tagemader of Tahrodan, governor of the fortress city in the Shebarader's name.
- **[[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]]**: He keeps the rites and honors the priests, as the governor must be seen to do.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The thief.** Ghezrun hires a party to bring the king's signet out of the vault of [[place-mezhvadez|the Dry Stair]] ([[scenario-kprdrystr|A Stair of Veiled Women]]). He pays in advance, asks no questions and does not care how it is taken.
2. **The sealed bales.** The toll-house's clerk believes a priest has lent his temple's seal to a whole caravan, and Ghezrun cannot open the bales without the priests' consent. He wants strangers to travel with the caravan to the coast and learn what was under the seal and who sold it.
3. **The handover well.** A tribal escort reaches the handover well and finds only the troop's horses and nobody to hand the caravan to. Ghezrun wants a party to carry his word to the tribe that keeps the blood-debt, and to bring back whether it will treat.

:::secret {title="The lock of the door"}
The signet is the lock of the door of black stone in the vault, and the keeper of the king's household holds that door shut by her will. A tagemader who gets the signet out has the proof he wanted and a debt he does not know about. The one who carries it out is followed, and the debt is Ghezrun's as soon as the seal is in his hand.
:::

:::secret {title="The standing no word can revoke"}
Ghezrun's mother's house keeps a genealogy it has never recited at any council, which runs to a younger brother of the king whose name is struck from the walls. Ghezrun believes the signet would prove the line older than the Shebarader's and make him the one man in Amradad whose standing is his by blood. The Kingdom's law holds otherwise: a standing is the Shebarader's to grant, and he knows it. He does not want the throne. He wants to hold Tahrodan as a right, so that, as he believes, no word of the Shebarader could take it back.
:::
