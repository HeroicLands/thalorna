# Appearance {#appearance}

Samardan Sivadar is a 58-year-old man who stands 5'10" tall and is of medium build. His skin is tanned and clear, his short hair is gray, and his dark brown eyes rest on whoever is speaking and do not move to the next face until the speaker has finished. His face is an oval with an aquiline nose, arched brows and a thin mouth. He wears a long robe of undyed wool over court silk and no jewel but a plain iron ring on his left hand.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Samardan Sivadar is the Shebarader of [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|the Kingdom of Amradad]], and the throne is the only source of standing in his realm. He rules in his own right from [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]]. His orders pass through the Grand Vashkader to the ministers of the treasury, the army, the post and the waters, and from them to a governor in every city and oasis. Every rank in the realm is his grant and his to revoke, so his brothers and his sons hold none by blood: each of them stands where an appointment or a governorship puts him.

He holds the crossroads where the roads from Bizaria, Vedyara and the Khazrin meet, and the caravan tolls make him far richer than his thinly peopled land would suggest. He needs the court's great houses to govern and the Tellumi merchant-houses to carry the trade his treasury taxes. Four Tellumi princely houses keep their courts in exile in his capital on his courtesy, and they press him for three privileges: their own Delkoshekhs judging Tellumi cases, their own wardens on the gate of their quarter, and places for their princes in the order of the court beside his great officers. He grants a little and refuses most, and he lends the Council of the Princes the ground it meets on once a year.

He claims authority over every Dunhari people, and the tribes find the claim hilarious. He knows it, and he does not press it: grain and iron buy the tribes' herds and escorts, and a compact that holds at one well is worth more to him than a title.

## Background

**The son.** Samardan is one of several sons of the Shebarader before him, and as a boy he held no more standing than any of his brothers. He learned the court as a clerk in the Grand Vashkader's divan and governed an oasis of the wadi towns as its _tagemader_, because his father held that a son who might rule should first learn what it is to hold a revocable post.

**The succession.** His father named him heir before the court in his last illness, so the great officers had a throne to serve on the day the old Shebarader died, and the realm passed without a war between brothers. His brothers kept the posts they held, and none of them holds more.

**The reign.** He has ruled since by the patience he learned in the divan: he hears every petition to its end, grants a little and refuses most, and keeps the great houses and the Tellumi merchant-houses each in need of him.

## Psyche

### Personality

Samardan is patient, courteous and sparing of speech. He answers a petition with a question, and the question is usually the one the petitioner hoped to avoid. He is slow to anger and does not forget.

### Motivation

He wants the throne to remain the only door to standing, and the crossroads to remain open. He wants neither the great houses nor the exile courts to learn how much he needs them.

### Strengths

He knows every office in the realm and who holds it, and he knows which holder owes his place to which patron. He has kept the Tellumi and the great houses balanced against each other through a long run of small crises.

### Weaknesses

Because he needs both sides, he cannot punish either. A great house that is outspent and an exile court that is refused both know it, and each presses the more.

## Social

### Companions

The Grand Vashkader, through whom his orders pass, and the Gholvabun, who keeps the door to his person. The Master of the Post tells him what the realm says when it thinks he is not listening.

### Patrons

None. Every other standing in Amradad is his grant.

### Enemies

The great houses that hold their standing by his word resent being outspent in their own capital. [[being-aravdnstrgz|Aravdán the Stargazer]], who convenes the Council of the Princes, is the one man at court whose weight rivals his, and the two are careful with each other.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]]**: Samardan holds the office of Shebarader, the throne itself.
- **[[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]]**: The Kingdom's established faith is his, and its fire-temples are the wonders of his realm.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The three privileges.** The Shebarader wants a party that is neither of the court nor of an exile house to learn what each house would pay for each privilege before he refuses them again.
2. **The officer on two payrolls.** A vashkader of one of the great houses believes an officer of the Gholvabun's household takes Tellumi silver to move the exile princes up the order of the court. The Shebarader wants the officer and the purse found by people the Gholvabun's household does not know by sight.
3. **The treaty in the post-bag.** A courier carrying his terms for a herd treaty to the tribes is overdue beyond Qarvelakhend. He wants the bag back before the sheikhs hear the terms from someone who has read them first.
