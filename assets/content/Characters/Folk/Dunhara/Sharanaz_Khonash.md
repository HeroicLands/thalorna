# Appearance {#appearance}

Sharanaz Khonash is a 60-year-old woman who stands 5'5" tall and is of light build. Her skin is tawny and weathered, her gray hair is braided close to her head, and her hazel eyes are quick under straight brows. She wears the plain brown wool of the palm gardens with a green sash and carries a staff of date-palm wood that she strikes once on the ground to open a sitting and once to close it.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Sharanaz Khonash is the Speaker of the common council of [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|the Dunhara Tribes]] at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]]. She keeps the peace of the meeting and the order of speaking and has no other power. The council takes its Speaker from the garden families who live at the well year round, whose households follow no tribe's circuit and have no herds to defend, and hers is one of them. The delegates who argue before her may speak and may not bind, and her office is to see that each is heard in turn.

The council is sitting on the Kingdom of Amradad's offer of grain and iron for escort on the roads into the Kingdom. The northern tribes want the compact sworn. The tribes whose Keepers of the Feud carry a tally of Amradi blood will not sit at a fire with the envoys at all, and [[being-shadznkmrz|Shadezan Kamruz]] accepts it only if his riders hold the whole road. She has no power to end the argument, so each sitting takes it up again and binds nobody. Meanwhile the Lawkeeper hears more disputes than one man can, because no guide will take a poet to [[place-vzhmvd|Vazhmavad]], and the common council has not agreed whether the bench there sits by right.

## Background

**The gardens.** Sharanaz was born into a palm-garden family of Ghavulavad and grew up tending the date palms and carrying water to the delegates' fires when the council sat. She learned the genealogies of every tribe by listening at those fires, and she learned which delegates would not sit beside which.

**The order of speaking.** For many years she kept the order of speaking for the Speaker before her, calling each delegate in turn and remembering who had spoken and who was owed a hearing. When that Speaker died, the delegates gave her the staff, and every tribe accepted her, because no tribe could say she had favored another.

**The staff.** She has held the staff through droughts, feuds and the long argument over the Amradi compact. She has never ended an argument, because the office gives her no power to, and she has never let one end in blood at the council's fires.

## Psyche

### Personality

Sharanaz is dry, even-tempered and exact. She lets a delegate finish and then asks him to say the part he skipped. She does not raise her voice, and the tents quiet when she lowers hers.

### Motivation

She wants one sitting to end in a ruling that holds. She wants the council to settle by its own custom whether the bench at Vazhmavad sits by right, so that the Lawkeeper has a third tribe's bench to send disputes to.

### Strengths

She remembers who spoke in what order at every sitting in living memory, and no tribe can claim a precedent she cannot answer from memory.

### Weaknesses

She has no power and knows it exactly. A Speaker who steps beyond the order of speaking loses the one thing the delegates grant her, and she has not found a way to be useful that stays inside it.

## Social

### Companions

The council's Lawkeeper, Truce-Warden and Water-Warden, who keep the customs, the peace and the well between sittings, and the garden families of Ghavulavad, who feed the delegates.

### Patrons

The delegates, who grant her the staff for as long as every tribe can accept her.

### Enemies

None who say so. Delegates who want a sitting to end their way find her slow, and say it.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]]**: Sharanaz holds the office of Speaker of the Council, presiding over the common council at Ghavulavad.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The bench of a third tribe.** The Lawkeeper has more disputes than he can hear. Sharanaz wants a party to find a tribe whose poets will sit as a bench, and to bring its sheikh to Ghavulavad before the council rises.
2. **The envoys' salt.** The Amradi envoys camp at the edge of the gravel as guests with salt and no seat. She wants someone the delegates do not read as any tribe's man to carry a plain question between the envoys and Shadezan's riders, so that his terms are known before the next sitting.
3. **The tally disputed.** A Keeper of the Feud has recited a count of blood the Amradi owe, and a rival tribe says he has added to it. Sharanaz wants a witness who can check it against the wells where the blood was shed, and who will report to the council and to nobody else.
