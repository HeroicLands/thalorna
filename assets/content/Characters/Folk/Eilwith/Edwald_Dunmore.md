# Appearance {#appearance}

Edwald Dunmore is a 51-year-old man who stands 6'0" tall and is of light build, with smooth pale skin, auburn hair and hazel eyes. He wears the temple's plain vestments a little loose on him and walks the nave slowly, hands folded, stopping to straighten a bench or a lamp.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Edwald Dunmore is a priest of the temple of the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]] in [[place-ashford|Ashford]], addressed as Father. He views [[being-aldthchltn|Dunith Chilton]] as a useful asset but no more than that. Their relationship is cordial but tense, and his spiritual authority stands in implicit competition with her charismatic power.

He keeps the temple's daily worship: the morning rite, the blessing of the bridge on market days and the settling of small disputes brought to the altar by drovers and townsfolk. Ashford's merchants send their money to the suppers and their troubles to him, and he knows the difference.

## Psyche

Edwald is patient, precise and wary of anything that turns worship into an occasion. He measures the temple by whether the poor of the town are served at the door, and he distrusts a blessing that can be bought a seat at a table. He says so rarely and in mild words.

## Social

[[being-godrunwinley|Godrun Winli]], the high priestess, relies on his steadiness and does not ask him to attend the suppers. He insists to [[being-aldthchltn|Dunith Chilton]] that worship needs room among the negotiations, and she answers him courteously and does not change her seating.

## Plot Hooks

The altar dispute
: Two drovers bring a quarrel over a strayed herd to Edwald's altar before the autumn fair. He asks the party to walk the ford with both men and report what the tolls and the herd's route actually were, so that his judgment rests on what happened there.

The poor box
: The temple's alms for the town's poor have run short while the suppers prosper. Edwald asks a trustworthy traveler to learn which merchant families give to the suppers and not to the door.
