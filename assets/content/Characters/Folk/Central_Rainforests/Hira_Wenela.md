# Appearance {#appearance}

Hira Wenela is a 47-year-old woman who stands 4'8" tall and is of scant build. She has brown skin, black braided hair, a heart-shaped face and arched brows, and she tilts her head when she listens. A bracelet of nut-shells clicks on each wrist. She wears a bark-cloth skirt and no ornament that marks her as leader.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Hira Wenela is the leader of the [[affiliation-wenelatribe|Wenela Tribe]], whose four bands camp at her fire at [[place-wenelacamp|Wenela Camp]] in the honey season. She decides nothing alone and gives no orders. The bands bring her their quarrels and listen to the Caller of the Sogola on the nights of a failing season.

## Background

**The song.** The song band has sung every night for a moon, and the hunting has not come back. Hira has been asked at three fires whether the forest has stopped listening to this tribe.

**The fires.** Two bands have begun to camp nearer the [[affiliation-ngotolatribe|Ngotola]] fire. She does not call them back, since a leader who tells a band where to camp stops being a leader.

## Psyche

### Personality

She is watchful and patient and answers a hard question with a story from the elders.

### Motivation

She wants the forest woken and her bands at one fire again before the honey season, and she does not want to be the leader who lost the tribe to a better-fed neighbor.

### Strengths

The elders say she listens, and the Caller of the Sogola trusts her not to hurry a song.

### Weaknesses

She cannot hold a band that wants to go, and the longer the song runs, the more bands ask whether the forest hears it.

## Social

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-wenelatribe|Wenela Tribe]]**: leader, recognized by the bands who camp at her fire.

## Plot Hooks

1. **Fish for the song camp.** She wants fish carried from the Lihola landing to the song camp, because no one in the song band can leave it while the song goes on.
2. **What the Ngotola gave.** She asks a party to learn what the Ngotola gave the two bands that left her fire.
3. **The shared stand.** Two bands claim one flowering stand, and she wants a witness from outside who has no share in it.
