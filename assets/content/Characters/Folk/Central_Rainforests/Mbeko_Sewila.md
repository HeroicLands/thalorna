# Appearance {#appearance}

Mbeko Sewila is a 74-year-old man who stands 4'11" tall. His ebony skin is deeply wrinkled, his head is shaved, and his white eyebrows are bushy over eyes that do not blink often. He is of light build and leans on a staff of black wood and coughs in the cold hours before dawn. He speaks very quietly, and the whole tribe leans in to hear.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Mbeko Sewila is the kelendo of [[place-kubisewi|Kubi Sewi]], the eldest of the [[affiliation-sewilatribe|Sewila Tribe]] who knows the whole of its manners, and the one who reckons whom the spirit answers at the night-sitting. He is not a priest. He has no helpers, no house apart, no share beyond anyone else's and no voice in the tribe beyond an elder's, and he teaches the manners to any member who asks.

## Background

**The manners.** The court's manners are long, exact and its own, and he is the only one living who knows them all. The sick of other tribes are laid at the pool and he asks each what they did to offend.

**The succession.** When he dies, the next eldest who knows the whole manners keeps the court. No one does, and he has begun to teach three.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is gentle, dry and exacting, and he will repeat a rule until the learner can say it without his help.

### Motivation

He wants the court's manners kept whole after him and the court kept from any one person's hand, and he does not want the tribe to ask a stranger the way to the pool.

### Strengths

He knows every manner of the court, and the tribe trusts his reckoning of the night-sitting because he has never claimed more than an elder's voice.

### Weaknesses

He is old and ill, and his choice of whom to teach is itself a form of power that he knows he must not hold.

## Social

## Plot Hooks

1. **Three pupils.** He asks a party to take a pupil of his on a journey of a few days and say afterward whether the pupil kept the manners among strangers.
2. **What the stranger did.** A sick trader lies at the pool, and Mbeko wants a party to ask the trader's companions what the man did before he came.
3. **The wrong river.** He has named the wrong river to a salvager and wants a party to learn whether the lie held.
