# Appearance {#appearance}

Tsamalkalu is a 57-year-old woman who stands 5'2" tall with a medium build. She has brown skin, ruddy complexion, black hair, and dark brown eyes.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Tsamalkalu, Counts the Salmon, is the Keeper of the Count (_kalumis_) of the [[affiliation-bearhouse|Bear House]] at [[place-lamekis|Bear Beach]], and the mother's sister of its chief, [[being-yaldukita|Yaldukita]]. She has kept the house's count for thirty years and has never been wrong in public. At every feast of the Bear House she stands behind her sister's son and says aloud what each guest is given, and every house on the coast that has taken a strand of the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} from her hand owes the Bear House a greater gift.

She is a small, dry woman with a voice that carries the length of the great house without rising. The Cedar People's other Keepers treat her as their equal, which the coast's chiefs do not always do for one another. The Keeper of the Orca House, [[being-kalunukwas|Kalunukwas]], has said that she will count the Strand as whatever is given at the feast. Tsamalkalu has said nothing at all about where the beads come from.

:::secret {title="The Strand buys less"}

Tsamalkalu knows what Yaldukita has not admitted. There are more beads on the Shell-Bead coast every year, and each strand buys less: the grease, blankets and copper a strand fetched when the Strand began now take a strand and a half. The Bear House's feasts count the beads at the old worth, but the debts those feasts create come back to Lamekis as gifts it must answer in grease and copper, at the new worth. The house is already further in debt than the coast knows. Yaldukita means to cover it with more captives, and has told his war canoe captains that after this winter's feast they may raid the Shell-Bead coast towns themselves. Tsamalkalu has not decided whether to stop him at the count, as the first Kalunukwas stopped the killing.

A stranger who can tell her what a strand of beads buys on the Shell-Bead coast this year has something she wants.

What she wants is a count she can say aloud at the feast without lying, and a house still standing after she has said it.

:::

A party that trades in grease, copper or blankets at Lamekis deals with her in the end, because every gift the house receives passes through her memory. She pays fairly and remembers everything.

What she wants is every gift counted right, at every feast, as she has counted them for thirty years.
