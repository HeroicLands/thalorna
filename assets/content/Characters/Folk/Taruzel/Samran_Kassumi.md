# Appearance {#appearance}

Samran Kassumi is 47 years old, broad through the chest, with closely cut black hair and hazel eyes. Dust settles in the seams of his pale coat because he walks between meetings. He drinks his tea slowly, holding the cup in both hands, and tilts his head toward a speaker whose words he is trying to catch.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Samran lives in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] and serves as First of the Council of [[affiliation-qudumel|Kudumel]]. The council elects him for a fixed term from its Councilors. He presides and holds the city’s seal among merchants and representatives of the hill tribes, whose own peoples name them and whose households may camp outside the walls.

He wants the missing Tribal Councilor found and able to speak before the quarrel over the [[place-caravangate|Caravan Gate]] hardens into a council decision made without him. The Gate’s takings fall while cheap goods fill the bazaars. Samran asks what the goods cost and where they are bought before treating the gap as proof against any one household.

## Background

**The market.** Bargaining is familiar work to him, including the patient explanations needed when a buyer and seller understand different things by the same offer. He asks an interpreter to repeat the terms back before the parties clasp hands.

**The two seats.** Merchants and Tribal Councilors bring different obligations into the chamber. The tribes keep their own law on the hill roads; Samran’s seal does not turn a request to their representatives into an order to their guides.

**The city’s year.** [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]] holds the Confederation’s Arch-Consulship this year. Samran keeps the local council’s business moving while Danashu resides at Besikar, and neither man’s office supplies the other’s votes.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is curious about a stranger’s work and blunt about what he does not understand. He dislikes an answer given solely to end a conversation.

### Motivation

He wants the city to hear the people its trade depends on, even when their answer delays a profitable decision.

### Strengths

He can reduce a disputed bargain to the points that need agreement. He notices when a speaker answers a different question from the one the council asks.

### Weaknesses

He spends too long trying to turn a refusal into an offer. Some disputes concern a promise or an insult that a better price cannot settle.

## Social

[[being-llmnblnn|Ellamuna Balinuni]] wants the strait’s trade at the Gate, while the Saliruni guides refuse her house’s trains over the hill roads. Samran can ask both sides to appear; he cannot settle the house’s unpaid wartime promise for them. [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]]’s request to register the [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]]’ Flame-Readers remains before the council after two deferrals, adding another claim to its crowded business.

### Affiliations

- **Kudumel**: a Councilor elected First for a fixed term.

## Plot Hooks

1. **An empty place.** Samran asks a party to speak with the missing councilor’s household at the west gate and seek news along the roads they name. He asks them to bring an answer freely given, not a representative under arrest.
2. **The price at the stall.** A merchant offers examples of goods sold below the cost of the Gate’s dues. Samran pays the party to follow an actual sale and identify its sellers before the council hears another accusation without witnesses.
3. **Both accounts aloud.** The council needs the Order’s registration request and the Flame-Readers’ answer heard in the same session. He asks a party acceptable to both to arrange their attendance, with no promise about how the council votes.
