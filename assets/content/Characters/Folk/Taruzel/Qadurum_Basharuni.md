# Appearance {#appearance}

Qadurum Basharuni is 60 years old. He has a broad face, close-cut gray hair, brown eyes and a stiff left knee that makes stairs slow work. He wears dark cloth that shows the quarry dust on his cuffs. At the board’s table he turns a petition toward the light before reading it, and asks for the clerk when the handwriting defeats him.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Qadurum is First of the [[affiliation-gldbrdshrmn|Guild Board of Sharimun]], in the second year of his three-year term. His house cuts the largest quarry terrace above [[place-sharimun|Sharimun]]. The quarry seats vote with him, giving him reliable support within the seven-member board.

He wants the town’s quays, lodgings and workshops to profit from the Arbitrators’ sessions without a local quarrel driving merchants away. His board licenses the town’s trades, sets harbor dues and pays for the beacon. Its magistrates judge the town’s cases. The [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation]]’s Arbitrators across the square answer to the [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]] alone; his seal grants him no say in their court.

## Background

**The terrace.** His house’s working ground is the largest cut on the hill. He knows the distance from a block on the terrace to a load at the quarry quay, and the wages and teams required between them.

**The guild hall.** Quarry interests hold two of the board’s seats. He relies on that support while making bargains with the harbor, chandlers, teamsters, glassworkers and gladiators represented around the table.

**The present term.** The board’s magistrates send a young furnace-hand to the arena over a theft his mother denies. [[being-nbrnfsnn|Niburan Fusanuni]], the free arena champion, seeks the truth about the accusation that put him there as a convict. Both inquiries bring petitioners to the guild hall.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is practical, blunt about costs and generous with an explanation when the listener accepts his premises. He becomes terse when asked who benefits from a sentence.

### Motivation

He wants the quarry houses to keep their influence and the town’s business to continue through the court’s season.

### Strengths

He can mobilize suppliers, teams and votes. He understands what a delayed cargo costs and which local office can actually remedy it.

### Weaknesses

He treats the board’s customary arrangements as sufficient justification. Evidence from a laborer carries less weight with him than a master’s assurance.

## Social

[Belgina Sinnuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the harbor member and Keeper of the Beacon, is often outvoted. Her nightly work on the headland keeps ships arriving regardless of the board’s quarrels. Qadurum needs that work while resisting the claims she brings with it.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-gldbrdshrmn|Guild Board of Sharimun]]**: a Member of the Board from the quarry masters and its elected First.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The furnace-hand.** His mother asks for the sentence to be examined before the [Gladiators’ Guild of Sharimun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} collects him. Qadurum asks a party for the accuser’s account in writing, to compare with the mother’s petition.
2. **The quarry load.** A merchant awaiting stone also needs to attend the Arbitrators. He wants a party to establish where the load is held without pretending his board can change the court’s calendar.
3. **The champion’s witness.** Niburan seeks the man behind his old accusation. Qadurum offers access to the town clerk’s public records while insisting the inquiry remain a local matter.

:::secret {title="What the quarry seats know"}
The Gladiators’ Guild pays the magistrate’s clerk for every convict sent to the arena, and the quarry masters on the board know the arrangement. Qadurum belongs to that group. A laborer who argues over wages is particularly likely to face an accusation; the furnace-hand’s accuser owes him three months’ pay. Qadurum’s wish for orderly business includes keeping this bargain from a public hearing. A party with the clerk’s account can make his support for a local inquiry expensive.
:::
