# Appearance {#appearance}

Ulamar Gazzuni is 61 years old, a lean man with silver hair, brown eyes and a deep crease between his eyebrows. He walks with short, quick steps and stops completely to greet someone. His coats are finely made and usually buttoned unevenly; he straightens them before a public reading, checking the fastening by touch.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Ulamar lives in [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] and is First of the Council of [[affiliation-besikar|Besikar]]. The [Council of Elders]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} elects him from its members for a fixed term, entrusting him with the city’s seal. He chairs local business among Elders seated by the property-holders’ votes, under the weight of the three great guilds.

He wants the dispute around the [[place-tamzirmonument|Tamzir Monument]] kept from turning into a fight on the quays. The Harbormaster wants the veterans’ night watch gone before foreign hulls arrive; the veterans want to know who threatens the ship. Ulamar needs both to describe what happens at the dock before he puts a proposal to the Elders.

## Background

**The local seal.** His desk concerns the city’s charters, revenue and harbor through their separate officers. The [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]] meets nearby, but Confederation business belongs to that body and its Arch-Consul, [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]].

**The harbor morning.** At [[lore-harbordue|The Harbor Due]], Ulamar walks the breakwater without his seal, reads the old roll and splits the repair tally between the Treasurer and the sea. He practices the reading aloud until he can give it clearly over the wind.

**The petitioners.** The closing of the electoral Roll leaves the veterans without votes. Their halls still organize neighbors, funerals and protests. Ulamar hears requests from the waterfront that the Elders have little electoral reason to answer.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is brisk about routine business and awkward with thanks. A direct request usually receives a direct answer; a flattering introduction makes him suspicious of the price at its end.

### Motivation

He wants a council decision to carry enough trust that it can be carried out without a militia line behind it.

### Strengths

He separates the officers’ responsibilities and finds the person who can give a useful answer. He is willing to hear a complaint outside the council-house.

### Weaknesses

He treats a carefully arranged meeting as progress even when neither side changes its position. The great guilds can offer him orderly proceedings while keeping the substance out of reach.

## Social

[[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]] carries the veterans’ petition to the Arch-Consul and preaches patience in the halls. Ulamar seeks his account of the waterfront’s temper without claiming that Zilmar speaks for every veteran. [[being-trmnqzkn|Tirmana Qizikuni]] presses an audit and hereditary seats; her house’s weight makes her proposals difficult for the Elders to put aside.

### Affiliations

- **Besikar**: an Elder elected First of the Council for a fixed term.

## Plot Hooks

1. **Lanterns at the dock.** Ulamar asks a party to spend a night beside the Monument with the veterans’ consent and report any approach to the ship. He needs witnesses both the watch and the Harbormaster can question.
2. **The houses’ answer.** Tirmana seeks support for instructing the city’s representatives on the audit. Ulamar asks a party to carry invitations to Elders who avoid the same table and bring back their stated objections.
3. **A hearing by the water.** Shopkeepers facing the Compact’s debt calls want to speak together. He asks the party to arrange a meeting the petitioners can attend without leaving their stalls unattended all day.
