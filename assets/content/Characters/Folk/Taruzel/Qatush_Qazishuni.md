# Appearance {#appearance}

Qatush Qazishuni is 72 years old, a spare man with sun-darkened skin, brown eyes and white hair worn to his shoulders. His right hand trembles when it rests, but closes firmly around his walking staff. He wears a goat-hair cloak over patched wool. When reciting, he looks across the listeners rather than down at the fire.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Qatush is an elder of the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]] and Lawspeaker of the [[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]]. His winter home is among the Qazishuni camps in [[place-ralumar|Ralumar]], where the council sits each autumn. He travels with the camps of [[place-tulurel|Tulurel]] on Qalirel’s spine through the grazing season. The whole sitting chooses the Lawspeaker for life.

He holds the law by heart: blood-price, water, hospitality and passage between tribes. He presides and gives speakers leave to address the court. A decision requires the spoken assent of the elders of all three tribes. He cannot make a disputed price binding merely by pronouncing it, and he is no chief over the Qazishuni’s other camp elders.

He wants the coast to answer the obligations it accepts from the hills. The unpaid [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] toll share returns at every sitting, while a gate the tribes do not build takes money in their name on the pass road.

## Background

**The forts.** As a young man he serves on imperial pay in the forts of [[lore-easternline|The Eastern Line]]. He learns what a coastal order asks of the people who must keep a road through the hills.

**The war.** He carries a spear in the independence war. Its promises remain matters for the living elders, not old bargains he considers discharged by victory.

**The sitting.** He opens each council by reciting the law, a task that takes most of the morning. The elders carry decisions home in memory and repeat them to their camps; no written roll replaces that responsibility.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is deliberate, hospitable within the guest’s obligations and sharp with someone who interrupts a recitation to improve its wording.

### Motivation

He wants the hills’ remembered agreements to remain enforceable when the coast offers a different account on paper.

### Strengths

He remembers the terms people accept and keeps separate a tribe’s grievance, a camp’s quarrel and a matter requiring all three tribes.

### Weaknesses

He depends on coastal messengers to carry an oral decision honestly. A coastal council can hear his words without accepting the elders’ account of the bargain.

## Social

The Keeper of the Doors calls the sitting and keeps its peace. Qatush respects that separate duty. The tribes’ councilors at Kudumel carry the hills’ word to the one coastal government that gives them seats; the Lawspeaker’s authority does not extend to voting in their place.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]]**: an Elder and its Lawspeaker.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The false toll.** A caravan blames the tribes for the gate below the third fort. Qatush asks a party to identify its keepers and carry their claimed authority back to the elders.
2. **The price of a copy.** [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] copyists seek the lintels of Ralumar without paying the council’s price. He asks a party to convey the terms plainly and return with an answer the sitting can hear.
3. **The coastal hearing.** The hill councilors need a sponsor for their grievance in the [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]]. He entrusts a party with the spoken terms, to repeat before the councilors and a willing sponsor together.

:::secret {title="The words over his head"}
Qatush knows the witness clause as “the coast pays at the Doors” and recites it each year. He cannot read the lintels and does not know that the same obligation is cut into them. The stones name a witness’s share of one part in fifty and omit the guild-wardens whom the Vezakuni place in their restored tradition.

A copyist’s rubbings of two lintels are locked in the Vezakar at Besikar. A house that reads those copies sends the factors offering to buy the stones. A complete reading brought to Qatush can join the hills’ oral claim to written evidence; it does not give him power to decide the council’s response alone.
:::
