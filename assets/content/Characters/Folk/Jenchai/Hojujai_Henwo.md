# Appearance {#appearance}

Hojujai Henwo was a 46-year-old man when he died, who stood six feet tall and was of heavy build. He had tawny, weathered skin crossed by old scars, a broad face, black hair in a topknot and dark brown eyes under heavy brows. He wore the red cord of the Teng Ja at his sword's hilt, and the retainers of Yanwu describe the weight of his step on the castle's boards.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Hojujai Henwo (Tiger Warrior) was the Dauzhwo of the [[affiliation-hojujaidmn|Hojujai Domain]] and the last Teng Ja, Heaven's Blade. The Emperor conferred the office on him in 693 AF, and for sixteen years he was the lord the other lords obeyed. He spoke for the Emperor to the houses, kept the peace between them by force and forwarded the rice of the court estates to the court that depended on him. He died in 709 AF, and the office, which lapses with its holder, has stood vacant since.

He left a widow, [[being-reweihojujai|Hojujai Rewei]], and a son of three, [[being-zijahojujai|Hojujai Zija]].

## Background

**The formation.** He was born at [[place-yanwu|Yanwu]], heir of the burning mountain. The retainers of the house taught him the sword, and the shrine's Tanchei taught him the mountain's signs.

**The conferral.** He held Hojai by patent when the Emperor conferred the office of Teng Ja on him in 693 AF, at thirty. The retainers of Yanwu became the household of the Teng Ja, and the Nine had peace between the houses for as long as he held the office.

**The marriage.** He married [[being-reweihojujai|Rewei]], eighteen years his junior, and their son Zija was born in 705 AF, when Henwo was forty-two.

**The death.** He died in 709 AF, leaving the court to decide whether to confer the office on a regency. Shuiwojai and Wojai pressed it not to, and the War of the Empty Seat followed. His house keeps the mountain and the claim to the office.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The promise of the Teng Ja.** A Shwizhwo of Wojai shows a party a promise Henwo gave him in his tenure, a village in return for service, and the claim is good only if the house acknowledges its dead lord's word. Rewei would sooner not.
2. **The red cord.** A cord of the Teng Ja's red has been offered for sale in a market of the Strait, and the household at Yanwu wants to know who held it and how it left the mountain.
3. **The unsigned offering.** Each year on the day of his death an offering reaches Henwo's shrine in Yanwu from the Strait, and no one has signed for it. The household asks a party to learn who sends it and what the sender means.
