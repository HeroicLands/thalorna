# Appearance {#appearance}

A garrison captain of Muwojai wears the house badge on a lacquered cuirass and keeps a house blade at his belt. He meets travelers at the gate of his post on the road over [[place-zijai|Zijai]], reads every pass slowly and gives it back with both hands.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The garrisons of the [[affiliation-muwojaidmn|Muwojai Domain]] hold the passes on either side of [[place-zujufrry|The Zuju Ferry]], on the roads that every envoy and petitioner takes to the Emperor. Each is held by a retainer of the house. [[being-chaiwomuwoj|Muwojai Chaiwo]] has no grown heir, and three of these captains are named as possible successors. Whoever succeeds him holds the gate to the capital.

## Background

A captain comes from a retainer family that has served the house for generations, holds a granted name and a stipend counted in bales, and commands the Tsaihwo soldiers posted from the barracks at [[place-kowu|Kowu]]. The three named as possible successors have each received an approach from a different bloc. An approach is no offense, and a captain asked about one says so.

## Psyche

### Personality

Polite, watchful and careful with words. A captain answers a question about his loyalty with a statement of his duty.

### Motivation

Hold the pass, keep his standing in the hall, and stand nearest the seat when the lord dies.

### Strengths

He holds a gate that every traveler to the capital must pass, and the men at it answer to him.

### Weaknesses

Every bloc wants the man who holds the gate to the capital, and a captain's answer to one of them is watched by the other two and by his lord.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

He serves [[being-chaiwomuwoj|Muwojai Chaiwo]] and the Muwojai Domain, and the soldiers of his post answer to him.

2. A party asks a captain whether a bloc has approached him, and he says that one has and states his duty in the same breath.

:::secret {title="The captain who has accepted"}
One of the three captains has accepted his bloc's offer, and the referee chooses which. He does not say it, and he shows it in his garrison before he shows it in his speech: his men's arrears are paid in new silver that no stipend from Kowu covers, they wear new winter coats, and he sends a runner down the road to the house that bought him whenever someone of note passes his gate. That bloc pays him for word of who passes. The two who refused have nothing new at their gates.
