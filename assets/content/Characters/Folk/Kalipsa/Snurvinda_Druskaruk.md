# Appearance {#appearance}

Snurvinda Druskaruk is a 63-year-old woman who stands five feet four inches tall and is of medium build. Her fair skin is weathered and lined by the wind on the headland, her silver hair is braided and pinned flat, and her green eyes are narrowed from a lifetime of looking into lamplight and darkness by turns. Her fingertips are burned smooth from trimming wicks, and she carries a brass keeper's horn on a cord at her belt. An old burn scars the back of her left hand.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Age 63. Keeper of the Lantern of Kalipsa. Wants the light to burn as it always has, clear and yellow, whatever the oil costs.**

Snurvinda Druskaruk keeps [[place-lantrnklps|the Lantern of Kalipsa]], the seamark tower on the northern headland above the harbor of [[place-calypsacity|Kalipsa]]. The keeping is hereditary in her family, [Druskaruk]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, and she took it from her father twenty years ago. She lives in the two lower stories of the tower with her two sons, who share the night's trimming with her, and a granddaughter who is learning the lamps. She walks behind the Harbormaster at the spring sitting, and her word on what the light showed on a given night is taken at [[place-sealawhall|the Sea-Law Hall]] without a second witness.

The oil is her quarrel. Olive oil from the south has risen two years running, the treasurers of the [[affiliation-captnscncl|Captains' Council]] want the cost cut, and the elder of [Glervhavan]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} on [[place-calypsashore|the South Shore]] has offered the city fish and seal oil at a third of the price. Snurvinda has refused it. A fish-oil flame burns red, and she holds that a master who has steered by the clear yellow light all his life might take a red one for a ship on fire, or for another headland, and put his hull on [the Hounds]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. [[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]] has kept the olive oil coming out of the harbor revenue.

## Psyche

### Personality

Snurvinda is blunt, tireless and suspicious of anyone who has not climbed the headland path in the rain. She speaks little to strangers and a great deal to her granddaughter, and she has no patience with treasurers.

### Motivation

She wants to pass the light to her granddaughter as her father passed it to her, burning the same oil behind the same mirror. She believes the light is the city's oldest promise, and that a promise changed to save money is broken.

### Strengths

She knows every night the light has burned in twenty years and what the weather did on it. Her word at the Sea-Law Hall needs no second witness, and every master on the Run knows her horn.

### Weaknesses

She will not consider any change at all, and she has made enemies on the Council's treasury who would like a keeper less certain of herself. The oil store in the tower holds a month's oil, and the next cargo is overdue.

## Social

### Companions

Her two sons and her granddaughter, and the pilots who row past the headland at dawn and wave up at the tower.

### Patrons

[[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]], who pays for the oil out of the harbor revenue against the treasurers' wishes.

### Enemies

[[being-thruldsklf|Thruldmil Skulfendik]], the Glervhavan elder who offered the cheaper oil, and the treasurers who took his offer to the Council.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-calypsa|Kalipsa]]**: She is a citizen and the hereditary keeper of the Lantern.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The oil cart.** The next oil cargo from the south is overdue, and Snurvinda wants to know whether it was delayed at sea or bought up on the quay before it reached her.
2. **The trial lamp.** The Glervhavan elder wants a cask of his oil burned beside the olive oil for one night, before witnesses from the Council, and Snurvinda will not have it in the lantern room. Both want a stranger to carry the cask, and to say what the night showed.
3. **The night of the fog.** The Norodari want Snurvinda's word on whether the light was seen from the eastern approaches on the nights their hulls were lost. She has not been asked at the hall, and she will speak only there.
