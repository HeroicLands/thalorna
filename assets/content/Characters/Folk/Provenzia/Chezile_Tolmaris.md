# Appearance {#appearance}

Chezile Tolmaris is a 46-year-old man who stands 5'8" tall and is of light build. He has a long, mobile face that he can fold into anyone he has met, quick brown eyes and gray hair worn long and pushed back with both hands while he talks. The fingers of his right hand are stained with ink that no amount of washing removes. He wears the gold mask-pin of the First Speaker on a coat that is better than it looks.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Age 46. First Speaker of the Theater Collective. Wants the stage free, the Collective's word kept, and his own plays the ones the city quotes.**

Chezile Tolmaris is a playwright of [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] and the First Speaker of [[affiliation-prvnzthtrclctv|the Provénzia Theater Collective]]. He presides over the Convocation of Companies, which meets every two months in the Jalzemis's theater, and decides which plays come before it for endorsement. He has been elected four years running.

He writes political comedy, and he writes it well. His plays of the Council's long maneuver with the crown are the ones the capital quotes at table, and two of them were endorsed over the protests of the men they mocked. He is also an operator: he knows which patron funds which company, which Peer is negotiating which marriage and which target can be cut this season without the Collective bleeding for it. The Collective's doctrine of the prudence of the season is, in practice, his judgment.

This season his judgment is being tested from every side. A company has brought the Convocation a satire of the Pirnebris merchants, another a comedy of a magnate who keeps two households, and the Council of Peers is too divided to protect anyone who angers half of it.

## Psyche

### Personality

Chezile is quick, funny and restless, and he talks in the rhythms of his own plays. He is vain about his work and careless of his dignity, and he cannot resist a line even when he knows it will cost him.

### Motivation

He wants the stage to go on doing what Provénzian theater does: cutting the powerful and surviving it. He also wants to be remembered as the best playwright of his generation, and the two wants do not always agree.

### Strengths

He knows the capital's patrons, players and politics better than anyone in the Collective, and he can make a room laugh at a man who is sitting in it.

### Weaknesses

He has made enemies with every endorsed play, and he cannot stop writing new ones.

## Social

## Plot Hooks

1. **Two plays, one patron.** The comedy of the magnate with two households may mean the Count of Ravenshold, who pays for Chezile's company. Chezile wants a party to learn who wrote it, and for whom, before he must put it to the Convocation.
2. **The endorsed play that went too far.** A comedy the Convocation endorsed has grown sharper in its second week, as Provénzian comedies do, and its target has sent signed letters demanding it close. Chezile wants the players kept safe until the Convocation decides whether its endorsement stands.
3. **The foreign commission.** A foreign patron has paid in advance for a play that argues one side of the succession, which the Charter never settled. The fee is large and the interference is plain, and Chezile wants to know who the patron really serves before he decides whether to take it.
