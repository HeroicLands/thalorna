# Appearance {#appearance}

Bulachsovid Yovakur is a 44-year-old man who stands 6'2" tall and is of heavy build. He has tawny skin with a sun-kissed complexion, black hair oiled flat in the townsman's way, a soft, beardless jaw and green eyes that people remember. He dresses like a clerk of the held city rather than a man of the steppe stock he comes from: a long cotton coat, a sash of Atarzád weave and slippers, with ink on the cuffs. He carries a tally-stick and a wax tablet everywhere and makes a mark on one or the other whenever someone tells him a number. He is polite to everyone and smiles a great deal.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Bulachsovid Yovakur is the headman of a farming town of a few hundred households in the oasis country of the [[place-oasishirvn|Oasis of Shirvan]], a town of steppe families settled on the land two and three generations ago, who farm the edge of the irrigated ground on terms from an [Ozhmarod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} landholder of the city. He keeps the town's dues for the landholder, the tally of its fields and its share of the canal water, and the town calls him the Administrator, a word he chose himself.

He is ambitious, capable and resented. The town runs better since he came to the office, the dues are paid on time, and the landholder's steward has stopped sending men to count the sheaves. The town respects [[being-bazdrshhrn|Yovaqbeyuk Qibudur]], the animal trainer, more than it respects its headman, and Bulachsovid knows it.

## Background

**The family.** Bulachsovid was born in the town to a family of steppe stock that keeps the tribe-name Yovakur, the _dog people_, though no tribe of that name rides now. His grandfather came in from the steppe in a drought and stayed. His own name, _dust-rider_, is a steppe name, and he has never ridden farther than the city.

**The clerk.** He learned to read and reckon from a Tellumi scribe of the lower town of Shirvan in return for carrying water, and he kept the landholder's tallies for twelve years before the steward made him headman. He is good at it.

**The present.** A faction of wealthy merchants wants the town's animals bred and trained in numbers, for the caravans, and has asked Bulachsovid for rules that would let them do it. He has been quietly drafting regulations that would force Yovaqbeyuk to train by the merchants' methods or give up his place.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is diligent, ingratiating and thin-skinned. He loves order, numbers and a dispute settled by a written rule, and he distrusts any authority he cannot write down. He is not cruel, and he does not understand why people dislike him.

### Motivation

He wants the town to prosper, and he wants to be thanked for it. He believes the old steppe ways that the town keeps, the horse-lore, the oaths sworn outdoors and the respect for men who talk to animals, keep it poor, and he wants it to be a town of the held city and not a camp that stopped moving.

### Strengths

He can read, write and reckon in two tongues, the landholder's steward trusts him, and he knows the water-share of every field in the town. The merchants pay him well and listen to him.

### Weaknesses

He is jealous of Yovaqbeyuk, who commands more real respect than he does despite having no office at all, and the jealousy shows. He has no friends in the town, only people who owe him.

## Social

### Enemies

- [[being-bazdrshhrn|Yovaqbeyuk Qibudur]]: The animal trainer the town loves. Bulachsovid views his traditional methods as an obstacle to progress, and he resents him personally.

### Others

- The landholder's steward, an Ozhmarod clerk of the city, who appointed him and judges him by the dues.
- The merchants who want the town's animals bred for the caravans, who pay him and expect rules in return.

### Affiliations

- He holds no standing in any polity. He is a tenant of the Ozhmarod, a headman by the steward's appointment, and of no tribe.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The regulation.** Bulachsovid publishes a rule that every animal trained in the town must be trained to a tally of days. Yovaqbeyuk refuses to keep a tally, and the younger farmers take sides. A party in the town is asked by both men to say which of them is right.
2. **The missing dues.** A season's dues are short, and the steward is coming. Bulachsovid wants a party to find out which household did not pay before the steward finds out that the headman cannot say.
3. **The merchants' herd.** The merchants have bought a string of half-wild horses for Bulachsovid to have trained by their methods. Their trainer is hurt on the first morning, and nobody in the town but Yovaqbeyuk can go near them.

:::secret {title="The second tally"}
The town's dues are not short. Bulachsovid keeps two tallies, the one he shows the steward and the true one, and the difference is the town's: a share of every harvest kept back and stored in the old granary pit under his house against a drought. He learned the practice from the Tellumi scribe who taught him to reckon, who kept two ledgers for the water-masters of Shirvan, and he has never told anyone, because a headman who cheats an Ozhmarod landholder is a headman the fire-wardens hang.

The town does not know. It thinks him the landholder's man, and he has let it, because the store only works if nobody talks. Yovaqbeyuk has seen the pit, because one of his dogs found it, and has said nothing. Bulachsovid does not know whether that silence is a kindness or a hold over him, and the regulations are partly an attempt to find out.

**Hook: the dog.** The steward is coming to count, and a dog has dug at the corner of the headman's house. Bulachsovid wants a party to move the store in a night, and Yovaqbeyuk is the only man in the town whose dogs will not bark at them.
:::
