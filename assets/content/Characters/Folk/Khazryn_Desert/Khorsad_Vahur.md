# Appearance {#appearance}

Khorsád-Vahur is a 46-year-old man who stands 5'7" tall and is of medium build, with the broad shoulders of a man who dug channels for twenty years. He has tanned skin, leathery at the neck and hands, dark brown hair cut short, and hazel eyes that move to his steward before he answers a question. A shovel-callus runs across his right palm. He dresses in the red of the house of the setting sun on feast days and in a settler's undyed cotton the rest of the time, and he wears no ring. He is courteous in the careful way of a man who has learned that anything he says is carried to Amradad.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Khorsád-Vahur is the head of the [[affiliation-hsevahurdash|House of Vahurdash]], revived with the settlement at [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]] on the dry northern rim of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], beside the stones of [[place-velkunar|Veshkun]], Old Vahurdash. He holds the title by the acclamation of the settlers and not by ancient right, and he sits at the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]] as a kind of seventh among equals. The town numbers about four thousand, drinks from two old wells and irrigates from a single channel cut across the sand from a spring higher up the rim. In a dry year the channel stops, and the town measures its harvest by how many days the water ran.

He receives an outsider only with his steward beside him. The house keeps a token guard, and when it needs a dusk watch it hires hard men at a daily wage in barter-weights of dates and iron. The [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] of the central belt tolerate the settlement because it costs them nothing, pays a quiet tribute in dates, and holds the poorest ground on the rim.

## Background

**The settler.** Khorsád-Vahur was born in the Tellumi quarter of [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]] to a family of channel-diggers, and came north to the rim at twenty with the first settlers. He dug the channel. He is called after the god [[lore-khorsadradty|Khórsadra]] and the city, a name the settlers gave him in the third year, when he found the spring that feeds the channel by following a line of green the herders' goats had grazed.

**The acclamation.** When the settlement was large enough to need a voice at the Council, the households met at the channel's head and acclaimed him, because he had kept the water running through two dry years. He went to Amradad for his first session in a borrowed coat.

**The house.** He married a woman of the free oasis of [[place-mehrnagord|Mernagord]] and has three children. His steward is his wife's brother, and the two of them run the house between them.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is careful, practical and slow to anger, and he is uneasy in his title. He prefers the channel to the Council and says so at home. He is shrewder than the exile courts think, and he lets them think it.

### Motivation

He wants the water to keep running and the title to hold, in that order. He wants proof that the house is the house of Vahurdash, because without it his children inherit an argument instead of a seat.

### Strengths

The settlers trust him, the free houses find him useful, and every exile court tells its children that Vahurdash is the one Tellumi city ever recovered. He knows the rim's water better than anyone living.

### Weaknesses

His title rests on acclamation, which the old houses do not accept. His town has one channel, and a spring that weakens leaves it weeks to find another. The herders of the uplands own the small oases north of the fields, and a settler who waters a goat at the wrong spring meets their owners.

## Social

### Enemies

- [[being-nartemayusht|Nartemai of Ushtra-ber]]: The most exacting of those who say an acclamation cannot make a prince. He does not stand when Khorsád-Vahur enters the Council's hall, and Khorsád-Vahur has stopped expecting him to.

### Others

- [[being-hemvanaymern|Hemvanai of Mernagord]]: The prince of the largest free oasis, his wife's prince and the one prince at the Council who treats him as an equal. They talk about water.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-hsevahurdash|House of Vahurdash]]**: head of the house by acclamation, who also keeps its channel in practice if not in office.
- **[[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]]**: a seated prince, seventh among equals.
- **[[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]]**: prince of a house.
- **[[affiliation-zurathra|Faith of Zúrathra]]**: one of the faithful, who keeps a shrine to the Mother of the Oasis at the channel's head.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The title.** The house pays a party to find the old archive's tablets in the stones of Veshkun and bring them to Khorsád-Vahur unbroken. A rival house in exile pays the same party to leave them lost.
2. **The spring.** If the spring on the rim weakens, Khorsád-Vahur needs a water-diviner, or a map of the old channels, within weeks, and the herders who own the oases to the north want a price for every day the town's goats drink there.
3. **The dusk watch.** The house is hiring hard men for the dusk watch again, and it does not say what the watch is for until the first night.

:::secret {title="The town is failing"}
The spring is failing, and has been for three years. The channel ran ninety-one days last year and sixty the year before that, and the town has kept the numbers from the Council, from the exile courts and from the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]]. Khorsád-Vahur knows that a city recovered and lost again is worse for every exile court than a city never recovered, and that a rival house in Amradad would pay a great deal to learn it.

He wants the archive's tablets for the title, and he wants them more for the channels: the old city's water-rolls would show where its own channels ran before the drought emptied it. He does not know what the genealogies in the archive will show about his house, and he has decided not to care until the water is found.

**Hook: the old channels.** Khorsád-Vahur asks a party to bring him the water-rolls from the archive and nothing else, and to tell no one at the Council what the rolls are for.
:::
