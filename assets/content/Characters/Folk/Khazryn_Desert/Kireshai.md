# Appearance {#appearance}

Kireshai is a 44-year-old man who stands 6'1" tall and is of light build, long-armed and spare. He has tanned skin with a smooth complexion, hair gone gray early and cropped to the skull, a clean-shaven face and brown eyes that do not blink much. He wears the quarter watch's brown coat with the blue cord of a captain, clean and pressed, and carries the watch's iron-shod quarterstaff, since the Tellumi of the held cities bear no blade outside the militias the Atarzád permit and the watch bears none at all. He speaks in short sentences and does not raise his voice.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Kireshai is the captain of the City Guard of the Tellumi quarter of [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], the watch of a hundred men the [Tozhirod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} magistrate of the city permits the quarter to keep. The watch keeps the quarter's own peace: its markets, its night streets and its gates into the Atarzád city. It carries staves and no blades, it answers to the quarter's elders and to the magistrate both, and its captain is the man the magistrate summons when something in the quarter has gone wrong.

He has made begging and vagrancy a personal crusade. A beggar in the quarter is a beggar the Atarzád fire-wardens see, and a quarter the fire-wardens see too often is a quarter they enter. Kireshai drives the beggars, the drunk and the vagrant out of the streets every night, and the poet [[being-kamdkhvndr|Kamid Khavandar]] in particular, whom he considers a troublemaker to be driven out or locked up.

## Background

**The name.** Kireshai was named for the rider of the House of Zargandur who held the left of the line on the last morning of [[place-zargandur|Zargandur]], as the exile house recites it. His father was a water-carrier who wanted a son who would not give the ground.

**The watch.** He joined the watch at seventeen and was a sergeant at twenty-five. During the last Tellumi rising, in the Shirvan district fourteen years ago, the young men of Kethramír's quarter wanted to rise too, and Kireshai held the watch on the quarter's gates for nine nights and kept them in. Nobody in the quarter died that season, and nobody in the quarter has forgiven him.

**The captain.** He was made captain at thirty-two. The magistrate trusts him, the elders need him, and the quarter calls him the magistrate's dog behind his back.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is rigid, honest and humorless, and he is not corrupt. He believes the quarter survives by giving the Atarzád no reason to look at it, and that every disorder he stops is a raid that does not happen. He has no patience with poets.

### Motivation

He wants the quarter quiet, because a quiet quarter is a quarter nobody burns. He wants to be thanked for it and has stopped expecting to be.

### Strengths

He knows every street, roof and cellar of the quarter, the watch obeys him, and the magistrate listens to him. He is good with a staff and better at seeing trouble before it starts.

### Weaknesses

The quarter resents him, the militant young men hate him, and he has no friends among the Atarzád he keeps the peace for. His crusade against the vagrant has made him cruel to people who did nothing but sleep in a doorway.

## Social

### Enemies

- [[being-kamdkhvndr|Kamid Khavandar]]: The vagrant poet, whose verses are recited in the quarter's wineshops and whose crowds the fire-wardens notice. Kireshai has driven him out of the streets four times.

### Others

- [[being-kenbeshay|Kenbeshai]]: The priest of the Northern Temple, who lets the beggars sleep in the back court. Kireshai lets him, because there they are off the streets.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]]**: of the tablets, his household's name kept in the quarter's court. He holds his captaincy from the quarter's elders and the magistrate, not from any house.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The crowd.** Kamid Khavandar has drawn a crowd in the quarter's square with a new poem about the Breaking. Kireshai wants it dispersed before the fire-wardens see it, and he asks a party of strangers to do it, because the crowd will not listen to the watch.
2. **The gate.** Someone has been opening the quarter's east gate into the Atarzád city at night. Kireshai wants to know who before the magistrate asks him.
3. **The young men.** A rumor runs through the quarter that the House of Zargandur is preparing a reclamation. The young men are talking, and Kireshai hires a party to learn which of them have been buying bows.

:::secret {title="The watch's ledger"}
Kireshai reports to the Tozhirod magistrate, as the watch's captain must, and he reports more than the elders know: the names of the young men who talk of rising, the households that keep a lamp for another god, the carriers who bring books in bales of cloth. He has never given the magistrate a name he believed would hang. He gives the small names to keep the large ones safe, and he keeps a ledger of every name he has given, so that if he is asked he can say what he did.

The quarter's militant young men suspect an informer in the watch. They have not considered the captain, because he is too obvious.

**Hook: the ledger.** The ledger is under a loose stone in the watch house. A party hired by the young men to find the informer finds the ledger, reads the small names and the large ones he never gave, and must decide what it is.
:::
