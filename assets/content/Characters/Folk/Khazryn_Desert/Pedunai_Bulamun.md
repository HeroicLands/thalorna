# Appearance {#appearance}

Pedunai Bulamun is a 54-year-old man who stands 6'0" tall and is of heavy build. He has golden skin with a sun-kissed complexion, black hair thinning at the crown and combed carefully across it, a short black beard and green eyes behind a pair of Kaldar reading lenses he wears on a cord. His fingertips are stained green with verdigris that no washing removes. He dresses in a master's long gray gown, very clean, with the University's copper badge pinned high. He is genial, a little pompous and a very good listener, and he takes notes while other people are talking.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Pedunai Bulamun is the favored elder statesman of alchemical research at the [University of Kaldar]{.name data-type="affiliation"} in [[place-caldar|Kaldar]], the copper town of the belt where the faculty works the minerals that the city's green water carries. He holds a master's chair and the confidence of the mineral merchants who fund research in exchange for first option on any transmutation with a commercial use, and the students who want a career study under him.

He made his name by unmaking another's. A longtime colleague and rival of [[being-kayvondzrd|Kaivonad Zarid]], he published a paper showing mathematical errors in Kaivonad's foundational work on copper-to-silver transmutation. Kaivonad answered with accusations of plagiarism, the scandal divided the department and damaged both reputations, and Pedunai recovered first.

## Background

**The student.** Pedunai was born in Kaldar to a Tellumi family of assayers and studied at the University from fourteen. He and Kaivonad were students together under different masters, and for twenty years they shared a laboratory bench, a wineshop and most of their ideas.

**The paper.** He found the errors in Kaivonad's work while repeating his experiments for the merchants, who wanted to know whether the transmutation could be done at scale. It could not, and he said so in print before he said so to Kaivonad.

**The present.** He sits on the faculty's council, advises the merchants and has more students than any other master. The faculty's Atarzád students attend his lectures, and he is careful about what he says in them.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is affable, vain and careful. He likes to be liked, likes to be right more, and has never quite decided which comes first. He is a generous teacher and a cautious colleague.

### Motivation

He wants to be remembered as the master who made Kaldar's alchemy honest. Under that he wants Kaivonad to forgive him, and he would never ask.

### Strengths

He is a fine experimentalist, a lucid writer and a skilled politician of the faculty. The merchants trust him because his results can be repeated.

### Weaknesses

Kaivonad's charge of plagiarism has never been fully answered, because some of the ideas in Pedunai's own work began at their shared bench. He is afraid of the day someone reads both men's notebooks side by side.

## Social

### Enemies

- [[being-kayvondzrd|Kaivonad Zarid]]: His old colleague and rival, who believes Pedunai ruined him out of jealousy. Pedunai believes he ruined him out of honesty, and is not sure.

### Affiliations

- **[[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]]**: of the tablets, his household's name kept by the Tellumi court of Kaldar. He holds his chair from the University, which belongs to nobody.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The repeat.** A merchant wants Kaivonad's newest result repeated before he funds it, and Pedunai is the obvious master to repeat it. He asks a party to carry the request to Kaivonad, who will not open a letter in Pedunai's hand.
2. **The notebooks.** A student has stolen Kaivonad's early notebooks from a lodging-house and offered them to Pedunai. He wants a party to buy them and return them to Kaivonad unread, and to make sure the student has kept no copy.
3. **The water.** The settling-basins are giving a water greener than usual, and the faculty's assays are going wrong. Pedunai wants a party to go up the adits and see what has changed in the copper spur.

:::secret {title="The shared bench"}
Kaivonad's charge is partly true. The method at the heart of Pedunai's best-known paper, the one that made his chair, was Kaivonad's idea, sketched in Kaivonad's hand in a notebook they shared at their bench in their twenties. Pedunai has the notebook. He has kept it for twenty-five years in a locked chest, because burning it would be a confession and keeping it is one too.

The errors he exposed were real, and he would expose them again. The method he took was also real, and he has never said so.

**Hook: the chest.** Pedunai's house has been broken into and the chest opened, though nothing seems to be missing. He hires a party to find the thief, and does not tell them what was in the chest.
:::
