# Appearance {#appearance}

Hemvanai of Mernagord is a 63-year-old man who stands 5'9" tall and is of medium build. He has tanned skin weathered and lined by sixty summers on the edge of the salt pans, white hair, a white beard trimmed square, and brown eyes that look tired until he is interested. His hands are cracked from salt, because he walks the pans with the Warden every spring and puts his hand in the brine to judge it. He dresses in the gray-green of the wind peak's house, good wool, a little out of the Kingdom's fashion. He listens with his head on one side, nods often, and agrees to nothing in the room where it is asked.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Hemvanai of Mernagord is the ruling prince of [[place-mehrnagord|Mernagord]], the largest of the three Tellumi oases still under their own princes, at the western end of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] where the road over [[place-wstrndscnt|the Western Descent]] comes down. His [[affiliation-hsemernagord|house]] governs about thirty thousand people in the city and a hundred and ten thousand more in the gardens, salt-works and villages of the oasis country, with the [Shekhulukhem]{.name data-type="affiliation"} keeping the law beside it. His household guard of a hundred men is the largest of the three free houses' guards, and it could not hold the city for a season.

He keeps Mernagord free by being useful. His salt reaches markets the Atarzád cannot reach, his court is a channel to the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], and the [Ozhmarod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} at [[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]], a few days east, find a free Tellumi city more use to them than a conquered one. Atarzád merchants have sounded him out about a limited normalization, a trade compact between his city and theirs. He has answered neither yes nor no.

## Background

**The prince.** Hemvanai succeeded his father at thirty-one, in a drought year, and spent his first summer buying grain from the Kingdom on the house's credit. The oasis did not starve, and he has governed on the lesson since: a free city is a city that can pay for what it needs.

**The west.** He leads the houses of the west in the oldest argument at the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]], that the first lineages walked down the Western Descent with their herds and their tablets and did not come through the labyrinth of [[place-vurdil|Vurdish]] at all. He says so to the old houses' faces at every session.

**The family.** He has buried a wife and one son. His heir is his daughter, a woman of thirty-five who keeps the house's accounts and disagrees with him about the compact.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is cautious, courteous and slow to decide, and he is not as timid as the exile houses think. He enjoys the company of merchants more than princes, likes a good bargain for its own sake, and laughs at himself before anyone else can.

### Motivation

He wants Mernagord kept free and its salt moving, and he wants it to outlast him. He believes the exile houses are fighting the last war, and that the city that pays its way will still be standing when they are done fighting it.

### Strengths

He rules a real city with real revenues, the Kingdom trusts him, and the Atarzád find him reasonable. He knows the price of salt in every market on the Descent.

### Weaknesses

He is a city of thirty thousand with a hundred guards, a few days' ride from an Atarzád tribe. The exile houses call him insufficiently militant and the oasis is full of people who say the compact is treason, and some of them are in his own household.

## Social

### Enemies

- [[being-gureshayzarg|Gureshai of Zargandur]]: The young heir whose reclamation would bring the war to the one Tellumi city that can least afford it. Hemvanai is fond of him and means to stop him.

### Others

- [[being-mureshashirv|Muresha of Shirvan]]: The First of the Seven, who opposes him on both counts, the compact and the coming down. He thinks her the cleverest person at the Council and the least practical.
- [[being-nimeshayashk|Nimeshai of Ashkarad]]: The merchant prince of the exile houses, who has offered to carry his salt. Hemvanai has not accepted, because a prince who sells through an exile court has chosen a side.
- [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]]: His patron in all but name. The Kingdom's grain and the Kingdom's courtesy keep his city fed and his court heard.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The envoy.** Hemvanai wants an envoy he can trust to carry an offer to Amradad and a reply back, and the exile houses want to be sure he never sends one. A party can carry both messages and be paid by both.
2. **The crust.** The Warden of the Pans says the crust will bear a camel a month early this year. A Kingdom salt buyer wants the first loads and an Atarzád buyer wants them too, and Hemvanai sends a party with the first string to see which buyer is waiting at the far side of the pans.
3. **The lean season.** The Dunhari tribes are coming east over the Descent after a hard winter, in numbers and hungry. Hemvanai wants to know which sheikhs are leading them and what they need before they reach the gardens.

:::secret {title="The compact"}
Hemvanai has said neither yes nor no to the Atarzád merchants because he has said yes to someone else. A rider came to Mernagord in the winter from the man who is gathering the steppe tribes into a union, and offered the city guest-right on the road for its salt caravans, and protection for its gardens, in return for a tithe of salt and an open gate. Hemvanai sent the rider back with a gift and no answer, and the rider has come twice more.

He has worked out what the exile houses have not: that if the union rises, every caravan on the road will pay it, the Atarzád held cities first among them, and a Tellumi city that has already made its peace will be the one free city on the belt. He also knows what an open gate meant to the cities of the steppe's last union. He has not told his daughter, because she would tell the Council.

**Hook: the third rider.** The next time the rider comes, Hemvanai wants a party to follow him back to his master's camp, count what is there and return before the Council's spring session, so that he knows the size of what he is bargaining with.
:::
