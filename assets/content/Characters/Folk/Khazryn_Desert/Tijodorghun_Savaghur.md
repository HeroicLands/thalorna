# Appearance {#appearance}

Tijodorghun Savaghur is a 77-year-old man who stands 5'11" tall and is of light build, thin and upright. He has olive skin browned and deeply lined, white hair worn loose to the shoulders, and brown eyes that look past a speaker to the sky behind him. He goes bareheaded in every weather, as a shaman does. A branching lightning scar runs from his left shoulder down the arm to the wrist, pale against the brown, and he wears his coat with the left sleeve pinned back so it shows. He carries a staff hung with small bells that ring in the wind, and he hums under his breath when he is thinking.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Tijodorghun Savaghur is the eldest shaman of the circle of the [[affiliation-jbzrtrb|Jobizur]], the largest tribe of the central steppe of the [[place-hskrrgn|Ghoshiqor]]. The circle advises the dovaq on the weather, the rites and the moving of the camps, and it can halt a migration for an omen. When the circle reads the sky together, Tijodorghun reads last, and the others wait for him. He keeps the tribe's laying-out ground by the mesa of [Ekon]{.name data-type="place/site"}, where the Jobizur dead lie with their faces to the sky, and he knows whose bones lie where back to his grandfather's time.

He has mentored [[being-rznshmrvrn|Razanash Mervaran]] in the tribe's own ways since her initiation and still guides her, though he sometimes worries that her methods are too unconventional. His name means _star-orghun_, the name his mother gave him on a night of falling stars, and he has always disliked it.

## Background

**The marked child.** Lightning struck the tent Tijodorghun slept in when he was nine and killed his elder brother beside him. He lived, with the scar, and the circle took him as a called child that winter. He went out into the deep desert for the forty days at nineteen and came back.

**The weather-reader.** For fifty years he has read the sky for the dovaq before each move of the camps. He halted the spring migration three times in that span, and two of the three springs the late snow came. The tribe remembers the third, when nothing came and the herds went hungry on the winter grass for a month.

**The present.** He is the last of the circle who remembers the old men who remembered the last orghun, and he tells their stories at the winter fires. He has watched [[being-khilodlunbii|Khilodlun Biilokur]] take in family after family with approval, and he is beginning to wonder where it ends.

## Psyche

### Personality

He is mild, slow and stubborn, and he has a dry sense of humor that the young shamans do not always catch. He prefers the sky to people and children to adults. He is not easily impressed, and he is impressed by Razanash.

### Motivation

He wants the tribe to keep the rites as they were kept, and he wants Razanash ready to lead the circle when he dies. He fears that she will lead it somewhere he cannot follow.

### Strengths

The tribe trusts his weather and his laying-out, and the dovaq does not move a camp without him. He knows the old songs, the old oaths and the sky of the northern grass better than anyone living.

### Weaknesses

He is old, his eyes are failing at night, and the stars he reads by are harder to see each winter. The rival shaman [Kesukyemok]{.name data-type="being/npc"} has begun to say at the fires that the old man's readings come from Razanash.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The halted spring.** Tijodorghun has read the sky and halted the migration. The herds are thin, the elders are angry, and a party camped with the tribe is asked to ride north and see whether the snow is coming.
2. **The old songs.** A scholar of [[place-caldar|Kaldar]] wants the songs of the last orghun written down. Tijodorghun will sing them to a guest who has eaten the tribe's salt, and to nobody else.
3. **The witness.** Khilodlun asks Tijodorghun to witness the Koserur terms under open sky, and he asks a party to stand with him, because he does not trust his failing eyes to read the sky alone while the words are said.

:::secret {title="The third spring"}
On the third spring he halted the migration, nothing came, and Tijodorghun has known since why. He did not read snow in the sky that spring. He read a banner, a line of riders under it, and the Jobizur grass beneath them, and he halted the camps because he did not know what else to do. He told the circle it was snow.

He has seen the banner twice since: once in the year Khilodlun was chosen, and once this winter. He believes it is the sign of the union that a man is gathering on the steppe, and he does not know whether it shows the Jobizur riding under it or ridden over by it. He has not told Khilodlun, because her family's name is the banner people, and he is afraid of what she would make of it.

**Hook: the reading.** Tijodorghun asks Razanash to read the sky with him on the night of the Koserur oath, without telling her what he has seen. A party standing with the shamans that night sees both of them go pale at the same moment.
:::
