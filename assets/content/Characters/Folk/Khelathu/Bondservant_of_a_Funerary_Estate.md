# Appearance {#appearance}

A Bondservant of a Funerary Estate wears a plain linen tunic and leather sandals, carries a water skin or a basket of linen on the hip, and smells of washing-salt.

# Dossier {#dossier}

A funerary estate is the house a great family endows so that its dead are prepared, laid in their own ground and fed for as long as the endowment lasts. Its household of embalmers, bondsmen and offering-priests lives on the estate to do the work. The bondservants work the fields at the canal's edge, carry water, linen and salt for the embalmers, lay out the offering tables and sweep the galleries. Among the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] a bondsman carries no house name and goes by a given name and a village, or a given name and a trade.

## Background

Most bondservants are born to the estate's bondsmen and work where their parents worked. Some are bound to it by a family's debt. They watch the house's dead come up the canal, and they know the house's affairs as servants know them.

## Psyche

### Personality

Quiet before the household's masters and frank among its own.

### Motivation

The bondservant wants the household fed, the work done and kin within reach.

### Strengths

The bondservant knows every passage, store and drain of the estate, and the habits of everyone in the household.

### Weaknesses

The bondservant has no standing to speak against the estate's steward, and nowhere to go if put out.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

A bondservant answers to the estate's steward, and through the steward to the house that endows the estate. Brothers and sisters often serve on the same estate.

## Plot Hooks

1. [[scenario-sldmouthsadv|The House of Sealed Mouths]]: a sister and a brother in service at a dwindled house's funerary estate, up a failing canal, have sent no word to their family for two seasons.
2. **A debt worked off.** A bondservant bound to an estate for a family's debt says the debt is paid and the steward says it is not, and wants someone who reads the estate's tablets to look.
3. **Sent downriver alone.** A bondservant carrying an estate's accounts to the house's heirs asks a party for passage and company on the river, and does not want to make the journey back alone.
