# Appearance {#appearance}

A Scribe of Weth'ur sits at a temple table in a linen robe and leather sandals, with a reed pen, a cake of ink and a clay tablet, and finishes the line before looking up at a visitor.

# Dossier {#dossier}

A Scribe of Weth'ur keeps the records of a mortuary temple of the [[affiliation-wethur|Faith of Weth'ur]]: who was buried, at what grade, at whose cost and in which ground, and the genealogies that tie the dead to the living who feed them. A family proving its claim to a house name, an heir going to law over an estate and a burial club settling a member's account all come to the temple's tablets, and the scribe who holds them is the one who answers.

## Background

Scribes of Weth'ur come from the scribal lineages and the temple schools. They learn the sacred hand and the Qalezu, the law of entries and releases, and the temple's own forms, and they spend their working lives at one temple's tables, copying, checking and entering.

## Psyche

### Personality

Exact and unhurried.

### Motivation

The scribe wants every entry complete and every record in the temple's hands.

### Strengths

The scribe reads both hands, knows the law of entries and releases, and can find a burial in the temple's jars that a family has forgotten.

### Weaknesses

The scribe trusts what is written over what is said, and sends others where the records are rather than going.

## Social

### Affiliations and Companions

The scribe answers to the temple's priests and serves the families whose dead the temple keeps.

## Plot Hooks

1. [[scenario-sldmouthsadv|The House of Sealed Mouths]]: the scribe holds the older burial records of a dwindled house, has had no new tablets from its funerary estate in years, and sends a party up a failing canal to fetch them before the heirs go to law.
2. **A name without a house.** A family claims a house name before the Genzet, and the scribe needs a genealogy tablet brought from a village shrine before the hearing, because without it the claim reads as fraud.
3. **One niche, two payments.** Two families have paid the temple for the same niche, and the scribe wants strangers to find which entry is false before either burial is held.
