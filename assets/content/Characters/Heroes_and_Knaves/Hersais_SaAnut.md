# Appearance {#appearance}

![[hersaisant|Her'sa'îs Sa'Anut]]{float: top-left}

Her'sa'îs is still 6'2" of soldier, heavy through the chest and shoulders, with olive skin weathered by campaigns, black hair, dark amber eyes, and a high-cheekboned face — a straight narrow nose, a pointed chin, well-shaped lips, elegantly curved brows, angular cheeks.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The boy is eight, and he drills at dawn, and his father watches from the colonnade and does not praise him, because Hersaîs was not praised and became a general. That is the whole of his theory of raising a son, and he has never seen a reason to revise it.

He was born the eldest son of a tenant-in-chief of the Empire of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]], which meant land and the duty of soldiers with it, and was made for command from childhood — swordplay from masters, and the thinking that moves men in a real fight. His father died when he was eighteen. He took the Sa'Anut lands and the levy with them and went to war.

Three great campaigns against the empire's neighbours made his name. He was a general by his middle thirties, and after that the Per-Aás sent for him when they wanted to know about war; for years he was the voice in the court that spoke on defence. At forty-five he gave up active command and kept the lands, the seat and an unofficial word in every military question.

He married at thirty-eight: Nebet Meshenet, of a great merchant family, a match that added her family's weight to his. They have the one son. She has a political mind of her own and has become a real adviser, and she is still, in every matter that counts, second to him, which is how he understands a marriage.

## Psyche

### Personality

He says little and is not doubted, because everyone can hear that he does not doubt himself. He does not apologise and does not explain, unless the explanation will make the next order better obeyed. The world is a chain of command to him, and order is what discipline and respect for rank produce.

He is not cruel. He is indifferent to comfort and to feeling — his own and everyone else's — and holds that duty comes before happiness for his soldiers, his household, his wife and above all his son. He honours competence and has no patience for weakness, failure or a departure from the forms.

He cannot understand a man who puts his feelings before his duty, and thinks it a moral fault. His loyalty is to the Per-Aá, as the principle of ordered authority made flesh; it is not sentimental, and if the Per-Aá ever proved unable to hold authority, the loyalty would go where authority went.

### Motivation

A lifetime under arms has taught him that order is the highest good, and that what order costs — including what it costs particular people — is always worth paying. He means to keep the empire ready for war and to build the alliances that will prevent the next one. He means, too, to make the Sa'Anut one of the empire's permanent houses, a name that will hold authority for generations.

Underneath, he is afraid of chaos, and has built his whole life as a wall against it. He believes, and needs to believe, that discipline and hierarchy and the forms observed will keep the random suffering he has seen fall on other men from falling on his. So the structures grow more elaborate, and he cannot see that they sometimes make the chaos they are built against.

### Strengths

- **Military Genius**: He sees a battle as it will be and finds the answer commanders twice his experience miss.
- **Physical Prowess**: Strong, enduring, and a swordsman who can still take a younger man and beat him on skill.
- **Authority**: When he speaks the room listens; his presence and his record move soldiers and hold an army together.
- **Foresight**: He sees the problem before it is visible and has the answer ready before it is needed.
- **Ritual Mastery**: A devout follower of Íšét and a man of rank, he knows every form, military and religious, and performs each exactly.

### Weaknesses

- **Emotional Distance**: His marriage is dutiful and cool, and his son is being taught duty rather than loved. Both work. Neither is warm.
- **Rigidity**: He applies the old answer to the new problem, and does not see when a situation has changed shape.
- **Fear of Loss of Control**: Hersaîs cannot abide what he cannot command, and treats independence as a threat.
- **Limited Diplomatic Skill**: He can command respect and cannot negotiate; he thinks bargaining is weakness, and it shows.
- **Moral Uncertainty**: Where the rules do not reach, and duty and mercy pull apart, he becomes indecisive, and then something worse than indecisive.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Per-Aá**: He serves the throne directly, as one of its most trusted military advisers, and the throne has paid him in lands, authority and honour.
- **The Military Council**: He still has a voice in the hierarchy, and the younger commanders he trained still come to him.
- **Nebet Meshenet**: His wife, who works her merchant connections for the house's advancement and is cleverer at it than he is.
- **The Temple of Íšét**: He is a devout follower and a major donor, and the priesthood supports him in return.

### Enemies

- **Iry'pat Amenhotep**: The Per-Aá's nephew, who wants Hersaîs out of the way of his own ambitions and has been cultivating other military men.
- **Semer Khentiri**: A rival courtier who wants civilian authority set over military affairs and sees Hersaîs as the obstacle.
- **Haty'a Thutmosis**: A fellow nomarch who resents that a soldier's reputation has earned Hersaîs honours a better-bred man was denied.
- **The Reformist Faction**: A loose alliance who make him the symbol of imperial rigidity and argue that his influence keeps the army too large and too dear.

### Affiliations

- **The Imperial Military**: His connections run through the whole hierarchy, and his influence exceeds his rank.
- **The Local Nobility Council**: A member, and usually the voice of the traditional, military-minded side.
- **The Temple of Íšét**: A major supporter, present at the great ceremonies, on formal terms with the priesthood.
- **The Sa'Anut Family**: Its head, and the man who means to raise it to permanent standing in the empire.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Dishonorable Order**: The Per-Aá has given an order that Hersaîs knows to be a military mistake, and perhaps a disaster. It came from the throne and cannot be questioned without seeming to question the gods. He can obey and try to blunt it, or speak, and lose his seat and perhaps his life. The order, he suspects, was written on false intelligence, and the intelligence came from Semer Khentiri.

2. **The Worthy Opponent**: A commander from a neighbouring realm has come under truce with a treaty of mutual defence against a common enemy. It is sound. To back it he would have to admit that a realm he has always counted an enemy is needed to keep the region safe — and this commander once beat him in single combat, honourably, and he has not forgotten it.

3. **The Unstable Heir**: The Per-Aá is failing, and the son who should follow him is not fit, in mind or temper, for military authority. Iry'pat Amenhotep is offering the army a more aggressive future in exchange for its support. The lawful heir who cannot rule, the ambitious nephew who could, or something else: his choice shapes the empire for a generation, and he knows it.

4. **The Betrayal Within**: His most trusted lieutenant, a man he trained and raised, has been selling the empire's military secrets. He has to find out how far it goes, who else in his household is bought, and how to make the army safe again without admitting in public that his own circle was rotten.

5. **The Moral Collapse**: A soldier, without orders and in the face of something monstrous, acted, saved lives, and broke the chain of command doing it. His code says punish it, absolutely. Some part of him has begun to suspect that the forms themselves can fail, and if that is true, then so can everything he has built his life on.
