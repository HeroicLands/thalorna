# Appearance {#appearance}

![[kyrksptrks|Kyriákos Patrikîos]]{float: top-left}

Kyriákos stands 6'0" tall with a heavy build. He has tanned weathered skin, gray hair, and brown eyes. His features include a diamond-shaped face, a hawkish nose, a strong jawline, and expressive brows. He has a scar running down his back.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Early Life and the Mercenary Years

Kyriákos was born in a small village on the margins of the Byzarian League to a blacksmith and a healer. His childhood was marked by poverty and the constant presence of war—his village changed hands between different powers repeatedly, and he learned early that survival meant adaptation and ruthlessness. When he was fourteen, mercenary recruiters came through looking for able-bodied youths, and desperate to escape the grinding hopelessness of village life, Kyriákos signed on. For thirty-five years, he served in mercenary companies across the known world, becoming eventually one of the deadliest warriors his employers had ever seen. He was not evil by nature—he was simply a hired soldier, following orders, collecting his pay, and trying to survive each contract with as few scars and as few nightmares as he could manage.

The specific campaigns and battles of those years have faded into a blur of blood and fire. He fought in wars whose cause he never understood. He led charges into fortified positions. He held bridges against overwhelming odds. He killed hundreds of men, many of them levied men no more guilty than himself. He participated in atrocities that he has never fully forgiven himself for. He was wounded dozens of times. He watched comrades die. He had lovers and lost them. He betrayed people who trusted him. He was betrayed by those he trusted. The line between survival and monstrosity became increasingly blurred, until Kyriákos was no longer certain which side of that line he occupied.

### Turning Point and Redemption

At forty-five, while serving with the **Iron Wolves Company**, Kyriákos was hired, along with his company, to massacre the inhabitants of a village that had refused to submit to a local lord's authority. Something in him broke that day. He survived the massacre and was paid his standard rate, but he did not return to the company's camp. Instead, he made his way to the Byzarian League's heartland, seeking obscurity and the possibility of redemption that he suspected he no longer deserved. He settled in a small village on the periphery—deliberately choosing the kind of forgotten place he had once despised—and spent years in silence and isolation, working as a day laborer and attempting to become someone other than what he had been.

### Current Situation

Over the past five years, the village has begun to recognize Kyriákos's rare martial skill and his implicit commitment to their defense. When bandits or wolves threatened, he stood against them. When young people wished to learn combat, he began teaching reluctantly, convinced he had nothing worth passing on. Yet slowly, almost despite himself, he became the village's protector and teacher—not the famed warrior he had been in his mercenary days, but a man trying to use his terrible skills to defend the innocent rather than to oppress them. He lives in a small cottage on the village's edge, maintains a small smithy, and passes his days teaching sword forms to villagers and young adventurers who occasionally come seeking instruction from the famed retired warrior whose true identity they rarely fully comprehend.

## Psyche

### Personality

Kyriákos is a man of deep silence and restraint. He speaks sparingly, choosing words with the care of someone who knows that words, carelessly deployed, can wound as deeply as blades. He is gruff with most people, not out of hostility but out of a conviction that he does not deserve their friendship or warmth. Yet he is committed to those under his protection. He will stand against overwhelming odds for people he barely knows, driven by a compulsion to atone that he will never fully accomplish. He is capable of dark humor—a habit from his mercenary years—but it surfaces rarely.

His faith in **Vénusia**, goddess of prosperity and abundance, was rekindled late in life through an unexpected source. He came to understand that Vénusia stands for the abundance of a life lived with purpose and integrity as well as for material wealth, and that his journey from violence to attempted redemption was itself a form of prayer to that goddess. He does not attend temple regularly, but he makes small offerings and performs his duties as a teacher and protector with the reverence of spiritual practice.

### Motivation

Kyriákos is driven by a need to atone that he consciously recognizes can never be fully satisfied. He cannot unmake the deaths he caused or undo the harm he inflicted. But he can dedicate whatever years remain to him to protecting the innocent, teaching virtue to young warriors, and standing as a living warning against the seductions of mercenary life. He also seeks redemption through building something positive—he has recently begun collaborating with the village blacksmith to craft excellent weapons, not for hire but as a contribution to the village's defense. On a deeper level, he struggles with a desire for peace—the peace of conscience that he will likely never achieve—and with the related desire to find meaning in his remaining years beyond mere survival.

### Strengths

- **Unmatched Practical Combat Experience**: In the sword, the thrown weapon, and the weaponsmith's craft, Kyriákos possesses the kind of experience that comes only from three and a half decades of real warfare. He knows how to kill quickly, how to read an opponent's intentions, and how to maintain consciousness and function while wounded and exhausted.
- **Exceptional Willpower**: His will, combined with his survival skills and his quickness to act, makes him nearly impossible to break mentally or physically. He can function under extreme stress, maintain focus when others would break, and push through pain that would incapacitate normal people.
- **Strength and Endurance**: His strength and endurance mean he can still perform feats of physical power despite his age. He can wield weapons that younger fighters cannot and maintain exertion over extended periods.
- **Battlefield Awareness**: His sharp awareness allows him to read situations quickly and understand what's happening around him with tactical clarity. He notices threats before others and can anticipate danger.
- **Teaching and Instruction**: His command of others and willingness to teach have proven surprisingly effective with young warriors. His experience, hard-won and nearly fatal, carries authenticity that cannot be faked.
- **Skilled Craftwork**: His metalcraft, weaponcraft, and other craft skills allow him to maintain and improve equipment, making him largely self-sufficient.
- **Wisdom of Age**: His knowledge of lore, his skill in argument, and his life experience give him a wisdom that young warriors respect, even if they sometimes struggle to understand his cautions.

### Weaknesses

- **Haunted by Guilt**: His inner wounds run deep, and he is vulnerable to depression, dark thoughts, and moments of despair. He sometimes seems barely held together by will alone, and a fresh shock could shatter what remains of his mind.
- **Limited Modern Influence**: His thirty-five years away from conventional society have left him somewhat isolated from current politics and power structures. He doesn't understand or fit well into court intrigue or formal hierarchies.
- **Age and Wear**: At fifty, his reflexes are no longer what they once were. While still dangerous, he cannot match young warriors in pure speed. His dexterity and agility are adequate but far below his skill with a blade, so he relies more on experience than speed.
- **Difficulty with Civility**: Years of military life have left him blunt and sometimes harsh in his communication. He can offend people through blunt honesty or apparent rudeness without intending to.
- **Vulnerability to Betrayal**: Though he has learned caution, he can still be manipulated through appeals to his sense of honor or his desire to protect others. Those who understand his values can sometimes lead him into traps.
- **No Magical Training or Resistance**: Like many lifelong warriors, he has no training in magic and is vulnerable to magical attacks or enchantments. Magic essentially terrifies him, as it represents a form of threat he cannot counter with skill.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Village Elder Mâria**: The leader of the village that has become Kyriákos's home. She values his protection and guidance and acts as his primary connection to the village community, mediating between him and those who fear his past.
- **Young Warriors and Adventurers**: Dozens of youths and adventurers seeking training have benefited from Kyriákos's instruction, and many maintain loyalty to him as their teacher.
- **The Village Blacksmith**: A collaborative partnership with **Master Thorne**, the village blacksmith, who has become something like a friend to Kyriákos. They work together to create quality weapons.

### Enemies

- **Sergeant Karûsh**: A former lieutenant from the Iron Wolves Company who was present at the massacre that broke Kyriákos. Karûsh has never forgiven Kyriákos for abandoning the company and suspects he's living in the very village they were hired to destroy. If Karûsh discovered Kyriákos's location, he would likely attempt to capture or kill him for his perceived betrayal.
- **The Iron Wolves Company**: The mercenary company views Kyriákos as a deserter who could reveal their operations and methods. There is likely a standing bounty on him, though few realize the old village instructor is worth finding.
- **Lord Varkûn**: The regional noble who hired the Iron Wolves to massacre the village still rules the region. Kyriákos fears that if his identity were known, Varkûn might move against him or the village he's come to protect.
- **His Own Conscience**: More than any external enemy, Kyriákos battles himself—his guilt, his memories, his conviction that he deserves punishment for what he has done.

### Affiliations

- **The Village**: Though he would never formally affiliate with any group, the village has become his implicit family and the focus of his protective commitment.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Ghost from His Past**: One of Kyriákos's former comrades from the Iron Wolves Company, a warrior named **Darius**, tracks him down with seemingly friendly intentions—a reunion of old soldiers. Yet Darius carries orders from above to either recruit Kyriákos back into service or eliminate him as a liability. Kyriákos must get through the dangerous reunion without compromising the village and without becoming the killer he's spent five years trying to stop being.

2. **The Mercenary Company's Return**: The Iron Wolves Company, facing a decline in their fortunes, discovers that the massacre they conducted was illegal and the lord who hired them has disowned them to avoid scandal. Several company members recognize Kyriákos and believe he could be forced to testify against the lord, vindicating the company. They attempt to capture him to force his cooperation—or eliminate him if he refuses. 3. **The Young Knight's Burden**: A young noble arrives in the village seeking training from the famed warrior Kyriákos, desperate to learn how to survive warfare. Yet Kyriákos recognizes in this youth a future version of himself—destined to become a mercenary unless something intervenes. He becomes invested in changing the youth's path, potentially putting himself in conflict with the youth's family, who view military service as the only honorable path.

4. **Redemption Through Skill**: An official from the Byzarian League's capital arrives with evidence that one of the major battles Kyriákos fought in, which he believed was a pointless atrocity, was actually essential to preventing a greater catastrophe. The official attempts to absolve Kyriákos of guilt for that campaign. Kyriákos has to face the possibility that some of his moral reckoning was wrong—that perhaps he's not as much a monster as he believes, which makes him question everything he's built his redemption around.

5. **The Final Challenge**: A young, skilled warrior arrives seeking to challenge Kyriákos, hoping to defeat the famed retired mercenary and establish her own reputation. Kyriákos doesn't want to fight—he's trying to move beyond violence. Yet the young woman is relentless, and when he refuses repeatedly, she challenges him publicly, questioning his courage. The duel becomes inevitable, and Kyriákos must find whether he can still fight when he must, and whether he can do so without becoming the killer he was in his mercenary days.
