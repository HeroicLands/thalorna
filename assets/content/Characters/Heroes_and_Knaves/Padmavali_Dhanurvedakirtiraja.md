# Appearance {#appearance}

![[pdmvldhnrvdkrtrj|Padmàvali Dhanurvedakīrtirāja]]{float: top-left}

Ink under the nails, always, and usually a smudge of ochre somewhere she has not noticed. She is 5'2" and lightly built, with warm caramel skin, black hair and deep brown eyes in an oval face — a delicate nose, a soft jawline, well-shaped lips, full cheeks, deep brows.

# Dossier {#dossier}

A desert on one of her maps is ochre and sand-coloured, and the wells are drawn as wells, each one a small careful picture of the place a traveller will actually find. A coast is drawn with the set of the currents in the wave-lines. It is exact — Master Ananda saw to that — and it is also the reason merchants who never leave Saṃgha hang her work on their walls. She would rather they used it.

She was born into the Dhanurvedakīrtirāja, a merchant family of the city-state of Saṃgha, one of the great trading cities of Vedyara. Her father, a spice merchant, had little use for a bookish daughter; her mother had seen her, at seven, sketching the fronts of buildings with the proportions right, and at twelve working out for herself why a road looks narrower as it goes away. She went to **Master Ananda**, whose maps were bought by merchants, commanders and noble houses across the city-states, and who wanted from a pupil two things that seldom come together: the arithmetic of a true map, and the hand to make one that people would keep.

Through her twenties the commissions grew: trade routes for merchants, blank country for explorers, illuminated maps of their own domains for nobles. At thirty she was given the largest work of her life — a single map of all five city-states, to mark the hundredth year of the trade alliance that binds them. It has taken two years, and it is nearly done.

It has also made her nervous. The commission is paid for by a coalition of merchant lords, each of whom has an opinion about where his city's boundary ought to fall and which routes ought to look important. Nobody has said the word "adjust" aloud yet. She can hear it coming. She had believed a map was a record of what is there; she is discovering that her patrons believe it is a claim about what is theirs. At the same time scholars and explorers keep arriving at her workshop with offers to take her somewhere no map reaches, and she has not said no to any of them.

## Psyche

### Personality

She disappears into work for days and forgets to eat. She talks little and slowly, picking words as she picks a colour, and has no patience for small talk; her mind is on how a city drains, how a coast bends, how a pattern repeats in rock and river. She is curious about people the way she is curious about a watershed — as a system — and can miss entirely what the person in front of her wants.

She doubts herself. She is never sure the colour on a map is more than decoration, and she lies awake over the mistake that would send a traveller into a dry valley. The worry is constant and it makes her easy to lean on, because someone with a louder certainty can move her off her own. On the one thing she is sure of — what a map is for — she does not move at all.

### Motivation

Truth and beauty in one object; she does not think they are different jobs. A map, to her, is a sacred document, an account of a people's dealings with the ground they live on, and it should be as faithful as a hand can make it. She wants to make things that last, that help, that spare somebody the misery of being lost. Underneath that is a romance she admits to no one: to leave the workshop, go where nothing is charted, and draw it. And under that, the wish for someone she respects to tell her that her choices were right.

### Strengths

- **Cartographic Precision**: Distance, bearing, relief and the translation of ground into a flat sheet — she gets them right, and knows when a projection is lying.
- **Artistic Technique**: She draws and paints as well as she surveys, so a map of hers is a picture that happens to be correct.
- **Mathematical and Analytical Mind**: She solves a problem of scale or projection in her head and sees the pattern in a set of flood records that others read as noise.
- **Attention to Detail**: She notices what is small and puts it in.
- **Physical Capability**: She climbs well and moves over rough ground better than her frame suggests, and has done her own surveying in country a city cartographer would send a servant into.

### Weaknesses

- **Naive About Power**: She believes that what is true matters more than what is wanted, and has no defence against a patron who does not.
- **Anxiety and Self-Doubt**: Under pressure she stalls, going over a choice until she can no longer see it.
- **Physical Frailty**: She cannot carry a heavy pack, fight, or last long in the open without help.
- **Withdrawn**: She loses track of friendships for months at a time and is read as cold by people who do not know where she has gone.
- **Little Experience Outside the Craft**: Trade, survival and a fight are all things other people would have to handle for her.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Five City-States Merchant Coalition**: The merchant lords of the five cities, who commissioned the Great Map together and have five different ideas of what it should show.
- **Master Ananda**: Her teacher, retired to a house in the country, who still commissions a small map now and then and is the one person she asks about the ethics of the work.
- **House of Saṃgha**: The leading merchant family of her city, which has offered her a permanent workshop in return for first call on her time.

### Enemies

- **No One Named, Yet**: Each city's faction suspects she is quietly working for a rival's. Her refusal to bend a boundary for anyone has not made her friends either.

### Affiliations

- **Independent Cartographers' Fellowship**: A loose company of master cartographers who hold each other to accuracy, commission the occasional joint work, and know what it costs to refuse a patron.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Great Map's Secret Demand**: The lords whose cities have shrunk in trade have come to her privately: draw their territories larger, their routes busier, their rivals smaller, for a great deal of money — or lose every commission they control. One of them has already had a hand at the nearly finished sheet. She needs someone to find out who, and to find her a patron who is not one of them.

2. **The Expedition to Uncharted Lands**: **Captain Vihara**, whose reputation is large and not entirely good, is mounting an expedition to a lost kingdom rumoured in the jungles past the southern border, and wants Padmàvali as its cartographer. The pay is enormous; so is the chance of fever, hostile country and never finding the way back. She is frightened, and she has not said no.

3. **The Map That Predicts Disaster**: Surveying the farmland around Saṃgha, she has seen in the rock, the watercourses and the old flood records a pattern that says a great flood is coming within a year or two. To warn the city is to admit a risk that would empty its markets; the merchant lords, who have heard something of it, would rather she said nothing. She has said nothing so far.

4. **The Cartography of the Soul**: A priest of Mahájaya, god of order, has told her that her maps are something more than drawings of ground, and that she has a share of the god's ordering power. He wants her to chart what cannot be seen — sacred sites, the lines of power under the land — and offers protection and means. She cannot tell whether she is being called or used, or what a map of that kind would be worth to a general.

5. **The Stolen Masterwork**: The Great Map has been taken from her workshop by people who knew their trade. Not by a rival, it turns out, nor by a faction: by a collector in a distant realm who thinks it the finest thing of its kind and does not mean to give it back. His house is well guarded, and he is said to own maps older than any archive in the city-states.

6. **The Anomaly**: Two maps made five hundred years apart, by different hands in different cities, show the same mountain pass in slightly different places. Master Ananda has found the same in old texts about other places. Either the old cartographers erred — which she cannot bear — or the land has moved. She wants to go and measure it. She has begun to suspect the difference is growing.
