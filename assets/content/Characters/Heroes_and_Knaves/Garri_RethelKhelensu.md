# Appearance {#appearance}

![[garrrthlkhln|Garri Reth'el'Khelensu]]{float=top-left}

Garri is a heavy-set man of 5'8" with golden-brown skin, white hair, a long face and warm brown eyes. His nose is aquiline and his mouth full, and a crescent moon is tattooed on the side of his neck, which his collar shows when he lifts his chin to speak. He dresses in a plain linen tunic and wears a string of meditation beads wrapped twice about his left wrist. A leather case of quills, ink and parchment hangs from his shoulder, and a few well-handled volumes of philosophy go with him wherever he is asked to teach.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Life and Calling**

Garri was born in the capital city of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] during a period of cultural flourishing. His father was a merchant of considerable means, his mother a woman of noble birth whose family had declined in fortune but retained their intellectual traditions. Garri showed a rare aptitude for learning from childhood—he was fluent in three languages by age ten and had absorbed the entire classical philosophical canon by his mid-teens. Rather than follow his father into commerce, Garri pursued formal study under the most respected philosophers of Au'Khelathu, eventually earning the title of Master Philosopher and securing a prestigious position as a teacher in the great library-schools.

However, at age twenty-eight, after a powerful spiritual experience during a pilgrimage to the sacred temples of [[affiliation-uznera|Uzn'era]], Garri made a decision that shocked his family and peers: he abandoned his position of privilege and security to become a wandering scholar-teacher. He spent the next two decades traveling throughout the Khelathi lands—and beyond—seeking knowledge, teaching wherever he went, and refining his philosophical understanding through direct experience rather than theoretical study alone.

**The Seeker's Journey**

For more than twenty years, Garri moved from city to city, village to village, establishing temporary schools wherever he settled, teaching anyone willing to learn regardless of their station or means. He studied from observation of life itself as well as from written texts: working in agriculture to understand the cycles and patterns of creation, living with fishermen to grasp the philosophical meaning of human dependence on natural forces, and dwelling in temples to deepen his spiritual understanding. During this period, he earned the affection and respect of diverse peoples, though his ideas—which often challenged conventional wisdom and religious orthodoxy—also earned him enemies among the rigid and powerful.

**Return and Current Situation**

Five years ago, now at fifty years of age and bearing the physical toll of decades of wandering, Garri settled in a modest house on the outskirts of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]. He offers his teaching and counsel freely to those who seek him out, supporting himself through modest donations, the sale of his written collections of philosophical essays, and occasional patronage from grateful students who have prospered. His reputation has grown in his later years—he is now recognized as one of the foremost philosophers of the age, a living link to ancient wisdom combined with practical insight. However, his contentment is precarious. Some aspects of modern [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] society—the growing materialism of merchants, the rigidity of religious doctrine, the exploitation of the poor—trouble him deeply. He senses that the cultural renaissance of his youth is giving way to a kind of spiritual stagnation, and he finds himself increasingly compelled to speak out, knowing that doing so may renew old conflicts.

## Psyche

### Personality

Garri has a rare combination of intellectual rigor and spiritual depth. He speaks with authority born of knowledge rather than arrogance, and he listens with deep attention, honoring others' perspectives even when he disagrees. He is a natural teacher, with the ability to make complex ideas accessible and compelling, and he seems interested in understanding how different people perceive the world. His humor tends toward gentle irony and wisdom-tinged observation rather than jokes or mockery.

Despite his considerable achievements, Garri harbors no pride. He views his knowledge as gift and responsibility rather than accomplishment. He is patient with those struggling to understand difficult concepts, but he has no patience for willful ignorance or deliberate cruelty. He can be cryptic and indirect when discussing matters he considers sacred, and he sometimes frustrates those seeking direct answers to complex questions—his tendency is to respond with a question or parable rather than prescriptive guidance.

There is a melancholy undertone to Garri's demeanor, a sense that he has seen much of both the beauty and tragedy of human existence, and that this seeing has left him with a kind of sorrowful wisdom.

### Motivation

Garri is driven by a quest for understanding that has shaped his entire life. He believes that truth exists beneath the surface of conventional wisdom and that each person's life experience contains valuable knowledge that contributes to a larger human understanding. His deepest motivation is to help others see beyond the limited perspectives that narrow their lives, to recognize their interconnection with all beings and the cosmos itself, and to live with greater awareness and compassion as a result. He is also motivated by duty: he feels that those who have been given the gift of extensive learning are obligated to share that knowledge with others, particularly with those who have had fewer opportunities. In his later years, he is increasingly driven by a concern for the direction his culture is taking and by a need to speak truth even when it is unpopular or dangerous.

### Strengths

Extraordinary Intellect and Reasoning
: Garri's reasoning is paired with high skill in folklore, discourse, mathematics, and script. He can analyze complex systems, understand historical patterns, and articulate subtle ideas with great clarity.

Comprehensive Knowledge Across Disciplines
: His skill breadth is extraordinary. He possesses mastery-level competence in cooking, merchant practice, medicine, agriculture, seamanship, survival, awareness, and music, paired with deep philosophical knowledge. This unusual combination allows him to understand life from multiple perspectives and to draw insights from diverse domains.

Spiritual and Ritualistic Understanding
: His training in ritual and spirit lore, combined with his deep personal spiritual practice, gives him real insight into matters of faith, meaning, and the higher aspects of existence.

Compelling Communication
: His eloquence combined with his command, theatrics, and singing make him an engaging speaker and teacher. People naturally gather to listen to him.

Practical Wisdom and Adaptability
: Despite his theoretical knowledge, his extensive practical experience—shown in his many trades and his history of living in varied contexts—gives him a real understanding of how ideas apply to real life.

### Weaknesses

Limited Physical Capability
: His low agility, mediocre strength, and moderate climbing and physical skills mean he is not physically capable of extended strenuous activity, combat, or demanding travel. Age and years of philosophical contemplation rather than physical training have left him relatively fragile.

Abstraction and Impracticality
: Garri's strength in abstract reasoning is sometimes a weakness. He can become so engaged in exploring what a situation means in theory that he fails to attend to practical necessities. His guidance, while wise, can sometimes be impractically abstract or indirect.

Social Inflexibility
: Despite his ability to engage audiences, his limited fellow-feeling means he sometimes fails to recognize or honor the emotional needs of individuals. He can be insensitive to personal suffering while exploring its philosophical meaning, and he sometimes alienates those seeking comfort or support rather than intellectual analysis.

Controversial Ideas
: His tendency to challenge orthodoxy, while philosophically admirable, has earned him enemies among the powerful and the dogmatically committed. His safety is periodically threatened by those who view his teaching as dangerous or heretical.

Isolation and Limited Practical Resources
: His independent status and modest means leave him vulnerable. He has no institutional backing and few truly loyal allies, making him susceptible to suppression or manipulation by those with greater power.

## Social

### Patrons

The [[affiliation-lutuznera|Lut-Uzn'era]]
: The priesthood of his patron goddess recognize him as a spiritual authority and occasionally provide modest financial support and sanctuary. However, this relationship is complex, as Garri's ideas sometimes challenge official temple doctrine.

Grateful Former Students
: Several of Garri's former students have prospered in their lives and maintain their connection with him through regular donations and commissions for written works. These relationships are the foundation of his current modest livelihood.

The City's Intellectual Community
: The city's more open-minded scholars, liberal merchants, and teachers recognize his value and occasionally commission him to lead lectures, conduct seminars, or advise on matters requiring ethical consideration.

### Enemies

[[affiliation-qetharlemu|The Qethar'lemu]] and Rigid Orthodoxy
: Conservative elements within the Khelathi religious establishment view Garri's questioning of orthodox interpretations as dangerous heresy. They would like to silence him but lack direct cause to do so, creating ongoing tension.

Merchant Lords Uncomfortable with Critique
: Wealthy merchants who have built fortunes through practices Garri considers exploitative or morally questionable view him with hostility. He has been known to critique materialism and greed publicly, earning their enmity.

### Affiliations

Independent Scholar Network
: Garri maintains informal connections with other independent philosophers, teachers, and spiritual seekers throughout the Khelathi lands. However, he is not formally affiliated with any institution or organization.

## Plot Hooks

The Charge of Heresy
: The Qethar'lemu bring a formal charge of heresy, built on written copies of his lessons. In Galezkara a temple enclave answers to its high priest before the Zeglar, and the only sheltering ground is the enclave of Uzn'era, whose priests have supported him and do not like his doctrines. If he accepts sanctuary he buys safety with silence; if he refuses it, the Meglai have no standing to stop the charge.

The School in the Warrens
: A merchant lord offers to build him a grand lecture hall on the dry ground if he will close the free school he runs in the flood-prone warrens of Gar-Qethar, because the lord wants the land. Every year when the water rises Garri's students move out with their families and come back to rebuilding. The hall would be dry, endowed and far from the people who need him.

The Student Who Quotes Him
: A gifted young student is using Garri's sayings in the halls of the grain-factors, where they are quoted to justify withholding the dole from the registered poor. Garri recognizes his own words in a mouth he did not teach. The student is sincere, and has made a name by the argument.

The Library of All Wisdom
: A collector of rare philosophical texts offers to fund a library that gathers and translates the wisdom of every land, with Garri at its head. The collector's ship lies at the foreign shore of the Great Quay, and the cost, which Garri learns slowly, is that the finished copies leave the empire.

The formal charge was assembled from notes taken in his lectures by a student he trusts, who is the Qethar'lemu's informer. She believes she is saving him from a worse charge by controlling the evidence.

The collector is a Taruzelian factor who wants the temple libraries' philosophical texts copied abroad. The student who quotes Garri to the grain-factors is the factor's agent in the same house.
