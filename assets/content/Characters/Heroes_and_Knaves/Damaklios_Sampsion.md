# Appearance {#appearance}

![[damklsmpsn|Damáklios Sampsiôn]]{float: top-left}

The mouth is the tell — slightly pursed, as if he were holding a word back — in an angular face with a hawkish nose and a pronounced chin. He is 5'6" and of medium build, with light tanned skin, hazel eyes, brown hair going gray and brows going with it, and a crescent moon tattooed on his neck.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The story in the guild hall is that the cabin of an admiral of Tarvénia has a chart of the League's coast on the wall that is better than the one in the League's own archive, and that the hand is Damáklios Sampsiôn's. Nobody has seen it. Everybody repeats it. He has heard it too, and says nothing, which is taken as an answer.

He was born in the Byzarian League to a mapmaker of modest name and a weaver of a much better one, drew before he could write, and was apprenticed in his teens to the **Cartographers' Guild** under **Master Cartographer Vésalon** — a man who demanded fidelity to the source to the point of illness, and got it. By his twenties Damáklios was making maps for merchant houses, for the League's administrators and for collectors, and they were very good.

They were also, he came to see, weapons. A map shows where the trade goes, where the ore is, where a wall is thin. Fifteen years ago, short of money and offered a great deal of it, he began taking work from people who wanted maps for those reasons: smuggling lords, the agents of foreign courts, the syndicates that carve up the docks. He told himself he was recording what was there, that the use was the buyer's business, that merchants and kings had always done what maps only made easier. The money was more than honest work would ever have paid.

The telling has worn thin. The city guard suspects him of spying, or treason, and has said so in the wrong rooms. The guild is cool. Rivals ask, in public, how his maps are so accurate. He has bought himself a little mercy by passing a few things to the guard, which the syndicates noticed, and the honest commissions have dried up, which leaves him more dependent on the other kind than he was when he started.

## Psyche

### Personality

There are two men, and he has arranged never to have to look at them together. One is an artist and scholar who can lose a day in getting a coastline right and comes out of it happier than anything else makes him. The other sells the coastline to whoever asks, and has a sentence ready for every death it leads to: that he is an observer, not an actor, and the map is only true.

He does not like people and is not good with them. He would rather be with his measurements and the imagined inhabitants of far places than with anyone in the room. Forced into company he is awkward and then irritable, or retreats into a chilly correctness; when he does speak, his wit cuts, and sometimes it is meant to.

He drinks more than he says. He dreams about places his maps have led people to. He has a way of hurting himself and calling it reflection. And he keeps explaining — to himself, at night — that it was practical, it was necessary, it was the buyer's fault.

### Motivation

Enough money to leave: a house somewhere remote and far from the League, and years spent mapping country nobody wants for any reason but the wanting to know. That dream is what keeps him doing the work that makes him need it. There is also a small and stubborn wish to be respected again in the guild, which every year makes less likely; and a belief, older than either, that a map is the truest account of the world there is — truer than any general's or philosopher's — and that someone ought to say so.

### Strengths

- **Cartographic Mastery**: Among the best living. His maps are accurate to a degree people find uncanny, and they show how ground, people and resource sit together, not only where the rivers are.
- **Drawing and Artistic Skill**: He can put the character of a place or a face on paper in a few lines and say with them what a page of writing could not.
- **Perception and Attention to Detail**: He sees the small feature in the landscape, and the thing a person is feeling under what they are saying.
- **Mathematical Reasoning**: Scale, distance, projection, and the pattern in a set of figures — he does them in his head.
- **Survival and Practical Knowledge**: He has done his own surveying in wild country and can read a landscape on foot as well as on paper.

### Weaknesses

- **Moral Compromise and Self-Deception**: He has explained away so much that he can no longer see how much danger he is in, or what he has done.
- **Physical Fragility**: Not strong, not fast, soon tired. In a fight he would need someone else.
- **Social Dysfunction and Isolation**: He has no allies, only customers, and a customer will sell him.
- **Drink and Decline**: The bottle manages the guilt and is eating the judgement, and he is sure it has not.
- **Notoriety and Legal Vulnerability**: His name is in the guard's files. Proof would mean a rope.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Military Intelligence Operatives of the Byzarian League**: Still his largest honest customer. They suspect him, they investigate him, and they go on ordering, because nobody else's maps are as good.
- **Merchant-Lord Davos the Uncouth**: A rich smuggler with interests down the whole coast, who buys charts of hidden coves and unpatrolled routes. Also the nearest thing Damáklios has to a drinking companion.
- **Unknown Foreign Power**: The commissions that pay best come through intermediaries for a court he cannot name — the Kingdom of Tarvénia, perhaps, or one of the northern confederations. Not knowing lets him sleep. It also frightens him.

### Enemies

- **The Byzarian League's City Guard (Official)**: They are watching him, and have hired **Cartographer Méchon** to make rival maps of the same ground, to see whether his accuracy is skill or access.
- **Rival Cartographer Méchon**: Talented, jealous, and convinced Damáklios's commissions come by dishonest means. He has friends in the guard now and says so.
- **The Theft Syndicate of the Eastern Docks**: He mapped their caches for them, at a high price, and then gave the caches to the guard. They have marked him.

### Affiliations

- **Cartographers' Guild of the Byzarian League**: Still a member, pending charges the guild knows are coming.
- **Flórania's Mystery Cults**: The crescent moon on his neck is an initiate's mark of one of the fertility mysteries. He goes less than he did. It is the one place he is still comfortable.
- **The Black Ledger (Underground Cartography Network)**: Cartographers who will map forbidden ground for anyone. Admission is by reputation. He keeps his standing in it as insurance.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Compromised Archives**: The League's Cartographic Archive has been robbed — maps centuries old stolen, others copied — and the guard's trail leads to the one commissioned artist who had access: Damáklios. He did not do it; a foreign court did. He can confess to a theft that was not his, or maintain his innocence while the evidence circles him and the investigation walks nearer, every day, to the sales that were.

2. **The Mapmaker's Betrayal**: A map he sold is being used to run something large in the city — slaving, extortion, the buying of officials. Herald [[being-carengrvth|Cárenna Gráveth]] has found the map and the hand that drew it. Her terms: everything, on his buyers and his network and every map he has sold, in exchange for silence; or the guard gets the map and his name. His buyers do not forgive either.

3. **The Perfect Map**: A patron he cannot identify wants the whole city — every building, every guard post, who holds what, when the watch changes — and will pay for it. He knows what the map is for. He has been told, gently, what will happen to his old mother and to his old master if he refuses. He could draw it false. He is not sure the buyer would not know.

4. **The Cursed Territory**: A rich, secretive patron wants a region mapped that has killed or broken every cartographer who tried. Damáklios has started, and has begun dreaming the ground before he walks it, and meeting people on it who are not quite there. It may be the drink. It may be that the place is not where the maps say.

5. **The Guild's Reformation**: The guild, having found several members selling maps to enemies, is about to require that every map be reviewed before delivery and embargo any it judges dangerous. That ends his trade and opens his past. Its leaders have also come to him privately: they know, they will protect him, and they would like him to go on with his "special commissions" — for them.
