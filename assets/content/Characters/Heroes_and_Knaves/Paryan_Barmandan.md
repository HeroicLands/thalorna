# Appearance {#appearance}

![[prynbrmndn|Paryan Barmandan]]{float: top-left}

A hawkish nose in an oval face, with green eyes, black hair, light olive skin, an angular jawline, well-defined lips and prominent brows. She is 5'8", light in build, and usually wearing one piece of her own and nothing else that glitters.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Life and Apprenticeship**

Master Dhavani taught her that a piece of jewelry is a conversation between the one who makes it and the one who wears it; that each piece should say something; and that the best work honors both the stone and the body it will sit on. Dhavani also taught by example that a jeweler may be afraid of nothing — that materials can be put together in ways nobody has tried, and tradition can be honored and pushed at once. Paryan has kept all of it.

She was born in the Sultanate of Amradad to a merchant of moderate success and a woman of noble birth who had married down for love, and showed no interest in either trade or rank. What she loved was light on a surface, the way colors move against each other, and raw stuff becoming something beautiful. At twelve she was apprenticed to Dhavani, one of the greatest jewelers in the Sultanate, whose name was known in the cities of the neighboring realms.

**Journey to Mastery**

She was a journeyman at eighteen and a master at twenty-three, which is fast. Her own style showed early: stones set where nobody sets them, metals paired that are not supposed to pair, pieces that argued with symmetry and won. Collectors who wanted something other than expensive convention found her, and at twenty-five she opened her own workshop. Four years on, she is among the most inventive and technically able jewelers in Amradad.

**Current Situation**

The commissions now come from the nobility and from the royal family, and the pieces go where the whole court will see them. The guild has begun to mention her when it talks about who should lead it, which she is not sure she wants. Collectors and patrons from distant lands are offering contracts that would mean moving or greatly enlarging the shop. And a scholar she cannot quite read has asked her to make jewelry with particular symbolic — perhaps magical — properties, an idea that excites her and worries her in about equal parts. She is trying to work out how much of herself and her community she is willing to trade for the larger stage.

## Psyche

### Personality

Warm, and when she is working, elsewhere. At the bench she loses the hours and forgets to eat; the work seems to come through her rather than from her, and people who do not make things mistake the state for obsession. Away from it she is easy company — music, dancing, the pleasures of a town she likes.

She sees possibilities where other people see limits, which is why her work looks the way it does and why her accounts look the way they do. She undercharges because she cannot put a figure on what she added, and she runs past the promised date because there is always one more thing the piece could be. She has no arrogance about any of it. She wants to hear what people think of the work, helps apprentices and other artisans with her time and her hands, and still seems a little surprised to have done well.

### Motivation

Making. Turning raw material into something beautiful that will sit on someone at the important moments of a life and be handed down after it. She worships Árávēštä, goddess of prosperity and abundance, and thinks of the work as a way of honoring her by putting beauty and worth into the world.

She also wants to be recognized — to have her name mean mastery, and to show that her methods beat the conventional ones. And she believes, without embarrassment, that a piece of jewelry can change how a person sees herself, and that making one well is therefore a kind of service.

### Strengths

- **Extraordinary Technical Mastery**: Setting, metalwork, drawing and the hands to execute all three exactly. Hard materials and complicated pieces do not stop her.
- **Visionary Artistic Sense**: An eye for proportion and harmony informed by drawing, dancing and singing, and designs that nobody would have predicted and everybody wants.
- **Material Mastery**: She knows stones and metals — where they come from, what they are worth, what they can be made to do — to an unusual depth.
- **Interpersonal Warmth and Charm**: She understands what a patron wants and makes them feel understood, and they come back and bring friends.
- **Physical Capability**: Fit and strong, more than most artisans, and able to keep up on a journey without being a burden.

### Weaknesses

- **Commercial Naïveté**: Business defeats her. She underprices, negotiates badly, and has been taken advantage of by patrons who could see it.
- **Experimental Excess**: She takes on pieces that cannot be made, and extends the ones that can past every deadline, chasing perfection. Patrons wait, and sometimes money runs short.
- **Emotional Sensitivity and Self-Doubt**: She is invested in every piece and bruises easily. A harsh judgment can put her into weeks of doubt, and the guild's rivalries wear on her.
- **Limited Combat Capability**: No training with weapons. She can defend herself a little and would be in trouble in a real fight.
- **Distraction from Detail Work**: New ideas pull her away from finishing old ones, and projects lie half done while she chases the next.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Royal Family of Amradad**: Several members have commissioned her, and her work is in the royal treasury. It has raised her standing a great deal.
- **Master Dhavani**: Semi-retired, still her most trusted adviser, still commissioning a personal piece now and then and talking her up to the collectors she knows.
- **The Jewellers' Guild**: Commissions her for official pieces and for exhibition work, though she keeps her own shop.
- **Wealthy Collectors and Merchants**: A growing circle who want her designs for themselves or as gifts and will pay for them.

### Enemies

- **Master Farrokh, Conservative Guild Master**: Senior in the Jewellers' Guild and the traditionalists' spokesman, who considers her experiments disrespectful, has quietly opposed her rise, and says her work lacks a classical foundation. They have never quarreled openly. Everyone can feel it.

### Affiliations

- **The Jewellers' Guild of Amradad**: Full member and recognized master, in some tension with its conservative wing.
- **The Artisans' Collective**: Master craftspeople across several trades — sculptors, weavers, potters — who support each other's experiments and share ideas.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Mysterious Commission**: A scholar and collector has commissioned a piece to exact symbolic specifications, hinted that it will be used in esoteric practice, and offered a great deal for it. As she works, the symbols have started to look to her like real magic, and the purpose like a dangerous one. It is the largest commission of her life.

2. **The Stolen Masterpiece**: Her best work — two years of it, the piece that shows what she can do — has been stolen from an exhibition by professionals working for a buyer. She wants it back. The trail may end with a foreign collector, or with someone who wanted a piece of evidence out of sight, or in something larger involving magical objects and politics.

3. **The Guild's Crisis**: Master Farrokh's faction has proposed new guild standards that would make her kind of work impossible, on the grounds that novelty without a classical foundation debases the craft. She has been asked to answer — to make a piece that shows her methods honor the old principles — and, if the guild will not accept it, to decide whether she can stay.

4. **The Collector's Obsession**: A powerful foreign noble is buying every piece of hers he can find and has offered her an exclusive contract as his personal artist, with resources beyond anything she has. It would cut her off from Amradad and from choosing her own work. There are signs that he is assembling her pieces for a working of some kind, and that the pieces are components.

5. **The Apprentice's Betrayal**: She took on a gifted, troubled apprentice from a bad home and treated the young woman like a sister. The apprentice was working for a rival — or for Farrokh's faction — and has been passing on her techniques and designs. Paryan is grieving the trust more than the work, and cannot decide whether to go to the law or to try to save the girl, who was used.

6. **The Magical Artifact**: A stone supplied for a noble commission, described to her as rare but ordinary, is not ordinary. It answers her touch, and her designs for it have become vivid past anything she has drawn before, as if the stone were choosing. She does not know whether she is being guided or fooling herself, and she has not told the family who gave it to her.

7. **The Master's Final Work**: Dhavani is dying, and has asked her for a last piece: a memorial that holds something of Dhavani herself — ashes set like stone, or bone worked into the design. It means everything to both of them, and it breaks guild standards and probably the law, and the materials it needs are not the kind a jeweler usually buys.
