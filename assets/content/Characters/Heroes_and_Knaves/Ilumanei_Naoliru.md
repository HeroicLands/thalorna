# Appearance {#appearance}

![[ilmnnlr|Ilumanei Naoliru]]{float=top-left}

Ilumanei stands 6'0" tall with a medium build. He has deep brown skin, dark close-cropped black hair, and brown eyes. His features include an oval face, a narrow nose, well-defined lips, deep brows, and prominent cheeks. He has a scar on his chin.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Early Life

Ilumanei was born in a poor street behind the quay of [[place-vashuran|Vashurán]], the port of [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] where the Tarûzelian captains trade, the son of a dock worker and a laundress. His parents saw that he was clever and scraped together every coin they could spare to send him to a temple school in the foreign quarter, where he learned his letters and his numbers. The other pupils came from merchant families; Ilumanei outworked all of them, and mastered five scripts in use on the quays—the island's own, the foreigners' and the merchant hands between them. At twelve his teachers sent him to a master scribe, **Vaelumo the Precise**, who took him as an apprentice.

### Training Under Vaelumo

Vaelumo was hard. For seven years Ilumanei copied texts, mixed inks, prepared parchment and learned the forms of contract and record, and Vaelumo tore up whole pages for a single faulty letter and made him write them again long after dark. The old man was not cruel: he was preparing his pupil for a trade in which a document binds, and one mistake can ruin a family or lose it a claim. When Ilumanei became a journeyman at nineteen, Vaelumo recommended him to the **High Registry of the Kalihara Confederation**, the register of contracts kept at the wardens' hall in [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] for the dealings of all four ports.

### The Registry Years

For fifteen years Ilumanei recorded everything that passed between the foreign quarters and the port Kaliharans: cargo contracts and partnerships, marriages between settled foreign families and port households, debts, disputes and the letters of credit the merchant houses honor among themselves. He became known for his accuracy, his elegant hand and his readiness to help poor families set down their claims properly. The dockhands and laundresses loved him for it, and the great merchants thought him presumptuous for treating a porter's contract with the care he gave a merchant-prince's.

### Present Circumstances

At thirty-four Ilumanei left the Registry to work for himself, which surprised everyone who thought him settled. He wanted to work for the people who needed him most. He keeps a small office in a disused temple archive in Vashurán, where porters, boatmen, craftsmen, settled foreign families and merchants come to have their documents drawn up, witnessed and sealed. Several evenings a week he teaches young people the craft, hoping to raise a generation of honest record-keepers. He keeps his standing in the Scribes' Guild, and the Registry still consults him in private, though he refuses any post that would oblige him to favor the rich.

## Psyche

### Personality

Ilumanei is decent to the bone, so much so that harder people take it for naivety. He treats everyone who comes through his door with the same courtesy, whether it is a laundress with a marriage agreement or a factor with a partnership worth a cargo. His warmth is real: he remembers clients from years ago and asks after their children, he advises them on fair terms and warns them of loopholes, and he has refused payment from people being cheated when he could not talk them into better terms.

The same feeling for people, joined to a quick and inventive mind, makes him a fine finder of compromises where nobody is shouting. He is less good where somebody is. He dislikes asserting himself, and in a hard bargain he gives ground he should not. Once he has decided what is fair, though, he will not move, and he says so with regret instead of anger, which forceful people mistake for weakness.

He is tougher than he looks. The scar on his chin dates from his youth as a quayside brawler, before he found what the written word could do. He cannot be bullied, and he will never start a fight. Under his mildness is a conviction: that clear records and plain speech can settle most of the world's troubles, and that violence is nearly always what happens when they fail.

### Motivation

Ilumanei believes the written word protects the weak. He has seen a careful contract save a family's boat, a proper record stop a swindle and an honest register give a porter a voice against a merchant-prince. He wants a port where the poorest boatman can prove what is his, where a woman's word carries the weight of a sealed contract, and where agreements are kept because they are clearly written and honestly witnessed. He works hardest for those who can pay least, often taking his fee in goods or labor, and he hopes his pupils will carry his principles on.

### Strengths

- **Languages and scripts**: he reads, writes and speaks the island's tongue, the harbor pidgin and several foreign languages, and knows how to make words mean exactly one thing.
- **Perception and sympathy**: he understands what a client needs from a document before the client can say it, and he can tell when someone is lying or being led.
- **Reasoning**: he solves tangled problems of contract with wording every party can accept and no court can fault.
- **Persuasion**: he explains hard points simply and mediates disputes with grace.
- **Practical skill**: he cooks, knows his herbs and can treat a fever, and his clients get advice that reaches past the page.

### Weaknesses

- **No head for business**: he cannot refuse an unreasonable request or put profit first, so he is always short of money, and clients talk him into working for a pittance.
- **Weakness of body**: he is not strong or quick, and against violence he needs others to protect him.
- **Trust**: clever deceivers can work on his sense of fairness, and he assumes good faith where there is none.
- **Distraction**: outside his work he lacks discipline, and his clients' troubles pull him from tasks that matter more.
- **No fighter**: he can throw, shoot and swing a blade passably, and his temper makes him avoid fighting whenever he can.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Kalihara Confederation's High Registry**: the Registry still brings him its delicate and difficult work, in private, and takes care that his good name stays clean.
- **Merchants' and Traders' Guild**: several powerful merchants of the ports hire him for their contracts. They pay well, and he annoys them by insisting on terms fair to both sides.
- **Village Councils and Local Magistrates**: the quarter councils of the smaller ports and the wardens' magistrates at Nalurithan and Telvári hire him to record disputes and witness agreements, and they guard his reputation in their towns.
- **Poor Farmers and Craftspeople**: the porters, boatmen and craftsmen of the quays pay him little and love him most, and would stand between him and any threat.

### Enemies

- **A foreign merchant**: a trader whose business Ilumanei refused after finding that he used contracts to defraud partners who could not read them. The merchant spreads word that Ilumanei's documents are unreliable and that he favors the poor.
- **Guild Master Noravei**: the master of the Scribes' Guild, who grows rich on high fees from wealthy clients and discourages scribes from working for the poor. Ilumanei's success outside his control is a standing rebuke, and Noravei means to end it.

### Affiliations

- [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]]: a craftsman's standing among the port Kaliharans. He is a member of the port's Scribes' Guild, upholding its standards and resisting its slide toward serving only those who can pay.
- [[affiliation-florania|Faith of Flórania]]: he keeps the faith of the Nurturer that he learned in the temple school, and his office stands in the old archive of her temple, among the Temple Archive Cooperative of scholars who keep its records.
- **Free Scribes' Alliance**: a loose fellowship of scribes who have left the guild or the Registry to work for themselves, who share methods and help each other with hard commissions.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Forged Inheritance**: A young widow of a foreign family long settled in Vashurán brings Ilumanei her marriage contract, which he drew up and witnessed fifteen years ago. Her husband's brother now calls it a forgery, to take the house, the boat and the warehouse share that should be hers. Ilumanei must prove the document genuine and find who tampered with the copies, and the trail leads toward Guild Master Noravei and a trade in forged records for clients who want an inconvenient contract undone.
2. **The Secret Documents**: A dying scholar brings Ilumanei a bundle of documents in a script older than any writing the island uses, which tell of a people who came before the Kaliharans and of works they left in the interior that are failing. The scholar dies of poison before he can say more. Ilumanei can hide the bundle, give it to the wardens and see it vanish, or try to read it himself, and if he reads it, he learns why the custodians of the interior would want it buried, and finds people already searching for him.
3. **The Witness to Murder**: From his office window Ilumanei sees a high official of the port kill a rival whose death suits several powerful factions. Everyone from the harbor-warden to hired knives now wants his testimony, some true, some false, some silenced for good. He must decide whether to set down what he saw in a sealed statement and live with what follows.
4. **The Underground Records**: Ilumanei learns that beneath the High Registry lies a hidden archive of records the councils have kept secret for centuries: betrayals, murders and bargains that would shake the island's trust in its own government. Loyalists want him to destroy them and others want them read aloud on the quay, and among the papers is evidence that Vaelumo the Precise had a hand in the original concealment.
5. **The Apprentice's Destiny**: A gifted street orphan, **Ruvaneli**, begs Ilumanei to teach him, and Ilumanei takes him on with delight. In time he discovers that Noravei planted the boy to learn his methods and steal his clients. But Ruvaneli has come to love his master and is torn by his task, and Ilumanei must find a way to reach him and help him choose honesty over Noravei's promises.
