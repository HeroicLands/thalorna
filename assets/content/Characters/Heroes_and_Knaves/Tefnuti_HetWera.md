# Appearance {#appearance}

![[tefnuthtwr|Tefnutî Het'Wera]]{float: top-left}

Tefnutî stands 5'10" tall with a medium build. He has warm amber skin, gray hair, and brown eyes. His features include an oval face, a noble nose, a smooth jawline, an expressive mouth, fine brows, and angular cheeks.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Tefnutî was born into a family of toymakers in the Empire of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]], in a tradition stretching back seven generations. His great-grandfather had been commissioned by an imperial prince to create mechanical toys of extraordinary ingenuity, work that established the family's reputation for invention and precision. Tefnutî's own father, Hetmosis, had expanded the family trade significantly, introducing mechanisms of increasing complexity while maintaining a commitment to beauty and craftsmanship.

From earliest childhood, Tefnutî was trained in the family traditions. Unlike some toymakers who view their work as frivolous entertainment, the Het'Wera family understood that toys were miniature worlds—opportunities to teach mechanics, aesthetics, and imagination. Tefnutî exceeded even his father's capabilities, developing new techniques for creating clockwork mechanisms of breathtaking intricacy. By his thirties, his toys were so much in demand among wealthy families and collectors that his wait list extended years into the future.

However, beginning in his forties, Tefnutî became increasingly troubled by what he perceived as the cheapening of the craft. Simpler toys turned out in bulk began undercutting the market, and younger toymakers enthusiastically adopted new materials and methods that Tefnutî viewed as compromising quality. Rather than adapt, he doubled down on his traditional techniques and began work on increasingly elaborate creations that became less toys and more works of engineering art. This shift in focus—away from practical toys children could play with and toward elaborate mechanical sculptures meant for display—has simultaneously enhanced his prestige among collectors and left him increasingly isolated from the rest of the toymaking trade.

Now at fifty-one, Tefnutî maintains a prestigious workshop in the capital, where he continues to produce extraordinary works. However, the constant pressure from younger craftsmen and the changing market has made him bitter and defensive about his methods.

## Psyche

### Personality

Tefnutî's character is full of contradictions. In matters of craftsmanship, he is confident to the point of arrogance—he knows his work is extraordinary and has no patience for criticism of his techniques. Yet in social situations, he becomes almost diffident, preferring the company of machines and mechanisms to people. He is capable of surprising warmth with those who show real respect for craftsmanship, but he is swift to become cold and dismissive toward those he judges as lacking proper appreciation.

He can be philosophically whimsical, expounding at length about the nature of time, motion, and mechanical perfection, but these flights of fancy are often followed by sharp criticism of others' inadequate understanding. He takes real pleasure in the work itself—the careful fitting of gears, the testing of mechanisms—but increasingly displays impatience with clients, viewing most patrons as interruptions to his craft rather than reasons for it.

### Motivation

Tefnutî's primary motivation is the creation of mechanical perfection—toys that function flawlessly, that are beautifully proportioned, and that will survive intact for generations. Underlying this is a fear: that in his absence, the techniques that took seven generations to develop will be lost to cheaper, faster, inferior methods. He works for commission and, beyond that, to build a legacy, creating works that will prove the superiority of traditional craftsmanship long after he is gone.

### Strengths

- **Extraordinary Mechanical Aptitude**: His lockcraft, with five specializations, combined with his creativity, reasoning, drawing, and music, allows him to conceive and execute mechanisms of astonishing complexity. Few living craftsmen can match his precision with clockwork.

- **Multi-Disciplinary Mastery**: He possesses significant abilities across woodworking, metalcraft, ceramics, and textile craft. This allows him to create complete works integrating multiple materials and techniques as one whole.

- **Meticulous Attention to Detail**: His sharp perception and dexterity allow him to work at scales and tolerances that would challenge lesser craftsmen. He can create gear teeth so fine they require magnification to observe clearly.

- **Inventor's Mind**: His creativity and reasoning combine to let him devise new solutions to mechanical problems while keeping to the traditional approach as a whole.

- **Profound Understanding of Motion**: His knowledge of how mechanisms function at deep levels allows him to design toys and sculptures that perform with seemingly impossible fluidity and precision.

### Weaknesses

- **Intolerance of Modern Methods**: His creativity, while extraordinary for design, is constrained by his unwillingness to experiment with new materials or methods. He views any deviation from traditional methods as inherent compromise, blinding him to potential improvements.

- **Poor Business Acumen**: His modest eloquence and charm make him a poor negotiator and a worse salesman. He struggles to explain the value of his work to potential patrons and often prices his commissions far below what the market would bear, then resents that he is undercompensated.

- **Social Isolation**: His endurance for company is limited. Long client meetings drain him, and he frequently offends patrons with brusque dismissals. This has limited his ability to build a strong body of patrons despite his renown.

- **Perfectionism Paralysis**: His desire for mechanical perfection sometimes prevents him from completing works. He has been known to spend months perfecting small components that patrons already considered adequate, delaying projects and increasing costs.

- **Declining Physical Capability**: His strength and endurance are declining with age, and his eyesight, while still precise for close work, deteriorates if forced to work for extended periods, requiring him to take more frequent breaks.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Lady Sekhmet Ashkahar**: An elderly widow of great wealth and refined taste who has become Tefnutî's primary patron over the past decade. She commissions elaborate mechanical sculptures for her private collection and appreciates his perfectionism as an expression of respect for craft itself.

- **Scholar-Collector Thaddeus Chronos**: A historian and antiquarian who employs Tefnutî to create accurate mechanical reproductions of ancient devices based on historical records and archaeological findings. Their collaboration has produced some of Tefnutî's most celebrated works.

- **the Imperial Treasury**: Tefnutî has several standing commissions from the imperial court to create elaborate gifts for diplomatic purposes and state ceremonies. These are highly visible works that enhance his reputation significantly.

- **Merchant Prince Kassor the Learned**: A collector of mechanical curiosities who maintains a private museum and commissions elaborate pieces specifically for display. Kassor pays handsomely and allows Tefnutî complete creative freedom, though he can be demanding about timelines.

### Enemies

- **Toymaker Mavrik the Innovator**: A successful younger toymaker who has built wealth by turning out toys in bulk with newer materials that Tefnutî views as inferior. Mavrik has directly challenged Tefnutî's supremacy by advertising his toys as "superior because they are affordable" and has actively poached several of Tefnutî's potential clients. Mavrik sees Tefnutî as an arrogant relic and publicly questions whether his work is worth the extraordinary cost.

- **Imperial Tax Assessor Renek Graystone**: A bureaucrat who has begun examining Tefnutî's finances closely, questioning how commissions that should be financially ruinous result in Tefnutî's continued wealth. Renek suspects hidden income or tax evasion and has become increasingly adversarial in his investigations.

### Affiliations

- **the Toymakers' Guild of Ta'Kheperu**: Tefnutî holds membership and retains significant prestige within the guild, though he frequently clashes with guild leadership over standards and methods. He serves on the guild's standards committee, where he advocates for stricter quality requirements.

- **the Het'Wera Craftsman Lineage**: An informal family legacy and tradition spanning seven generations, of which Tefnutî is the current master. He has become increasingly protective of family techniques and secretive about his methods.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Rival's Theft**: Tefnutî discovers that his designs—exact reproductions of mechanisms and aesthetic approaches he developed—are being produced by Mavrik in bulk. Someone has stolen detailed plans or documented his work in sufficient detail that his unique approaches can be copied. Tefnutî must determine who is responsible, how extensive the theft is, and how to prove the designs are his under a law that gives craftsmen no such protection.

2. **The Mechanical Anomaly**: A patron brings Tefnutî a broken mechanical device—not his creation—from an ancient ruin, asking him to restore it. As he examines it, he becomes disturbed: the mechanisms are far too advanced for any known ancient civilization, and they use principles he doesn't fully understand. The patron warns him not to discuss the device with anyone, but Tefnutî becomes obsessed with understanding how it was created, potentially violating his patron's confidence to seek answers.

3. **The Apprentice Prodigy**: A young, brilliant craftsperson appears seeking apprenticeship with Tefnutî, demonstrating talent that exceeds anyone he's encountered in decades. However, Tefnutî quickly discovers the apprentice already possesses knowledge of advanced techniques—knowledge that should be impossible to acquire outside his family training. He must determine where this knowledge came from and whether accepting this apprentice will compromise seven generations of family secrets.

4. **The Cursed Commission**: A mysterious patron commissions an extraordinarily elaborate mechanical sculpture with very specific requirements that Tefnutî finds aesthetically unsatisfying but technically compelling. As he works, strange things begin occurring—pieces fit together incorrectly, mechanisms behave unpredictably, and workers in his shop report disturbing dreams. Tefnutî begins to suspect he is being asked to craft something impossible or actively harmful, but the patron has paid a substantial deposit and has significant leverage over him.

5. **The Imperial Inspection Crisis**: The imperial Treasury announces that Tefnutî's extraordinary mechanical gifts to the court will be examined and evaluated by foreign experts to potentially commission reproductions for diplomatic purposes. This threatens to expose Tefnutî's techniques to scrutiny and potential copying. Simultaneously, he receives a private message from the inspector claiming to possess documentary evidence of "unauthorized" imperial work—works he created but which cannot be publicly acknowledged. Someone is attempting to coerce him into cooperation through blackmail.
