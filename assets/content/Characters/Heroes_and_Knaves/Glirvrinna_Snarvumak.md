# Appearance {#appearance}

![[glrvrnstrmrt|Glirvrinna Snarvumak]]{float=top-left}

Glirvrinna stands 5'7" tall with a light build. She has pale fair skin, blonde hair, and green eyes. Her features include an oval face, a narrow nose, a rounded chin, rosy lips, and light brows.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Glirvrinna** was born in the [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Kingdom of Malagna]] to **[[being-mirvirsnarvu|Mirvir Snarvumak]]**, a skilled tentmaker of considerable reputation, and [[being-ilthyra|Ilthira]], a woman known for her excellence in textile dyeing and pattern design. From infancy, she was surrounded by fabrics, dyes, and the rhythmic sounds of looms and needles. Where her father Mirvir was practical—designing tents to withstand mountain gales and desert heat—her mother Ilthira was artistic, creating patterns that made functional items beautiful.

Glirvrinna inherited gifts from both parents, but early on demonstrated her mother's inclination toward artistry over pure function. She trained first under her father, learning structure and practical construction, but spent her true passion studying under her mother, discovering the secrets of dyes and the possibilities of decorative design. At twenty-four, Mirvir retired due to arthritis in his hands and left the shop to Glirvrinna on the condition that she maintain both their family's traditions—functional excellence and artistic beauty.

Since then, Glirvrinna has transformed the family shop at [[place-braldheim|Braldheim]] from a utilitarian tentmaker into a workshop celebrated across Malagna and beyond for bespoke, beautiful, and surprisingly durable textiles. Her commissions are sought by nobles, wealthy merchants, and traveling companies that value appearing grand as much as being protected. At thirty-two, she is unmarried by choice—she sees her craft as her life's partner and views most potential suitors as threats to her independence rather than complements to it.

## Psyche

### Personality

Glirvrinna is animated, passionate, and wholly absorbed in her creative work. She experiences conversation as secondary to the world of form, color, and texture; people are interesting primarily as they relate to art. This makes her seem scattered or self-absorbed to those who don't understand her nature, though those who work with her recognize a mind operating at considerable depth, just not on conventional social channels.

She is ambitious in an artistic sense—not seeking power or wealth but recognition that her work equals that of the great painters, sculptors, and craftspeople of the realm. She views each commission as an opportunity to advance her technique and prove that textiles are capable of conveying the same aesthetic sophistication as more "elevated" arts. She can be frustrated by clients who want beauty but refuse to pay the price such artistry demands, or who compromise her vision for practicality.

Despite her artistic temperament, Glirvrinna is surprisingly pragmatic about the business aspects of her work. She maintains scrupulous records, meets deadlines (mostly), and understands that her vision must occasionally bend to client reality. However, her pragmatism has limits—she will refuse work that compromises her principles, losing profitable commissions rather than create something she considers mediocre.

### Motivation

Glirvrinna is driven by a desire to create beauty that endures, to produce work that moves the heart and demonstrates that functional craft and artistic expression can be joined rather than opposed. She also harbors a quiet wound from always being compared to her father—recognized as talented but somehow derivative of his example rather than an original in her own way. She works, in part, to establish that Glirvrinna Snarvumak is an artist in her own standing and not only Mirvir's daughter.

### Strengths

Extraordinary Decorative Technique
: Glirvrinna's embroidery, dyeing, and pattern design are superb. Her work conveys emotion and narrative through purely textile means, creating garments and furnishings that tell stories through color and form.

Color Theory Mastery
: She possesses an intuitive understanding of how colors interact, complement, and transform each other. Her dye work produces hues that seem impossible—shades of green that appear to shift in different light, crimsons that seem to pulse with warmth.

Innovative Design
: While rooted in traditional forms, Glirvrinna's designs push boundaries, incorporating techniques and combinations that are truly novel. Her work frequently becomes copied by lesser artisans attempting to replicate her success.

Steady Production
: Despite her focus on beauty, she has learned to balance artistic ambition with practical schedules. She manages her workshop expertly, maintaining quality while meeting deadlines that would overwhelm less organized makers.

Patron Engagement
: Unlike many artists, Glirvrinna enjoys discussing commissions with clients, translating their desires into artistic visions. She can elevate a vague request into something beautiful through collaborative process.

### Weaknesses

Aesthetic Over Durability
: Her commitment to beauty sometimes comes at the expense of practical longevity. A stunning, richly embroidered tent may sacrifice structural integrity in small ways, or delicate dyes may fade faster than more stable pigments. Clients who use items harshly rather than displaying them sometimes experience disappointment.

Perfectionism Paralysis
: She can become so focused on perfecting one element of a design that projects stall. A single color choice might occupy her for days; a particular stitch pattern might be reworked dozens of times in pursuit of absolute rightness.

Intolerance for Compromise
: When clients request changes that contradict her artistic vision, Glirvrinna struggles to accept them gracefully. She may comply but will do so with visible resentment, damaging client relationships.

Limited Technical Diversity
: Her genius lies in decoration and artistry, but she is less skilled at the structural engineering and advanced construction techniques her father mastered. Complex structural problems cause her frustration.

Financial Naïveté
: She undercharges for her work, not fully valuing her own skill. She has lost money on commissions that consumed more time and materials than initially calculated, reflecting poor business acumen despite her pragmatic inclinations.

## Social

### Patrons

[[being-vraldmylbral|Vraldmil Braldaruk]]
: A wealthy noble with appreciation for fine art and textiles. He has commissioned multiple tapestries and furnishings from Glirvrinna and frequently recommends her work to his peers, providing steady access to high-value commissions.

A Traveling Company of Players
: A prestigious performance group that requires stunning costumes and set dressings. They commission Glirvrinna multiple times yearly and have become her most visible patrons, as her work appears before large audiences.

The Merchant Captains of Gnarthborg
: Successful captains who value appearing prosperous commission elaborate tent pavilions and shipboard furnishings from her workshop. These provide both income and maritime visibility for her work.

The king's daughter [[being-hvarnthyra|Hvarnthira]] of Malagna
: The royal family has begun commissioning items from Glirvrinna, including ceremonial banners and royal furnishings. Though royal patronage is recent, it promises to elevate her status considerably.

### Enemies

The craftsman [Knarvorv the Practical]{.name data-type="being/npc"}
: An older male tentmaker who represents the "function-first" philosophy Glirvrinna's father stood for. Knarvorv publicly criticizes her work as beautiful but impractical and has won contracts she sought by undercutting her prices and emphasizing durability over aesthetics.

[Draskselda the Copyist]{.name data-type="being/npc"}
: A jealous rival artisan who specializes in producing close imitations of Glirvrinna's original designs at lower cost. Draskselda's plagiarism has cost Glirvrinna several clients, though legal action has proven difficult given the custom nature of textile work.

Her Father, Mirvir Snarvumak
: Retired and no longer a direct business threat, Mirvir occasionally makes critical comments about Glirvrinna's prioritization of artistry over durability, implying she is abandoning family traditions.

### Affiliations

The Craftswomen of Braldheim
: Glirvrinna is a leading voice among the district's textile workers, though she remains somewhat isolated due to her focus on textiles rather than more traditionally respected arts like carving or metalwork.

## Plot Hooks

The Trousseau
: The king's daughter [[being-hvarnthyra|Hvarnthira]] of Malagna comes to Glirvrinna's workshop in person and asks for the wedding trousseau and ceremonial garments for her betrothal to a foreign prince. The fee would settle the workshop's future, and the wedding is six months away. The autumn cutting at [[place-braldheim|Braldheim]] needs the camps' tents and hide-coverings in the same months, and the camps' households are her neighbors. Glirvrinna can give the trousseau her whole season and leave the camps to the next-nearest tentmaker, or hold to the camps and tell the princess a smaller piece is all she can promise.

The Boundary Banner
: Two Braldheim households that quarrel over cutting rights in the forest behind the settlement each ask her for a banner to carry to the ting. A banner from the finest hand in the district tells the assembly whose cause Braldheim thinks is just. She can sew for one and be known for it, sew for both and be known for that, or refuse both and lose two households as customers.

The Copyist's Bolt
: Draskselda the Copyist sells a bolt of cloth at [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]] that matches Glirvrinna's design stitch for stitch, at half her price, and a captain has already ordered pavilions from it. A claim at the ting would need witnesses who saw the original drawn, and custom work leaves none. She can bring the claim and risk a ruling against her, undercut the copy with her own labor, or make the next design too difficult for Draskselda to copy.

Knarvorv's Bid
: [Knarvorv the Practical]{.name data-type="being/npc"} bids against her for a shipyard order of tents and awnings for the season's fleet, promising weatherproofing she cannot match without giving up her dyes. Her father Mirvir advises her to take the plainer work. She can take the order at a price that loses money, hold her price and lose it, or win it by making the plain work well, which means doing what she has refused to do.

:::secret {title="What the referee knows"}
The estate of a dead dyer at Braldheim leaves Glirvrinna a chest of formulae for colors that seem to shift with the mood of whoever wears them. The dyer was a mage who set her intentions into the pigments before she died. Cloth from the formulae draws wearers to keep it on, and the colors turn at moments of grief and joy. Glirvrinna can use them in the trousseau and risk what they do to the wearer, destroy the formulae and the one chance at colors no one else can make, or work out what the dead dyer intended.
:::
