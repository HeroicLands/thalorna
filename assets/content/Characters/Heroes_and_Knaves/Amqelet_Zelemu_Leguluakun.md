# Appearance {#appearance}

![[amqltzlmlglq|Amqelet-Zelemu Legulu’akun]]{float=top-left}

Amqelet-Zelemu is a man of 5'9" with dark skin, close-cropped dark hair, a short mustache and goatee, and brown eyes under high arched brows that lift when he is working a sum. His mouth is full and quick to settle into the half-smile of a man who has found an error in someone else's chart. His skin is clear and his hands are ink-stained. He wears a blue robe embroidered with stars over a pale tunic, with a gold chain and a green stone at his chest, and he carries an astrolabe and a quadrant in a padded case, a roll of star charts, a satchel of prophecy scrolls copied from his own readings and a worn book of the sacred texts of Reth'Sa'ar. Incense and offering-grain ride in a small pouch at his belt, and he burns a pinch at every sighting.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Origin and Early Life

Amqelet-Zelemu was born into a respected family of scholars and astrologers in the grand city of [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]], a cultural and scholarly center of the **[[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]**. The Empire, with traditions dating back thousands of years, continues to blend mysticism and scholarly pursuits, with astrology playing a critical role in its governance and daily life. The empire’s priests and astrologers are key advisors to the ruling elite, using celestial signs to predict outcomes of war, trade, and political alliances.

Amqelet-Zelemu, from a young age, showed a rare affinity for reading the stars. His family, who had long served the high courts of Au'Khelathu, saw that he was trained by the greatest scholars and priests of the empire. By the age of 16, he had already earned a reputation for accurate star readings and predictions, and his name spread through the noble circles of Au'Khelathu.

### Astrological Discoveries and Vision

When Amqelet-Zelemu reached his early thirties, he began experiencing disturbing visions during his astrological readings. Unusual alignments of stars and planets foretold the arrival of a significant celestial event, one that could reshape the very balance of power in the known world. After months of intense study, he concluded that this event, which he referred to as “[The Convergence of the Nine Moons]{.name data-type="lore"},” was not a simple astronomical occurrence, but a mystical one with divine consequences.

His readings indicated that this event was tied to the fate of the **[[affiliation-genzetyalgu|Genzet'Yalgu]] (the Court of the Nine Moons)**, the council of the nine most powerful clans in Au'Khelathu. His predictions warned that if the empire did not take action, the Convergence would bring about disaster, either in the form of internal strife or an invasion from external powers. The stars, however, did not reveal the full extent of the threat—only that it was imminent and inevitable.

### The Gar-Au’s Response

Amqelet-Zelemu presented his findings to [[being-garaumeqesxv|Gar-Au Meqes XVI]], who ruled the empire from the capital city of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]. The Gar-Au was a practical woman, but she had learned to trust the warnings of astrologers. Yet, the ambiguity of Amqelet-Zelemu’s predictions frustrated the court. While some powerful lords demanded immediate action, others dismissed it as a misreading of the stars.

Faced with skepticism from the court and the increasing pressure of the imperial council, Amqelet-Zelemu decided he could no longer wait for the empire’s leaders to act. His readings indicated that the truth behind the Convergence could only be found by traveling to key sacred sites scattered throughout the empire, where ancient celestial knowledge was hidden.

### The Sacred Journey

Amqelet-Zelemu embarked on a **pilgrimage** to these ancient locations, which were sacred to the early astrologers of Au'Khelathu. His journey would take him deep into the deserts of [[place-beshakan|Beshakan]], across the [[place-venariasea|Venaria Sea]], and through forgotten ruins where ancient star maps were carved into the stones by the earliest astronomers. He lives at [[place-amqelulegez|Amqelu-Legez]] between journeys and set out from its wharves.

His first stop was [[place-anlaghura|Anlaghura]], an ancient monolithic obelisk standing in the middle of the [[place-targavalley|Targa River Valley]]. There, Amqelet-Zelemu discovered fragments of a lost prophecy inscribed in ancient runes, pointing him to a mountain shrine in the highlands of [[place-chenosolis|Chenosolis]], where the oldest records of the empire’s astrologers were kept.

### Mystical Challenges and Allies

Amqelet-Zelemu’s journey is fraught with challenges. Rival astrologers and priests who either do not believe in the prophecy or seek to use the event for their own ends send agents to stop him. His journey into the ruins also reveals ancient powers long forgotten—spirits of the desert, creatures bound to the stars, and followers of ancient cults who view the Convergence as an opportunity to usher in a new age of chaos.

Despite these dangers, Amqelet-Zelemu finds unlikely allies along his path. He meets a wandering desert ranger named **[[being-atenhershr|Atazkharu Oroqidur]], known as “The Silent Jackal,”** who, though initially skeptical of the astrologer’s quest, pledges to protect him after witnessing a celestial omen. Amqelet-Zelemu also encounters a scholar, **[[being-legiretilmyz|Legiret Ilmy-Za]]**, and an exiled priest, **[[being-legezaqu|Legez Aqu]] Anva**, who together join him in his quest to unlock the true power of the Convergence.

### End Goal

Amqelet-Zelemu seeks to reach the [[place-lutzelutha|Lut-Zelutha]], an ancient site hidden deep within the **Sands of [[place-lareshu|Lareshu]]**, where the final clue to stopping or mitigating the effects of the Convergence can be found. However, time is against him, as the stars grow ever closer to alignment, and forces—both seen and unseen—move to either stop him or claim the celestial power for themselves.

His journey ends in a confrontation with the empire's ruling elite, who understand his predictions only when it is too late to act.

## Psyche

## Motivations and Personality

Amqelet-Zelemu is driven by a deep duty to the stars and his empire. Though he is soft-spoken and contemplative, he harbors a strong will and fierce dedication to uncovering the truth behind the celestial signs. He believes that the stars do not lie and that his role is to observe and then to act on the guidance they provide, even if it means challenging the empire’s most powerful figures.

His journey is one of discovery and of self-knowledge. Along the way, Amqelet-Zelemu grapples with his faith in the stars and the responsibility that comes with interpreting their signs. He sometimes questions whether the Convergence is truly inevitable or if his interpretations are flawed, a weight that grows heavier with every step of his journey.

## Plot Hooks

The Reading for the Wharves
: The granary-keepers of Anlagh-Zetun send to the port for the astrologer who calls himself the best reader of the stars on the river, because two low floods have left the stores thin and the Halzi'a wants a reading of the next inundation before the Measure is proclaimed. Amqelet-Zelemu's charts say the flood will come high and that something worse will come with it. If he says the first half only, the granaries open and the stores run down; if he says both, he is asked to prove the second half before the Halzi'a's clerks.

A Berth for a Branded Man
: Barges leave Amqelu-Legez only once the load is weighed and sealed, and the wharves talk. Legez Aqu is under sentence of death in the selat of Khelunu, and word of a branded priest on a barge travels upriver faster than the barge. Amqelet-Zelemu needs the river road to the first shrine and has no time for the overland one. He asks the party to find a boatman who will carry Legez as a hired hand and keep quiet, or to carry the company by another way, before the Convergence closes the window he has calculated.

The Altered Charts
: A scribe of the tally-bureau, copying an old chart for him, shows him two versions of the same alignment that differ by one conjunction, and the older copy is the one whose seal is broken. Someone has rewritten the temple libraries' record of the Nine Moons. The only other copies sit in the libraries of Galezkara and of Khelunu, and the scribes who tend them answer to the same high priests who dismissed his warning. He cannot ask for them openly.

The Court's Answer
: A courier from the palace of the Gar-Au calls at the wharf with a sealed letter. The court's own astrologers demand his readings for review and name a date to bring them. If he hands over his only copies, the court owns the Convergence and he cannot continue. If he keeps them, he is a man who refused the throne, and the lords who demanded action and the lords who dismissed him find the same reason to be rid of him.

:::secret {title="What the Game Master knows"}
The Convergence is real, and it falls on the day the nine clans of the Court of the Nine Moons confirm their seats. One clan altered the oldest charts so that the date reads as a harvest omen and not as the night its seat is struck from the list. The rival astrologers and the agents who follow him are in that clan's pay.

The final clue at Lut-Zelutha in the sands of Lareshu is a star-map carved before the Court existed, and it names the clan. Whoever carries the original to Galezkara unseats it; whoever destroys it keeps the seat.
:::
