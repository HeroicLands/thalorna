# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Training:** Tvurnthann was born the youngest son of a merchant-seal-hunting family in [[place-thrunborg|Thrunborg]], the capital of [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]]. Groomed initially for a life of commerce and the whaling trade, his path changed irrevocably when he manifested the shamanic gift at age twelve—the ability to see and commune with spirits in the way his people's velvur have done for centuries. His family recognized the talent and brought him to Velva [Aldurhildur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the most respected spirit-worker in Thrunborg, to learn the ancient traditions of seid and spirit-binding. The apprenticeship was rigorous and transformative, grounding him in the sacred knowledge of the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian Pantheon]] and teaching him to navigate the realms of spirit. Yet it also marked him as ergi—unmanly by strict [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] standards—a stigma that would follow him throughout his life no matter how much power and knowledge he accumulated.

**Years of Exile and Hiding:** As Tvurnthann grew into manhood, the weight of his ergi status became increasingly unbearable. In Vithgard, male spirit-workers—practitioners of the sacred arts that were historically dominated by women—existed in a state of permanent cultural ambiguity and shame. Though his people needed his gifts as a seer and spirit-walker, they despised him for the gender transgression that seid represented. At thirty, desperate to escape this impossible contradiction, Tvurnthann traveled south to seek recognition in the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]]. There he studied within the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]]'s chapterhouse, attempting to reframe his spirit-work into the formal language of the [[affiliation-zemorhodhis|Zemor Hodhis]] and the [[affiliation-neumenos|Neumenos]] fellowship (the Fellowship of Spirit). He became respected as a scholar of spirit-binding and ancient ritual traditions, publishing several influential treatises that claimed to systematize the shamanic knowledge of the north into academic [[lore-vylarianclt|Vilérian]] frameworks. Yet he never truly belonged in the Epistémion (the Academy) either—his approach was too rooted in Nordmen traditions, too mystical and intuitive, too shaped by the lived experience of communion with spirits rather than theoretical classification.

**Current Path:** Now in his twilight years, Tvurnthann has returned to Vithgard, seeking some form of peace with his origins. He maintains a private dwelling on the northern outskirts of Thrunborg, where he trains selected younger spirit-workers—those with shamanic gifts but no family connections to the traditional velvur orders—in the synthesis of Nordmen sacred knowledge and the academic frameworks he learned in the south. Yet he lives in a state of contradiction: he is too Nordmen for the Epistémion, too academic for the traditional velvur, and too ergi for mainstream Vithgard society. His paranoia about discovery has become deeply personal and historically rooted. He fears that the Epistémion's ambitions to regulate all magical practice will eventually reach into Vithgard and criminalize the sacred traditions his people have preserved for millennia. He also fears that traditional Nordmen society will reject him entirely if his gender transgression becomes too widely known. More fundamentally, he fears that he has betrayed his own traditions by trying to translate them into a foreign language, and that his attempt to reconcile two irreconcilable ways of understanding magic has produced only a hollow synthesis that serves neither.

## Psyche

### Personality

Tvurnthann is a study in contradictions—simultaneously eager to share sacred knowledge with worthy students and fiercely protective of traditions he fears are being erased by forces beyond his control. His paranoia, while occasionally tiresome to those around him, stems from cultural and personal sources: the historical threat of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] (the Arcane Order) reaching into independent magical traditions, the erasure of Nordmen shamanic practice under the weight of foreign academic frameworks, and his own shame about his ergi status in a culture that despises gender transgression. He speaks slowly and deliberately, often pausing mid-sentence as though wrestling with what he can safely reveal, and his conversations frequently veer into cryptic warnings about "those who would reduce sacred mystery to academic formula" and "the creeping poison of foreign institutions that devour local knowledge."

Despite his suspicious nature, Tvurnthann possesses surprising warmth toward those he trusts—particularly younger spirit-workers who show the same ergi traits he bears, giving them what acceptance he can within his own conflicted framework. With his closest students, he becomes almost grandfatherly, his gray eyes warm as he teaches the sacred knowledge of spirit-communion and spins stories of his shamanic journeys. But his emotional core is scarred by irreconcilable contradictions: he loved Velva Aldurhildur as the closest thing he had to a mother figure, yet her teachings also locked him into the very gender transgression that has haunted him. He sought respect in the Epistémion, yet felt himself betraying his traditions with every lecture. He is torn between a visceral need to protect sacred Nordmen knowledge from external corruption and an intellectual understanding that all cultures must evolve and adapt. This conflict has never been resolved, only buried under layers of defensive paranoia.

### Motivation

Tvurnthann is driven by three interlocking and contradictory motivations: first, the desire to preserve the sacred Nordmen traditions of spirit-work and shamanism—knowledge he fears will be lost if external pressures (particularly from the Epistémion and the Order) continue to increase. Second, the compulsion to prevent that same knowledge from being weaponized, codified, or drained of its spiritual meaning by those who would transform sacred mystery into academic system. Third, though he rarely acknowledges this even to himself, a desperate hope that by training younger spirit-workers—giving them what cultural acceptance and wisdom he can offer—he might somehow heal the wound in himself, might model a way of being ergi that Vithgard society could one day accept rather than merely tolerate. Yet this third motivation directly contradicts the first two, creating an internal paralysis: if he successfully normalizes gender-transgressive spirit-workers, he weakens the cultural boundaries that protect Nordmen traditions; if he protects those traditions fiercely, he perpetuates the shame that destroyed his own life.

: He carries no weapons and no armor beyond enchanted robes; if his magic is suppressed, he becomes nearly defenseless.

## Social

### Patrons & Connections

Velva Aldurhildur (deceased)
: Though long dead, Tvurnthann's former teacher remains his spiritual anchor and the source of both his greatest gifts and his deepest wounds. She recognized his shamanic talent and trained him in the sacred traditions, yet also locked him irrevocably into the ergi status that would define his suffering. He has never freed himself from her teachings, and often asks himself "What would the Velva counsel?" when facing decisions about which traditions to protect and which to transform.

[Knirvorv Ironsmith]{.name data-type="being/npc"} (local ally)
: A respected shipwright and sealing captain in Thrunborg who is one of the few people in his community who accepts Tvurnthann without shame, valuing his counsel and his gifts as a seer. Their friendship is deep but carefully hidden—publicly, Knirvorv shows Tvurnthann the same cool distance that most Vithgard men do toward ergi practitioners.

Scholar-Marendos [Zenotha Tholmaris]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Epistémion (distant connection)
: An elderly member of the Zulen Neumenos who once mentored Tvurnthann and remains his most sympathetic contact within the Epistémion. They correspond rarely and carefully, but she has provided him with information about the Order's expanding interest in regulating shamanic practices in the northern kingdoms.

### Enemies & Threats

Traditional Velvur Hierarchy
: The established order of female spirit-workers in Vithgard views Tvurnthann as an anomaly and a threat. His attempt to formalize and teach shamanic practice to other young people—particularly other ergi individuals—is seen as both a dilution of sacred traditions and an unacceptable challenge to the velvur's exclusive authority. Whispers suggest that some of the elder velvur have considered taking formal action to suppress his teaching.

Thordimalon Expansion
: Tvurnthann's greatest institutional fear is the Order's slow expansion northward. He has learned through fragmentary reports that the Order's agents are asking questions about Vithgard's shamanic traditions, attempting to catalog and regulate independent magical practitioners. If the Order ever establishes a formal presence in Thrunborg, his entire way of life becomes legally impossible.

Nordmen Cultural Rejection
: More personally dangerous than external institutions is the possibility of total social ostracism within his own people. His ergi status is already a stigma; if he becomes known as someone actively promoting gender-transgressive practice, he risks becoming not merely tolerated but openly despised—or worse, driven into exile from his homeland.

His Own Irreconcilable Contradictions
: Most dangerous of all is Tvurnthann's internal conflict between protecting Nordmen traditions and opening them to change, between hiding his ergi nature and embracing it, between the sacred knowledge he learned from Velva Aldurhildur and the academic frameworks he learned in the south. This internal war is far more destructive than any external enemy, and it manifests as paranoia, indecision, and the slow erosion of his ability to act decisively toward any goal.

### Affiliations

Epistémion (the Academy of Knowledge) (Distant & Precarious)
: Tvurnthann maintains a loose affiliation with the Epistémion, having studied there decades ago. However, his formal credentials are suspect, his current activities are at best tolerated and at worst unknown to Epistémion leadership, and his reputation as a "provincial spirit-worker" rather than a true _marendos_ (master) means he has little institutional standing. The Epistémion sees his work as ethnographic curiosity rather than legitimate arcane scholarship.

Vithgard Community (Complicated & Hidden)
: Tvurnthann is both embedded in and alienated from Thrunborg society. The community needs his shamanic gifts—his ability to divine the future, to commune with spirits, to provide counsel on matters spiritual. Yet this same need comes paired with cultural shame around his ergi status. Publicly, respectable people maintain distance from him; privately, they seek his counsel and pay for his services.

The Informal Network of Northern Spirit-Workers (Self-Imposed Isolation)
: Tvurnthann once moved in circles with other shamanic practitioners—velvur, seers, and priests of the faiths of the Asguardian Pantheon. But his attempt to formalize and teach shamanic practice, and his gender transgression, have increasingly isolated him. He is no longer fully part of this community, though he desperately wishes to protect it from external threats.

## Plot Hooks

The Envoy at the Dark
: Tvurnthann learns through Zenotha Tholmaris of the Epistémion that Thordimalon agents are cataloging spirit-workers in the northern kingdoms, and an envoy will reach [[place-thrunborg|Thrunborg]] when the hersvaldar come within its walls for the long dark. The envoy's list would make his teaching illegal. He can warn the other spirit-workers and be seen as their leader, hide his students, or meet the envoy himself and risk being exposed.

The Student at the Door
: One of his most promising students begins to show the same ergi nature he carries and is caught between the joy of spirit-work and the weight of contempt. Tvurnthann sees himself in the student and wants to protect them. The velvur who keep the town's seasonal portents want the student's practice stopped. He can shelter and teach the student openly, which brings the velvur to his door, or keep the student hidden and unprotected.

The Sealed Message
: A young velva from a remote community in northern Vithgard brings Tvurnthann a sealed message written decades ago by Velva Aldurhildur, meant for him alone. It holds knowledge she never taught him and her reasons for never acknowledging him. He is desperate to read it and afraid of what it says. He can read it with the young velva present, read it alone and refuse to say what it holds, or burn it.

The Dying Velva's Price
: An elderly velva in a far northern settlement, one of the last keepers of the deepest mysteries, wants to pass them on before she dies. She will teach him only if he agrees to mentor her apprentice, a young ergi spirit-worker more ostracized than he is. The training would take months or years in the far north, away from his students and the house at Thrunborg. He can go and return changed, or stay and let the knowledge die.

:::secret {title="What the referee knows"}
In the far north of Vithgard the boundary between the spirit world and the material world is becoming unstable. Spirits manifest often and aggressively, and the dead do not rest. Tvurnthann at first suspects a magical attack. The deeper cause is that the velvur, in suppressing male spirit-workers, have disrupted a balance between genders and between old and new practice that the tradition must keep.
:::
