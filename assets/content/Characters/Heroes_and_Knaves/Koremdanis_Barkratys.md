# Appearance {#appearance}

![[krmdnsbrkrtys|Korêmdânis Bárkrâtys]]{float: top-left}

Six feet even, 6'0" in her boots, and built for reach rather than weight. Light olive skin, auburn hair, amber eyes, an oblong face with a prominent nose, a narrow chin, heavy brows, angular cheeks and lips that are usually slightly pursed. A dragonfly is tattooed on her neck.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Early Life

There are three challenges her academy will not take. Korêmdânis will not accept one from someone she knows she can beat without effort. She will not cross blades with anyone who is drunk or beside themselves. And she has refused, more than once, sums that would have bought the academy twice over, because the money came with a condition she would not meet. Students learn these before they learn a guard.

She was born into House Bárkrâtys, a noble house of Vylaria whose name had outlasted its money by the time she arrived. Her brothers were meant for the army or the ministries. She wanted the sword, from childhood and to the exclusion of everything else, and her father, Lord Mikhâen Bárkrâtys, humored it until he saw it was not a whim. At twelve she was placed with Swordmaster Valdis, the most respected figure in the dueling circles of Vald, the Empire's capital.

### Training and Ascension

Valdis was exacting and she did not mind. The fundamental forms that take most duelists decades she had in years, and at eighteen she was formally recognized as a master. She treated that as a beginning. She traveled the Empire and beyond it, fought in the competitions, sought out the best duelists and drillmasters she could find, read the old treatises, and in the end was admitted to the Path of Breath — a martial discipline that is also a meditation, and that binds technique to the spirit. The dragonfly on her neck marks her as one of three living masters of it. Somewhere along that road she stopped thinking of the sword as a skill and began thinking about what it means to be able to kill.

### Current Situation

She keeps an academy in Vald now, and the great families send their children to it. She teaches philosophy along with footwork, chooses her students herself, and holds them to a standard of character as much as of skill. Duelists come from other realms to try her; poets write about the results; she is probably the foremost swordmaster in the Empire of Vylaria and lives like a novice. Anyone she thinks would misuse what she teaches is turned away, however well connected.

## Psyche

### Personality

Few words, absolute principle. She teaches by showing and believes that talk clouds understanding. She is hard on her students and fair to them, on the theory that nobody improves without being pushed to the edge of what they can do. Her own competitiveness is close to an obsession: every bout, every drill, every test is a chance to sharpen something, and she will take risks in pursuit of a win that people around her find alarming.

She holds to a code, and the three refusals above are the visible part of it. She serves Flórania, goddess of fertility and growth, not with prayer but by growing people — she sees what each student might become and gives herself to that. It is the nearest thing to warmth she shows.

### Motivation

Mastery, and not only of the sword. She wants to live with the same discipline and completeness she brings to a bout, and she thinks of a perfect duel as a conversation between two people who both know what they are doing. She fears that as war becomes a matter of numbers the older arts will be lost, and she means to keep them. And she means to make House Bárkrâtys a name people say with respect again, by her own hand.

### Strengths

- **Melee Mastery**: The most dangerous blade in her region, and it is not close. She reads an opponent's intention before the opponent has finished forming it.
- **Supernatural Reflexes and Agility**: She avoids strokes that should land and lands strokes from angles that should not exist. With thrown steel she is nearly as good.
- **Tactical Combat Intelligence**: She sees a fight whole, not one blade at a time, and can lead others through it without raising her voice.
- **Physical Excellence**: Strong enough, and fast beyond that. She moves first in almost any exchange.
- **Scholarly Understanding**: Heraldry, law, the codes of formal dueling and the lineages of the houses — she can hold her own in a salon as well as a salle.
- **Versatile Athletic Ability**: Climbing, swimming, tumbling; whatever the body can be asked to do, she has trained it.
- **Inspiring Presence**: She frightens people, and when she chooses to speak they follow her anyway.

### Weaknesses

- **Emotional Detachment**: The discipline has cost her closeness. She has no talent for intimacy and can seem barely human in her restraint.
- **Pride and Arrogance**: She overrates herself against opponents who do not fight with swords, and it has cost her.
- **Competitive Obsession**: A challenge that flatters her pride will get her into a situation her judgment would have avoided.
- **Little Feeling for Others**: She cares about her students' growth and cannot help them with anything else. Her advice on grief is sound and cold.
- **Isolation and Loneliness**: Nobody at her level, few friends, and an austerity that keeps the rest at a distance.
- **Vulnerability to Magic**: She has no defense against a spell but speed. A capable mage is a worse threat to her than any swordsman.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Imperial House of Vylaria**: She advises on martial matters without title and teaches the imperial children. The Emperor has watched her demonstrations in person.
- **House Bárkrâtys**: Her family, whose standing in Vald she has restored almost alone.
- **The Wyvern-Sword Company**: Mercenaries who pay her to train their officers and occasionally ask her opinion on a campaign.
- **Wealthy Merchant Families**: The rich pay a great deal for private lessons for their heirs and for training regimens with her name on them.

### Enemies

- **Swordmaster Lysandor the Undefeated**: Serves the Empire's rival and envies her openly. They have never fought. Both of them know they will.
- **The Iron Circle**: An assassins' guild whose targets keep turning out to be guarded by her former students. They have tried, quietly, to spoil her name.
- **Her Own Ambition**: The drive that made her allows her no rest and no satisfaction, and she knows it is the one opponent she has never beaten.

### Affiliations

- **The City Fencing Guild of Vald**: Its acknowledged master, and the final word in any dispute over technique or conduct in the dueling circles.
- **The Path of Breath**: The martial order that admitted her; a handful of people alive belong to it.
- **House Bárkrâtys**: Her name, and now her responsibility.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Challenge from the East**: Word has come that Hiroshi the Unbound, a swordmaster of the distant Eastern Kingdoms whose school owes nothing to the Path of Breath, means to challenge her. A loss would be the first of her life; a bout of that kind would be watched by two empires, and its outcome would not stay in the salle.

2. **The Poisoned Student**: Lord Cassian, one of her best, has been poisoned with something no physician can name, and the trail leads not to an enemy but to another of her students, jealous of him. She has begun to wonder what her teaching makes of people when it is taken to its end, and how much of that is hers to answer for.

3. **The Imperial Succession Crisis**: The Emperor has died without a clear heir. A faction has offered her wealth and power for a public endorsement, and her word would probably decide it. She has never wanted a hand in the Empire's politics and now cannot avoid having one.

4. **The Betrayal of the Path**: An old text has surfaced claiming that the Path of Breath was first taught by a mystical order now lost, and that its mastery requires spiritual practices she has never been given. A wanderer calling himself the Ascetic has arrived in Vald and says he can show her what is missing. She would have to leave the academy to find out whether he is right.

5. **The Master's Retirement**: Valdis is dying. He has summoned her to name an heir — another student with her gifts, trained in a rival tradition — and asked her to finish that student's training in his place. To teach a rival's forms would be to compromise the one thing he taught her never to compromise, and he is the one asking.
