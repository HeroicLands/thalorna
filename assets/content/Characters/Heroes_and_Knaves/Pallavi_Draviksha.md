# Appearance {#appearance}

![[palvdrvksh|Pallàvi Drâviksha]]{float: top-left}

A heart-shaped face with a broad nose and a soft jawline, tawny brown skin, dark black hair and hazel eyes under tawny brows. She is 5'9" and lightly built, and her hands are always stained somewhere.

# Dossier {#dossier}

**Early Life and Calling**

The commission that made her name was a philosophical text for a Dharmapur merchant who asked that the copy be as good as the ideas. When it was delivered he did not shelve it. He hung it on the wall of his receiving room, where his visitors could see it, and within a season the visitors were asking who had made it. By twenty-four Pallàvi had a waiting list.

She was born in the city-state of Dharmapur to a spice merchant and a seamstress whose work was better than her reputation. Her brothers and sisters went into trade. She went, at fourteen, to Keśava the Illuminator, an old master scribe who saw that she could draw before she could copy and taught her both, along with the thing she still says first when anyone asks what she does: that how a word is written is part of what it means.

**Guild Training and Breakthrough**

She entered the Scribes' Guild at eighteen and was noticed there for the wrong reasons before the right ones. She took the classical Vedyaran letter-forms and bent them a little; she drew borders that framed a text instead of smothering it; she worked out how to lay colored inks together so that a page read easily and looked like nothing else. The older masters called it decoration. The merchant's commission ended that argument, at least outside the guild hall.

**Current Situation**

She is one of the best-known scribes in Dharmapur now, with work for the temples of Vyālendra, for collectors and for a few minor nobles. The guild would like her to teach its younger scribes the traditional hands, and she resents every hour of it. Scholars have begun bringing her projects that would each take years — an illustrated natural philosophy, a full genealogy of the noble houses in illumination, a translation of a foreign scripture — and taking any of them would mean walking away from her commissions and probably from her guild contracts. She has not chosen. She has not stopped thinking about it either.

## Psyche

### Personality

Restless. Her mind goes from one thing to the next faster than her pen can follow, and the slow, repetitive part of her trade is the part she has to force. She will argue for an hour about why a particular letter should lean or why a blue is wrong for a text about grief, and people who care about that find her good company. People who do not find her scattered and a little rude.

She respects skill and has no patience for rules kept for their own sake. Give her a project that interests her and she is exact to the hair; ask her to work in a hand she thinks ugly and she becomes difficult. The guild has admirers of hers and enemies of hers and not much in between. Her patrons stay because she makes them feel that their book matters to her, and it does.

### Motivation

Beauty and meaning in the same object — she does not think a text is finished until the page says what the words say. She wants to be remembered as someone who widened the craft, not as a good copyist. She worships Vyālendra the creator and calls her work a kind of prayer, and she means it without solemnity.

### Strengths

- **Visionary Artistic Technique**: She draws as well as she writes, and the two together produce pages that are correct and unlike anyone else's.
- **Versatility in Scripts and Styles**: Several languages and several scripts, well enough to blend them on one page without the seams showing.
- **Understanding of Color and Harmony**: She has worked in ceramics and sung in choirs, and it shows in how she balances a page.
- **Precision**: When she is interested her spacing and proportion are exact; she counts, and it shows.
- **Intellectual Depth**: She reads what she copies and understands it, and the illumination answers the argument rather than just decorating it.

### Weaknesses

- **Distraction and Inconsistent Focus**: Long plain copying bores her, and bored she makes mistakes.
- **Little Feeling for Others, and Social Difficulty**: She misses what people feel, forgets what a patron actually asked for, and is bad at the politics of the guild.
- **Resistance to Authority and Tradition**: Anyone who insists on the old way of doing a thing will find her unmanageable, and several of the guild's masters have.
- **Limited Practical Capabilities**: She is not strong and has never had to be. A day's heavy work would finish her.
- **Vulnerability to Impulsive Decision-Making**: Enthusiasm carries her. She has agreed to commissions she could not complete and said things she could not take back.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Temple of Vyālendra**: Sacred texts and illuminated prayers, regularly, and a standing that is nearly official.
- **The Merchants' Cooperative**: Contracts and ledgers made beautiful, and gifts to trading partners meant to show off the giver's taste.
- **Master Keśava**: Very old, no longer taking work, and still the person she trusts most. He asks her now and then for a copy of something that matters to him.
- **The Guild of Scribes**: For all the friction, the guild's own documents — certificates, formal records — come to her when they are meant to impress.

### Enemies

- **Master Yajña, Conservative Guild Elder**: The leader of the traditionalists, who has said in public that her work is frivolous decoration that hides the text, and who has enough support to make the guild's next vote on standards a real question.

### Affiliations

- **The Scribes' Guild of Dharmapur**: Full member, with some formal duties, and a relationship with the guild that neither side would call comfortable.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Forbidden Text**: A scholar has offered her a great deal of money to illuminate a suppressed work — a philosophy that disputes the orthodox account of Vyālendra's nature. Owning the text is enough to bring the priesthood down on her; making it beautiful would be worse. She has read the first page and cannot stop thinking about it.

2. **The Guild's Choice**: The guild's leadership has given her an ultimatum: produce a masterwork that proves her departures honor the classical forms, or leave. The work will need old texts, historians and philosophers, and time she does not have, and Master Yajña's friends will be doing what they can to see it fail.

3. **The Collector's Obsession**: A nobleman from far away has been buying her finished pieces back from their owners at any price, and has now offered her an exclusive contract as his personal scribe. It would be comfortable. It would also cut her off from everyone else, and there are hints he wants her work for something other than looking at it.

4. **The Lost Library's Tragedy**: Scholars have hired her to copy the most important texts of an old library before the sea or the politics takes it. Partway through, it becomes clear that somebody hurried the library's ruin along to bury particular books, and that the same somebody would rather her copies were never finished.

5. **The Living Text**: A manuscript she is illuminating seems to be doing something as she works — as if the rendering were waking a meaning the words alone did not carry. The original author was rumored to study where language and magic meet. She has started consulting mages and would rather not say why.

6. **The Scribe's Apprentice**: A gifted young apprentice has attached herself to Pallàvi, learned her hand, and used it. Forgeries in her style have been sold to patrons who believed them hers, and the guild's name is on the line with her own. She is fond of the girl, and understands the hunger better than she would like to.
