# Appearance {#appearance}

![[skfrnstrmrt|Skúfrún Stormrót]]{float: top-left}

Pale, fair-haired and 5'7", with dye under her nails in whatever colour the week has been, green eyes in an oval face, a narrow nose, a rounded chin, rosy lips and light brows.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Vigram's daughter. She has been called it in every workshop in Malagna, kindly and otherwise, and she is thirty-two and it still lands.

She was born in the Kingdom of Malagna to **Vigram Stormrót**, a tentmaker whose work stood up to mountain gales and desert heat and was known for it, and to **Ashjora**, a dyer and pattern-maker whose cloth made useful things beautiful. She grew up among fabric and dye-vats and the noise of looms and needles, and had both parents' gifts, though it was her mother's she loved. Her father taught her structure and how a thing is built to last. Her mother taught her dyes, and what a pattern can say.

At twenty-four, when arthritis took her father's hands, he gave her the shop on one condition: that she keep both halves of the family's name, the sound work and the beautiful. Since then the Stormrót workshop has gone from a maker of good plain tents to a name known across Malagna and past it for textiles made to order that are beautiful, and last longer than anyone expects them to. Nobles, rich merchants and travelling companies who want to look as grand as they are safe come to her. She is unmarried by choice. The craft is the partner; suitors have looked, to her, like threats to it.

## Psyche

### Personality

Animated, quick, and lost in the work most of the time. Conversation comes second to form, colour and texture, and people interest her mostly as they bear on the work, which makes her seem scattered or self-absorbed until someone has worked beside her long enough to see the depth she is operating at.

Her ambition is an artist's: not money or standing, but to have it acknowledged that what she does is as much an art as painting or sculpture. Every commission is an argument for that. She is short with a patron who wants beauty and will not pay what it costs, or who wants it made plainer for practicality.

She is more practical about the business than the temperament suggests. Her books are kept, her dates are met, mostly, and she knows that a design sometimes has to bend to what the buyer will actually use. The bending has a limit: she has turned away good money rather than make something mediocre.

### Motivation

Beauty that lasts, and work that moves people, and proof that a useful thing and a beautiful one can be the same thing. There is an old bruise in it, too: being told, all her life, that she is talented and that it is her father's talent. Part of what she is making, with every commission, is a Skúfrún Stormrót whom nobody calls **Vigram's daughter**.

### Strengths

- **Decorative Technique**: Her embroidery, dyeing and pattern work carry feeling and story in cloth alone.
- **Colour**: She knows how colours act on each other, and her dye-work produces greens that shift with the light and reds that seem warm to the touch.
- **Design**: Rooted in the traditional forms and always going a step past them; lesser makers copy her within the season.
- **Production**: For all the artistry, she runs a workshop that delivers, on dates that would sink a less organised maker.
- **Patron Engagement**: She likes talking a commission through, and can turn a patron's vague wish into something better than they asked for.

### Weaknesses

- **Beauty Over Durability**: A heavily embroidered tent gives up a little strength; a rare dye fades faster than a plain one. Patrons who use her work hard sometimes come back disappointed.
- **Perfectionism Paralysis**: One colour can take her days, one stitch pattern dozens of attempts, and the commission waits.
- **Intolerance for Compromise**: When a patron wants a change that spoils the design she complies, resentfully and visibly, and the patron notices.
- **Limited Structural Skill**: The engineering her father mastered she has only in part; a hard structural problem stalls her.
- **Financial Naïveté**: She undercharges, does not value her own time, and has lost money on commissions that ate more than she reckoned.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Lord Brennan Silvercrest**: A rich noble with an eye for cloth, who has commissioned hangings and furnishings from her several times and sends his peers.
- **The Traveling Muse Theater Company**: A company of standing that needs costumes and scenery several times a year, and shows her work to more people than anyone else does.
- **The Merchant Consortium's Flagship Crews**: Captains who want to look prosperous, and order pavilions and shipboard furnishings from her that are seen in every port.
- **Princess Solveig of Malagna**: The royal house has begun to order from her — banners for ceremonies, furnishings for the palace. It is recent, and it changes everything if it lasts.

### Enemies

- **Master Craftsman Thorvald the Practical**: An older tentmaker of her father's school, who says in public that her work is lovely and useless, and has taken contracts from her by undercutting and promising durability.
- **Magdala the Copyist**: A rival who makes close copies of Skúfrún's designs for less. Several patrons have gone to her, and the law has little to say about a copied pattern.
- **Her Own Father**: **Vigram** Stormrót, retired and no rival in trade, still remarks, now and then, that she puts art before strength and is letting the family's tradition go.

### Affiliations

- **The Artisans' Collective of Malagna**: A prominent member with a seat on its council, and a little apart in it, since textiles do not rank with sculpture and painting in the members' minds.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Royal Commission That Changes Everything**: Princess **Solveig** has come to the workshop in person: the trousseau and ceremonial garments for her marriage to a foreign prince, a match meant to end a war between kingdoms, in six months, for a fortune. The workshop is already stretched. The betrothal has enemies at court, and as the work goes on her finished pieces are being used as tokens in a fight over the succession. She can finish and watch her work become someone's banner, or stop and lose everything.

2. **The Dye Master's Curse**: From a dead artisan's estate she has bought a set of old dye formulae, and the colours are beyond anything she has made. Patrons have begun to report that a garment in those colours is hard to take off, and that a red goes pale when the wearer is sad and deepens when she is glad. The dyer was a mage, and put herself into the recipes. Skúfrún can keep using them, destroy them, or try to reach the woman in them.

3. **The Commissioned Lie**: A rich merchant wants a costume and banner so exact a copy of a rival house's that it could pass at that house's own gatherings, and offers enough to secure the workshop for years. He calls it a trick of trade. The technical problem delights her. As the work goes on it becomes clear that what is planned is a theft, and that people may die in it.

4. **The Weave That Shows the Future**: A noble has commissioned a great woven hanging of his family's victory in an old battle. As she works, figures appear in the weave that she did not put there, and the battle is not the old one but one that has not happened yet. Nobles who have seen the unfinished piece recognise themselves and their children in it, dying and betraying, and the word has begun to spread. She does not know whether the loom is prophesying, haunted, or hers.

5. **The Gender Challenge**: The guild has announced a great competition for the finest craftsmen in Malagna, with a place at the royal court for the winner, and it is open to men. She entered under a man's name, was found out, and was shamed in public for it. The argument about women in the trades is now the kingdom's, not hers. She can fight the rule and lose her conservative patrons, take the insult and go on proving them wrong by the work, or make something so clearly the best that the rule looks like what it is.
