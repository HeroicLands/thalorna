# Appearance {#appearance}

![[ailemkrmys|Ailêmâ Kêrîamyês]]{float: top-left}

She is long-faced, 5'11" and of medium build, with light olive skin, chestnut hair and brown eyes; an aquiline nose, a pointed chin, full lips, heavy brows and sculpted cheeks. Ink under the nails of the right hand, always.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### The Child of Letters

Ailêmâ cannot call a page finished. It is the fact about her that her clients know least and her apprentices know best: a commission that surpassed what was asked for a month ago is still on her table being improved, and the words "good enough" are not ones she has ever been able to say about her own work. It is the perfectionism that made her, and it has begun to feel like a cell.

She was born in Alyssa, the capital of the Empire of Vylaria, to the Kêrîamyês family — scholars and administrators, not rich, well regarded. Her father, Veridân Kêrîamyês, was a scribe of some standing, known for exact work on chronicles and legal texts; her mother, Istrylla d'Alyssa, came of merchants and taught the children of the nobility. Ailêmâ learned her first letters watching her father's pen. By five she was being tutored in script and by ten she was working on his commissions.

### Scholarly Apprenticeship

At fourteen she was apprenticed to Master Scriptorium Director Vexillus, head of the Imperial Scribal Academy and by most reckonings the finest calligrapher in the Empire. His standards were notorious and she found them a relief; here at last was someone as exacting as she was. In six years with him she mastered the Vylarian script and three historical and ceremonial hands besides, learned ink and parchment from the making up, and came to think of her work as the point where a thought becomes permanent.

At twenty she was given the commission that made her: an illuminated copy of the Codex of Imperial Law, eighteen months of work, the largest thing ever entrusted to a scribe of her age. It hangs in the Imperial Palace. She was made Guildmaster at twenty-two, which almost never happens, and with that standing came the best materials and the best commissions in Alyssa.

### Current Life and Growing Complications

At twenty-nine she runs a small workshop in Alyssa that takes everything from legal documents and historical transcription to illuminated texts and the private letters of nobles. Her name means work delivered on time, to the standard promised, at a price that reflects both, and she earns well and puts most of it back into the shop.

The trouble is not the work. She has begun to notice how little else there is. She serves Flórania, whose province is growth and change and the turning of the seasons, and she has spent her life making things that do not change. She has few close friends, no lover, and a world that runs from her table to her clients' doors. She has started to think that she has been living too small a life, and the thought is doing something to her.

## Psyche

### Personality

Quick, exact and cerebral. She argues well and enjoys it, though her speech is precise rather than moving — she can make a point and cannot make a crowd. With clients she is composed and gracious. In company she is reserved and a little distant, and she misses what people are feeling more often than she would like.

She holds herself and everyone else to a high standard of work and honesty, which makes her trustworthy and can make her seem hard. She has no patience for excuses and hears most explanations as excuses. Her wit is pointed, not warm. The people who know her well know that there is a great deal of principle under the composure, and that she simply cannot show affection and has decided that being reliably present, year after year, will have to say it for her.

### Motivation

Two things pulling in opposite directions. She wants to make work that lasts — pages that people will still value in a hundred years — and it has become the whole of her. And she wants, more each month, to live: to take a risk, to go somewhere, to know someone, to grow rather than to perfect. She has started to wonder whether her best work might be a life instead of a manuscript, and she is frightened by how much she wants to find out.

### Strengths

- **Intellectual Acuity**: She takes in complicated material fast, sees the pattern and the flaw, and understands how any system works well enough to use it.
- **Masterful Calligraphy and Script**: One of the finest hands in the Empire, in several scripts, and an illuminator whose pages are collected as art.
- **Perception and Awareness**: She notices what others miss — in a text, and between its lines. She can also follow a trail, which surprises people who know only her pen.
- **Scholarly Knowledge**: Old stories, mathematics and medicine, all of it real learning rather than a scribe's passing acquaintance.
- **Physical Precision**: Her hands are the best of her physically. She climbs well, and she can shoot and throw, which suggests training a scribe does not usually get.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited Physical Strength**: She is not strong. Heavy work or a real fight would be beyond her.
- **Social Awkwardness**: She misreads feeling, gives offense without meaning to, and cannot charm. People find her cold.
- **Perfectionism as Dysfunction**: The habit of never finishing has cost her deadlines and chances, and it can stop her acting at all.
- **Difficulty with Improvisation**: Given a plan, she executes it exactly. Deprived of one, she struggles.
- **Emotional Withdrawal**: Faced with feeling, hers or anyone's, she retreats into analysis. People take it for not caring.
- **Limited Resilience in Adversity**: She does not recover fast from a setback or a hard road, and when a plan fails she is brittle.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Imperial Archivist Salûdor**: Head of the Imperial Archives, who sends her historical documents to transcribe and preserve and trusts her with the standards of the archive.
- **Merchant-Lady Savîna d'Corieth**: A merchant of real influence who has Ailêmâ keep her private correspondence and records, values her discretion, and has become something of a patron beyond the work.
- **Master Vexillus**: Old and retired, still in touch, and still the person she asks about a historical hand or a decision in her trade.

### Enemies

- **Scribe-Master Thelvûn**: Runs a rival shop in Alyssa on volume rather than quality, considers her prices elitist, undercuts them, and has been asking aloud whether her work is really so much better.
- **The Merchant-Bureaucrats of Trade**: A faction in the merchant guilds who think hand-written documents an expensive indulgence and would replace them with something cheaper. To them she is the whole problem in one person.

### Affiliations

- **The Imperial Scribal Guild**: Full Guildmaster, respected, holding no office, and consulted on questions of standards and authenticity.
- **The Temple of Flórania**: A nominal affiliation and an occasional attendance, much less than in her youth. It troubles her conscience now and then, and lately more than then.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Codex of Lost Kings**: An elderly nobleman has brought her a damaged manuscript in an old dialect of Vylarian — an account, as far as she can read it, of the Empire's founding — and asked for a restored, readable copy. The further she gets, the clearer it becomes that the account contradicts the official history, and that a faithful transcription would be a weapon against the present dynasty.

2. **The Cipher Protocol**: A stranger — imperial agent, criminal, or something else — has offered her enough to run the workshop for five years to transcribe a set of coded documents, on the condition that she speak of it to no one. She cannot read them and can see from their shape that they are political. What she has slowly worked out is that the cipher is being used to track dissidents whose only crime is disagreement.

3. **The Restoration of the Forgotten Archive**: The Temple of Flórania has hired her to restore and catalogue an old temple archive. In it she has found evidence that the temple's present teaching is not what Flórania's was, that texts were suppressed and others altered, and that her own master, Vexillus, was part of the suppression decades ago.

4. **The Duchess's Secret**: Duchess Minerva d'Alyss wants her private letters put into a cipher and hidden inside illuminated religious texts that look like nothing more, and has made clear, without saying it, what refusal would cost. The duchess has dangerous enemies and is not wrong to fear them. The documents Ailêmâ would make are made for deceiving people, and several factions have noticed what she can do.

5. **The Wanderer's Request**: A woman named Kest, who says she is a scholar of ancient languages, has brought Ailêmâ samples of a script she has never seen and an argument: decipher it together, and it will lead to a lost civilization's library. Kest will not say where the samples came from, who is paying her, or what she means to do with what they find. Going would mean leaving the workshop, which is exactly the kind of thing Ailêmâ has begun to wish she could do.
