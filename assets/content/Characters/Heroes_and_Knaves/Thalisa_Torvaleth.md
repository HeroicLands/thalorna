# Appearance {#appearance}

![[thlstrvlth|Thalísa Torvaleth]]{float: top-left}

Green eyes, dark brown hair, warm olive skin, and a wolf tattooed on one shoulder that she does not usually cover. She is 5'10" and light, with a diamond-shaped face, a hawkish nose, a pronounced chin, dark brows and lips that sit a little pursed.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The cathedral in the town where Thalísa Torvaleth grew up had colored glass in its windows, and she remembers, at seven or eight, lying on the floor of the nave to watch the light change on the stone as the sun moved. Nothing since has interested her as much. Her father worked metal, competently; her mother kept an herb garden and grew things nobody else in the Kingdom of Tarvénia bothered with. At twelve she was standing outside the workshop of the cathedral's glassworker, Master Aldwin, asking to be taught.

Aldwin had no children and could see what he was looking at, and took her on. For eight years she learned the trade as it is: cutting, leading, soldering, the making of a window that keeps out the weather and tells the story it is meant to tell. Her own leanings showed early. She put colors together that he called garish, drew patterns that did not come from any church tradition, and kept asking what else glass could be made to say.

Aldwin died when she was twenty-three and she opened her own workshop in a smaller city of Tarvénia. The work is bold, unconventional and sometimes nearly abstract, and it has found its buyers — churches whose bishops do not mind an argument, collectors, merchants who want something no one else has. It has also found its enemies among the traditional glassworkers, who consider it a betrayal of what the craft is for. She has not softened it for anyone, and the result is a reputation and a set of people who would like to see her fail.

## Psyche

### Personality

Sure of what she is doing to the point of stubbornness, and at the same time easily hurt by criticism and sometimes stopped cold by doubt. She can lose a week to a detail. She keeps her private life private, and yet she feels other people's trouble quickly and gives her time to it, and she has cut her fee more than once for someone who needed the window more than she needed the money.

She has a dry humor that shows only when she feels safe. She thinks of the glass as a kind of devotion, though not one any temple would recognize. She has arranged her life so that nobody can get too close to her, and she misses the closeness she has arranged away, and does not say so.

### Motivation

To make things that are beautiful and true as she sees the truth, whether or not that is how the church sees it, and to show that glass can carry a person's meaning and not only a scripture's. Under that, quieter and less admitted: to be counted an artist of the first rank, and to be counted one for having changed the craft rather than for having obeyed it.

### Strengths

- **Artistic Vision**: She sees relationships of color and balance that other people miss entirely, and she can draw, throw a pot, and hold a stage as well as cut glass.
- **Extraordinary Technical Skill**: Her hands are exact. She can bring a delicate piece through the kiln and lift a heavy one into a frame without help.
- **Inventive Problem-Solving**: She has invented several techniques other glassworkers now use without knowing where they came from.
- **Physical Grace and Awareness**: Heights do not trouble her. She works on scaffolds and in awkward corners without a thought.
- **Versatile Craftsmanship**: Ceramics, metal, leather and lettering, all to a workshop standard, so a piece of hers can be a whole object and not only its glass.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited Large-Scale Experience**: She has never done a major building. Big commissions frighten her, and the planning and timekeeping they need are not her gift.
- **Sensitivity to Criticism**: She knows in the abstract that critique is useful and takes it personally anyway. A bad response can stop her working for weeks.
- **Emotional Isolation**: She is good at keeping people out — it has its uses in her trade — and it has left her lonely.
- **Commercial Naïveté**: She is easily taken in business. She undercharges and negotiates badly.
- **Lack of Patrons and Institutional Support**: No guild behind her to speak of, and none of the patron circles a traditional glassworker inherits, so a bad year is a dangerous one.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Bishop Matthias of Riverside Cathedral**: Commissioned windows from her that show spiritual ideas without the usual pictures, and has defended them against his own colleagues. Her first and loudest advocate.
- **Merchant Collector Lady Esther Whitmore**: A rich widow with an educated eye who has bought several large pieces for her manor and paid well for them.
- **Artist-Patrons the Ravencroft Collective**: Artists, musicians and argumentative thinkers who admire her and keep commissioning her. They are the nearest thing she has to peers.
- **Master Aldwin's Estate**: His will left a small trust that commissions one work from her every year. It is a steady income and it keeps the conversation with him going.

### Enemies

- **Master Glassworker Helmut Thorne**: A traditionalist of great standing who calls her a charlatan in public and steers churches and institutions toward glassworkers he considers authentic. Her success reads to him as a threat to his life's work.
- **Rival Artisan Cassian Darkglass**: Once her student, left in bitterness, and now a traditionalist by choice — the better to distance himself from her. He tells anyone who asks that her novelties are only the abandonment of proper technique.

### Affiliations

- **the Glassworkers' Guild of Tarvénia**: She is a member, attends when she must, and ignores the guild's periodic suggestions that she work more conventionally.
- **the Ravencroft Artistic Circle**: An informal community of artists and thinkers who count her as one of their voices.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Commission from Beyond**: A patron she cannot place has ordered an enormous window of a technical difficulty she has never attempted, with imagery she cannot read, on a strict schedule and under strict secrecy, for a great deal of money. As she works she has begun to suspect the design does something, and to wonder for whom.

2. **The Lost Master's Final Work**: In an abandoned cathedral she has found fragments of glass by Aldwin she never knew existed — work that was moving in her own direction years before she began. It looks as if he destroyed some of it rather than be criticized for it. She wants the rest, and she wants to know what he was trying to do.

3. **The Architectural Opportunity**: She has been invited to submit designs for the windows of a major civic building — the kind of commission that makes a name for good. Helmut Thorne is also submitting. The committee prefers the conventional. She can offer them what they want or what she means, and she has not decided which would cost her more.

4. **The Cursed Glass**: From a collector's estate she has acquired old glass of a color and quality she cannot account for, and worked it into new windows. People who look at those windows report dreams, visions and compulsions. She does not know whether the glass is cursed or whether her own work has found something in the viewers, and she does not know whether to break the windows or show them.

5. **The Rival's Accident**: Cassian Darkglass has been badly hurt in his workshop and may never work again, and the accident was not one. Several people had reason to want him hurt. Some of the evidence suggests that the intended victim was Thalísa, and that some of the people involved are people she trusted.
