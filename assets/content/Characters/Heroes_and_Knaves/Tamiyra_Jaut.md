# Appearance {#appearance}

![[tamiyrajat|Ta'miyra Jâut]]{float: top-left}

Small — Ta'miyra is 5'3" and lightly made — with warm bronze skin, jet black hair and black eyes in a diamond-shaped face that tapers to the chin, under fine brows. A faint scar on the left side of her neck is usually covered and sometimes, deliberately, not.

# Dossier {#dossier}

When Tamiyra was fourteen her father's loyalty to a disgraced official came to light, and the family of a minor imperial scribe in the Empire of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]] was suddenly worth nothing. Her mother did not wait for ruin. She placed the girl in the household of a rich merchant lord, to learn charm and conversation and the uses of influence, as a temporary measure. It was not temporary. Tamiyra turned out to have a gift beyond anything her mother had bargained on — she could make a man or a woman believe she understood exactly what they wanted — and a childhood spent among ledgers and ceremonial robes had already taught her how power talks when it thinks nobody is listening.

By twenty-eight she was the most sought courtesan in the capital, and not for her looks. Her mind kept up with scribes and her tongue could cut or heal as she chose, and she gathered patrons the way scholars gather books, each one cultivated and kept. Then the scandal: an affair with a high-ranking officer, Azaes Korvac, and a set of forged diplomatic letters that came within a hair of wrecking a trade treaty. It nearly finished her. Careful footwork and the right friends got her out with her position mostly intact and her illusions gone.

She is thirty-six now and has widened her circle to scholars, merchants and people who work in the dark as well as nobles. The scar on her neck is from an assassin five years ago, whom she survived narrowly. She wears it as a fact about herself rather than a wound.

## Psyche

### Personality

There are two of her. In company she is grace itself — witty, warm, apparently open, and listening far more than she speaks, so that everyone she talks to leaves feeling heard. Behind that a mind is working the whole time, weighing and placing. She is neither cruel nor purely for hire; she does feel for people, and it is the feeling that makes the performance convincing.

Alone with someone she trusts she is blunt, and capable of a long evening on power, love and death. She keeps her distance as a matter of survival, having learned that only the powerful can afford to be vulnerable. Her idea of honor has nothing to do with knights: it is keeping one's word, meeting one's obligations, and treating the people under her as people.

### Motivation

To survive, and to hold something that cannot be taken away by the next scandal or the next shift at court. The web of patrons and allies exists because she knows how little beauty and favor weigh. Under that, she wants to prove that a woman from where she comes from, in her trade, can hold real power of her own — not borrowed from the men who keep her, but hers. She wants to build something that lasts in a world that has shown her nothing does.

### Strengths

- **Master of Social Navigation**: She reads a room in a breath and becomes what it needs. She can talk, persuade or charm her way through nearly any company.
- **Keen Perception and Awareness**: A trembling hand, a glance avoided, the half-beat before a lie. Very little is hidden from her.
- **Physical Grace and Agility**: Her dancing is talked about in the noble courts. She moves so that people watch her and forget to watch anything else.
- **Artisan of Words**: She sings, writes a fine hand, and can act. She can build a story, forge a letter when it is needed, and tell a version of the truth that is hard to argue with.
- **Intellect for the Long Game**: She thinks several moves out, and in a political game she is as dangerous as anyone with a sword.

### Weaknesses

- **Emotional Isolation**: Years of masks. She finds it hard to be truly close to anyone, and the distance she keeps reads as coldness to anyone who tries.
- **Reputation Fragility**: The old scandal is a loaded weapon in someone else's hands. The knowledge makes her over-careful, and there have been moments when boldness would have served and she hesitated.
- **Physical Vulnerability**: She is small and not strong, and cannot fight her way out of anything. In danger she depends on other people.
- **Limited Survival Skills**: She knows how to survive in the wild in the way a scholar knows it. Without servants, comforts and a city around her she is miserable, and wild country frightens her.
- **Dependence on Patrons**: For all her independence, the money and the protection come from her network, and that puts limits on what she can afford to do.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Lord Amenhotep Kajem**: Very rich, a collector of art, and the nearest thing she has to an equal among her patrons. He funds her and shields her from anyone who would use the scandal against her. It is her steadiest relationship, and it rests on respect more than on desire.
- **Lady Nefertiti Shalûr**: An old widow with a great deal of political weight, who has taken Tamiyra up as a protégée and possible heir to her network. Lady Shalûr recognizes the ambition; she had it once herself.
- **Merchant Prince Vasken Tharcot**: A foreign trader who comes to Ta'Kheperu on business and pays generously for her company and, more, for what she knows about imperial politics and the trade treaties.
- **High Scribe Demetrios Callias**: A scholar who pays her to copy rare texts and argue philosophy with him. One of the few patrons whose company she would keep for nothing.

### Enemies

- **Lord Commander Azaes Korvac**: The officer from the scandal, whose rise stopped when hers did not. He would like to see her ruined completely, spreads what rumors he can, and pays informants to find him a new one.
- **Merchant Rival Selena Moros**: A younger courtesan who rose in the years after the scandal by offering what Tamiyra offered without the history. She poaches patrons and lets it be known that Tamiyra is getting old.

### Affiliations

- **The Circle of the Veil**: Courtesans, companions and women of influence across Ta'Kheperu, who trade information, protect each other and keep standards. She advises it without title.
- **The Scribal Consortium**: Scholars and historians who commission her copying and admit her to their circles, which gives her both knowledge and a respectable standing as a patron of learning.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Forged Seal**: The diplomatic letters at the heart of her scandal — which she always believed real — were a fabrication by some third party, made to play her patrons and their enemies against each other. That third party, never identified, is now moving to blackmail her with proof of the forgery. She needs to find them before the proof reaches her network.

2. **The Daughter's Claim**: A young woman has arrived claiming to be Tamiyra's daughter by a powerful noble long dead, born just after his death, and wanting her to testify so as to claim his inheritance. Tamiyra has no memory of ever being pregnant. Either someone has built a very careful lie, or something was taken from her mind.

3. **The Competitor's Peril**: A patron drifting from Tamiyra to Selena Moros has asked her why Selena has suddenly stopped receiving him. The answer is that Selena has been taken and is being held in servitude somewhere far from Ta'Kheperu. Tamiyra can risk herself to get a rival out, or leave her there and have the field.

4. **The Encrypted Archive**: While looking into a prospective patron she has stumbled on references to a hidden archive inside the imperial archives — centuries of forbidden documents, love letters and political intelligence. Several factions are hunting it, and because her father worked in the archives they believe she knows where it is. She does not. That will not protect her.

5. **The Scribe's Redemption**: An old scribe named Pthahotep, who worked beside her father, has come to her dying with a confession: the scandal was not her enemies' work but her mother's, part of a larger plan to keep Tamiyra safe from something worse. He died before he could say what. He left a journal, in a hand she can barely read, about her family, her early life and a secret the imperial court would kill to keep.
