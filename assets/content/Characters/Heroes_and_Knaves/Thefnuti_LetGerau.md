# Appearance {#appearance}

![[thefnutiltgr|Thefnuti Let'Gerau]]{float=top-left}

Thefnuti is a muscular man of 5'10" with a medium build, golden skin, gray hair to his shoulders and brown eyes that have stopped looking at faces. His face is oval and fine-boned, and his hands are always at work on something small, turning a spring or a gear while he talks. He works bare-chested under a leather tool belt of pouches and wears a loupe on a cord. His workshop smells of wax and of the oil he uses on his gears, and he carries a clockmaker's files, drills and gauges in his pockets and a wooden chest of tiny gears and springs under his arm when he calls on a patron.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Thefnuti was born into a family of toymakers in the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], in a tradition stretching back seven generations. His great-grandfather had been commissioned by an imperial prince to create mechanical toys of extraordinary ingenuity, work that established the family's reputation for invention and precision. Thefnuti's own father, [Gakhoti]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, had expanded the family trade significantly, introducing mechanisms of increasing complexity while maintaining a commitment to beauty and craftsmanship.

From earliest childhood, Thefnuti was trained in the family traditions. Unlike some toymakers who view their work as frivolous entertainment, the [[affiliation-garletgerau|Let'Gerau]] family understood that toys were miniature worlds—opportunities to teach mechanics, aesthetics, and imagination. Thefnuti exceeded even his father's capabilities, developing new techniques for creating clockwork mechanisms of breathtaking intricacy. By his thirties, his toys were so much in demand among wealthy families and collectors that his wait list extended years into the future.

However, beginning in his forties, Thefnuti became increasingly troubled by what he perceived as the cheapening of the craft. Simpler toys turned out in bulk began undercutting the market, and younger toymakers enthusiastically adopted new materials and methods that Thefnuti viewed as compromising quality. Rather than adapt, he doubled down on his traditional techniques and began work on increasingly elaborate creations that became less toys and more works of engineering art. This shift in focus—away from practical toys children could play with and toward elaborate mechanical sculptures meant for display—has simultaneously enhanced his prestige among collectors and left him increasingly isolated from the rest of the toymaking trade.

Now at fifty-one, Thefnuti maintains a prestigious workshop in [[place-anlaghzetun|Anlagh-Zetun]], where he continues to produce extraordinary works. However, the constant pressure from younger craftsmen and the changing market has made him bitter and defensive about his methods.

## Psyche

### Personality

Thefnuti's character is full of contradictions. In matters of craftsmanship, he is confident to the point of arrogance—he knows his work is extraordinary and has no patience for criticism of his techniques. Yet in social situations, he becomes almost diffident, preferring the company of machines and mechanisms to people. He is capable of surprising warmth with those who show real respect for craftsmanship, but he is swift to become cold and dismissive toward those he judges as lacking proper appreciation.

He can be philosophically whimsical, expounding at length about the nature of time, motion, and mechanical perfection, but these flights of fancy are often followed by sharp criticism of others' inadequate understanding. He takes real pleasure in the work itself—the careful fitting of gears, the testing of mechanisms—but increasingly displays impatience with clients, viewing most patrons as interruptions to his craft rather than reasons for it.

### Motivation

Thefnuti's primary motivation is the creation of mechanical perfection—toys that function flawlessly, that are beautifully proportioned, and that will survive intact for generations. Underlying this is a fear: that in his absence, the techniques that took seven generations to develop will be lost to cheaper, faster, inferior methods. He works for commission and, beyond that, to build a legacy, creating works that will prove the superiority of traditional craftsmanship long after he is gone.

### Strengths

Extraordinary Mechanical Aptitude
: His lockcraft, with five specializations, combined with his creativity, reasoning, drawing, and music, allows him to conceive and execute mechanisms of astonishing complexity. Few living craftsmen can match his precision with clockwork.

Multi-Disciplinary Mastery
: He possesses significant abilities across woodworking, metalcraft, ceramics, and textile craft. This allows him to create complete works integrating multiple materials and techniques as one whole.

Attention to Detail
: His sharp perception and dexterity allow him to work at scales and tolerances that would challenge lesser craftsmen. He can create gear teeth so fine they require magnification to observe clearly.

Inventor's Mind
: His creativity and reasoning combine to let him devise new solutions to mechanical problems while keeping to the traditional approach as a whole.

Understanding of Motion
: His knowledge of how mechanisms function at deep levels allows him to design toys and sculptures that perform with seemingly impossible fluidity and precision.

### Weaknesses

Intolerance of Modern Methods
: His creativity, while extraordinary for design, is constrained by his unwillingness to experiment with new materials or methods. He views any deviation from traditional methods as inherent compromise, blinding him to potential improvements.

Poor Business Acumen
: His modest eloquence and charm make him a poor negotiator and a worse salesman. He struggles to explain the value of his work to potential patrons and often prices his commissions far below what the market would bear, then resents that he is undercompensated.

Social Isolation
: His endurance for company is limited. Long client meetings drain him, and he frequently offends patrons with brusque dismissals. This has limited his ability to build a strong body of patrons despite his renown.

Perfectionism Paralysis
: His desire for mechanical perfection sometimes prevents him from completing works. He has been known to spend months perfecting small components that patrons already considered adequate, delaying projects and increasing costs.

Declining Physical Capability
: His strength and endurance are declining with age, and his eyesight, while still precise for close work, deteriorates if forced to work for extended periods, requiring him to take more frequent breaks.

## Social

### Patrons

Lady [[being-uznerashkag2|Uzner Ashkagar]]
: An elderly widow of great wealth and refined taste who has become Thefnuti's primary patron over the past decade. She commissions elaborate mechanical sculptures for her private collection and appreciates his perfectionism as an expression of respect for craft itself.

Scholar-Collector [[being-kheladenthlt|Kheladen Itha'Letu]]
: A historian and antiquarian who employs Thefnuti to create accurate mechanical reproductions of ancient devices based on historical records and archaeological findings. Their collaboration has produced some of Thefnuti's most celebrated works.

[The Imperial Treasury]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}
: Thefnuti has several standing commissions from the imperial court to create elaborate gifts for diplomatic purposes and state ceremonies. These are highly visible works that enhance his reputation significantly.

A merchant prince
: A collector of mechanical curiosities who maintains a private museum and commissions elaborate pieces specifically for display. He pays handsomely and allows Thefnuti complete creative freedom, though he can be demanding about timelines.

### Enemies

A younger toymaker
: A successful craftsman who has built wealth by turning out toys in bulk with newer materials that Thefnuti views as inferior. He has directly challenged Thefnuti's supremacy by advertising his toys as "superior because they are affordable" and has actively poached several of Thefnuti's potential clients. He sees Thefnuti as an arrogant relic and publicly questions whether his work is worth the extraordinary cost.

[[being-rethekuthght|Retheku Iuthi'Gehtiu]]
: A bureaucrat who has begun examining Thefnuti's finances closely, questioning how commissions that should be financially ruinous result in Thefnuti's continued wealth. Retheku suspects hidden income or tax evasion and has become increasingly adversarial in his investigations.

### Affiliations

[[affiliation-linzaglkhlth|Lin'Zaglu elu Au'Khelathu]], the Toymakers' Guild
: Thefnuti holds membership and retains significant prestige within the guild, though he frequently clashes with guild leadership over standards and methods. He serves on the guild's standards committee, where he advocates for stricter quality requirements.

the [[affiliation-garletgerau|Gar-Let'Gerau]]
: An informal family legacy and tradition spanning seven generations, of which Thefnuti is the current master. He has become increasingly protective of family techniques and secretive about his methods.

## Plot Hooks

The Copied Gears
: Toys with the Let'Gerau mechanisms begin to appear in the markets at a tenth of his price. They are made in bulk by a younger rival whose workshop he has never entered. Someone has copied his plans in detail, and the law of the empire gives a craftsman no suit for his designs. Thefnuti asks the party to find the hand that opened his chest, and he warns them that he does not want it known that he can be robbed.

The Tally-Engine
: The scholar-collector Kheladen Itha'Letu asks him to build a working copy of a counting-engine described in the oldest rolls of the survey-courts. The only drawings are in the tally-bureau's strong-room, which the bureaucrat who is auditing Thefnuti's accounts controls. To build the engine he must let Retheku into the work, and into his books.

The Gifts for the Embassy
: The Treasury announces that his mechanical gifts for the diplomatic season are to be inspected by foreign judges so that reproductions can be ordered. An inspector sends him a private note saying that the gifts are to be marked unauthorized unless Thefnuti shows him his methods first. The inspector is offering a favor or a blackmail, and Thefnuti does not know which.

The Apprentice Who Knew Too Much
: A young craftsperson asks for an apprenticeship and demonstrates a technique that only the Let'Gerau teach. Thefnuti has taught it to no one outside the family. He can send her away and lose the first talent he has met in decades, or take her in and learn who gave her the knowledge.

:::secret {title="What the referee knows"}
The apprentice is the granddaughter of a second pupil of Thefnuti's father Gakhoti, who taught a man outside the family and left Thefnuti's grandfather ignorant of it. That pupil's line is the source of the bulk-made mechanisms, and the copying is not theft in any sense the law recognizes.

Retheku is right about the income. Thefnuti inflates the Treasury's gift commissions with the connivance of a clerk, and what the audit would find would end his standing with the court.
