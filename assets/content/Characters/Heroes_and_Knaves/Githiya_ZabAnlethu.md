# Appearance {#appearance}

![[githiyzbnlth|Githiya Zab'Anlethu]]{float=top-left}

Githiya is a tall woman, six feet in her sandals, with ebony skin, long black hair and hazel eyes that rest on a thing a moment before she moves it. Her face is a long oval, composed, and she keeps it that way. She wears a straight household dress with a red sash, kept spotless, and the iron ring of household keys at her belt is the only sound she makes when she walks. A ledger bound in cord hangs from her other hip, and a pouch of cooking herbs and a bundle of remedy-herbs ride in her sleeve.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Githiya Zab'Anlethu was born into the free classes of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], neither enslaved nor nobility, but precisely placed in that careful middle where service becomes the foundation of survival. Her mother was a cook, her father a minor functionary in [[affiliation-lutuznera|Lut-Uzn'era]], the temple of Uzn'era. From childhood, Githiya showed an almost obsessive need for order, and by her late teens, she had already begun managing the household of a moderately wealthy merchant. That position lasted seventeen years until the merchant's death, when Githiya found herself without patronage and with many rivals for the next place.

For the past four years, she has served in the household of Master [[being-lenti|Lenti]], a wealthy intellectual and collector of antiquities. Lenti values her genius for order and the way she has transformed his somewhat chaotic holdings into a model of good management. Yet Lenti, like many employers, takes her service for granted, referring to her competence as simply "what Githiya does" rather than acknowledging it as anything out of the ordinary. She manages the physical household, deals with merchants, oversees the preparation of elaborate entertainments, and acts as Lenti's informal advisor on matters of domestic stability.

Her spiritual devotion to [[lore-uzneradty|Uzn'era]], goddess of fertility and the domestic sphere, runs deep—though some might argue it borders on obsession. Githiya sees her work not as mere service but as sacred duty, maintaining the fertile, ordered space from which all civilization springs.

## Psyche

### Personality

Githiya is a woman of deep contradictions. On the surface, she is unswervingly correct, composed, and calm in crisis. Under that calm is a roiling ocean of resentment and wounded pride. She speaks softly and rarely volunteers opinion, yet when she does speak, people listen—her words carry the weight of someone who has watched everything while remaining largely invisible. She possesses a dry, biting humor that emerges only among those she trusts, revealing a keen intelligence often overshadowed by her role as a functionary.

What disturbs those who know her well is her capacity to hold grudges with almost architectural precision. She can remember, in perfect detail, every slight, every moment of ingratitude, every instance when her work was praised while she remained in shadow. These grievances do not make her cruel, but they do make her wary. She gives her loyalty completely to those who earn it, but once lost, that loyalty is not easily recovered.

### Motivation

Githiya's deepest drive pulls two ways: she seeks both complete control over her domain and acknowledgment of that mastery by those above her. She works with exacting precision not solely for the satisfaction of order but for the recognition that such order brings. She remains in Master Lenti's service despite her resentment because leaving would mean losing the stage for her excellence—without a grand household to manage, what proof would she have of her worth? Yet this same bind increasingly irritates her. She yearns for a position where her contributions would be seen and valued, where her name would be known beyond the servants' quarters.

### Strengths

Organizational Mastery
: Can manage complex households of any size with near-perfect economy, tracking stores, staff, schedules, and deliveries with minimal effort.

Problem Solver
: Possesses practical wisdom for solving the hundred small crises that arise in domestic life—from preventing conflicts between staff to stretching the household purse without visible compromise.

Ritual Knowledge
: Deeply versed in the practices and observances necessary for proper worship of Uzn'era, allowing her to structure household activities in spiritually appropriate ways.

Command Authority
: Can direct servants and staff without raising her voice—people obey her not from fear but from respect for her evident competence and fairness.

Adaptability
: Though she values order, Githiya can adjust routines and arrangements when circumstances demand, maintaining her vision of perfection while accommodating necessity.

### Weaknesses

Bitterness Corrodes
: Her growing resentment of being taken for granted makes her increasingly sharp with staff and prone to petty, unspoken acts of control.

Difficulty Delegating
: She trusts her own standards absolutely and struggles to allow others to manage tasks, leading to exhaustion and bottlenecks.

Inflexibility in Principles
: While adaptive in practice, Githiya possesses fixed beliefs about how things "should" be, making her harsh toward those who deviate.

Emotional Isolation
: Her distance and formality make it difficult for others to approach her with real care; she is respected but rarely loved.

Vulnerability to Manipulation
: Those who offer her sincere recognition or challenge her to prove her worth can lead her into ill-advised situations, as her pride becomes engaged.

## Plot Hooks

The Short Sack
: Every sack sent to Lenti's house is weighed and sealed on the grain-wharf, and Githiya signs for it at the door. For three months the weight on the seal and the weight on her scale have differed by a handful, always the same. Someone on the wharf or inside the household is skimming under a seal that nobody questions. She asks the party to find the hand before the next audit of the factor's tallies, because her name is on every receipt.

The Attentive Guest
: A merchant captain with a berth at the wharf has become a regular at Lenti's table, and he is the first guest in years to remark on the order of the house. Githiya knows what she is being given, and takes it. Two bronzes from Lenti's collection have gone missing, and the next manifest the wharf-scribe seals is for a crate of Amqelu-Legez pottery that the captain is shipping downriver. She must choose between the recognition and opening a crate that is already sealed.

The Month at the Temple
: The temple of Uzn'era asks Githiya to take charge of a month of preparation for a household's fertility festival, with a title the temple's servants will use for the rest of her life. Lenti will need a steward in her absence, and the one who offers is Akhoku's cousin. If she goes, the household she has built is run by the man's relative; if she refuses, the name the temple would have given her goes to someone else.

The Scapegoat
: The silver cup that disappears from Lenti's table is found in the kitchen worker Amqelifu's bundle, and he says Githiya put it there. Lenti, who cannot stand scenes, dismisses the quarrel by dismissing her. She asks the party to prove the cup left the house with someone else before the story reaches the households that would take her next.

:::secret {title="What the referee knows"}
Lenti's nephew is selling the household's flour and oil to a wharf factor to pay a gambling debt, and he has been using the sealed-sack weights to hide it. Lenti knows, says nothing and has written Githiya into his will as steward for life, because he is afraid she would leave if she knew what she was worth to him.

The merchant captain is not stealing the bronzes. Lenti sold them to him, to cover the debt without a scene, and told nobody. Githiya's suspicion of the captain is the only thing that is false in the account she gives.
:::
