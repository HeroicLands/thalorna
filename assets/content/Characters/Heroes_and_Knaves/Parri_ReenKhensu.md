# Appearance {#appearance}

![[parirnkhns|Par'ri Re'en'Khensu]]{float: top-left}

White-haired and heavy, Par'ri is 5'8", with the golden brown skin and warm brown eyes of the capital, a long face, an aquiline nose, a smooth jawline, generous lips and golden brows. A crescent moon is tattooed on his neck.

# Dossier {#dossier}

The class meets wherever he happens to be sitting. This year that is the step of a house on the edge of a prosperous city in the Ta'Kheperian lands, and whoever comes — a fisherman's boy, a scribe on his rest day, a widow with a question about death — is taught, for nothing, until Parri gets tired. He has been doing it that way for twenty years and sees no reason to stop.

He was born in the capital of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]], to a merchant of real means and a mother whose noble family had lost its money and kept its books. He had three languages by ten and the philosophers by his middle teens, and instead of the counting-house went to the library-schools, where he was made a Master Philosopher and given a teaching chair young. He held it until he was twenty-eight. Then a pilgrimage to the temples of Íšét did something to him he has never described plainly, and he walked away from the chair, the house and the salary, and did not come back.

For more than twenty years after that he moved from city to village to city, setting up a school wherever he stopped and taking anyone who came. He worked a season in the fields to learn what the texts on the cycles of creation had not told him. He lived with fishermen. He slept in temples. People who had never met a philosopher grew fond of him; people whose orthodoxy he questioned did not, and there were places he learned to leave quickly.

Five years ago, at fifty and worn by the road, he settled in the small house. He lives on gifts, on the sale of his collected essays, and on what former students who have done well send him. His name is larger now than when he was in the schools. It is not a comfortable position. The merchants of the city grow greedier, the priesthood stiffer, the poor poorer, and he finds himself, at an age when he had meant to be quiet, working up to saying so.

## Psyche

### Personality

He speaks like a man who knows, and listens like one who does not, and he manages both without contradiction. He can make a hard idea plain to a child and is more interested in how the child sees the world than in whether the child agrees with him. His humour is a slow irony, never a joke at anyone.

He has no pride to speak of; his learning was given to him and he owes it onward. He is patient with anyone who is trying and short with anyone who is cruel or has decided not to think. Ask him a plain question about a sacred matter and he will answer with a story or another question, which is honest and can be maddening.

There is a sadness in him that he does not hide. He has seen a great deal of what people do to each other, and it has left him wise in a way that costs.

### Motivation

Understanding, still, after fifty years of it. He believes there is a truth under what everyone says, and that every person's life holds a piece of it. What he wants for other people is that they see past the edges of their own lives, and treat each other better for it. What he owes, he thinks, is to share what he was given — especially with those who were given nothing. And lately what moves him most is alarm at where his people are going, and the knowledge that saying so will bring the old trouble back.

### Strengths

- **Intellect and Reasoning**: He takes a tangled argument apart and puts it back together so that a fisherman can follow it.
- **Breadth of Knowledge**: He can cook, trade, doctor, farm, sail, forage and sing, and every one of those has ended up in his philosophy.
- **Spiritual and Ritual Understanding**: His own practice is deep and old, and priests come to him with questions about their rites.
- **Compelling Speaker**: People gather when he talks and stay after he stops.
- **Practical Wisdom**: He has lived his ideas in fields and boats, and he knows which of them survive that.

### Weaknesses

- **Limited Physical Capability**: Fifty years of thinking and twenty of walking have left him slow, stiff and no use in a fight.
- **Abstraction and Impracticality**: He can lose the question in front of him in the larger one behind it, and his advice is sometimes true and no help.
- **Social Inflexibility**: He will examine a grief instead of comforting it, and not notice what he has done.
- **Controversial Ideas**: He has enemies who would call his teaching heresy if they could make it stick, and they keep trying.
- **Isolation and Few Resources**: No institution stands behind him. He has students and admirers, and very few people who would take a risk for him.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The Temple of Íšét**: The priesthood of his goddess counts him a real authority, gives him a little money and, when needed, sanctuary — and winces at some of what he says.
- **Grateful Former Students**: Several have prospered, and they send money and commissions for written work. They are most of his living.
- **The City's Intellectual Community**: The scholars and open-minded merchants of the city hire him to lecture and to advise where a question has a moral edge.

### Enemies

- **Religious Fundamentalists and Rigid Orthodoxy**: The conservative priests think his readings dangerous and would silence him if they had cause.
- **Merchant Lords Uncomfortable with Critique**: He has named greed in public, and the men he was describing heard him.

### Affiliations

- **Independent Scholar Network**: He writes to and is visited by other unattached philosophers and seekers across the Ta'Kheperian lands. He belongs to nothing with a roof.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Heresy Accusation**: The temple has gathered his writings and charged him with heresy before its Council of Orthodoxy — the real offence being what he has said about corruption and money in the priesthood. He needs witnesses, his students' testimony, and a defence that shows his ideas inside the doctrine rather than against it, while the priesthood splits over him.

2. **The Declining Culture**: He wants to go out to the villages and record the crafts, songs and arguments that are dying with the old people who hold them, and to found schools that teach them. He is one old man. He needs company on the road, hands to build with, and someone to deal with the authorities who think the whole thing subversive.

3. **The Dangerous Student**: His most brilliant pupil is using what Parri taught to bend people, and perhaps working for a faction that wants his ideas as a tool. Parri does not believe it. Proving it to him will be as hard as stopping the student.

4. **The Prophetic Vision**: Íšét has sent him a vision — a catastrophe, or a transformation; he cannot tell — and he cannot tell either whether it is prophecy or an old man's fear wearing the goddess's face. He wants it read: sacred sites visited, seers consulted, signs gathered. He is not sleeping.

5. **The Lost Texts and the Collector**: A collector has offered to pay for the great work of his life, a gathering of every philosophy of every people into one library. It is what he would have asked for. The collector wants the texts for what is hidden in them, and Parri will find that out late, with the work half done, and have to choose between the library and the man paying for it.

6. **The Successor**: He is training the young scholar who will carry his teaching after him. Others can see what he cannot: that the successor does not share his values, or means to take the teaching somewhere else, or is simply not the one. Telling him will be hard. He is too fond of the young to look.
