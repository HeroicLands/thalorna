Shebas stands 5'7" tall with a light build. She has deep bronze skin, jet black hair, and black eyes. Her features include an aquiline nose, a gentle jawline, and elegantly curved brows. She has a scar on her left arm.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Early Life and Loss

Shebas was born in the capital city of the Empire of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]] into the moderately wealthy Thutmosa family, merchants of spices and exotic goods. Her childhood was comfortable, filled with the abundance that merchant wealth provided. However, at age eight, a terrible plague swept through the lower quarters of the city where her family maintained warehouses and trading posts. Shebas's younger brother, only four years old, contracted the plague despite their family's wealth and protection. She watched her parents employ physician after physician, spend vast sums on treatments and remedies, but none could save the boy. The child died screaming in pain, and young Shebas was left with the conviction that there must be a better way, that someone could have saved her brother if only they had possessed proper knowledge.

After her brother's death, Shebas became obsessed with learning every remedy, every healing art, every herb that might have helped him. Her parents, grieving and indulgent, allowed her to apprentice with an elderly apothecary named **[[being-mesharawse|Meshara the Wise]]**, a woman of great skill who had served the city's poor for decades.

### Training and Awakening

Under Meshara's mentorship for sixteen years, Shebas learned the craft of herbalism and remedy-making and the deeper calling of healing. Meshara was a woman of firm principle—she treated everyone with equal care, rich and poor alike, and her practice was sustained by the gratitude of the poor whom she charged minimally, balanced by the wealthy patients who paid well. Meshara taught Shebas that a healer's greatest power was not wealth but knowledge and compassion, and that the true measure of a healer's success was the health of their community, not the coins in their purse.

When Meshara died at an extraordinary age, Shebas inherited her practice and her philosophy. She took over the apothecary shop, located in the warehouse district where her family's business was based, and gradually transformed it into a destination for the city's poorest and most desperate patients. She began giving discounts, then free treatments, then accepting payment in goods or services from those who could not afford coins. Her reputation grew as a healer of uncommon skill and boundless compassion.

### Present Struggles and Moral Conflict

Now at thirty-four, Shebas has become a legend in the lower quarters—a saint to some, a source of hope to many. However, her generosity has created severe financial difficulties. She barely maintains her shop, and she has been forced to sell family properties and assets to continue her work. Her family views her as wasteful and have largely disowned her, considering her commitment to the poor as a betrayal of merchant principles. Recently, an ambitious apothecary named **Corven Marchant** opened an expensive shop catering to wealthy patients, spreading rumors that Shebas's free treatments are low-quality and that her methods are outdated. He has begun systematically trying to drive her out of business.

## Psyche

### Personality

Shebas is defined by a deep, abiding compassion that colors every aspect of her existence. Her fellow-feeling is such that she feels others' suffering almost as acutely as her own, and this drives her decisions in ways that others often find naive or impractical. She is gentle and kind, speaking softly even to those who snap at her, and she has infinite patience for the sick and desperate. However, this same compassion has become something of a burden—she cannot bring herself to turn away anyone in need, regardless of her own financial or physical condition.

Her sound reasoning and inventiveness allow her to approach problems thoughtfully and come up with new solutions, particularly when it comes to managing symptoms or making remedies from limited materials. She is often found experimenting with new preparations or techniques, driven by a desire to improve her healing abilities. Her strong will means that while she may doubt herself in social or business situations, her commitment to healing never wavers.

Shebas is not naturally assertive or commanding. Her modest eloquence and charm let her communicate well one-to-one, but she lacks the forceful presence needed to dominate conversations or stand firm in negotiations. She tends to apologize profusely, assume blame for situations outside her control, and second-guess her own excellent decisions. This self-doubt, combined with her compassion, makes her vulnerable to exploitation by those less scrupulous than herself.

### Motivation

Shebas is fundamentally driven by a desire that no one should suffer as her brother suffered, that no child should die needlessly of disease or injury when knowledge and care might have saved them. She envisions a world where every person, regardless of wealth, has access to competent healing and where diseases that kill thousands might be prevented or cured. Beyond this noble vision, Shebas also harbors a private shame—she wonders if her generosity is truly virtuous or if it is actually a form of guilt, an attempt to save others because she could not save her brother. This uncertainty drives her to work even harder, to give even more, as though enough service might finally absolve her of a guilt that rationally she knows is not hers to bear.

### Strengths

- **Exceptional Herblore and Natural Knowledge**: Shebas possesses rare knowledge of natural remedies and the properties of various substances. She can identify rare plants, create complex tinctures, and diagnose conditions through close observation.

- **Skilled Physician and Healer**: Her physician's training, combined with her fellow-feeling, allows her to understand patients' conditions both physically and emotionally. She can treat wounds, manage pain, and provide comfort that contributes to healing.

- **High Empathy and Emotional Intelligence**: Her fellow-feeling allows her to understand what patients truly need, often going beyond their stated complaints to address the troubles of heart and mind that contribute to illness. This makes her unusually effective at treating sicknesses that begin in the mind.

- **Animalcraft and Natural Collection Skills**: Shebas can procure raw materials from the natural world, identify safe plants, and understand the healing properties of animal products. She can forage and gather materials others would overlook.

- **Creative Problem-Solving**: Her inventiveness allows her to approach healing challenges in new ways. She can synthesize solutions from limited resources and create new remedies when traditional approaches prove insufficient.

### Weaknesses

- **Poor Business Acumen and Financial Management**: With little physical strength and no commanding presence, Shebas is terrible at managing her business. She cannot negotiate prices effectively, cannot bring herself to demand payment from those in need, and is easily manipulated by those with sharper business instincts.

- **Emotional Vulnerability to Others' Suffering**: Her fellow-feeling, while an asset in healing, makes her emotionally vulnerable. She absorbs her patients' suffering and despair, often becoming depressed or emotionally exhausted by exposure to too much human pain.

- **Difficulty with Assertiveness and Boundary-Setting**: Uncomfortable with deception and limited in eloquence, Shebas struggles to set boundaries or assert herself against those more forceful. She apologizes when others are wrong and blames herself for others' mistakes.

- **Physical Fragility**: Shebas is physically weak and cannot defend herself effectively. She is vulnerable to violence and has no means to protect herself or her patients from physical threats.

- **Vulnerability to Exploitation**: Shebas's compassion and poor business sense make her a target for manipulation. Those seeking free services, discounts, or advantage often exploit her kindness, knowing she will struggle to refuse them.

## Social

### Patrons

- **The City's Poor and Laboring Classes**: Shebas's primary patrons, though they rarely pay in conventional coin. They repay her kindness with loyalty, gratitude, and protection. If anyone threatened Shebas, they would respond with fierce devotion.

- **The Temple of [[lore-setdty|Sét]]**: Though Shebas worships Sét, the god of storms, the local temple occasionally sends patients to her and provides her with access to their herbalist's library and resources.

- **Wealthy Merchants Seeking Discretion**: A few affluent clients seek Shebas specifically because they trust her discretion and know she will never betray their confidences. These clients pay well and help sustain her practice.

### Enemies

- **Apothecary Corven Marchant**: A wealthy, ambitious apothecary who views Shebas as competition and a threat to his practice. He spreads rumors about her methods, undercuts her prices with wealthy clients, and has tried to convince the city's merchant council to regulate her practice out of existence.

- **Debt Collectors and Loan Sharks**: In her financial desperation, Shebas has taken loans from unsavory sources. These creditors grow impatient with her inability to repay and are beginning to threaten her shop and her safety.

- **The City Guard Commander**: A military official who views independent healers as outside the proper authority structure. He has begun harassing Shebas, threatening to arrest her for practicing medicine without proper licensing, though the licensing he references is not officially required.

### Affiliations

- **Independent Apothecary**: Shebas operates her own practice without formal affiliation to any larger institution, though she maintains good standing with the Apothecaries' Guild (which she pays dues to, even if she often disagrees with their practices).

- **The Temples of Ta'Kheperu**: While not a priestess, Shebas maintains relationships with multiple temples and shares knowledge with their healers.

- **The Community of the Lower Quarters**: Informal but strong affiliation with the neighborhoods she serves, who view her as one of their own and would rally to her defense.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Plague Returns**: A new disease begins spreading through the city's lower quarters—a sickness with symptoms Shebas has never encountered before. Wealthy physicians dismiss it as merely a variant of common ailments, but Shebas recognizes a graver disease. As deaths mount among her patients, she becomes obsessed with discovering a cure, working without sleep, neglecting her own health. Her investigation reveals that the plague may be deliberately spread, either as a weapon or by a group seeking to clear the poor from land it wants. Shebas must prove the deliberate spread while fighting to save her patients, and exposing the truth may unsettle the city's government.

2. **The Merchant's Dying Daughter**: A powerful merchant from Shebas's own family comes to her in desperation—his daughter is dying of a rare condition that no physician in the city can treat. He offers an enormous sum of money if Shebas can save her. Shebas becomes deeply invested in the case, spending all her time researching and developing new treatments. She eventually saves the daughter's life through a revolutionary technique, and the grateful merchant offers to fund her practice permanently. However, the merchant expects to become Shebas's patron and eventually her "partner," seeking to direct her toward wealthy clients and away from the poor. The offer is security and means in exchange for her principles.

3. **The Black Market Ingredient**: To develop a cure for a deadly disease affecting her poorest patients, Shebas discovers she needs a rare ingredient that can only be obtained from criminal organizations. A black market merchant approaches her, offering the ingredient in exchange for Shebas using her apothecary as a cover for smuggling dangerous compounds. She can use her practice to pass criminal goods in exchange for the means to save lives, or refuse and let her patients die.

4. **The Rival Healer's Challenge**: A traveling healer of extraordinary skill named **Dominik Silvanus** arrives in the city and begins establishing a healing practice that is even more generous and effective than Shebas's own. Patients flock to him, and Shebas finds herself becoming jealous and competitive—a feeling that shocks her and contradicts her self-image. As she investigates Dominik, she discovers that his practice is being secretly funded by a criminal organization seeking to build loyalty among the poor for purposes of recruitment and control. Exposing the truth means confronting her own capacity for envy.

5. **The Forbidden Knowledge**: An old scholar approaches Shebas with ancient texts describing healing techniques far beyond what any physician knows—techniques that might cure cancer, extend life indefinitely, or prevent aging. The texts also suggest these techniques require unethical practices: cutting organs from the poor to give to others, conducting dangerous experiments on prisoners, or other violations of the body. The scholar is willing to teach Shebas these techniques if she will help him procure test subjects. The choice is between her principles and her capacity to heal, and behind it lies what she believes healing means and which boundaries cannot be crossed whatever good might result.
