# Appearance {#appearance}

![[helnmystks|Hélénaia Mystákês]]{float: top-left}

The scar comes out early. It runs down her right leg from knee to ankle, and she will hitch her robe and show it to anyone ashamed of a mark of their own, as proof that a scar can become something worth looking at.

She is 5'9" and of medium build, light olive skin, green eyes, dark hair worn in a coiled bun dressed with flowers and sacred cords that change with the season and the holy day, so that anyone who knows the calendar can read the date off her head. An oblong face with a prominent nose, a pronounced chin, well-defined lips, angular cheeks, heavy brows. Her robes carry floral work for fertility and growth, and the smell of her rituals — lavender, rose, cinnamon — stays in a room after she has left it.

# Dossier {#dossier}

When the fire came through the city she was seven, and by the time anyone found the children who had been cut off from their parents she had them in a line, counted, and quiet. A priest of the Sacred Forge was among the rescuers. He did not forget it.

Her father worked leather, her mother was a midwife, and she was learning herbs from her mother before she could properly walk. Two years after the fire the priest came back for her with a proposal her parents accepted: she entered the temple at nine, as a novice, and was given the training in full — the texts, the rites, the long cultivation of the part of her that could sit with the god, and the practical arts a priest needs to be any use to a town.

At fourteen a cart struck her on the road while she was carrying medicines to an outlying village. The leg was broken so badly that the physicians talked about whether she would walk. She walked, after a year of prayer and other priests' healing work and a will that surprised the priests who had it to deal with. The scar stayed. She has come to think of it as the beginning of her real training.

She went into healing after that — bodies, at first, and then the other kind. By her late twenties people came to her from across the Byzarian League: married couples after a betrayal, families years into an estrangement, the bereaved who could not put the grief down. She is forty now and has never married, though she has loved. She has trained several younger priestesses in her way of working, leads the temple's transformative healing rites, and is being handed, a little at a time, the training of the temple's next generation.

## Psyche

### Personality

People tell her things within minutes of meeting her. She listens for what they are afraid to say, answers what she heard rather than what she was told, and laughs easily — a real laugh, at the world being lovely.

She is also very sharp about how people lie to themselves, and will say so. The saying is done so kindly that it lands as a gift, but it lands. She has learned, on her own account, that care sometimes means no, and a boundary, and a refusal to help someone go on doing what is destroying them.

She has met the god. The moments of communion are real to her and she holds them loosely — she does not require anyone else to have had them and does not claim they make her right. She is a romantic and an idealist about what people can become, and the world's plain indifference still shocks her more than she would like. She knows it, and works at it.

### Motivation

Healing — of body, of feeling, of spirit — is the highest work there is, she thinks, and the turning of suffering into wisdom is the most sacred. She wants to be the one there when a person is at their lowest, because she believes that is where the divine is served. She wants what she does to outlast her, in the priestesses she trains. And she wants to make beauty in a world that has too little of it; a rite done well, a kindness, a scar turned into a thing that is looked at without shame, are to her the same act of defiance.

### Strengths

- **Reading People**: She sees what a person is feeling and where it comes from, and answers exactly that.
- **Ritual Mastery**: Her rites are exact, and she has made new ones for wounds the old rites did not reach.
- **Healing Knowledge**: Herbs, medicines and the body, learned from her mother and from Lyrian, and joined to the other kind of healing.
- **Authentic Presence**: With her, a person feels attended to. That alone does half the work.
- **Creative Adaptation**: She understands what a tradition is for, and can change its form without losing that.
- **Eloquence and Charm**: She can move a room with words, or with a silence.

### Weaknesses

- **Idealism and Naïveté**: She believes in people, and some of them do not deserve it.
- **Emotional Vulnerability**: What she takes in from others stays in her, and she notices the exhaustion late.
- **Lack of Political Awareness**: Temple politics and the play of power bore her, and someone who understands them can use her.
- **Willpower Limitation**: She knows what has to be done and sometimes cannot make herself do it.
- **Physical Fragility**: The leg works. It tires first, and a fall on it would be bad.

## Social

### Patrons

- **High Priestess Amara**: The temple's head, who counts Hélénaia among its best and consults her on everything to do with healing and the town.
- **The Citizens of the Byzarian League**: Merchants, nobles and the poor whom she has helped, and who have not forgotten.
- **The Order of the Sacred Forge**: The priesthood sends people to her, and holds her up as what a priest of the Forge is.
- **Master Herbalist Lyrian**: Old now, and the source of much of what she knows about plants. He is the closest thing she has to a mentor.

### Enemies

- **Bishop Theron**: A conservative who calls her new rites heresy and works to cut down her authority.
- **The Merchant Syndicate**: Its members think the temple's healing meddles with their labourers, and have moved against the temple more than once.
- **Lord Merchant Valdis**: His daughter left her husband after talking to Hélénaia. He blames her, and has the money and friends to make that matter.

### Affiliations

- **The Temple of the Sacred Forge**: A priest in good standing.
- **The Byzarian League**: Known through it, and on speaking terms with its leaders and its poor alike.
- **The Healing Circle**: Herbalists, physicians and priests who share the hard cases.
- **The Order of Compassion**: A loose fellowship devoted to healing and service, in which she is active.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Impossible Healing**: Someone has come to her with a wound she cannot reach — the defences are too old and the person is sure they deserve the hurt. Working with them she has found the wound is one of many made by the same institution. Healing this one person, or going after what made them: she cannot do both, and she has started to feel that she must.

2. **The Corrupted Colleague**: A fellow priest is healing faster than she can, and more of them, and people are delighted. She has begun to see the seams: promises the god does not make, and a kind of hold on the healed. Saying so will split the priesthood. Not saying so is worse.

3. **The Heretical Offer**: Strangers outside the church say they have found a quicker, surer path to healing than the temple's, and want her help to build it. She feels the institution's weight more than she admits, and the offer is tempting. It might be a real discovery. It might be the kind of thing Bishop Theron has always said she was.

4. **The Love That Complicates**: She has fallen for someone who came to her for healing, and who is asking her to leave the temple. The person is fragile and outside the community, and she does not know whether what she feels is love or a failure of the care she owes them.

5. **The Ritual Gone Wrong**: A healing rite she has done a hundred times has hurt the people in it. Sabotage, a flaw in the rite, something one of them carried in — she does not know, and until she does she does not know whether anything she does is safe.
