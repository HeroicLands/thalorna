# Appearance {#appearance}

![[banutjaut|Bânut Jâ'Utu]]{float: top-left}

Bânut stands 5'3" tall with a light build. She has golden brown skin, dark hair, and hazel eyes. Her features include a high-cheekboned face, a straight narrow nose, a narrow chin, full lips, golden brows, and high cheeks. She has a scar on her left ear.

# Dossier {#dossier}

## Background

### Early Life and Loss

Bânut was born to a family of herbalists and plant cultivators in [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]], where her parents worked for a minor noble house cultivating medicinal and ceremonial plants in carefully maintained garden terraces. From earliest childhood, she was immersed in the world of plants—learning to identify hundreds of species by sight and scent, to understand their properties, to cultivate them in sometimes brutal desert conditions. Her childhood was marked by joy in this work and a close, loving family relationship. Her greatest love was her younger brother **Khôr**, to whom she was intensely devoted.

When Bânut was twenty-eight and Khôr only sixteen, he was seriously injured in a fall from a cliff face—an accident during a botanical expedition to gather rare species. He developed a severe fever and infection, and despite her desperately creative use of every herb and remedy in her knowledge, he died after three months of suffering. The scar on her ear is not, in fact, from violence—it is a ritually inflicted mark of mourning, done in accordance with the traditions of Ta'Kheperu, a visible acknowledgment of her grief that she has never allowed to fade.

### Transformation and Independence

Khôr's death transformed Bânut's relationship to her craft. Rather than simple cultivation for a noble house, she became obsessed with the healing application of plant matter, viewing her work as a spiritual obligation to her brother's memory. She began studying alchemy in earnest, learning to concentrate plant essences into potent remedies, to create poultices and potions of real medicinal value. By thirty, she had built a sufficient reputation that she was able to establish herself independently, moving to a region where she maintained both a small herb garden and a workspace for alchemical work.

### Current Station

Now thirty-five, Bânut has achieved local recognition as a highly skilled healer whose remedies work. She has resisted all attempts to formalize her work or expand her practice into a larger trade, preferring to work at intimate scale, treating individuals whose cases come to her attention. This decision has both strengths and vulnerabilities—she maintains independence and refuses to compromise her standards, but she remains perpetually poor and vulnerable to pressure. Her tendency to give her remedies to those who cannot pay and to undercharge those of limited means keeps her chronically underfunded.

## Psyche

### Personality

Bânut is warm, generous, and concerned with the wellbeing of those who seek her help. She approaches her work with the intensity of someone for whom every healing success is a small victory against death itself, a way of honoring her brother's memory. She is quick to smile, prone to gentle humor, and surprisingly playful given the seriousness of her work. Yet there is a current of deep melancholy in her—she carries the weight of Khôr's death as though it occurred yesterday, and every patient reminds her of all the suffering she cannot prevent or cure.

Her greatest weakness is her tendency to see the best in people and to trust implicitly those who approach her with apparent need. She has been exploited repeatedly by those who use emotional manipulation to convince her to provide valuable remedies at little or no cost, and she seems incapable of hardening herself against this exploitation. Her fellow-feeling, while admirable, undermines her own livelihood.

### Motivation

Bânut is driven by the desire to heal suffering and to prove that her brother's death was not meaningless—that through her work, she can prevent other families from experiencing the loss that devastated hers. She believes her gifts are sacred, intended by the divine [[lore-radty|Rā]] for the benefit of the suffering, and that to charge excessive prices or deny treatment to those in need would be a betrayal of her purpose. She also harbors a secret desire for redemption—though Khôr's death was not her fault, she has never fully absolved herself of the belief that she could have done something more, something better. Her healing work is, in part, an attempt to achieve that impossible absolution through countless other successes.

### Strengths

- **Exceptional Herbal and Alchemical Knowledge**: Her herblore and alchemy combine to make her extraordinarily knowledgeable about plant-based remedies. She understands the properties of hundreds of herbs, knows how to cultivate rare species, and can create complex potions and poultices of real medicinal value.

- **Outstanding Medical Skill**: Her physician's training gives her formal knowledge of anatomy, diagnosis, and treatment that complements her herbal knowledge. She can diagnose complex illnesses, understand the course of a disease, and prescribe treatments that address root causes rather than mere symptoms.

- **Scholarly Foundation**: Her script, folklore, heraldry, and mathematics provide the grounding that allows her to understand texts, maintain careful records, and grasp the theoretical foundations of her practice. She is well-read and capable of advanced study.

- **Exceptional Reasoning and Perception**: Her reasoning and perception make her an excellent diagnostician. She can analyze complex medical situations, recognize patterns, and synthesize information from multiple sources into coherent understanding.

- **Combat Capability and Self-Defense**: Though not a warrior, her quickness, her archery, and her skill with a blade, combined with her dexterity, make her capable of defending herself. She is not defenseless, though she avoids violence when possible.

- **Ritual and Spiritual Knowledge**: Her knowledge of ritual and spirit connects her to the spiritual dimensions of her work, allowing her to understand and participate in the ceremonial aspects of healing that are important in Ta'Kheperu's culture.

### Weaknesses

- **Insufficient Strength**: Strength is her weakest quality; she is physically weak. She cannot overpower anyone or perform tasks requiring brute force. This is primarily relevant only in combat or situations demanding physical strength.

- **Poor Eloquence**: Her eloquence is notably poor, making it difficult for her to advocate for herself, negotiate prices, or defend her position against criticism. She struggles to articulate her values clearly, leaving her vulnerable to those more verbally skilled.

- **Naive Generosity and Poor Judgment of Character**: Her fellow-feeling and charm are strong, but combined with her emotional vulnerability regarding her brother's memory, they lead her to consistently misjudge people's intentions. She trusts too easily and can be manipulated by those who present themselves as needing help.

- **Inadequate Pricing and Business Management**: She has no explicit business skills and tends to drastically undercharge for her remedies. Those well able to pay often pay less than market value, while those without resources receive treatment at no cost. This chronic underpricing prevents her from saving money or improving her situation.

- **Vulnerability to Religious Pressure**: While not explicitly weak, she is sensitive to accusations of heresy or improper practice. Church officials' suspicions cause her real distress, and she lacks the confidence to stand firmly against religious authority.

- **Emotional Fragility Regarding Her Brother**: Any mention of loss, death, or family tragedy can bring on a grief that temporarily incapacitates her. She has not truly come to terms with her loss, instead pouring it into her work.

## Social

### Patrons

- **Master Herbalist Karesh**: An elderly herbalist who has become something of a mentor figure to Bânut. Karesh has quietly helped her acquire rare plant specimens and occasionally provides financial support when Bânut's circumstances become desperate, though without Bânut's explicit knowledge.

- **The Village Healer Netesh**: A local healer who refers complex cases to Bânut and defends her against criticism, seeing in her work a worthy extension of his own practice.

- **Noblewoman Kiya of House Theperu**: A wealthy patron who employs Bânut to create custom remedies. Though Kiya pays well, her actual motivation may be less pure than it appears—she may be cultivating Bânut for purposes Bânut does not yet understand.

### Enemies

- **Church Inquisitor Malachi the Censorious**: A zealous religious official who views Bânut's independent practice and her use of certain botanicals as potentially heretical. He has begun investigating her work, questioning whether she employs forbidden magical techniques, and warning others away from her services. His scrutiny is partially motivated by religious concern, but also by his general hostility toward women working independently outside male supervision.

- **The Merchant Alchemist Pheron**: A wealthy alchemist who produces remedies in bulk and who views Bânut as a threat to his hold on the trade. Pheron produces cheaper, less effective alternatives and has begun spreading rumors that Bânut's high-quality remedies contain dangerous ingredients and that her methods are unsanitary.

- **A Manipulative Former Patient**: A man who obtained multiple valuable remedies from Bânut by claiming to be terminally ill, then used them to poison a political rival. When the crime was discovered, Bânut's remedies were identified as the murder weapon, and though she was exonerated of wrongdoing, the scandal damaged her reputation and made her vulnerable to further accusations.

### Affiliations

- **Independent Alchemists Alliance**: A loose collective of practitioners who maintain independence from large commercial organizations or the formal Guild. Bânut is loosely affiliated, though her participation is minimal and her commitment is primarily to her own practice rather than to collective action.

- **The Society of Botanical Scholars**: An informal network of naturalists and herbalists who correspond and occasionally meet to discuss botanical discoveries. Bânut's knowledge is valued, though her limited literacy and confidence prevent her from fully participating in scholarly discourse.

## Plot Hooks

1. **The Poisoning Accusation**: A prominent figure dies under mysterious circumstances after seeking treatment from Bânut. Though the death was not caused by her remedies—indeed, her potions were the only thing temporarily alleviating the victim's suffering—Inquisitor Malachi seizes the opportunity to formally accuse her of poisoning. She must gather evidence of her innocence while discovering the true cause of death, all while her reputation is systematically destroyed and patients cease coming to her out of fear.

2. **The Brother's Resurrection Possibility**: A scholar arrives claiming to represent an organization that has developed a ritual that might allow temporary resurrection of the recently dead—a procedure that would allow Bânut to speak with Khôr one final time. The cost is extraordinary, and the scholar's claims are suspiciously convenient to Bânut's deepest emotional desire. 3. **The Plague That Defies All Remedies**: A disease of mysterious origin begins spreading through the region, and despite her best efforts, Bânut's remedies prove ineffective against it. She must either develop entirely new treatments while the disease spreads, potentially requiring collaboration with those she distrusts, or admit that her knowledge, for all its depth, has limits. 4. **The Apprentice She Cannot Refuse**: A young person with a real healing gift and desperate circumstances seeks apprenticeship with Bânut. Taking an apprentice would expand her capacity and potentially preserve her knowledge, but it would also force her to emotionally engage with another person in ways she has avoided since Khôr's death. The young person may also harbor dangerous secrets or abilities that attract unwanted attention.

5. **The Merchant Noble's Dark Request**: Noblewoman Kiya reveals her true purpose: she wishes Bânut to create a remedy that produces the appearance of death while maintaining life—useful for faking someone's demise for political purposes. The request is explicit blackmail; Kiya controls information that could destroy Bânut, and demands she comply. Bânut can create something that violates her ethics, refuse and risk exposure, or find a third path that defeats both Kiya's plan and the blackmail, which would likely require dangerous allies and hard choices.
