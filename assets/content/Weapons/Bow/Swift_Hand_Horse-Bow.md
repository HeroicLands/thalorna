Barely a yard unstrung and recurved hard enough to fight the string back into its case, the Swift Hand bow is built to be drawn from a moving horse with one hand while the other holds the reins. The academy that teaches it—the mounted-archery tradition, "sought by every Vedyari kingdom that fields cavalry"—takes a recruit only after he can already ride, and the bow itself is the smallest and most expensive thing the four academies of Dhanurkota sell.

Horn and sinew laid over a wood core give it the draw weight of a much longer stave in a bow short enough to clear a saddle-bow and a horse's neck without catching. It loses reach beside the High Draw longbow and never pretends to compete on it: what it sells is a mounted skirmisher's rate of fire and the maneuverable range that reach cannot buy. Every major Vedyari kingdom that fields horse keeps standing contracts with the Swift Hand academy, and a graduate's bow case is stamped with the hall's mark the day he is passed fit to ride out with it.
