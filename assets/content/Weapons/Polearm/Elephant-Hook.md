A short iron spike backed by a blunt hook, set on a haft no longer than a man's arm and bound with rawhide for a grip that will not slip when wet. It is the mahout's everyday tool—the spike for direction, the hook for purchase behind an elephant's ear when a word will not do—and every kingdom that fields elephants keeps a supply of them coming out of the same smithies that arm the hosts.

In a footman's hand it is a poor substitute for a proper polearm and nobody pretends otherwise: the haft is too short to keep a swordsman at range and the head too light to punch through mail. What it does is close a grip a footman would rather not offer bare-handed, the hook catching a limb or a weapon-arm long enough for a second man to finish the work, which is why it goes to war in the first place rather than staying in the picket lines.
