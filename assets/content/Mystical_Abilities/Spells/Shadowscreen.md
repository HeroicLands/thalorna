The caster draws both hands apart as though parting a curtain, and between them a shimmering wall of agitated air springs into being, its surface rippling with heat-haze distortions that warp every image passing through it. Targets viewed through the screen appear displaced—shifted several feet from their true position—turning aimed shots into guesswork and melee charges into flailing lunges at empty air. Projectiles that strike the screen are deflected by unpredictable micro-gusts, their trajectories bent just enough to miss vital targets.

The screen is translucent from the caster's side, providing a hazy but usable view of the enemy, while from the opposing side it presents a wall of migraine-inducing visual noise. It can be anchored to a fixed position—across a doorway, along a battle line—or moved slowly with the caster's advance. Physical passage through the screen is possible but disorienting; anyone who steps through experiences a momentary vertigo as their visual reference frame snaps back to reality. Multiple overlapping Shadowscreens compound the displacement effect, making the area behind them a near-impenetrable visual maze.
