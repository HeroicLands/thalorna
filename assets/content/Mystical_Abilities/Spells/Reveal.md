The caster extends their awareness through the earth and stone beneath their feet, and the hidden world below reveals itself to their magical perception. Buried metal objects glow in the caster's mind's eye – buried weapons, hidden vaults, mineral veins, old coins lost in garden soil, or the iron fittings of a concealed trap mechanism. The perception extends both downward and laterally, providing a three-dimensional map of metallic objects within the spell's radius.

The spell's sensitivity can be adjusted from a broad sweep that detects large deposits and significant objects to a fine scan that reveals individual nails in a buried coffin. The depth of perception depends on the caster's power, ranging from a few feet below the surface for novices to substantial depths for masters. Non-metallic objects are invisible to the spell, meaning a wooden chest buried without metal fittings would go undetected, while a sword wrapped in cloth would glow as clearly as one lying exposed on the ground.
