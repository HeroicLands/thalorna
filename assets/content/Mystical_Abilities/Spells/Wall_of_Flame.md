The caster raises both hands and a wall of flame erupts from the ground – but these are not ordinary flames. The fire burns black at its heart, edged in deep crimson, and it radiates not just heat but an oppressive psychic weight that fills observers with a visceral, primal dread. The temperature at the wall's surface exceeds what natural fire can produce, hot enough to soften stone and turn sand to glass.

Those who stand near the doom curtain experience an instinctive terror that overrides rational thought, driving them away with the same urgency as actual immolation. The wall cannot be extinguished by water, smothered by earth, or scattered by wind – only magical counter-force or the exhaustion of the sustaining energy can bring it down. Its creation is enormously draining, and the caster's control over its positioning is absolute but temporary; once placed, the curtain remains fixed until it expires.
