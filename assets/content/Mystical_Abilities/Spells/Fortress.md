The caster touches the ground with a hand, foot, or elemental focus and commands the earth to heave upward, creating a mound of packed soil with a volume of five-foot cubes proportional to their Spell Index. The mound must distend from existing ground – it cannot be conjured in thin air or atop solid metal, stone, or wood. The caster directs the mound's growth in any configuration of contiguous five-foot cubes, provided the final shape is no more than ten feet high and no higher than it is wide, giving it the stability of a natural earthwork rather than an impossible pillar.

The rising earth flows around metal objects, wooden structures, and plants, enveloping and potentially burying them, but it also lifts creatures standing atop it as it grows. At the end of the spell's duration the mound collapses flat, returning the terrain to its original grade. At higher masteries the mound's range extends, it may be sculpted twice as high while maintaining stability, and at the highest levels it can rise to fifteen feet with a width of merely five feet – a true rampart. The casting takes two rounds and the mound persists for minutes proportional to the caster's Spell Index, making it suitable for impromptu fortification, creating elevated firing positions, or blocking passages.
