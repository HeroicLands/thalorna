The caster raises both hands toward the sky and brings them sweeping down, and fire falls like rain. Streams of flame pour from empty air, splashing across surfaces, pooling in low areas, and spreading with terrifying speed. Within seconds, the targeted area becomes an inferno—a churning hell of heat, smoke, and roaring flame that consumes everything flammable and blisters everything that is not.

The conflagration is self-sustaining once ignited, feeding on whatever fuel the environment provides and generating its own wind as superheated air rises and cooler air rushes in to replace it. The caster can direct the initial placement of the flames but has limited control once the firestorm begins to feed itself, making it a blunt instrument best deployed against areas the caster is willing to see utterly destroyed. The heat at the center of a fully developed firestorm can soften metal and crack stone.
