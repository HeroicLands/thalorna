The caster weaves strands of tangible shadow into a sticky, clinging web that entangles and restrains. The shadows thicken and reach out like dark tendrils, wrapping around limbs, sealing shut doors, and binding objects in place. The web has real physical substance—cold, damp, and slightly yielding, like touching something that exists halfway between cloth and smoke.

Shadowbind draws on water magic's dominion over darkness and concealment, manifesting shadow as a quasi-physical substance. The bindings are strongest in dim light and weaken in bright sunlight or near strong fire, eventually dissolving into wisps of harmless darkness. Targets caught in the web describe a sensation of being held underwater—pressure from all sides, cold seeping in, and a muffling of sound that makes calling for help difficult.
