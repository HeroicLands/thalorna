The caster extends two fingers and a needle-thin beam of concentrated light lances from the fingertips, searing through the air with an audible hiss. The beam is hot enough to char wood, blister exposed skin, and leave a glowing score-line on stone, though it lacks the raw destructive force of fire magic—its danger lies in precision rather than area devastation. A single well-aimed Lightstrike can blind an eye, sever a bowstring, or ignite an oil-soaked wick from across a courtyard.

The beam travels in a perfectly straight line and cannot curve around obstacles, making cover an effective defense. In fog or heavy rain, water droplets scatter the beam's coherence, reducing its range and intensity to a fraction of its clear-weather potential. The caster can sustain the beam for a few seconds at most before the concentration required becomes unbearable, and each successive casting in rapid sequence diminishes in power as the surrounding air heats and loses its focusing properties. At extreme range the beam diffuses into a harmless warm glow.
