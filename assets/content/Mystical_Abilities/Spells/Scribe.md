The caster enchants a writing instrument – quill, stylus, charcoal stick – and it springs to life, hovering above a prepared surface and transcribing with mechanical precision. The quill can be directed to record spoken words as they are uttered, taking faithful dictation at conversational speed. Alternatively, the caster can direct it mentally, inscribing their thoughts onto parchment without the need to speak or even to focus their full attention on the task.

The transcription is literal and precise, capturing exactly what is said or thought without interpretation, editing, or summary. This can be both a strength and a limitation – the quill faithfully records every stammer, false start, and tangential digression alongside the substantive content. Skilled practitioners learn to think in clean, organized sentences when using the quill for direct thought transcription, a discipline that requires considerable practice. The enchantment persists for hours, making it invaluable for recording lengthy proceedings, capturing testimony, or documenting research.
