The caster tears a luminous aperture in the fabric of space, its edges crackling with the strain of holding two distant points in forced proximity. Through the shimmering disc, the destination is visible – wavering slightly as though seen through heated air – and passage is instantaneous for anyone who steps through. The portal connects two points the caster has personally visited, and the accuracy of the connection depends entirely on the clarity of their memory.

Maintaining an open portal demands enormous concentration and a continuous expenditure of energy; most casters can sustain one for mere minutes before exhaustion forces them to let it collapse. The portal is bidirectional – creatures at the destination can step through to the origin just as easily – making careless placement a significant tactical risk. Establishing a portal to an area protected by wards is extremely difficult, as the ward's resistance must be overcome before the spatial connection can stabilize. The most skilled practitioners can anchor a portal to a physical framework, extending its duration at the cost of portability.
