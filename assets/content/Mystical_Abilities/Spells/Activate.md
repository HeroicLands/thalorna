The caster weaves a conditional clause into a prepared spell or magical construct, setting the terms under which it will spring to life. The conditions can be remarkably specific – the presence of a particular individual, the utterance of a password, the passage of a celestial body across a certain point in the sky, or the breaking of a physical seal. Until those conditions are met, the spell lies dormant and nearly undetectable, consuming no energy.

The complexity of the trigger is limited by the caster's skill; novices might set a simple proximity trigger, while masters can create elaborate conditional chains involving multiple simultaneous criteria. Poorly defined conditions risk premature or failed activation – a trigger set to "when an enemy approaches" requires the magic to somehow judge intent, a notoriously unreliable proposition. The most reliable triggers are objective and physical: a door opening, a weight upon a pressure plate, the rising of the sun.
