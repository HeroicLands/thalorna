The caster raises both arms and chants a rising incantation into the wind; the air above responds by coalescing into a luminous, vaguely avian figure of compressed atmosphere and crackling static, its wingspan throwing dancing shadows across the ground. This wind-spirit possesses rudimentary intelligence and considerable speed, capable of scouting miles of terrain in minutes, relaying simple sensory impressions back to its summoner, and engaging physical threats with buffeting gusts and razor-edged vortices that slice like invisible blades.

The spirit's loyalty is absolute but its comprehension is limited; commands must be simple and unambiguous, or the entity defaults to circling protectively overhead. It cannot speak, but communicates through shifts in air pressure and temperature that the caster learns to interpret with practice—a warm pulse for safety, a cold shudder for danger. The spirit is vulnerable to earth-based magic, which grounds its constituent winds and causes it to dissipate, and to enclosed spaces, where its freedom of movement is fatally curtailed. When its energy is spent, the spirit unravels in a spiraling column of warm air that carries a faint scent of rain.
