The caster extends their hand toward a metallic object and exerts an invisible magnetic grip upon it. The targeted metal lurches toward the caster—or in whatever direction the caster wills—with a force proportional to the caster's power and inversely proportional to the object's mass. A sword can be wrenched from a warrior's grip. A ring of keys can fly across a room into the caster's waiting hand. Loose coins scatter toward the caster's position like iron filings drawn to a lodestone.

The spell affects only metallic materials, passing harmlessly through wood, leather, stone, and flesh. This selectivity makes it ideal for disarming metal-weapon-bearing opponents while leaving them otherwise unaffected, or for manipulating metal mechanisms—bolts, latches, chains—from a distance. The force is sustained rather than instantaneous, allowing the caster to guide metal objects along specific paths rather than simply yanking them in a straight line. Heavier objects require more sustained effort, and items firmly secured may resist rather than break free.
