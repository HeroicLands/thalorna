The caster cups a hand beside their mouth and speaks in a low tone; the words are lifted by an invisible thread of wind so fine that it passes through crowds, around corners, and over walls without disturbing so much as a candle flame. The message arrives at the intended recipient's ear as a soft, intimate whisper indistinguishable from a companion leaning close—private, clear, and utterly inaudible to anyone standing even a pace away.

The spell requires the caster to know the recipient's location with reasonable accuracy; aiming at a vague direction produces garbled fragments that arrive at random ears, potentially compromising the very secrecy the spell is meant to ensure. The wind-thread can navigate moderate obstacles—an open window, a gap beneath a door, a ventilation shaft—but is blocked by sealed stone or dense magical wards. In heavy wind the thread must fight against cross-currents, reducing its effective range. Despite these limitations, Murmur remains the preferred tool of spymasters, conspirators, and lovers throughout the realms.
