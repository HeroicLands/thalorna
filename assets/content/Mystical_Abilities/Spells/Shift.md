The caster channels destructive Physéra energy into a wooden object, inflicting warpage points proportional to their Spell Index that twist, bend, and distort the target's structure. When accumulated warpage equals or exceeds an object's warpage resistance, the object is destroyed outright – a spear snaps, a door splinters, a drawbridge buckles. Even partial warpage degrades function: reaching half an object's resistance makes it unusable, and exceeding a quarter of its resistance imposes penalties to any test involving the object's function. The effect scales with target size – arrow shafts and clubs require minimal warpage to break, while carts and drawbridges demand substantially more.

Successive castings against the same object increase warpage by one point each, up to a maximum proportional to the Spell Index, though mages cannot improve upon an initial Critical Success casting. At higher masteries the spell may be cast in reverse to remove existing warpage, restoring bent or damaged wooden objects to their original condition. The caster can also redistribute excess warpage from one object to another within range, and maximum warpage per casting increases further. The spell requires two rounds at close range and its effect is instantaneous – the wood warps and holds its new distorted shape permanently. Shift is the bane of wooden fortifications, siege equipment, and ship hulls, and prudent defenders reinforce critical wooden structures with metal fittings specifically to resist it.
