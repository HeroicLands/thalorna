The Blackpine Wolves are a small, vicious band of road bandits who prey on travelers along the crumbling old Vylarian trade road that threads through the dark pine forests of [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]]. They are not a disciplined military company or a romantic band of outlaws—they are desperate, violent men and one very dangerous woman, held together by fear of their leader and the absence of better options.

## Organization

The gang is led by [[being-dagulfvthr|Dagbán yra Vethar wur Thrareth]], a [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] runaway serf with a cruel streak and a gift for ambush. He rules through unpredictability and sudden violence, rewarding loyalty one day and punishing imagined slights the next. There is no formal hierarchy below him, though [[being-thrwldhldskrn|Thrawald yra Hildskorn wur Thrastein]] is a de facto second-in-command by virtue of seniority and sheer physical size.

The current membership stands at six:

- [[being-dagulfvthr|Dagbán]]—Balthár. Former shepherd turned brigand. Cold, watchful, and feared.
- [[being-thrwldhldskrn|Thrawald]]—Muscle. A huge, dull-witted former deserter from the Vylarian garrison. Follows orders because thinking is harder.
- [[being-skthldfrdbn|Skathilda yra Frodbán wur Vandgrund]]—Scout and knife-fighter. Small, fast, and the most dangerous member of the gang after Dagbán himself. Everyone is afraid of her.
- [[being-vndrcgrwld|Vandarik yra Garwald wur Fródhaven]]—The talker. A confidence man and gambler who gathers intelligence in border taverns. Charming, dishonest, and loyal to nothing.
- [[being-hrodrrnthl|Hrodar yra Arnthúl wur Erthhaven]]—Woodsman and fighter. A bitter former timber cutter who hates the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] with a cold, personal fury.
- [[being-brunjarskathhel|Brunjár yra Skathhelm wur Druthholt]]—Lookout. The youngest and most reluctant member, a freckle-faced boy with a guilty conscience and a good sling arm.

## Territory

The Blackpine Wolves operate along a stretch of the old imperial trade road roughly two days' ride north of the Vylarian border garrison. Their territory is a dense section of ancient dark pine forest, riddled with fog-filled valleys, overgrown ruins, and crumbling Vylarian milestones. The terrain strongly favors ambush—narrow road, poor sightlines, abundant cover—and the gang knows every trail, gully, and hiding place within a day's walk.

Their camp shifts regularly to avoid detection, but tends to cluster around a set of half-collapsed Vylarian waystation ruins deep in the pines, where stone walls provide shelter and the ruins offer multiple escape routes into the forest.

## Methods

The gang's operations are simple and brutal. Vandarik scouts targets in border taverns, identifying merchant caravans and estimating their escort strength and cargo value. The gang sets up ambush positions along the road—felled trees, concealed bowmen, and Dagbán's preferred tactic of attacking from both sides of the road simultaneously. Brunjár is a forward lookout, using bird calls to signal the caravan's approach and composition.

Resistance is met with swift, overwhelming violence. Compliance is met with robbery and, usually, survival—though Dagbán's mood is unpredictable, and captives are sometimes treated with gratuitous cruelty. The gang avoids targets with serious military escorts and never attacks Vylarian garrison patrols directly.

Stolen goods are fenced through contacts Vandarik maintains in border towns, at a fraction of their value.

## Internal Tensions

The Blackpine Wolves are not a stable organization. Several fault lines threaten to tear the gang apart:

- **Dagbán's paranoia** is worsening. He suspects betrayal from every direction and has become more erratic in his punishments.
- **Skathilda's ambition** is poorly concealed. She is smarter, faster, and more ruthless than Dagbán, and everyone knows it—including Dagbán.
- **Vandarik's loyalty** extends exactly as far as his self-interest, and he is always calculating whether the gang is still his best option.
- **Brunjár's conscience** makes him a liability. He has shown mercy to captives and may eventually break under the weight of his guilt.
- **Hrodar's vendetta** against the empire could lead him into reckless action that exposes the gang.

Only Thrawald is stable—because he doesn't care enough about anything to cause trouble.

## Using the Blackpine Wolves

The gang works best as a recurring low-level threat for parties traveling through Vristwald. They can serve as:

- A straightforward combat encounter (road ambush)
- A moral dilemma (Brunjár's guilt, Skathilda's offer to betray the gang)
- A source of information about the Vristwald interior (captured or cooperative members)
- A complication in larger plots (the gang's activities draw unwanted attention to the area)
- An object lesson in what happens to people ground down by imperial exploitation (every member of the gang except Vandarik was driven to banditry by the empire's failures)
