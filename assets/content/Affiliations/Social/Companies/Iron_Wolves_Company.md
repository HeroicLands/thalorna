_Byzarian: Lupi Ferri_

- **Type:** Independent mercenary company
- **Region:** [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] principally; operates throughout western Ankaris under contract
- **Strength:** Approximately one hundred and twenty effectives at present muster, with reserve and auxiliary perhaps doubling that figure
- **Status:** Operating; unsigned with the [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Málalidabandalag]]; has held no consistent contract with the Byzarian League's military in over a decade

## Overview

The Iron Wolves are the kind of mercenary company that other mercenary companies refer to in the third person at length and avoid sharing camps with when possible. They are not the largest free company in western Ankaris and they are not the most prestigious; what they are is _available_. The contracts other companies refuse—the ones that involve the suppression of villages that have failed to submit to a local lord's authority, the protection of operations that the regional authorities have declared unlawful, the breaking of strikes in mining concerns where the workers have proven inconveniently organized—find their way, eventually, to the Iron Wolves.

The company's reputation is what makes it valuable to its employers and what makes it loathed by everyone else. An employer who hires the Iron Wolves knows what he is getting: a unit that will execute the contract as written, that will not flinch at orders that other companies would refuse, and that will not, afterward, complain. The premium charged for this dependability is substantial. So is the body count that has accumulated, over thirty years of operation, in the wake of the company's contracts.

The current captain, **Lukás Themiras**, is the third in the company's history. He inherited a company already widely feared and has, by all accounts, made it more so. He is professional, methodical, and personally disinclined to cruelty; the cruelty is institutional and predates him. Lukás's working philosophy is that the company exists to execute paid work without sentiment, that the moral character of the work is the employer's problem, and that the company's reputation for ferocity is itself an asset that should be cultivated. He is, in his own assessment, a businessman.

## Backstory

The Iron Wolves were founded forty-one years ago by **Marcos Themiras** (no relation to the current captain, despite the shared family name; the coincidence is one of the company's running internal jokes) as a small heavy-infantry outfit operating on the Byzarian-Aurèldían frontier. Marcos was a former League legion officer cashiered for ordering reprisals on civilians during a frontier campaign; the cashiering had cost him his career but not his connections, and the company found steady contract work with merchant houses and minor lords whose interests the League's regular forces would not serve.

The defining incident in the company's history occurred twenty-six years ago, in a small village called **Trianthion** in the Byzarian hinterland. The village had refused to submit to a local lord's authority—refused his tax collectors, refused his garrison, and (most provocatively) sheltered a fugitive bandit chief who had robbed the lord's caravan. The lord hired the Iron Wolves to take the village. The company's instructions were standard: enter the village, seize the bandit chief, and demonstrate the cost of resistance. The standard demonstration, in such cases, was the burning of a few houses and the public hanging of the bandit chief.

What occurred at Trianthion was substantially more than the standard. By the end of the day every adult in the village had been killed. The bandit chief was among them but indistinguishable, by the time the bodies were counted, from any other victim. The company collected its standard rate and departed. The lord paid without comment.

Trianthion is the central wound in the company's modern history. It produced one famous defection ([[being-kyrksptrks|Kyriákos Patrikios]], whose subsequent decades of attempted obscurity in a peripheral village have not protected him from the company's standing interest in his whereabouts). It produced the lasting reputation that has since made the company synonymous, in League political discourse, with the kinds of work that the League prefers not to acknowledge purchasing. And it produced the internal culture—a self-perception of professional ruthlessness—that has shaped the company's recruitment, training, and contract selection ever since.

## Organization

The company is structured along legion lines, with adaptations for mercenary work:

- **Captain**—Lukás Themiras; commands the whole.
- **Two First-Sergeants**—operational deputies; one handles training and standing administration, the other handles field operations.
- **Five Section-Lieutenants**—each commanding a section of approximately twenty effectives organized for combined operations (heavy infantry, light infantry, archery, scouts, support).
- **Quartermaster and Paymaster**—civilian staff under contract; manage logistics and accounts.
- **Standing Cadre of Sergeants**—six to ten experienced veterans below the lieutenants; the company's backbone in any engagement.

### Notable Personnel

- **Captain Lukás Themiras**—professional, methodical, cold-blooded by selection rather than by temperament. He has no record of personal cruelty and is regarded by his men as fair, even generous. His decisions about which contracts to accept are the moral question that he, alone among the company, refuses to ask.
- **First-Sergeant Karûsh**—Lukás's senior field deputy and a Trianthion veteran. Karûsh was a lieutenant at Trianthion under the previous captain and has never forgiven [[being-kyrksptrks|Kyriákos Patrikios]] for his subsequent desertion. Karûsh keeps an informal standing inquiry running for any rumor of Kyriákos's whereabouts and is widely understood to be the principal reason the company has not formally retired the bounty.
- **First-Sergeant Demetrion Aikastos**—the administrative deputy; a former Vylarian quartermaster recruited for his logistics. Universally respected, occasionally feared.
- **Lieutenant Ariana Pelagiou**—commands the company's archery section; the only senior woman in command; widely considered the most tactically gifted officer in the company.

## Territory and Operations

The Iron Wolves maintain a permanent base of operations at a fortified compound outside the small town of **Veridon** in the Byzarian hinterland. The compound includes barracks, training fields, an extensive armory, stables for the cavalry detachment, and the company's archives. The town of Veridon depends economically on the company's presence, an arrangement that has produced both stability and a population whose attitudes toward the company are uniformly opaque to outsiders.

Operational deployments take the company throughout western Ankaris under whatever contract is current. Recent work has included:

- A two-season campaign in the Provenzian frontier suppressing brigandage on behalf of a coalition of merchant lords.
- A short, brutal mining strike-breaking operation in a Heliónite copper concern.
- A long-running security contract for a Vylarian provincial governor whose name the company will not disclose.
- Standing offers from at least two of the [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]] for direct retainer; the company has, so far, declined.

## Methods

The company's operational doctrine is conventional military, applied at mercenary scale. The Iron Wolves favor disciplined heavy infantry supported by archery and light scouting elements; they do not specialize in any exotic warfare style but execute conventional tactics with notable competence. What distinguishes them is their willingness to apply this competence in contexts other professional companies refuse: against civilian populations, against insurrectionary movements with substantial popular support, against targets that the company's officers privately understand to be legitimately defending themselves.

Training is rigorous and selective. The company recruits primarily from cashiered League regulars, from frontier-hardened freemen, and from the surviving children of villages it has previously suppressed (a recruitment pipeline that the officers privately consider valuable; a recruit with personal cause to hate the company is a recruit who will execute its work without questioning whether it should be done).

## Internal Dynamics

- **The Karûsh Question.** First-Sergeant Karûsh's obsession with hunting down Kyriákos Patrikios consumes administrative bandwidth and produces standing tensions with Lukás, who considers it a personal grievance being conducted on the company's time. Lukás tolerates it because Karûsh is otherwise indispensable.
- **The Reform Whisper.** Lieutenant Ariana Pelagiou has, quietly, begun arguing in officers' council for the company to refuse the worst class of contracts—strike-breaking and civilian suppression—on the grounds that such work is increasingly attracting League investigation. Lukás has not ruled but is privately persuadable.
- **The Generational Split.** The younger officers, recruited under Lukás's tenure, have less personal investment in the company's hard reputation than the older sergeants do. A succession crisis on Lukás's eventual retirement (he is fifty-three) is likely to be defined by which faction holds the senior cadre's vote.

## Relations

- **The Byzarian League**—formally at arm's length; informally, the League knows what the company does and lets it operate because the company occasionally executes contracts the League itself would have preferred to commission. League investigations into Iron Wolves activity are reliably inconclusive.
- **The [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Málalidabandalag]]**—unsigned and unwelcome. The Compact has refused the Iron Wolves' application for membership twice, both times for the company's pattern of civilian-target contracts. The Compact's senior captains regard the company with professional disdain.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]]**—interested counterparty. Both the [[affiliation-velvethand|Velvet Hand]] and the [[affiliation-theashroad|Ash Road]] have, at various points, attempted to retain the company; the company has declined direct syndicate retainers while accepting contracts that it understood to be syndicate-funded through intermediaries.
- **[[being-kyrksptrks|Kyriákos Patrikios]]**—the great unfinished business. The company's standing position is that Kyriákos is a deserter and a security risk whose silence cannot be assumed indefinitely. There is a quiet bounty on him; no one has yet claimed it because no one has yet located him.

## Identifying Marks

The company's heraldry is a wolf's head, in profile, in dull gray on a black field; the wolf's eye is marked in red. Effectives wear the badge as a small enamel pin on the cloak; officers wear it as a brass medallion at the throat. The captain's medallion is silver. Veterans of long standing wear additionally a tattoo of the company mark on the inside of the right forearm; the tattoo is given on completion of the first full year of service.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Located Deserter.** A rumor reaches First-Sergeant Karûsh that Kyriákos Patrikios has been seen, still alive, in a particular village. The company is contracted in the field; Karûsh requests detachment of a small unit to pursue the rumor. Lukás's response—grant the request, refuse it, or grant it under modified terms—will shape the company's next several months.
- **The Refused Contract.** A wealthy patron offers the company an extraordinary fee to suppress a developing peasant rebellion in the Aurèldían countryside. The contract is, by Iron Wolves standards, well within scope; Ariana Pelagiou is the first officer in years to argue formally against accepting. The officers' council vote will be the company's first real test of the reformist faction's strength.
- **The Compromised Officer.** Demetrion Aikastos, the administrative First-Sergeant, has been quietly accepting payments from a [[affiliation-velvethand|Velvet Hand]] handler in exchange for the company's contract and movement records. The discovery, when it comes, will be a major internal crisis—Demetrion is universally respected, and his information is operationally devastating.
- **The Trianthion Survivor.** An elderly woman has begun, openly and publicly, claiming to be a survivor of the Trianthion massacre and demanding the League's intervention against the company. The woman's claim is plausible. Her testimony, if credited, would force the League to act. The company must decide between waiting out the political storm and acting to silence her—and acting carries risks neither captain Lukás nor the League's quiet patrons are quite ready to authorize.
