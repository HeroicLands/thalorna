A barge-master who brings grain down the [[place-vylarisrvr|River Vylaris]] from the [[place-hylen|Hilen]] landings describes the company in two sentences: "Nobody has robbed a barge string above the river gate for three years. The men who stopped it ride better horses than [[affiliation-sorvan|The Imperial Guard]]." The first sentence is the company's contract. The second is the question every faction in [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] would like answered.

Type
: company in imperial pay

Region
: the [[place-outskirtsorz|Outskirts]] of Orzara, upriver of the city along the River Vylaris and the grain road

## Overview

The Clean Shields have no other name in common use. The war council's book enters them as the company of Captain [[being-shttrkssm|Shattar Kassumi]], and the city calls them by their shields, which carry no device. A company in Orzara's sight usually paints its shields with its own badge or its paymaster's arms. The Clean Shields' are faced with bare gray leather, and the company's answer is that a shield is painted when there is something worth painting on it.

The company holds the grain road and the river outside the city's line, where the city's law ends and the province's law sends nobody. It keeps the barge moorings upstream of the river gate, where the grain barges from Hilen lie up by day and wait for dark to come down to the quays, and it escorts the barge strings down from the landings. That is the whole of its contract. Its arms, its horses and its pay are those of a company with a much larger one.

## The Company

The Clean Shields are a mixed company, and the mixture is part of what the city distrusts. About half are men born in the empire without its citizenship, _palvethoi_ (residents without citizenship) from the provinces who could not or would not give the legions twenty years. A quarter are former auxiliaries of the legions, mostly men of the units cashiered or disbanded after the war in which the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] won its independence. The rest are Helionites, Tarvenans, a few Bizarians and a handful of men from the coast who do not say where they learned the river.

Every man draws his kit from the company's stores. The sergeants carry blades of [[miscgear-vylsteel|Vylarian Steel]], guild-marked by the [Steel Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} and bought at the prices the guild charges the great houses. The company's mail is new and its horses are remounts of the quality the legions buy for their cavalry prefects. The company's camp is a walled square of brick on the river bank upstream of the city, among the garden-villas of the rich, on ground leased from an estate whose name the lease does not give.

## The Articles

The company is governed by written articles, which the captain reads to each recruit and the recruit signs or marks. They are short. A man is a Hired Man for a wage paid in coin on the day, in the camp's yard, by the purser's own hand. He takes no share of plunder, because the contract gives the company nothing to plunder; whatever a patrol takes from river thieves goes to the purser and is entered. A man of three years' service is a Veteran, draws a larger wage and keeps his issued arms as his own while he serves.

A man who robs a barge, sells the company's movements, or draws a blade inside the city's line is paid off with the month's wage and turned out of the gate without his kit, and the company has never taken one back. The captain holds the contract and the articles; his lieutenant commands the horse; the purser keeps the money; the quartermaster keeps the kit; the master-at-arms keeps the discipline; and the river-master keeps the boats and the escort.

## The Contract and the Posting

The outskirts belong in law to the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]], whose governor sits at [[place-alyssar|Alessar]] and sends nobody. The City Watch holds the gate-roads and the gate-yards. Between the yards and the Hilen landings, the river road and the barge moorings belonged to nobody until the lean years before the contract, when river thieves took two barge strings in one summer and the city's bread rose by a third in a week.

The war council of [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|The Vylarian Military]] answered with a contract, because it had no cohort to spare and the Watch's charter stops at the gate-yards. The contract is modest. It pays a company of two hundred to keep the river road, guard the moorings and escort the barges down from the landings to the river gate, where the Watch takes them over at the edge of the [Clearance]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. It was put out for bids, and [[affiliation-irnwlvscmpny|The Iron Wolves Company]] bid lower and lost. The contract went to a captain with no company raised, on the recommendation of a member of the war council who has never said why, and within a season the captain had three hundred men under arms.

A company of three hundred armed men camped a mile above the city is a fact the throne has accepted. No armed company may cross the Clearance into the city, and the Clean Shields have never tried. Their posting stops where the city begins.

## Pay

The imperial contract pays two hundred men at four Ardin a day and a captain's sum by the quarter, in traveler's notes drawn on [[affiliation-tarmex|The Tarmeks Orzan]], and the notes, like all the throne's payments, are drawn against money it has borrowed. The purser collects the notes each quarter and carries them to a chapterhouse of [[affiliation-danvar|The Bankers' Guild]]. The company's men draw six Ardin a day, half again what the contract pays, three hundred of them where the contract pays two hundred, in coin, on the day, every day.

The throne's quarter has come late twice in the company's four years, once by nineteen days. The men of the Clean Shields were paid on the day both times and did not hear of it.

**An unpaid month would not touch the company.** Every other company in imperial pay watches the quarter-day, and an unpaid month sends a Tarvenan company home and a Nordman company to whoever pays next. The Clean Shields would not notice. A company that can lose its paymaster's money and keep its men is a company with a second paymaster, and that is what the city's factions have concluded.

## Whose Company

The question of who pays the Clean Shields is asked in every faction of the capital, and each faction's answer is the one it most fears.

- **The veterans' stairs** say Taruzel. The captain was born in the Taruzelian islands and served the empire's auxiliaries for twenty years before he was cashiered with his unit at the start of the war, and the veterans who lost friends at [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]] see a Taruzelian with three hundred men a mile from the bread.
- **The hawks in [[affiliation-seldan|The Vylarian Senate]]** say Khelathi silver, because every unexplained fortune near the throne is read in Orzara as the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] buying a second Taruzel.
- **The claimants' houses** each suspect another claimant's house, and each has sent someone to the camp to find out.
- **The Steel Guild's masters** know what the company paid for its blades and who paid it, and have said nothing.

The captain's answer is the contract and good management, and he gives it to everyone who asks.

## The Legion's Officers

The legion's officers distrust the Clean Shields more than any company in imperial pay. A company of foreigners and non-citizens camped beside the capital's grain is a scandal to the officers' societies, who hold that the city's bread is a legion's charge and that a Taruzel-born captain should be nowhere near it. The officers who blame the Taruzelian war on the commission market say less about the captain, because he was cashiered by the same purchased officers they blame. The war council member who recommended the contract has been asked about it in council twice, and has answered that the barges have not been robbed since.

## The City Watch

The quarrel with the City Watch is a daily one. The Watch is a branch of the legions staffed with veteran-citizens, and it holds the river gate and the quays, where the Clean Shields hand the barges over at dawn. Each morning at the river gate a company river-master and a watch-sergeant count the barges across to each other. Each side writes its own count, and the two counts have differed often enough to fill a magistrate's afternoon.

The veterans of the Watch dislike the company for the reason every veteran dislikes a mercenary in the capital: it draws the throne's silver without twenty years under the eagle. They hate this company more for its captain's birthplace, and they resent its kit, which is better than theirs. The company's men drink in the gate-yards outside the line, where the Watch keeps order, and the two have fought there more than once. Last winter a watchman of the River Circle was knifed in a gate-yard brawl with men of the company. The man who did it was paid off and turned out the same night, and the Watch has not found him.

## Other Companies

The Iron Wolves bid for the river contract below the winning price and lost. The Iron Wolves' administrative deputy, [Ghorigios Aikastos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, was a quartermaster of the Vylarian legions and knows what a company's kit costs, and he has told his captain that the contract cannot pay for the Clean Shields' horses.

[[affiliation-slntlncmpny|The Silent Talon Company]] has had men in the outskirts all spring asking the barge crews and the camp's suppliers about the company, for a client the Silent Talon does not name. The Clean Shields know, and the captain has let them ask.

## The Present {#present}

The Orzas is failing and the succession is unsettled, and the Clean Shields are the only armed company within a day of the capital that answers to neither the legions, the Watch nor the Imperial Guard. Every house that hopes to matter after the funeral has done the arithmetic. Two claimants' agents have dined at the camp this spring, and both left with the captain's compliments and nothing else.

The veterans' stairs on the River Circle have petitioned the [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]] to have the company removed from the river, and a tribune has taken up the petition. The company bought forty remounts in the spring horse fairs, more than the contract can explain, and its purser paid for them in coin.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The missing knifeman.** The Watch's veterans want the man who knifed their comrade, and a watch-sergeant hires a party to find him in the outskirts. The man is alive, he is afraid of the company more than of the Watch, and he says he knows what the purser keeps in the strongbox.
- **The barge that came in light.** A grain barge escorted down by the Clean Shields reaches the river gate with a third of its sacks short, and each side's count blames the other. The Veramus of the Grain wants the truth before the bakers hear.
- **The Talon's question.** A man of the Silent Talon offers a party good silver to get into the company's camp and copy one page of the purser's book. The page he wants is the one that shows where the coin comes from.

:::secret {title="Who pays the Clean Shields"}
The company is paid by the [House of Veldis]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, the great house of the Bankers' Guild that holds the largest block of the throne's borrowed paper. The house lends the throne the silver that pays the company's contract, and pays the rest of the company's wage, its horses and its blades from its own strongroom. The coin comes up the river road in the house's own carts, entered in the purser's book as deposits returned from the company's account at the house's chapter, and the account is fed from a Veldis account that the purser and the captain alone have seen.

The house cannot let the throne default, and it cannot let the city burn. Its fortune is the throne's debt, and the throne's debt is worth what the next Orzas agrees to honor. A short pension day, a riot over the price of bread, or a broken succession could each end with a reign that repudiates its predecessor's paper, or a mob at the chapterhouse doors demanding silver for notes the house has issued beyond its reserves. The Clean Shields are the house's answer to all three. When the fire comes, the only armed company outside the legions' command sits on the city's grain road a mile from the river gate, and the house can offer it to whichever claimant agrees to honor the debt. The house means to sell the city the means of putting out the fire, at the price of the next reign's signature.

Shattar Kassumi knows who pays him and why. The house has promised him what the legion took from him: a grant of the citizenship from the Senate at the end of ten years' service, for himself and for every man of the company who serves the ten, bought with the house's influence at the next reign's first Senate. The purser knows the source of the coin and not the promise. The lieutenant knows neither. The man of the war council who recommended the contract is in the house's debt for the price of a Vandraks commission, and does not know what the company is for.
:::
