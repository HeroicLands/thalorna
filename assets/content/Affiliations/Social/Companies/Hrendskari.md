"Count the smoke, and if it is three, light the fire before you wake me," [[being-knrvsldvrnrk|Knarvselda Dvurnaruk]] tells every new man she sends up to a tower. It is the rule the legion's watch sergeants teach their recruits in [[place-moktur|Moktur]], with one change of her own: a sergeant wants to be woken first, and a Nordman on a ridge three hours from help wants the fire lit. Her company holds seven of the towers that watch the [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]] above [[place-dolvar|Dolvar]], and it holds them because the legion does not have the men.

## Overview

Hrendskari, "the Boundary Troop" in the north's own speech, is a company of the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordheimn]], the Nordmen, raised in the coastal steads of [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]] and hired by the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] to man the watchtowers the legion at Dolvar cannot fill. Its name is a compound of _hrend-_, a boundary marker, and _-skari_, a troop, and its standard is a gray boundary-stone on black. The empire has hired Nordmen to hold its frontier since the generation after the raids ([[lore-compactsignd|The Signing of the Compact]]), and Hrendskari is the latest of them.

The company is cheap, steady and foreign, which is what the war council wants and what the legion holds against it. Its men do the legion's work in the legion's towers on the legion's road, under the legion commander's orders, for silver that comes up the road on the same mules as the legion's.

## The Towers

A tower of the Vristwald march is a square of stone three floors high with a fire-basket on its roof, a cistern in its foot and a walled yard for the horses, set in sight of the next tower so that a fire on one is seen from two. A tower holds a watch of twenty to thirty, and Hrendskari holds seven of them: the line that runs northwest from Dolvar along the ridge above the pass to [[place-vandgrund|Vandgrund]], the pass the Vandstein villages' men come over when the truce fails.

Every evening the tower-warden counts the smoke from the forest at dusk and marks it on the tower's _karvumrozima_ (smoke tally), the wax board the legion's towers keep, and the full boards go down to Dolvar with the dispatch. The legion's clerks copy Hrendskari's tallies into the march's book beside their own. The company's tallies are kept in the legion's numerals and nothing else, and its wardens answer to the _vandraks_ (legion commander), [[being-sthrskrmrs|Satheras Kiramaris]], for every mark on them.

## How the Company Lives

The towers are cold, dull and far apart, and a Nordman fills a dull watch the way the north does. The company keeps its own speech at its own fires, its own feasts at Jel and Midsomar, and its own dead: a man killed on the march is burned on the tower's roof in its fire-basket, and his ashes go north in a jar with the next man going home. The company sings the deaths of its fallen the way the north does, though no sworn skald has come south to sit at its fires. It has a woman who learned the old lays from her mother and sings them, and she holds no rank of the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] and claims none.

The company's men marry. A Spear-Sworn who has wintered three years in Dolvar's lower town usually has a wife there, a Mokturan woman whose father shod the legion's horses, and the company's winter quarter is a row of houses below the fortress wall. The children speak Vylari in the street and Nordmal at the table. The legionaries in the same town cannot marry in law until their twenty years are served, and they notice.

## The Articles

A man or woman joins Hrendskari by swearing to the captain at a spear-point, as a war-band in the north swears to its leader, for the length of the company's contract. The sworn are Spear-Sworn. Those who have served more than one contract are Old Hands, and the captain calls the Old Hands to the fire before she renews a contract or takes the company anywhere new.

The captain holds the contract and the pay chest. Each tower has a warden, an Old Hand who holds it, keeps its watch and its tally, and goes down to Dolvar once a month to stand before the _vandraks_'s clerks. The purser counts the chest on the day it comes and pays each tower its share, and keeps the company's debts in the lower town, which in a late month run to bread, ale and boots. The standard-bearer carries the boundary-stone.

A man who sleeps on watch is beaten. A man who leaves his tower without leave is put out of the company before the fire, his spear broken and his month's pay forfeit, and the company has a word in its own speech for him and does not say it in front of strangers.

## Why the Company Is Not Signed

The free companies of the north answer to the [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Málalidabandalag]], the Compact that keeps its Hall at [[place-hringstead|Hringstead]], and a signed company swears to bring its disputes with an employer before the Council of the Hall and to annul its contracts there. The empire's war council does not answer to a northern hall. Its contracts carry a clause that a signed captain cannot swear: that every dispute under the contract goes before the court of the Dukran, the empire's war-minister, at [[place-orzara|Orzara City]], and nowhere else. The companies that hold the empire's frontier are therefore unsigned companies, and Hrendskari is one.

The Compact does not count an unsigned company an outlaw. It counts it a company without a warranty, and the Hall's captains say that a company which signs away its appeal to the Council has sold its only protection against a paymaster who stops paying. Knarvselda answers that the empire pays better than the jarls and more often, and that a hall a month's sail away is no protection on a ridge in Moktur.

## Pay

The empire pays a Spear-Sworn three and a half Ardin a day, an Old Hand five, and the captain a fixed sum by the month, all in silver coin. The chest comes up the military road from [[place-tyrellan|Tirellan]] under the legion's guard, on the same mule-train as the legion's pay, and its silver, like the legion's, is coin the throne has borrowed. A legionary draws less by the day and earns the dole and the citizenship at twenty years ([[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vylarian Citizenship]]). A Nordman earns neither and goes home with what he has saved, which makes him the cheaper soldier by a lifetime.

The war council pays its companies first. A legion that is paid late grumbles and stays. A company that is paid late leaves, and the war council's clerks pack the companies' chests before the legions'. The legion's coin has come up the road late more than once in recent winters, and the company's has not.

**An unpaid month would empty the towers.** Hrendskari has no hall to complain to and no appeal but the Dukran's court in a city its captain has seen once. The company's contract runs by the month, and a month without pay releases it. The towers would be held through that month on the lower town's credit, and then the company would come down off the ridge. Everyone on the march knows where it would go: three days south to the yard of the Keep at Tirellan, where the _therkos_ (governor), [[being-thlmrsnlmn|Tholmaras Nelamen]], pays his own riders in coin on the first day of every month and has never once paid them late. Two hundred Nordmen sworn to the _therkos_ by name would change the balance of the province, and the _vandraks_ at Dolvar counts that as well as the smoke.

## The Legion's Officers

The legion's centurions respect the company more than its officers do. A centurion who has stood a winter in a tower knows what holding one costs, and the company's tallies are good. The legion's officers, who hold purchased commissions and answer for the march to the capital, hold three grievances against the company.

- **The pay.** The company's chest comes when the legion's does not, and every legionary on the march has watched it go up the road.
- **The towers.** A tower the legion gave up is a tower the legion lost, and the officers who signed the order would sooner have it back with fewer men than leave it to foreigners.
- **The old enemy.** The Nordmen burned the empire's western coast in the generation before the empire hired them ([[lore-southrnfleets|The Southern Fleets]]), and the officers' houses keep the memory better than the Nordmen do.

The _vandraks_ himself is careful with the company, because he needs its towers manned and its captain sober, and he gets both.

## The City Watch

Hrendskari is three weeks' road from the capital, and its men see the City Watch only when the captain goes south to renew the contract. She went once, in the company's second year on the march, and the Watch put her and her two Old Hands across the [Clearance]{.name data-type="place/feature"} at dusk with the rest of the non-citizens, after a watch-sergeant had made a speech in the gate-yard about Nordmen and the burned coast. The veteran-citizens who staff the Watch hold that a man who has not served his twenty years should not draw the throne's silver for soldiering, and a Nordman drawing it twice offends them. The company tells the story of the speech at its fires, and the line the captain answered with is the best-known verse in the company's lays.

## Other Companies

[[affiliation-irnwlvscmpny|The Iron Wolves Company]] has sought Hrendskari out. The Compact has refused the Iron Wolves twice, and their captain, [Filazur Zighidek]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds that two unsigned companies should help each other to contracts. He offered to lend the march a hundred men for a share of the next contract. Knarvselda refused, because she knows what the Iron Wolves did at [[place-trianthion|Trianthion]] and does not want their name in the march's book beside her towers.

[[affiliation-slntlncmpny|The Silent Talon Company]] has had no dealings with Hrendskari. One of its men came up the road last summer as a fur buyer and asked the towers' wardens how many men they kept, and the wardens told him a number that was wrong.

## The Present {#present}

The summer's truce market is near, and the company's three highest towers look down on the pass to Vandgrund, where the truce is held by the sons of the Vandstein villages living in the fortress as surety. The _vandraks_ has asked Knarvselda for thirty more men for the towers above the pass this summer, and she does not have them. She has written north for a draft of new men, who will not reach Dolvar before the market.

The smoke counted from the company's towers has risen through the spring. The tower-wardens have marked camps where the tallies of past summers marked hearths, and the clerks at Dolvar have copied the marks without remark.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The draft from the north.** Thirty new Spear-Sworn are on the road from the coast, and their leader has been arrested at the provincial boundary for carrying a sword across a toll-gate. The captain hires a party to get them free and up to the towers before the truce market.
- **The tower that lit its fire.** A Hrendskari tower lights its basket at midnight, the signal for a war-band crossing. No other tower saw anything. The warden swears to what she saw, and the _vandraks_ needs to know whether she lied or the other towers slept.
- **The therkos's offer.** A rider from Tirellan brings the captain a sealed letter from the _therkos_ and waits for an answer. Knarvselda asks a party she trusts to carry her reply and to watch who opens it on the road.
