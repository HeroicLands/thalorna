**Wuldjagâr erund Gárskald:** _Varokh Deep Forest Hunting Party_ _Varokhi: "The Wild Hunt of the Speakers of the Spear"_

The deep Vrystwald forests belong to those who know them, and the Wuldjagâr of clan Gárskald—the Speakers of the Spear—know them as well as anything human can. This is no rabble of amateur poachers. These are the clan's dedicated hunting company: skilled trackers and killers of great game who operate under strict tribal law and the direct authority of their chief. They hunt elk, boar, and brown bear in the primeval forests north of the Vylarian border, where the trees grow so thick that midday looks like dusk. They are prideful, territorial, and absolutely deadly to those foolish enough to interfere with their kill or poach their grounds.

The Wuldjagâr exists because the Gárskald cannot survive without it. The clan numbers perhaps two hundred souls—warriors, farmers, children, elders—spread across a handful of forest settlements in the central Vrystwald. The forest provides, but only to those who know how to take from it. The Wuldjagâr feeds the clan.

## Backstory

The Gárskald have hunted the central Vrystwald for as long as anyone can remember. Their territory—bounded by the Moldûn River to the south and Blackthorn Ridge to the north—is rich hunting ground: dense stands of oak and pine sheltering elk, boar, bear, and the smaller game that keeps the clan fed between the great hunts. The Gárskald's skill as hunters has always been their identity and their pride. Other clans are warriors or smiths or farmers. The Gárskald are hunters. It is who they are.

But the forest is changing, and the Gárskald are struggling to understand why.

It started eighteen months ago, when the elk herds that normally winter in the Deepwood valleys failed to arrive. Thâralund Gárskald—the clan's huntmaster and the Wuldjagâr's leader—led scouting parties deep into the northern forest and found the answer: the herds had shifted their migration routes, moving west into territory claimed by the Hildskorn clan. The cause was unclear—Thâralund suspects that Vylarian logging operations along the southern edge of the forest have disrupted the ancient paths the herds follow—but the result was immediate. The Gárskald's winter meat stores fell to half their normal levels. Children went hungry. The clan chief, an aging warrior named Helmrík Gárskald, was forced to trade weapons and iron tools to the Hildskorn for dried meat—a humiliation that still burns.

Then the boar population crashed. A disease—something that causes swelling in the joints and eventual paralysis—swept through the central Vrystwald's boar herds, killing or disabling nearly half the breeding population. Isgard Waldaric, the Wuldjagâr's healer and naturalist, has examined the sick animals and believes the disease originated in the south, possibly carried north by animals fleeing the Vylarian logging. She has not been able to cure it, and she fears it may spread to the domestic pigs the clan keeps in their settlements.

The Gárskald face a winter they may not survive. The elk are in Hildskorn territory. The boar are dying. The bear—always dangerous, always the hunt of last resort—remain, but bear-hunting in winter is deadly work, and the clan cannot afford to lose hunters. Thâralund has proposed something unprecedented: a great hunt beyond the clan's traditional territory, pushing north past Blackthorn Ridge into the wild country where no Varokh clan holds recognized rights. The land beyond the ridge is unclaimed for good reason—it is rough, poorly mapped, and haunted by stories of things that live in the deep forest that are not animals. But Thâralund believes there are elk herds in the northern valleys, untouched by the migration shift, and if the Wuldjagâr can reach them before the deep snows, the clan will eat this winter.

Chief Helmrík has given his reluctant blessing. The Wuldjagâr is preparing to march north.

## Organization

The party is led by **Thâralund Gárskald**, a scarred veteran huntmaster in his fifties with an unforgiving reputation and three kills of the great northern bear to his name. He leads by example and unquestioned authority—a Varokh hunting company is not a democracy. Thâralund answers directly to the clan chief and holds his position through demonstrated skill and the respect of every warrior in the Vrystwald. But this season's failures have shaken confidence in his leadership for the first time, and the younger hunters smell opportunity.

The current roster stands at seven—every experienced hunter the clan can spare:

- **Thâralund Gárskald**—Huntmaster. Scarred and methodical, with eyes that miss nothing in the forest. He has led the Wuldjagâr for twenty years, and this is the first season he has failed to fill the clan's meat stores.
- **Hârundar Hildrik**—Scout and tracker. Young, ambitious, and openly questioning Thâralund's decision to hunt north rather than negotiate with the Hildskorn for shared territory. He believes the old man's pride will get them killed.
- **Frodmûn Fulkír**—Spear-master. Quiet and patient, with an uncanny ability to predict animal behavior. The most skilled hunter in the company after Thâralund—and the one most likely to succeed him, if he wants the job. He does not seem to.
- **Erinthil Raedwulf**—Bow-hunter. Deadly at range, but growing old and increasingly bitter about younger rivals. His eyesight is going—he's missed shots he would have made five years ago—and his pride makes him sharp-tongued and difficult.
- **Skáldar Arnthúl**—Heavy weapons specialist. Massive and strong, carries a battle-axe and makes ambush kills on the largest game. He is the clan chief's nephew, which gives him political weight beyond his skill, and he knows it.
- **Isgard Waldaric**—Healer and naturalist. Knows the forest's plants and can treat wounds; trusted advisor to Thâralund. She has been studying the boar disease obsessively, and her concern is deepening—she believes it may not be natural.
- **Wulfrin Thornik**—Youngest hunter, only recently inducted. Still learning the ways, haunted by his first kill of an elk cow earlier this season. The others watch him for signs of weakness—Varokh hunters cannot afford squeamishness—but Thâralund sees potential in the boy that the others miss.

## Territory

The Gárskald's traditional hunting grounds stretch across roughly sixty miles of the central Vrystwald, bounded on the south by the Moldûn River and on the north by Blackthorn Ridge. Three seasonal camps serve as bases: the Summer Lodge in the eastern meadows, the Winter Hold in the protected Deepwood valleys, and the Swift-Elk Camp along the Moldûn where river crossings make good ambush points for migrating herds.

Trails are marked subtly—notches in bark, arrangements of stone, carved symbols only a Varokh hunter would recognize. Trespassers are warned once. Those who violate the hunting grounds a second time do not leave.

The territory beyond Blackthorn Ridge—the target of Thâralund's northern hunt—is a different matter entirely. No Varokh clan claims it. The forest there is older, darker, and denser than anything in the central Vrystwald. The trees are enormous, their canopy so thick that snow barely reaches the ground. There are game trails—Thâralund has seen them from the ridge—but no one in living memory has hunted there systematically. The Gárskald's elders tell stories about the ridge country: stories of stone circles in forest clearings, of sounds that carry wrong in the mist, of hunters who went north and came back changed—or did not come back at all. Thâralund dismisses these as grandmother tales. Isgard is not so certain.

## Methods

Thâralund's hunts are elaborate affairs, planned weeks in advance through careful observation of animal movements, weather patterns, and the tribal calendar. The Wuldjagâr uses a combination of tactics: long-distance tracking to locate prey, ambush positions set with patience, and coordinated drives to herd game toward prepared killing zones. Frodmûn reads the forest like text, identifying the age and health of animals from tracks and droppings. Erinthil waits in tree stands for hours at a time. Skáldar prepares blind positions where a single strike with his axe drops a boar or brings down a wounded elk.

When a major kill is made—a great bear, a prime bull elk—the entire party participates in the ceremony. The animal is butchered according to ancient custom, with prayers to the forest spirits and the ancestors. Prime cuts go to the clan chief and the clan's elders; the bones are carved for tools and amulets; the hide is worked into leather and furs. Nothing is wasted.

The Wuldjagâr does not hunt for sport or trophy. They hunt to feed the clan, to honor the animals they kill, and to maintain the balance of the forest. This is sacred work in Varokh culture, and they execute it with absolute seriousness.

The northern expedition will test every skill the Wuldjagâr possesses. They will be operating in unknown territory, without prepared camps or familiar trails, in weather that may turn lethal. Thâralund has allocated three weeks for the hunt—one week to reach the northern valleys, one week to hunt, one week to return with the meat. If they take longer, the deep snows will close Blackthorn Ridge behind them, and they will not get home until spring. The clan cannot afford to lose its hunters for that long.

## Internal Dynamics

The Wuldjagâr functions with remarkable cohesion, but the food crisis has exposed fault lines:

- **Hârundar's challenge** to Thâralund's authority is the most visible tension. He argues—not without reason—that negotiating shared hunting rights with the Hildskorn is safer than gambling the entire company on an expedition into unknown territory. The fact that Hârundar has been courting the Hildskorn chief's daughter adds a personal dimension to his preference for diplomacy. Thâralund sees the boy's argument as cowardice dressed up as prudence, and has said so publicly.
- **Erinthil's decline** is a slow-motion catastrophe. His eyesight is failing, his reflexes are a half-step slower than they were, and he knows it. The northern hunt will demand the best from every hunter, and Erinthil may no longer be the best. He has compensated so far with cunning and experience, but a missed shot at the wrong moment—a charging bear, a startled elk herd—could cost lives. He will not step down voluntarily. In Varokh culture, a hunter who admits he can no longer hunt is admitting he is ready to die.
- **Isgard's theory** about the boar disease has unsettled the company. She has been examining the sick animals with increasing alarm and has told Thâralund—privately—that she believes the disease is not natural. The symptoms do not match anything in her considerable knowledge of forest ailments. She suspects the disease is connected to something in the southern forest—something related to the Vylarian presence—but she has no proof, and saying so publicly would cause panic.
- **Skáldar's political weight** as the chief's nephew creates an uncomfortable dynamic. He is a good hunter but not a great one, and he knows his position in the Wuldjagâr owes something to his bloodline. If the northern hunt goes badly, Skáldar's report to the chief will carry more weight than Thâralund's—and Skáldar and Hârundar have been talking.
- **Wulfrin's sensitivity** stands out in a group of hardened killers. He struggles with the violence of the hunt, despite his growing skill. Thâralund sees in the boy something the others do not—a depth of attention to the forest that the more experienced hunters have lost. Wulfrin notices things: shifts in birdsong, changes in the soil, the subtle wrongness that Isgard has been trying to articulate. Whether this sensitivity is a gift or a liability depends on what they find beyond the ridge.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Northern Hunt.** The Wuldjagâr is preparing to cross Blackthorn Ridge into unclaimed territory—a desperate gamble to find elk herds that will keep the Gárskald alive through winter. They could use additional hands: fighters who can handle themselves in deep forest, a healer to supplement Isgard, or anyone who has traveled the country beyond the ridge. The work is hard, the territory is unknown, and the clan's survival depends on success.
- **The Hildskorn Dispute.** The elk herds that normally feed the Gárskald have migrated into Hildskorn territory. Hârundar Hildrik wants to negotiate—his relationship with the Hildskorn chief's daughter could open a door. Thâralund refuses, viewing it as surrendering the clan's independence. A mediator who could broker a deal between the two clans would be worth a great deal—but both sides have pride invested in the outcome, and a clumsy intervention could trigger a feud.
- **The Boar Plague.** Isgard is convinced that the disease killing the Vrystwald's boar herds is not natural. She wants to trace it to its source—south, toward the Vylarian border, where the empire's logging operations have been pushing deeper into the forest for the past three years. If the disease is connected to the Vylarian presence, the implications are enormous: not just for the Gárskald, but for every Varokh clan that depends on the forest. Isgard needs someone to accompany her on a scouting expedition south—someone who can handle Vylarian soldiers if the investigation takes them into imperial territory.
- **The Stone Circles.** The elders' stories about the land beyond Blackthorn Ridge are more than superstition. There are stone circles in the northern forest—ancient, moss-covered, carved with symbols that are neither Varokh nor Vylarian. The few hunters who have seen them from a distance report an unsettling feeling of being watched. If the Wuldjagâr crosses the ridge, they will enter territory where these ruins are common—and whatever purpose they served may not be entirely in the past.
- **The Vylarian Logging Camp.** A new Vylarian logging operation has established itself along the Moldûn River, within sight of the Gárskald's southern boundary markers. The loggers are cutting deep into the forest, felling ancient trees that the Varokh consider sacred. The clan chief wants them gone but cannot afford a military confrontation with the empire. A more subtle approach—sabotage, negotiation, or finding leverage over the logging company's patron—would be worth exploring.

## Using the Wuldjagâr

The company works well as:

- A territorial threat to parties traveling through the Vrystwald who appear to be encroaching on hunting grounds
- A source of deep knowledge about Varokh politics, tribal movements, and the forest's ecology
- An encounter that forces moral choices about competing claims to resources
- A complication in wilderness travel—the Wuldjagâr's ambush sites and territorial markers are everywhere, invisible to outsiders
- A potential resource if the party can establish peaceful relations: guidance through dangerous forests, information about Varokh culture and politics, and a safe camp in hostile territory
- A gateway to the mysteries of the northern Vrystwald—the stone circles, the ancient ruins, and whatever is driving the animals south
- A window into the economics of subsistence—the brutal arithmetic of feeding a clan when the forest turns hostile
