Rhezin **Neumenos** is the current of mind, soul, communion with otherworldly entities, and the immaterial. It occupies the lower-left vertex of the [[affiliation-hexhodai|Thizaron]], directly opposite Rhezin [[affiliation-physera|Fisera]]. Where Earth trusts only what can be touched, Spirit reaches for what lies beyond touch—the unseen architectures of consciousness, the entities that inhabit the spaces between the material and the divine, and the deep structures of will and awareness that underlie all magical practice. Neumenos is the most respected, the most feared, and the most closely supervised of the six schools.

## Philosophy

The Neumenos mage perceives reality as _layered_—the material world that most people inhabit is merely the outermost shell, and beneath it (or behind it, or within it) lie deeper strata of existence where mind, spirit, and pure arcane force move without the constraints of matter. To a Spirit mage, a stone is not merely a stone—it is a material object overlaid with a spiritual echo, a history of every consciousness that has perceived it, and a lattice of arcane potentials invisible to purely material perception. The body is not the person; it is the vessel the person inhabits, and the person—the will, the awareness, the soul—is something far more interesting.

This philosophy produces practitioners who are intense, introspective, and sometimes deeply unsettling to their colleagues. A Neumenos mage's attention is directed inward as much as outward—they study their own consciousness as rigorously as they study external phenomena, because the mind is both the instrument of perception and the medium through which Spirit magic operates. Self-knowledge is not a luxury for the Spirit mage; it is a survival skill. The forces they work with—entities from beyond the material, the raw stuff of consciousness, the boundaries between self and other—are dangerous precisely because they interact with the mage's own psyche. A Neumenos practitioner who does not know their own mind risks losing it.

The school's signature domains are the most esoteric in [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s repertoire. Summoning—the calling and binding of entities from beyond the material world—is the most dramatic and most closely regulated. Telepathy and the magic of mental communion allow Spirit mages to touch other minds, share perceptions, and (at the highest levels) influence thought itself. Prophecy—not the water-gazing of Rhezin [[affiliation-hydalis|Hidalis]] but the deeper, more dangerous communion with forces that perceive time differently than mortals—is a Neumenos specialty. And the study of the soul itself—its nature, its relationship to the body, its fate after death—is the school's most controversial pursuit.

The deeper principle of Neumenos is _the power of the immaterial_. What has no body cannot be destroyed. What has no location cannot be contained. What exists as pure awareness can perceive realities that matter cannot touch. The Spirit mage understands that consciousness is not an epiphenomenon of the material world but a fundamental force—perhaps _the_ fundamental force—and that the mage who masters it masters something more essential than fire or stone.

## The Opposition: Fisera

Neumenos stands directly opposite Rhezin Fisera on the Thizaron (the Seal), and their opposition is the arcane tradition's version of the oldest question in philosophy: what is more real, the body or the soul? The Earth mage trusts matter, distrusts abstraction, and regards the Spirit mage's explorations of consciousness and the immaterial as a dangerous departure from the solid ground of reality. The Spirit mage regards the Earth mage as someone who has mistaken the container for the contents—competent but blind to the deeper truth that the material world is a surface, not the whole story.

A Neumenos mage attempting Earth magic finds the experience strangely deadening. The magic is all _substance_—heavy, slow, resistant to the lightness of thought. It demands engagement with matter on matter's terms, with no room for the leap of insight, the flash of intuitive communion, or the dissolution of boundaries that Spirit magic requires. It feels, to the Spirit mage, like being buried alive.

The wariness between the two Zulethalar (fellowships) is mutual and deep. Fisera practitioners worry—not without justification—that Spirit mages are playing with forces that can damage their minds, compromise their identities, or invite catastrophic breaches between the material world and whatever lies beyond it. Neumenos practitioners grow weary of being treated as a liability by colleagues who cannot understand what they do. The [Sekran]{.name data-type="affiliation"} ([[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]) shares Fisera's concerns and monitors Neumenos practitioners more closely than any other school—a fact that the Spirit mages resent and that the Sekran regards as entirely justified.

## Adjacencies

Rhezin Neumenos shares its edges of the Thizaron with Rhezin Fisera and Rhezin [[affiliation-pyrethos|Pirethos]].

The sympathy with Rhezin [[affiliation-sideros|Sideris]] is one of _binding and precision_. Both _rheza_ deal in the imposition of order on what resists it—Metal through material craft, Spirit through the binding of wills and entities. A summoning circle is, at its core, a structure of arcane force that contains and constrains an entity that would rather not be contained—and the precision required is pure Sideris craftsmanship, applied to immaterial subjects. The two schools collaborate more than their mutual reserve might suggest: Sideris provides the structural framework, Neumenos provides the understanding of what is being contained. Many of the Order's most powerful wards and containment systems are joint creations of Metal and Spirit practitioners.

The sympathy with Rhezin Pirethos is one of _intensity_. Both _rheza_ are willing to push past the boundaries where lesser mages would stop—Pirethos through the application of overwhelming force, Neumenos through the opening of the self to forces beyond ordinary perception. There is a shared quality of daring in the best practitioners of both schools, a recognition that the most powerful magic lies on the far side of a threshold that most people are wise to leave uncrossed. This creates a wary mutual respect. Fire mages admire Spirit mages' courage, even if they find their methods eerie; Spirit mages admire Fire mages' power, even if they find their approach crude.

## The Zulen Neumenos

The **Zulen Neumenos**—the Fellowship of Spirit—is the most mystical and the most closely watched of the six Zulethalar. Its members are viewed with a mixture of awe and suspicion by the other fellowships—respected for the depth of their insight and the power of their magic, but mistrusted for the nature of the forces they engage with. Summoners, seers, and practitioners who traffic with entities beyond the material world are by definition working at the edge of what the Order considers safe, and the Sekran's vigilance over Neumenos activities is the fellowship's permanent companion.

The fellowship's internal culture prizes self-mastery above all else. Where Pirethos measures its members by their power and Sideris by their precision, Neumenos measures by depth of understanding—of the self, of the forces one works with, and of the consequences of carelessness. A Spirit mage who can summon a powerful entity is competent; a Spirit mage who understands _why_ that entity responds to the summons, what it wants, and what it will do if the binding falters is wise. The fellowship's elders have seen enough colleagues damaged or destroyed by insufficient self-knowledge to make wisdom, not power, the measure of worth.

Politically, the Zulen (fellowship) Neumenos is the most isolated of the six fellowships. Its members are too few, too specialized, and too mistrusted to build the broad alliances that other Zulethalar rely on. They compensate with the one resource that is always in demand: knowledge. A Neumenos _marendos_ (master) who has spent decades studying the entities that haunt a particular region, or who understands the spiritual dimensions of a problem that baffles purely material analysis, possesses information that no other school can provide. This makes the fellowship indispensable even to those who would prefer not to rely on it—a dynamic that the Spirit mages have learned to exploit with a quiet pragmatism that their reputation for otherworldly mysticism tends to obscure.
