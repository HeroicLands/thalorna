The Faith of Pozeras honors [[lore-pozerasdty|Pozeras]], god of journeys, exploration, and wanderlust. His name means the hallowed road in the [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]]. The faith teaches that he protects travelers and inspires mortals to seek the unknown.

## Worship

The worship of Pozeras is characterized by a spirit of adventure and movement rather than stationary temple ritual. While Pozeras’s faith does maintain temples and shrines in cities, the most authentic expressions of his faith occur on roads, at crossroads, in harbor towns, and wherever travelers gather to prepare for journeys. The faithful approach Pozeras with a mixture of reverence and practical supplication, recognizing him as both a spiritual guide and a very present protector against the mundane dangers of travel.

The greatest festival honoring Pozeras is the [Wanderer's Blessing]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, celebrated at the spring equinox when travel becomes safer and more common after the winter season. During this festival, temples of Pozeras open their doors to blessing ceremonies where merchants departing on trade voyages, soldiers marching to distant postings, and adventurers beginning quests present themselves to the priesthood for formal commissioning. The priest anoints the traveler with oil, provides them with a symbol of Pozeras (often a small carved staff or compass), and offers prayers for safe passage and successful completion of their journey. The ceremony includes an oath—the traveler swears to travel honorably, to aid other travelers in distress, and to return safely if possible to make a further offering to the god.

Another important observance is the [Celebration of Arrivals]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, held at the autumn equinox as the primary travel season wanes. During this festival, temples of Pozeras are thronged with travelers who have safely completed their journeys and who come to give thanks through substantial offerings. Stories of adventures are shared, dangers survived are recounted, and new knowledge brought from distant lands is discussed. The priesthood gathers these stories, sometimes compiling them into travel guides and maps that are then shared with other travelers.

Individual devotion to Pozeras often centers on the making of vows and offerings before departure. A merchant will vow that if Pozeras grants safe passage and profitable trade, he will donate a portion of his profits to build a way-shrine—a small, rudimentary temple maintained by the priesthood and available to all travelers for prayer and brief rest. A soldier might vow to offer a weapon or piece of armor won in distant battle if the god grants her safe return home. Sailors make offerings of wine or the first catch of fish before embarking on sea voyages, understanding that the cooperation of Pozeras’s faith with the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] is essential to safe passage across unpredictable waters.

## Organization

The priesthood of Pozeras is uniquely decentralized and mobile compared to other Aureldian faiths. At the nominal apex stands the **Tramar**, the Master of Wayfaring, who maintains headquarters at one of the great port cities but spends considerable time traveling among the secondary centers of the priesthood. The Tramar is the keeper of maps, route knowledge, and travel wisdom accumulated over generations, and the final arbiter of Pozeras's theological interpretation. The current Tramar, based in a major port city, is a navigator and former merchant-captain who has traveled to every major coast of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] and beyond.

Below the Tramar stand the **Tramori**, the Keepers of Roads, a scattered body of perhaps fifty to one hundred ordained priests stationed at major junctions, ports, and city centers throughout western Ankaris. These priests maintain temples and waystations, oversee the network of way-shrines along major trade routes, and serve as guides and advisors to traveling merchants, soldiers, and adventurers. Many Tramori are former travelers themselves, individuals who have spent years on the road and who know the territories they oversee intimately. Some specialize in overland routes, others in coastal and maritime navigation; some are experts in desert crossing, others in mountain passage.

The **Acolytes of the Road**, young initiates serving apprenticeships within the faith, often spend years traveling with experienced priests, learning the routes, the practical skills of navigation, and the spiritual significance of journeys. Rather than remaining in single locations for formal training, these acolytes typically accompany priests on their travels, learning by doing. Many never advance to full priestly status but instead remain as traveling companions, guides, and practical assistants, finding their calling in the work of supporting safe travel rather than in formal priestly authority.

A distinctive feature of Pozeras's priesthood is the network of **Way-Shrines**, thousands of small temples and way-stations maintained along the major routes of western Ankaris. These range from elaborate stone structures at major junctions to simple roadside shelters that offer protection from weather and space for brief prayer. The upkeep of this extensive infrastructure depends upon donations from grateful travelers and upon the labors of the faithful. Many individuals fulfill vows by sponsoring the construction or maintenance of a particular way-shrine, ensuring that the network remains available to all.

The priesthood is funded through donations from those seeking blessings before travel, through the collection of offerings left at way-shrines and temples, and through fees charged for navigation advice and route guidance in particularly dangerous territories. Additionally, successful merchants and military leaders often provide direct patronage to the priesthood, recognizing that Pozeras's networks of temples and shrines facilitate commerce and military mobility that benefit everyone.

## Relations

The Faith of Pozeras shares a powerful and complementary alliance with the Faith of Brazemas, which honors [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]], the god of storms. Storms and weather define the conditions of all travel, particularly sea voyages. Travelers invoke both deities together before embarking, and the priesthoods maintain active cooperation. Way-shrines dedicated to both deities are common in coastal regions, and the priesthood of Pozeras maintains close relationships with weather-readers and storm-interpreters from Brazemas's faith who help predict safe sailing conditions.

The Faith of Pozeras maintains a crucial relationship with the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]], which honors [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], the goddess of prosperity. Merchants and trade depend utterly upon the ability to travel safely and to move goods between markets. In their theology, Ólvenia blesses commerce and abundance, but Pozeras enables the practical movement that makes trade possible. The priesthoods cooperate extensively in supporting merchant guilds and in blessing trading expeditions. In major trading cities, temples of both deities are often constructed in proximity or even connected by shared structures.

The relationship with the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]] is one of respect and pragmatic cooperation. Árdavon’s faith establishes legal frameworks for contracts and safe passage guarantees, while Pozeras’s priesthood provides the practical blessing and protection of journeys. In settled lands under stable authority, the two priesthoods work together to maintain road safety and to enforce protections for merchants and travelers. In less stable regions, priests of Pozeras often serve as arbiters of disputes that Árdavan law cannot easily reach.

Pozeras maintains a relationship of distant respect with [[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]]. The master smiths forge the tools—weaponry, tools, and talismans—that travelers carry with them. Some temples of Pozeras maintain close ties with Sacred Forge craftspeople, commissioning the creation of blessed talismans and protective objects that are distributed to travelers.

Finally, Pozeras’s faith stands in implied opposition to those who would restrict travel or knowledge. [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]]'s followers, who hoard power and restrict access to distant lands, implicitly oppose Pozeras's values of free movement and the sharing of knowledge across borders. The priesthood of Pozeras does not engage in direct warfare or active resistance, but they are understood as fundamental opponents of tyranny and isolation, and in regions under Black Flame control, they operate carefully and often in secret.
