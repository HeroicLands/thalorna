The Faith of Zunelas honors [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]] as the goddess of dreams, celestial visions, and the veil between worlds. Its teaching holds that she brings divine inspiration and omens to mortals through their dreams and is patron to seers, oracles, healers of the mind, and all who seek truth in the world beyond waking.

"Zunelas", the hallowed night in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], is the Aureldian name for [[lore-goddreams|The God of Dreams]]—a deity far older than any human pantheon, worshipped by the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale Folk]] and [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]] since before humanity existed. The Asguardian peoples know the same deity as [[lore-bjartrdty|Bjart]].

## Worship

The worship of Zunelas is contemplative and private, centered on dreams, visions, and states of deep rest. Her temples are designed as sanctuaries of silence and shadow—spaces intentionally constructed to encourage sleep, meditation, and the vivid dreaming believed to bring divine insight. Temple chambers are draped in soft fabrics in shades of midnight and silver; the air is perfumed with herbs that encourage sleep; the very architecture seems designed to quiet the waking mind. Unlike the processional grandeur of temples honoring other deities, Zunelas's sanctuaries are intimate spaces where the faithful come to rest, to dream, and to seek communion with the goddess through visions.

The priesthood maintains the temple as a place of healing sleep for the sick, the injured, and the spiritually troubled. Those suffering from wasting illnesses, deep wounds, or psychological torment may spend nights or even weeks in Zunelas's temple, cared for by priestesses while they rest and dream in the belief that they are under the goddess's protection. Such rest is understood as more healing than any mundane remedy—in the faith's teaching, Zunelas can mend what waking physicians cannot touch.

Oracular shrines dedicated to Zunelas exist in major cities, where the faithful may come to seek divine wisdom through dreams. A supplicant comes to the shrine with a question or concern, sleeps within the temple's sacred precinct after performing purification rituals, and awakens with dreams that the faithful often understand as divine guidance. The priestess-oracles interpret these dreams, helping the faithful understand what they regard as the goddess's counsel. Some shrines are renowned across kingdoms for the accuracy of their omens—the Oracular Sanctuary at [[place-argenna|Argenna]] in the [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]] attracts pilgrims from across the continent who trust that dreams received there come from the goddess herself.

There is no great public festival of Zunelas in the conventional sense, but rather a gentle, widespread celebration during the darkest nights of winter when the veil between waking and sleeping grows thin. During these nights, the faithful deliberately spend extended time in sleep and dreams, while temple priestesses maintain vigils, tending the sacred flames that keep the temples luminous through the dark hours. Many families sleep together in their homes' most comfortable chambers during these nights, understanding their rest as a shared benediction from Zunelas.

Offerings to Zunelas are subtle and personal—a lock of hair bound with silver thread, a small stone from a beloved's grave, a cup of milk left before sleep, a written prayer sealed and buried beneath a sacred tree. The faith teaches that Zunelas accepts gifts from the dreaming mind and the unconscious heart.

## Organization

The priesthood of Zunelas is led by the **Nirvena**, the High Priestess or High Priest—a title used regardless of gender. This figure is typically an elderly priestess of visionary power, someone whose dreams have guided kingdoms and whose wisdom is sought by monarchs and councils. The Nirvena is the chief interpreter of the goddess's will and a teacher and mentor to younger priests.

Below the Nirvena stand the **Ozeli**, ordained priestesses (and occasionally priests) who serve as dream-healers and interpreters of divine vision. The Ozeli are trained for years in the techniques of lucid dreaming, in the interpretation of symbols and omens, and in the pastoral care of those seeking Zunelas's guidance. They maintain the temple sanctuaries, oversee ritual purifications, and guide supplicants through the process of seeking divine dreams.

The third rank consists of **Dunari**, acolytes and servants of the night, young initiates who maintain the temples through the hours of darkness, tend the sacred lamps, prepare the sleeping chambers, and assist the Ozeli in their ministry. Training as a Dunir typically involves a long apprenticeship—often five to seven years—during which the initiate learns to enter and maintain deep meditation, to recognize and record their own dreams, and to understand the symbolic language in which the faithful believe the goddess speaks.

The priesthood maintains a significant body of written knowledge: dream journals maintained by priests across centuries, interpretive texts explaining the meaning of common symbols and visions, and ritual manuals for inducing states of lucid dreaming. These texts are kept in temple libraries, and scholars sometimes spend months in residence at major temples, studying the extensive archives of recorded dreams and their interpretations.

A distinctive institution within Zunelas's faith is the **House of Sleep**, major temple complexes dedicated specifically to the healing of the afflicted through sacred rest. These establishments employ dozens of priestesses and servants, and they often serve the dual purpose of temple and hospital. They are supported by donations from grateful patients, by the sale of dream-interpretation services to wealthy clients, and sometimes by direct patronage from nobility.

## Relations

Within Aureldian theology, Zunelas is universally respected within the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], for she represents a realm that transcends the everyday conflicts of waking deities. Nearly all the other faiths acknowledge her wisdom and occasionally consult with her priesthood on matters of great importance. The faith describes her role as that of the quiet advisor, the voice of deeper wisdom speaking from dreams and visions when mortal councils are deadlocked.

In its theology, the relationship with [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] is one of complementary opposition. Árdavon demands rigid order and clear contracts; Zunelas offers wisdom through the ambiguous, symbolic language of dreams. Yet they serve complementary roles: Árdavon brings clarity to waking disputes, while Zunelas provides insight into deeper truths that lie beneath conscious debate. Some theologians suggest that Árdavon and Zunelas together represent the complete wisdom—justice balanced with mercy, order balanced with intuition.

Theology describes Zunelas's relationship with [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]] as one of deep respect and frequent cooperation. Both goddesses value knowledge and wisdom, but Marendas pursues it through conscious study and rational thought while Zunelas reveals it through dreams, visions, and the unconscious mind. Priesthoods of the two faiths often coordinate: libraries of the [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]] sometimes employ priests of Zunelas to interpret the deeper symbolic meanings of texts; oracular shrines of Zunelas sometimes consult with scholars of Marendas to understand what the goddess's messages mean.

The Faith of Zunelas and the [[affiliation-morvana|Faith of Morvana]] share an understanding of transitions and transformations. Death itself is sometimes described as a kind of sleep from which there is no waking, and Zunelas's priesthood sometimes works with Morvana's priests to comfort the dying, offering visions of peace and rest rather than fear of the end.

In Zunelan theology, the relationship with [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] is strained but not hostile. Karnavos represents chaos and wild disruption in the waking world; Zunelas represents the chaos of dreams and the unconscious mind. While related, they are fundamentally different—Karnavos seeks to shatter civilization itself, while Zunelas merely reminds mortals that there are truths beyond rational order. Some renegade priests of the [[affiliation-karnavos|Faith of Karnavos]] have attempted to corrupt worship of Zunelas into a cult of dangerous ecstasy, but the mainstream priesthood regards such efforts as perversion of the goddess's true nature.
