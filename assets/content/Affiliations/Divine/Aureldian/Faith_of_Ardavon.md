The Faith of Árdavon teaches that [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]], god of justice, cosmic law, and honorable struggle, watches over all contracts, pacts, and conflicts, ensuring that both divine and mortal order and justice are maintained. His is the faith of chivalry, of duty before desire, of the strong defending the weak not because it is easy but because it is right. Where other gods may inspire devotion through love or fear, Árdavon demands it through principle.

The Faith of Árdavon is the religion of the nobility in much of the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]—not merely because nobles worship him, but because his doctrines underpin the feudal order itself. Oaths of fealty, the codes of knightly conduct, the laws of war, and the sanctity of contracts all fall under his purview. To break a sworn oath is held to offend Árdavon directly. His temples serve as courts of law, and his priests as judges and mediators whose rulings are held to carry divine weight.

## Worship

The worship of Árdavon is characterized by solemnity, ceremony, and the absolute sanctity of vows. Unlike faiths that emphasize personal emotional connection or intuitive spirituality, Árdavan worship is formal and hierarchical, with every ritual performed according to ancient, unchanging protocols. The faithful approach his temples with the same reverence they would show a throne room, understanding that they stand before the embodiment of cosmic law and order.

The primary festival honoring Árdavon is the [Feast of Thresholds]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, celebrated at the turning of the year when old passes to new. During this festival, which spans four days, the temples of Árdavon host elaborate ceremonies of oath-taking and covenant renewal. Lords and vassals publicly reaffirm their bonds of fealty; merchants renew their contracts; spouses ceremonially renew their marriage vows. The presence of Árdavan priests at these ceremonies is essential—their witnessing and recording of the oath in temple records is believed to give it divine force. Citizens of lesser status may also use the festival to make personal vows to Árdavon, promising to uphold principles of honesty, duty, and righteous conduct in the coming year.

Another major observance is the [Sanctification of Swords]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, held each spring. Knights and soldiers who have been newly invested with their orders present themselves before the temple altar, where Árdavan priests bless their weapons and armor with holy oils. The blessing is not mere ceremonial show—it represents a theological understanding that a righteous weapon, wielded in a just cause by a warrior sworn to Árdavon, becomes an instrument of divine order itself. Conversely, a cursed or bloodstained blade may be brought before the temple for ritual cleansing and re-sanctification, a practice that allows warriors to absolve themselves of guilt for past transgressions if their cause was just.

Individual piety to Árdavon takes the form of adherence to a personal code of honor. The devout are expected to maintain their oaths without exception, to defend the weak, to punish the wicked through lawful means, and to submit to legitimate authority while remaining vigilant against tyranny. Many Árdavan households maintain a shrine with a twin-faced representation of the god, and the faithful may spend hours in quiet meditation before the shrine, contemplating the balance between compassion and justice, between mercy and law. The making of vows to Árdavon is treated with great gravity—a broken oath is not merely social disgrace but cosmic offense, believed to bring direct divine punishment upon the oath-breaker and their bloodline.

## Organization

The priesthood of Árdavon is the most powerful and politically influential of any Aureldian faith, second only to [[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]] in sheer institutional wealth and territorial control. At its head stands the **Volsarnor**, the High Judge and keeper of cosmic law, a position of supreme ecclesiastical and often political authority. The Volsarnor is final arbiter of Árdavan doctrine and maintains headquarters in the greatest temple, typically located in the capital city of whatever kingdom grants the faith its primary patronage. The current Volsarnor, based in the [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]], is a formidable figure whose counsel influences the decisions of kings and city councils across western Ankaris.

Below the Volsarnor stands the **Sarnal**, a body of perhaps fifteen to twenty senior **Sentani**—Temple Judges—who oversee the major temples and serve as the priesthood's administrative body. These individuals are chosen for both theological knowledge and judicial wisdom, and they often serve as royal judges, interpreters of law, and settlement arbiters in disputes between nobles. A Sentan in a major city commands respect approaching that of a local lord, and their judgments carry weight in both religious and secular domains.

The **Volsari**, ordained priests scattered throughout cities and settlements, form the middle tier of the priesthood. These priests maintain local temples, witness oaths and contracts, perform ceremonies of blessing and condemnation, and serve as spiritual counselors to the devout. Many Volsari serve dual roles as royal advisors or military chaplains, bringing the authority of Árdavon into battlefields and courts.

The **Acolytes of the Threshold** are young initiates undergoing training in Árdavan theology, law, and ritual. Their education is rigorous and lengthy, typically lasting a decade or more, and only a fraction advance to full priestly status. Many who complete part of their training but do not pursue ordination become scribes, notaries, and legal experts whose training in Árdavan principles allows them to draft binding contracts and records.

A distinctive feature of Árdavan organization is the **Volgel**, knightly orders dedicated to the enforcement of Árdavon's will in the mortal world. These are not mere religious fraternities but semi-military organizations, some with significant military power and extensive territorial holdings. Knights of these orders pledge themselves to defend the righteous, punish oath-breakers, and serve as the god's instruments of justice. The [Order of the Eternal Gate]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"}, based in Provenzia, maintains a standing force of perhaps three thousand knights and is a counterweight to the temporal power of secular nobles. Conflicts have occasionally erupted between local lords and these knightly orders when judicial decisions favored the order over the lord's interests.

The priesthood maintains vast temple complexes that serve simultaneously as religious centers, courts of law, archives of recorded oaths and contracts, and in some cases fortified strongholds. The financial power of the priesthood derives from royal support, from fees charged for witnessing oaths and contracts (a percentage of all major commercial transactions in some regions), and from endowments left by pious nobles. This creates a degree of independence from secular authority that some kings view with suspicion and others with pragmatic acceptance.

## Relations

The Faith of Árdavon exists in a state of perpetual, irreconcilable war with [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]]. Where Árdavon demands order, honor, and the protection of the weak, the Black Flame preaches domination, the supremacy of the strong, and the burning away of all that cannot resist. They regard this not as a theological disagreement but as a cosmic struggle, fought in every kingdom, every court, and every battlefield where their followers meet. Árdavan knights and Black Flame warlords regard each other as existential threats, and neither faith will rest while the other endures.

The Faith of Árdavon and The Sacred Forge are natural allies. Both value discipline, craftsmanship, and the defense of civilization. Sacred Forge smiths arm Árdavan knights; Árdavan courts protect Sacred Forge temples. The alliance is deep and longstanding, though the Sacred Forge's own war with the Black Flame is a matter of doctrinal schism rather than the fundamental opposition of values that defines the Árdavan struggle. In some regions, particularly Provenzia, the two priesthoods work in such close coordination that their temples are physically connected by covered passages, and joint rituals honoring both deities are common.

The Faith of Árdavon maintains a relationship of intellectual partnership with the [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]]. Its deity, [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]], is the goddess of knowledge and wisdom. The two deities are understood as complementary expressions of civilization: Marendas illuminates the mind and provides understanding of natural and philosophical truths, while Árdavon establishes the legal and moral frameworks within which such knowledge can be preserved and transmitted. Árdavan temples often incorporate libraries and schools, recognizing that education and law are both essential to maintaining civilized order.

The [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]], which honors [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], goddess of prosperity, stands in a relationship of mutual dependence with Árdavon. Prosperity cannot flourish where oath-breakers gho unpunished and contracts are honored only when convenient. Conversely, Árdavon's order creates the stable legal frameworks within which merchants can conduct trade and farmers can plan for future harvests. The priesthoods cooperate particularly in commercial cities where contract law and the enforcement of merchant agreements are central to economic life.

The Faith of Árdavon maintains formal diplomatic relations with the [[affiliation-pozeras|Faith of Pozeras]], whose followers hold that [[lore-pozerasdty|Pozeras]], the god of voyages, oversees the natural laws governing journeys, weather, and trade routes. While not intimate allies, the two priesthoods recognize shared interests in maintaining stable borders, protecting trade routes from banditry, and enforcing maritime law. Some coastal cities maintain joint temples honoring both deities.

Finally, the Faith of Árdavon regards the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] with formal respect. [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]], the god of death, is understood as an ally in maintaining cosmic order—Sélmoros enforces the boundaries of mortality and ensures that injustice does not continue perpetually into an extended life. In their teaching, where Árdavon judges conduct and assigns consequences in the mortal realm, Sélmoros judges the soul and assigns eternal consequence. The two priesthoods occasionally cooperate in matters involving oaths sworn by the dying or inheritance disputes where supernatural phenomena suggest divine judgment.
