The Faith of Lusinia teaches that [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]], goddess of life, earth, and creation, spins the fabric of existence and nurtures all living beings, from the smallest seed to the mightiest beast.

## Worship

The worship of Lusinia is characterized by intimate connection to the living world. Her temples are rarely grand fortresses of stone but rather sanctuaries surrounded by gardens, groves of ancient trees, and cultivated spaces where mortals and nature coexist in harmony. The priesthood maintains extensive herb gardens, seed libraries, and nurseries where saplings are raised for replanting in forests damaged by logging or fire. Worship often takes place outdoors beneath open sky, with the faithful gathered in clearings or beside springs and rivers that flow from sacred sources.

The great festival honoring Lusinia occurs in early spring as the land awakens from winter's grip. The [Festival of First Growth]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} is a time of joyous celebration and ritual planting. The faithful process through cities and countryside planting seeds in communal gardens, while priestesses bless the fields and offer prayers for abundance. Young couples may pledge themselves to Lusinia during this festival, and such unions are thought to be blessed with fertility and long life. In rural communities, the festival includes feasting on early vegetables and wines made from the previous year's harvest, with portions left as offerings at Lusinia's shrines.

Individual worship centers on tending and nurturing—gardeners, farmers, and herbalists invoke Lusinia as they work the soil and tend growing things. Many homes maintain small shrines to the goddess with fresh flowers or growing herbs, and a family's first harvest of the season is presented at these shrines before consumption. Pregnant women often make pilgrimage to temples of Lusinia to pray for safe delivery and healthy children. Sick individuals are brought to her healing sanctuaries, where priestesses prepare remedies from sacred plants grown in the temple gardens.

The symbol of Lusinia—the spinning wheel encircled by vines—represents both the labor of creation and the interconnected growth of all life. Weavers and textile workers revere her, and many guilds of craftspeople maintain small shrines to the goddess, expressing their belief that all creation flows from her divine hand. Offerings to Lusinia are simple and organic: flowers, grain, honey, and fresh fruit placed upon her altars.

## Organization

The priesthood of Lusinia is organized around the **Sirela**, the High Priestess or High Priest, typically a figure of great age and wisdom who has spent decades in service to the goddess and the natural world. Below the Sirela stands a council of **Sarnori**, ordained priests responsible for maintaining temples, performing rituals, and serving as healers and counselors to the faithful. Many Sarnori are accomplished herbalists or naturalists, and their knowledge of plants, agriculture, and healing is often as spiritually important as formal theology.

The third rank consists of **Acolytes of the Spring**, young initiates who train in both spiritual practice and practical gardening, herbalism, and midwifery. These acolytes spend years working the temple gardens, learning to recognize every plant and its properties, before advancement to Sarnor status is even considered. Many Acolytes never proceed to full ordination, instead becoming master gardeners or herbalists who maintain ties of respect to the priesthood throughout their lives.

A distinctive feature of Lusinia's temples is the **Marendalon of Growing Things**, a research and teaching institution attached to major temples where scholars and priests study agriculture, botany, medicine, and the cultivation of plants. These schools maintain extensive libraries of botanical knowledge and seed archives, preserving rare and ancient plant varieties. They are supported by noble patrons and wealthy merchants who understand that Lusinia's blessings flow through agricultural prosperity.

## Relations

Lusinia stands in complementary partnership with [[affiliation-lizelas|Lizelas]], together embodying the full cycle of growth and fertility. In their theology, where [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]] represents the joy of sexual fertility and the explosion of spring blooms, Lusinia offers the patient, methodical creation and nurturing that sustains life across generations. The two goddesses are often invoked together in harvest blessings and pregnancy rites, their domains harmonious and mutually supportive.

The relationship with [[affiliation-morvana|Morvana]] is acknowledged with respect rather than conflict. Lusinia’s faith teaches that creation and decay are inseparable—a plant must fall and rot to nourish the soil that feeds new growth; a forest must have wildfire and renewal. The priesthoods recognize this cycle, though Lusinia's faithful emphasize growth while Morvana's emphasize endings. Together they represent the eternal wheel.

Lusinia maintains cordial relations with the [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]], whose followers believe that [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]], the goddess of knowledge, helps unlock the secrets of growing things. Temple libraries of the two faiths often share information about agriculture, medicine, and natural philosophy.

The attitude toward [[affiliation-karnavos|Karnavos]] is wary but not hostile. Lusinia’s faith teaches that wild chaos and unexpected disruption are forces of nature—storms bring rain, forest fires clear deadwood and release seeds—but the priesthood teaches that such chaos must be balanced with cultivation and order. In their theology, where [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] represents untamed nature, Lusinia represents nature brought into harmony with civilization.
