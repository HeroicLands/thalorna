God of storms, thunder, and lightning. Táranon commands the skies, both nurturing the land with rain and unleashing destructive tempests.

## Worship

The worship of Táranon is volatile and passionate, characterized by rituals performed under open skies and ceremonies that embrace the unpredictability of his domain. The faithful invoke Táranon with fervent prayer during drought, beseeching him to break the heat with life-giving rain. Conversely, during violent storm seasons, the temples hold ceremonies of appeasement, acknowledging the god's power and seeking to direct his wrath away from inhabited lands. Unlike the measured liturgies of Jánus or the scholarly observances of Ménérva, the worship of Táranon is raw and elemental.

The greatest festival honoring Táranon is the Thunderhead Vigil, celebrated at the onset of spring when storms return to western Ankaris after the dry season. During this festival, which spans one full night, priests and faithful gather atop high places—the peaks of hills, the towers of cities, even the decks of ships—waiting for the first thunderstorm of the season. As lightning split the sky, the assembled faithful raise their voices in a wordless cry of acknowledgment, a sound meant to honor the god's return to active participation in the mortal world. The first lightning strike of the Thunderhead Vigil is interpreted as a sign of Táranon's blessing for the coming year, and the priesthood publicly announces their reading of the omen to the gathered crowds.

Another significant observance occurs in late summer when the growing season depends upon timely rainfall. The Invocation of the Waters is performed by priests who take ritual processions into fields, vineyards, and olive groves, chanting prayers and making offerings to encourage Táranon to send gentle, beneficial rains rather than destructive downpours. Some priests perform acts of sympathetic magic—sprinkling water while chanting, or waving ritual objects meant to mimic the patterns of wind and cloud formation. These ceremonies reflect a theology that understands Táranon not as an inanimate force but as a conscious, responsive deity who can be reasoned with if one speaks in language he understands.

Individual devotion to Táranon often involves vows and promises. A farmer might vow that if Táranon grants sufficient rain for a bountiful harvest, he will dedicate a portion of his profits to the temple and sponsor a festival in the god's honor. Sailors and merchants dependent on favorable winds maintain small shrines to Táranon aboard their vessels, making daily offerings of wine or the first portion of their meal. Those caught in dangerous thunderstorms may make impulsive bargains with Táranon, promising future service or piety if the god grants safe passage. These vows are understood as binding contracts, and their violation is believed to invite Táranon's active malice.

## Organization

The priesthood of Táranon is structured around the **Flamen Tempestas**, the High Priest of the Storm, who serves as the primary voice of the god and the interpreter of his will as revealed through natural phenomena. The Flamen Tempestas is traditionally a figure of considerable authority, often consulted by kings and city councils on matters affecting agriculture, trade, and maritime ventures. The current Flamen Tempestas, based in Vylaria, maintains an extensive network of weather-watchers throughout the empire who track storm patterns, record rainfall, and report omens to the central temple.

Below the Flamen are the **Sacerdotes Tempestatis**—the Priests of Storms—a body of perhaps thirty to forty ordained clergy dispersed across major cities and coastal settlements. These priests maintain the temples, perform seasonal rituals, and serve as advisors to farmers, merchants, and sailors. They are trained in the interpretation of weather signs and in the theological significance of particular storm patterns. A Priest of Storms in a major port city holds considerable social standing, as maritime captains and merchants depend upon their wisdom in reading the moods of Táranon.

The **Vestales Tempestatis**, young acolytes and apprentices, form the foundation of the priesthood. These devotees are typically youths who have survived near-fatal encounters with storms or who have shown unusual sensitivity to weather patterns. Their training emphasizes practical observation—learning to read cloud formations, wind shifts, and atmospheric pressure changes—alongside theological study. Many Vestales remain in training for years before advancing to full priestly status, and some find their calling lies in becoming specialists: storm-readers who sell their predictions to maritime merchants, or weather-mages who work with [[affiliation-sacredforge|Sacred Forge]] craftsmen to devise protective charms.

The temples of Táranon are typically positioned on high ground, often incorporating towers and exposed courtyards designed to attract lightning and weather. Some temples maintain elaborate copper conductors and metal structures that serve both practical and spiritual purposes, channeling the god's direct manifestation through lightning into sacred chambers below where inscriptions record the patterns and locations of strikes. These records become part of the priesthood's theological archive, allowing the Flamen Tempestas to discern patterns and meanings in Táranon's communications across generations.

The priesthood is funded through donations from those seeking favorable weather, fees paid by merchants and farmers for weather consultation, and in some cases through direct support from kingdoms dependent upon agriculture or maritime trade. Coastal cities and trading centers maintain particularly well-funded Táranonic temples, recognizing that the priesthood's ability to predict and interpret storms contributes directly to their prosperity and safety.

## Relations

Táranon shares a powerful and complementary alliance with [[affiliation-murkir|Faith of Múrkír]], the god of voyages. Together, they govern the seas and all travel dependent upon weather and wind. Táranon's storms define the sailor's experience, and while some are destructive, others carry ships swiftly to their destinations. The priesthoods of both deities work closely in maritime communities, jointly blessing voyages and interpreting the divine will as expressed through weather and wind patterns.

The relationship with [[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]] is one of mutual respect and practical cooperation. Sacred Forge smiths craft copper lightning conductors, protective talismans, and ceremonial implements used in Táranon's worship. In turn, the priesthood of Táranon works to protect foundries from destructive lightning strikes, understanding that the god's power should serve civilization rather than destroy it. This partnership is particularly strong in [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]], where Sacred Forge influence dominates the religious landscape.

Táranon maintains a distant but acknowledged relationship with [[affiliation-venusia|Faith of Vénusia]], the goddess of prosperity and harvest. The rains that Táranon provides are essential for fertility and agricultural abundance—Vénusia cannot grant prosperity without Táranon's cooperation. This is understood not as alliance but as complementary domains: the god of storms provides the raw elemental force, while the goddess of prosperity ensures that force is channeled toward abundance rather than destruction.

The priesthood of Táranon regards [[affiliation-aetheria|Faith of Aethería]] with fascination and some theological tension. Aethería, the Veiled Dreamer, represents the intuitive and mysterious forces that defy rational prediction. Storms themselves seem to embody this duality—while Táranonic priests track weather patterns with scientific precision, thunderstorms often surprise them with their ferocity or unpredictability. Some liberal Táranonic theologians suggest that Aethería whispers to the storms, introducing an element of intentional mystery into Táranon's otherwise comprehensible domain.
