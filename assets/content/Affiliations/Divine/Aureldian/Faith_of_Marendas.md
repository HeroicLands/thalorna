Goddess of learning, language, and invention. Marendas illuminates the minds of scholars, craftsmen, and leaders, guiding them to wisdom.

## Worship

The worship of Marendas is conducted in temples that are simultaneously places of prayer and centers of learning. Unlike the solemn severity of Árdavan temples or the martial energy of Sacred Forge shrines, Marendasan temples are characterized by intellectual vitality—scribes copying manuscripts, scholars debating philosophy, apprentices learning crafts, students of language conjugating foreign tongues. To worship Marendas is to participate in the pursuit of knowledge itself.

The greatest festival honoring Marendas is the Festival of Illumination, celebrated at the spring equinox when day and night are in perfect balance and the light of longer days begins to return. During this week-long celebration, the temples hold public lectures on philosophy, theology, and natural science. Scholars from across regions gather to debate fundamental questions about the nature of reality, the proper organization of society, and the place of mortals in the cosmos. Winners of philosophical competitions are awarded scrolls and manuscripts as prizes and are granted prominent positions in temple libraries. The festival culminates in a ceremony of oath-taking where students formally commit themselves to the pursuit of knowledge and teachers pledge to transmit wisdom to the next generation.

Another significant observance is the Dedication of the Apprentice, held at the beginning of autumn when young people traditionally enter training in crafts and professions. Apprentices present themselves before Marendasan priests for blessing and instruction in the intellectual and ethical foundations of their chosen trades. A smith apprentice is taught not only forge-work but also the geometry underlying architectural structures; a scribe is taught not only letter-forms but also the ethical responsibility of accurately recording knowledge; a merchant is taught not only accounting but also the logical principles of fair trade.

Individual devotion to Marendas takes the form of scholarly and intellectual pursuit. The devout are expected to develop mastery in at least one field of knowledge—whether that be medicine, mathematics, history, languages, craftsmanship, or philosophical theory—and to share that knowledge with others through teaching or the creation of works that benefit civilization. Many households maintain libraries and reading corners dedicated to Marendas, and the faithful may spend hours in study and contemplation. Making offerings to Marendas typically involves donating books and scrolls to temple libraries, commissioning the creation of new manuscripts, or sponsoring the education of promising youth who could not otherwise afford training.

## Organization

The priesthood of Marendas is fundamentally different from other Aureldian faiths in that it prioritizes scholarship over hierarchy. At the apex stands the **Mentara**, the Master of Wisdom, traditionally chosen by vote of the senior scholars within the faith rather than appointed by secular or ecclesiastical authority. The Mentara is the keeper of Marendasan doctrine and as the final authority on matters of theological interpretation. The current Mentara maintains residence at the Grand Temple of Illumination in a major center of learning, from which she oversees the priesthood's diverse scholarly endeavors.

Below the Mentara stands the **Daskeral**, a body of perhaps twenty to thirty senior scholars who specialize in different domains of knowledge—mathematics, medicine, philosophy, languages, craftsmanship, astronomy, and so forth. These individuals serve simultaneously as priests, teachers, and researchers. Many maintain their own schools and research initiatives, which are loosely coordinated but operationally independent. The Daskeral meets quarterly to discuss matters of theological importance and to coordinate efforts where knowledge from different disciplines intersects.

The **Daskeni**, ordained teachers scattered throughout cities and settlements, form the active teaching priesthood of Marendas. These individuals maintain schools, mentor apprentices, perform research in their areas of expertise, and serve as community leaders in matters of education and intellectual culture. In wealthy cities, a Dasken might oversee a school with hundreds of students; in smaller settlements, a single teacher might educate a dozen apprentices in various crafts. These teachers are expected to maintain their scholarly work even as they teach, ensuring that Marendasan knowledge advances rather than merely being preserved.

The **Lerini**, students and junior scholars, form the foundation of the priesthood. Many are young people undergoing intensive training in specific fields of knowledge; others are mature individuals who have come to scholarship later in life. The path to full priesthood is highly variable—there is no fixed tenure, no rigid hierarchy. A brilliant young mathematician might be ordained as a Daskena after five years of study, while a slower student might spend a decade in training or may find their calling lies in advanced apprenticeship rather than formal priesthood.

A distinctive feature of Marendasan organization is the network of **Notral Maldor**, great libraries that serve as centers of knowledge preservation and research. These libraries are jointly maintained by the priesthood and by wealthy patrons who understand the value of preserved knowledge. The libraries in [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]] and [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] contain thousands of manuscripts and are destinations of pilgrimage for scholars throughout western Ankaris. Librarians within these institutions hold significant authority within the priesthood, as the preservation and organization of knowledge is understood as sacred work.

The priesthood is funded through a combination of sources: endowments from wealthy patrons of learning, fees charged for education and apprenticeship training (often waived or reduced for the poor), and direct patronage from secular rulers who recognize that a learned priesthood brings prestige and practical benefit to their kingdoms. Unlike some other Aureldian faiths, Marendas's priesthood deliberately avoids accumulating temporal power or territorial holdings, instead maintaining focus on intellectual and educational work.

## Relations

Marendas stands in a relationship of deep intellectual partnership with [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]]. Where Árdavon provides the legal and moral frameworks within which knowledge can be preserved and transmitted, Marendas illuminates the understanding that gives those frameworks meaning and applicability. The two priesthoods cooperate extensively in education and governance—Árdavan judges often consult Marendasan scholars on complex legal questions, and Marendasan schools operate under the protection of Árdavan law. Many temples of Árdavon include substantial libraries and host Marendasan scholars as permanent residents.

The relationship with [[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]] is one of close practical alliance. Sacred Forge smiths and artificers benefit immensely from Marendasan knowledge in mathematics, geometry, metallurgy, and engineering. Conversely, the practical experimentation and innovation of craftspeople contributes new knowledge that enriches Marendasan understanding. Joint research initiatives between the two priesthoods have produced advances in architecture, metalworking, and other applied sciences. In major cities, temples of both deities are often built in proximity, and their priesthoods maintain regular scholarly exchanges.

Marendas maintains a cordial relationship with [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]]. The goddess of prosperity recognizes that knowledge and education are essential components of economic development and that an educated populace is more prosperous and productive. While not intimate allies, the priesthoods cooperate in sponsoring merchant guilds' training and in supporting commercial education.

Marendas regards [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] and [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]] with interest and some theological curiosity. Lusinia's creative force and Lizelas's generative power are understood as expressions of divine knowledge made manifest—the universe itself is Lusinia's creation and Lizelas's garden. Some Marendasan theologians study the relationships between creation, growth, and knowledge, exploring whether there is a deep philosophical connection between these domains.

Finally, Marendas stands in principled opposition to [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]]. The Black Flame denies the value of wisdom, rejecting knowledge in favor of raw will and power. The priesthood of Marendas actively works to preserve knowledge and support education in regions threatened by Black Flame influence, understanding that an educated, thinking population is resistant to the seductive simplifications of tyranny and domination.
