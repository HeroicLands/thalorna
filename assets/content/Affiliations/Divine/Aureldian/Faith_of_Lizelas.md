The Faith of Lizelas teaches that [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]], goddess of fertility, growth, and healing, ensures the fertility of people, animals, and the land. She is invoked during planting seasons, childbirth, rites of renewal, and healing.

## Worship

The worship of Lizelas is joyous, exuberant, and deeply sensual—a celebration of abundance, pleasure, healing, and the creative power of life itself. Her temples are built to capture light, decorated with flowers, fountains, and gardens of extraordinary beauty. Priestesses of Lizelas are trained in the cultivation of flowers and healing herbs, in the arts of medicine and midwifery, and in the celebration of love and desire. Where other temples may be austere or solemn, Lizelas's sanctuaries are places of beauty and comfort, filled with fragrant blooms, singing birds, and the sound of water.

The great festival honoring Lizelas occurs in early spring at the first signs of new growth, and it is celebrated with elaborate pageantry, music, dancing, and feasting. The [Festival of the First Bloom]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} lasts seven days in most cities and is marked by processions of priestesses crowned with fresh flowers, by the opening of new gardens, and by communal planting ceremonies where all are invited to place seeds in the earth. Marriages and handfastings frequently occur during this festival, the unions believed to receive the goddess’s blessing of fertility and lasting joy. Young couples exchange flower garlands, and the priesthood performs elaborate blessings for couples hoping to conceive. Markets overflow with fresh produce, flowers, and honey-cakes, and the air itself seems to shimmer with possibility.

Another significant celebration, the [Blessing of the Harvest]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, occurs in late summer when the first fruits ripen. Families bring the first of their abundance to Lizelas's temples, where priestesses bless the crops and the farmers, offering thanksgiving for fertility and abundance. Part of the harvest is left as offering to the goddess, while the remainder is distributed to the poor, embodying the goddess's principle that abundance must be shared.

Individual worship of Lizelas is warm and personal. Pregnant women visit her temples frequently, seeking blessings for easy delivery and healthy children. The sick and injured come seeking healing from priestesses who are herbalists and healers, understanding that Lizelas's gifts extend to the restoration of bodies damaged by illness or wound. Those mourning the loss of a child sometimes keep vigil at Lizelas's shrines, seeking comfort and renewal. Young people on the eve of their first romantic involvement often leave offerings at her altars, seeking her blessing for love.

Offerings to Lizelas are abundant and sensory—fresh flowers, honey, wine, sweet herbs, and the first fruits of harvest. Many homes maintain small shrines to the goddess with blooming plants or fresh flowers, which are replaced regularly as they fade. Gardens and herb-lofts maintained for healing purposes are often dedicated to Lizelas, and those who work in healing professions frequently maintain personal devotion to the goddess.

## Organization

The priesthood of Lizelas is led by the **Lizena**, the High Priestess (occasionally High Priest, though the role is more frequently filled by women). This figure is typically a middle-aged priestess of renowned skill in healing and gardening, someone whose wisdom and warmth draw the faithful and whose authority extends throughout the priesthood. The Lizena is a healer, teacher, and intermediary between the goddess and her people.

Below the Lizena stand the **Sarnori**, ordained priestesses and priests who serve as healers, midwives, gardeners, and officiants at marriages and fertility blessings. These priests are chosen for their skill in healing arts, their knowledge of herbs and plants, and their capacity for compassion. Many Sarnori maintain their own herb gardens or healing practices and are supported by their temples. The finest Sarnori are renowned healers sought by noble families and common people alike, trusted to deliver babies safely and to cure illnesses that other physicians cannot treat.

The third rank consists of **Acolytes of the Garden**, young initiates who train in herbalism, midwifery, healing, and the cultivation of gardens. These acolytes typically spend three to five years in apprenticeship, learning the properties of hundreds of plants, the techniques of childbirth assistance, and the pastoral care of the afflicted. Many eventually establish themselves as independent herbalists or midwives while maintaining ties to the priesthood.

A distinctive institution within Lizelas's faith is the **House of Healing**, a temple-hospital found in nearly every major city. These establishments employ dozens of priestesses, nurses, and servants, and they serve the poor and sick without regard for wealth or social status. They are supported by donations from grateful patients, by the sale of herbal remedies, and by direct patronage from the nobility. The finest Houses of Healing are renowned across kingdoms as places where the dying are comforted, the sick are healed, and women in difficult childbirth receive care from skilled priestesses who have delivered thousands of babies.

## Relations

Lizelas stands in joyful partnership with [[affiliation-lusinia|Lusinia]], together representing the full cycle of fertility and growth. In their theology, where [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] emphasizes patience, cultivation, and the methodical work of creation, Lizelas celebrates joy, pleasure, and the abundant flowering of life. The two goddesses are frequently invoked together at planting and harvest times, and their priesthoods often cooperate in festivals and healing work.

The relationship with [[affiliation-morvana|Morvana]] is one of gentle contrast rather than conflict. Lizelas celebrates abundance and renewal; in their teaching, [[lore-morvanadty|Morvana]] reminds mortals that all things must eventually fade. The two faiths acknowledge each other as complementary truths—enjoy the flowers while they bloom, knowing that they will wilt and return to earth. Some temples maintain joint shrines where Lizelas's fresh blooms are displayed beside withered flowers sacred to Morvana, teaching both celebration and acceptance.

Lizelas maintains cordial relations with [[affiliation-marendas|Marendas]], recognizing that knowledge of plants, medicine, and healing comes through both intuition and study. Many priestesses of Lizelas are accomplished herbalists who consult with scholars of Marendas to understand the deeper nature of the plants they cultivate. Libraries of Marendas often employ priestesses of Lizelas as consultants on medical and botanical texts.

The relationship with [[affiliation-olvenia|Ólvenia]] (Prosperity) is one of natural affinity. In their theology, where [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] blesses commerce and wealth, Lizelas blesses the agricultural abundance that creates that prosperity. A thriving farm blessed by Lizelas's priestesses will produce the crops that a merchant of Ólvenia's faith may sell for profit. The two goddesses together represent the full cycle of fertility translating into material prosperity.

With [[affiliation-pozeras|Pozeras]] (Voyages), Lizelas maintains a relationship of support and blessing. Sailors and merchants departing on journeys often visit temples of Lizelas, seeking blessings for safe travel and successful ventures. Priestesses of the goddess pray for favorable winds and abundant harvests for those who will return, understanding that Lizelas's blessings extend to all journeys of fertility and return, whether agricultural or mercantile.

The attitude toward [[affiliation-karnavos|Karnavos]] is wary but not hostile. Lizelas understands that wild chaos and unexpected disruption are forces of nature, and she does not shrink from them. Wild storms bring rain that nurtures crops; forest fires create space for new growth. However, the priesthood teaches that chaos must be balanced with cultivation and care. In their theology, where [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] represents untamed wilderness, Lizelas represents the blessing of nature brought into relationship with civilization, both parties enriched by the connection.
