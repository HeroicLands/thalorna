## Daotou Wochai—The Lord of Metal

The Elemental Lord of Metal manifests in the celestial records as a figure of absolute rigidity and reflection—an official whose appearance never varies, whose rulings are issued in identical format regardless of circumstance, whose authority extends equally across all classifications of metal without preference or exception. Some Tanchéi scholars suggest that Daotou Wochai does not merely _administrate_ the metallic element but _is_ the principle of hardness itself made bureaucratic: unyielding, reflective, and utterly indifferent to appeal.

Like all Elemental Lords, Daotou Wochai answers directly to the Jade Sovereign (Wéizhuo Daonéi) rather than to a specific bureau overseer. His authority extends across all manifestations of metal—precious and base, worked and raw, decorative and functional. In the Tantsou cosmology, metal is not merely a material substance but a _category of being_: the hard boundary that separates one thing from another, the reflective surface that reveals truth, the conductor of forces that would otherwise dissipate. Daotou Wochai administers all of these simultaneously, maintaining the distinction between metal-as-boundary and metal-as-substance with obsessive precision.

### The Classification of Metals

The Lord of Metal maintains the boundary between four primary administrative categories, each governed according to canonical protocols and subordinate clerk-spirits:

**Base Metals (Wochao Zhou)**—Iron, copper, tin, lead, and other common metallurgical materials. This category includes the raw ore in the earth (supervised jointly with the Earth Lord), its extraction and initial refinement, and its delivery to craftspeople. Daotou Wochai delegates most routine supervision of this category to the clerk-spirit **Tuigui**, who maintains the registry of all known ore deposits and their spiritual allocations. A mining operation that wishes to extract metal from a given site must file a petition through proper channels, requesting authorization from both Daotou Wochai and the territorial Earth Official (Touwo).

**Precious Metals (Wochai Fa)**—Gold, silver, platinum, and rarer metallic substances of ceremonial or commercial value. This category is supervised with particular strictness, since precious metals serve as the material substrate for the seals that authenticate celestial documents. A single falsified seal could theoretically compromise the integrity of the entire celestial filing system. For this reason, Daotou Wochai maintains direct personal oversight of the precious metal registry and delegates its supervision to no clerk-spirit. Access to precious metals requires not merely a petition but a character assessment filed by the requesting party's household monitor (Tsaikén) and explicit approval from the local Touwo (territorial administrator).

**Worked Metals (Ho Wochai)**—Tools, weapons, decorative objects, and ceremonial implements forged or cast from metallic material. This category overlaps with the Bureau of Transformation (Ho Sei) and creates significant jurisdictional tension. The formal understanding is that Daotou Wochai maintains authority over the _metallic integrity_ of the finished object (its purity, hardness, and reflective properties) while Zhenho of the Bureau of Transformation claims authority over the _functional transformation_ involved in the working (the forging, casting, and shaping). In practice, disputes over where metalworking ends and transformation begins are filed constantly with the Supreme Administrative Court.

**Symbolic Metals (Wochai Rui)**—Metal used in ritual contexts, ceremonial garments, temple furnishings, and administrative seals. This is the category most directly supervised by Daotou Wochai in person. Every seal used in the celestial bureaucracy is registered in the Lord of Metal's personal records, and the spiritual weight of incorrect seal usage falls directly upon Daotou Wochai's authority. For this reason, the Lord of Metal is intensely conservative in granting permission for new ritual uses of metal. Petitions to use metal in novel ceremonial contexts are frequently denied, on the grounds that unprecedented metallic symbolism creates unclassifiable boundary conditions.

### Authority and Jurisdictional Tensions

Daotou Wochai's primary administrative relationship is with the **Earth Lord** (Wéngdao Zhouwo), since all metal begins as ore in the earth. The two Elemental Lords maintain a formal protocol of coordination: Wéngdao Zhouwo authorizes the presence of metal ore within the earth's domain, and Daotou Wochai authorizes its extraction and classification as separate from the earth-mass. This is theoretically clear but practically fraught—disputes over whether a particular ore deposit remains "part of the earth" or has been "classified as metal awaiting extraction" are filed with such frequency that a standing committee of the Supreme Administrative Court has been established to hear nothing else.

Daotou Wochai's secondary tension is with **Zhenho** (Bureau of Transformation), over the boundary between metalworking and raw metal. This dispute is less formally resolved than the Earth Lord conflict, and Daotou Wochai frequently files complaints that the Bureau of Transformation overreaches its authority by claiming jurisdiction over metals during the working process. The Lord of Metal argues that metal maintains its essential character throughout all transformation—a sword is still fundamentally metal, even when it is being hammered—and therefore remains under his jurisdiction. Zhenho counters that the _function_ of the object determines its administrative category, not its material composition.

A tertiary administrative burden falls to Daotou Wochai through his role in the **celestial record-keeping system**. The bureaucratic seals that authenticate all documents and petitions throughout the Téngwou Rong Lei are metallic and therefore fall under his domain. Any disruption to the precious-metal supply directly threatens the administrative integrity of the entire celestial bureaucracy. For this reason, Daotou Wochai petitions the Jade Sovereign regularly for expanded authority to intervene in mortal metalworking and mining—requests that are typically denied on the grounds that such direct intervention would violate administrative precedent. Nevertheless, the Lord of Metal maintains informal networks of influence through his clerk-spirits and through the priests of the Tanchéi caste who serve as intermediaries.

### Mortal Engagement

Mortals petition Daotou Wochai through formal offerings at temple shrines dedicated to the Elemental Lord, through the burning of spirit-documents requesting metallic prosperity or protection, or through the mediation of the Tanchéi priesthood. Petitions to Daotou Wochai are characteristically formal and rigid—the Lord of Metal will not accept improvised or emotionally-charged prayers. All petitions must be filed according to established protocol, with correct spacing, proper seal placement, and unambiguous language.

**Blacksmiths and Metalworkers** form the primary petitioning constituency. A smith petitions Daotou Wochai before beginning work with a new ore source, requesting that the metal comply with technique and expectation. Unlike petitions to Wojing Ja (the Fire Lord), which focus on the behavior of the flame, petitions to Daotou Wochai focus on the _integrity_ and _responsiveness_ of the metal itself. A smith might petition: "Grant that this iron, being drawn from the Tuichai mines and identified in the registry as Wochai-grade base metal, shall maintain its purity through the forging process and respond reliably to temperature and technique." The metal's responsiveness to the smith's craft is understood as a celestial administrative grace, not a mechanical property.

**Miners and Prospectors** petition Daotou Wochai for permission to extract ore from specific locations. This petition is theoretically filed with the territorial Touwo (Earth Official), but in practice all mining petitions are forwarded to Daotou Wochai's office for final approval. A prospector who discovers a promising ore deposit must first petition the Earth Lord for permission to disturb the earth, then petition Daotou Wochai for permission to classify that ore as "extracted metal." Disputes over ore deposits between competing mining interests are adjudicated through the Lord of Metal's jurisdiction.

**Merchants and Traders** handling precious metals petition Daotou Wochai for protection against adulteration and theft. A trader transporting gold across trade routes may offer to the Lord of Metal in hopes that the precious metal will remain unmolested and maintain its purity during transit.

**Temple Officials and Record-Keepers** petition Daotou Wochai as part of routine maintenance of the bureaucratic infrastructure. New seals must be registered; old seals must be properly retired and recycled. The creation of new official documents that require authentic metallic seals flows through Daotou Wochai's office, making the Lord of Metal a subtle but essential gatekeeper in the celestial administrative system.
