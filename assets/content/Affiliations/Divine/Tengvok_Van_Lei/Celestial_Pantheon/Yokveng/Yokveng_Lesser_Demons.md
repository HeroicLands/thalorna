## The Lesser Demons—Yōkvëng

The [[affiliation-yokveng|Yokveng]] is home to far more than its five great Demon Lords. The Infernal Realm teems with a population of lesser demonic beings—the **Yōkvëng** proper—whose nature and function is less grand and more pragmatic than that of the Lords. Where the Demon Lords represent the fundamental failures of the celestial classification system, the lesser demons are the _operators_ of those failures, the functionaries through which the Yokveng engages with the classified cosmos.

The lesser demons are not malevolent in a theological sense. They do not embody cosmic rebellion or fundamental anti-classification. They are, rather, the spiritual equivalent of bureaucratic subordinates—clerks, messengers, enforcers, and minor officials whose role is to execute the orders of higher powers or to exploit the gaps in the celestial administration for their own advantage. Many lesser demons were once functionaries of the celestial bureaus before corruption, dismissal, or unauthorized initiative led them to seek refuge in the Yokveng. Some were never bureaucratic at all, but rather coalesced from the raw demonic potential of the Infernal Realms, forming consciousness and will in the shadows of the Demon Lords.

### Those Who Petition Them

The lesser demons are petitioned far more often than the Demon Lords, for the ordinary reason that
they are cheaper to reach and their answers are smaller. A ledger-gnawer asked to lose one entry is
a modest transaction; a Demon Lord asked for anything at all is not.

Their practitioners are correspondingly modest—not rings but individuals, scattered through the
empire's clerical classes exactly where the note above says they end up: scribes, monks, minor
bureaucratic officials. A man who can reach a seal-thief is worth knowing; he is also, usually,
employed doing something entirely respectable, and the two facts are not in tension.

What such a person holds is a small and specific inheritance: the forms of address for one or two
classes of lesser demon, the appeasements each expects, and the knowledge of which requests are
worth making. It is taught by one practitioner to one other, rarely written, and lost constantly.
The Tānthëi consider the resulting variation the reason lesser-demon petitions go wrong so much
more often than Demon Lord petitions, which are drafted by professionals.

They go wrong in the same direction as everything else in the Yokveng. A ledger-gnawer asked to
remove an inconvenient entry will remove the entry—and, if the request was loosely drawn, the
ledger, the archive it sat in, and the clerk who filed it.

### Nature and Hierarchy

The lesser demons occupy a space of severe inequality. A powerful demon may command dozens of subordinates; a weak one may exist in near-thralldom to a stronger entity. There is no formal hierarchy, no unified administration, and no appeal process. Authority in the Yokveng is asserted through power—spiritual strength, accumulated secrets, access to stolen seals, or proximity to one of the Demon Lords.

Yet for all their chaos, the lesser demons operate according to consistent principles. Each has a specialized function or domain: ledger-gnawers who devour records, seal-thieves who steal celestial authority, possession-spirits who inhabit mortal vessels, threshold-whispers who insinuate doubt and temptation at the margins of consciousness. These specializations are not assigned by higher power but emerge through self-organization and predatory selection. A demon good at corrupting officials accumulates more influence and begins to gather apprentices. A demon skilled at possessing mortals becomes a resource to other, less talented entities. Over time, functional hierarchies crystallize, though they remain fluid and subject to violent rearrangement.

Lesser demons possess corporeal form, but it is neither stable nor material in the ordinary sense. A lesser demon might appear as smoke, as shadow, as writing that moves across a surface, as a voice without source, or as the sensation of wrongness in a space where something ought to be. Some take on persistent shapes when they remain long in a particular location or attach themselves to a particular mortal host. Others shift constantly, as though their form has not yet settled into any consistent category.

### Categories and Functions

The Tānthëi have identified several broad classes of lesser demon, though they acknowledge that this taxonomy is incomplete and that the Yokveng continuously generates new types:

#### **Ledger-Gnawers** (Sīkvūrzhïk)

Lesser demons specialized in the corruption and destruction of celestial records. A ledger-gnawer will infiltrate archives, devour pages, or alter entries in order to obscure accounts or create false trails. They serve the Demon Lord [[affiliation-vekthor|Vëkthōr]], the Forger of False Records, though many operate independently. Mortals in league with ledger-gnawers often become scribes, monks, or bureaucratic officials—positions from which a demon can most effectively damage the celestial record-keeping apparatus.

#### **Seal-Thieves** (Zhükrëi)

Demons whose function is the theft, counterfeiting, and improper deployment of celestial seals and stamps. These beings understand bureaucratic authentication as a form of power and extract authority from the celestial system by acquiring documents or seal-implements. Seal-thieves are closely affiliated with [[affiliation-yokzhuk|Yökzhük]], the Custodian of Lost Seals, and access to Yökzhük's archive is typically controlled by seal-thieves who guard the infernal repository.

#### **Threshold-Whispers** (Lüngvörsīk)

Demons who specialize in temptation, insinuation, and the exploitation of moral weakness. A threshold-whisper does not typically possess a mortal, but rather positions itself at the edge of consciousness—suggesting, encouraging, doubting. Over time, a persistent threshold-whisper can reshape a mortal's understanding of what is possible, what is permissible, what is necessary. They are favored agents for recruiting mortals into the [[affiliation-nuszokreit|Nüszōk Rëit]], as their methods are more subtle than direct possession or coercion.

#### **Possession-Spirits** (Zhëtvök)

Lesser demons specialized in the inhabitation and control of mortal vessels. A possession-spirit is typically weaker than other demon classes and unable to exert meaningful influence on the classified cosmos without a mortal host. In exchange, they gain access to physical sensation, appetite, and the ability to take action in the material world. Possession-spirits are the most commonly encountered lesser demons by ordinary mortals, though most such encounters are not recognized as demonic—they are attributed to illness, madness, or moral failing.

#### **Archive-Shadows** (Thōkvōr)

Demons whose domain is the hidden spaces between records, the forgotten marginal notes in celestial documents, the erased corrections that still leave traces. An archive-shadow consumes the _substance_ of archives—not the visible text but the authority and coherence that holds a record system together. They are parasites on the celestial bureaucracy itself, and they are particularly valued by entities like [[affiliation-vekthor|Vëkthōr]] who wish to poison the archive without leaving obvious evidence of tampering.

#### **Entropy-Vermin** (Hürnvëthzhük)

Lesser demons that serve the Demon Lord [[affiliation-nushot|Nüshöt]] and embody his principle of annihilation without remainder. These beings specialize in the complete erasure of classified things—objects, persons, memories, administrative categories. Wherever an entropy-vermin feeds, what it consumes does not decompose or recycle. It is simply gone, falling back into the Hürnlëi as undifferentiated potential.

#### **Wayward Spirits** (Dōkzhëkvōr)

Demons of passage and misdirection, affiliated with the Demon Lord [[affiliation-dokyok|Dōkyök]]. These lesser demons maintain the hidden roads of the Infernal Realms and guide (or mislead) those who seek passage. Some wayward spirits are conscripted into serving [[affiliation-dokyok|Dōkyök]] by circumstance; others genuinely prefer the role of guide, as it grants them singular power over the spaces they maintain.

### Mortal Engagement

The vast majority of mortal encounters with the Yokveng occur through lesser demons rather than Demon Lords. A mortal who seeks to contact the Infernal Realms—whether through accident, desperation, or deliberate ritual—will most likely find a lesser demon.

Direct bargains with lesser demons are possible but risky. A demon will agree to a bargain if the terms offer advantage—typically the mortal provides something the demon desires (access to records, a valuable host, contact with a person of influence) in exchange for the demon providing something the mortal needs (knowledge, power, aid in a criminal or transgressive act). The contract is typically unwritten and unenforceable by any power except the demon itself, which means the terms are whatever the demon claims they are. Mortals who bargain with lesser demons frequently discover that they have agreed to far more onerous terms than they understood, and repayment is extracted over years or even across generations of a family line.

The **Nüszōk Rëit** maintains the most sustained organized relationship with the lesser demons. Cult operatives include those who have been possessed by lesser demons and never wholly purged themselves, those who have made formal bargains for esoteric knowledge or power, and those who serve as intermediaries between the cult's mortal structure and the Yokveng's demonic hierarchy. The cult provides the lesser demons with resources they value—records to corrupt, mortals to possess, opportunities to expand their influence—in exchange for aid in the cult's program of bureaucratic subversion.

Lesser demons can be exorcized through properly performed celestial rituals that invoke the authority of the Sun Bureaus and seal the boundaries between the classified cosmos and the Infernal Realms. However, such exorcisms require precise knowledge of celestial procedure and are most effectively conducted by trained members of the priesthood. Amateurs who attempt exorcism risk triggering a more violent demonic response or, alternatively, merely driving the demon temporarily away without achieving true expulsion. Some exorcized demons merely hide until the protection fades; others exact revenge on the mortals who sought to banish them.
