## The Severers

"The words exist on every day," the First Severer tells each Hearer. "The day does not. A priest who speaks them on a closed day has not made a mistake. He has made something else."

The **Severers**, _Shaq'al'ob_ in Kì'ì'chek, are the body of [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] that forbids. Their name is built on _shaq'al_, apart from, separate from. What they keep apart is the working and the closed day, and what they cut away is the person who joins them. They answer to no branch. They are drawn from all of them, and they judge all of them.

## The Working

The Severers speak almost no workings. Their task is the law that governs everyone else's, and it rests on one rule of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]]: on a day closed to a god, the grammar has no sentence. A priest who speaks a working on such a day has, by orthodox teaching, said nothing. The Severers' law adds the second half. If something follows, it was not a working of the Ten, and whoever spoke it has worked outside the tables.

So the order tries the closed day. An **Inspector** establishes the day a disputed working was spoken, checks it against [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]] table the city issued, and finds whether anything was worked. A working spoken on an opened day is no concern of the order's, however badly it went. A working spoken on a closed day that did nothing is a priest's error, and is sent back to his branch. A working spoken on a closed day that did something is a closed-day working, and is the Severers' to sentence.

The sentence is the **Blood-Denied**, the lowest rung of the priesthood's ladder: barred from offering blood, from the calendar and from the funerary rites. The **Speaker of the Sentence** pronounces it before the temple, with the condemned's lineage present, and the Kì'ì'chek count it the one sentence that outlives the body. Death may follow it. Death never replaces it.

The Severers hold a reason for the law that the rest of the priesthood is not asked to share. They take the account the Wardens of the Sealed Chambers keep, that the Fourth Age's count was broken by a working spoken on a closed day, as the law's foundation, and every Hearer is taught it in his first year. The order treats it as the reason a closed-day working is not one priest's error but a danger to the age.

## What the Order Forbids

**The Awakeners.** [[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil) work on no day the tables open, and what they do works. The serpent-keepers of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|Faith of P'uqil Ch'akun]] hunt the cells; the Severers try what the serpent-keepers bring them, and pronounce.

**The charm-sellers.** In every market there are people who sell a blessing, a cure or a curse on any day a buyer asks for one. The Severers hold that such a charm is either nothing, which is fraud and the city's business, or a closed-day working, which is theirs. Most are found to be nothing. The order keeps the few that are not.

**Foreigners who work.** A mage from beyond the ocean knows nothing of the tables and works on whatever day he chooses. In the Severers' law such a working is speech without a day, which is the Awakeners' kind, and the **Examiner of Foreigners** questions any foreigner known to work. A foreigner who does nothing in sight of a shrine is left alone. One who works where the count is kept is tried like a priest.

**Forbidden writings.** A codex that sets down a working outside the set forms is burned, and the **Keeper of the Burned** records that it was burned and why, and nothing of what it said.

The law reaches as far as the count is kept, and no farther. A shaman of the northern peoples, working in his own country where there are no white roads, speaks into no day the tables know, and the Severers hold that he is outside their law. The same shaman working on a white road is not.

## Recruitment

Each branch gives priests to the order, and none may give more than one Severer in five in any city. A branch that supplies too many has its next candidate refused. Most Severers are Ch'alishbal who have served as **Witnesses**, the priests a branch lends to stand at an inspection and swear to what they saw. Witnesses who prove steady are asked to stay. Reckoners are prized, because an Inspector must read a table as well as the Reckoner who issued it.

A Severer is never judged by his own branch, and the order's first sentence in any case is on itself: a Severer found to have worked on a closed day, or to have hidden one who did, is **Severed** before the order pronounces on anyone else.

## The Sealed Chambers

At each turning of the moon's count the **Warden of the Sealed Chambers** opens the serpent-skin calculations of the age's ending before witnesses from two other branches. The Severers supply those witnesses. The **Inspector of the Chambers** brings them, stands with them and records that the seals were whole when the Warden broke them and whole again when he closed them. The order may not read what the chambers hold. It answers only for the seals.

## Relations

The Severers answer to the Q'emul Tq'an through the **First Severer**, and in each city to its Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an. The serpent-keepers of P'uqil Ch'akun are their closest allies, and the two divide the Awakeners between them: one hunts and the other judges.

With the Star House the order is aligned and dependent. Every sentence rests on a table the house computed, so the Severers need its honesty more than any other body does, and they ask for the arithmetic behind a disputed day more often than any other body does. The house shows it to an Inspector in K'ankul and nowhere else.

With [[affiliation-tsumulob|the Blood-Workers]] the order is a rival. The **Examiner of Ledgers** inspects every band's field ledger on its return. The Blood-Workers hold that a band beyond the cords must work on its best reckoning. The Severers hold that a closed day is a closed day, wherever a band stands.

Toward [[affiliation-pikob|the Owl Keepers]] (Pik'ob) the order is wary. A grove is a place where the owl keepers have undertaken to speak nothing, and a place where nothing is spoken is also a place where nobody is watching for what is. Pik'ob who vanish into the jungle are the Severers' business, and the order asks after each one.

[[affiliation-pikultzumaq|The Owl of the Serpent]] (Pik'ul Ts'umaq) is its nemesis beside the Awakeners. The order counts every Keeper of the Door it finds as Blood-Denied, and every place where the boundary has been opened is sealed and recorded.
