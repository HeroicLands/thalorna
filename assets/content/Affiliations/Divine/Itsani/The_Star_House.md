## The Star House

"We do not open the days," the First of the Star House says to each year's new reckoners. "The goddess opened them at the creation. We are the only people who can find out which."

The **Star House**, _Ish'ilan Sh'ulum_ in Ki'i'chek, is the college of reckoners at [[place-kankul2|K'ankul]] that computes the day-tables of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]]. Every city's **Reckoner of Conjunctions** issues the opened and closed days to his priests and his people. In every city the cords reach, the tables he issues them from were made here. The house belongs to the astronomers' branch of [[affiliation-ixilanchalix|Faith of Ish'ilan Ch'alish]], and its reckoners are her priests, but its work is the whole priesthood's: no working of any of the Ten is spoken on a day the Star House has not found open.

Its name joins _ish'ilan_, a star and the count of the fixed stars, with _sh'ulum_, a house. It is not one of the star-houses where each observatory keeps its codices. It is the house those star-houses answer to.

## The Working

The Star House enables. It speaks one working, the reckoning of [[skill-ixilanchalix|Ritual: Ish'ilan Ch'alish]], a few drops of blood on the page at the instant of a conjunction, and it speaks that working more often than any other body speaks any working at all. Everything else it does is arithmetic.

A day-table is the four counts joined. The **Ts'obanal** of the house each carry one count forward: the sun's year, the 260 days, the moon, the fixed stars. A **Table-Maker** joins their figures into one table for one city, because a city's sighting lines and its sacred peak set its counts apart from its neighbors' by a small, fixed amount. The finished table names, for each day of the coming twenty-day months, which of the Ten it is opened to and which it is closed to. The First of the Star House signs it, the **Master of the Cords** has it knotted into _k'otsel_ cord, and the Speaking Road carries it to the city, where the Reckoner checks it against his own stones and issues it.

The tables travel as conclusions. A city receives its days and never the arithmetic behind them, and the Star House keeps that arithmetic in K'ankul. The rest of the priesthood calls this secrecy. The house calls it the only way to keep one error in one place, where it can be found.

What the tables cannot do, the house says plainly. They are computed for the cities the cords reach. Past the end of the white roads, at sea out of sight of a shrine and south of [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]], the tables give fewer answers with every day's distance, and of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]] they give none. The house does not say why. It says that the tables give no answer there, and it refuses to compute one.

## Recruitment and Standing

The house takes children. Parents bring a child with a head for figures to an observatory's steps at the new year, the city's astronomers set a first examination, and the best of each year are sent to K'ankul as **Children of the Steps**. Birth counts for nothing. The child of a salt-boiler and the son of a Ts'enmah sit the same examination, and the house's records show more of the first than of the second at every rank above Copyist.

Every rank above the first is won the same way. The **Examiner of Predictions** gives a candidate a set of recorded observations, the candidate derives from them a prediction to be checked against the sky, and the prediction is sealed until its night. The **Keeper of Errors** enters every failure beside the candidate's name, and none is ever removed. A reckoner with many errors can still rise. A reckoner who hides one is **Struck from the Tables**, and the house tells his city why.

K'ankul's standing among the realms rests on the house. Ish'ilankul holds the seat of the priesthood and the Q'emul Tq'an, but every city of Ish'ilankul works on days computed in K'ankul, and the realm is treated as a partner in the management of the faith rather than a tributary because no one has found a way to do without its tables.

## Relations

Within [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] the house answers to the Q'emul Tq'an through its **Envoy to [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]]**, and the Q'emul Tq'an consecrates each First of the Star House. He cannot choose one. The house elects its First on its own record of prediction and sends the name to Kul'taq'an for consecration.

With [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] the house is aligned and uneasy. Every sentence the Severers pronounce on a closed-day working rests on a Star House table: the day was closed because the house found it closed. So the Severers need the house's honesty more than anyone, and they ask for the arithmetic behind a disputed day more often than anyone. The house shows it to a Severer's inspector, in K'ankul, and nowhere else.

With [[affiliation-tsumulob|the Blood-Workers]] the house quarrels every campaigning season. An army in the field wants its days computed months ahead and as far from the cords as it means to march. The house computes field tables, marks every day beyond the cords' reach as an answer it cannot vouch for, and refuses to open a day because a war-leader needs it. The Blood-Workers call the tables timid. The house calls a day opened to suit a war-leader the shortest road to a closed-day working.

[[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil) are its nemesis and its standing danger. A reckoner who works the counts back far enough reaches the first day of the Fifth Age, where every count the house keeps begins. The Awakeners teach that the first day was chosen and not found in the sky, and that a count which begins by choice can end by one. A few reckoners in each generation come to believe it. The house records each one as a failed prediction, in the Keeper of Errors' book, under the reckoner's name.
