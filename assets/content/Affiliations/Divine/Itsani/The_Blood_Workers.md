## The Blood-Workers

"A captive is a working that has not yet been spoken," the Master of [[place-ishtalhun|Ishtal'hun]] tells each new Taker. "Bring him home alive and he is a storm on the next field. Kill him and you have spent the storm in the mud."

The **Blood-Workers**, _Ts'umul'ob_ in Kì'ì'chek, are the warrior-priests who carry the workings of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]] out of the cities and onto the field. Every one of them is an ordained priest of [[affiliation-tzenultqan|Faith of Ts'en'ul Tq'an]] and every one has taken a captive alive. Their name is built on _ts'umul_, red, the root of _ts'umulal_, blood.

They train at Ishtal'hun in [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]], the Place of Obsidian Blood, where the captive-warriors of the plateau are also made, and they keep a garrison at [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]], the border at the running water, from which their bands go south with the expeditions.

## The Working

The Blood-Workers harness. A city's greatest workings wait on the city's tables, the city's ledger and the city's pyramid, and an army in the field has none of the three. The Blood-Workers carry substitutes for all of them.

**The tables.** A band marches with copied day-tables, computed at [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]] for the ground the army means to cross, and its **Bearer of the Tables** reads the day each dawn. Inside the reach of the cords the copies are as good as a city's. Beyond it, the Star House has marked each day as an answer it cannot vouch for, and the band speaks a working on such a day at its own risk and enters it as such.

**The ledger.** Each Blood-Sworn binds his own blood to the order's account by vow, and a band's small workings are paid from its members' bodies: a traveler's blessing on the march, the binding of a vow before battle, a rain-calling on a field small enough for a few men's blood to pay. A great working in the field is paid from the band's home city. The **Keeper of the Field Ledger** enters every such spending against the city's account and brings it to the **Warden of the Debt** at the month's close, so a city learns what its army spent only after the army has spent it. Cities complain about this every season.

**The captives.** In a city, a warrior's vow over a captive is received by the Dedicator of Captives and the captive waits for a festival. In the field, the band's **Dedicator of the Field** receives the vow on the spot and assigns the captive to a working the band will speak on its next opened day. A band that has taken well can bring a storm onto the next field days ahead of the battle. A band that has killed where it could have taken has nothing to bring.

The workings a band spends are the ones a city spends, in set forms learned whole at Ishtal'hun: the rain-calling and the storm of [[skill-puqilchaqun|Ritual: P'uqil Ch'akun]], the binding of vows of [[skill-tzenultqan|Ritual: Ts'en'ul Tq'an]], the traveler's blessing of [[skill-xilanixlan|Ritual: Shilan Ish'lan]]. Each band marches with a death-walker of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] lent from the city, because soldiers who know they will be recited for climb a stair they would not otherwise climb.

## Recruitment

The order recruits from the captive-hunts. A warrior who takes a captive alive and presents him to the Dedicator may ask to be tested, and the Master of Ishtal'hun takes those who took cleanly, without maiming the captive or losing a man. Most are Ts'omilbal, younger sons of lord-houses, and the order ordains them as priests of the sun before they march. A few are Ch'alishbal of the sun's branch who have fought. Commoners who take a captive are accepted on the same terms and are more numerous at Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok than anywhere else.

A Blood-Worker who kills a captive he could have taken is **Spent**. The order expels him, the temple counts the dead captive against his city's ledger, and the Spent are commonly found afterward in the southern garrison's secular companies, where nobody asks.

## Relations

Within [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] the order answers to the Q'emul Tq'an for its ledger and to each city's Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an for the blood it spends from that city. It fights for the Ts'enmah whose army it marches with and takes no orders from him about its workings.

With the Star House it quarrels every campaigning season. The Blood-Workers want days computed farther from the cords and further ahead than the house will vouch for, and they call its field tables timid. The house refuses to open a day because a war-leader needs it.

With [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] it is a rival under watch. Every band's field ledger is inspected on its return, and every working the Bearer of the Tables recorded on an unvouched day is examined to find whether the day was in fact closed. The Blood-Workers hold that a band beyond the cords must work on its best reckoning or die. The Severers hold that a working spoken on a closed day is a closed-day working, whoever spoke it and however far from home.

[[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil) are the enemy the southern garrison exists to meet. The Awakeners' dead walk the deep jungle and the country south of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok, and the Blood-Workers fight them more often than any other body of the priesthood does. The bands meet [[affiliation-pikultzumaq|the Owl of the Serpent]]'s Threshold-Held there too, and the order holds both to be one enemy.
