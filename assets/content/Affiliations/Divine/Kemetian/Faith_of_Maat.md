Qe'âret is the great balancing force of the cosmos itself. She is not a god of compassion or mercy, but of absolute truth and immutable law. Before creation, chaos reigned supreme. Qe'âret imposed order upon that primordial darkness, establishing the laws by which the universe operates and by which all creation must abide. To live in harmony with Qe'âret is to live truthfully, to honor your oaths, to speak with clarity, and to balance every action with restraint and wisdom.

The theology of Qe'âret centers on a single, overwhelming doctrine: after death, every soul must face the Weighing of the Heart. In the great Hall of Two Truths, the deceased stands before the assembled gods while their heart is placed upon one side of a golden scale and a single feather of Qe'âret is placed upon the other. If the heart is heavy with lies, cruelty, theft, or cowardice, it will outweigh the feather and the soul is devoured by Álgit, the Devourer of the Dead. If the heart balances the feather, or proves lighter still, the soul passes onward into the blessed Zulaten, the realm of eternal peace. This doctrine shapes every aspect of Khelâthi civilization. The fear of that judgment, the desire to be "justified" before Qe'âret, is the very foundation of Khelâthi ethics.

The temples of Qe'âret are also temples of justice. A Lem'Nelgir of Qe'âret may serve as judge in disputes between nobles, between merchants, between the common people. The priesthood maintains the legal codes, interprets divine law, and ensures that contracts and covenants are honored. To swear an oath before the altar of Qe'âret is to invoke her direct judgment; to break such an oath is to invite divine retribution. The current Thâz'Lekhau of Qe'âret in the capital is the formidable Hathura Ise'Fréu, whose authority rivals even that of the Gar-Aû in matters of justice and law. She has deposed corrupt magistrates, overturned unjust decrees, and ensured that the scales of justice remain in perfect balance. Her word carries the weight of cosmic law itself.

The Kemetians understand that Qe'âret's order is not kindness. It is neutral and absolute. A just man who suffers poverty may be as "justified" before Qe'âret as a rich merchant, provided both have lived truthfully. A warrior who kills in honorable combat has nothing to fear from her judgment, while a king who breaks his oath faces her wrath. This understanding shapes the Khelâthi character: there is a deep respect for law and truth, coupled with a fatalism about one's place in the cosmic order.

## Worship

The temples of Qe'âret are vast complexes of white stone and dark granite, their walls inscribed with every law and principle under her dominion. Worshippers who enter these temples do so with profound solemnity. There is no festival of joy in Qe'âret's worship—there are only ceremonies of affirmation and balance. At the great solar noon, when the sun stands directly overhead, priests and supplicants gather to recite the Forty-Two Negative Confessions, a litany of sins they have not committed. "I have not killed. I have not stolen. I have not borne false witness. I have not coveted what belongs to another." Each negative confession is a reaffirmation of one's commitment to truthfulness and order.

When a contract is to be sworn or a solemn oath taken, parties may come before the altar of Qe'âret to invoke her authority. The ceremony requires the oathtaker to speak their vow aloud, with full voice and clear intent, before at least three witnesses. The Lem'Nelgir records the oath in the temple archives, and from that moment forward, the oath-taker is bound by both mortal and divine law. Breaking such an oath is not merely a social shame—it is a cosmic transgression that promises damnation at the weighing.

Each month, the Wazu acolytes of Qe'âret's temples perform the ritual of the Scales. They inscribe the temple's ledger of disputes and resolutions, ensuring that every judgment is recorded in perfect detail. These archives serve as both historical record and spiritual validation: the temple's record proves to Qe'âret that justice has been upheld during her people's earthly lives.

## Organization

At the head of Qe'âret's priesthood stands the Thâz'Lekhau, the High Priest or High Priestess. This position is among the most powerful in all of Aû'Khelâthu, for the Thâz'Lekhau serves as final arbiter of divine law and justice. Below the Thâz'Lekhau stand the Lem'Nelgir, the ordained priests, who serve as judges, keepers of law, and interpreters of Qe'âret's will. These priests are chosen for their wisdom, their impartiality, and their unwavering commitment to truth. Many are trained in law, rhetoric, and theology for decades before ordination.

The third tier consists of the Wazu acolytes, young men and women who serve the temples in preparation for potential ordination. They study the legal codes, learn to read and write the sacred scripts, and serve as scribes and administrators within the temple complex. A Wazu must demonstrate absolute fidelity to the principles of Qe'âret before advancement is even considered.

The priesthood operates independently of the Gar-Aû's direct control, though the Gar-Aû is expected to support and protect the temples. Historically, those Gar-Aás who have defied the will of Qe'âret's priesthood have faced severe consequences—either their reign was marked by disaster and civil unrest, or in extreme cases, the priesthood has worked to depose them. The Gar-Aû is himself subject to Qe'âret's judgment like any mortal, and if he is found wanting at the scales, no crown or divine title can save his soul from damnation.

The temple complexes of Qe'âret employ hundreds of minor functionaries: record-keepers, scribes, builders, and servants. The priesthood is thus not merely a spiritual institution but a vast administrative body that manages legal proceedings, maintains records, preserves knowledge, and ensures that the laws are applied with impartial consistency throughout the empire.

## Relations

Qe'âret stands in harmony with [[affiliation-ra|Faith of Uqa'â]], for the solar god's power of illumination and clarity serves to reveal truth. Where Uqa'â burns away shadow and deception with his golden light, Qe'âret ensures that truth is universal and inviolable. The two faiths work in close partnership; the Thâz'Lekhau of Uqa'â and the Thâz'Lekhau of Qe'âret often consult on matters of law and divine will.

The relationship between Qe'âret and [[affiliation-iset|Faith of Uznêra]] is one of theological partnership. Uznêra's emphasis on balance—the necessary cooperation of masculine and feminine divine principles in creation—aligns perfectly with Qe'âret's vision of cosmic order. Together, the two goddesses provide the spiritual foundation for Khelâthi society: Uznêra ensures life and renewal through proper balance, while Qe'âret ensures that this balance is maintained through law and truth.

The Faith of Qe'âret maintains a complex relationship with the faith of Wethûr. Both are concerned with order and proper process—Wethûr ensures the spiritual passage of the dead, while Qe'âret ensures their judgment. When a soul reaches Wethûr's realm, it has already been weighed by Qe'âret. Wethûr reveres the authority of Qe'âret but maintains his distinct role in the underworld.

Qe'âret's priesthood stands in direct opposition to any force of chaos or deception. The worship of Azu'âthis, the chaos serpent, is actively suppressed by Qe'âret's followers. Those who spread lies, break oaths, or work to undermine the social order are considered enemies of Qe'âret herself. Similarly, those who abuse their power to commit injustice find themselves subject to the priesthood's righteous wrath.
