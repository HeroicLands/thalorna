## The Itsani Hierarchy

"Every Tq'an'ik in the most remote shrine on the continent is a step on the same stair," the Master of Initiates says at each admission. "The top of it is in Kul'taq'an. None of you will see it, and all of you stand on it."

The [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]] has one priesthood, and the **Itsani hierarchy** is that priesthood seen as an institution: who stands above whom, who chooses whom, and which bodies cut across the branches that serve the Ten. It reaches every city of [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]] and recognizes no border between them. Its arcane practice, the Speaking of the Day, is described under [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]].

## The One Ladder

A priest's rank is the same in every city. A Ch'alishbal of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] is a Ch'alishbal in [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]], and the stair he climbed is the same stair: **Tq'an'ik**, the initiate; **Ch'alishbal**, the ordained priest; **Ch'alish**, head of a branch within a temple complex; **Ch'alish Tq'an**, high priest of a temple complex; and **Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an**, the high priest of a city's solar pyramid, who fixes that city's calendar. Beneath the priests stand the faithful, from the Water-Marked child to the Debt-Bearer, and beneath them the **Blood-Denied**, whom the Severers have cut away.

At the top stands one more rung. The **Q'emul Tq'an**, the Supreme Boundary, is chosen from among the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an by all of them together. His title joins _q'emul_, supreme, with _tq'an_, the edge and boundary that every priestly title is built on. Outside the priesthood he is usually called simply the Ch'alish Tq'an, the supreme high priest, and the cities name him so.

## The Q'emul Tq'an

The Q'emul Tq'an is chosen once in each round of fifty-two years, after the [K'ayik Kul'al]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the Living Flame. Each Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an draws the new fire on his own city's pyramid. Then the **Keeper of the Conclave** summons them all to [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]], and each carries a coal of his city's new fire in a covered jar. They meet in [[place-tzomalchalix|the Sacred Foundation]] (Ts'omal Ch'alish), and the one they choose lays his coal on the precinct's hearth, where the others lay theirs on top of it. He holds the seat for the whole round, and may be chosen again after the next fire.

His authority is the hierarchy's and not a city's. He consecrates every Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an, and a city whose hierarch he has not consecrated has a high priest the other cities do not recognize. He rules on every dispute between one city's priesthood and another's. He names the First Severer, and he consecrates the First of the Star House and the First of the Blood-Workers when their own bodies have chosen them. He does not fix any city's calendar, which is each Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an's work, and he cannot choose the head of the Star House, which elects its own.

He holds no army and takes no tribute beyond the estates of the Sacred Foundation, kept by the **Steward of the Sacred Foundation**. The Ts'enmah of Ish'ilankul's cities treat him as their own, and the other realms resent it. When a Q'emul Tq'an dies within the round, the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an of Kul'taq'an holds the seat until the next fire, and the conclave that follows it is usually asked to confirm him.

## The Branches and the Bodies

The priesthood serves the Ten through its specialist branches: the solar priests, the fire-keepers, the water-priests and serpent-keepers, the astronomers, the agricultural priests, the builder-priests, the death-walkers and the owl keepers, the dream-keepers, the road priests and the water priestesses. A branch serves one god, and its priests answer to their temple's Ch'alish Tq'an.

Across the branches stand three bodies that belong to the hierarchy as a whole, and one that has broken from it. The framework has a body that opens its days, one that carries its workings out of the cities, one that forbids, and one that works outside it.

## Two Accounts of the South

Every city hears the orthodox teaching of the [Fourth Age]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}'s end. The Fourth Age was lost to wind, and its sun, the jaguar that walks the night, was thrown down in the south. [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]], the Land of the Dead Jaguar, is named for it, and the priests teach that the waste is the fallen sun's body, still cooling.

The Wardens of the Sealed Chambers hold a second account, and keep it where they keep the calculation of the age's ending, in serpent skin, in the binding temples of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|Faith of P'uqil Ch'akun]]. In that account the Fourth Age's count was broken. Its priests spent the whole ledger of an age in one working spoken on a closed day, and the south is what that working released. The Wardens do not publish it, and the hierarchy does not teach it to the cities.

The hierarchy keeps both. The first is the one the cities are taught. The second is the one [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] take as the foundation of their law, and the one [[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|the Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil) claim as proof that their own work has been done before.

## Relations

The hierarchy is the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]]'s priesthood, and every branch of the faith stands on its ladder. Its three bodies are its own, and each quarrels with the other two in the ordinary course of its work: the Star House with the Blood-Workers over how far ahead and how far away a day can be found, the Severers with the Blood-Workers over every working a band spends beyond the cords, and the Severers with the Star House over the arithmetic the house will show only in K'ankul.

Its nemesis is the movement that broke from it. The Ts'umaq K'alanil and the fallen owl whom they serve, [[affiliation-pikultzumaq|the Owl of the Serpent]] (Pik'ul Ts'umaq), deny the ladder, the ledger and the tables together, and the hierarchy answers them with the serpent-keepers who hunt them and the Severers who sentence them.
