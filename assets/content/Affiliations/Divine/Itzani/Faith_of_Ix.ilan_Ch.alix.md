**Domain:** Knowledge, Wisdom, Writing, The Calendar, Astronomy

The Faith of Ish'ilan Ch'alish venerates [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]], the goddess of knowledge, wisdom, and the sacred sciences—particularly the astronomical knowledge through which the Ki'ichek understand the cosmos and maintain the celestial calendar. She is also the goddess of writing and the sacred codices of bark-paper. In cosmological myth, Ish'ilan Ch'alish was present at the creation of the world itself, composing the very first codex—a perfect record of creation written in the language of the stars, in planetary conjunction and stellar alignment that would repeat eternally. In this theology, all subsequent human calendars are imperfect copies of her original. The calendar itself is held to be her sacred language—the means through which the goddess speaks to mortals through celestial signs. Every celestial event is read as a word or phrase in her infinite discourse.

## Worship

Worship of Ish'ilan Ch'alish is the least bloody and the most laborious in the Itsani tradition. The inscription and copying of codices is itself an act of worship—the preparation of bark-paper, the mixing of inks, the rendering of glyphs all conducted as ritual action. A scribe does not begin a page without the opening formula, does not correct an error by erasure but by a marked emendation that remains visible, and does not leave a page unfinished at nightfall. The doctrine behind these habits is that a copy is a prayer, and a careless prayer is worse than none.

Observation is worship of the same kind. A night spent at the sighting stones recording a planet's position is understood as listening rather than measuring, and the record is kept whether or not anything is expected to happen. The branch's archives therefore contain centuries of apparently pointless nightly entries, which is precisely what makes them able to predict eclipses when no other institution on the continent can.

Blood is given, since no Itsani god is served without it, but in small and precise quantities and at astronomically fixed moments—a few drops at the instant of a conjunction, offered on the page rather than at an altar. The branch's lay devotees are scribes, surveyors, merchants who keep accounts, and the parents of children showing an early head for figures, who bring them to the observatory steps at the new year in hope of a place.

The **Reckoner of Conjunctions** issues the auspicious and forbidden days to every other branch and, through them, to the city. This is the point at which the goddess's quiet worship becomes the most consequential act in Ki'ichek public life: no seed is planted, no war declared, no marriage consummated and no building begun on a day the Reckoner has not opened. The calculations are published as conclusions, never as workings.

## Organization

Her priesthood maintains sophisticated astronomical observatories at pyramid peaks, containing aligned stone monoliths marking solstice positions, ch'umbal ts'uqal (moon-houses) tracking the lunar cycle, and sighting devices for planetary observation. The senior priest, the Ish'tq'an (Star-Voice), must possess expertise in mathematics and astronomy—it is not enough to be well-born. This meritocratic requirement makes Ish'ilan Ch'alish's temples somewhat more open than other branches, and it is not unusual for a Ch'alishbal astronomer to rise to high rank despite relatively humble birth.

Advancement is by examination, conducted in the open before the assembled priests of the observatory: a candidate is given a set of recorded observations and asked to derive from them a prediction that will be checked against the sky. A failed prediction is recorded alongside the candidate's name and is not expunged. The branch is thoroughly aware that this makes it unlike every other institution in Ki'ichek society, and it guards the practice fiercely, on the grounds that a hierarchy of birth would within two generations be unable to compute an eclipse.

The most sacred codices are kept in ish'ilan ch'umbal (star-houses), consulted only by authorized priests. The **Keeper of the Star-Houses** holds the authorization list and is, in consequence, one of the most petitioned officials in the city; access to the archive is the branch's chief currency in its dealings with the others, and it is spent deliberately.

Unique among the Itsani gods, Ish'ilan Ch'alish is sometimes understood to know the future—to possess the knowledge of when the [Fifth Age]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} will end. This belief invests her priesthood with a mysterious authority that sometimes challenges even the supreme power of the Ch'alish Tq'an.

### Beyond the Temple

Because the branch trains the only people on the continent who can reliably compute, its members are found far outside the observatories. Temple treasuries, royal surveys, the laying-out of the white roads and the assessment of tribute all pass through astronomer-scribes seconded from Ish'ilan Ch'alish's service, and they remain the goddess's priests while doing it. City-states compete to retain them; a Ts'enmah who mistreats a seconded astronomer finds his successor slow to arrive.

## Relations

With [[affiliation-tzenultqan|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]] the branch is indispensable and resented in equal measure. The solstice hours and the fifty-two-year realignment come from the observatories, yet the solar priesthood claims to guard the knowledge of the age's ending which the goddess is held actually to possess. Predictions have been ordered suppressed, and the astronomers have made sure it was known they were overruled.

The serpent-keepers of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|P'uqil Ch'akun]] take the branch's calculation of the final configuration and seal it in chambers the astronomers may not enter. The astronomers regard this as the safekeeping of arithmetic by people who cannot check it; the serpent-keepers regard publication as the surest way to bring on the panic they exist to prevent. Neither position has moved in living memory.

Toward [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|P'iqal Kul'qat]] the relationship is warm, since both branches advance by demonstrated competence and both are staffed largely by people of ordinary birth. Observatories are built to astronomer's specification by builder-priests, and the alignment of a new sighting-stone is a joint rite of the two branches.

The road priests of [[affiliation-xilanixlan|Shilan Ish'lan]] carry the branch's corrections and observations between cities, and the branch in turn surveys their causeways; the white roads run straight for hundreds of miles because astronomers laid the lines. The dream-keepers of [[affiliation-naliktzuqal|Nal'ik Ts'uqal]] are the branch's one real intellectual rival, and the rivalry is amiable: both maintain vast classified archives of recorded phenomena, and they argue endlessly about whether a pattern found in dreams is knowledge of the same kind as a pattern found in the sky.
