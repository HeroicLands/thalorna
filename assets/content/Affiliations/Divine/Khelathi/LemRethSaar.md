## Overview

Lem'Reth'Sa'âr is the sworn priesthood of [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'âr]], god of knowledge and of the written hand, drawn from scribes who pass beyond the guild halls into his direct service. Its priests keep the god's archives and set the disciplines of the written hand that every scribal text is measured against. A document this order refuses to recognize carries no standing anywhere a court or a temple will accept it.

## Character

"Which hand are you writing in?" is the first thing a priest of Lem'Reth'Sa'âr asks a visiting clerk, and the clerk who answers "the people's hand" has already told the priest what the order will and will not take from him. The Khelâthi write in two hands. The sacred hand belongs to the temple and the tomb; the people's hand belongs to the counting-house and the contract. This order keeps the sacred hand, and through its Keeper of the Disciplines it sets the standard that every scribal text is measured against.

The priests are scribes who have passed beyond the guild halls into the god's direct service. Many of them still copy a passage of sacred literature each morning as an act of prayer, since in Reth'Sa'âr's faith the discipline of the hand is part of the worship. A new Lem'Nelgir (servant of the god) swears to the archives and to the hand; the High Priest, the Thâz'Lekhau, stands over the whole order.

If you come to a priest of the order with a document, expect to be told its faults, not its virtues. The order is exacting, and a refusal is final: a text it will not recognize has no standing before any court or temple. Learn that early, before you commission anything.

## Relations

The order works inside a small circle of bodies, and each stands in a different place.

The temple of the same god, [[affiliation-lutrethsaar|Lut-Reth'Sa'âr]], holds the archives the order keeps. The temple is the house with the shelves and the schools; the order is the priesthood that sets the standard of every hand that writes for it. The two share a god and a gate and do not share a function.

The scribal guild halls feed the order. A scribe passes out of a hall and into the god's direct service, and the halls know it.

## Commerce and Currency

The order deals in recognition, and a single refusal from it can cost more than any fee. Say a landholder in the capital has a lease drawn up by an ambitious clerk who has mixed the hands, setting a temple formula into a counting-house text. The landholder carries it to the court, and the court sends it to the order. The Keeper of the Disciplines reads it, finds the mixture, and declines to recognize it. From that hour the lease carries no standing before any court or temple, and the landholder pays the clerk a second time to start again.

The order takes no coin for a ruling. It accepts the god's offerings, the fine papyrus, the rare inks made from precious minerals, and the carefully copied texts that scholars of means commission and give to the temple. A text it accepts can be presented anywhere in the empire; a text it refuses cannot.
