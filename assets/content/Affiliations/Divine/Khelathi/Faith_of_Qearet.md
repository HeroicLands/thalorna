A Wazu of [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]] in a white linen kilt stops a petitioner at the door of the temple archive and looks at the scroll under his arm. "A contract with a man from the next town? Good; that is written, and the scribe will read it to you. But you came in asking whether she will forgive you for what you did to your brother. Nothing in this building can answer that. She keeps that account herself, and she is not kind."

Qe'âret is the great balancing force of the cosmos, and she is a goddess of absolute truth and immutable law, not of compassion or mercy. Before creation chaos ruled; she imposed order on that primordial darkness and set the laws by which the universe runs and by which all creation must abide. To live in harmony with her is to live truthfully, honor your oaths, speak with clarity and balance every action with restraint and wisdom.

## The Weighing

Her theology rests on one overwhelming doctrine, **the Weighing**. The deceased stands before the assembled gods and both of his accounts are opened. The temple account is read as a scribe took it, every entry closed or short, and beside it the gods set the account they kept themselves, which no witness attended and no tablet holds. A soul short on either is taken by **Álgit**, the **Devourer of the Dead**. A soul whose entries close passes onward into the blessed [[place-zulaten|Zulaten]], the realm of eternal peace.

The doctrine shapes every part of [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] civilization. The fear of that judgment and the wish to go in clean before Qe'âret are the foundation of Khelâthi ethics. Because a written entry can be closed late while the unwritten account can only be met, a Khelâthi spends his last years asking rather than paying.

Her order is neutral and absolute, not kind. A just man who lives in poverty can stand as "justified" before her as a rich merchant does, provided both lived truthfully. A warrior who kills in honorable combat has nothing to fear from her judgment, and a king who breaks his oath faces her wrath. That teaching gives the Khelâthi character its two sides: a deep respect for law and truth, and a fatalism about one's place in the cosmic order.

## The Temples of Justice

Her temples are temples of justice as well. A Lem'Nelgir of Qe'âret may sit as judge in a dispute between nobles, between merchants or between common people. The priesthood keeps the legal codes, interprets divine law and sees that contracts and covenants are honored. To swear an oath at her altar is to invoke her direct judgment, and to break it is to invite divine retribution.

The Thâz'Lekhau of Qe'âret in the capital is [[being-thaluraiqelr|Thalura Iqe'Lêru]], a formidable woman whose authority in matters of justice and law rivals the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Aû]]'s. She has deposed corrupt magistrates and overturned unjust decrees, and her word carries the weight of cosmic law.

## Worship

Her temples are vast complexes of white stone and dark granite, their walls inscribed with every law and principle under her dominion. People enter them with solemnity. Her worship has no festival of joy, only ceremonies of affirmation and balance. At the great solar noon, when the sun stands directly overhead, priests and supplicants gather to recite the **Forty-Two Negative Confessions**, a litany of sins they have not committed: "I have not killed. I have not stolen. I have not borne false witness. I have not coveted what belongs to another." Each one reaffirms a commitment to truthfulness and order.

To swear a contract or take a solemn oath, the parties come before her altar. The oathtaker speaks the vow aloud, in full voice and with clear intent, before at least three witnesses. The Lem'Nelgir records the oath in the temple archives, and from then on the oathtaker is bound by mortal and divine law. Breaking such an oath is a cosmic transgression that promises damnation at the Weighing, far worse than a social shame.

Each month the Wazu of her temples perform the **ritual of the Scales**. They inscribe the temple's ledger of disputes and resolutions so that every judgment is recorded in full. The archive is a historical record and also a spiritual proof: it shows Qe'âret that justice was upheld during her people's lives on earth.

## Organization

At the head stands the Thâz'Lekhau, the High Priest or High Priestess, among the most powerful positions in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]] because the holder is the final arbiter of divine law and justice. Below stand the Lem'Nelgir, the ordained priests who serve as judges, keepers of law and interpreters of Qe'âret's will. They are chosen for wisdom, impartiality and unwavering commitment to truth, and many train in law, rhetoric and theology for decades before ordination.

The third tier is the Wazu, young men and women who serve the temples in preparation for possible ordination. They study the legal codes, learn to read and write the sacred scripts, and serve as scribes and administrators within the temple complex. A Wazu must show absolute fidelity to Qe'âret's principles before advancement is even considered.

The priesthood runs independently of the Gar-Aû's direct control, though the Gar-Aû is expected to support and protect the temples. Gar-Aûu who defied the priesthood have met severe consequences: their reigns were marked by disaster and civil unrest, or in extreme cases the priesthood worked to depose them. The Gar-Aû himself is subject to Qe'âret's judgment like any mortal, and if he is found wanting at the scales no crown or divine title saves his soul from damnation.

Her temple complexes employ hundreds of minor functionaries: record-keepers, scribes, builders and servants. The priesthood is a vast administrative body that manages legal proceedings, keeps records, preserves knowledge and sees that laws are applied with impartial consistency throughout the empire.

## Relations

Qe'âret stands in harmony with the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'â]], since the solar god's power of illumination and clarity reveals truth. Where [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] burns away shadow and deception with his golden light, Qe'âret makes truth universal and inviolable. The two faiths work in close partnership, and their Thâz'Lekhau often consult on matters of law and divine will.

With the [[affiliation-uznera|Faith of Uznêra]] the tie is theological partnership. [[lore-uzneradty|Uznêra]]'s emphasis on balance, the necessary cooperation of masculine and feminine divine principles in creation, fits Qe'âret's vision of cosmic order. The two goddesses are the spiritual foundation of Khelâthi society: Uznêra ensures life and renewal through proper balance, and Qe'âret ensures that the balance is kept through law and truth.

The relation with the [[affiliation-wethur|Faith of Wethûr]] is complex. Both care about order and proper process: [[lore-wethurdty|Wethûr]] ensures the spiritual passage of the dead and Qe'âret their judgment. When a soul reaches Wethûr's realm it has already been weighed by Qe'âret, and Wethûr reveres her authority while keeping his own role in the underworld.

Her priesthood stands against every force of chaos and deception. Her followers actively suppress the worship of [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'âthis]], the chaos serpent. Liars, oath-breakers and anyone working to undermine the social order count as her enemies, and those who abuse power to commit injustice meet the priesthood's righteous wrath.
