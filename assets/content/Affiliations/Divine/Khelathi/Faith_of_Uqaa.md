Before dawn a Wazu of the Faith of Uqa'a kneels at the sacred fire in an eastern forecourt and does not look up when a soldier in a dusty cloak stops beside him. "You have come from the border," the acolyte says. "Everyone who comes at this hour has. Stand there, where the light will reach you first, and say nothing until the priest comes with the oil. It does not matter that the killing was justified. The stain is real either way, and he will burn it off you."

The faith teaches that [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]], the **Solar Flame**, is the primordial fire from which all creation springs. He is the sun that rises each morning, burning away darkness and shadow and bringing warmth, light and life to the world, and he is more than the sun in the sky: he is the principle of illumination itself, the divine force that makes truth visible, purifies the corrupted and drives back the primordial chaos that eternally threatens creation. Each day he makes his sacred journey across the heavens. Each night he descends into the underworld to wage war on [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'athis]], the chaos serpent, so that the morning will come and creation will gho on existing.

To worship Uqa'a is to worship life itself and the perpetual triumph of order over chaos and light over darkness, and the demand it makes on a life is hard. His followers are called to live with clarity, purpose and righteous intensity. Just as he illuminates the physical world, a righteous person illuminates the moral and spiritual one: burns falsehood out of their own words first, cleanses their society of corruption and keeps up the vigilance that stops chaos returning. His heat is understood as purifying, never hostile. It burns away weakness, indulgence and spiritual corruption.

## The Temples and the Throne

His temples face east, and their central sanctums have vast openings that let the sun flood the inner chambers through the hours before noon. The architects set the sanctums so that the sun's rays fall directly on the altar at solar noon, a moment of perfect illumination, and worshipers gather then to feel the warmth of the god's presence on their own skin.

Foreign observers describe his priesthood as the second most powerful institution in the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], rivaled only by the priesthood of the [[affiliation-qearet|Faith of Qe'aret]], and the **Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a** is among the most politically influential figures in the empire. More than one [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]] has owed his throne to that priesthood's support. When the solar priesthood withdraws its blessing, kingdoms shake, and when it endorses a new Gar-Au even the most ambitious rival commanders rarely resist. The reason is theological: the Gar-Au is understood as Uqa'a's earthly manifestation, the god's representative in the physical world, and a Gar-Au without the priesthood's support lacks legitimacy.

## Worship

The greatest festival falls on the summer solstice, when the sun stands highest and its rays are most intense. All work stops. People gather at temples and public spaces to greet the rising sun and celebrate the triumph of light. Priests lead elaborate ceremonies in which the Gar-Au himself, or in some cases the Thaz'Lekhau as his proxy, offers the first grains of the new harvest to Uqa'a. The ceremony gives thanks for the god's protection through the past year and asks for his blessing through the next.

Daily worship is a salute to the sun at its rising and its setting. Many [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] start the day facing east with a simple prayer of thanks and commitment:

> Uqa'a, illuminator of all things, as you bring light to the world, so shall I bring light to my duties. As you burn away shadow, so shall I burn away falsehood from my words and actions.

In the temples the priests perform fuller rituals, lighting sacred fires at dawn and pouring libations at sunset.

One of the most important rituals is the purification ceremony. Anyone who has sinned or been exposed to spiritually corrupting influences may come to a temple of Uqa'a to be ritually cleansed. The supplicant stands in direct sunlight while a priest anoints them with sacred oils and recites prayers of purification, and the belief is that the god's light burns the corruption away and leaves them spiritually renewed. The ceremony matters most to warriors who have killed in combat. The killing may have been justified, but it leaves a spiritual stain that must be cleansed before the warrior can take a full part in temple life.

## Organization

The priesthood is hierarchical, with the Thaz'Lekhau at its apex holding great political and spiritual authority. The choice of a new Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a has historically been decided by the sitting Gar-Au in consultation with the outgoing Thaz'Lekhau, though the priesthood has occasionally asserted the right to choose, and those contests have on rare occasions led to open conflict between throne and temple.

Below stand many Lem'Nelgir, the ordained priests who serve as temple administrators, teachers and ritual leaders. The priesthood trains its priests at length in theology, astronomy and natural philosophy. Young priests study the sun's movement through the heavens and learn to predict its paths with precision, knowledge that is practical, for maintaining the temple's solar observatories, and spiritual, for understanding the god's will as expressed in the sky.

The Wazu (acolytes) serve apprenticeships that may last a decade or more. They learn to tend the sacred fires, perform basic rituals and understand the theological foundations of the faith. The most promising are trained in its secret mysteries: hidden knowledge about the god's nightly battle against chaos and the hidden names of power through which the priesthood communes with him.

The priesthood also employs countless minor functionaries: temple servants, builders, astronomers and record-keepers. The temples serve as centers of astronomical learning as well as worship. The priests keep observatories from which they track the sun, moon and stars, refining their understanding of the cosmos and making practical predictions about the seasonal changes that affect agriculture and trade.

## Relations

The faith's closest alliance is with the Faith of Qe'aret. In their theology, truth and light are complementary: where Uqa'a burns away shadow and deception, [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'aret]] ensures that truth is immutable and justice upheld. The two Thaz'Lekhau confer regularly on matters of theological and imperial importance, and when the priesthoods of other gods quarrel, the two faiths often act together to keep order and see that divine law is respected.

The faith stands in protective alliance with the [[affiliation-uznera|Faith of Uzn'era]]. In their theology, [[lore-uzneradty|Uzn'era]]'s principle of renewal through balance complements Uqa'a's power to cleanse and purify. His heat ripens the crops her followers nurture, and her regenerative power ensures the world survives the sometimes harsh intensity of his light. The two faiths celebrate the alliance at the spring renewal festivals, when life returns to the land and his warmth turns from a trial into a blessing.

The priesthood of Uqa'a wages an unrelenting battle against the forces of chaos and above all against the worship of Azu'athis, an existential struggle that goes well beyond theological disagreement. It teaches that each night Uqa'a descends to fight the chaos serpent directly, and understands itself as his earthly army in that cosmic war. It works actively to suppress chaos cults, to purify those spiritually contaminated by exposure to chaotic forces and to keep up the vigilance that prevents chaos returning.

Unlike the **Aureldian** traditions divided by [[lore-ustaranschism|The Ústaran Schism]], the **Faith of Uqa'a** has no schism and no division into light and dark sects. The solar priesthood is unified in its theology and purpose, and the god's nature is held to be singular: fire as purification and illumination, never as destruction or domination. Some foreign observers find the unity suspicious and suggest hidden mysteries or a latent heresy. The Khelathi insist that the unity is one of Uqa'a's greatest gifts to Au'Khelathu: a priesthood undivided, a faith unambiguous and a purpose clear and absolute.
