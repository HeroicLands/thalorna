Nehle'ât is the Khelâthi name for [[lore-goddreams|The God of Dreams]], a deity far older than any single civilization. Known to the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] since time immemorial and worshipped by countless peoples across the lands of Thalorna, this ancient god manifests uniquely in the Khelâthi faith as a protective guardian of sleep and the visions it brings. While the Asguardian peoples call this deity Baldr and the Aurèldían peoples know them as Aethería, the Khelâthi understand the god through Nehle'ât—not as a distant celestial force, but as an intimate companion who walks through the night with each sleeping mortal.

In Aû'Khelâthu, where the brilliant southern sun burns long across the river valley and the great river's floods reshape the land in cycles both predictable and mysterious, sleep is more than mere rest. It is a sacred passage into the otherworld, a time when mortals commune with the divine and the deceased, when prophecy flows like water and truth reveals itself unburdened by the constraints of daylight. Nehle'ât stands as guardian at the threshold between these worlds, shielding the vulnerable sleeper from the predations of hostile spirits and darker forces that stalk the night. The crescent moon that crowns Nehle'ât's symbol represents not only the night itself but also the cyclical nature of rest—the eternal rhythm by which mortals renew themselves.

The protective eye at the center of Nehle'ât's symbol holds particular significance in Khelâthi theology. This is the All-Seeing Eye, eternally vigilant, never closing even as mortals rest. It watches over dreams for signs of divine communication, distinguishing true visions sent by the gods from the meaningless phantasms conjured by an unsettled mind. In this role, Nehle'ât functions as intermediary between the divine realm and the mortal consciousness, filtering and interpreting the gods' messages so that mortals might receive them without their minds being shattered by unmediated revelation.

The veneration of Nehle'ât cuts across all social strata in Aû'Khelâthu. The poorest farmer sleeps beneath the stars in open fields, vulnerable to both physical dangers and spiritual threats—Nehle'ât protects them. The wealthy merchant rests in a secure chamber surrounded by servants and guards—Nehle'ât still watches over their dreams, perhaps delivering visions that guide their trade. The Gar-Aû himself, the divine anchor of the entire kingdom, relies upon Nehle'ât to bring true prophecy in sleep, allowing them to rule with divine wisdom. In this way, Nehle'ât is both intimately personal and cosmically important, making this deity universally beloved across the realm.

Among the hill-nomads of the western steppe and the herder-peoples of the borderlands, Nehle'ât's role expands considerably. Travelers who rest under the vast canopy of stars invoke Nehle'ât not merely for undisturbed sleep but for protection against the genuine perils of the night—predators both bestial and human, treacherous terrain invisible in darkness, the cold that can kill an unwary traveler. Nehle'ât's protective eye becomes a literal guardian, warning of danger before it strikes. Many nomadic songs depict Nehle'ât as a celestial companion, riding beside them on their journeys, ensuring that their rest in the wilderness brings restoration rather than death.

In the cities and temples of Aû'Khelâthu, Nehle'ât is particularly honored by those whose vocations depend upon interpreting dreams: the temple seers who divine the will of the gods through prophetic sleep, the physicians who use dream-knowledge to heal their patients, the astrologers who read the night sky for omens. These learned practitioners maintain that while Uqa'â illuminates the waking world with divine fire, it is Nehle'ât who illuminates the inner world of consciousness, making them equals in cosmic significance—one lighting the external realm, the other lighting the internal.

## Worship

Worship of Nehle'ât centers on the evening hours, particularly the transition from waking to sleep. In temples dedicated to the deity, priests and supplicants perform ritual preparations for sleep, chanting invocations that ask Nehle'ât to stand guard throughout the night. The most common prayer is whispered just before closing one's eyes: "Nehle'ât, Guardian of the Threshold, keep vigil while I rest. Guide my visions with your All-Seeing Eye. Grant me dreams of truth and protection from the shadows of night."

The most sacred night in the Khelâthi calendar is the Festival of Deep Sleep, celebrated during the height of the inundation season when the igelu's flooding renders much of the land impassable and work ceases. For three days and nights, the land enters a state of rest-like suspension, mirroring the cosmic sleep from which creation itself emerged. During this festival, temples of Nehle'ât remain open throughout the day and night, offering safe spaces for pilgrims to sleep under the deity's direct protection. Priests maintain vigils around the sleeping faithful, ready to interpret any dreams that come with particular clarity or power. These interpreted dreams are recorded, studied, and sometimes proclaimed as divine guidance for the coming year.

Beyond the temples, every home keeps a small shrine to Nehle'ât—often a simple carved crescent moon or a small amulet placed near the sleeping quarters. Before sleep, families offer Nehle'ât small tokens: a cup of milk or water, a pinch of incense, a whispered prayer. Parents teach their children to invoke Nehle'ât's protection before closing their eyes, and elders share the wisdom that a bad dream, when recognized as such by Nehle'ât's All-Seeing Eye, becomes a teaching rather than a curse. This pervasive domestic practice makes Nehle'ât perhaps the most frequently invoked deity in daily Khelâthi life, more so even than the great gods honored in stone temples.

## Organization

The priesthood of Nehle'ât maintains a unique structure within the broader Khelâthi religious hierarchy. While all priests answer ultimately to the Gar-Aû as high priest of all the gods, the Thâz'Lekhau (High Priest) of Nehle'ât typically oversees a smaller, more specialized organization than the clergy of gods like Wethûr or Qe'âret. This reflects not lesser importance but rather Nehle'ât's intimate, individualized nature—this is a god encountered in solitude, in the privacy of sleep, and so the priesthood functions less as administrators of grand temple complexes and more as guides and interpreters.

The Lem'Nelgir (ordained priests) of Nehle'ât undergo extensive training in the interpretation of dreams, studying the symbolic language through which the gods communicate with mortals. They learn to distinguish true visions from anxiety, prophecy from mere fantasy, divine warning from the mind's random wanderings. This specialized knowledge cannot be taught to everyone; it requires both natural aptitude and years of disciplined study. The Wazu (acolytes) serve these priests by maintaining the shrines, preparing the incense and offerings, and assisting in the nightlong vigils during festivals.

Notably, many seers, oracles, and dream-interpreters who are not formally ordained by temples still operate within the broader faith of Nehle'ât, their authority deriving from genuine visions and a demonstrated ability to guide others toward truth through dream-knowledge. The formal priesthood tolerates these independent practitioners, as Nehle'ât is understood to speak directly to mortals regardless of priestly intermediaries. However, those who abuse this freedom—who use dream-interpretation as a cover for fraud or manipulation—are swiftly denounced by the legitimate clergy.

## Relations

Nehle'ât stands in complementary relationship with several other deities in the Khelâthi Pantheon. Most prominently, Nehle'ât works in concert with [[affiliation-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'âr]], the god of wisdom and divine knowledge. While Reth'Sa'âr recording and preserving truth in waking hours, Nehle'ât delivers truth through visions in sleep. Where Reth'Sa'âr's sacred ibis-quill writes upon golden scrolls, Nehle'ât's All-Seeing Eye reads the symbols written by the gods in the night sky and mortal consciousness alike. Many theologians hold that Reth'Sa'âr and Nehle'ât are two aspects of a single cosmic principle: the preservation and communication of divine truth.

The relationship between Nehle'ât and Wethûr, the Lord of Silent Passage, is more subtle but profoundly important. Both deities preside over transitions: Wethûr over the ultimate transition from life to death, and Nehle'ât over the nightly transition from waking to sleep. In Khelâthi theology, sleep is sometimes called "the lesser death," and the dreams experienced in sleep are understood as rehearsals for the greater journey that awaits after physical death. Thus, those who learn to navigate their dreams with awareness and acceptance under Nehle'ât's protection are better prepared for their final passage under Wethûr's care. Some advanced priests maintain that the two deities may actually guide the same soul at different thresholds.

Nehle'ât also maintains a protective relationship with Uznêra, the goddess of healing and renewal. While Uznêra restores the body through direct intervention and healing touch, Nehle'ât enables the body's own restoration through the restorative power of undisturbed, protected sleep. Pilgrims who come to temples of Uznêra seeking healing often spend nights in sanctuaries dedicated to Nehle'ât, believing that the combination of physical healing and protected sleep works synergistically to restore wholeness.
