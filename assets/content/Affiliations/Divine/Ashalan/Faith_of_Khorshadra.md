Khóršádrä is the goddess of knowledge, eternal light, and the clarity that reveals truth hidden beneath layers of deception. She is depicted as a radiant female figure crowned with a sunburst, holding aloft a lamp or torch that illuminates even the deepest shadows. Her eyes are said to perceive all things without illusion—she sees through darkness and falsehood as easily as daylight pierces fog. In Āsháian theology, she embodies the principle that knowledge is the foundation of all virtue, that a person who understands truth cannot help but align with Āsha, while one who operates in ignorance cannot help but stumble into Druj. She is revered by scholars, astronomers, physicians, and all those who pursue understanding as a form of spiritual practice. Where [[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]] is the fire that transforms and purifies through heat, Khóršádrä is the light that shows things as they truly are—the two are complementary aspects of divine revelation.

The cult of Khóršádrä teaches that ignorance is the root of all evil. The person who lies deliberately chooses Druj, but the person who lies unwittingly because they are ignorant is trapped in deception against their will. The person who commits cruelty with understanding of what they do is wicked, but the person who causes harm through ignorance is equally damnable, for they have failed to seek the knowledge that would have guided them toward Āsha. This creates a theological imperative toward learning—it is a sacred duty to pursue knowledge, to educate oneself and others, to shine the light of understanding into all the dark corners of the world. The great libraries and academies of [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Amradad]] are understood as temples to Khóršádrä, sacred spaces where the eternal light of wisdom burns undiminished, where knowledge accumulated by generations of scholars is preserved and transmitted to those who come after.

Among the nomadic peoples of the desert and steppe, Khóršádrä holds a special place as the goddess of navigation and the reading of omens. The stars are her primary domain—she is the one who arranged them in their courses and continues to speak through their movements to those wise enough to listen. A skilled stargazer or desert navigator is understood to be a priest of Khóršádrä, reading the heavens as if reading a sacred text, interpreting the positions of planets and stars as divine messages about the future. In the great oases and caravan cities, Khóršádrä's temples maintain sophisticated astronomical observatories where precise records of celestial movements are kept. These records are not mere scientific curiosity—they are understood as the actual words of the goddess, written in the sky for those with eyes to see and minds to understand. A merchant planning a journey will consult with a priest of Khóršádrä to learn what the stars foretell about the success of the expedition; a leader planning military campaigns will seek the goddess's guidance through astrological interpretation; a young couple seeking marriage will time their wedding ceremony according to favorable astronomical configurations.

## Worship

The worship of Khóršádrä is centered on the pursuit and sharing of knowledge. The daily fire-ritual, while honoring [[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]] and the other deities, includes a specific invocation to Khóršádrä asking for clarity of mind and truth-seeing throughout the day: "O Khóršádrä, bearer of eternal light, grant me eyes to see truth clearly, grant me mind to understand wisdom, grant me courage to speak truth and to live according to what I understand." Scholars and students will make special morning prayers to the goddess before beginning their studies, invoking her blessing on their work. Physicians will invoke her before treating patients, asking that she guide their hands and grant them the knowledge to heal. Judges and arbiters, before hearing disputes, will make offerings to Khóršádrä requesting clarity and wisdom to perceive the truth of the matter and render just judgment.

The major festival honoring Khóršádrä occurs at the spring equinox, coinciding with and integrating into the broader Festival of New Order that honors [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]]. However, the specific celebrations dedicated to Khóršádrä focus on light itself. In the great cities, enormous bonfires are lit atop the highest buildings and hills, creating a landscape of light visible for miles. These fires burn continuously throughout the night of the equinox, symbolizing the eternal vigilance of wisdom, the flame of understanding that never dims. In temples, scholars present their works and discoveries to assembled audiences—new treatises on astronomy, mathematics, medicine, philosophy, and natural history are read aloud, debated, and celebrated. The priesthood of Khóršádrä uses these festivals as occasions to advance human knowledge, to share discoveries with the widest possible audience, to inspire young people to pursue learning as a sacred path.

A second, smaller festival celebrates the heliacal rising of major stars—the moments when particular bright stars first become visible in the dawn sky after a period of invisibility. These astronomically significant moments are understood as Khóršádrä revealing herself and reasserting her presence in the heavens. The exact dates vary by latitude and by which star is being observed, but in all Āsháian lands, these moments are marked by gatherings of astronomers, scholars, and common people who come to witness the star's first appearance. Priests conduct ceremonies honoring the goddess, and astronomical observations are recorded in temple archives for comparison with observations from previous years.

Individual worship of Khóršádrä involves devotion to learning and truthfulness. A merchant might maintain a small shrine to the goddess in their counting-house, making daily offerings of incense or oil-lamp fuel while seeking her guidance on business decisions. A scholar will spend evening hours in study and meditation, deliberately seeking to increase their understanding of a subject as a form of prayer to the goddess. Some devotees practice a discipline of daily truth-telling—a commitment to avoid any deception in speech, no matter how small or convenient, as a way of aligning themselves with Khóršádrä's principle and making their own lives an offering to her. A group of philosophers in [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Amradad]] is known to gather weekly to debate fundamental questions—not to reach definitive answers (which may be impossible), but to expose themselves and each other to new perspectives, to refine their understanding, to push thinking to sharper edges. They consider these philosophical debates a form of worship more profound than any ritual.

## Organization

The priesthood of Khóršádrä is perhaps the most intellectually demanding of all Āsháian religious orders. Unlike the warrior-priests of [[affiliation-bahramis|Báhrámiš]] or the flame-keepers of [[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]], the priests of Khóršádrä must combine spiritual devotion with genuine scholarly attainment. The training is rigorous and extended, often lasting a decade or more.

The **Mōbad** of Khóršádrä's temples is invariably a scholar of the highest order—someone who has made original contributions to knowledge, who has published works recognized across multiple kingdoms, whose understanding is so deep and sophisticated that they can guide others through complex material. In [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Amradad]], the Mōbad of Khóršádrä's Great Temple serves as the chief astronomer and chief advisor on scholarly matters to the Sultan. The current Mōbad, Khazafir the Stargazer, has written definitive treatises on planetary motion, on the classification of herbs and minerals, and on the interpretation of ancient scripts. His recommendations regarding the construction of a new astronomical observatory influenced the Sultan's decision to fund the massive Temple of the Eternal Star, now the largest such observatory in all Ankaris.

The **Hērbad** of Khóršádrä are scholars and teachers who maintain temples and academies dedicated to learning. Many are specialists in particular fields—mathematics, medicine, astronomy, history, law, engineering. The largest temples function essentially as universities, with multiple Hērbads each teaching their specialization, with libraries containing thousands of volumes, with laboratories where medical and alchemical research is conducted. These temples are centers of intellectual life, places where ambitious young people come to study, where scholars migrate to discuss their work with peers, where the accumulated knowledge of generations is preserved and advanced. The Hērbads maintain high standards for accuracy and methodology—sloppy thinking is not tolerated, claims require supporting evidence, theories must be coherent and testable.

The **Āthravān** of Khóršádrä are students who have shown aptitude for learning but have not yet achieved the expertise required for ordination as Hērbad. The training period is typically seven to twelve years, depending on the student's progress and chosen specialization. Āthravān study under senior Hērbads, copying texts, conducting experiments, gradually advancing through increasingly sophisticated material. Many famous scholars are products of this system—individuals who spent their formative years under the guidance of a master, who absorbed not merely factual knowledge but the deeper methodology of rigorous thinking.

Notably, the priesthood of Khóršádrä is highly meritocratic. Advancement is based strictly on demonstrated knowledge and intellectual ability—a brilliant student from a poor family will advance as rapidly as the child of a noble, a foreign-born scholar who master the required knowledge is accepted as fully as a native-born candidate. This meritocratic system creates both strengths and tensions within the organization. It ensures the priesthood maintains high standards and attracts genuinely talented individuals. However, it also creates resentment among those with wealth or political power who expect their children to advance regardless of ability.

## Relations

Khóršádrä's relationship with [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] is one of profound spiritual partnership. If Āhúrdáén represents abstract cosmic order, Khóršádrä is the means by which that order becomes knowable to finite minds. She is sometimes described as Āhúrdáén's voice speaking to mortals, the embodiment of the principle that cosmic order is rational and can be understood through reason and observation. The two deities are frequently invoked together in intellectual and philosophical contexts—a scholar beginning a major work of research might make offerings to both, asking Āhúrdáén to guide the work and Khóršádrä to grant the insight and clarity necessary to carry it to successful completion.

Her relationship with [[affiliation-ataros|Ātáröš]] is complementary. If Ātáröš is the heat that transforms, Khóršádrä is the light that reveals. Together they represent the complete revelation of divine truth—through fire that purifies and light that illuminates. Some temples maintain side-by-side altars to both deities, and prayers often invoke them as a pair. The philosophical schools debate whether fire and light are ultimately manifestations of a single divine principle perceived in different ways, or whether they are genuinely distinct aspects of the divine.

The relationship with [[affiliation-bahramis|Báhrámiš]] is one of mutual respect and complementarity. Báhrámiš represents strength and the will to act; Khóršádrä represents wisdom and the knowledge of what should be done. A warrior trained only in combat without wisdom becomes a brute; a thinker without the courage to act on their understanding is merely ineffectual. The priesthoods cooperate in the training of leaders and commanders—military academies often include chaplains of both Báhrámiš and Khóršádrä, with the warrior-priests teaching martial skills and the scholar-priests teaching strategic thinking, history, and political knowledge.

The opposition with [[affiliation-angaros|Ángáróš]] is expressed through the cosmic struggle between truth and deception. Ángáróš embodies Druj—falsehood, illusion, corruption. Khóršádrä embodies Āsha—truth, clarity, understanding. Every act of genuine learning, every moment of clarity achieved through study and meditation, is understood as a small victory against chaos, a point of light pushed back into darkness. The priesthood teaches that deception requires ignorance to perpetuate itself—once truth is known and understood, deception cannot maintain its hold. Thus the pursuit of knowledge is quite literally a war against Ángáróš and the forces of chaos.

With [[affiliation-zarvanya|Zárványä]] (goddess of dreams), Khóršádrä maintains an interesting relationship. Dreams can be a path to truth—many scholars throughout history have reported sudden insights arriving in dreams, problems resolving themselves in sleep. Yet dreams can also deceive—visions that seem vivid and meaningful can prove to be mere fantasy. The theological teaching is that Khóršádrä and Zárványä work in partnership; the visions granted by the dream-goddess must be tested and verified through the rational methods taught by Khóršádrä's priesthood. A scholar might receive inspiration from a dream, but the work of turning that inspiration into genuine knowledge remains.

With the other deities, Khóršádrä maintains relationships of general respect and cooperation. [[affiliation-zurathra|Zúráthrä]] teaches about fertility and growth; Khóršádrä's scholars study the principles of agriculture and animal-husbandry to increase understanding of these natural processes. [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]] brings prosperity; Khóršádrä's priesthood studies economics and the principles of successful trade. [[affiliation-mithara|Míthárä]] sustains creation; Khóršádrä's scholars study the fundamental nature of reality itself, seeking to understand the creative principles through which existence maintains itself. Even [[affiliation-azravan|Ázráván]] (death) and [[affiliation-morvahna|Mórváhñä]] (decay) are subjects of serious scholarly investigation—the priesthood of Khóršádrä maintains the most advanced medical knowledge of any civilization, studying the processes of disease and aging in hopes of ameliorating their effects, understanding that true wisdom includes understanding even those aspects of existence that mortals would prefer to deny.
