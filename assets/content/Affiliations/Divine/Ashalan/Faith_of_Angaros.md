The Faith of Angaros follows [[lore-angarosdty|Angaros]] as the great adversary, the god of chaos, destruction, deception, and entropy. In its teaching, he is the living principle of [Druj]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}—falsehood, corruption, and the slow dissolution of all imposed order. Where [[lore-ahurdaendty|Ahurdaen]] creates and sustains, Angaros tears down and unmakes. Where [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]] purifies through flame, Angaros corrupts through poison and shadow. Where [[lore-khorsadradty|Khórsadra]] reveals truth through light, Angaros obscures it through lies and deception. He is not a minor or regional deity—he is the supreme cosmic force opposing all righteousness, order, and truth. The entire [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]] exists primarily to wage war against him.

The worship of Angaros is absolutely forbidden throughout all Asháian-dominated lands. No temples are built in his name. No priests wear his colors openly. No fires are kindled in his honor. To acknowledge Angaros openly, to speak his name aloud without protective prayer, to so much as suggest sympathy with his cause is to invite immediate execution in civilized lands and brutal punishment even among the most lawless desert tribes. Yet his worship persists—driven underground, hidden in secret cells and shadow-cults, whispered about in fearful tones. The very prohibition against his worship is paradoxically his greatest strength, for it forces his followers into secrecy, making them impossible to root out entirely and lending them an air of forbidden power that attracts certain temperaments of mind: those who despise the comfortable order of civilization, those who hunger for power regardless of its cost, those who see in Angaros's vision of dissolution a terrible and seductive liberation.

The theology of Angaros begins with a single, revolutionary premise: creation is a cage. The cosmos that Ahurdaen constructed is beautiful to the enslaved, perhaps, but it is slavery nonetheless. The cosmic order that the righteous gods sustain is oppressive, limiting, denying. Angaros teaches that true freedom lies only in the dissolution of all imposed structure, all morality, all authority. He promises liberation through destruction—if all things are unmade, if the world returns to the primordial chaos that preceded creation, then all beings are freed from the bonds of law, consequence, and meaning. To the desperate and the ambitious, to the powerless and the power-hungry, to those who have been wronged by a system they cannot change, Angaros offers a terrible promise: that by serving chaos, they can overturn the order that oppresses them.

The followers of Angaros do not worship him out of love, as one might love [[lore-zarvanyadty|Zárvanya]] or [[lore-mitharadty|Míthara]]. Rather, they serve him out of cold recognition of cosmic truth as they understand it. They believe that [Asha]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}—the principle of order—is an illusion, a tyranny masquerading as truth. They see the structure of society, law, morality, and consequence as chains. And they see in Angaros the power to break those chains. His followers work toward the slow corruption of all things, the introduction of entropy wherever order exists, the sowing of doubt and deception wherever truth is claimed. They are arsonists, assassins, poisoners, and seducers—not because they are inherently evil, but because they serve a god who believes that all of civilization's structures, all of its moral frameworks, deserve to be burned.

## Worship

The secret cults of Angaros operate with the understanding that discovery means death. Unlike the open priesthoods of other deities, who conduct their rituals in great fire-temples visible to all, the followers of Angaros meet in hidden chambers—sealed caves in the desert, cellars beneath seemingly innocent buildings, hidden chambers within apparently orthodox fire-temples where heretical priests maintain a secret altar. Because public worship is impossible, the rituals of Angaros take perverse, inverted forms. Where other Ashaian worship begins with the kindling of fire, Ángárós-worship involves the extinguishing of flames—the supplicant enters a dark chamber and, by will and meditation, mentally unmakes the sacred fire that all Ashaian culture takes to be central.

The followers of Angaros perform rituals of corruption and inversion. They may recite the righteous prayers of the [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]] backward, corrupting them into heretical meanings. They anoint themselves with poisons and substances that induce hallucination and delirium, seeking to perceive the "truth" hidden beneath the illusions of ordered reality. They practice ritual lying, deliberately and methodically cultivating the ability to speak falsehood with such conviction that even they can no longer distinguish lie from truth. In the most extreme cults, they engage in acts of ritual destruction—burning crops, poisoning wells, murdering the innocent—not from personal hatred but from theological conviction: each act of chaos and destruction is a prayer to Angaros, a contribution to the great work of unmaking.

The communion of Ángárós-followers involves the consumption of poisoned wine or fermented substances derived from corruption and decay. Unlike the clear wine of the prosperity-feasts of the [[affiliation-aravesta|Faith of Aravesta]], the drink of Angaros is bitter, toxic, and induces visions of dissolution. The followers believe they are imbibing the essence of chaos itself, preparing themselves to survive the day when Angaros finally defeats Ahurdaen and the world dissolves back into formless void. They emerge from these dark rituals with dilated pupils, trembling hands, and a fierce certainty that they are serving a truth that is too terrible for the comfortable masses to comprehend.

In some settlements where Ángárós-cults have taken root, a more subtle form of worship emerges: the infiltration of orthodox fire-temples. A seemingly pious Roqeshekh or Delkoshekh works their way up through the priesthood of another deity, all while secretly serving Angaros. From within the priesthood, they can corrupt doctrine, teach subtle heresies, introduce confusion and doubt into the minds of the faithful. A fire that burns with strange colors, an oracle whose interpretations are consistently misaligned with the truth, a Shekhul who begins to issue decrees that undermine the social order—these are the subtle victories of Angaros worship, far more dangerous than open rebellion.

## Organization

The priesthood of Angaros is structurally the inverse of legitimate Ashaian cults. Where the faith-cults of other deities emphasize clarity, truth, and the preservation of knowledge through careful documentation and transmission, Angaros's priests deliberately obscure and fragment their teachings. There is no single canonical scripture, no universally recognized hierarchy, no agreed-upon liturgy. Instead, each cell of worshippers develops its own variations, its own heresies, its own interpretations. This radical decentralization makes the cult impossible to eradicate—there is no center to strike, no high priest whose death would cripple the movement, no temple whose destruction would suppress the faith.

The closest thing to leadership among Ángárós-followers are the Devashers ("Corruptors" or "Truth-Destroyers"), individuals who demonstrate particular talent at spreading chaos and confusion. A Devasher is not appointed but recognized through reputation: other cultists know their name, know they have accomplished significant works of corruption, and defer to their judgment. A Devasher might spend years infiltrating a legitimate priesthood, or might be known for particularly lethal poisons or destructive plots. But their authority is contingent and temporary—a Devasher who fails or becomes known to the orthodoxy is abandoned without hesitation. Loyalty to person is antithetical to service to Angaros; the only loyalty demanded is to the principle of chaos itself.

Among the cultists, initiates are called Tasfans ("Those-Dwelling-in-Shadow"). A Tasfan undergoes training in the art of deception, studying how to identify the vulnerable points in others' belief-systems and how to introduce seeds of doubt that will grow into corrosive cynicism. They are taught the arts of assassination and poisoning—not merely for their direct destructive value, but because the act of killing the righteous and seeing their gods fail to protect them is itself a form of theological argument. They practice ritualized lying and become adept at the manipulation of social currents.

Those who have proven themselves through successful corruption-work may be recognized as Azdhans ("Blade-Bearers")—the warrior-priests of Angaros. These are individuals who have moved beyond theoretical corruption to practical destruction on a grand scale. They lead assassination plots against priests and nobles, they organize rebellions against established rule, they commit acts of sabotage intended to destabilize entire regions. An Azdhan is understood to be a weapon wielded by Angaros, a human instrument of cosmic entropy.

The most feared Ángárós-worshippers are those rare individuals who have become Devajahns ("Serpents" or "Soul-Corrupted")—those who have practiced chaos-worship for so long and so intensely that they are believed to have become partially merged with Angaros himself. These are figures whispered about in fearful tones: sorcerers said to command poison, assassins who leave no trace, prophets of doom whose words inevitably come true. Whether such individuals truly exist or are merely legends cultivated by the cult to inspire terror is uncertain, but their supposed existence is enough to make the cult terrifying to even the most confident authorities.

## Relations

In this theology, Angaros exists in eternal, irreconcilable war with every other deity in the Ashaian pantheon. His opposition to Ahurdaen is the central theological and cosmic struggle. Ahurdaen represents the principle of order that Angaros is committed to destroying. Everything that Ahurdaen creates, Angaros seeks to unmake. Every law Ahurdaen establishes, Angaros encourages his followers to break. Every civilization Ahurdaen's principles support, Angaros works to tear down.

Ataros (The Sacred Flame) is perhaps Angaros's most direct opponent. Fire purifies, burns away corruption, destroys entropy. Every sacred flame is a defiance of Angaros's influence. The priesthoods of the [[affiliation-ataros|Faith of Ataros]] and the Faith of Angaros are in constant, direct conflict: wherever a fire-temple is established, the Ángárós-cults move to extinguish it; wherever an Ángárós-cell operates, the flame-priesthood works to discover and destroy it.

Angaros also stands opposed to Zárvanya, whose clear visions of truth are antithetical to his principle of deception. He opposes Khórsadra, whose knowledge and revelation are weapons against ignorance and the chaos that feeds in darkness. He opposes [[lore-azravandty|Azravan]], whose principle of cosmic consequence and the weighing of the soul ensures that actions have meaning—a meaning Angaros seeks to dissolve. And he even opposes the gentler deities: he opposes [[lore-zurathradty|Zúrathra]]'s cultivation of fertility and life, [[lore-aravestadty|Aravesta]]'s blessing of prosperity, and Míthara's sustaining of creation itself.

However, [[lore-morvahnadty|Mórvahna]] (The Silent Keeper) occupies a more ambiguous position. Mórvahna governs natural decay and the cycles of dissolution that are necessary for renewal. While the priesthoods of Angaros and Mórvahna are not allied, neither are they in open war. The Ángárós-cultists sometimes argue that Mórvahna's process of decay is a preview of the ultimate dissolution that Angaros promises—the return of all things to primordial chaos. Some scholars have noted with alarm that certain Ángárós-cults have begun to target priests of Mórvahna with unusual frequency, suggesting that the heretical worshippers may be attempting to corrupt the doctrine of natural decay into a theology of total annihilation. If true, such an alliance—even unspoken—would represent a new and terrifying development in the cosmic struggle between Asha and Druj.

The fundamental conviction that unites all the cults opposed to Angaros is that they recognize his existence as the existential threat. In this regard, Angaros is the villain against whom all the righteous deities define themselves. To be a follower of any legitimate Ashaian faith is, ultimately, to be a soldier in the war against chaos, entropy, and the corrosive nihilism that Angaros represents. The Ashaian world is not a place of comfortable piety—it is a battlefield where the cosmic struggle between Asha and Druj is fought daily through human hands.
