## Nkaru'thar: The Eternal Flame

- **Region:** [[affiliation-okharis|Kingdom of Okháris]]
- **Nature:** Monotheistic with tripartite aspects and animistic sub-traditions
- **Symbol:** Three interlocking flames (one white, one gold, one red)

The people of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]] worship **Nkaru'thar** – the Eternal Flame – as the singular divine force from which all creation, destruction, and renewal flows. Unlike the polytheistic pantheons of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] or the celestial bureaucracy of [[affiliation-tngvkvnlei|Tānvür]], the Okháric faith centers on a single godhead whose power manifests in three aspects, each embodied by one of the kingdom's three great city-states and their pyramidal temple complexes.

Nkaru'thar is not depicted in human or animal form. The Eternal Flame is understood as a living force – the fire that burned before the world existed, that kindled the first life, and that will one day consume all things so they may be reborn. Every hearth fire, every forge flame, every bolt of lightning is a fragment of Nkaru'thar's presence in the mortal world.

### The Three Aspects

The theology of Nkaru'thar holds that the Eternal Flame's power divides into three sacred aspects, each tended by a city-state and its priesthood:

**Zarhánis – The Spirit Flame** (white fire) The flame of faith, wisdom, and prophecy. Zarhánis is the spiritual capital of Okháris, home to the largest temple complex, believed to be the birthplace of the kingdom's religion. Its great pyramid is adorned with murals and carvings detailing Nkaru'thar's creation of the world. Zarhánis is ruled by the **High Pyremant**, the supreme spiritual leader of all Okháris and advisor to the Overlord. The Spirit Flame governs the inner life: dreams, visions, communion with the ancestors, and the passage of the soul after death.

**Kaljékor – The Golden Flame** (gold fire) The flame of abundance, prosperity, and commerce. Kaljékor sits at the crossroads of major trade routes, and its pyramidal temple complex doubles as a vast marketplace bustling with merchants from [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]], the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Sea]], and beyond. Ruled by a council of priest-nobles, Kaljékor's influence extends far through trade alliances. The Golden Flame governs the material world: harvest, craft, wealth, negotiation, and the bonds of exchange that hold communities together.

**Vuthráka – The Wild Flame** (red fire) The flame of war, protection, and destruction. Vuthráka guards the southern frontier of Okháris. Its temple complex features intricate statues of the demi-gods of war and chaos, believed to protect the city from outside threats. Ruled by the **Warden of the Flame**, a warrior-priest who commands Okháris's armies during times of conflict. The Wild Flame governs the body and the battlefield: strength, courage, sacrifice, and the violent renewal that follows destruction.

The three aspects are not rivals but necessary complements – spirit without strength is helpless, strength without wisdom is blind, and neither endures without prosperity. The Overlord, currently [[being-takorozanethar|Tákoro Zanethar]] (the Keeper of the Three Flames), embodies the unity of all three, regularly visiting each city-state's temple to participate in grand ceremonies that reaffirm the wholeness of the Eternal Flame.

### The Zohira: Intermediary Spirits

Between Nkaru'thar and the mortal world stand the **[[lore-zohira|zohira]]** – demi-gods or intermediary spirits tied to specific aspects of life such as fertility, storms, rivers, death, and the hunt. The shamanic tradition that deals with them is the [[affiliation-njiayaroho|Njia ya Roho]], which is not a branch of this faith but the older ground it stands on. Their shrines are scattered across the kingdom, from the pyramidal complexes of the city-states to simple stone cairns in tribal territories.

The zohira are not worshipped as independent gods but understood as emissaries and servants of the Eternal Flame, each governing a particular domain of mortal experience. Rural tribes maintain especially close relationships with their local zohira, with shamans who can commune with these spirits, interpret their will, and intercede on behalf of their people. The Shókuma tribe's tradition of **spirit communion** and the Magara people's connection to **Spirit Blades** – weapons blessed by the zohira – exemplify this deeply personal dimension of the faith.

The distinction between urban temple worship and rural spirit practice is one of emphasis, not doctrine. The city priesthoods acknowledge the zohira as part of Nkaru'thar's design, and the rural shamans recognize the Eternal Flame as the ultimate source. In practice, however, tension sometimes arises between the formalized temple hierarchy and the more intuitive, shamanic traditions of the outlying tribes.

### Sacred Objects

- **Pyrestone:** A fragment of volcanic glass, carried by the faithful as a personal connection to the Eternal Flame.
- **Three-Wick Oil Lamp:** Lit during prayers, each wick representing one aspect of the Flame. Extinguishing any wick before the others is considered deeply inauspicious.
- **Ashmark Paste:** A mixture of temple hearth-ash and sacred oils, applied to the forehead and hands during ceremonies of passage, mourning, or consecration.
- **Spirit Bone Pendant:** Carved from the bones of animals taken during the Spirit Hunt, worn by shamans and warriors as a mark of their spiritual bond.

### Relics

- **The First Ember:** (Unique) Housed in the deepest chamber of the Zarhánis temple, the First Ember is said to be an undying flame kindled by Nkaru'thar at the moment of creation. It has never been extinguished. The High Pyremant alone may enter its chamber, and all temple fires in Okháris are symbolically descended from it.
- **The Warden's Brand:** A ceremonial iron blade kept at Vuthráka, heated in sacred fire and used to mark warriors who have proven themselves in the Trial of Flames. The scars it leaves are borne with pride as proof of Nkaru'thar's favor.
- **The Scales of Kaljékor:** A gilded balance kept in the temple-marketplace, used to weigh offerings and seal trade oaths. Merchants who swear false oaths upon the Scales are said to suffer ruin within a year and a day.

### Clergy

The priesthood of Nkaru'thar is divided among the three city-states, each maintaining its own hierarchy. All three ultimately defer to the High Pyremant of Zarhánis in matters of doctrine, though in practice the Warden of the Flame and the council of Kaljékor exercise considerable autonomy.

#### Zarhánis (Spirit Flame)

- **Mwanafunzi** ("Student of the Flame") – Initiate
- **Mtaalamu wa Moto** ("Scholar of the Fire") – Priest/Priestess
- **Mlinzi wa Roho** ("Guardian of Spirits") – Senior Priest
- **Nabii wa Mwanga** ("Prophet of Light") – Temple Master
- **Pyremant Mkuu** (High Pyremant) – Supreme spiritual leader of Okháris (unique title, one holder)

#### Kaljékor (Golden Flame)

- **Mwanafunzi** ("Student of the Flame") – Initiate
- **Mhudumu wa Dhahabu** ("Servant of Gold") – Priest/Priestess
- **Mlinzi wa Utajiri** ("Guardian of Wealth") – Senior Priest
- **Mtawala wa Soko** ("Master of the Market") – Temple Master
- **Baraza ya Moto** ("Council of Fire") – The ruling priest-nobles (collective authority)

#### Vuthráka (Wild Flame)

- **Mwanafunzi** ("Student of the Flame") – Initiate
- **Askari wa Moto** ("Soldier of Fire") – Warrior-Priest
- **Mlinzi wa Vita** ("Guardian of Battle") – Senior Warrior-Priest
- **Jemadari wa Moto** ("Commander of Fire") – Temple Master
- **Mlinzi wa Mwali** (Warden of the Flame) – Supreme military-spiritual leader of Vuthráka (unique title, one holder)

#### Tribal Shamans

Outside the city-states, the faith is maintained by **shamans** (often called **mwalimu wa roho**, "teachers of the spirit") who are initiated through the **Spirit Hunt** – a sacred rite in which the candidate must track and defeat a powerful jungle predator, consuming its heart in a ritual that binds their soul more closely to the spirit realm and the zohira. These shamans do not hold formal rank within the temple hierarchy but are respected as intermediaries who maintain the spiritual balance of the rural communities.

**Key Skills:** Herbalism, Physician, Trance, Folklore, Singing, Survival, Tracking

### Divine Servants

- **Zohira:** The intermediary spirits – powerful but not omniscient, each tied to a specific domain (storms, rivers, fertility, death, the hunt, etc.). They appear in visions, dreams, and moments of crisis to guide or warn the faithful.
- **Moto-Walinzi** ("Fire Guardians"): Spectral presences said to inhabit the great temple fires. When the flames burn in unusual colors or patterns, priests interpret these as messages from the Fire Guardians.

### Ceremonies and Festivals

**Low Ceremonies:**

- **The Lighting of the Hearth:** When a new household is established, a priest or shaman kindles its first fire from a flame carried from the nearest temple, binding the home to Nkaru'thar's protection. This ceremony is also performed after a death in the household, symbolically renewing the family's connection to the Eternal Flame.
- **The Ashmark Blessing:** A monthly ceremony held at local shrines where the faithful receive ashmark paste on their foreheads, renewing their bond with the Eternal Flame and receiving the blessing of the attending zohira.

**High Ceremonies:**

- **The Rite of Three Fires:** Performed only by senior clergy, this ceremony involves the simultaneous lighting of three sacred fires – white, gold, and red – in a triangular arrangement. The priests chant the creation hymn while the fires burn, and the pattern of the flames is read as prophecy for the coming season.
- **The Spirit Hunt:** The initiation ordeal for shamans. The candidate enters the wilderness alone to track and defeat a spirit-beast, consuming its heart to forge their bond with the zohira. Not all candidates survive.

**Festivals:**

- **The Rekindling:** Held at the summer solstice, all temple fires across Okháris are extinguished and then relit from embers carried from the First Ember at Zarhánis. Pilgrims from every corner of the kingdom travel to the city-states, and the week-long festival features processions, feasting, and tribal gatherings that reinforce cultural unity across Okháris.
- **The Night of Ashes:** Held at midwinter, this solemn festival honors the dead and the destructive aspect of the Eternal Flame. Families burn offerings to their ancestors, and shamans perform rites to appease restless spirits. The zohira are said to walk openly among mortals on this night.
- **The Market of Flames:** An annual trade festival at Kaljékor, combining commerce and devotion. Merchants make offerings at the Golden Flame temple before conducting their most important transactions of the year, and oaths sworn during the festival are considered sacred and unbreakable.

### Ordeals for Favor

- **The Firewalk:** The most common ordeal, in which the faithful walk barefoot across a bed of coals drawn from a temple fire. Those who cross without injury are said to have Nkaru'thar's direct favor.
- **The Trial of Three Flames:** A grueling ordeal for those seeking high office in the priesthood, requiring the candidate to fast for three days while maintaining three sacred fires without allowing any to go out, even through storm and wind.
- **The Spirit Hunt:** Described above – the initiation ordeal for shamans, but also undertaken by warriors and leaders seeking to prove their spiritual worthiness.
- **The Vigil of Ashes:** An ordeal of mourning and purification, in which the penitent covers themselves in ash and sits before a dying fire from dusk to dawn, meditating on loss, destruction, and renewal. Often undertaken after a great failure or transgression.

### Orders

**Priestly Orders:**

- **Keepers of the First Ember:** An inner circle of Zarhánis priests who tend the First Ember and guard the deepest temple chambers. They are sworn to silence about what they witness in the Ember's presence and are said to receive visions directly from Nkaru'thar.
- **The Flameweavers:** Priest-scholars of Kaljékor who study the patterns of sacred flames for prophecy and maintain the records of trade-oaths sworn upon the Scales.

**Fighting Orders:**

- **The Ashborn:** Elite warrior-priests of Vuthráka who have passed the Trial of Three Flames and bear the Warden's Brand. They fight with ritual scarification visible on their arms and chest, and are said to be immune to fear. In battle they serve as both shock troops and spiritual anchors for the army, chanting war-hymns that invoke the Wild Flame.

### A Worshipped Faith on Older Ground

Nkaru'thar is **worshipped**, and that distinguishes it from the ground it stands on. To be Okháric
is already to be of the spirits – the [[affiliation-njiayaroho|Njia ya Roho]] is not joined, not
professed and not chosen; it is what being Okháric consists of, and nobody is received into it
because nobody was ever outside it.

The Eternal Flame is different in kind. It is a religion in the full sense: a deity worshipped, a
doctrine assented to, a priesthood ordained, a hierarchy climbed. It came to a people who already
had their cairns, and it did not replace them.

Because it is professed, it can in principle be professed otherwise. An Okháric who takes up
another god – a Kemetían deity met through the Ta'Kheperu trade, a Vylarian import at the coast –
has not stopped being Okháric and has not left the zohira, who were never a matter of profession.
What they have done is decline the Flame, and the Flame is the state religion of a kingdom whose
Overlord is titled Keeper of the Three Flames.

In the tribal country this is survivable and is treated as an eccentricity. In the city-states it
is not. The Vuthráka priesthood regards refusal of the Flame as a matter touching the defense of
Okháris itself, and the practical distinction between heresy and treason has never been drawn with
any care. People do it. Rather a lot of them do not do it twice.

The reverse case does not arise. There is no apostasy from the Spirit Path, because there is
nothing to renounce – only [[affiliation-njiayaroho|Spirit-Deafness]], which is inflicted rather
than chosen, and which the Okhárics regard as a far worse thing to have happen to a person than
being burned for refusing the Flame.

### Relationship to Other Faiths

Nkaru'thar is a distinctly Okháric tradition with no direct counterpart in any other Thalorna pantheon. The fire-worship elements bear a superficial resemblance to the [[affiliation-surtr|Faith of Súrtr]] in the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian]] tradition, but the theological foundations are entirely different: where Súrtr represents destruction and chaos as ends in themselves, Nkaru'thar's destructive aspect is always understood as part of a cycle of renewal.

The [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Kemetian]] faith of neighboring [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]] has had some contact with Okháric beliefs through trade and border interaction, but the two traditions remain distinct. Kemetian scholars tend to view Nkaru'thar as a primitive fire cult, while Okháric priests consider the elaborate Kemetian pantheon an unnecessary fragmentation of what is fundamentally one divine force.

The animistic spirit traditions of the Xerathian interior share significant common ground with the zohira veneration of the Okháric faith, suggesting deep ancestral roots that predate the formalized temple worship of the city-states.
