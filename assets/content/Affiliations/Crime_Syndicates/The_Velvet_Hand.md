## Overview

Born from the intersection of Helionite mystery cults and the Vylarian court's insatiable appetite for intrigue, the Velvet Hand trades in the most valuable commodity of all: secrets. Where the Crimson Ledger follows the money and the Gray Tide follows the cargo, the Velvet Hand follows the whisper—the unguarded word in a bedchamber, the letter left unsealed on a senator's desk, the confession extracted from a dying man by a sympathetic healer who happens to have a very good memory.

The organization grew in Heliónis, where the Academy of the Silver Veil already trained sophisticated political agents—educated courtesans, philosophical companions, and social intermediaries. The Velvet Hand recruited from its graduates and dropouts alike, recognizing that the same skills that make an excellent hetaira also make an excellent spy. From Heliónis, the network spread to Vylaria's rigid, faction-ridden court, where information is currency and a senator's valet hears more than the Senate itself. In Provènzia, theatrical troupes and artistic salons provide perfect cover for operatives who can adopt any persona.

## Territory

**Primary:** Vylarian cities (especially Magnápolis and Alyssa), Provenzian courts and cultural centers, Helionite city-states (Thyrenae, Pelagora, Kalydria)

**Secondary:** The Velvet Hand maintains small cells in every major city where powerful people gather. A single agent in a Haradian guild hall or a Ta.Kheperian court can be worth more than a dozen enforcers.

## Operations

**Espionage:** The core business. The Velvet Hand maintains networks of informants in the households of the powerful—servants, tutors, musicians, courtesans, personal physicians. These agents collect information passively, reporting on conversations overheard, visitors received, letters glimpsed, moods observed. The intelligence is collated by handlers and sold to interested parties: rival nobles, foreign governments, competing merchants, or the other syndicates.

**Blackmail:** Information gathered through espionage is weaponized. A senator's affair, a merchant's fraudulent books, a priest's crisis of faith—all become leverage. The Velvet Hand rarely makes crude threats; instead, a target receives a polite note suggesting that a "mutual friend" would appreciate a small favor. The implicit alternative is never stated but universally understood.

**Political assassination:** The Velvet Hand's most feared service. Kills are designed to look natural—poison in wine, a fall from a balcony, a sudden illness. Blade work is considered crude and is reserved for messages. The organization maintains a small cadre of specialist poisoners, some trained in Helionite pharmaceutical traditions, others in Ta.Kheperian embalming arts.

**Theft of sensitive documents:** State secrets, trade agreements, military plans, Ordo Arcanis research notes—anything written down can be copied or stolen. The Velvet Hand's document forgers are among the best in the world, capable of producing copies indistinguishable from originals.

## Organization

The Velvet Hand operates in isolated cells, each knowing only its immediate handler. A pleasure house madam in Magnápolis might manage six informants but knows nothing about operations in Provènzia. Handlers report to regional coordinators, who communicate through coded letters delivered via the Velvet Hand's own courier network—often traveling performers or itinerant scholars whose movements attract no suspicion.

Leadership is deliberately opaque. The organization is rumored to be run by a council, possibly based in Heliónis, but no one outside the highest levels knows for certain. This ambiguity is intentional—you cannot betray what you do not know, and you cannot decapitate what you cannot find.

Legitimate fronts include pleasure houses, theaters, art dealerships, and a handful of prestigious servant placement agencies. The last are particularly valuable: when a noble family hires a new chambermaid or cook through a Hand-controlled agency, the organization gains a permanent listening post inside that household.

## Relations with Other Syndicates

The Velvet Hand sells information to everyone, including about the other syndicates. This makes it both indispensable and universally loathed. The Crimson Ledger buys financial intelligence from the Hand, knowing full well that the Hand is also selling intelligence about the Ledger to the Auric Compact. The Gray Tide has standing orders to kill confirmed Velvet Hand agents found operating in Tide territory without prior arrangement—orders enforced about half the time, depending on whether the agent has something useful to trade first.

The Velvet Hand considers itself more sophisticated than its rivals and cultivates an air of aristocratic disdain for the "crude commerce" of the other syndicates. This attitude masks genuine fear—the Hand has no enforcers, no ships, no fighters. If a rival syndicate decided to wage open war, the Velvet Hand would be destroyed. Its survival depends on being too useful to kill and too dangerous to ignore.

The Ash Road is the Hand's most reliable client for assassination contracts, particularly when the Road needs a political obstacle in a coastal city quietly removed. The Hand charges premium rates for these services and has never been caught—a track record it guards jealously.

## Identifying Marks

Members wear no visible marks. Recognition is through a system of coded conversational phrases—literary references, philosophical quotations, and musical allusions that sound like educated small talk to outsiders but convey specific meanings to initiates. A new contact might quote a particular line of Helionite poetry; the correct response is a complementary verse. Getting the exchange wrong can mean death.
