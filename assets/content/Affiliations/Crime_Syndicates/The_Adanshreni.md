**Pronunciation:** _AA-daa-na-SHRAY-nee_

A surety-agent of **the Adanshreni** explains the arrangement to a caravan-master buying cover for the first time, and he starts with the charter and not with the theft. "You have read our charter. Everyone has; it is a public document, and it calls us a shrena of receivers, whose work is the recovery, appraisal and restoration of goods gone astray. That is not entirely a lie. Pay the standing fee and your cargo is under our guarantee, and if it goes astray we recover it or we pay."

The Adanshreni is the shrena of thieves of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] and the largest criminal body in [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]], and it is not a syndicate. That distinction is the whole of its character. The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|syndicates]] of the Vylarian basin are enterprises: they hold territory, they answer to a boss, and they can be broken by taking the territory or killing the boss. The Adanshreni is a craft shrena, hereditary in its lines, chartered by law in some forty polities. These are the forty that hold a seat at the great pilgrimage assembly in their own right, kingdoms and city-states together, which is the count a Vedyari means by _the kingdoms_. It keeps a court of its own and a treasury of its own, and its craft-lines pass from parent to child.

No king has ever destroyed it, because there is nothing to destroy that is not also the social order itself. Dynasties fall and the weavers' shrena persists, and so does this one. Its name means _the shrena of taking_.

## The Doctrine of Adun

The shrena has a theology, which it takes seriously and most priests do not. _**Adun**_ means both receiving and seizing, and the shrena has built its self-understanding on that ambiguity.

Nothing, the shrena teaches, is owned. Wealth is held for a term, as a body is held for a term, and karmu sets the term and not the holder. Everything in a strongbox is already in transit, and the shrena is the hand it passes through. In this reading the taker performs a service. He takes on the karmic weight of the taking, and the owner is relieved of an attachment he would otherwise have carried into the next life.

The [[affiliation-triyangasampradeya|Triyanga]] tradition condemns this as self-serving nonsense of the most transparent kind, and says so at length. The clergy of [[affiliation-vahrati|Vahrati]] (Decay) are markedly less hostile, since dispossession and dissolution are their doctrine too, and a few of their temples accept shrena endowments. Every taker makes an offering to [[lore-pvnjtrsdty|Pavanajitras]] before going out on the roads, which means that the shrena and the caravan it is about to rob have prayed to the same god that morning.

## Structure

The shrena's form is entirely conventional, and only its craft is not.

- **The charter.** Each of the forty charters the shrena separately, as it charters weavers or goldsmiths: a crown grants it where there is a crown, and a council or a sabhra where there is not. Each charter is negotiated, paid for and renewed. A charter fixes what the shrena may take, from whom, and what share the granting body receives. It is the shrena's most valuable possession, and every other rule serves to protect it.
- **The Sabhra.** A shrena court of elders judges disputes between craft-lines, rules on whether a taking was proper, and orders restoration when it was not. The courts of the forty recognize its judgments, which saves them an enormous volume of work.
- **The craft-lines.** These are hereditary specialisms, exactly like the weavers' craft-lines: housebreakers, road-takers, cutpurses, appraisers, seal-makers, and the line that handles temple property under restrictions the others regard as terrifying. Marriage across craft-lines is negotiated as any marriage between hereditary lines is negotiated.
- **The register.** Every member above **Antevosin** is written down. This is not carelessness. A registered thief is a thief the shrena can produce, disown or defend, and the register is what makes the charter enforceable.

There is no head of the Adanshreni. There are forty **Shreshthins**, who acknowledge one another's law, settle their disputes at a triennial assembly, and cooperate readily because their charters do not compete.

## What It Actually Does

_Most of the shrena's income is not theft._ The larger half is surety. A merchant house, a temple or a caravan pays the shrena a standing fee and the shrena guarantees the goods. Nothing chartered touches them, and if an unchartered thief takes them the shrena recovers the goods or pays out of the surety pool. This is why [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective]] has houses in arrangements it does not discuss, and why [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking]] and the shrena are difficult to disentangle: a body that can move value across forty polities without legal exposure performs a banking function whether it means to or not.

The rest is craft. The shrena breaks into houses, takes on licensed stretches of road, receives and appraises, forges seals and letters, enforces debts for lenders who prefer not to be seen doing it, and trades in information, much of it with [[affiliation-twilighths|the Twilight House]], whose **Factor to the Shrena** exists for exactly this.

And it hunts. _The shrena's most implacable enemy is the unchartered thief_, who steals its trade, voids its sureties and endangers its charter. Unchartered thieves are found and handed to the magistrates of whichever polity granted the line its charter, and this service is what makes a charter worth granting.

## The Taboos

The shrena's prohibitions read like piety and are almost entirely pragmatic. Each protects the charter.

- **No killing during a taking.** A theft is the shrena court's business, and a killing is the chartering power's. A taker who kills forfeits his share, his standing and often his lineage's.
- **Nothing from a sanctum.** The doctrine of adun requires that the gods consent, and the shrena is unwilling to test the proposition.
- **Nothing from a house in mourning, and nothing from a wedding.** Both would cost the shrena the public tolerance it lives on.
- **Restore on demand.** If the Sabhra rules a taking improper, the goods go back, whatever the taker has already done with them.

Outsiders get the first of these wrong. The shrena does not forbid killing. It forbids killing _during a taking_, because that is what converts its business into the chartering power's. A commission carried out cleanly, by a hand that cannot be traced to a charter, breaks no written rule. The persistent, credible and furiously denied accusation against the Adanshreni is that certain Shreshthins accept exactly such commissions and route them through people who are not quite members. The shrena has been arguing this with itself for two generations, and it is what the schism below was about.

## The Rest of Vedyaran Crime

Vedyara is a subcontinent, and the Adanshreni is dominant without being sole.

- **The port syndicates.** The coastal city-states support outward-facing organizations that deal in what the charters do not cover: trafficking, contraband, and business with [[affiliation-graytide|the Gray Tide]], [[affiliation-theashroad|the Ash Road]] and the shippers of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]. The shrena regards them as unchartered and beneath contempt, and does business with them constantly.
- **Local gangs.** As everywhere in Thalorna, every town has its own petty operators, who subcontract to the shrena and are the first casualties of any dispute.
- **The [Chinnasutra]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"}.** A schism, perhaps two generations old, of craft-lines that rejected the taboos, chiefly the prohibition on killing, and were cut off. They are the only rivals the shrena treats as an existential matter, because crowns and councils read a Chinnasutra killing during a taking as a shrena killing, and charters have been suspended over it. The shrena pays outsiders very well to deal with them, precisely because it cannot be seen to. The cutting is [[lore-cutthread|the Cut Thread]], about 660 AF.
- The hill and forest raiders stand outside any charter, any polity and any conversation.

## Adventure Hooks

- **The charter falls due.** A reforming young ruler intends to let the shrena's charter lapse. The shrena wants it renewed, the Merchant Collective is quietly divided, and someone has begun arranging robberies designed to look like the shrena's work.
- **The surety was called.** A caravan the party is guarding is under shrena surety. If it is taken the shrena pays, and the shrena would much rather recover the goods, which means the party will be working alongside professional thieves who know exactly what the cargo is worth.
- **A cut thread.** The Sabhra wants a Chinnasutra cell removed and cannot be seen anywhere near it. The contract is generous and deniable, and will not be honored in writing.
- **The improper taking.** Something was taken that should not have been, out of a sanctum or from a house in mourning, and the Sabhra has ordered restoration. The taker has fled with it, and the shrena needs it back before the temple makes the matter public.
- **The wrong client.** The Twilight House has sold intelligence to a client whose interests the shrena opposes. The standing arrangement between them is about to become a standing quarrel.
- **A succession.** A Shroshtin has died without the Sabhra agreeing on a successor, and two craft-lines are arming.
