## The Chaya-vrata—The Shadow-Vow

- **Practice:** The waking dream—illusion, misdirection, the unremembered face
- **Broken from:** [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]]
- **Pronunciation:** _CHAH-yah VRAH-tah_

The [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]] works the dream of a sleeper who has come and asked for it. The **Chaya-vrata** works the dream of a person who is awake and has not.

That is the whole of the difference, and it is enough. The Council of the Dream condemned the vow and holds the condemnation open; the Samāja's monasteries will not seat a Chaya-vrata and will not name one; and the vow has gone on being taught, one student at a time, for as long as the condemnation has been open.

### What It Does

Three things, and the third is the one people pay for.

The **misdirection**: a person looks at the thing and does not see it, or looks away at the moment that matters and cannot afterwards say why.

The **errand nobody recalls**: a message delivered, a door opened, a seal carried, and a person who did all three and remembers a dull afternoon.

The **unremembered face**: the one that will not fix. A witness knows there was a person, knows what the person said, and cannot assemble the face out of any of it. Courts across Vedyara have learned to recognize the shape of this testimony, which has not helped them at all, because recognizing it proves only that it was done.

### The Shadow-Brotherhoods

The brotherhoods take contracts. They are not the vow and they do not teach it; they are the arrangement by which a line's work reaches a buyer without either party learning anything about the other. They supply poisons, watchers, and the kind of instruction in silent movement and clean killing that a house pays for when it has decided something, and they hire Chaya-vrata preparers by the job.

Several lines will have nothing to do with them, hold that the trade is what turned a discipline into a service, and teach students accordingly. The brotherhoods are entirely indifferent to this.

### Why Nobody Hunts It

The vow is deniable, and that is a design rather than an accident. A working that leaves no mark on a body, no residue in a room and no witness who can describe anyone has nothing for an investigating court to seize. A line that suspects it has been noticed stops working and waits, sometimes for years, and a line that ends is not recorded as having ended, so nobody knows how many there are.

What the vow fears is not a court. It is the gap between the conch and the pyre: a [[affiliation-thresholdkeepers|Threshold-keeper]] with a warrant and one well-framed question is the only instrument in Vedyara that reaches past the unremembered face, and every line teaches its students that a death is the dangerous end of the work.
