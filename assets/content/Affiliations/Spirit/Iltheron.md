A Vylarian family that has just had a child sends for a star-reader before it sends for the priest. The reader asks two things, the hour of the birth and the room it happened in, and goes away with a fee. A week later he sends back a sheet with the child's sign at the top and its six elements below, and the household copies it into the book where it keeps its births, marriages and deaths. When the child is betrothed, the contract cites that sheet.

The Iltheron (the Star Circle) is that wheel of twelve signs and the tradition that reads it. Its readers, each an _ozethos_ (diviner) licensed by a temple of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], cast a nativity from the hour and place of birth and say which sign stands over it. Every sign's name takes the hallowed ending that a god's name takes, and Vylarians speak of a sign as standing over a birth, never as causing it.

## The Wheel

Twelve signs stand on the wheel, and each gives way to the next around the year. Each favors some aptitudes and hinders others by as much.

The wheel divides every aptitude a person has among six elements, earth, metal, fire, air, spirit and water, and a sign's whole character is where it stands in each. A child born as one sign gives way to the next is read under both, and the reader writes "on the threshold" across the top of the sheet. Families count a threshold birth lucky.

## The Readers and the Order

The readers have no order of their own. A temple that licenses them keeps a copy of the wheel's tables, examines anyone who wants the license, and takes it back from a reader caught selling a sheet for a birth he never asked about. A reader without the license can still cast a nativity, and the sheet he sends back is worth nothing at a betrothal.

[[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]] (the Thordimalon, the Arcane Order) classes the reading of a nativity as tolerated: a reckoning from the hour of a birth, not an arcane working, and so outside its monopoly. The two traditions share the six elements, and they disagree about which had them first. The Order's masters teach that the wheel borrowed its elements from the six currents of [[affiliation-zemorhodhis|The Six Ways]] (the Zemor Hodhis). The star-readers say the wheel is the older of the two.

## The Present {#present}

The Star Circle is content, and its trade is steady. Every Vylarian birth still sends for a reader before a priest, every betrothal contract still cites the sheet, and the great houses' betrothals this season cite theirs as every betrothal does. The temples' licenses are the tradition's only discipline, and a reader caught selling a sheet for a birth he never asked about loses his. Its one quarrel is old and bloodless: the Order's masters teach that the wheel borrowed its six elements from the Zemor Hodhis, the readers answer that the wheel is the older, and because the Order classes the reading as tolerated, the dispute never comes before a hearing of the Sekran.
