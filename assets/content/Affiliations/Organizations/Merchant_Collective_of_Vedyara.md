_Vedyaran: Vyāpārī Sangha—"the Merchants' Assembly"_

- **Type:** Mercantile federation
- **Region:** All five seats of the Assembly of the Compact—[[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]], [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]], [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Vindhyālaya]], [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]] and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]]—with caravan stations as far west as Amradad and as far north as the Khazryn frontier
- **Founded:** Traditionally dated to the Third Compact, four centuries ago
- **Magnum Collegium status:** Not a member; independent. The [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]] has, for two generations, attempted to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with the Collective; the Collective has declined every overture. The Haradian [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] has likewise been refused.

## Overview

The Merchant Collective is what the five seats of the Compact have instead of a unified state. The seats keep separate councils, separate watches and separate courts. Their great houses intermarry until they nearly amount to a single class, and they have never amounted to a single throne. The Collective is the one institution that crosses every border, taxes every road and has a hand in every mine of consequence the Compact reaches—outright where it can hold the ground, by lease where the ground is somebody's by older right, and not at all where a constitution shuts it out.

A seat that wishes to know the silver price at the Gorges asks the Collective. A seat that wishes to ship grain to Rājapur hires a Collective caravan. A seat that wishes to fortify a frontier negotiates with the Collective for the steel, and for the loan.

The councils and the courts are not weak. The Collective has made itself the connective tissue of Vedyaran life across four centuries, and nothing moves without it. Its founders were three generations of merchant princes working across polities that were then at war with each other, and the Collective's telling of its own founding ends where such tellings end: no court or council would unify the subcontinent, and the merchants could. Vedyara has no tradition of unification and the Collective supplied none. What it supplied was a body of standing law that made the question unnecessary, which is the smaller claim and the true one.

They wrote the **Compact**, a body of standing trade law, arbitration procedure, and shared infrastructure the merchants would keep in common: the roads, the caravanserai, the watch posts and the currency standards. They wrote an Assembly of the signatories to renew and amend it. Each signatory faced the choice of refusing and watching its territory grow poorer than its neighbors, and each signed. They have signed every renewal since, and the five who hold seats today are the five that share the [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking system]]. [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]] never signed and is not kept out: a seat obliges its holder to let Collective factors weigh, assay and lend against the metal crossing its territory, and the Gold Constitution vests the weighing in a temple station and the lending in a common treasury that no outside house may reach. The Assembly has wanted a sixth seat, and the gold behind it, for as long as it has kept minutes.

The Collective's central paradox is that its members compete with one another fiercely in every market while collectively dominating every market. A Sūryāgarha silver merchant will undercut a Rājapur silver merchant on every shipment they both bid for, then sit beside him on the Collective's standards committee and vote together against any outside competitor. The discipline holds because the alternative—open competition with non-Collective merchants—would impoverish them both.

## Membership and Structure

Membership comes by inheritance for the great mercantile houses (the _kulinas_), by sponsorship and probation for new entrants (the _pratisthas_), and by craft-guild affiliation for the artisan suppliers (the _karmakaras_). The three tiers hold distinct rights. A kulina's vote in the Collective's assemblies carries weight in proportion to his house's contributions to the common fund. A pratistha votes only on matters concerning his probationary terms. A karmakara takes part through his guild's elected representative.

The Collective's governing body is the **Assembly of the Compact** (_Sangha-Sabhā_), which meets twice a year, once in the spring at Chandrapur and once in the autumn at Vyalendra. The Assembly's standing committees sit continuously through the year and handle the administration. The **Roads Committee** keeps the caravan infrastructure and adjudicates road-tax disputes. The **Standards Committee** sets weights, measures, currency and quality grades. The **Foreign Trade Committee** negotiates with non-Vedyaran polities and licenses members to operate beyond the Compact. The **Disputes Committee** arbitrates the internal conflicts the parties would rather not take to a ruler's court.

At the apex sits the **High Speaker of the Collective**, elected by the kulinas from among themselves for a seven-year term. The current Speaker is **Suvirya Rāmadāsa**, an elderly silver-merchant of Sūryāgarha whose tenure has been notable for caution and for the quiet expansion of the Collective's mining holdings.

## Operations

- **Mining.** The Collective works the copper, silver, tin and lapis of the [[place-slvrgorges|Silver Gorges]] under lease from the hereditary smithing clans who hold the rock, at prices the customs-house at Sūryāgarha sees only after the fact, and it owns the gem-bearing river-workings of the upper [[place-chandrmahi|Chandramahī]] outright. Suvarnagiri's gold is outside its reach altogether. [[being-vshnshvdysgrml|Vishnusha Vidyāsāgaramandala]] is one of its most respected mine-masters. The Collective's mining operations employ tens of thousands of workers, most of whom never see the surface for weeks at a time, and the labor terms are a continuing source of internal friction.
- **Caravan trade.** The long-distance caravan routes between Vedyaran cities and the markets of Amradad by the march road, of Khazryn by the passes, and of the eastern frontier are Collective infrastructure, operated by Collective members under Collective standards.
- **Banking and credit.** The Collective issues standardized letters of credit redeemable at any Collective member's house, the closest thing the realm has to a unified currency. The system is the backbone of Vedyaran commerce and the source of the Collective's leverage over the councils and courts themselves, several of which owe substantial debts.
- **Quality grading and standards.** A Collective grade-mark on a bolt of silk, a measure of grain, or a refined ingot of silver carries reliable warranty across the seats of the Compact and most of the western buyers' markets.

## Internal Tensions

- **The labor question.** Reform-minded younger kulinas have proposed substantial improvements to mining labor conditions; the older houses, whose fortunes were built on the present system, resist. The reform faction has not yet found a champion willing to risk his standing.
- **The expansion question.** The Foreign Trade Committee is divided between those who wish to push aggressively into Amradad's caravan markets and those who fear retaliation from the [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate]] and prefer the present arrangement of careful cooperation.
- **The compromised members.** Several houses are known or suspected to be in arrangements with the [[affiliation-twilighths|The Twilight House]] or the [[affiliation-adanasreni|Ādānaśreṇī]] beyond what the Collective's standing rules permit. Investigations have begun and stalled multiple times.

## Relations

- **The five seats of the Compact**—two courts and three councils, and every one of them a client, a debtor and an occasional opponent. The relationship is one of mutual dependence and mutual resentment.
- **The [[affiliation-twilighths|The Twilight House]]**—major client of intelligence services; the Collective uses Twilight House product to inform pricing and contract negotiations. The arrangement is acknowledged at the top and denied below.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]]**—particularly the [[affiliation-theashroad|Ash Road]], whose desert caravan routes complement and compete with Collective operations. The Collective and the Ash Road maintain an awkward truce based on each understanding what the other could do to it.
- **The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]**—limited engagement. The Ordo has tried to establish a presence in Vedyaran markets and has been quietly frustrated by Collective non-cooperation; the Ordo retaliates by occasionally licensing competing operators outside the Compact.

## Identifying Marks

Kulina-rank members wear a gold pin at the collar in the shape of a balance-scale resting on a coiled rope (the rope representing the caravan, the scale the standards). Pratistha members wear the same pin in silver. Karmakaras wear no Collective mark beyond their guild's. Senior officers wear additional rank bars below the pin.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Mine Collapse.** A lower working in the Silver Gorges has collapsed; hundreds are trapped or dead. The Collective's Standards Committee finds that the terms of the lease were violated. The kulina responsible is too senior to expel without fracturing the Collective; the survivors and the families of the dead are organizing for redress that neither the clans' gate nor the crown's customs-house has shown any interest in providing.
- **The Counterfeit Letters.** Collective letters of credit have been forged at scale, redeemed at multiple member houses, and traced back through a chain that disappears into the [[affiliation-twilighths|The Twilight House]]. The Collective must investigate without making the scandal public—and without confronting the Twilight House directly.
- **The Reform Petition.** A younger kulina, supported by a coalition of pratisthas and a sympathetic sabhā, formally proposes a labor reform at the Assembly of the Compact. The proposal would cost the older houses substantial profits. The Speaker's procedural rulings will determine whether the petition reaches a vote at all.
- **The Crown's Default.** Vindhyālaya has defaulted on its Collective loans and is offering, in lieu of repayment, a permanent grant of the Silver Gorges—rock the crown has never established is the crown's, and which the smithing clans already lease to the Collective over its objection. Accepting turns a quiet arrangement into a claim the clans must answer, and every other seat of the Compact reads it as the precedent it is.
