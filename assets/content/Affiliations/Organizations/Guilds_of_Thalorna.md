- **Type:** Overview note describing the guild system as a continent-spanning phenomenon
- **Region:** All settled regions of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]], [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]], and trading enclaves elsewhere; form and authority vary substantially by region

## Overview

Across the settled lands of Thalorna, the guild is the institution by which trades are organized. Wherever the population of a city has reached the density at which specialized trades can sustain full-time practice—and wherever a regional authority has emerged that finds it useful to deal with practitioners as a group rather than individually—guilds have, sooner or later, formed. They vary enormously across the continent in formality, in legal authority, in social prestige, in size, and in the kinds of trades they cover; what they share is the underlying function. A guild is the body that defines who is qualified to practice a trade, that maintains the standards of that practice, that trains the next generation, that adjudicates disputes between practitioners and between practitioners and clients, and that speaks for the trade in its dealings with the authorities and the public.

The single most consequential fact about the continent's guild map is the **[[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]**—the imperial Vylarian "guild of guilds"—and the territory across which its authority runs. Vylaria, all five Aurèldían kingdoms, and Hellád have, for three centuries, organized their guild life as a single integrated federation under the Collegium's umbrella; this single system covers more than half of settled Ankaris and the bulk of the continent's commercial output. Outside the Collegium's reach the picture fragments: the Northern Fertile Region (Bethûa, Okháris, Ta'Kheperu) shows the Collegium's influence on form without being part of its structure; the Confederation of Haradian States was a member territory until its violent twelve-years-past secession and now operates its own institution, the **[[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]**—built deliberately on Haradian terms, in the haradi-language, and refusing the Vylarian forms; the rest of the continent—the Nordlands, Vrystwald, Vedyara, the Khazryn and Dunhara steppes, K'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, Kalihara, and the southern Xerathian regions—runs on guild traditions that owe nothing to Magnápolis at all.

A character in any sizeable Thalornan city who reports being "affiliated with the Local Foo Guild" is reporting a relationship that is meaningful but whose particular meaning depends entirely on what trade, what city, and crucially what side of the Collegium's reach he stands on. The cooper's guild in a Byzarian port is, structurally, the same kind of body as the cooper's guild in a Tarvénian one and the cooper's guild in a Vylarian provincial city—all three roll up to Magnápolis, all three issue Collegium-recognized credentials, all three's masters can travel and seal work across the whole Vylarian-influenced map. None of those bodies remotely resembles the cooper's guild in a Nordheim hall-town, the cooper's _kula_ in a Vedyaran janapada capital, or the cooper's _per-_ in Ta'Kheperu. This note describes the common functions of the guild system, the Magnum Collegium's sphere, and the principal independent traditions outside it.

## Common Functions

Across the continent's guild traditions, the guild generally performs some or all of the following:

- **Licensing.** Determines who is qualified to practice the trade. Licensing may be formal (with examinations and chartered authority) or informal (with peer recognition and social pressure).
- **Standards.** Sets the standards of quality, the conventions of measurement and pricing, and the warranties that members' work carries.
- **Training.** Operates the apprenticeship system through which new practitioners enter the trade. The apprenticeship form varies: master-apprentice contracts in the western tradition, family-lineage transmission in much of Vedyara, hall-cohort schooling in the Nordlands, examined initiation in Ta'Kheperu.
- **Arbitration.** Adjudicates disputes between members, between members and clients, and (in some regions) between members and the broader public.
- **Representation.** Speaks for the trade in dealings with the municipal authorities, the regional lord or council, and (in matters of trade policy) the realm-level government.
- **Mutual aid.** Provides for members in distress—injury, age, the deaths of practitioners leaving dependents—through a common fund maintained by member dues.
- **Ritual life.** Most guilds have associated religious observances, often tied to a patron deity (the smiths to Vúlcan or Ptah; the scribes to Jánus or Thoth; the brewers to Florania or Mahájaya; etc.). The ritual life is rarely the guild's primary function but is often the matrix in which the more practical functions are conducted.

## The Magnum Collegium and Its Sphere

The single most consequential institution shaping the continent's guild map is the **[[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]**—the chartered umbrella body to which every guild in its member territory must belong, which holds genuine authority over guild charters, standards, discipline, and inter-guild jurisdiction. Founded by the second Vylarian Augustar at the close of the empire's western expansion three centuries ago, the Collegium began as an instrument of imperial governance: a way of absorbing diverse local guild traditions into a single legible system that the empire could regulate, tax, and depend on.

What is striking is what the Collegium has become _since_ the empire's political grip on most of those territories slackened. Today, only Vylaria proper and Heliónis remain under direct imperial governance. The five Aurèldían kingdoms have reasserted their sovereignty, the Byzarian League runs its own affairs, and (twelve years ago, violently) Harad seceded from the entire imperial system. And yet the Magnum Collegium continues to operate across Vylaria, Heliónis, Aurèldía, and Byzaría—_because the smaller polities benefit from participation more than they would benefit from withdrawal_. The Collegium has become, in effect, a cross-realm institution: imperial in origin, imperial in central leadership, but operating today as the post-imperial commonwealth's shared trade infrastructure rather than as a tool of imperial enforcement.

The reason the kingdoms stay is straightforward: the Collegium provides what they could not provide for themselves. A Tarvénian artisan's work is valued at a Tarvénian price unless and until that artisan can sell into a market larger than Tarvénia. The Collegium's mutual-recognition arrangements mean that a Tarvénian guild master can travel to Provènzia, Byzaría, Heliónis, or anywhere in Vylaria and seal work as a master without re-examination. He can hire apprentices, take commissions, register his seal, and operate as a full member of the trade. The kingdoms individually are too small to negotiate such reciprocal arrangements with every other polity; the Collegium does it for them at scale. The cost—submission to Magnápolis-centered governance of the trade system, plus the quiet 2% issuance fee that flows to the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|imperial treasury]] every time anyone in the commonwealth buys a traveler's note—is judged generation after generation as worth paying.

A guild operating in any Collegium territory holds its charter by Collegium recognition. A master's seal is registered with the Collegium. An apprentice is examined by procedures the Collegium has standardized. A jurisdictional dispute between guilds is adjudicated by the Curia Collegiorum at Magnápolis. A guildmaster whose conduct has escaped his home guild's discipline can be summoned before the Censores Collegiorum. The teeth are real and exercised; revocation of a guild's charter has, in living memory, ended the existence of two minor guilds outright.

### Vylaria and Heliónis (The Imperial Core)

The Collegium operates here within a fully imperial framework. All trades in the four Vylarian provinces and in the Heliónite city-states are organized as **collegia**—bodies chartered through the Magnum Collegium's machinery and standing in the imperial courts, with substantial regulatory authority delegated from the imperial throne. A Vylarian collegium is less a trade body than a small chartered jurisdiction within the imperial structure; the [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]] is the most consequential example. Membership confers genuine legal privileges (tax exemptions in some cases, court representation, access to imperial procurement) and corresponding obligations including [[affiliation-curiafscls|imperial taxation]]. The system is highly bureaucratized—seven-year apprenticeships, formal examinations, registered seals, substantial archives—and the most internally factionalized of the Collegium's regional manifestations.

### The Aurèldían Kingdoms (Post-Imperial Members)

Tarvénia, Provènzia, Élavendre, Aelwyth, and Calypsa have reasserted political sovereignty over the past two centuries, but all five remain full members of the Magnum Collegium. Their crowns set their own tax rates, run their own legal systems, and maintain their own armies; what they participate in through the Collegium is the trade infrastructure—mutual recognition of guild credentials, access to the Aerarium-backed banking system (via the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Argentariorum]]'s local chapterhouses), inter-territory jurisdictional adjudication, common standards. Aurèldían guilds hold Collegium charters on the same terms as Vylarian or Heliónite ones; their masters sit on the Curia Collegiorum at Magnápolis alongside imperial members; the [[affiliation-crtgrphrsgldbyzrnlg|Cartographers' Guild of the Byzarian League]] and the [[affiliation-prvnzthtrclctv|Theater Collective]] of Provènzia exemplify the pattern of locally-grounded guilds operating within the cross-realm framework.

The arrangement has chronic frictions. Censores Collegiorum traveling in Aurèldían kingdoms operate under Collegium authority alone, not under any imperial writ the local crown would recognize; investigations of locally-prominent guildmasters become diplomatic events. The crowns periodically propose modifications to the 2% issuance fee or to Collegium governance arrangements (always declined). Reformist factions in some kingdoms argue for withdrawal; conservative interests (the great merchant families, the established guilds, the banking houses) defend continued participation. The arrangement holds because the alternative—building credentialing and banking infrastructure from scratch and negotiating reciprocity treaties bilaterally with every other commonwealth polity—is impossibly more expensive.

### Byzaría (Post-Imperial Member)

The Byzarian League similarly retains full Collegium membership while having reasserted political sovereignty. The League's guilds operate in the Vylarian style with relatively little local distinctiveness in form (the strong municipal traditions of the League's cities translated easily into the Collegium's framework long ago and have persisted unchanged). Like the Aurèldían crowns, the League pays the 2% issuance fee and accepts Collegium governance of the trade system in exchange for the market access the participation provides.

### Heliónis

Heliónis is the unusual case: it remains under direct imperial governance (unlike the Aurèldían kingdoms or Byzaría) while operating an unusual sub-arrangement in which the city-states retain substantial autonomy in how Collegium rulings are administered. The Heliónite political culture—short factional tenures, dense web of city-state competition—makes stable imperial enforcement difficult, and the empire has long accepted a degree of Heliónite institutional creativity that would be unthinkable in Vylaria proper. Helionite guilds answer to the Collegium and to the imperial throne; their actual day-to-day functioning depends substantially on which faction is currently dominant in their city; the formal Collegium structure carries on regardless.

### The Haradian Schism

Harad was a Collegium member territory for over a century before the secession war twelve years ago—incorporated during the empire's eastern expansion, integrated into both the trade infrastructure and the imperial governance system. The violent break removed Harad from _all three_ Vylarian institutions: the Collegium, the imperial governance, and the Aerarium-backed banking system. This was a substantially deeper withdrawal than the Aurèldían kingdoms or Byzaría had ever undertaken; both of those polities had retained Collegium membership and banking participation while throwing off political imperial rule. Harad alone left everything.

The Haradian guilds were not dismantled. The merchant princes who had led the rebellion had no intention of preserving the Vylarian umbrella beyond the war's closing weeks but they did want to preserve the trade life the umbrella had organized. They constituted the **[[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]**—"the Council of the Guild-Wardens"—in the haradi-language, under Haradian officer titles (Rab-Naqîr, Sôd-Rabbânîn, Pāqîdîn, Adônîm al-Mudunîn), framed deliberately as a restoration of the pre-conquest Haradian compact tradition rather than as any kind of successor to the Latin institution. The Sôd performs the Collegium's role _and_ (through its Bayt al-Khazînah treasury arm) the Aerarium's role for the Confederation. Procedural continuity with the old Collegium work is real but is presented as a parallel inheritance, not a borrowing. The Vylarian Collegium has, for twelve years, refused to use the Sôd's chosen name and continues to address its correspondence to "Magnum Collegium Haradicum," which the Haradian council treats as a standing diplomatic offense. Haradian masters trained before the secession retain personal credentials valid in both systems; masters trained or elevated since hold only Sôd credentials. Trade routes ignore the political break; merchants and masters cross the frontier all the time; the Collegium's non-recognition and the trade economy's practical recognition produce constant friction.

## Influenced but Independent

### The Northern Fertile Region

[[place-bethuargn|Bethûa]], [[place-okharisrgn|Okháris]], and [[place-takheperurgn|Ta'Kheperu]] were never conquered by Vylaria; none belongs to the Magnum Collegium. All three have, however, over the past two centuries developed guild structures that visibly borrow from the Vylarian model—multi-tier ranks, formal examinations, registered seals, standing inter-guild councils, the procedural conventions of charter and discipline. The borrowing was not enforced from outside; it was adopted by the local trades themselves, often deliberately, as a way of making their members' credentials legible to the Vylarian trading partners on whom much of the regional economy depends.

The **Ta'Kheperan houses** ([[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]], [[affiliation-pernuw|Per'Nuw]], and the others) are religiously framed—trades organized as _houses_ (_per-_) with charter from the Per-Aá, religious affiliation with a patron deity, and formal initiations conducted in temple rite. Their administrative procedures, however, are unmistakably Collegium-derived: the master-journeyman-apprentice ranks, the registered seals, the examined elevation, the inter-house councils. A master smith of [[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]] is, by his elevation, an initiate of Ptah and Horus as well as a craftsman of bronze; the elevation is a religious rite as much as a professional one, but the _procedure_ of the elevation could be lifted with minor changes from a Vylarian provincial chapter's manual. The Per-Aá's charter is, however, the underlying authority. Houses without a current charter can be summarily dissolved (a rare but consequential occurrence; the last major dissolution was the Per-Khentj of the Pearl-Divers, dissolved seventy years ago after a doctrinal dispute with the Temple of Hâpi).

**Bethûa** has matriarchal trade-houses with formal authority comparable to Vylarian collegia; the matriarchs of the major houses sit as a standing council that handles inter-house matters in a manner explicitly modeled on the Collegium's Curia. **Okháris** has a small number of major chartered guilds (the King's Smiths, the River-Pilots, the Royal Goldworkers) operating in a recognizably Vylarian-derived style, alongside a much larger informal craft economy that follows older Xerathian traditions.

Where members of Northern Fertile Region trades come into contact with the Magnum Collegium, the institutions deal with one another as peers, not as parent-and-subordinate. Several formal trade-recognition agreements exist on a per-trade basis (Per'Mesnu and the Vylarian Smiths' federation, for instance, have negotiated credential equivalence for routine work, with master-rank elevations requiring case-by-case examination); the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]], in the twelve years since its founding, has aggressively pursued and concluded considerably broader agreements with the same bodies.

## Independent Traditions

### The Nordlands and Vrystwald

The Nordlands' guild tradition is informal compared to the southern traditions and shows no Collegium influence at all. The northern kingdoms organize trade through _hall-cohorts_ (groups of practitioners attached to a hall or jarl's establishment) and _kin-craft_ (trade transmitted through family lineage). Formal guilds exist in the larger Nordland towns—typically modeled on Aurèldían imports that crossed in pre-conquest centuries, before the Aurèldían guilds themselves became Collegium-affiliated—but their authority is more advisory than regulatory, and the older hall-cohort and kin-craft forms remain dominant in most rural areas.

The notable exception is the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]], which functions as a fully realized continent-class fellowship covering the skald-craft across the entire Nordlands and parts of Vrystwald and Aelwyth. The Circle and the Magnum Collegium have a standing reciprocal hospitality recognition—a skald in Collegium territory is acknowledged as a sworn craftsperson of standing, and the Collegium's chartered bards extend the courtesy in return.

Vrystwald's guild forms are a hybrid of Nordlands kin-craft and the more formal Varokh urban traditions; the Vrystwald cities have guilds in the southern sense, while the forest interior runs on kin-craft and ad-hoc associations. No Collegium presence.

### Vedyara

The Vedyaran guild tradition is the continent's most lineage-bound and is wholly independent of the Collegium. Trades are organized through _kulas_ (extended hereditary families) and _karmakaras_ (working confraternities of practitioners across kulas). A Vedyaran "guild" in the southern sense is more accurately understood as a federation of kulas practicing the same trade. The federation maintains standards and provides representation; the kula provides the apprenticeship, the family name, and the social standing.

The [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] is the most powerful federation of this kind, and many of the smaller trade federations operate under its informal influence. The Magnum Collegium has, for two generations, attempted to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with the Merchant Collective; the Collective has, characteristically, declined every overture. The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] has had no better success but is, by report, planning a fresh round of approaches.

### Dunhara, Khazryn, and the Tribal Regions

The pastoral and tribal regions do not have guilds in the southern sense. Trades are organized through the same lineage and tribal structures that organize everything else. A Dunhari smith is the smith of his tribe; his apprentice is his son or his nephew; his standards are those his father taught him; his recourse in disputes is the chieftain's hall. The institutional form of the guild is incompatible with the tribal organization of production and has, where attempted, failed to take hold.

The Khazryn Confederation has, in its urbanized trading-cities, begun to develop formal guild structures along Vedyaran lines; the development is recent, uneven, and shows no Collegium influence.

### Southern Xerathia and the Far Continents

The Southern Savannahs (including Nyáluba) operate on tribal and lineage lines similar to Dunhara. K'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, and Kalihara each maintain their own indigenous trade-organization forms, varying enormously in formality and scope and described in their respective regional notes. None has any formal relation with the Magnum Collegium.

## Inter-System Relations

Within the Magnum Collegium's territory, guild interoperability is essentially complete—a Tarvénian master can travel to Byzaría or to Heliónis and seal work under his Collegium credentials without further formality, as can a Vylarian provincial scribe relocating to an Aurèldían chapter. The integration is the single largest practical fact about the continent's commercial life.

Across the Collegium's boundary, however, the picture fragments. The Collegium maintains formal trade-recognition agreements with some non-member systems (the Skalds' Circle, several Ta'Kheperan houses on a per-trade basis, a small number of Bethûan trade-houses); it has none with others (the Vedyaran Merchant Collective most prominently). Where no agreement exists, foreign masters operating in Collegium territory require case-by-case examination—a process that ranges from cursory formality (a Per'Mesnu master taking a routine commission in Provènzia) to functionally prohibitive (a Vedyaran kula-trained craftsman attempting the same).

The Haradian schism has introduced a particular complication: post-secession Haradian credentials issued under the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] use Haradian language, Haradian seals, and a deliberately distinct visual identity from the Vylarian Collegium's documents—they are not mistakable for Collegium credentials, and that is the point. Pre-secession Vylarian credentials still held by Haradian masters remain valid in Vylarian territory; Sôd credentials are not recognized at all. Vylarian officers in border territories spend substantial time distinguishing between the two among Haradian visitors, and Haradian masters traveling and working have learned a variety of fine procedural games for presenting the appropriate document at the appropriate moment.

The continent's traders, scholars, and travelers maintain various informal compendia of inter-system equivalencies—a Vedyaran merchant traveling in Collegium territory consults a guidebook explaining which Collegium chapters his Vedyaran federation credentials may be presented to, and on what terms; a Ta'Kheperan house-master planning trade abroad consults equivalents from the other direction—but no formal continent-wide recognition exists, and the Collegium's reach defines, in practice, where credentials simply work and where they have to be argued for.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Charter Revocation Hearing.** The Censores Collegiorum have brought charges against a major Aurèldían guild whose Collegium charter is at risk of revocation; the guild's home crown is loudly committed to its defense; the case is the most politically charged in a generation. The Curia at Magnápolis will rule.
- **The Haradian Master.** A Haradian master holding both pre-secession Vylarian credentials and post-secession [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] credentials has presented himself at a Vylarian provincial chapter for a new commission. The local Magister Provinciarum must rule on which set of credentials applies—and which set of political consequences he is willing to bear.
- **The Apprentice's Crossing.** A skilled apprentice has been formally trained in a Vedyaran kula and now wishes to enter the Magnum Collegium's framework, having moved west on the death of his Vedyaran master. The Collegium's procedures do not accommodate his form of training; the kula's correspondence with the Collegium has produced no movement. The apprentice's skill is real and his prospects are limited.
- **The Charter Revoked (Ta'Kheperu).** The Per-Aá has, in a fit of personal displeasure, revoked the charter of a minor house-guild whose senior master committed a public discourtesy. The dissolution is technically lawful and politically explosive; the house's masters and apprentices are now, by law, unable to practice their trade. The other Ta'Kheperan houses are weighing whether to advocate for restoration. The Magnum Collegium has, through Per'Mesnu intermediaries, quietly inquired whether any of the dispossessed masters might be receptive to Vylarian recruitment.
- **The Recognition Embassy.** A [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] embassy has arrived in a Bethûan capital seeking a formal trade-recognition agreement of the kind the Vylarian Collegium has so far failed to negotiate. The Bethûan matriarchs are interested; the Vylarian observer who has been hastily dispatched to attend the negotiations is not.
