_Khelâthi: Gár-Hálzi—"the House of the Account"_

- **Type:** Religious-institutional treasury network; the Khelâthi temple banking system
- **Region:** [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] (direct presence); the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethûa]] and the kingdom of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]] (through correspondent arrangements with local institutions)
- **Authority:** The senior Khelâthi priesthood, under the formal patronage of the Gar-Aû
- **Founded:** Predates the current dynasty; the institution is older than reliable record

## Overview

Gár-Hálzi is the institutional name for the network of temple-treasuries that constitutes the [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Khelâthi]] banking system—and, by extension, the banking system of the entire Northern Fertile Region of Xerathia. Each major Khelâthi temple of consequence maintains a Gár-Hálzi chapter: a treasury arm whose responsibilities include attesting the weight-pieces that circulate as the empire's currency, holding the great granaries that anchor the grain-credit economy, maintaining the ledgers that record every Khelâthi noble house's commercial affairs, and issuing the **temple chits** that serve as the region's equivalent of paper script.

The institution is _religious_ in form. It is administered by the priesthood, governed by religious authority, and theologically embedded in Khelâthi doctrine—every Gár-Hálzi act is conducted as a form of temple service, every fee is framed as a votive offering, every senior officer is a priest of one of the great gods. This distinguishes Gár-Hálzi structurally from its Vylarian counterpart, the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]], which is a _state_ institution under imperial governance. The functional services are similar (treasury, central bank, ledger-clearing, credit issuance); the institutional logic and political position are fundamentally different.

The distinction matters substantially. The Aerarium Imperii's authority derives from the imperial throne and serves the imperial state; political pressure on it flows through the Dux Bellorum and the imperial court. The Gár-Hálzi's authority derives from the senior priesthood and the great gods; political pressure on it flows through religious channels, theological argument, and the politics of the priestly orders. A Gar-Aû who wished to alter the Gár-Hálzi's practices would need to do so through religious means—and the priesthood holds the Gar-Aû to be semi-divine, but also holds the temples to be the ultimate authority on religious matters, so the relationship is one of mutual deference and occasional substantial tension.

## Structure

The Gár-Hálzi is a _network_ of chapter-treasuries rather than a single centralized institution. Each major temple operates its own chapter, with local autonomy in day-to-day affairs and substantial weight in the institution's central councils.

### The Council of the High Priests (_Zêlget Halzi_)

The Gár-Hálzi's governing body. The high priests of the eight greatest Khelâthi temples (the principal Galezkara, Gar-Ankhet, Khelzuret, Meru-Hotep, Ankh-Setun, and three lesser but anciently-established temples) constitute the Council, which meets four times a year at the great temple complex in the imperial capital and at any other moment when crisis demands. The Council sets the canonical inter-metal ratios (the gold-silver-copper conversion rates), reaffirms the attestation standards for weight-pieces, issues the major doctrinal rulings on commercial-religious questions, and approves new correspondent arrangements with foreign institutions.

### The Ilmy'Zâ Halzi (the "Overseer of the Account")

The Gár-Hálzi's chief officer. Appointed by the Council from among the high priests, serves for life or until religious retirement. The current Ilmy'Zâ Halzi is **Setmery-Khâ**, a high priest of Galezkara of substantial scholarly reputation and twenty years' service at the office. The Ilmy'Zâ Halzi administers the network's central affairs, coordinates between chapter-temples, and represents the Gár-Hálzi to the Gar-Aû's court when religious-political matters require it.

### Chapter-Treasuries

Each major Khelâthi temple maintains its own Gár-Hálzi chapter. The chapter is administered by a **Hem-Hâti** ("Servant of the Account")—a senior priest assigned to the treasury function, working under the local high priest's authority but with substantial day-to-day autonomy. Each chapter holds its own strongroom, its own granary, its own ledgers, and its own staff of clerks, assayers, and scribes. Chapter-treasuries coordinate with one another through standing protocols for inter-chapter clearing; a chit issued at Galezkara is honored at Gar-Ankhet because the two chapters maintain clearing accounts with each other, and the Galezkara chapter eventually reimburses Gar-Ankhet through the broader Gár-Hálzi network.

### The Ilmy'Qelt Weglat (the "Scribe of the Weights")

The Gár-Hálzi's discipline arm; senior priests trained in metallurgy and assay who inspect the weight-pieces in circulation, audit the chapter-temples' attestation practices, and investigate accusations of counterfeiting. Smaller in number than the Aerarium's Censores (perhaps thirty across the empire) but extremely well-trained. The Ilmy'Qelt Weglat hold ritual seniority comparable to a Chapter Hem-Hâti and answer only to the Council.

## Operations

### Attestation of the Weight-Pieces

The Gár-Hálzi's core economic function is the **attestation of weight-pieces**—the certification that a given piece of copper, silver, or gold meets the canonical standard for its denomination. The attesting temple stamps its seal into the piece during the production process at a [[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]] smithy; the piece then circulates as authenticated currency.

The Gár-Hálzi maintains reference weights (the "true weights of the gods," kept in the most sacred area of the central temple complex) against which any disputed piece can be verified. Pieces that prove substandard on assay are destroyed and the responsible smithy investigated; pieces that prove acceptable receive a fresh attestation if needed.

### The Granaries and Grain-Credit Ledgers

Every chapter-temple maintains a great granary—a vast clay-walled silo complex holding wheat, barley, and other staples—that serves as the local grain bank. Depositors bring grain at harvest and receive credit in their temple account, drawing against it through the year for daily rations or for conversion to silver-piece payment at the posted exchange rate. The grain-credit economy runs in parallel to the metal-piece economy and accounts for a substantial fraction of total Khelâthi commerce.

The granaries also serve as **strategic reserves** against famine. In years when the river-flood fails or harvest disappoints, the Gar-Aû can call upon the temple granaries to release grain into the markets at controlled prices. This emergency authority is theologically grounded—the grain belongs to the gods, the temples are the gods' stewards, the Gar-Aû speaks for the gods—but is in practice an act of substantial state intervention that the priesthood occasionally resists.

### Issuance of Temple Chits

For transactions too large to carry as physical metal, the Gár-Hálzi issues **temple chits**—clay-sealed tablets (for small chits) or sealed papyrus letters (for large ones), bearing the issuing chapter's seal, the value (in silver or gold gezan), the recipient's name or "to bearer," and a registered serial mark. Functionally equivalent to a Vylarian traveler's note; theologically framed as a temple service rather than a commercial product.

The chits are honored at any Gár-Hálzi chapter throughout Aû'Khelâthu and, through correspondent arrangements, at the principal Bethûan trade-houses and Okhárian crown-trade offices. A trader presenting a chit at a chapter or correspondent receives face value minus a small handling fee (typically 1–3%, framed as a votive offering to the receiving temple).

### Issuance Fee

The Gár-Hálzi charges a **2–3% issuance fee** on every chit it produces, framed not as a commercial commission but as a votive offering to the gods. The fee flows into the temple's general endowment, supporting the priesthood, the granaries' maintenance, the temple complexes' upkeep, and the institution's broader operations. Across the network this constitutes a substantial revenue stream—comparable in scale to the Aerarium Imperii's own 2% issuance fee on Vylarian traveler's notes, though smaller in absolute terms because the Northern Fertile Region's commercial economy is smaller than the Vylarian commonwealth's.

### Foreign Currency Exchange

The Gár-Hálzi chapters at the trading-port cities (most notably Gar-Ankhet and the lesser delta cities) operate currency-exchange services for foreign traders. Vylarian Aurion and Argentus pieces are accepted, weighed, assayed for purity (the Vylarian standard is well-known to the Gár-Hálzi assayers; a Vylarian-attested coin passes assay quickly), and exchanged for Khelâthi weight-pieces at the canonical conversion rates minus a 3–5% spread. The reverse exchange is also available: a Khelâthi merchant departing for Heliónis or the Vylarian coast can convert silver pieces to Vylarian coin at the same spread.

Foreign hack-metal—pieces of unattested origin—is accepted at a larger discount (typically 5–10%), reflecting the assay friction and the practical need to re-mint the metal into attested pieces before re-circulating it.

## Notable Members and Officers

- **Ilmy'Zâ Halzi Setmery-Khâ**—current chief officer of the Gár-Hálzi; high priest of Galezkara; widely regarded as the most consequential non-political figure in the Northern Fertile Region's commerce.
- **The Council of the High Priests**—eight seats; meets quarterly at the imperial capital; comprises the high priests of the eight greatest temples.
- **Various Hem-Hâti**—chapter-treasury administrators; each a senior priest of substantial local standing.

## Relations

- **The Gar-Aû's court**—patron and ultimate religious-political authority. The relationship is one of mutual deference and occasional tension: the Gar-Aû is semi-divine but cannot lightly override the priesthood's judgment on temple affairs; the priesthood depends on the Gar-Aû's patronage and protection but maintains the institution's structural autonomy. The Council and the Gar-Aû's court coordinate through standing protocols and through the Gar-Aû's chief religious officer.
- **The [[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]] smithy network**—operational partner. Per'Mesnu smiths produce the weight-pieces under Gár-Hálzi supervision; the two institutions coordinate constantly on metallurgical standards, smithy certification, and the suppression of counterfeit operations.
- **The [[affiliation-pernuw|Gar'Nuw]] hunters' guild** and other Khelâthi guilds—peer Khelâthi institutions; receive deposits, hold accounts, draw chits as needed. The relationship is correct and bureaucratic.
- **The [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|The Bethûan Trade-Houses]]**—Bethûan commercial counterparts. The principal Bethûan trade-houses maintain correspondent accounts with specific Gár-Hálzi chapter-temples (most often at Galezkara, Gar-Ankhet, and Khelzuret), through which they conduct credit business in Khelâthi currency without needing to physically transport metal between the territories. The arrangements are private and vary by relationship.
- **The [[affiliation-crwntrdskhrs|The Crown Trades of Okháris]]**—Okhárian commercial counterparts. Similar correspondent arrangements with Gár-Hálzi chapter-temples, but on a smaller scale than the Bethûan trade-houses given Okháris's smaller commercial economy.
- **The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]**—Vylarian institutional counterpart. The two institutions do _not_ maintain formal correspondent relationships (their currencies are different, their political frameworks are different, their institutional logics are different). They coexist as parallel systems with the changer profession bridging them at the trading ports. Senior officers of the two institutions are aware of each other professionally and occasionally exchange formal courtesies but conduct no shared business.
- **The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s Bayt al-Khazînah**—Haradian institutional counterpart, similar in role to the Aerarium Imperii but for the post-secession Haradian system. The Gár-Hálzi has no formal arrangement with the Bayt; Haradian merchants in Aû'Khelâthu transact in metal at the changer spread, the same as Vylarian merchants.

## Identifying Marks

The Gár-Hálzi's seal—a stylized falcon (the bird of Horus) standing on a balance-scale with the sun-disc above—is struck into every attested weight-piece, every issued temple chit, every official correspondence. Gár-Hálzi officers wear the white-and-gold formal vestments of the senior priesthood, with the falcon-and-balance pendant at the throat distinguishing them from other priestly ranks. The Ilmy'Zâ Halzi wears the great pectoral of the Account, kept at the central temple between holders.

Temple chits are produced on distinctive papyrus stock (the watermark, when held against light, shows the Gár-Hálzi falcon), with the issuing chapter's seal in red wax, the recipient's name (or "to bearer") inscribed in formal hieratic, the value spelled in both silver and gold gezan equivalents, and a registered serial mark.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Failed Attestation.** A batch of recently-issued silver gezan pieces has, on independent assay, been found to be substantially debased—perhaps 20% below the canonical silver content. The pieces bear the legitimate seal of the chapter at Khelzuret; if the seal is genuine, the chapter's attesting smithy has been compromised; if the seal is forged, the counterfeit operation is unprecedentedly sophisticated. The Ilmy'Qelt Weglat are investigating quietly; the political consequences of either finding will be substantial.
- **The Granary Crisis.** The annual river-flood has failed by a substantial margin for the third consecutive year; the Gár-Hálzi's strategic grain reserves are running low; the Gar-Aû's court is demanding accelerated release into the markets to suppress prices; the priesthood is reluctant to deplete the reserves further. The Council of the High Priests is meeting in emergency session; the political consequences of the Council's ruling—whether to honor the Gar-Aû's demand or to refuse it—will reshape religious-state relations for a generation.
- **The Stolen Chit.** A temple chit for 2,000 silver gezan—a substantial sum, drawn on the great chapter at Gar-Ankhet for a Vylarian merchant's commercial deal—has been stolen in transit. The thief's identity is unknown; the chit will, when presented, be honored by any Gár-Hálzi chapter or correspondent. The chapter must decide whether to formally revoke the chit (which would damage the system's reliability and embarrass the issuing chapter) or to honor it on first presentation while the investigation continues (which would mean paying out a substantial sum to a thief). The Vylarian merchant who originally bought the chit is, of course, demanding immediate replacement.
- **The Correspondent Default.** A major Bethûan trade-house has fallen into commercial distress and is now unable to honor its correspondent obligations to its associated Gár-Hálzi chapter at Galezkara. The chapter's Hem-Hâti must decide whether to extend additional credit (hoping the trade-house recovers), to formally call the correspondent arrangement (which would collapse the trade-house and disrupt commerce across Bethûa), or to negotiate a quiet restructuring that preserves face for both sides while addressing the underlying problem.
- **The Vylarian Probe.** The Quaestor Imperii of the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]] has, through diplomatic intermediaries, made discreet inquiries about whether the Gár-Hálzi might be open to formal correspondent arrangements with the Vylarian system. The proposal would be commercially attractive (reducing the changer spread, accelerating cross-border commerce) but theologically delicate (treating the Aerarium as a peer institution). The Council is debating; the priesthood is divided; the Gar-Aû's court is watching to see whether the proposal threatens the institutional separation that has served the empire well for centuries.
- **The Counterfeit Wave.** A coordinated counterfeiting operation has produced a substantial volume of false copper gezan pieces circulating in the markets of Galezkara and Gar-Ankhet. The pieces are good enough to pass casual inspection but fail assay at the temple balance. Several chapter-treasuries have been reluctantly accepting the counterfeit pieces at face value to preserve market confidence; the Ilmy'Qelt Weglat's investigation is closing in on a suspected smithy, but the suspected smith is the cousin of a powerful selat official whose protection complicates prosecution.
