- **Type:** Overview note describing the guild system as a continent-spanning phenomenon
- **Region:** All settled regions of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]], [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]], and trading enclaves elsewhere; form and authority vary substantially by region

## Overview

The Guilds of Thalorna describes the institution by which trades are organized across the settled lands of [[place-worldthlrn|The World of Thalorna]]. Wherever the population of a city has reached the density at which specialized trades can sustain full-time practice—and wherever a regional authority has emerged that finds it useful to deal with practitioners as a group rather than individually—guilds have, sooner or later, formed. They vary enormously across the continent in formality, in legal authority, in social prestige, in size, and in the kinds of trades they cover; what they share is the underlying function. A guild is the body that defines who is qualified to practice a trade, that maintains the standards of that practice, that trains the next generation, that adjudicates disputes between practitioners and between practitioners and clients, and that speaks for the trade in its dealings with the authorities and the public.

The single most consequential fact about the continent's guild map is [[affiliation-tevran|The Tevran Maldor]]—the imperial Vylarian "guild of guilds"—and the territory across which its authority runs. The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], all five Aureldian kingdoms, and the [[place-heladrgn|Hellád Region]] have, for three centuries, organized their guild life as a single integrated federation under the Tevran's umbrella; this single system covers more than half of the settled [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] and the bulk of the continent's commercial output. Outside the Tevran's reach the picture fragments: the [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]] (the [[place-bethuargn|Bethua Region]], the [[place-okharisrgn|Okharis Region]], the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]]) shows the Tevran's influence on form without being part of its structure; the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] was a member territory until its violent twelve-years-past secession and now operates its own institution, the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]]—built deliberately on Taruzelian terms, in the Taruzeli tongue, and refusing the Vylarian forms; the rest of the continent—the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands Region]], [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]], the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]], the Khazryn and Dunhara steppes, the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik Continent]], the [[place-isrncntnnt|Iseron Continent]], the [[place-arnscntnnt|Aurionis Continent]], the [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara Continent]], and the southern Zerathian regions—runs on guild traditions that owe nothing to [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] at all.

A character in any sizeable Thalornan city who reports being "affiliated with the Local Foo Guild" is reporting a relationship that is meaningful but whose particular meaning depends entirely on what trade, what city, and crucially what side of the Tevran's reach he stands on. The cooper's guild in a Bizarian port is, structurally, the same kind of body as the cooper's guild in a Tarvénian one and the cooper's guild in a Vylarian provincial city—all three roll up to Orzara, all three issue Tevran-recognized credentials, all three's masters can travel and seal work across the whole Vylarian-influenced map. None of those bodies remotely resembles the cooper's guild in a Nordheim hall-town, the cooper's _kulra_ in a Vedyaran janapoda capital, or the cooper's _per-_ in Au'Khelathu. This note describes the common functions of the guild system, the Tevran Maldor's sphere, and the principal independent traditions outside it.

## Common Functions

Across the continent's guild traditions, the guild generally performs some or all of the following:

- **Licensing.** Determines who is qualified to practice the trade. Licensing may be formal (with examinations and chartered authority) or informal (with peer recognition and social pressure).
- **Standards.** Sets the standards of quality, the conventions of measurement and pricing, and the warranties that members' work carries.
- **Training.** Operates the apprenticeship system through which new practitioners enter the trade. The apprenticeship form varies: master-apprentice contracts in the western tradition, family-lineage transmission in much of Vedyara, hall-cohort schooling in the Nordlands, examined initiation in Au'Khelathu.
- **Arbitration.** Adjudicates disputes between members, between members and clients, and (in some regions) between members and the broader public.
- **Representation.** Speaks for the trade in dealings with the municipal authorities, the regional lord or council, and (in matters of trade policy) the realm-level government.
- **Mutual aid.** Provides for members in distress—injury, age, the deaths of practitioners leaving dependents—through a common fund maintained by member dues.
- **Ritual life.** Most guilds have associated religious observances, often tied to a patron deity (the smiths to [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]] or [[lore-psaqarudty|Saq'aru]]; the scribes to [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] or [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'ar]]; the brewers to [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]] or [[lore-mahajayadty|Mahajaya]]; etc.). The ritual life is rarely the guild's primary function but is often the matrix in which the more practical functions are conducted.

## The Tevran Maldor and Its Sphere

The single most consequential institution shaping the continent's guild map is the Tevran Maldor—the chartered umbrella body to which every guild in its member territory must belong, which holds authority over guild charters, standards, discipline, and inter-guild jurisdiction. Founded by [Arzanas]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the second Orzas of the Restoration house ([[lore-arzanasedict|The Edicts of Arzanas]]), at the close of the empire's western expansion three centuries ago, the Tevran began as an instrument of imperial governance: a way of absorbing diverse local guild traditions into a single legible system that the empire could regulate, tax, and depend on.

What is striking is what the Tevran has become _since_ the empire's political grip on most of those territories slackened. Today, only Vylaria proper remains under direct imperial governance; [[place-helionis|Helionis]] is a province on paper and practically independent. The five Aureldian kingdoms have reasserted their sovereignty, the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] runs its own affairs, and (twelve years ago, violently) Taruzel seceded from the entire imperial system. And yet the Tevran Maldor continues to operate across Vylaria, Helionis, the [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia Region]], and the [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]]—_because the smaller polities benefit from participation more than they would benefit from withdrawal_. The Tevran has become, in effect, a cross-realm institution: imperial in origin, imperial in central leadership, but operating today as the post-imperial commonwealth's shared trade infrastructure rather than as a tool of imperial enforcement.

The reason the kingdoms stay is straightforward: the Tevran provides what they could not provide for themselves. A Tarvénian artisan's work is valued at a Tarvénian price unless and until that artisan can sell into a market larger than the [[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Kingdom of Tarvénia]]. The Tevran's mutual-recognition arrangements mean that a Tarvénian guild master can travel to the [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]], Bizaria, Helionis, or anywhere in Vylaria and seal work as a master without re-examination. He can hire apprentices, take commissions, register his seal, and operate as a full member of the trade. The kingdoms individually are too small to negotiate such reciprocal arrangements with every other polity; the Tevran does it for them at scale. The cost—submission to Orzara-centered governance of the trade system, plus the quiet 2% issuance fee that flows to [[affiliation-tarmex|The Tarmeks Orzan]], the imperial treasury, every time anyone in the commonwealth buys a traveler's note—is judged generation after generation as worth paying.

A guild operating in any Tevran territory holds its charter by Tevran recognition. A master's seal is registered with the Tevran. An apprentice is examined by procedures the Tevran has standardized. A jurisdictional dispute between guilds is adjudicated by the [Council of the Tevran]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} at Orzara. A guildmaster whose conduct has escaped his home guild's discipline can be summoned before the _rozimoi_ (auditors) of the Tevran. The teeth are real and exercised; revocation of a guild's charter has, in living memory, ended the existence of two minor guilds outright.

### Vylaria and Helionis (The Imperial Core)

The Tevran operates here within a fully imperial framework. All trades in the four Vylarian provinces and in the Heliónite city-states are organized as **tevrani**—bodies chartered through the Tevran Maldor's machinery and standing in the imperial courts, with substantial regulatory authority delegated from the imperial throne. A Vylarian tevran is less a trade body than a small chartered jurisdiction within the imperial structure; [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]] is the most consequential example. Membership confers legal privileges (tax exemptions in some cases, court representation, access to imperial procurement) and corresponding obligations including imperial taxation administered by [[affiliation-tolvan|The Tolvan]]. The system is highly bureaucratized—seven-year apprenticeships, formal examinations, registered seals, substantial archives—and the most internally factionalized of the Tevran's regional manifestations.

### The Aureldian Kingdoms (Post-Imperial Members)

Tarvénia, the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provenzia]], the [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Kingdom of Élavendre]], [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], and [[affiliation-calypsa|Kalipsa]] have reasserted political sovereignty over the past two centuries, but all five remain full members of the Tevran Maldor. Their crowns set their own tax rates, run their own legal systems, and maintain their own armies; what they participate in through the Tevran is the trade infrastructure—mutual recognition of guild credentials, access to the Tarmeks-backed banking system (via [[affiliation-danvar|The Danvar]]'s local chapterhouses), inter-territory jurisdictional adjudication, common standards. Aureldian guilds hold Tevran charters on the same terms as Vylarian or Heliónite ones; their masters sit on the Council of the Tevran at Orzara alongside imperial members; [[affiliation-crtgrphrsgldbyzrnlg|The Cartographers' Guild of the Bizarian League]] and [[affiliation-prvnzthtrclctv|The Provenzia Theater Collective]] of Provenzia exemplify the pattern of locally-grounded guilds operating within the cross-realm framework.

The arrangement has chronic frictions. _Rozimoi_ of the Tevran traveling in Aureldian kingdoms operate under Tevran authority alone, not under any imperial writ the local crown would recognize; investigations of locally-prominent guildmasters become diplomatic events. The crowns periodically propose modifications to the 2% issuance fee or to Tevran governance arrangements (always declined). Reformist factions in some kingdoms argue for withdrawal; conservative interests (the great merchant families, the established guilds, the banking houses) defend continued participation. The arrangement holds because the alternative—building credentialing and banking infrastructure from scratch and negotiating reciprocity treaties bilaterally with every other commonwealth polity—is impossibly more expensive.

### Bizaria (Post-Imperial Member)

The Bizarian League similarly retains full Tevran membership while having reasserted political sovereignty. The League's guilds operate in the Vylarian style with relatively little local distinctiveness in form (the strong municipal traditions of the League's cities translated easily into the Tevran's framework long ago and have persisted unchanged). Like the Aureldian crowns, the League pays the 2% issuance fee and accepts Tevran governance of the trade system in exchange for the market access the participation provides.

### Helionis

Helionis is the unusual case: it is a province of the Empire on paper and practically independent in fact, with the city-states keeping an unusual sub-arrangement in which the city-states retain substantial autonomy in how Tevran rulings are administered. The Heliónite political culture—short factional tenures, dense web of city-state competition—makes stable imperial enforcement difficult, and the empire has long accepted a degree of Heliónite institutional creativity that would be unthinkable in Vylaria proper. Helionite guilds answer to the Tevran and to the imperial throne; their actual day-to-day functioning depends substantially on which faction is currently dominant in their city; the formal Tevran structure carries on regardless.

### The Taruzelian Schism

Taruzel was a Tevran member territory for over a century before the secession war twelve years ago—incorporated during the empire's eastern expansion, integrated into both the trade infrastructure and the imperial governance system. The violent break removed Taruzel from _all three_ Vylarian institutions: the Tevran, the imperial governance, and the Tarmeks-backed banking system. This was a substantially deeper withdrawal than the Aureldian kingdoms or Bizaria had ever undertaken; both of those polities had retained Tevran membership and banking participation while throwing off political imperial rule. Taruzel alone left everything.

The Taruzelian guilds were not dismantled. The merchant princes who had led the rebellion had no intention of preserving the Vylarian umbrella beyond the war's closing weeks but they did want to preserve the trade life the umbrella had organized. They constituted the Vezakuni—"the Council of the Guild-Wardens"—in the Taruzeli tongue, under Taruzelian officer titles (Dirabum, Gatitush, Pazasuni, Zuvarum), framed deliberately as a restoration of the pre-conquest Taruzelian compact tradition rather than as any kind of successor to the Vylarian institution. The Vezakuni performs the Tevran's role _and_ (through its [Merazuni]{.name data-type="affiliation"} treasury arm) the Tarmeks's role for the Confederation. Procedural continuity with the old Tevran work is real but is presented as a parallel inheritance, not a borrowing. The Vylarian Tevran has, for twelve years, refused to use the Vezakuni's chosen name and continues to address its correspondence to "Tevran Maldor Taruzel," which the Taruzelian council treats as a standing diplomatic offense. Taruzelian masters trained before the secession retain personal credentials valid in both systems; masters trained or elevated since hold only Vezakuni credentials. Trade routes ignore the political break; merchants and masters cross the frontier all the time; the Tevran's non-recognition and the trade economy's practical recognition produce constant friction.

## Influenced but Independent

### The Northern Fertile Region

[[place-bethuargn|Bethua]] and [[place-okharisrgn|Okharis]] were never conquered by Vylaria, and [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]] was held by a Vylarian army only from 377 to 395 AF ([[lore-vylconquest|the Vylarian Conquest]]); none of the three belongs to the Tevran Maldor. All three have, however, over the past two centuries developed guild structures that visibly borrow from the Vylarian model—multi-tier ranks, formal examinations, registered seals, standing inter-guild councils, the procedural conventions of charter and discipline. The borrowing was not enforced from outside; it was adopted by the local trades themselves, often deliberately, as a way of making their members' credentials legible to the Vylarian trading partners on whom much of the regional economy depends.

The **Khelathi houses** ([[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]], [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]], and the others) are religiously framed—trades organized as _houses_ (_per-_) with charter from the Gar-Au, religious affiliation with a patron deity, and formal initiations conducted in temple rite. Their administrative procedures, however, are unmistakably Tevran-derived: the master-journeyman-apprentice ranks, the registered seals, the examined elevation, the inter-house councils. A master smith of Gar'Melnu is, by his elevation, an initiate of the [[affiliation-psaqaru|Faith of Saq'aru]] and the [[affiliation-gewaatis|Faith of Gewa'atis]] as well as a craftsman of bronze; the elevation is a religious rite as much as a professional one, but the _procedure_ of the elevation could be lifted with minor changes from a Vylarian provincial chapter's manual. The Gar-Au's charter is, however, the underlying authority. Houses without a current charter can be summarily dissolved (a rare but consequential occurrence; the last major dissolution was [[affiliation-garqeltu|Gar-Qeltu]] of the Pearl-Divers, dissolved seventy years ago after a doctrinal dispute with [[affiliation-lutthubai|Lut-Thuba'i]]).

**Bethua** has matriarchal trade-houses with formal authority comparable to Vylarian tevrani; the matriarchs of the major houses sit as a standing council that handles inter-house matters in a manner explicitly modeled on the Tevran's Council. **Okharis** has a small number of major chartered guilds ([The King's Smiths]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, [The River-Pilots]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, [The Royal Goldworkers]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}) operating in a recognizably Vylarian-derived style, alongside a much larger informal craft economy that follows older Zerathian traditions.

Where members of Northern Fertile Region trades come into contact with the Tevran Maldor, the institutions deal with one another as peers, not as parent-and-subordinate. Several formal trade-recognition agreements exist on a per-trade basis (Gar'Melnu and the Vylarian Smiths' federation, for instance, have negotiated credential equivalence for routine work, with master-rank elevations requiring case-by-case examination); the Vezakuni, in the twelve years since its founding, has aggressively pursued and concluded considerably broader agreements with the same bodies.

## Independent Traditions

### The Nordlands and Vristwald

The Nordlands' guild tradition is informal compared to the southern traditions and shows no Tevran influence at all. The northern kingdoms organize trade through _hall-cohorts_ (groups of practitioners attached to a hall or jarl's establishment) and _kin-craft_ (trade transmitted through family lineage). Formal guilds exist in the larger Nordland towns—typically modeled on Aureldian imports that crossed in pre-conquest centuries, before the Aureldian guilds themselves became Tevran-affiliated—but their authority is more advisory than regulatory, and the older hall-cohort and kin-craft forms remain dominant in most rural areas.

The notable exception is [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|The Skalds' Circle]], which functions as a fully realized continent-class fellowship covering the skald-craft across the entire Nordlands and parts of Vristwald and Aelwyth. The Circle and the Tevran Maldor have a standing reciprocal hospitality recognition—a skald in Tevran territory is acknowledged as a sworn craftsperson of standing, and the Tevran's chartered bards extend the courtesy in return.

Vristwald's guild forms are a hybrid of Nordlands kin-craft and the more formal Varokh urban traditions; the Vristwald cities have guilds in the southern sense, while the forest interior runs on kin-craft and ad-hoc associations. No Tevran presence.

### Vedyara

The Vedyaran guild tradition is the continent's most lineage-bound and is wholly independent of the Tevran. Trades are organized through _kulas_ (extended hereditary families) and _karmakaras_ (working confraternities of practitioners across kulas). A Vedyaran "guild" in the southern sense is more accurately understood as a federation of kulas practicing the same trade. The federation maintains standards and provides representation; the kulra provides the apprenticeship, the family name, and the social standing.

[[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|The Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] is the most powerful federation of this kind, and many of the smaller trade federations operate under its informal influence. The Tevran Maldor has, for two generations, attempted to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with the Merchant Collective; the Collective has, characteristically, declined every overture. The Vezakuni has had no better success but is, by report, planning a fresh round of approaches.

### Dunhara, Khazryn, and the Tribal Regions

The pastoral and tribal regions do not have guilds in the southern sense. Trades are organized through the same lineage and tribal structures that organize everything else. A Dunhari smith is the smith of his tribe; his apprentice is his son or his nephew; his standards are those his father taught him; his recourse in disputes is the chieftain's hall. The institutional form of the guild is incompatible with the tribal organization of production and has, where attempted, failed to take hold.

The [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]] has, in its urbanized trading-cities, begun to develop formal guild structures along Vedyaran lines; the development is recent, uneven, and shows no Tevran influence.

### Southern Zerathia and the Far Continents

The Southern Savannahs (including Nyáluba) operate on tribal and lineage lines similar to Dunhara. K'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, and Kalihara each maintain their own indigenous trade-organization forms, varying enormously in formality and scope and described in their respective regional notes. None has any formal relation with the Tevran Maldor.

## Inter-System Relations

Within the Tevran Maldor's territory, guild interoperability is essentially complete—a Tarvénian master can travel to Bizaria or to Helionis and seal work under his Tevran credentials without further formality, as can a Vylarian provincial scribe relocating to an Aureldian chapter. The integration is the single largest practical fact about the continent's commercial life.

Across the Tevran's boundary, however, the picture fragments. The Tevran maintains formal trade-recognition agreements with some non-member systems (the Skalds' Circle, several Khelathi houses on a per-trade basis, a small number of Bethuan trade-houses); it has none with others (the Vedyaran Merchant Collective most prominently). Where no agreement exists, foreign masters operating in Tevran territory require case-by-case examination—a process that ranges from cursory formality (a Gar'Melnu master taking a routine commission in Provenzia) to functionally prohibitive (a Vedyaran kula-trained craftsman attempting the same).

The Taruzelian schism has introduced a particular complication: post-secession Taruzelian credentials issued under the Vezakuni use Taruzelian language, Taruzelian seals, and a deliberately distinct visual identity from the Vylarian Tevran's documents—they are not mistakable for Tevran credentials, and that is the point. Pre-secession Vylarian credentials still held by Taruzelian masters remain valid in Vylarian territory; Vezakuni credentials are not recognized at all. Vylarian officers in border territories spend substantial time distinguishing between the two among Taruzelian visitors, and Taruzelian masters traveling and working have learned a variety of fine procedural games for presenting the appropriate document at the appropriate moment.

The continent's traders, scholars, and travelers maintain various informal compendia of inter-system equivalencies—a Vedyaran merchant traveling in Tevran territory consults a guidebook explaining which Tevran chapters his Vedyaran federation credentials may be presented to, and on what terms; a Khelathi house-master planning trade abroad consults equivalents from the other direction—but no formal continent-wide recognition exists, and the Tevran's reach defines, in practice, where credentials simply work and where they have to be argued for.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Charter Revocation Hearing.** The _rozimoi_ of the Tevran have brought charges against a major Aureldian guild whose Tevran charter is at risk of revocation; the guild's home crown is loudly committed to its defense; the case is the most politically charged in a generation. The Council at Orzara will rule.
- **The Taruzelian Master.** A Taruzelian master holding both pre-secession Vylarian credentials and post-secession [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] credentials has presented himself at a Vylarian provincial chapter for a new commission. The local _therkalen_ (provincial deputy) must rule on which set of credentials applies—and which set of political consequences he is willing to bear.
- **The Apprentice's Crossing.** A skilled apprentice has been formally trained in a Vedyaran kulra and now wishes to enter the Tevran Maldor's framework, having moved west on the death of his Vedyaran master. The Tevran's procedures do not accommodate his form of training; the kulra's correspondence with the Tevran has produced no movement. The apprentice's skill is real and his prospects are limited.
- **The Charter Revoked (Au'Khelathu).** The Gar-Au has, in a fit of personal displeasure, revoked the charter of a minor house-guild whose senior master committed a public discourtesy. The dissolution is technically lawful and politically explosive; the house's masters and apprentices are now, by law, unable to practice their trade. The other Khelathi houses are weighing whether to advocate for restoration. The Tevran Maldor has, through Gar'Melnu intermediaries, quietly inquired whether any of the dispossessed masters might be receptive to Vylarian recruitment.
- **The Recognition Embassy.** A [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] embassy has arrived in a Bethuan capital seeking a formal trade-recognition agreement of the kind the Vylarian Tevran has so far failed to negotiate. The Bethuan matriarchs are interested; the Vylarian observer who has been hastily dispatched to attend the negotiations is not.
