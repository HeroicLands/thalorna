## Overview

**Lin'Rethu elu Galezkara**, the **Scholars' Circle**, gathers the learned of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]: open-minded scholars, liberal merchants and teachers who find the temple schools too narrow. The circle commissions its own lectures and seminars and funds study the temples do not recognize as worth pursuing. A thinker whose work the temple schools dismiss can find an audience and a stipend here if the circle judges the work has value.

## Character

The invitation arrives on good paper and says only: "A lecture on the tides of the delta, and a question the temple schools would not take." The Scholars' Circle is a circle of the capital's learned: scholars, liberal merchants and teachers who find the temple schools too narrow. Its members meet regularly and commission their own lectures and seminars. A member is Of the Circle, and what the circle does with its funding is for the members to judge.

The circle is open-minded and independent. It funds the study the temples pass over and pays stipends to thinkers whose work the temple schools dismiss. The members include merchants who can afford a scholar's time, teachers who can find the audience and scholars who need both. The test of a proposal is whether the circle judges it to have value, and the judgment is made in company, over a good meal.

If your own work has been turned away by a temple school, bring it here. If it has been turned away because it is wrong, expect to be told so.

## Relations

The circle's quarrel is with the temple schools, which dismiss the work it funds. The circle does not set itself against the temples and does not seek their approval; the temple schools and the circle fill different rooms.

## Commerce and Currency

Say a young scholar with a theory of the river's silt has been turned away from the schools of [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'âr]]. She brings her case to the circle. A merchant who has watched his barges ground in the delta offers a stipend for a season's study, a teacher offers a room, and a lecture is announced. The stipend is paid in silver, as wages are.
