Letters still come to [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] from [[place-orzara|Orzara]] addressed to the "Tevran Maldor Taruzel," and the house they are addressed to answers none of them. That house is the Vezakuni, the Council of the Guild-Wardens, and it has no Vylarian name: it charters [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel's guilds]], registers their masters, examines their apprentices, judges disputes between trades and, through its treasury, runs the banking of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]], all in [[skill-taruzelilng|Taruzeli]] and on Taruzelian forms. The Vylarian guild umbrella, [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]], refuses to acknowledge the name it chose, and the refusal is a standing diplomatic offense that the Taruzelian council protests in public and enjoys in private, because it costs [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] credibility every time it is repeated.

## How It Was Made

When the Confederation won its war against Vylaria, the merchant princes who had led the rebellion faced a problem they had thought through with more care than the Vylarian Tevran's officers later gave them credit for. The Tevran Maldor at Orzara had run Taruzelian trade life for more than a century. It had standardized credentials, charters, dispute resolution and the jurisdiction between guilds, and it set the daily procedures by which Taruzelian masters lived and worked. Walking away from it required something to walk to. The merchant princes meant to keep Vylarian forms not one day longer than necessary, and they meant to keep every bit of the trade life those forms had supported.

Their answer was the Vezakuni. The founding council met in the smoke-blackened ruins of the Besikar compact-house, which Vylarian artillery had destroyed in the last weeks of the war, called the chief _vezakum_ (guild-wardens) of every Taruzelian trade to assemble, and constituted the institution in Taruzeli and on Taruzelian forms. The Vylarian terminology was discarded, not translated. The councilors took their titles—_dirabum_, _gatitush_, _zuvarum_—from the compact tradition of the old Taruzelian merchant princes, which had survived through the imperial period in reduced form as the internal vocabulary of the great trading houses. The procedures were rewritten to fit the Confederation's oligarchic, deliberative politics instead of the empire's top-down form. The new seal was struck without reference to the Tevran's, and the new credentials were issued on parchment of a different cut, in a different hand, sealed in a different wax.

Twelve years on, the Vezakuni is what it set out to be: a Taruzelian institution speaking Taruzeli to Taruzelian guilds and organizing trade life without conceding that the Vylarian Tevran was ever its parent. Its procedures do continue Vylarian practice, and the Vezakuni presents that continuity—sometimes accurately, sometimes more hopefully than the record supports—as a parallel inheritance from the old Taruzelian compacts, not a borrowing from the empire. The institution works, and the Confederation backs it.

## Structure

The Vezakuni's structure mirrors the Confederation's own politics on purpose: oligarchic at the top, deliberative in the middle and distributed at the edges.

- **Dirabum** ("He Who Presides")—the head. There is one, elected by the assembled [Gatitush]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} from among the sitting wardens of the great trades for a seven-year term, renewable only by a vote of reaffirmation. The present Dirabum is **[Qizikush Mugiluni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, a former Vylarian _kelros_ (guild master) of substantial standing who went over to the secession early and was rewarded with the founding leadership. He is in his second term, and a third is being quietly canvassed.
- **Gatitush** ("the Sitting")—the standing council of thirty-two seats, one for each recognized trade-category. Each seat is held by the senior _vezakum_ of its trade, elected by that trade's masters in conclave for a five-year term. The council sits in continuous session at Besikar and holds four full convocations a year.
- **[Pazasuni]{.name data-type="affiliation"}** ("Overseers")—the discipline arm of eight officers, who inspect, investigate charter violations and prosecute disciplinary cases before the Vezakuni. They differ from the Vylarian _rozimoi_ (auditors) in two ways that matter: they cannot prosecute on their own initiative without a referral from the council, and their findings can be appealed to a full convocation of the Vezakuni. The senior officer is the **Chief Overseer**, at present **[Pazasuya Lataruni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, counted the most procedurally rigorous of the Vezakuni's officers.
- **Kubasum** ("the Counter")—the treasurer and keeper of the Vezakuni's common fund _and_ of the Confederation's parallel banking system, the **[Merazuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}** ("the Company of the Ledger"), described under _The Kubasum's Office_ below.
- **Zuvarum** ("City Deputies")—the provincial deputies, one for each major Taruzelian state, who administer the Vezakuni's work in their cities and report to the Dirabum through the Vezakuni. There are five, one for each of the Confederation's city-states.

## Authority and Practice

Within Taruzel the Vezakuni's authority equals the Vylarian Tevran's within the empire. A guild must be chartered, a master registered and an apprentice examined, and the Gatitush adjudicates disputes between guilds. The Confederation backs its enforcement as firmly as the empire backs the Tevran's.

In practice the Vezakuni does five things differently from its imperial predecessor, all of them by design:

- **Credentials carried over.** A Taruzelian master who held Vylarian Tevran credentials at the moment of independence keeps them at full standing within the Vezakuni. Requiring a whole generation of masters to sit re-examination would have destroyed the trade life the secession was meant to preserve. In 720 AF the accommodation is twelve years old and is beginning to phase out as those masters retire.
- **Foreign credentials recognized.** The Vezakuni has unilaterally given full recognition to Aureldian and Helladic Tevran credentials. The Vylarian Tevran has not returned the gesture, and the Confederation hopes the practical needs of the trade routes will push the empire toward recognition in fact.
- **Vylarian masters recruited.** The Vezakuni offers expedited Taruzelian credentials to any _kelros_, _rozimos_ or _therkalenoi_ (provincial deputies) who moves to Taruzel. The Vylarian Tevran understands the offer as a quiet poaching operation; several mid-career officers have accepted it, to substantial resentment in Orzara. By Vezakuni protocol a Vylarian officer who relocates gives up his Vylarian titles and takes Taruzelian ones.
- **The [Recovered Compacts]{.name data-type="lore"}.** A chartered Vezakuni program researches, documents and re-establishes Taruzelian trade traditions that were absorbed, suppressed or assimilated under Vylarian rule. Some of these traditions are old, survivals from the centuries before the conquest, when each Taruzelian city's merchant princes kept their own compact-houses and trade between the cities ran on compact-treaties negotiated among them. Others are creative reconstructions whose claims to ancient pedigree nobody in the Vezakuni cares to examine. The Confederation's federal council funds the program, which turns out a steady run of ceremonial restorations that the Vylarian Tevran calls historical fabrication. The Vezakuni is unconcerned with what the Tevran calls them.
- **Rulings by vote.** The Vylarian Tevran's Council rules under the authority of its Tevraks. A ruling of the Gatitush needs a deliberative majority of the councilors present and is issued in the council's name; the Dirabum presides and may speak last, but he does not rule.

## The Kubasum's Office

The first crisis of the secession was financial, not military. For more than a century Taruzel's commerce had run on paper script issued by moneylenders of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] and cleared, in the end, through [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]], the Vylarian treasury at Orzara, and within weeks of the war's outbreak those clearing accounts were frozen. Taruzelian moneylenders woke one morning unable to honor their own script, merchants found the notes in their strongboxes unredeemable, and the harbor cities on which the Confederation's wealth depended faced commercial collapse before the first major engagement was fought.

Taruzel's break was therefore unusually deep. The [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldian kingdoms]], over the past two centuries, threw off imperial governance and kept the Tevran Maldor and the Tarmeks-backed banking system, because they could not afford to leave them. Taruzel's war forced it out of all three institutions at once: the Tevran Maldor suspended Taruzelian guild standing, the Danvar revoked the chapter affiliation of Taruzelian moneylenders, and the Tarmeks froze the clearing accounts. The Confederation had to build replacements for all three from nothing, and the Vezakuni is the institution that grew to provide them, combining the Tevran's guild umbrella, the Danvar's banking role and a treasury arm of its own.

That treasury arm is the **Merazuni**, the Company of the Ledger, run as the Kubasum's office within the Vezakar at Besikar. It is the Taruzelian counterpart of the Tarmeks: it is the Confederation's treasury and mint: it strikes the coin, accepts bullion deposits, issues master script against its reserves, and clears for the network of Taruzelian moneylender houses that anchors the Confederation's banking. The present Kubasum is **[Yushuduya Jakuduni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, a _thurden_-trained banker whose Taruzelian lineage and twenty years in the Danvar before the secession made her the obvious choice for the founding office; she has held it for twelve years.

The Merazuni is built on the Tarmeks's pattern. The Taruzelian moneylender houses, reorganized as the **[Treasurers' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}**, deposit bullion with it, and so does the Confederation's federal treasury; that bullion is held against the master script. The moneylenders draw retail script against their accounts, and Vezakuni chapterhouse clearing keeps the script good anywhere in Confederation territory. The notes are printed on distinctive paper struck with the Taruzelian eight-pointed star and the issuing moneylender's seal, and they are denominated in **Ardin** and **Soldeni**, the Vylarian units—a deliberate concession to the realities of trade between realms, though Taruzel has never had a strong tradition of coining gold. The Taruzelian Solden coin is slightly underweight by the Vylarian standard, and the discount is acknowledged in cross-border dealings and not advertised (see [[lore-trzlncrncy|Taruzelian Currency]]).

The Merazuni's reserves are small by the Tarmeks's measure, because the Confederation has nothing like the bullion that three centuries of imperial accumulation stored at Orzara. They are sufficient for the Confederation's commercial volume, and the federal council periodically authorizes additions drawn from confiscated Vylarian-loyalist property and from the war indemnities Vylaria continues to refuse to pay. The Merazuni's strongroom at Besikar is guarded jointly by the Kubasum's office and a permanent detachment of Confederation marines.

### Cross-Border Script

Neither institution recognizes the other's paper. A merchant who presents Merazuni script at a Vylarian Tevran chapterhouse is told politely that the chapter cannot honor it, and a merchant who presents Tarmeks script at a Vezakuni chapterhouse hears the same. Merchants who cross the border regularly keep accounts with both; those who cross only now and then accept the substantial spread of converting through specie, physical Ardin or Solden.

Script is the most constant point of friction in twelve years of post-secession commerce. Several Heliónite moneylender houses, working in territory that is Vylarian but commercially bound up with Taruzel, have made large fortunes as clearing intermediaries: they accept both institutions' script and convert between them through their own accounts with both. These houses stand formally on the Tarmeks's books and informally on the Merazuni's. Both institutions treat the arrangement as an irregularity to be tolerated for as long as a working cross-border script agreement remains politically impossible.

## Notable Members

Dirabum Qizikush Mugiluni
: the founding chief, who left Vylarian Tevran service in the second year of the secession war. He is both Dirabum and the public face of the schism, and for twelve years he has refused every Vylarian letter that addresses him by his former Vylarian title.

Chief Overseer Pazasuya Lataruni
: the senior discipline officer and the most rigorous of the Vezakuni's procedural officers. The Taruzelian commercial elite quietly fears her, and several of its members are the subjects of investigations she is in no hurry to conclude.

Kubasum Yushuduya Jakuduni
: head of the Merazuni and the Vezakuni's chief financial officer. She is commonly held to be the second most powerful figure in the institution after the Dirabum, and her decisions touch the Confederation's daily commerce more directly than any other Taruzelian official's.

The thirty-two seat-holders of the Gatitush
: the Taruzelian guildmasters whose elevation to the council gives the Vezakuni its working authority.

## Relations

The Vylarian [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]]
: unrecognized in both directions. The Tevran refuses the Vezakuni's Taruzelian name, and the Vezakuni refuses any communication that uses the Vylarian "Tevran Maldor Taruzel." Practical business passes through intermediaries, usually Helladic merchants of standing, who carry messages neither side will admit to having sent. The arrangement is awkward and weighed down with personal history, because many senior officers on both sides served together before the war.

The Aureldian and Helladic Tevran chapters
: formally beyond the Vezakuni's reach under the Vylarian Tevran's structure. The Vezakuni has nonetheless built working personal contacts with several of their _therkalenoi_ through quiet diplomatic effort. Orzara has not approved those contacts, and they are conducted accordingly.

The Confederation's federal council
: the Vezakuni's patron and ultimate authority. The tie is closer and more political than the Vylarian Tevran's tie to the imperial throne: the federal council intervenes in Vezakuni decisions more often than the empire intervenes in the Tevran's, and the Dirabum is in practice accountable to a coalition of merchant princes whose interests do not always match the institution's stated mission.

The guild systems outside Vylaria
: the Vezakuni has pursued recognition agreements with every major independent tradition, on terms generally kinder to the foreign party than the Vylarian Tevran ever offered. With [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] the talks on per-trade equivalence are in their middle stage; with [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] the talks on desert and frontier work are in their early stage; with [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|the Bethuan trade-houses]] full recognition was concluded last year; and with [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|the Skalds' Circle]] it has a reciprocal hospitality recognition. [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|The Vedyaran Merchant Collective]] has declined every overture from the Vezakuni, as it has declined every overture from the Vylarian Tevran.

The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]], above all [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|the Crimson Ledger]]
: the Vezakuni has formal anti-corruption protocols and an informal admission that the Crimson Ledger's financial reach into the Taruzelian guild economy is substantial. The Pazasuni have brought cases against guild members with Ledger ties, and most have ended in censure, not in a charter revoked. The Vezakuni's reformist faction is losing patience with that pattern.

## Identifying Marks

Every charter, ruling and credential the Vezakuni issues carries its seal: a stylized open ledger over a coiled rope and an anchor, crowned with the Confederation's eight-pointed star. The distance from the Vylarian seal, a wreathed open book over a crossed measuring-rule and quill, is deliberate, and the ledger, rope and anchor speak to Taruzel's maritime trade instead of the empire's academic tradition.

At formal sessions the members of the Gatitush wear a deep crimson stole embroidered at the hem with the eight-pointed star; outside session they wear their own trades' marks. The Pazasuni wear a saffron-yellow mantle with the silver mark of the open eye, an inversion of the Vylarian _rozimoi_' black mantle chosen so that no one can mistake one for the other. The Dirabum wears the **great gold pectoral of the Vezakuni**, struck for the institution at its founding and worn by every Dirabum since.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Disputed Title.** For the eighth month running, the official correspondence of a Vylarian _therkalenoi_ has addressed the Dirabum as "Tevraks Taruzelicus." The Dirabum has complained formally to the Confederation's federal council, which is preparing a public diplomatic protest. The Vylarian Tevran's leadership knows the wording is a calculated insult ordered by a faction at the imperial court, and the open question is whether the empire will quietly correct the practice or escalate.
- **The Recovered Compact.** The Recovered Compacts program has chartered a new trade-guild for a craft that Taruzelian scholars say was practiced openly before the Vylarian conquest and suppressed afterward; Vylarian scholarship holds the trade largely invented for political ends. The new guild's masters have begun seeking work outside Taruzel, and their credentials are about to be tested for the first time. The Vylarian Tevran's answer, recognition or refusal, will set the precedent for every Recovered Compact that follows.
- **The Defected Rozimos.** Vezakuni agents have quietly approached a Vylarian _rozimos_ on inspection in southern Aureldia with an offer of relocation, full Taruzelian credentials among the Pazasuni and a substantial settlement. He has not refused. Tevran and Vezakuni officers will both read his next inspection report closely, because it will show which way he is leaning.
- **The Joint Investigation.** A trade scandal of smuggled goods, falsified credentials and at least three murders has crossed the Vylarian–Taruzelian border so thoroughly that neither institution can investigate it without the other. Qizikush Mugiluni and the Tevran's Tevraks, **[Marcus Velerianus]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, would both privately prefer a joint investigation, and neither can openly ask for one. A back channel is being tried through a Heliónite intermediary, who is being paid by both sides without either knowing.
- **The Reformist Vote.** A coalition of younger councilors of the Gatitush has formally proposed strengthening the Pazasuni's enforcement powers against the merchant-prince factions whose interests the present Pazasuni have visibly declined to pursue. If it passes, it ends the comfortable arrangement that has left the Crimson Ledger's reach into the Taruzelian guilds largely untouched. Pazasuya Lataruni has signaled quiet support, the merchant princes are mobilizing against it, and the vote will be very close.
- **The Heliónite Arbitrage.** Over the past three years one Heliónite moneylender house has become the largest cross-border script intermediary on either side of the Vylarian–Taruzelian frontier, accepting both Tarmeks and Merazuni paper and keeping accounts with both institutions on a scale neither fully appreciates. Its senior partners are in delicate negotiations with both the Imperial _Tarkelos_ (treasury officer) and the Kubasum. The arrangement earns enormous profits and is formally irregular under both institutions' rules. The _rozimoi_ of the Tarmeks have begun preliminary inquiries, and the Pazasuni have not yet been asked to. Disclosure could end the arrangement, damage both treasuries' political positions and ruin the house; silence carries its own risk if either institution's auditors notice on their own.
