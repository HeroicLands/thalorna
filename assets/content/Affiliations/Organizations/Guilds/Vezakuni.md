# terran_analog: the Vylarian tongue and institutions are the tongue of the Roman Empire.

Letters still come to Besîkar from [[place-orzara|Ôrzâra]] addressed to the "Tevran Maldor Tarûzel," and the house they are addressed to answers none of them. That house is the Vezâkûnî, the Council of the Guild-Wardens, and it has no Vylarian name: it charters Tarûzel's guilds, registers their masters, examines their apprentices, judges disputes between trades and, through its treasury, runs the Confederation's banking, all in Tarûzeli and on Tarûzelian forms. The Vylarian [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]] refuses to acknowledge the name it chose, and the refusal is a standing diplomatic offense that the Tarûzelian council protests in public and enjoys in private, because it costs Vylaria credibility every time it is repeated.

- **Tarûzeli name:** Vezâkûnî, "the Company of the Warding", the Council of the Guild-Wardens; a guild-warden is a _vezâkum_
- **Kind:** the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Tarûzelian States]]' chartered guild umbrella, which took over the Tevran Maldor's Tarûzelian operations
- **Seat:** the **Vezâkar** ("where the warding is kept", the House of the Wardens) at [[affiliation-besikar|Besîkar]], in the converted hall of the old compact-house of Besîkar's merchant princes. The choice is deliberate: it puts the institution on ground sacred to the pre-conquest Tarûzelian commercial tradition and in no building the empire built.
- **Founded:** twelve years ago, by act of the Confederation's founding council, in the closing weeks of the War of Independence

## How It Was Made

When the Confederation won its war against Vylaria, the merchant princes who had led the rebellion faced a problem they had thought through with more care than the Vylarian Tevran's officers later gave them credit for. The Tevran Maldor at Ôrzâra had run Tarûzelian trade life for more than a century. It had standardized credentials, charters, dispute resolution and the jurisdiction between guilds, and it set the daily procedures by which Tarûzelian masters lived and worked. Walking away from it required something to walk to. The merchant princes meant to keep Vylarian forms not one day longer than necessary, and they meant to keep every bit of the trade life those forms had supported.

Their answer was the Vezâkûnî. The founding council met in the smoke-blackened ruins of the Besîkar compact-house, which Vylarian artillery had destroyed in the last weeks of the war, called the chief _vezâkum_ (guild-wardens) of every Tarûzelian trade to assemble, and constituted the institution in Tarûzeli and on Tarûzelian forms. The Vylarian terminology was discarded, not translated. The councilors took their titles—_dirâbum_, _gatîtûsh_, _zuvârum_—from the compact tradition of the old Tarûzelian merchant princes, which had survived through the imperial period in reduced form as the internal vocabulary of the great trading houses. The procedures were rewritten to fit the Confederation's oligarchic, deliberative politics instead of the empire's top-down form. The new seal was struck without reference to the Tevran's, and the new credentials were issued on parchment of a different cut, in a different hand, sealed in a different wax.

Twelve years on, the Vezâkûnî is what it set out to be: a Tarûzelian institution speaking Tarûzeli to Tarûzelian guilds and organizing trade life without conceding that the Vylarian Tevran was ever its parent. Its procedures do continue Vylarian practice, and the Vezâkûnî presents that continuity—sometimes accurately, sometimes more hopefully than the record supports—as a parallel inheritance from the old Tarûzelian compacts, not a borrowing from the empire. The institution works, and the Confederation backs it.

## Structure

The Vezâkûnî's structure mirrors the Confederation's own politics on purpose: oligarchic at the top, deliberative in the middle and distributed at the edges.

- **Dirâbum** ("He Who Presides")—the head. There is one, elected by the assembled Gatîtûsh from among the sitting wardens of the great trades for a seven-year term, renewable only by a vote of reaffirmation. The present Dirâbum is **Qizîkûsh Mugîlûnî**, a former Vylarian Master Magister of substantial standing who went over to the secession early and was rewarded with the founding leadership. He is in his second term, and a third is being quietly canvassed.
- **Gatîtûsh** ("the Sitting")—the standing council of thirty-two seats, one for each recognized trade-category. Each seat is held by the senior _vezâkum_ of its trade, elected by that trade's masters in conclave for a five-year term. The council sits in continuous session at Besîkar and holds four full convocations a year.
- **Pazâsûnî** ("Overseers")—the discipline arm of eight officers, who inspect, investigate charter violations and prosecute disciplinary cases before the Vezâkûnî. They differ from the Vylarian Censors in two ways that matter: they cannot prosecute on their own initiative without a referral from the council, and their findings can be appealed to a full convocation of the Vezâkûnî. The senior officer is the **Chief Overseer**, at present **Pazâsûya Latârûnî**, counted the most procedurally rigorous of the Vezâkûnî's officers.
- **Qubâsum** ("the Counter")—the treasurer and keeper of the Vezâkûnî's common fund _and_ of the Confederation's parallel banking system, the **Merâzûnî** ("the Company of the Ledger"), described under _The Qubâsum's Office_ below.
- **Zuvârum** ("City Deputies")—the provincial deputies, one for each major Tarûzelian state, who administer the Vezâkûnî's work in their cities and report to the Dirâbum through the Vezâkûnî. There are five, one for each of the Confederation's city-states.

## Authority and Practice

Within Tarûzel the Vezâkûnî's authority equals the Vylarian Tevran's within the empire. A guild must be chartered, a master registered and an apprentice examined, and the Gatîtûsh adjudicates disputes between guilds. The Confederation backs its enforcement as firmly as the empire backs the Tevran's.

In practice the Vezâkûnî does five things differently from its imperial predecessor, all of them by design:

- **Credentials carried over.** A Tarûzelian master who held Vylarian Tevran credentials at the moment of independence keeps them at full standing within the Vezâkûnî. Requiring a whole generation of masters to sit re-examination would have destroyed the trade life the secession was meant to preserve. In 720 AF the accommodation is twelve years old and is beginning to phase out as those masters retire.
- **Foreign credentials recognized.** The Vezâkûnî has unilaterally given full recognition to Aurèldían and Helladic Tevran credentials. The Vylarian Tevran has not returned the gesture, and the Confederation hopes the practical needs of the trade routes will push the empire toward recognition in fact.
- **Vylarian masters recruited.** The Vezâkûnî offers expedited Tarûzelian credentials to any Master Magister, Censor or Provincial Magisters who moves to Tarûzel. The Vylarian Tevran understands the offer as a quiet poaching operation; several mid-career officers have accepted it, to substantial resentment in Ôrzâra. By Vezâkûnî protocol a Vylarian officer who relocates gives up his Vylarian titles and takes Tarûzelian ones.
- **The Recovered Compacts.** A chartered Vezâkûnî program researches, documents and re-establishes Tarûzelian trade traditions that were absorbed, suppressed or assimilated under Vylarian rule. Some of these traditions are old, survivals from the centuries before the conquest, when each Tarûzelian city's merchant princes kept their own compact-houses and trade between the cities ran on compact-treaties negotiated among them. Others are creative reconstructions whose claims to ancient pedigree nobody in the Vezâkûnî cares to examine. The Confederation's federal council funds the program, which turns out a steady run of ceremonial restorations that the Vylarian Tevran calls historical fabrication. The Vezâkûnî is unconcerned with what the Tevran calls them.
- **Rulings by vote.** The Vylarian Tevran's Council rules under the authority of its Tevrax. A ruling of the Gatîtûsh needs a deliberative majority of the councilors present and is issued in the council's name; the Dirâbum presides and may speak last, but he does not rule.

## The Qubâsum's Office

The first crisis of the secession was financial, not military. For more than a century Tarûzel's commerce had run on paper script issued by moneylenders of the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]] and cleared, in the end, through the Vylarian [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]] at Ôrzâra, and within weeks of the war's outbreak those clearing accounts were frozen. Tarûzelian moneylenders woke one morning unable to honor their own script, merchants found the notes in their strongboxes unredeemable, and the harbor cities on which the Confederation's wealth depended faced commercial collapse before the first major engagement was fought.

Tarûzel's break was therefore unusually deep. The [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]], over the past two centuries, threw off imperial governance and kept the Tevran Maldor and the Tarmex-backed banking system, because they could not afford to leave them. Tarûzel's war forced it out of all three institutions at once: the Tevran Maldor suspended Tarûzelian guild standing, the Danvar revoked the chapter affiliation of Tarûzelian moneylenders, and the Tarmex froze the clearing accounts. The Confederation had to build replacements for all three from nothing, and the Vezâkûnî is the institution that grew to provide them, combining the Tevran's guild umbrella, the Danvar's banking role and a treasury arm of its own.

That treasury arm is the **Merâzûnî**, the Company of the Ledger, run as the Qubâsum's office within the Vezâkar at Besîkar. It is the Tarûzelian counterpart of the Tarmex: it is the Confederation's treasury and mint: it strikes the coin, accepts bullion deposits, issues master script against its reserves, and clears for the network of Tarûzelian moneylender houses that anchors the Confederation's banking. The present Qubâsum is **Yushûdûya Jakûdûnî**, an Aerean-trained banker whose Tarûzelian lineage and twenty years in the Danvar before the secession made her the obvious choice for the founding office; she has held it for twelve years.

The Merâzûnî is built on the Tarmex's pattern. The Tarûzelian moneylender houses, reorganized as the **Treasurers' Guild**, deposit bullion with it, and so does the Confederation's federal treasury; that bullion is held against the master script. The moneylenders draw retail script against their accounts, and Vezâkûnî chapterhouse clearing keeps the script good anywhere in Confederation territory. The notes are printed on distinctive paper struck with the Tarûzelian eight-pointed star and the issuing moneylender's seal, and they are denominated in **Ard** and **Soldeni**, the Vylarian units—a deliberate concession to the realities of trade between realms, though Tarûzel has never had a strong tradition of coining gold. The Tarûzelian Solden coin is slightly underweight by the Vylarian standard, and the discount is acknowledged in cross-border dealings and not advertised (see [[lore-trzlncrncy|Tarûzelian Currency]]).

The Merâzûnî's reserves are small by the Tarmex's measure, because the Confederation has nothing like the bullion that three centuries of imperial accumulation stored at Ôrzâra. They are sufficient for the Confederation's commercial volume, and the federal council periodically authorizes additions drawn from confiscated Vylarian-loyalist property and from the war indemnities Vylaria continues to refuse to pay. The Merâzûnî's strongroom at Besîkar is guarded jointly by the Qubâsum's office and a permanent detachment of Confederation marines.

### Cross-Border Script

Neither institution recognizes the other's paper. A merchant who presents Merâzûnî script at a Vylarian Tevran chapterhouse is told politely that the chapter cannot honor it, and a merchant who presents Tarmex script at a Vezâkûnî chapterhouse hears the same. Merchants who cross the border regularly keep accounts with both; those who cross only now and then accept the substantial spread of converting through specie, physical Ard or Solden.

Script is the most constant point of friction in twelve years of post-secession commerce. Several Heliónite moneylender houses, working in territory that is Vylarian but commercially bound up with Tarûzel, have made large fortunes as clearing intermediaries: they accept both institutions' script and convert between them through their own accounts with both. These houses stand formally on the Tarmex's books and informally on the Merâzûnî's. Both institutions treat the arrangement as an irregularity to be tolerated for as long as a working cross-border script agreement remains politically impossible.

## Notable Members

- **Dirâbum Qizîkûsh Mugîlûnî**—the founding chief, who left Vylarian Tevran service in the second year of the secession war. He is both Dirâbum and the public face of the schism, and for twelve years he has refused every Vylarian letter that addresses him by his former Vylarian title.
- **Chief Overseer Pazâsûya Latârûnî**—the senior discipline officer and the most rigorous of the Vezâkûnî's procedural officers. The Tarûzelian commercial elite quietly fears her, and several of its members are the subjects of investigations she is in no hurry to conclude.
- **Qubâsum Yushûdûya Jakûdûnî**—head of the Merâzûnî and the Vezâkûnî's chief financial officer. She is commonly held to be the second most powerful figure in the institution after the Dirâbum, and her decisions touch the Confederation's daily commerce more directly than any other Tarûzelian official's.
- The thirty-two seat-holders of the Gatîtûsh—the Tarûzelian guildmasters whose elevation to the council gives the Vezâkûnî its working authority.

## Relations

- **The Vylarian [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]]**—unrecognized in both directions. The Tevran refuses the Vezâkûnî's Tarûzelian name, and the Vezâkûnî refuses any communication that uses the Vylarian "Tevran Maldor Tarûzel." Practical business passes through intermediaries, usually Helladic merchants of standing, who carry messages neither side will admit to having sent. The arrangement is awkward and weighed down with personal history, because many senior officers on both sides served together before the war.
- **The Aurèldían and Helladic Tevran chapters**—formally beyond the Vezâkûnî's reach under the Vylarian Tevran's structure. The Vezâkûnî has nonetheless built working personal contacts with several of their Provincial Magisters through quiet diplomatic effort. Ôrzâra has not approved those contacts, and they are conducted accordingly.
- **The Confederation's federal council**—the Vezâkûnî's patron and ultimate authority. The tie is closer and more political than the Vylarian Tevran's tie to the imperial throne: the federal council intervenes in Vezâkûnî decisions more often than the empire intervenes in the Tevran's, and the Dirâbum is in practice accountable to a coalition of merchant princes whose interests do not always match the institution's stated mission.
- **The guild systems outside Vylaria**—the Vezâkûnî has pursued recognition agreements with every major independent tradition, on terms generally kinder to the foreign party than the Vylarian Tevran ever offered. With [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] the talks on per-trade equivalence are in their middle stage; with [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] the talks on desert and frontier work are in their early stage; with the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Bethûan trade-houses]] full recognition was concluded last year; and with the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] it has a reciprocal hospitality recognition. The Vedyaran [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] has declined every overture from the Vezâkûnî, as it has declined every overture from the Vylarian Tevran.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]], above all the [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]**—the Vezâkûnî has formal anti-corruption protocols and an informal admission that the Crimson Ledger's financial reach into the Tarûzelian guild economy is substantial. The Pazâsûnî have brought cases against guild members with Ledger ties, and most have ended in censure, not in a charter revoked. The Vezâkûnî's reformist faction is losing patience with that pattern.

## Identifying Marks

Every charter, ruling and credential the Vezâkûnî issues carries its seal: a stylized open ledger over a coiled rope and an anchor, crowned with the Confederation's eight-pointed star. The distance from the Vylarian seal, a wreathed open book over a crossed measuring-rule and quill, is deliberate, and the ledger, rope and anchor speak to Tarûzel's maritime trade instead of the empire's academic tradition.

At formal sessions the members of the Gatîtûsh wear a deep crimson stole embroidered at the hem with the eight-pointed star; outside session they wear their own trades' marks. The Pazâsûnî wear a saffron-yellow mantle with the silver mark of the open eye, an inversion of the Vylarian Censors' black mantle chosen so that no one can mistake one for the other. The Dirâbum wears the **great gold pectoral of the Vezâkûnî**, struck for the institution at its founding and worn by every Dirâbum since.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Disputed Title.** For the eighth month running, the official correspondence of a Vylarian Provincial Magisters has addressed the Dirâbum as "Tevrax Tarûzelicus." The Dirâbum has complained formally to the Confederation's federal council, which is preparing a public diplomatic protest. The Vylarian Tevran's leadership knows the wording is a calculated insult ordered by a faction at the imperial court, and the open question is whether the empire will quietly correct the practice or escalate.
- **The Recovered Compact.** The Recovered Compacts program has chartered a new trade-guild for a craft that Tarûzelian scholars say was practiced openly before the Vylarian conquest and suppressed afterward; Vylarian scholarship holds the trade largely invented for political ends. The new guild's masters have begun seeking work outside Tarûzel, and their credentials are about to be tested for the first time. The Vylarian Tevran's answer, recognition or refusal, will set the precedent for every Recovered Compact that follows.
- **The Defected Censor.** Vezâkûnî agents have quietly approached a Vylarian Censor on inspection in southern Aurèldía with an offer of relocation, full Tarûzelian credentials among the Pazâsûnî and a substantial settlement. He has not refused. Tevran and Vezâkûnî officers will both read his next inspection report closely, because it will show which way he is leaning.
- **The Joint Investigation.** A trade scandal of smuggled goods, falsified credentials and at least three murders has crossed the Vylarian–Tarûzelian border so thoroughly that neither institution can investigate it without the other. Qizîkûsh Mugîlûnî and the Tevran's Tevrax, **Marcus Velerianus**, would both privately prefer a joint investigation, and neither can openly ask for one. A back channel is being tried through a Heliónite intermediary, who is being paid by both sides without either knowing.
- **The Reformist Vote.** A coalition of younger councilors of the Gatîtûsh has formally proposed strengthening the Pazâsûnî's enforcement powers against the merchant-prince factions whose interests the present Pazâsûnî have visibly declined to pursue. If it passes, it ends the comfortable arrangement that has left the Crimson Ledger's reach into the Tarûzelian guilds largely untouched. Pazâsûya Latârûnî has signaled quiet support, the merchant princes are mobilizing against it, and the vote will be very close.
- **The Heliónite Arbitrage.** Over the past three years one Heliónite moneylender house has become the largest cross-border script intermediary on either side of the Vylarian–Tarûzelian frontier, accepting both Tarmex and Merâzûnî paper and keeping accounts with both institutions on a scale neither fully appreciates. Its senior partners are in delicate negotiations with both the Imperial Quaestor and the Qubâsum. The arrangement earns enormous profits and is formally irregular under both institutions' rules. The Censors of the Tarmex have begun preliminary inquiries, and the Pazâsûnî have not yet been asked to. Disclosure could end the arrangement, damage both treasuries' political positions and ruin the house; silence carries its own risk if either institution's auditors notice on their own.
