_Vedyaran: Vyāpārī Sangha—"the Merchants' Assembly"_

A senior factor of the **Merchant Collective** gives a newly sponsored entrant the same first lesson, and it is a lesson about what the Collective is not: "We are not a kingdom, we are not a temple, and we do not own the roads. We keep them, and we keep them because every court on them would sooner pay us than do it themselves. Remember that when you are tired of being polite to a toll-keeper."

The Collective is what the five seats of the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Compact]] have instead of a unified state. It is a mercantile federation, known as the **Vyāpārī Sangha** or simply the Collective, and its caravan stations run as far west as [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]] and as far north as the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] frontier. Its five seats are [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]], [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]], [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharālaya]], [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]] and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]], the five polities that sit in its governing body, the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]]. Tradition dates its founding to the [[lore-thirdcmpct|Third Compact]], sworn at the **Mahā-Mela** of 320 AF.

## Overview

The five seats keep separate councils, separate watches and separate courts. Their great houses intermarry until they nearly amount to a single class, and they have never amounted to a single throne. The Collective is the one institution that crosses every border, taxes every road and has a hand in every mine of consequence the Compact reaches: outright where it can hold the ground, by lease where the ground is somebody's by older right, and not at all where a constitution shuts it out.

Your own dealings with it will be small and constant. A seat that wants the silver price at the [[place-slvrgorges|Gorges]] asks the Collective. A seat that wants grain shipped to Rājapur hires a Collective caravan. A seat that wants to fortify a frontier negotiates with the Collective for the steel, and for the loan.

The councils and courts are not weak. The Collective has made itself the connective tissue of Vedyaran life across four centuries, and nothing moves without it. Its founders were three generations of merchant princes working across polities that were then at war with each other, and the polities signed in the generation of [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the steppe host]] in the west and [[lore-conchdoor|the conch-door incursion]] in the east. The Collective's own telling of its founding ends where such tellings end, with the claim that no court or council would unify the subcontinent and the merchants could. [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] has no tradition of unification and the Collective supplied none. What it supplied was a body of standing law that made the question unnecessary, which is the smaller claim and the true one. The third generation dated its work from the year after [[lore-fieldelephnt|the Field of Elephants]], which broke both armies that fought it.

That law is the Compact: standing trade law, arbitration procedure and shared infrastructure that the merchants keep in common, meaning the roads, the caravanserai, the watch posts and the currency standards. The founders also wrote an Assembly of the signatories to renew and amend it. Each signatory faced a choice between refusing and watching its territory grow poorer than its neighbors', and each signed. They have signed every renewal since, and the five who hold seats today are the five that share the [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking system]].

[[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]] never signed and is not kept out. A seat obliges its holder to let Collective factors weigh, assay and lend against the metal crossing its territory, and the **Gold Constitution** vests the weighing in a temple station and the lending in a common treasury that no outside house may reach. The Assembly has wanted a sixth seat, and the gold behind it, for as long as it has kept minutes.

Here is the paradox a new entrant has to learn to live with: the members compete fiercely in every market while dominating every market together. A [[place-suryagarha|Sūryagarha]] silver merchant undercuts a Rājapur silver merchant on every shipment they both bid for, then sits beside him on the standards committee and votes with him against any outside competitor. The discipline holds because the alternative, open competition with merchants outside the Collective, would impoverish them both.

The Collective stands outside the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]]. The Collegium has spent two generations trying to negotiate trade-recognition agreements and the Collective has declined every overture. The Haradian [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] has been refused in the same way.

## Membership and Structure

There are three ways in, and each carries different rights.

- **Kulina houses.** The great mercantile houses (_kulinas_) belong by inheritance. A kulina's vote in the Collective's assemblies carries weight in proportion to his house's contribution to the common fund.
- **Pratisthas.** A new entrant comes in by sponsorship and probation, and votes only on matters concerning his probationary terms. This is where you start.
- **Karmakaras.** The artisan suppliers belong by craft-guild affiliation and take part through their guild's elected representative.

The Collective is governed by the Assembly of the Compact (_Sangha-Sabhā_), which meets twice a year, in the spring at Chandrapur and in the autumn at Vyālendra. Its standing committees sit through the year and carry the administration:

- The **Roads Committee** keeps the caravan infrastructure and adjudicates road-tax disputes.
- The **Standards Committee** sets weights, measures, currency and quality grades.
- The **Foreign Trade Committee** negotiates with non-Vedyaran polities and licenses members to operate beyond the Compact.
- The **Disputes Committee** arbitrates the internal conflicts the parties would rather not take to a ruler's court.

At the apex sits the **High Speaker of the Collective**, elected by the kulinas from among themselves for a seven-year term. The present Speaker is **Suvirya Rāmadāsa**, an elderly silver merchant of Sūryagarha whose tenure is known for caution and for the quiet expansion of the Collective's mining holdings.

## Operations

- **Mining.** The Collective works the copper, silver, tin and lapis of the [[place-slvrgorges|Silver Gorges]] under lease from the hereditary smithing clans who hold the rock, at prices the customs-house at Sūryagarha sees only after the fact. It owns the gem-bearing river-workings of the upper [[place-chandrmahi|Chandramahī]] outright, and Suvarnagiri's gold lies outside its reach altogether. [[being-vshnshvdysgrml|Shilāvīra Vidyāsāgaramandala]] is one of its most respected mine-masters. The mines employ tens of thousands of workers, most of whom do not see the surface for weeks at a time, and the labor terms are a continuing source of friction inside the Collective.
- **Caravan trade.** The long-distance routes between Vedyaran cities and the markets of Amradad by the march road, of Khazryn by the passes, and of the eastern frontier are Collective infrastructure, run by Collective members under Collective standards.
- **Banking and credit.** The Collective issues standardized letters of credit redeemable at any member's house, the closest thing the realm has to a unified currency. The system is the backbone of Vedyaran commerce and the source of the Collective's leverage over the councils and courts themselves, several of which owe it substantial debts.
- **Grading.** A Collective grade-mark on a bolt of silk, a measure of grain or a refined ingot of silver is a reliable warranty across the seats of the Compact and in most western buyers' markets.

## Internal Tensions

- **The labor question.** Reform-minded younger kulinas have proposed substantial improvements to mining labor conditions. The older houses, whose fortunes were built on the present system, resist, and the reformers have not yet found a champion willing to risk his standing.
- **The expansion question.** The Foreign Trade Committee is divided between members who want to push aggressively into Amradad's caravan markets and members who fear retaliation from the [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate]] and prefer the present careful cooperation.
- **The compromised members.** Several houses are known or suspected to hold arrangements with the [[affiliation-twilighths|Twilight House]] or the [[affiliation-adanasreni|Ādānashrenī]] beyond what the Collective's standing rules permit. Investigations have begun and stalled more than once.

## Relations

- The five seats of the Compact are two courts and three councils, and every one is a client, a debtor and an occasional opponent. The relationship is mutual dependence and mutual resentment.
- The [[affiliation-twilighths|Twilight House]] is a major supplier of intelligence. The Collective uses its product to inform pricing and contract negotiations, an arrangement acknowledged at the top and denied below.
- The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]], and particularly the [[affiliation-theashroad|Ash Road]], run desert caravan routes that complement and compete with the Collective's. The two keep an awkward truce, each understanding what the other could do to it.
- The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] has tried to establish a presence in Vedyaran markets and has been quietly frustrated by Collective non-cooperation. The Ordo answers by occasionally licensing competing operators outside the Compact.

## Identifying Marks

Kulina-rank members wear a gold pin at the collar in the shape of a balance-scale resting on a coiled rope, the rope for the caravan and the scale for the standards. Pratistha members wear the same pin in silver. Karmakaras wear no Collective mark beyond their guild's. Senior officers wear additional rank bars below the pin.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Mine Collapse.** A lower working in the Silver Gorges has collapsed, and hundreds are trapped or dead. The Standards Committee finds that the terms of the lease were violated. The kulina responsible is too senior to expel without fracturing the Collective, and the survivors and the families of the dead are organizing for redress that neither the clans' gate nor the crown's customs-house has shown any interest in providing.
- **The Counterfeit Letters.** Collective letters of credit have been forged at scale, redeemed at several member houses, and traced back through a chain that disappears into the [[affiliation-twilighths|Twilight House]]. The Collective must investigate without making the scandal public and without confronting the Twilight House directly.
- **The Reform Petition.** A younger kulina, supported by a coalition of pratisthas and a sympathetic sabhā, formally proposes a labor reform at the Assembly of the Compact. The proposal would cost the older houses substantial profits, and the Speaker's procedural rulings will decide whether it reaches a vote at all.
- **The Crown's Default.** Shikharālaya has defaulted on its Collective loans and offers, in lieu of repayment, a permanent grant of the Silver Gorges. The crown has never established that the rock is the crown's, and the smithing clans already lease it to the Collective over the crown's objection. Accepting turns a quiet arrangement into a claim the clans must answer, and every other seat of the Compact reads it as the precedent it is.
