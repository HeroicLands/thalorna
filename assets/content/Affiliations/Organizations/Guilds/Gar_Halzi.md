_Khelâthi: Gár-Hálzi—"the House of the Account"_

- Type: Religious-institutional treasury network; the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] temple banking system
- Region: [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] (direct presence); the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethûa]] and the kingdom of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]] (through correspondent arrangements with local institutions)
- Authority: The senior Khelâthi priesthood, under the formal patronage of the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Aû]]
- Founded: Before the current dynasty; the institution is older than reliable record

## Overview

Say you are a trader with two thousand silver gezan to move from [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] to [[place-garanlaghet|Gar-Anlaghet]]. You do not load a barge with metal. You walk into the account-house of a great temple, hand over your silver, and walk out with a sealed clay tablet that any **Gár-Hálzi** chapter in the empire will honor. A Gár-Hálzi Lem-Halzi would tell you why it works: "The temples keep accounts with each other. Your tablet is a promise between two houses of the gods, and the chapters settle it between themselves."

Gár-Hálzi is the network of temple-treasuries that forms the banking system of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] and, by extension, of the whole **Northern Fertile Region of Xerathia**. Every major Khelâthi temple keeps a chapter, a treasury arm with four jobs:

- attesting the weight-pieces that circulate as the empire's currency;
- holding the great granaries that anchor the grain-credit economy;
- keeping the ledgers that record every Khelâthi noble house's commercial affairs;
- issuing the temple chits that serve as the region's paper script.

The institution is religious in form. Priests administer it, religious authority governs it, and Khelâthi doctrine holds it in place: every act is temple service, every fee is a votive offering, and every senior officer is a priest of one of the great gods. That is the structural difference from its [[lore-vylarianclt|Vylarian]] counterpart, the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]], which is a state institution under imperial governance. The services are similar, covering treasury, central bank, ledger-clearing and credit issuance, but the logic and the politics differ.

The difference decides who can pressure whom. The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium]]'s authority comes from the imperial throne and serves the imperial state, so pressure on it flows through the **Dux Bellorum** and the imperial court. The Gár-Hálzi's authority comes from the senior priesthood and the great gods, so pressure on it flows through religious channels, theological argument and the politics of the priestly orders. A Gar-Aû who wanted to change Gár-Hálzi practice would have to do it by religious means. The priesthood holds the Gar-Aû to be semi-divine, and it also holds the temples to be the final authority on religious matters, so the relationship is one of mutual deference and occasional serious tension.

## Structure

Gár-Hálzi is a network of chapter-treasuries, not a single centralized bank. Each major temple runs its own chapter, with local autonomy in day-to-day affairs and real weight in the central councils.

### The Council of the High Priests

The **Council of the High Priests** (_Sêdjet Halzi_) is the governing body. It consists of the high priests of the eight greatest Khelâthi temples: the principal temples of Galezkara, Gar-Anlaghet, [[place-khelzuret|Khelzuret]], [[place-amqelulegez|Amqelu-Legez]] and [[place-anlaghzetun|Anlagh-Zetûn]], and three lesser but anciently established ones. It meets four times a year at the great temple complex in the imperial capital, and at any other moment when a crisis demands. The Council:

- sets the canonical inter-metal ratios, the gold-silver-copper conversion rates;
- reaffirms the attestation standards for weight-pieces;
- issues the major doctrinal rulings on commercial-religious questions;
- approves new correspondent arrangements with foreign institutions.

### The Overseer of the Account

The chief officer is the **Ilmy'Zâ Halzi**, the Overseer of the Account. The Council appoints him from among the high priests, and he serves for life or until religious retirement. The present Overseer is [[being-zetmeryukhe2|Zetmeryu-Khelâ]], a high priest of Galezkara with a strong scholarly reputation and twenty years in the office. He administers the network's central affairs, coordinates between chapter-temples and represents the Gár-Hálzi to the Gar-Aû's court when religious-political matters require it.

### Chapter-Treasuries

A Lem-Halzi ("Servant of the Account") runs each chapter. He is a senior priest assigned to the treasury function, working under the local high priest's authority with a good deal of day-to-day autonomy. Each chapter has its own strongroom, granary, ledgers and staff of clerks, assayers and scribes.

Chapters settle with one another through standing protocols for inter-chapter clearing. A chit issued at Galezkara is honored at Gar-Anlaghet because the two chapters keep clearing accounts with each other, and the Galezkara chapter in time reimburses Gar-Anlaghet through the wider network.

### The Scribes of the Weights

The **Ilmy'Qelt Weglat** ("Scribe of the Weights") are the network's discipline arm: senior priests trained in metallurgy and assay. They inspect the weight-pieces in circulation, audit the chapter-temples' attestation practices and investigate accusations of counterfeiting. They number perhaps thirty across the empire, fewer than the Aerarium's Censores but extremely well trained. They hold ritual seniority comparable to a chapter Lem-Halzi and answer only to the Council.

## What the Treasuries Do

### Attesting the Weight-Pieces

The core economic function is attestation: certifying that a piece of copper, silver or gold meets the canonical standard for its denomination. The attesting temple stamps its seal into the piece while it is being made at a [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] smithy, and the piece then circulates as authenticated currency.

Gold and silver pieces are worth the metal in them. Copper pieces are tokens, struck in bronze worth little more than a twentieth of their face, and the Gár-Hálzi alone issues and redeems them. It gives copper only in exchange for silver at face, holds that silver at the issuing chapter against the copper outstanding, and redeems copper for silver at the same face at any chapter on demand. Copper without a seal is bought by weight at the price of the metal, so no quantity of imported copper becomes face value.

The reference weights, the "true weights of the gods," are kept in the most sacred area of the central temple complex, and any disputed piece can be checked against them. A piece that fails assay is destroyed and its smithy investigated. A piece that passes receives a fresh attestation if it needs one.

### Granaries and Grain Credit

Every chapter-temple keeps a great granary, a clay-walled silo complex holding wheat, barley and other staples, and the granary is the local grain bank. Depositors bring grain at harvest and receive credit in their temple account. They draw on it through the year for daily rations, or convert it to silver-piece payment at the posted exchange rate. The grain-credit economy runs beside the metal-piece economy and accounts for a large share of all Khelâthi commerce.

The granaries are also strategic reserves against famine. In a year when the flood fails or the harvest disappoints, the Gar-Aû can call on the temple granaries to release grain into the markets at controlled prices. The theology is direct: the grain belongs to the gods, the temples are the gods' stewards and the Gar-Aû speaks for the gods. In practice it is a heavy act of state intervention, and the priesthood sometimes resists it.

### Temple Chits and the Issuance Fee

For sums too large to carry as metal, the Gár-Hálzi issues temple chits: clay-sealed tablets for small sums and sealed papyrus letters for large ones. Each chit bears the issuing chapter's seal, the value in silver or gold gezan, the recipient's name or "to bearer," and a registered serial mark. A chit is functionally a Vylarian traveler's note, framed as a temple service and not as a commercial product.

Any Gár-Hálzi chapter in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]] honors a chit, and so do the principal [[lore-bethuanclt|Bethûan]] trade-houses and **Okhárian** crown-trade offices through their correspondent arrangements. A trader who presents one at a chapter or correspondent receives its face value less a small handling fee, typically 1–3 percent, framed as a votive offering to the receiving temple.

The chapter that produces a chit charges a 2–3 percent issuance fee, also framed as a votive offering and not a commission. The fee goes into the temple's general endowment, which pays for the priesthood, the granaries, the temple complexes and the institution's wider work. Across the network it is a substantial revenue stream, comparable to the Aerarium Imperii's own 2 percent fee on Vylarian traveler's notes, though smaller in absolute terms because the [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]]'s commerce is smaller than the Vylarian commonwealth's.

### Foreign Exchange

The chapters in the trading-port cities, chiefly Gar-Anlaghet and the lesser delta cities, change money for foreign traders. They accept Vylarian Aurion and Argentus pieces, weigh them, assay them for purity and exchange them for Khelâthi weight-pieces at the canonical rates less a 3–5 percent spread. The Vylarian standard is well known to the assayers, so a Vylarian-attested coin passes assay quickly. The reverse exchange is available too: a Khelâthi merchant leaving for [[place-helionis|Heliónis]] or the Vylarian coast can convert silver pieces to Vylarian coin at the same spread.

Foreign hack-metal, meaning pieces of unattested origin, is taken at a larger discount, typically 5–10 percent. The discount covers the assay work and the need to re-mint the metal into attested pieces before it circulates again.

## Relations

- The Gar-Aû's court is patron and final religious-political authority. The Gar-Aû is semi-divine but cannot lightly override the priesthood's judgment on temple affairs, and the priesthood depends on the Gar-Aû's patronage and protection while holding on to the institution's structural autonomy. The Council and the court coordinate through standing protocols and through the Gar-Aû's chief religious officer.
- The [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] smithy network is the operational partner. Gar'Melnu smiths make the weight-pieces under Gár-Hálzi supervision, and the two institutions coordinate constantly on metallurgical standards, smithy certification and the suppression of counterfeiting.
- The [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] hunters' guild and the other Khelâthi guilds are peer institutions. They deposit with the chapters, hold accounts and draw chits as needed, and the relationship is correct and bureaucratic.
- The [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Bethûan Trade-Houses]] are the Bethûan commercial counterparts. The principal houses keep correspondent accounts with particular chapter-temples, most often those of Galezkara, Gar-Anlaghet and Khelzuret. Through those accounts they do credit business in Khelâthi currency without moving metal between the territories. The arrangements are private and vary by relationship.
- The [[affiliation-crwntrdskhrs|Crown Trades of Okháris]] are the Okhárian counterparts, with similar correspondent arrangements on a smaller scale than the Bethûan houses, because Okháris has a smaller commercial economy.
- The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] is the Vylarian counterpart. The two institutions keep no formal correspondent relationship, since their currencies, political frameworks and institutional logics all differ. They run as parallel systems, and the changer profession bridges them at the trading ports. Senior officers know each other professionally and sometimes exchange formal courtesies, but they conduct no shared business.
- The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s [[lore-hardncrncy|Bayt al-Khazînah]] is the [[lore-haradianclt|Haradian]] counterpart, similar in role to the Aerarium but for the post-secession Haradian system. The Gár-Hálzi has no formal arrangement with the Bayt, and Haradian merchants in Aû'Khelâthu transact in metal at the changer spread, as Vylarian merchants do.

### Notable Officers

- **Ilmy'Zâ** [[being-zetmeryukhe2|Zetmeryu-Khelâ]], the current chief officer and high priest of Galezkara, is widely regarded as the most consequential non-political figure in the Northern Fertile Region's commerce.
- The Council of the High Priests holds eight seats and meets quarterly at the imperial capital.
- The various Lem-Halzi are chapter-treasury administrators, each a senior priest of real local standing.

## Marks and Vestments

The Gár-Hálzi seal shows a stylized falcon, the bird of [[lore-gewaatisdty|Gewaâtis]], standing on a balance-scale with the sun-disc above. It is struck into every attested weight-piece, every issued chit and every item of official correspondence. Officers wear the white-and-gold formal vestments of the senior priesthood, and the falcon-and-balance pendant at the throat distinguishes them from other priestly ranks. The Ilmy'Zâ Halzi wears the great pectoral of the Account, which stays at the central temple between holders.

A chit is written on distinctive papyrus stock whose watermark, held to the light, shows the Gár-Hálzi falcon. It carries the issuing chapter's seal in red wax, the recipient's name (or "to bearer") in formal hieratic, the value spelled out in both silver and gold gezan equivalents, and a registered serial mark.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Failed Attestation.** A batch of silver gezan pieces fails an independent assay and proves substantially debased, perhaps 20 percent below the canonical silver content. The pieces bear the legitimate seal of the chapter at Khelzuret. If the seal is genuine, the chapter's attesting smithy has been compromised; if it is forged, the counterfeiters are unprecedentedly sophisticated. The Ilmy'Qelt Weglat are investigating quietly, and either finding will have heavy political consequences.
- **The Granary Crisis.** The flood has failed badly for the third year running, and the strategic grain reserves are running low. The Gar-Aû's court demands faster release into the markets to hold prices down, and the priesthood is reluctant to drain the reserves further. The Council of the High Priests is in emergency session, and its ruling on whether to honor or refuse the demand will reshape religious-state relations for a generation.
- **The Stolen Chit.** A chit for 2,000 silver gezan, drawn on the great chapter at Gar-Anlaghet for a Vylarian merchant's deal, has been stolen in transit. The thief is unknown, and any chapter or correspondent will honor the chit when it is presented. The chapter must choose between revoking it, which damages the system's reliability and embarrasses the issuer, and honoring it on first presentation while the investigation continues, which pays a large sum to a thief. The Vylarian merchant who bought the chit demands immediate replacement.
- **The Correspondent Default.** A major Bethûan trade-house in commercial distress cannot honor its correspondent obligations to its Gár-Hálzi chapter at Galezkara. The chapter's Lem-Halzi must decide whether to extend more credit and hope the house recovers, to call the arrangement and collapse the house and disrupt commerce across **Bethûa**, or to negotiate a quiet restructuring that spares both sides' face and deals with the underlying problem.
- **The Vylarian Probe.** The Quaestor Imperii of the Aerarium Imperii has made discreet inquiries through diplomatic intermediaries about whether the Gár-Hálzi would consider formal correspondent arrangements with the Vylarian system. The proposal is commercially attractive, since it would narrow the changer spread and speed cross-border commerce, and theologically delicate, since it treats the Aerarium as a peer. The Council is debating, the priesthood is divided, and the Gar-Aû's court is watching to see whether it threatens the institutional separation that has served the empire for centuries.
- **The Counterfeit Wave.** A coordinated operation has put a large volume of false copper gezan pieces into the markets of Galezkara and Gar-Anlaghet. They pass casual inspection and fail assay at the temple balance. Several chapter-treasuries have reluctantly taken them at face value to protect market confidence. The investigation of the Ilmy'Qelt Weglat is closing in on a suspected smithy, but the smith is the cousin of a powerful selat official whose protection complicates prosecution.
