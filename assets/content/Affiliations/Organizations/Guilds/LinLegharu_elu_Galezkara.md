## Overview

**Lin'Legharu elu Galezkara**, the Society of Botanical Scholars, gathers the people of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] who study the properties of plants, from physicians' apprentices to independent researchers. Members meet to compare findings on cultivation, preparation and effect and treat the study as a discipline in its own right. The society spends nearly as much effort defending that claim against the charge that its work is merely trade as it spends on the research.

## Character

The meeting begins, as it always does, with a plant on a table. Tonight it is a root, and a physician's apprentice is explaining how it is cut and dried, and a researcher from the other side of the city is explaining why she thinks the apprentice has the preparation wrong. The Society of Botanical Scholars is an informal society of the people of the capital who study the properties of plants, and it meets regularly so that cultivation, preparation and effect can be argued out in company. A **Scholar of Plants** is anyone who brings findings to the table and takes the questions that follow.

The society is scholarly and defensive. Half its energy goes to the study, and nearly as much goes to defending the study as a discipline and not a trade. The charge that plant-lore is merely commerce stings, because many members sell what they know. A member's standing within the society follows which side of that argument others see them serving.

Come with a specimen and a method. A scholar who arrives with only an opinion is thanked politely and not asked back.

## Relations

The society's membership is its relationship: physicians' apprentices and independent researchers sit at the same table, and the line between a trained healer and a gifted amateur is drawn by the quality of the findings and not by the origin. Outside the table, the society's quarrels are with its own critics, who say plants are a market and not a science.

## Commerce and Currency

The society sells nothing, and defending that is its commerce. Say an apothecary offers to fund a series of meetings in exchange for first sight of the findings. The members debate the offer for an evening. Some see a patron and some see a purchase. The argument over the apothecary's money is the society's larger argument in miniature.
