Letters still come to Tamavar from [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]] addressed to the "Magnum Collegium Haradicum," and the house they are addressed to answers none of them. That house is the Sôd-Naqîrîn, the Council of the Guild-Wardens, and it has no Vylarian name: it charters Harad's guilds, registers their masters, examines their apprentices, judges disputes between trades and, through its treasury, runs the Confederation's banking, all in Haradi and on Haradian forms. The Vylarian [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]] refuses to acknowledge the name it chose, and the refusal is a standing diplomatic offense that the Haradian council protests in public and enjoys in private, because it costs Vylaria credibility every time it is repeated.

- **Haradi name:** Sôd-Naqîrîn, "the Council of the Guild-Wardens"; also called the Sôd
- **Kind:** the [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Confederation of Haradian States]]' chartered guild umbrella, which took over the Magnum Collegium's Haradian operations
- **Seat:** the **Bêt-Naqîrîn** ("the House of the Wardens") at [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]], in the converted hall of the old compact-house of Tamavar's merchant princes. The choice is deliberate: it puts the institution on ground sacred to the pre-conquest Haradian commercial tradition and in no building the empire built.
- **Founded:** twelve years ago, by act of the Confederation's founding council, in the closing weeks of the War of Independence

## How It Was Made

When the Confederation won its war against Vylaria, the merchant princes who had led the rebellion faced a problem they had thought through with more care than the Vylarian Collegium's officers later gave them credit for. The Magnum Collegium at Magnápolis had run Haradian trade life for more than a century. It had standardized credentials, charters, dispute resolution and the jurisdiction between guilds, and it set the daily procedures by which Haradian masters lived and worked. Walking away from it required something to walk to. The merchant princes meant to keep Vylarian forms not one day longer than necessary, and they meant to keep every bit of the trade life those forms had supported.

Their answer was the Sôd-Naqîrîn. The founding council met in the smoke-blackened ruins of the Tamavar compact-house, which Vylarian artillery had destroyed in the last weeks of the war, called the chief naqîrîn (guild-wardens) of every Haradian trade to assemble, and constituted the institution in Haradi and on Haradian forms. The Vylarian terminology was discarded, not translated. The councilors took their titles—_rab-naqîr_, _sôd-rabbânîn_, _adônîm al-mudunîn_—from the compact tradition of the old Haradian merchant princes, which had survived through the imperial period in reduced form as the internal vocabulary of the great trading houses. The procedures were rewritten to fit the Confederation's oligarchic, deliberative politics instead of the empire's top-down form. The new seal was struck without reference to the Collegium's, and the new credentials were issued on parchment of a different cut, in a different hand, sealed in a different wax.

Twelve years on, the Sôd is what it set out to be: a Haradian institution speaking Haradi to Haradian guilds and organizing trade life without conceding that the Vylarian Collegium was ever its parent. Its procedures do continue Vylarian practice, and the Sôd presents that continuity—sometimes accurately, sometimes more hopefully than the record supports—as a parallel inheritance from the old Haradian compacts, not a borrowing from the empire. The institution works, and the Confederation backs it.

## Structure

The Sôd's structure mirrors the Confederation's own politics on purpose: oligarchic at the top, deliberative in the middle and distributed at the edges.

- **Rab-Naqîr** ("Chief of Chiefs")—the head. There is one, elected by the assembled Sôd-Rabbânîn from among the sitting naqîrîn of the great trades for a seven-year term, renewable only by a vote of reaffirmation. The present Rab-Naqîr is **Riyad al-Massoud**, a former Vylarian Master Magister of substantial standing who went over to the secession early and was rewarded with the founding leadership. He is in his second term, and a third is being quietly canvassed.
- **Sôd-Rabbânîn** ("Council of the Great Ones")—the standing council of thirty-two seats, one for each recognized trade-category. Each seat is held by the senior naqîr of its trade, elected by that trade's masters in conclave for a five-year term. The council sits in continuous session at Tamavar and holds four full convocations a year.
- **Pāqîdîn** ("Overseers")—the discipline arm of eight officers, who inspect, investigate charter violations and prosecute disciplinary cases before the Sôd. They differ from the Vylarian Censores in two ways that matter: they cannot prosecute on their own initiative without a referral from the council, and their findings can be appealed to a full convocation of the Sôd. The senior officer is the **Rab-Pāqîd** ("Chief Overseer"), at present **Layla Nazari**, counted the most procedurally rigorous of the Sôd's officers.
- **Gizbar** ("Treasurer")—keeper of the Sôd's common fund _and_ of the Confederation's parallel banking system, the **Bayt al-Khazînah** ("House of the Treasury"), described under _The Gizbar's Office_ below.
- **Adônîm al-Mudunîn** ("Lords of the City-States")—the provincial deputies, one for each major Haradian state, who administer the Sôd's work in their cities and report to the Rab-Naqîr through the Sôd. There are five, one for each of the Confederation's city-states.

## Authority and Practice

Within Harad the Sôd's authority equals the Vylarian Collegium's within the empire. A guild must be chartered, a master registered and an apprentice examined, and the Sôd-Rabbânîn adjudicates disputes between guilds. The Confederation backs its enforcement as firmly as the empire backs the Collegium's.

In practice the Sôd does five things differently from its imperial predecessor, all of them by design:

- **Credentials carried over.** A Haradian master who held Vylarian Collegium credentials at the moment of independence keeps them at full standing within the Sôd. Requiring a whole generation of masters to sit re-examination would have destroyed the trade life the secession was meant to preserve. In 720 AF the accommodation is twelve years old and is beginning to phase out as those masters retire.
- **Foreign credentials recognized.** The Sôd has unilaterally given full recognition to Aurèldían and Helladic Collegium credentials. The Vylarian Collegium has not returned the gesture, and the Confederation hopes the practical needs of the trade routes will push the empire toward recognition in fact.
- **Vylarian masters recruited.** The Sôd offers expedited Haradian credentials to any Master Magister, Censor or Magistri Provinciarum who moves to Harad. The Vylarian Collegium understands the offer as a quiet poaching operation; several mid-career officers have accepted it, to substantial resentment in Magnápolis. By Sôd protocol a Vylarian officer who relocates gives up his Vylarian titles and takes Haradian ones.
- **The Recovered Compacts.** A chartered Sôd program researches, documents and re-establishes Haradian trade traditions that were absorbed, suppressed or assimilated under Vylarian rule. Some of these traditions are old, survivals from the centuries before the conquest, when each Haradian city's merchant princes kept their own compact-houses and trade between the cities ran on compact-treaties negotiated among them. Others are creative reconstructions whose claims to ancient pedigree nobody in the Sôd cares to examine. The Confederation's federal council funds the program, which turns out a steady run of ceremonial restorations that the Vylarian Collegium calls historical fabrication. The Sôd is unconcerned with what the Collegium calls them.
- **Rulings by vote.** The Vylarian Collegium's Curia rules under the authority of its Princeps Collegii. A ruling of the Sôd-Rabbânîn needs a deliberative majority of the councilors present and is issued in the council's name; the Rab-Naqîr presides and may speak last, but he does not rule.

## The Gizbar's Office

The first crisis of the secession was financial, not military. For more than a century Harad's commerce had run on paper script issued by moneylenders of the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]] and cleared, in the end, through the Vylarian [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] at Magnápolis, and within weeks of the war's outbreak those clearing accounts were frozen. Haradian moneylenders woke one morning unable to honor their own script, merchants found the notes in their strongboxes unredeemable, and the harbor cities on which the Confederation's wealth depended faced commercial collapse before the first major engagement was fought.

Harad's break was therefore unusually deep. The [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]], over the past two centuries, threw off imperial governance and kept the Magnum Collegium and the Aerarium-backed banking system, because they could not afford to leave them. Harad's war forced it out of all three institutions at once: the Magnum Collegium suspended Haradian guild standing, the Argentariorum revoked the chapter affiliation of Haradian moneylenders, and the Aerarium froze the clearing accounts. The Confederation had to build replacements for all three from nothing, and the Sôd-Naqîrîn is the institution that grew to provide them, combining the Collegium's guild umbrella, the Argentariorum's banking role and a treasury arm of its own.

That treasury arm is the **Bayt al-Khazînah**, the House of the Treasury, run as the Gizbar's office within the Bêt-Naqîrîn at Tamavar. It is the Haradian counterpart of the Aerarium: it accepts bullion deposits, issues master script against its reserves, and clears for the network of Haradian moneylender houses that anchors the Confederation's banking. The present Gizbar is **Kashnûra Bel-Tarûzî**, an Aerean-trained banker whose Haradian lineage and twenty years in the Argentariorum before the secession made her the obvious choice for the founding office; she has held it for twelve years.

The Bayt is built on the Aerarium's pattern. The Haradian moneylender houses, reorganized as the **Naqîrîn al-Khazînah** (Treasurers' Guild), deposit bullion with it, and so does the Confederation's federal treasury; that bullion is held against the master script. The moneylenders draw retail script against their accounts, and Sôd chapterhouse clearing keeps the script good anywhere in Confederation territory. The notes are printed on distinctive paper struck with the Haradian eight-pointed star and the issuing moneylender's seal, and they are denominated in **Argo** and **Aurions**, the Vylarian units—a deliberate concession to the realities of trade between realms, though Harad has never had a strong tradition of coining gold. The Haradian Aurion coin is slightly underweight by the Vylarian standard, and the discount is acknowledged in cross-border dealings and not advertised (see [[lore-hardncrncy|Haradian Currency]]).

The Bayt's reserves are small by the Aerarium's measure, because the Confederation has nothing like the bullion that three centuries of imperial accumulation stored at Magnápolis. They are sufficient for the Confederation's commercial volume, and the federal council periodically authorizes additions drawn from confiscated Vylarian-loyalist property and from the war indemnities Vylaria continues to refuse to pay. The Bayt's strongroom at Tamavar is guarded jointly by the Gizbar's office and a permanent detachment of Confederation marines.

### Cross-Border Script

Neither institution recognizes the other's paper. A merchant who presents Bayt script at a Vylarian Collegium chapterhouse is told politely that the chapter cannot honor it, and a merchant who presents Aerarium script at a Sôd chapterhouse hears the same. Merchants who cross the border regularly keep accounts with both; those who cross only now and then accept the substantial spread of converting through specie, physical Argo or Aurion.

Script is the most constant point of friction in twelve years of post-secession commerce. Several Heliónite moneylender houses, working in territory that is Vylarian but commercially bound up with Harad, have made large fortunes as clearing intermediaries: they accept both institutions' script and convert between them through their own accounts with both. These houses stand formally on the Aerarium's books and informally on the Bayt's. Both institutions treat the arrangement as an irregularity to be tolerated for as long as a working cross-border script agreement remains politically impossible.

## Notable Members

- **Rab-Naqîr Riyad al-Massoud**—the founding chief, who left Vylarian Collegium service in the second year of the secession war. He is both Rab-Naqîr and the public face of the schism, and for twelve years he has refused every Vylarian letter that addresses him by his former Vylarian title.
- **Rab-Pāqîd Layla Nazari**—the senior discipline officer and the most rigorous of the Sôd's procedural officers. The Haradian commercial elite quietly fears her, and several of its members are the subjects of investigations she is in no hurry to conclude.
- **Gizbar Kashnûra Bel-Tarûzî**—head of the Bayt al-Khazînah and the Sôd's chief financial officer. She is commonly held to be the second most powerful figure in the institution after the Rab-Naqîr, and her decisions touch the Confederation's daily commerce more directly than any other Haradian official's.
- The thirty-two seat-holders of the Sôd-Rabbânîn—the Haradian guildmasters whose elevation to the council gives the Sôd its working authority.

## Relations

- **The Vylarian [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]]**—unrecognized in both directions. The Collegium refuses the Sôd's Haradian name, and the Sôd refuses any communication that uses the Vylarian "Magnum Collegium Haradicum." Practical business passes through intermediaries, usually Helladic merchants of standing, who carry messages neither side will admit to having sent. The arrangement is awkward and weighed down with personal history, because many senior officers on both sides served together before the war.
- **The Aurèldían and Helladic Collegium chapters**—formally beyond the Sôd's reach under the Vylarian Collegium's structure. The Sôd has nonetheless built working personal contacts with several of their Magistri Provinciarum through quiet diplomatic effort. Magnápolis has not approved those contacts, and they are conducted accordingly.
- **The Confederation's federal council**—the Sôd's patron and ultimate authority. The tie is closer and more political than the Vylarian Collegium's tie to the imperial throne: the federal council intervenes in Sôd decisions more often than the empire intervenes in the Collegium's, and the Rab-Naqîr is in practice accountable to a coalition of merchant princes whose interests do not always match the institution's stated mission.
- **The guild systems outside Vylaria**—the Sôd has pursued recognition agreements with every major independent tradition, on terms generally kinder to the foreign party than the Vylarian Collegium ever offered. With [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] the talks on per-trade equivalence are in their middle stage; with [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] the talks on desert and frontier work are in their early stage; with the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Bethûan trade-houses]] full recognition was concluded last year; and with the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] it has a reciprocal hospitality recognition. The Vedyaran [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] has declined every overture from the Sôd, as it has declined every overture from the Vylarian Collegium.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]], above all the [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]**—the Sôd has formal anti-corruption protocols and an informal admission that the Crimson Ledger's financial reach into the Haradian guild economy is substantial. The Pāqîdîn have brought cases against guild members with Ledger ties, and most have ended in censure, not in a charter revoked. The Sôd's reformist faction is losing patience with that pattern.

## Identifying Marks

Every charter, ruling and credential the Sôd issues carries its seal: a stylized open ledger over a coiled rope and an anchor, crowned with the Confederation's eight-pointed star. The distance from the Vylarian seal, a wreathed open book over a crossed measuring-rule and quill, is deliberate, and the ledger, rope and anchor speak to Harad's maritime trade instead of the empire's academic tradition.

At formal sessions the members of the Sôd-Rabbânîn wear a deep crimson stole embroidered at the hem with the eight-pointed star; outside session they wear their own trades' marks. The Pāqîdîn wear a saffron-yellow mantle with the silver mark of the open eye, an inversion of the Vylarian Censores' black mantle chosen so that no one can mistake one for the other. The Rab-Naqîr wears the **great gold pectoral of the Sôd**, struck for the institution at its founding and worn by every Rab-Naqîr since.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Disputed Title.** For the eighth month running, the official correspondence of a Vylarian Magistri Provinciarum has addressed the Rab-Naqîr as "Princeps Haradicus." The Rab-Naqîr has complained formally to the Confederation's federal council, which is preparing a public diplomatic protest. The Vylarian Collegium's leadership knows the wording is a calculated insult ordered by a faction at the imperial court, and the open question is whether the empire will quietly correct the practice or escalate.
- **The Recovered Compact.** The Recovered Compacts program has chartered a new trade-guild for a craft that Haradian scholars say was practiced openly before the Vylarian conquest and suppressed afterward; Vylarian scholarship holds the trade largely invented for political ends. The new guild's masters have begun seeking work outside Harad, and their credentials are about to be tested for the first time. The Vylarian Collegium's answer, recognition or refusal, will set the precedent for every Recovered Compact that follows.
- **The Defected Censor.** Sôd agents have quietly approached a Vylarian Censor on inspection in southern Aurèldía with an offer of relocation, full Haradian credentials among the Pāqîdîn and a substantial settlement. He has not refused. Collegium and Sôd officers will both read his next inspection report closely, because it will show which way he is leaning.
- **The Joint Investigation.** A trade scandal of smuggled goods, falsified credentials and at least three murders has crossed the Vylarian–Haradian border so thoroughly that neither institution can investigate it without the other. Riyad al-Massoud and the Collegium's Princeps Collegii, **Marcus Velerianus**, would both privately prefer a joint investigation, and neither can openly ask for one. A back channel is being tried through a Heliónite intermediary, who is being paid by both sides without either knowing.
- **The Reformist Vote.** A coalition of younger councilors of the Sôd-Rabbânîn has formally proposed strengthening the Pāqîdîn's enforcement powers against the merchant-prince factions whose interests the present Pāqîdîn have visibly declined to pursue. If it passes, it ends the comfortable arrangement that has left the Crimson Ledger's reach into the Haradian guilds largely untouched. Layla Nazari has signaled quiet support, the merchant princes are mobilizing against it, and the vote will be very close.
- **The Heliónite Arbitrage.** Over the past three years one Heliónite moneylender house has become the largest cross-border script intermediary on either side of the Vylarian–Haradian frontier, accepting both Aerarium and Bayt paper and keeping accounts with both institutions on a scale neither fully appreciates. Its senior partners are in delicate negotiations with both the Quaestor Imperii and the Gizbar. The arrangement earns enormous profits and is formally irregular under both institutions' rules. The Censores Aerarii have begun preliminary inquiries, and the Pāqîdîn have not yet been asked to. Disclosure could end the arrangement, damage both treasuries' political positions and ruin the house; silence carries its own risk if either institution's auditors notice on their own.
