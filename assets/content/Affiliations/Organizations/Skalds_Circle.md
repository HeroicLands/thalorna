Nothing a skald recites is written down, because parchment burns in a night and can be forged in an afternoon. An apprentice who will present a gyldra at the next **Skaldating** learns that some day he will be asked to say what was sworn in a hall, and that he will say it as it was said, even if the man who feeds him has stopped listening.

The Skalds' Circle, the Skaldahringr or "Ring of the Skalds," is the fellowship of every sworn skald in the north and the keeper of the north's spoken record. An outsider remembers it for the truth of the recital: a skald's account of an oath, a genealogy or a judgment counts in the Asguardian courts as documentary evidence, and the Circle breaks the ring of any skald who falsifies one.

- Type: pan-regional bardic fellowship
- Region: [[place-nrdlndsrgn|the Nordlands]], all five kingdoms, and [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]] and [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] where Asguardian custom holds
- Founded: tradition holds that the Circle was sworn at the great gathering at [[place-hringstead|Hringstead]], five generations before any present Nordland kingdom was founded

## What a Skald Is For

In the Nordlands a king's reign lasts as long as the songs that recall it. A jarl's deeds are what is sung of him, and an oath sworn in a hall with no skald present is suspect, because no one will be there to recite it in a generation. The Circle keeps the archive that makes this work. The Asguardian tradition distrusts parchment, so the archive lives in trained singers who can recite on demand the genealogies of kin going back ten generations, the standing kennings of every great house, the verse-records of contested judgments at every ting in the last century, and the saga of every campaign large enough to have earned one.

A skald does entertain, but the work is larger than that. A skald is the sworn keeper of his hall's record and his kingdom's history, and the impartial witness whose recitation the courts accept. Say a dispute breaks out over an inheritance, a contested oath, or who owed whom what tribute three winters ago. The skald is summoned, the skald recites, and the recitation binds.

The Circle is a fellowship, not a guild in the southern sense. It sets no prices, settles no quarrels between employers and employed, and licenses no one. It keeps the standards of the craft, the hospitality network that lets any sworn skald travel between halls, and the long memory of verses, traditions and saga-cycles that no single skald can hold alone.

## How a Skald Is Made

A skald is made by a skald. A sitting master picks out a candidate, typically in late childhood, and the apprenticeship runs ten to fifteen years of memorizing, composing and presenting. The master teaches verse, kennings, genealogy, the laws of his kingdom, the histories of all the great houses and the protocols of hall-craft, and the apprentice's life is the master's life until the master judges him fit to compose a first saga.

That first saga is the _gyldra_, a long formal poem delivered before the assembled skalds at the Circle's yearly gathering, the Skaldating. If the assembly accepts it, the master swears the apprentice into the Circle and he is a skald in his own right. If it is rejected, he may try again the following year, and twice more after that. After three rejections the apprenticeship ends without recrimination and without rank.

The Skaldating meets once a year, hosted in rotation by the five Nordland kingdoms and occasionally by Aelwyth or Vrystwald. New apprentices present their gyldra there; the standing repertoire is reviewed and verified by collective recitation; and the assembled body resolves disputes over the accuracy of a contested verse.

The Circle holds four ranks:

- Ring-Broken: a skald convicted of breaking the truth of the recital, whose arm-ring is broken at the next Skaldating. No one recovers from the punishment.
- Apprentice: a master's pupil for ten to fifteen years, entitled to nothing of the Circle's own until his gyldra is accepted.
- Skáld: the sworn singer, entitled to food, shelter and safe passage at any hall, and bound to recite truly even against his own employer.
- Stóraldstjóri: the "saga-master" (_Sögumadr_), a senior skald whose composed sagas the Circle has accepted into its standing repertoire. His peers acclaim him; no one appoints him.

Two offices sit above a sworn Skáld without raising him further. The Hirdskáld is a court skald in formal service to a jarl or king, the most prestigious working position the craft offers. The Lögskáld, the law-skald, specializes in reciting legal precedents and witnessing formal proceedings. Few in number, the Lögskáldar are indispensable to Nordland legal practice.

## The Three Principles

The Circle holds three principles.

The first is _the accuracy of memory_. The craft depends on the literal fidelity of what is preserved, and a verse remembered loosely is a verse lost. The second is _the hospitality of the hall_. Any sworn skald is entitled to food, shelter and safe passage at any hall in the Nordlands, and in exchange he must perform when asked. The third is _the truth of the recital_. A skald summoned to bear witness must recite what he knows accurately, even when the recitation harms his employer.

The third principle has cost skalds their positions and occasionally their lives. The Circle protects them as it can: a skald killed for honest recital becomes the subject of a saga at the next Skaldating, and the saga names the killer in terms that follow his name for generations.

## Who Wears the Ring

A sworn skald wears a silver arm-ring, the _skaldhringr_, forged at the swearing and worn for life. It bears the marks of his master and his master's master, traced back as far as the Circle's records permit. A skald convicted of breaking the truth of the recital has the ring broken in his presence at the next Skaldating, a punishment with no recovery that has been imposed perhaps seven times in living memory. Court skalds pin a brooch in the shape of a raven, [[affiliation-odvar|Ódvar]]'s bird, to the cloak.

The Hirdskáldar at the five courts are known by the sagas they compose more than by their names. The senior sögumenn number perhaps a dozen at any time, scattered across the kingdoms, and their compositions are the standing repertoire taught to every apprentice.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Málalidabandalag]]** and the Circle extend each other formal hospitality and recognition. Signed companies welcome skalds at their fires, and skalds carry company histories, sing the deaths of fallen captains and serve as messengers between the Hall at Hringstead and the scattered companies.
- **The Asguardian priesthood** overlaps with the Circle without answering for it. Many skalds are lay devotees of Ódvar, who is patron of poetry as of war and wisdom, and his priesthood cooperates closely with the Circle. Skalds answer to their masters and to the Circle, never to a priest.
- **The [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Colleges]] of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]** and the Circle respect one another across a cultural distance. The two traditions exchange visiting members regularly and acknowledge each other's craft without claiming to share it.
- **Kings and jarls** are the Circle's clients, employers and, occasionally, the targets of honest recital. The relationship is one of permanent mutual usefulness and occasional sharp friction.

## Disputes a Skald May Carry

- **The Disputed Saga.** Two skalds recite incompatible versions of a recent battle, both with first-hand authority and both with employers who gain from the version recited. The next Skaldating must resolve the dispute. Its resolution decides which version enters the standing repertoire, and which jarl's reputation survives the generation.
- **The Killed Skald.** A skald has been killed at the hall of a powerful jarl after his recitation embarrassed his host. The Circle convenes to compose his memorial saga, and by tradition the saga names the jarl in terms that follow his line for generations. The jarl is preparing to demand that the saga be softened, and the Circle's answer will define what the institution is willing to enforce.
- **The Lost Lineage.** A claimant to a contested throne produces genealogies tracing his line to a royal ancestor seven generations back. The skalds' standing record disagrees. The discrepancy is large enough to matter and small enough to pass for a memorial error. The Lögskáldar are summoned, and their finding decides the succession.
- **The Hidden Apprentice.** A girl has trained in secret under a master who has never acknowledged her publicly. Female skalds are permitted in principle and rare in practice, and the master has broken standing protocol. Her gyldra, when she presents it, is first-rate. The Circle must choose between recognizing her, which condones the master's breach, and refusing her, which loses a talent the craft cannot easily replace.
