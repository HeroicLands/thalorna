- **Type:** Royal-chartered trade guilds; small federation of urban commercial institutions
- **Region:** The kingdom of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]]—based in the three great temple-cities of **Zarhánis**, **Kaljékor**, and **Vuthráka**; activity extends into the surrounding savannah through tribal-clan trade relationships
- **Authority:** Royal charter from the Okháric crown; each charter renewed at the accession of each new king
- **Founded:** The major charters are ancient (predating the current dynasty by several centuries); the federation as a coordinating body is much newer (perhaps a century old)

## Overview

The Crown Trades of Okháris are the small number of major chartered guilds that handle the kingdom's settled-urban commerce. Unlike the dense guild ecology of Vylarian territory or the matriarchal trade-house network of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethûa]], Okhárian commercial life is dominated by a handful of large, royally-chartered institutions concentrated in the three temple-cities. The remainder of Okhárian economic activity—the nomadic-pastoralist herding that sustains most of the population—operates outside any guild structure, organized through tribal-clan relationships and seasonal exchange at the temple-city festivals.

The principal Crown Trades are:

- **The King's Smiths** (chartered at Zarhánis; ancestral metallurgy guild; produces tools, weapons, and the prestige metalwork that flows from Okháris into the broader Northern Fertile Region)
- **The Royal Goldworkers** (chartered at Kaljékor; specialty in fine gold jewelry, ceremonial pieces, and the worked-gold tribute pieces that the Crown sends to neighboring courts)
- **The River-Pilots** (chartered at Vuthráka; commercial-maritime guild that operates the seasonal rivers and the trade routes between the temple-cities and the southern jungle margin; the principal Okhárian-side counterparty for Kheperi traders descending the great river)
- **The Hide-Cutters** (chartered jointly at all three cities; processes the hides, wool, and leather that the pastoral clans bring to the temple-city festivals; coordinates export-grade tanning and finishing)
- **The Spice-Merchants** (chartered at Zarhánis; specializes in the savannah herbs, dried fruits, and specialty preserves that travel best across the Northern Fertile Region's longer trade routes)
- **The Crown Moneylenders** (chartered jointly across the three cities; the kingdom's principal credit institution; operates under direct royal supervision)

Each charter grants the holding guild a defined commercial monopoly (the right to be the _only_ royally-chartered practitioner of its trade in a given temple-city; competing operations either work under the charter as licensees or operate informally outside the guild structure). The charters also impose obligations: substantial annual payments to the Crown, formal representation at the Three-Flame Settlement festivals, supply of specific goods to the royal household at concessionary prices, and a standing duty to extend credit to royal officials traveling on Crown business.

The federation of the Crown Trades—the formal recognition that the chartered guilds act collectively on certain matters—is the most recent of the Northern Fertile Region's commercial coordinating institutions. Where Pér-Háti has operated for centuries and the Bethûan federation for two centuries, the Okhárian federation was only formalized about a century ago, after the Three-Flame Settlement had matured into its current institutional form.

## The Crown Moneylenders and Banking

The **Crown Moneylenders** are the Okhárian institution most analogous to a banking body. Chartered jointly at all three temple-cities, operating under direct royal supervision, the Crown Moneylenders handle the kingdom's credit infrastructure: accepting deposits from merchants and noble households, extending credit, issuing letters of credit honored at any of the three temple-city offices, and maintaining the [[affiliation-perhati|Pér-Háti]] correspondent accounts through which Okhárian commerce reaches the broader Northern Fertile Region.

The Crown Moneylenders are _not_ a banking guild in the Vylarian sense—they do not have the institutional weight or the political position of the Vylarian [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]], and Okháris's smaller commercial economy supports a much smaller credit infrastructure. There are perhaps thirty senior moneylender families operating across the three temple-cities, all royally chartered, all coordinating through the Crown's commerce office, all maintaining at least nominal accounts with the Pér-Háti.

The principal Pér-Háti correspondent relationships are with the chapter at Per-Ankhet (most important; handles the bulk of Okhárian-Kheperi trade) and with the chapter at Khensuret (secondary; handles specialty trades). Direct correspondent arrangements with Wasetkara are rare—the great Kheperi commercial cities prefer to deal with the Bethûan Great Houses, whose volume justifies the correspondent infrastructure.

The Crown Moneylenders' **letters of credit** are honored at any of the three temple-city offices and at the Pér-Háti correspondent chapters in Ta'Kheperu. They are not generally honored in Bethûa (the Bethûan trade-houses prefer to deal with each other or with Pér-Háti directly), in Vylarian territory (no formal Argentariorum recognition), or in Harad. An Okhárian merchant traveling to Bethûa typically converts his letters to Kheperi temple chits at his home moneylender before departing; from Ta'Kheperu the chits can then be exchanged through the Bethûan trade-houses' correspondent network as needed.

## Structure and Governance

The federation of the Crown Trades is governed by the **Council of the Three-Flame Charters**—a body comprising the senior officer of each major chartered guild, meeting twice a year at the temple-city festival schedule and at the Crown's call when commerce-political matters require it. The Council coordinates federation positions on questions affecting multiple charters (royal taxation, foreign trade negotiation, charter-boundary disputes between guilds) and serves as the principal commercial counterparty when the kingdom negotiates with foreign powers.

The Council is chaired by the **Steward of the Trades**—a royal appointment from the chartered moneylender ranks, serving at the Crown's pleasure for a typical term of five to ten years. The current Steward is **Mekhar Saharil**, a senior moneylender of Vuthráka whose moderate temperament and Pér-Háti correspondent relationships have made him acceptable to all three temple-cities and to the Crown.

Each individual charter is administered by its own guildmaster and council—varying by tradition (the King's Smiths use a traditional master-of-masters election; the Royal Goldworkers operate by hereditary council; the River-Pilots by elected captaincy; etc.)—but all charter-level decisions of inter-trade or inter-city consequence flow through the Council of the Three-Flame Charters.

## The Pastoral Hinterland

The Crown Trades' formal reach extends only to the temple-cities themselves and the immediate urban hinterland. The pastoral clans that constitute most of the Okhárian population operate outside the guild structure—moving herds between seasonal grazing lands, exchanging livestock for crafted goods at the seasonal festivals, conducting their internal commerce through tribal-clan customs that have no formal relationship with the chartered trades.

This creates the characteristic Okhárian commercial dynamic: a substantial settled-urban commercial economy (the Crown Trades, the chartered moneylenders, the temple-city marketplaces) sits atop a much larger pastoral economy that is largely outside the formal commercial system. The Crown Trades' commerce with the pastoral clans happens primarily at the festival markets—three times a year, the clans converge on a temple-city, bring herds and wool and leather and the seasonal craft goods of the savannah, and exchange them for the urban guilds' tools, fine cloth, metalwork, and prestige items.

Cash transactions at the festival markets typically use Kheperi copper kite for small exchanges and silver kite for larger ones; clan-to-clan and clan-to-merchant relationships often carry over from one festival to the next as standing arrangements without formal cash settlement. The Crown Moneylenders extend modest credit to clan chieftains for substantial purchases (a new bronze forge for a clan smithy, a year's supply of fine cloth for a clan's matriarch and her women) on the security of next-festival herd delivery.

## Notable Figures

- **Steward Mekhar Saharil**—current chair of the Council of the Three-Flame Charters; senior Crown Moneylender of Vuthráka; moderate political temperament; well-connected with the Pér-Háti chapter at Per-Ankhet.
- **The Master of the King's Smiths**—typically the senior Crown-chartered metalworker; the position carries substantial weight at the Zarhánis court and is currently held by a master named **Belthor Vakhin**, an Okháric of long service and conservative inclinations.
- **The Captain of the River-Pilots**—the senior officer of the Vuthráka-based maritime guild; currently a woman named **Sarena Velthrik**, the first woman to hold the captaincy in three generations; her election was contested and her tenure remains politically delicate.
- **The Grand Master of the Royal Goldworkers**—head of the most prestigious of the chartered trades; the current holder, **Auronak Beslin**, is an old man whose retirement is anticipated within the decade and whose succession is being quietly maneuvered over by several rival families.

## Relations

- **The Okháric Crown**—patron and ultimate authority. The Crown grants the charters, collects substantial revenues from charter dues, and depends on the Crown Moneylenders for both its own treasury services and the kingdom's commercial infrastructure. The relationship is one of mutual dependence with the Crown holding the decisive authority (charters can be revoked, though revocation is rare and consequential).
- **The [[affiliation-perhati|Pér-Háti]]**—principal foreign-banking counterparty. The Crown Moneylenders maintain correspondent accounts with the Pér-Háti chapters at Per-Ankhet and Khensuret; these accounts are the channel through which Okhárian commerce reaches the broader Northern Fertile Region. The relationships are professionally cordial; the Pér-Háti regards Okháris as a junior partner whose commercial scale is small but whose strategic position (controlling the southern rim of the Northern Fertile Region) makes the relationship worth maintaining.
- **The [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|The Bethûan Trade-Houses]]**—Bethûan commercial peer. Modest correspondent relationships exist but are limited; most cross-Bethûan-Okhárian commerce flows through Kheperi intermediaries rather than directly. The two systems have very different structures (Bethûan matriarchal-clan vs. Okhárian royal-charter) which makes direct correspondent integration difficult.
- **The pastoral clans**—the kingdom's larger demographic and the source of the wool, leather, livestock, and craft goods on which the Crown Trades' export economy depends. The relationship is mediated through the festival markets and through the standing courtesy of the clan chieftains who have informal arrangements with specific Crown Moneylenders.
- **Vylarian merchant traffic**—small but present. Vylarian Sea-port traders occasionally make the longer journey south to Okhárian territory for specialty goods (gold work, savannah herbs, hide products); the trade is conducted through a small number of specialist Heliónite merchant captains who maintain relationships with both the Crown Moneylenders and the Pér-Háti correspondent network.
- **The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]**—distant Haradian counterpart. No formal arrangements; Haradian merchants visiting Okháris convert currency through the Crown Moneylenders at the standard foreign-exchange spread.

## Identifying Marks

Each chartered trade maintains its own seal and ceremonial regalia, traditionally tied to the trade's patron deity within the Nkaru'thar pantheon. The King's Smiths wear bronze pendants in the form of a hammer-and-anvil; the Royal Goldworkers wear thin gold torcs with the trade's three-flame mark; the River-Pilots wear blue stoles edged in silver. The Crown Moneylenders wear simpler regalia—a red sash with the Crown's mark—emphasizing their position as royal officers rather than as autonomous craftsmen.

The federation as a whole does not maintain a unified emblem; its identity flows through the trades themselves and through the Steward's office.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Disputed Charter.** A new craft has been petitioning the Crown for a separate charter—the **Glassworkers**, who currently operate under the broader charter of the King's Smiths but whose distinct craft has grown to the point of justifying its own charter. The Smiths are opposing the petition (loss of jurisdiction would reduce their charter's value); the petitioner is well-connected with the Crown's court; the Council of the Three-Flame Charters is split. The Steward must rule, knowing that either decision will offend half of the senior craft establishment.
- **The Festival Default.** A clan chieftain who took substantial credit from a Crown Moneylender at last year's festival has, on this year's arrival, brought far fewer herds than the credit required. The chieftain claims the previous winter was unusually hard; the moneylender suspects the chieftain has been moving herds to a rival's account. The standing custom is that festival credit is honored on personal trust; the dispute will reshape the credit terms available to the pastoral clans for years to come.
- **The Goldworker Succession.** The Grand Master of the Royal Goldworkers is dying; three rival families are positioning their candidates for the succession; the Crown is signaling preferences that would advantage one family over the other two; the Council is being asked to certify the eventual successor, but the certification is meaningless without the Crown's seal. Whichever family wins gains the most prestigious craft-political position in Okháris; the losing families face substantial loss of standing.
- **The River-Pilot Politics.** Captain Sarena Velthrik's election as the first woman captain of the River-Pilots in three generations was contested at the time and remains controversial. A rival faction within the guild is attempting to force a no-confidence vote at the next council meeting; the maneuvering is intricate, involves family alliances stretching back two generations, and threatens to fracture the guild. Sarena is competent and her record in office is strong; her opponents' case is essentially political, but they have the votes if they can hold them together.
- **The Bethûan Approach.** The Bethûan trade-house federation has, through the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|matriarch of House Bera-Sehel]], approached the Council of the Three-Flame Charters with a proposal for substantially expanded direct correspondent arrangements between the two federations—bypassing the Kheperi intermediaries that currently handle most cross-border commerce. The proposal is commercially attractive but politically delicate (would reduce Kheperi Pér-Háti revenues; would create a new institutional axis the Per-Aá's court might find concerning). The Steward is consulting with Pér-Háti correspondents at Per-Ankhet before responding.
