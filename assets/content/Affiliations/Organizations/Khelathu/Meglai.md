## Overview {#overview}

"On a day of proclamation I stand at the river steps from dawn," a Meglai watchman says, "because by noon there is no ground left to stand on." **The Meglai** are the standing guard of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]]: the city watch, fire-watch and gate-wardens of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]. At the capital they are separate from the army, separate from the royal guard that holds the Sacred Island, and separate again from the necropolis guard across the water.

## Who Sits {#who-sits}

The Captain of the Meglai commands the standing city guard of Galezkara and is an officer of [[affiliation-capitalselat|the Capital Selat]], held by a Zabu. The word names two separate bodies. The hereditary hunter-wardens of the upper river, a noble caste whose sacred duty is the ritual hunt of [[affiliation-zeghetnelgu|the Zeghet'Nelgu]], are known as Meglai and hold ancient charters of their own; they are not the city guard. The frontier command at [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]], the Gar-Meglai, carries the same word.

## How It Decides {#how-it-decides}

The Meglai keep the peace by posting. Their posts stand in every district of the capital, the largest by the markets and the harbor where trouble gathers. Their men walk the quarters and the quays, guard the gates of the walled enclosures after dark, chase thieves, break up brawls and double as the fire-watch. Anything touching a temple, a noble house or the crown leaves the civic system, and the Meglai do not follow it onto temple ground.

## The Present {#present}

The Meglai hold the crowd. When the reading of the Measure is proclaimed at [[place-qeltge|Qelt-Ge]], the people of the city gather there before anywhere else, and with the flood failing the whole city waits on the number. A blaze in the crowded lanes of [[place-lutlemu|Lut-Lemu]] can run from workshop to workshop before the Meglai's fire-gangs reach it. With the temple-inquisitors the Meglai hunt the forbidden cults without rest.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

A party meets the Meglai at a post, at a gate after dark or on the quays, and a stranger who breaks the peace is brought before a magistrate's court of the quarter, the local Genzet. Outside the walls, the guard patrols only the river and the canal heads, and out in the desert a traveler answers to nobody.
