## Overview

A guild's certificate says a piece meets the trade's minimum; the name of **Gar-Let'Gerau** promises more. The Let'Gerau are a hereditary line of craftsmen whose family name carries weight in the guild halls of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu]] whatever guild any one member belongs to, and members are expected to hold to standards of workmanship the wider trade often finds inconvenient. A buyer willing to pay for the name expects work that a guild-certified piece alone does not promise, and the lineage's members know it.

## Character

You can hear a Let'Gerau in a guild hall before you see one. Someone puts down a plane, picks up a piece another man called finished, and says what is wrong with it, quietly and in front of everyone. The family holds its members to a standard above the guild's minimum, and the name stands for that standard. The master keeps the line's techniques and answers for work done under the name.

The standard is inconvenient to the wider trade, which prefers a certificate to a conscience. Members accept the inconvenience. It is what the name is for.

## Relations

A member of the line belongs to a guild like anyone else and stays within it; the Let'Gerau are a family, not a rival body. What they carry from guild to guild is the name, and the name carries weight in the guild halls of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu]] whatever guild the member belongs to. A master of any craft in the hall knows what it means when a Let'Gerau stands up at a quality dispute.

## Commerce and Currency

A buyer who asks for Let'Gerau work is asking for more than the guild's certificate promises, and the buyer pays for the difference. The line's members know what the name has cost the buyer and what it costs them to keep it.

So when you commission a piece, say plainly whether you want the certificate or the name. The two are priced apart, and only one of them survives a Let'Gerau's inspection.
