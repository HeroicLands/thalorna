## Overview

Anything taken unweighed is stolen. That is the whole law of **Gar-Gezanu**, the imperial service that collects the Gar-Aû's dues and enforces the law of trade across [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu]], and its assessors apply it at the point of sale or transit, with no regard for the taker's reasons. A weighmaster is therefore a fixture at every market and ford worth taxing, and a merchant who moves goods around the assessors rather than through them is, by the service's own definition, already a thief.

## Character

"Weigh it first" is the whole creed of the Exaction Service, and a weighmaster says it to every carter who drives down to the water with a loaded cart. The service is exacting and literal. Anything taken unweighed counts as stolen, and the rule hears no reasons: not haste, not a hungry mule, not ignorance of how this ford does its business. Its assessors therefore act as enforcers as readily as clerks, and the man holding the scale is the man who decides whether you are a merchant or a thief.

Treat the scale as a gate. Goods go through it, never around it.

## Relations

For most merchants the weighmaster is the face of the throne. Gar-Gezanu collects the Gar-Aû's dues across [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu]], and it does so at the places where goods change hands or cross water, which is why a weighmaster keeps the service's weights at every market and ford worth taxing. A trader who argues with an assessor is arguing with the sovereign's own accounts.

## Commerce and Currency

Say you bring six sacks of barley to a market gate. An assessor weighs them, a clerk enters the weight, and the dues follow from what the scale said. The entry is the part to remember: a weight written down is a weight nobody can dispute later, in your favor or against it.

The business of the service is weighing and taxing goods at the point of sale or in transit, and the sack that missed the scale is the one a court hears about. Carry your goods to the weights, and keep your temper while you do.
