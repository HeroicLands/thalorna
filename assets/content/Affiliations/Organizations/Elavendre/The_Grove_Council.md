## Overview {#overview}

The Grove Council is the oldest of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]'s three native magical institutions—older than the kingdom itself, older than the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]]'s arrival on the continent, older than the conflict with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] that now defines so much of Élavendren political life. Where [[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Bardic Colleges]] preserve song, memory, and the fae-touched arts of voice, and where the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] enforce the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} with sword and patrol, the Grove Council deliberates. It is the body that decides what the forests need, what the fae are owed, and what the kingdom must refuse the Order.

The Council meets at the great groves: ancient circles of standing oaks, holly, and ash that mark the places where the membrane between the mortal world and the fae thins. Each major grove has its own elder—a grove-elder—and the Council itself is the body of all elders gathered, traditionally on the four hinge-days of the year (the equinoxes and solstices). In practice the Council also convenes in emergency when a sacred site is threatened, an Order expedition is suspected, or a working of wild magic has gone so wrong that no one grove can hold the consequences alone.

The Council's authority is not formally chartered. The crown of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]] does not appoint its members, and no royal writ confirms its rulings. Its power is older than charter. The Élavendren rural population—farmers, herders, woodsmen, hedge-witches—treats the Council's word as binding, and the crown long ago learned that a king who crosses a grove-elder is a king who finds his harvests strange, his hunts empty, and his soldiers reluctant to march into forest country.

## Membership and Structure {#who-sits}

There are nineteen seats on the Council, one per recognized great grove. Eighteen are presently filled; the nineteenth (the grove at [[place-sirmenne|Sirmenne]], in [[place-sathvothe|Satvote]] below the Áelendan mountains) has been empty for a generation since its elder vanished into the fae and never returned. By tradition the seat remains open, awaiting either his return or the unambiguous appearance of a successor—neither of which has occurred.

Elders are chosen by their groves through long apprenticeship rather than election. A candidate is identified in childhood, often by visions or fae encounters, and trained from adolescence in herblore, ritual, the reading of weather and water, and the diplomatic protocols of dealing with fae kin. Promotion to elder happens only on the death or retreat of the prior holder, and is confirmed by the rest of the Council gathered. The process can take decades.

Below the elders sit the **grovekeepers** (working clergy attached to lesser groves), the **wandering circles** (itinerant grovekeepers who serve groves without permanent staffs), and the **hedge-affiliates**—village healers, herbalists, and folk-magicians who are recognized as walking in the Council's tradition without holding any formal rank. The hedge-affiliates are the Council's eyes and ears in every Élavendren village.

## The Nineteen Groves

A **great grove** is an old circle of standing oaks, holly or ash in Élavendren country, and each has its elder and its seat. They run from the river woods of the basin to the foothills of the [[place-drenavrspn|Drenavar Spine]]. Eighteen are filled and one is empty.

[[place-lirve|Lirve]]

[[place-auldalon|Áuldalon]]

[[place-lisere|Lísere]]

[[place-sorvanda|Sórvanda]]

[[place-sirmenne|Sirmenne]]
: One seat, in [[place-sathvothe|Satvote]] across the border below the Áelendan mountains: the empty seat.

The Council's groves ignore the frontier. The grove at Sirmenne stands in Provénzia and its seat belongs to the Council all the same.

### Council Groves and Áelendan Groves

Two kinds of grove stand in Élavendre's woods, and the words keep them apart.

A **Council grove** is one of the nineteen. It is a grove-elder's seat, Élavendren, and the crown's subject. Its keeper is a grove-elder in the Council's tradition, wearing a circlet of woven living wood, and no shaman.

An **Áelendan grove** is Old Way ground in the old growth between the river settlements. A shaman or a Tradition Keeper keeps it, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] guard it, so their writ runs inside the kingdom's own forests and not only in the interior. The crown does not own such a grove and does not pretend to. The Council does not own one either, and a grove-elder who treats one as a Council grove is told so by the Wardens.

The quarrel is the clearing. A charter to fell is the crown's to grant, and the license to cut is issued at Liranel. Neither reaches an Áelendan grove. Every new clearing is therefore a negotiation with the nearest tribe, and the Wardens' graduated response is what a license-holder meets when he has not held it. A party can tell the groves apart by who keeps them: a circlet and a leaf-marked wrist mean the Council, a shaman's cairn and a Warden's watch mean the Áelendan.

## Doctrine and Authority {#how-it-decides}

The Council holds three things sacred and inviolable, in order of weight: the Kindred Pact with the fae, the integrity of the great groves, and the right of the land's traditional inhabitants to govern their relationship with both. Everything else—kings, guilds, foreign mages, even the Bardic Colleges' more cosmopolitan ambitions—is negotiable.

Their working doctrine on wild magic is straightforward: magic is not a resource to be regulated and licensed, as the Order claims, but a relationship to be maintained. The Order's project of cataloging, certifying, and confining magical practice strikes the Council as both arrogant and dangerous—arrogant because it presumes the practitioner's authority over the source, dangerous because it severs the relationships that keep wild magic from turning ruinous. This conviction is the source of the Council's bitter, generations-long enmity with the Thordimalon (the Arcane Order).

The Council does not, however, make war, and it commands no force. The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] guard the sacred sites and enforce the Kindred Pact, but they answer to the Áelendan alone and take no writ from the Council. When a matter arises—an Order Zulen (fellowship) requesting access to a sacred site, a village asking for permission to drain a marsh, a fae lord demanding tribute—the Council itself deliberates.

## Relations

**The [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Colleges]]** are the Council's closest ally and most frequent friction. The two institutions share doctrine but compete for influence. Bardic-trained singers and grove-trained keepers draw from the same fae well, and the lines between their arts blur in practice. Tension flares when the Colleges accept Order patronage for some research project, or when the Council overrides a Bardic ruling on a wild-magic question.

**The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]]** hold the Kindred Pact as sacred as the Council does, but they are not the Council's to command: they answer to the Áelendan alone, and their captains take no writ from the grove-elders.

**The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]** is the enemy. The Council has never recognized the Order's authority within Élavendre and never will. Every Order chapterhouse in the kingdom—including the [[affiliation-univperavel|University of Péravel]]—operates over Council objection. The conflict is conducted through legal obstruction in the cities and through more direct measures beyond the city walls.

**The Crown of Élavendre** treats the Council with careful, public deference and quiet private exasperation. The crown needs the Council's tacit cooperation to maintain rural order; the Council needs the crown's protection from the Order. Both sides have learned to live with the other.

## The Araelwen {#present}

The [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]], the sacred thin places of the interior, are the Áelendan's to keep, and the Wardens keep them. The Council's quarrel over them is with the Order's study of them. Every season it petitions the crown against the licenses the Order holds to survey the interior; every season the crown receives the petition with deference and grants the licenses. The Council now presses a narrower point at court: that the guards the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] hires to take its scholars past the margins are hired to break the Pact, and that the crown that permits the hiring shares the breach. The hedge-affiliates bring the Council word of every Order camp in the countryside, and the Council passes it to the Wardens, who take no writ from it and take the word.

## Identifying Marks

Elders wear circlets of woven living wood—different species per grove—that are tended throughout life and burned with the elder at death. Grovekeepers wear simpler unworked bands. Hedge-affiliates wear no formal mark but carry a knotted cord of grove-blessed yarn, often hidden under the clothing. Initiates are tattooed on the inside of the wrist with the leaf-mark of their home grove; the mark is renewed by the elder at each return visit and fades visibly when the bearer has been long away.

## Plot Hooks {#hooks}

- **The Empty Seat.** Signs and portents suggest the lost elder of Sirmenne may at last be returning—or that something wearing his shape is. The Council must decide whether to receive what comes, kill it, or refuse to choose.
- **The Compromised Grove.** A grove-elder has been quietly accepting Order coin in exchange for permission to "study" his grove. The compromise is discovered. The Council must investigate without itself fracturing.
- **The Pact Broken.** A village destroys a fae-touched copse to expand pasture. The fae have responded—children sicken, crops fail, a herd-bull is found drowned in a dry well. The Council must broker terms before the fae's response escalates beyond what mortal authorities can manage.
- **Word to the Wardens.** A hedge-affiliate has seen an Order fellow hiring guards in her village for a survey of the interior, and the Council needs someone to carry the word up the valleys faster than the fellow can travel.
- **The Grove That Is Not Ours.** A license-holder has cut into a grove that a grove-elder believed was the Council's. The Wardens say it is Áelendan ground, and the elder asks a party to learn whose it is before the Wardens act.
- **The Singer's Petition.** A Bardic Master requests a Council ruling that a particular forbidden working—long suppressed—be reauthorized for a single performance. Grant the petition and risk catastrophe; refuse and risk a public schism with the Colleges.
