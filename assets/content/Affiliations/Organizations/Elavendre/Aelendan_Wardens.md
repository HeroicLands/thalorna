## The Áelendan Wardens

Type
: Ancient protective order

Parent people
: [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]

Region
: [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], principally the [[place-drenavrspn|Drenavar Spine]] and deep forests

### Overview

The **Áelendan Wardens** are an ancient order tasked with protecting the [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]]—the sacred thin places—of the mountains and deep forests of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], particularly those connected to the fae. The Wardens patrol the boundary between the settled lowlands and the wild interior, and they take their charge with absolute seriousness. They are the living instrument of the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"}—the ancient compact between the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] and the land itself—and their authority over sacred territory is recognized by both Áelendan custom and the crown of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]].

### Origin and Purpose

The Wardens are as old as the Kindred Pact. Tradition holds that the order was founded at the earliest Pelwar settlement of the Élavendre interior, when the ancestors of the Áelendan entered into sacred agreement with the land, the forests, and the fae beings who dwelled there. The Wardens have protected the sites of that agreement ever since, generation after generation, their membership passing down through family lines and through tribal selection. The order began as the watch set on the fog-hollow at the edge of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]] ([[lore-lowestpoolpct|The Pact at the Lowest Pool]]), and its charge widened to the passes when the Áelendan were given the wood ([[lore-firesnelgan|The Fires on Nelgan]]).

Their purpose is explicitly custodial, not expansionist. The Wardens do not conquer territory, do not pursue enemies beyond the boundaries of sacred ground, and do not involve themselves in political disputes of the lowland kingdom. Their charge is narrow and absolute: to ensure that the sacred sites are not violated, and that the Kindred Pact's terms are upheld.

### Skills and Training

Wardens are drawn from Áelendan tribal communities and trained from youth in a demanding array of disciplines:

Tracking and woodcraft
: Few outsiders can follow a trail through the deep forests; few Wardens cannot

Archery
: The preferred combat discipline, suited to forest and mountain engagements

Survival
: Wardens can live indefinitely in the wilderness, subsisting on what the land provides

Knowledge of sacred geography
: Every Warden knows the locations, histories, and specific protocols of the sites within their patrol territory

Fae lore
: Wardens are trained to recognize fae presence, to navigate fae-touched terrain, and to respond appropriately when fae beings choose to make themselves known

The old tongue
: All Wardens are fluent in [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]], the ancestral Pelwar language

### Doctrine and Response

The Wardens operate by a doctrine of graduated response. Trespassers who stumble into sacred territory by accident are warned once and escorted out. Those who ignore warnings, return after being escorted out, or who desecrate a site are dealt with harshly. The Wardens do not engage in elaborate trials or appeals; the sacred territory is not a place where outside authorities hold sway, and the Wardens' judgment is final within it.

This doctrine has come into particular prominence in recent decades as the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]—operating from its substantial presence at the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} and the Zulen (fellowship) chapterhouses in Élavendre's cities—has increasingly sent agents into rural territory to study and map the fae-touched landscapes. Arcane Order surveyors who venture too deep into the forests sometimes do not return. Research camps are found abandoned, notes scattered, equipment destroyed. The Wardens regard Arcane Order intrusions into sacred territory as violations of the oldest pacts of the land, and they respond accordingly—with warnings first, and with lethal force when warnings are ignored. The Arcane Order publicly deplores these "incidents" as barbarism; privately, it tallies its losses and sends more agents.

### Relationship with the Crown

The kingdom of Élavendre's authorities accept the Wardens' jurisdiction over sacred sites without question. Even the most ambitious Élavendri noble knows better than to challenge a Warden on their own ground. This arrangement is not a concession to necessity but a reflection of respect—the nobility's own cultural memory ties them to the Áelendan and the Kindred Pact, and they understand what the Wardens protect. Royal magistrates will not accept complaints from parties who entered sacred territory and were subsequently "mistreated"; the first question in any such case is whether the complainant had been warned, and the Wardens' answer is authoritative.

### Organization

The Wardens have no central headquarters in the manner of a lowland military order. Each major tribal territory maintains its own complement of Wardens, operating with considerable local autonomy. Coordination across tribal boundaries is handled through seasonal councils and through runners who carry messages between territories. Leadership within a territory falls to the most experienced Wardens, selected by peer recognition rather than formal appointment.

The principal concentrations of Wardens correspond to the most sacred sites:

[[place-drenavrspn|The Drenavar Spine]]
: The central sacred range, with the largest Warden presence

The Silver Forests
: Ancient fae-touched woodland under heavy Warden watch

Mountain sanctuaries and grove shrines
: Scattered across the interior, each with its own small complement of Wardens

The deep forests of the coast
: The Wardens who patrol [Siralonke]{.name data-type="place/feature"} behind [[place-lirve|Lirve]], and the watch that comes down by turns to [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]] in [Olvalonke]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the farthest from the mountains the order keeps

### Áelendan Groves in the Kingdom's Woods

The Wardens' writ runs wherever Áelendan ground is, and some of it lies inside the kingdom: Áelendan groves in the old growth between the river settlements, kept by a shaman or a Tradition Keeper and guarded by the Wardens of the nearest territory. The crown does not own such a grove and does not claim to. A grove-elder of [[affiliation-groveconcl|the Grove Council]] keeps a Council grove, which is Élavendren ground and the crown's, and the Wardens take no writ from the Council for either kind. A license to cut, issued at [[place-liranel|Liranel]], does not reach an Áelendan grove, and a license-holder who clears one is met by the Wardens' graduated response.

## The Araelwen {#present}

The Araelwen are the order's first charge and the reason it exists, and the Wardens keep them according to what each one does.

At a **door**, whether it lets in the fae or fell things, the Wardens keep the watch, the oldest form of their keeping: two sit by it through every night with spear and dog, turn what comes through by song, fire or stone, retire when it does not turn, and sing from shelter on the nights it wins. The watch on the fog-hollow [Talve]{.name data-type="place/site"} is the first, and a watch is never pulled.

At a place that **disturbs time**, such as [[place-hldhr|the Held Hour]], the Wardens keep the bounds and turn people away, and no Warden goes in.

At every one of them the law is the same. Warden leave can take a stranger along a sacred road; it never takes him into an Araelwen. The Wardens warn a trespasser once and escort him out, and one who returns or desecrates is dealt with finally. A device of the art carried inside, such as a [[miscgear-temprlcmpss|Temporal Compass]], counts as a working, and a working there as desecration.

What the order wants now is to hold that line against an Order that has stopped relying on the crown's guides. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] hires mercenaries and guards to protect its scholars from the Wardens, at Péravel and Liranel, and sends them past the margins in numbers. The Wardens make no distinction between the scholar and the guard, and warn each once. They are stretched: this season an Order party camps below the Held Hour, another waits above [[place-sngngtrn|the Singing Tarn]], a survey has lost a guard on [[place-wndrngrdg|the Wandering Ridge]], and the watch at the Fen Door is down to a single Warden.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **Hands they would not otherwise accept.** The lone Warden at [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]] needs a second at the cleft through the dark nights, and no Warden can come down before the passes open.
- **The other side of the contract.** A Warden of the Spine asks the party to carry a warning to the Order's hired guards below [[place-hldhr|the Held Hour]], plainly, so that no guard can say he was not told.
- **Whose grove.** A license-holder has cleared a stand between two river villages. A grove-elder says it was his, and the Warden of the territory says it was Áelendan ground and asks the party to say what stood there before the Wardens act.
- **Who cut the bark.** The Wardens of the Silver Forests want the man who cut bark from [[place-wkngstnd|the Waking Stand]], and they cannot go to Péravel to ask.
