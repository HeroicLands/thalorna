- **Type:** Cultural federation
- **Region:** [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] – based at the great theater quarter in the capital, with affiliated companies in every Provenzian city of consequence
- **Founded:** Formally constituted twenty-three years ago; informal predecessor traditions stretch back centuries
- **Magnum Collegium status:** Chartered member as the trade body covering theatrical production in Provènzia; holds the Performers' seat (jointly with the senior Aurèldían bardic guilds) on the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium's]] Curia Collegiorum. The Censores Collegiorum's interest in Collective productions is sporadic and has, to date, never produced a formal disciplinary action – a forbearance that the Collective is aware of and cultivates.

## Overview

Provènzia takes its theater seriously. The Provenzian public expects of an evening's performance not merely entertainment but commentary – on the court, on the merchant houses, on the most recent diplomatic embarrassment, on the love affairs of the season. A Provenzian play that does not gently cut someone is regarded as a failed play. The Theater Collective is the federation that produces most of these plays, and quietly arbitrates which cuts may be made and which targets are presently off limits.

The Collective is not, in any formal sense, a guild. It does not license performers, does not own the theaters in which it stages, does not impose dues. It is an association of working theater companies, established performers, playwrights, scenic designers, and a select circle of patrons whose money makes the productions possible. What holds it together is a shared interest in maintaining the conditions under which Provenzian theater can continue to do what Provenzian theater does: comment freely, draw substantial audiences, attract the patronage of the powerful, and avoid being shut down by any of the powerful figures the commentary inevitably annoys.

The Collective's central political function is the management of risk. A staged production that mocks a sitting baron risks the baron's retaliation. A production that mocks a sitting baron with the Collective's tacit approval has, behind it, the implicit support of the other patrons, including (often) the baron's rivals – which makes retaliation politically expensive. The Collective does not protect productions it has not blessed; it does protect those it has, and the calculation of which to bless is the Collective's ongoing internal politics.

## Membership and Structure

The Collective recognizes three categories of member:

- **Companies** – the working theater troupes. Each company holds a single vote in Collective decisions, regardless of its size or prominence. There are presently thirty-one companies of Collective standing across Provènzia, ranging from established institutions like the **Teatro della Luna** in the capital to small touring troupes that perform in market squares.
- **Performers, playwrights, and designers** – individuals of established reputation, admitted by acclamation of the existing membership. There are perhaps two hundred at any given time. Individuals do not vote but are entitled to speak at Collective gatherings and to participate in the informal discussions that shape decisions.
- **Patrons** – wealthy supporters who fund productions. Admission is by sponsorship of an existing member. Patrons do not vote but their views carry the weight their contributions confer. There are presently about forty patrons of standing, several of them noble, several of them mercantile, two of them rumored to be foreign agents quietly supporting productions favorable to their employers' interests.

Day-to-day administration is conducted by the **Convocation of Companies**, which meets every two months at the Teatro della Luna. The Convocation's standing officers – the **First Speaker**, the **Master of the Stage**, the **Mistress of the Patrons**, and the **Keeper of the Repertoire** – are elected annually and serve renewable terms. The current First Speaker is the playwright **Léonardo Castiranni**, a man celebrated for his political comedies and feared for the precision of his satire.

## Notable Members

- **[[being-kelnstylgn|Kêlena Stylgon]]** – a founding figure of the present Collective; she helped formalize the federation twenty-three years ago and continues to wield substantial influence through it, even as her primary work has shifted to high society. The Collective treats her counsel as senior even when she does not attend gatherings.
- **Léonardo Castiranni** – current First Speaker; playwright; political operator.
- The **Teatro della Luna** company – most prestigious of the standing companies, with its own permanent venue in the capital.
- The **Compagnia delle Maschere** – the leading touring troupe; specializes in commedia and the political mask traditions.

## Doctrine and Practice

The Collective holds two working doctrines. The first is **the freedom of the stage** – that the theater's function in Provenzian life is precisely the public examination of those who govern, and that any compromise of that function diminishes the theater's claim to public protection. The second is **the prudence of the season** – that the freedom of the stage is exercised with practical judgment about who can presently be safely mocked and who cannot, and that this judgment is what the Collective collectively makes.

A play is brought before the Convocation for endorsement at the playwright's option. Endorsed productions receive the Collective's tacit support: companies will be encouraged to consider staging them, patrons may be approached for sponsorship, and (most importantly) other companies will not be encouraged to mount competing or undermining productions. Unendorsed productions remain entirely possible; they simply lack the Collective's protective infrastructure.

The Convocation's deliberations on whether to endorse are conducted with theatrical solemnity. The arguments are recorded by the Keeper of the Repertoire and form an informal commentary on Provenzian political and cultural conditions that is, itself, occasionally circulated as a kind of underground publication.

## Activities

- **Coordination of repertoire.** Companies negotiate, through the Collective, who will mount which plays in which seasons. The negotiation prevents the situation in which three companies mount competing productions of the same satirical comedy and dilute their collective impact.
- **Endorsement of new work.** Playwrights bring new plays to the Convocation; endorsement carries practical weight.
- **Defense of staged work.** When a production attracts political retaliation, the Collective responds – through coordinated public statements, through patron pressure, through the orchestrated staging of related work at other theaters that makes the original production harder to suppress in isolation.
- **The Festival of Masks.** The Collective's annual festival, held in late summer, draws performers from across Provènzia and from neighboring kingdoms; the festival's culminating performance is a new play composed collectively by the senior playwrights for that year.

## Relations

- **The Provenzian Crown** – careful mutual tolerance. The crown patronizes the major companies and accepts the Collective's commentary up to a point; the Collective's prudence-of-the-season doctrine generally keeps performance well clear of that point.
- **The merchant houses** – patrons and targets. The relationship is symbiotic; the houses fund productions that mock their rivals and tolerate productions that mock themselves provided the mockery is witty rather than damaging.
- **The [[affiliation-velvethand|Velvet Hand]]** – quiet, active. The Hand uses theatrical troupes as cover for operatives; the Collective is aware of this and tolerates it within limits. Several touring companies are quietly understood to include Hand operatives among their members.
- **The [[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]]** – cordial, distant. The Bardic tradition produces some playwrights of distinction who occasionally bring work to the Collective; the Colleges' more solemn artistic concerns and the Collective's more profane ones coexist without intimacy.

## Identifying Marks

The Collective uses a small enamel pin in the form of two crossed masks (the comic and the tragic) – gold for First Speaker and standing officers, silver for company captains, copper for endorsed playwrights and designers. Patrons of standing wear an unmarked silver brooch in the shape of a stylized stage curtain, a discreet marker of their support.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Endorsed Play That Goes Too Far.** A Collective-endorsed comedy has, in its second-week performances, evolved (as Provenzian comedies do) into something substantially more pointed than what the Convocation reviewed. The original target has signed letters demanding the production close. The Collective must decide whether to honor its endorsement and risk genuine consequences, or to quietly withdraw protection and damage its credibility with the playwrights.
- **The Patron's Demand.** A senior patron, whose money has funded the Collective for years, demands the suppression of a production that mocks a man he is privately negotiating to marry his daughter to. The Convocation's response will determine whether the patron remains a friend or becomes an enemy.
- **The Hidden Operative.** A touring company's lead actor has been identified, by independent means, as a [[affiliation-velvethand|Velvet Hand]] operative using the company as cover. The Collective has tolerated such arrangements within limits; this one appears to have exceeded the limits. The company captain pleads ignorance, which may or may not be true.
- **The Foreign Commission.** A wealthy foreign patron has commissioned a new play, paid in advance, with the requirement that it endorse a particular political position in the present Provenzian succession debate. The fee is substantial; the position is plausible; the commission is, transparently, foreign interference in Provenzian politics. The First Speaker must decide whether to accept on the Collective's behalf, refuse, or maneuver toward a compromise that takes the fee while diluting the message.
