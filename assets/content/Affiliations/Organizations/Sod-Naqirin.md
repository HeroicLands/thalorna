_Haradi: Sôd-Naqîrîn—"The Council of the Guild-Wardens"_

- **Type:** Confederation-chartered guild umbrella; institutional replacement for the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]'s former Haradian operations
- **Region:** The Confederation of Haradian States
- **Seat:** **Bêt-Naqîrîn** ("The House of the Wardens") at [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]], in the converted hall of the old Tamavar merchant princes' compact-house—a deliberate choice that places the institution on ground sacred to the pre-conquest Haradian commercial tradition rather than in any building the empire built
- **Founded:** Twelve years ago, by act of the Confederation's founding council, in the closing weeks of the War of Independence

## Overview

When the Confederation of Haradian States won its war against Vylaria twelve years ago, the merchant princes who had led the rebellion faced a problem they had thought through with more care than the Vylarian Collegium's officers later credited them for. The Vylarian guild umbrella—the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]] at Magnápolis—had run Haradian trade life for more than a century. It had standardized credentials, charters, dispute resolution, inter-guild jurisdiction, and the daily procedures by which Haradian masters lived and worked. Walking away from it required something to walk to. The merchant princes had no intention of preserving Vylarian forms one day longer than necessary; they had every intention of preserving the trade life those forms had supported.

The answer was the **Sôd-Naqîrîn**—the Council of the Guild-Wardens. The founding council convened in the smoke-blackened ruins of the Tamavar compact-house (Vylarian artillery had destroyed it in the last weeks of the war), called the chief naqîrîn of every Haradian trade to assemble, and constituted the institution in the haradi-language under haradi-tradition forms. The Latin was not translated. It was discarded. The councilors took titles from the old Haradian merchant princes' compact tradition—_rab-naqîr_, _sôd-rabbânîn_, _adônîm al-mudunîn_—which had survived in attenuated form throughout the imperial period as the internal vocabulary of the great trading houses. The procedures were rewritten to reflect the Confederation's oligarchic-deliberative politics rather than the empire's top-down imperial form. The new seal was struck without reference to the Collegium's. The new credentials were issued on parchment of a different cut, in a different hand, sealed in a different wax.

Twelve years on, the Sôd-Naqîrîn is what it set out to be: a Haradian institution speaking Haradian to Haradian guilds, performing the work of organizing trade life without conceding that the Vylarian Collegium was ever its parent. The procedural continuity with Vylarian practice is real but is presented—sometimes accurately, sometimes more aspirationally than the historical record supports—as a parallel inheritance from the old Haradian compacts rather than as a borrowing from the empire. The institution functions. The Confederation backs it. The Vylarian Collegium refuses to acknowledge its chosen name and continues to file correspondence to it as "Magnum Collegium Haradicum"—a continuing diplomatic offense that the Haradian council cites publicly as evidence of imperial bad faith and quietly enjoys, because the offense costs Vylaria credibility every time it is repeated.

## Structure

The Sôd's structure deliberately mirrors the Haradian Confederation's political form: oligarchic at the top, deliberative in the middle, distributed at the periphery.

- **Rab-Naqîr** ("Chief of Chiefs")—the head; one position; elected from among the sitting naqîrîn of the great trades for a seven-year term, renewable by reaffirming vote rather than automatic. The election is by the assembled Sôd-Rabbânîn. The current Rab-Naqîr is **Riyad al-Massoud**, a former Vylarian Master Magister of substantial standing who defected to the secession early and was rewarded with the founding leadership; he is now in his second term and the question of a third is being quietly canvassed.
- **Sôd-Rabbânîn** ("Council of the Great Ones")—the standing council; thirty-two seats, one per recognized trade-category. Each seat is held by the senior naqîr of the relevant trade, elected by the trade's own masters in conclave; terms are five years. The council deliberates in continuous session at Tamavar, with four annual full convocations.
- **Pāqîdîn** ("Overseers")—the discipline arm; eight officers; conduct inspections, investigate charter violations, prosecute disciplinary cases before the Sôd. The Pāqîdîn are distinct from the Vylarian Censores in two important respects: they cannot prosecute on their own initiative without referral from the council, and their disciplinary findings are subject to appeal to a full Sôd convocation. The senior officer carries the title **Rab-Pāqîd** ("Chief Overseer"); the current holder is **Layla Nazari**, regarded as the most procedurally rigorous of the Sôd's officers.
- **Gizbar** ("Treasurer")—manages the Sôd's common fund _and_ the Confederation's parallel banking system, the **Bayt al-Khazînah** ("House of the Treasury"); see _The Gizbar's Office_ section below.
- **Adônîm al-Mudunîn** ("Lords of the City-States")—the provincial deputies; one per major Haradian state; administer the Sôd's operations in their cities and report to the Rab-Naqîr through the Sôd. There are presently eleven, reflecting the Confederation's eleven principal cities.

## Authority and Practice

Within Harad the Sôd's authority is equivalent to the Vylarian Collegium's: guilds must be chartered, masters must be registered, apprentices must be examined, inter-guild disputes are adjudicated by the Sôd-Rabbânîn. The Confederation backs enforcement with the same vigor.

In practice the institution does several things differently from its imperial predecessor, by design:

- **Continuity of pre-secession credentials.** Haradian masters who held Vylarian Collegium credentials at the moment of independence retain those credentials at full standing within the Sôd. This is a deliberate accommodation, since requiring re-examination of an entire generation of masters would have destroyed the trade life the secession was supposed to preserve. The accommodation is, in 720 AF, twelve years old and beginning to phase out as those masters retire.
- **Selective recognition of foreign credentials.** The Sôd has unilaterally extended full recognition to Aurèldían and Helladic Collegium credentials—a gesture the Vylarian Collegium has not reciprocated and that the Confederation hopes will eventually pressure the empire toward de facto recognition through the trade routes' practical realities.
- **Active recruitment of Vylarian-trained masters.** The Sôd offers expedited Haradian credentials to any Master Magister, Censor, or Magistri Provinciarum who relocates to Harad. The offer is widely understood in the Vylarian Collegium to be a quiet poaching operation; several mid-career officers have accepted, to substantial resentment in Magnápolis. Those who relocate are required, by Sôd protocol, to relinquish their Latin titles and take Haradian ones.
- **The Recovered Compacts.** A formally chartered Sôd initiative to research, document, and re-establish Haradian trade traditions absorbed, suppressed, or assimilated under Vylarian rule. Some of these compact-traditions are genuinely old—survivals from the pre-conquest centuries when each Haradian city's merchant princes maintained their own internal compact-houses and the inter-city trade was governed by negotiated compact-treaties between them. Others are creative reconstructions whose claims to ancient pedigree are politely declined to scrutinize. The program is funded by the Confederation's federal council and produces a steady output of ceremonial restorations that the Vylarian Collegium regards as historical fabrication. The Sôd does not care what the Collegium regards.
- **Deliberative-oligarchic procedure.** Where the Vylarian Curia's rulings are issued under the Princeps's authority, Sôd-Rabbânîn rulings require a deliberative majority of present councilors and are issued in the council's name. The Rab-Naqîr presides and may speak last but does not rule.

## The Gizbar's Office

When the secession began, the immediate practical crisis was not military but financial. Harad's commercial life had run for more than a century on paper script issued by [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Argentariorum]]-member moneylenders, clearing ultimately through the Vylarian [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|imperial treasury]] at Magnápolis, and within weeks of the war's outbreak those clearing accounts were frozen. Haradian moneylenders woke one morning unable to honor their own outstanding script. Merchants discovered the notes in their strongboxes were no longer redeemable. The harbor cities, on which the Confederation's wealth depended, faced commercial collapse before the first major engagement was even fought.

Harad's break was therefore unusually deep. Where the [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]] over the past two centuries had thrown off imperial governance _but kept_ the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]] and the Aerarium-backed banking system because they could not afford to leave them, Harad's secession war forced a full withdrawal from all three institutions at once. The Magnum Collegium suspended Haradian guild standing; the Argentariorum revoked Haradian moneylenders' chapter affiliation; the Aerarium Imperii froze the clearing accounts. The Confederation had to build replacements for all three from scratch, and the Sôd-Naqîrîn was the institution that emerged to provide them—combining the Collegium's guild umbrella function, the Argentariorum's banking-system role, and a treasury arm of its own.

The Confederation's response on the banking front was the **Bayt al-Khazînah**, the "House of the Treasury," operated as the Gizbar's office within the Bêt-Naqîrîn at Tamavar. The Bayt is the Haradian parallel to the Aerarium Imperii: it accepts bullion deposits, issues master script against its reserves, and operates as the clearinghouse for the network of Haradian moneylender houses that now anchor the Confederation's banking system. The current Gizbar is **Kashnûra Bel-Tarûzî**, an Aerean-trained banker whose Haradian family lineage and twenty years of pre-secession Argentariorum experience made her the obvious choice for the founding office and who has now served for twelve years.

The Bayt's structure deliberately mirrors the Aerarium's. Bullion deposits from Haradian moneylender houses (the Confederation's reorganized **Naqîrîn al-Khazînah**—Treasurers' Guild) and from the Confederation's federal treasury are held against issued master script; retail script is drawn by the moneylenders against their accounts; cross-territory portability is maintained within Confederation territory through Sôd chapterhouse clearing. The notes are produced on distinctive paper struck with the Haradian eight-pointed star and the issuing moneylender's seal, denominated in **Argo** and **Aurions**—the same units as the Vylarian system, a deliberate concession to the realities of inter-realm trade even though Harad has never had a strong gold-coining tradition. (Haradian Aurion coin is, in fact, slightly underweight by Vylarian standard; the discount is acknowledged in cross-border transactions but not advertised.)

The Bayt's reserves are not large by Aerarium standards—the Confederation does not have the bullion stockpile that three centuries of imperial accumulation built up at Magnápolis—but they are sufficient for the Confederation's commercial volume, and the federal council periodically authorizes additional reserves drawn from confiscated Vylarian-loyalist property and from the war indemnities Vylaria continues to refuse to pay. The Bayt's strongroom at Tamavar is jointly guarded by the Gizbar's office and a permanent detachment of Confederation marines.

### Cross-Border Script

Neither institution recognizes the other's paper. A merchant arriving at a Vylarian Collegium chapterhouse with Bayt script is told politely that the chapter cannot honor it; the same merchant arriving at a Sôd chapterhouse with Aerarium script receives the same response. Merchants who move regularly across the border maintain accounts at both institutions; merchants who move only occasionally accept the substantial spread that comes from converting through specie (physical Argo or Aurion).

The cross-border script problem is the most consistent friction-point in twelve years of post-secession commercial life. Several Heliónite moneylender houses—operating in territory that is Vylarian but commercially deeply tied to Harad—have made considerable fortunes acting as cross-border clearing intermediaries, accepting both institutions' script and effectively converting between them through their own accounts at both. These houses are formally on the Aerarium's books and informally on the Bayt's; the arrangement is regarded by both institutions as an irregularity to be tolerated for as long as the alternative (an actual functional cross-border script agreement) remains politically impossible.

## Notable Members

- **Rab-Naqîr Riyad al-Massoud**—the founding Chief; defected from Vylarian Collegium service in the second year of the secession war; serves as both Rab-Naqîr and the public face of the institutional schism. He has, for twelve years, refused all Vylarian correspondence addressed to him by his former Latin title.
- **Rab-Pāqîd Layla Nazari**—senior discipline officer; the most rigorous of the Sôd's procedural officers; quietly feared by the Haradian commercial elite, several of whose members are presently the subjects of investigations she is not in any hurry to conclude.
- **Gizbar Kashnûra Bel-Tarûzî**—head of the Bayt al-Khazînah; the Sôd's chief financial officer; widely regarded as the second most powerful figure in the institution after the Rab-Naqîr, and the single Haradian official whose decisions most directly affect the Confederation's commercial life day-to-day.
- The thirty-two seat-holders of the Sôd-Rabbânîn—the Haradian guildmasters whose elevation to the council gives the Sôd its working authority.

## Relations

- **The Vylarian [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]**—officially unrecognized in both directions. The Collegium refuses to acknowledge the Sôd's Haradian name; the Sôd refuses any communication that uses the Vylarian "Magnum Collegium Haradicum" designation. Practical correspondence between the institutions happens through intermediaries—usually Helladic merchants of standing—who carry messages that neither side will officially have sent. The arrangement is awkward, freighted with personal history (many senior officers on both sides served together before the war), and conducted entirely through these back channels.
- **The Aurèldían and Helladic Collegium chapters**—through the Vylarian Collegium's structure these chapters are formally above the Sôd's reach, but the Sôd has, with quiet diplomatic effort, established working contacts with several of their Magistri Provinciarum at the personal level. The contacts are not approved by Magnápolis and are conducted accordingly.
- **The Confederation's federal council**—patron and ultimate authority. The relationship is closer and more politicized than the parent Vylarian body's relationship with its imperial throne; the federal council intervenes in Sôd decisions more frequently than the empire does in the Collegium's, and the Sôd's Rab-Naqîr is, in practice, accountable to a coalition of merchant princes whose interests may not always align with the institution's stated mission.
- **The non-Vylarian guild systems**—the Sôd has aggressively pursued recognition agreements with all the major independent traditions on terms generally more favorable to the foreign counterparty than what the Vylarian Collegium historically offered. Agreements concluded with [[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]] (per-trade equivalence, mid-stage negotiations), [[affiliation-pernuw|Per'Nuw]] (early-stage on desert and frontier work), the Bethûan trade-houses (concluded last year, full recognition), and the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|The Skalds' Circle]] (reciprocal hospitality recognition). The Vedyaran [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] has, characteristically, declined every overture from the Sôd just as it has from the Vylarian Collegium.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]], particularly the [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]**—the Sôd has formal anti-corruption protocols and informal acknowledgment that the Crimson Ledger's financial reach into the Haradian guild economy is substantial. The Pāqîdîn have brought cases against guild members with Ledger ties; the cases have, to date, mostly resulted in censure rather than charter revocation, a pattern the Sôd's reformist faction is increasingly impatient with.

## Identifying Marks

The Sôd's seal—a stylized open ledger over a coiled rope and an anchor, crowned with the Confederation's eight-pointed star—is struck into every charter, ruling, and credential. The visual distance from the Vylarian seal (a wreathed open book over crossed measuring-rule and quill) is deliberate; the symbols (ledger, rope, anchor) refer to Harad's maritime commercial identity rather than to the imperial Latin academic tradition.

Members of the Sôd-Rabbânîn wear a deep crimson stole at formal sessions, embroidered with the eight-pointed star at the hem; outside session they wear their own trades' marks. Pāqîdîn wear a saffron-yellow mantle with the silver mark of the open eye—a deliberate inversion of the Vylarian Censores' black mantle, chosen so that no observer could mistake one for the other. The Rab-Naqîr wears the great gold pectoral of the Sôd, struck for the institution at its founding and worn by every Rab-Naqîr since.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Disputed Title.** A Vylarian Magistri Provinciarum's official correspondence has, for the eighth consecutive month, addressed the Sôd's Rab-Naqîr as "Princeps Haradicus." The Rab-Naqîr has now formally complained to the Confederation's federal council, which is preparing a public diplomatic protest. The Vylarian Collegium's leadership knows the wording was a calculated insult ordered by a faction in the imperial court; the question is whether the empire will quietly correct the practice or escalate.
- **The Recovered Compact.** The Sôd's Recovered Compacts program has formally chartered a new trade-guild—for a craft that Haradian scholars claim was practiced openly before Vylarian conquest and suppressed thereafter. Vylarian scholarship holds the trade is largely invented for political purposes. The new guild's masters have begun seeking work outside Harad, and their credentials are about to be tested for the first time. The Vylarian Collegium's response—recognition or refusal—will set precedent for every future Recovered Compact.
- **The Defected Censor.** A Vylarian Censor on inspection in southern Aurèldía has been quietly approached by Sôd agents with an extraordinary offer of relocation, full Haradian credentials under the Pāqîdîn, and a substantial settlement payment. He has not refused. His next inspection report will be carefully read by both Collegium and Sôd officers, and its contents will reveal which way he is leaning.
- **The Joint Investigation.** A trade scandal involving smuggled goods, falsified credentials, and at least three murders has crossed the Vylarian-Haradian border so thoroughly that neither institution can investigate without the other's cooperation. Riyad al-Massoud and Marcus Velerianus would, in private, both prefer a joint investigation; in public neither can openly request one. A back-channel approach is being attempted through a Heliónite intermediary, who is being paid by both sides without either knowing.
- **The Reformist Vote.** A coalition of younger Sôd-Rabbânîn councilors has formally proposed substantial reforms to the Pāqîdîn's enforcement powers—strengthening them against the merchant-prince factions whose interests the present Pāqîdîn have visibly declined to pursue. The reform would, if passed, end the cozy arrangement under which the Crimson Ledger's reach into the Haradian guilds has remained substantially unmolested. Layla Nazari has signaled quiet support; the merchant princes are mobilizing against. The vote will be very close.
- **The Heliónite Arbitrage.** A particular Heliónite moneylender house has, over the past three years, become the largest cross-border script intermediary on either side of the Vylarian–Haradian frontier—accepting both [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium]] and Bayt paper and operating accounts at both institutions on a scale that neither institution has fully appreciated. The house's senior partners are now in delicate negotiations with both the Quaestor Imperii and the Gizbar; the arrangement is generating extraordinary profits and is, formally, irregular under both institutions' rules. The Censores Aerarii have begun preliminary inquiries; the Pāqîdîn have not yet been asked to. Disclosure could end the arrangement, hurt both treasuries' political positions, and ruin the moneylender house; non-disclosure has its own risks if either institution's auditors notice independently.
