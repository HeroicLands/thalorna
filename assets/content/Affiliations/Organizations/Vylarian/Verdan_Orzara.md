## The Courts of Ôrzâra

Justice and civic government in the imperial capital run along three separate tracks that meet only at the throne. The **[[place-orzara|city]] beyond the Inner Wall**—its administration, its courts, its Watch, and its daily supply—is governed by the **Verdan Ôrzâra**, the Council of the City. The **Inner City** keeps its own order under the [[affiliation-sorvan|Sorvan]], the imperial guard. And the **imperial tribunals**—the throne's own courts—sit apart from both, within the **Imperial Offices** compound, answerable to the Ôrzâs, the emperor, alone.

### The Verdan Ôrzâra—the Council of the City

Ôrzâra is not ruled by a prefect. The Empire learned long ago that a single man set over a city of a million—commanding its courts, its granaries, and its armed Watch all at once—is a man with the means to make himself a rival to the throne. So the city is governed instead by a **council of five**, the **Verdan Ôrzâra**, whose members are appointed (and dismissed) by the Ôrzâs, most often drawn from the senatorial order.

The five **Curators** sit as equals and decide the weightier matters as a body, by majority. Each holds a standing portfolio:

- **The Curator of Law** (_Curator Iudiciorum_)—the city's chief judicial authority; oversees the Aediles, the Civil Courts, and the Criminal Tribunal, and presides when the council sits as the supreme civic court.
- **The Curator of Order** (_Curator Vigilum_)—commands the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|City Watch]], and answers for public order, the watch-houses, and the city gaols.
- **The Curator of the Grain** (_Curator Annonae_)—the granaries, the dole, the licensed markets, and the unceasing daily supply on which the capital lives.
- **The Curator of Works and Waters** (_Curator Operum_)—the fabric of the city: its streets and bridges, its aqueducts and sewers, its public buildings, baths, and the upkeep (or quiet neglect) of the Circles and avenues.
- **The Curator of the Rolls** (_Curator Tabularum_)—the census, the citizen-rolls, the city's records, and its correspondence with the throne; by custom the council's coordinating seat.

The chair rotates among them so that none becomes "the" head of the city, and the Ôrzâs takes deliberate care to appoint Curators who will check rather than collude with one another. A council that grows too harmonious is a council the throne begins to watch. In practice the five are perpetual rivals—for the Ôrzâs's favor, for the richest portfolios, and for the patronage that flows through each office—and a great deal of the city's real politics is the politics of the Verdan Ôrzâra.

### The Civic Courts

Beneath the council runs a tiered court system. The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|City Watch]] enforces and investigates; it does not judge. Judgment belongs to the courts.

A ward clerk of the Aediles, asked how the tiers look from the bottom, answers for most of the city: "Most people see one magistrate in their lives, and he is the Aedile of their ward. Everything above him is something that happens to somebody else."

- **The Aediles**—the magistrates of the wards, and the only court most Ôrzârans ever see. They keep the district law: markets and licenses, weights and measures, building and sanitation, nuisance and petty disorder. They levy fines, shutter premises, and condemn buildings; the Watch supplies the muscle behind their rulings.
- **The Civil Courts**—the civil courts, where citizen sues citizen over property, contract, debt, inheritance, and standing. Cases are heard by **Sentori** drawn from the propertied orders, the weightier disputes before panels.
- **The Criminal Tribunal**—for theft, violence, homicide, and sedition short of treason. The Watch brings the accused; the Tribunal judges; its sentences run from fines and forced labor to the arena and the executioner.

Appeals, and the gravest civic cases, rise to the **Verdan Ôrzâra** sitting as a body under the Curator of Law. There the civic system ends; it has no authority over the nobility, the Senate, or the crown.

### The Imperial Tribunals

Entirely separate from the civic courts stand the **imperial tribunals**—the courts of the throne itself. They sit within the Inner City, in the Imperial Offices, the great bureaucratic compound that also houses the treasury, the tax administration, and the imperial archives, and they answer to the Ôrzâs and his appointed judges, never to the Verdan Ôrzâra.

Their jurisdiction is everything the civic courts cannot touch. Any matter involving a member of the nobility, any cause touching the [[affiliation-seldan|Senate]] or the imperial house, the great suits between provinces and crowns, and **treason** above all, are imperial business from the first moment—and a case can be lifted out of the civic courts into the imperial tribunals at the throne's word. For senatorial persons in the gravest matters, judgment may rise higher still, to the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] itself. To be summoned from the Aediles' bench to the Offices is, for most of the city, a terror in itself: it means one's affair has caught the attention of powers far above the ward.

### The Reach of Influence

For all its tiers and titles, the law of Ôrzâra runs partly on influence, and everyone knows it. A defendant with the right friends, the right purse or the right patron finds the courts accommodating; one without learns how heavy they can be. The Aediles are famously bribable; a Curator's favor can move a case between courts or bury it in the Offices's bottomless archives; and the perpetual rivalry of the Verdan Ôrzâra means that a well-placed enemy on the council can be as dangerous to a citizen as any charge. Among the quieter profits of the courts, too, is the trade in **residency waivers**—the papers that let a non-citizen dwell within the walls in some great house's service (see [[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vylarian Citizenship]]); granted through the courts, they move fast for the right consideration. Justice is real in Ôrzâra—but it is also, always, a currency.
