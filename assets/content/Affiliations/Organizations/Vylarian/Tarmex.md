A Tarmex assayer, handing a traveler's note across the counter to a customer who has just paid for it, says what she says to every customer: "Hold it up to the lamp. See the eagle in the paper? It shows only against the light, and it is the first thing the clerk at the other end will look for. Keep that note dry and keep it close, and any moneylender from here to the Aurèldían kingdoms will give you coin for it. The Tarmex stands behind every valid note, whoever cashes it. Finding a moneylender with the coin in his drawer is your trouble, not ours."

## Overview

The Tarmex Orzan, the imperial treasury, is the empire's bullion vault and, since the consolidation of the script system three centuries ago ([[lore-arzanasedict|The Edicts of Arzánas]]), its central bank. The founding Ôrzâs (the Vylarian emperor) established it by edict when the empire began, and it has run without a break since. It holds the empire's bullion reserves, receives the imperial tax revenue that the [[affiliation-tolvan|Tolvan]] collects through its Tolari, and disburses imperial expenditure on the joint authorization of the Ôrzâs and the Dukran. It also runs the central-bank functions on which most large-value commerce in member territory depends. Its strongroom and clearing house stand within the **Imperial Offices**, the bureaucratic compound of the Inner City of [[place-orzara|Ôrzâra]]. The Imperial Quaestor, its chief officer, is widely regarded as second in power only to the Dukran among non-noble imperial appointments.

The Tarmex's direct offices operate only in [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper, where imperial governance currently runs, and on paper in [[place-helionis|Heliónis]]. The banking system it anchors reaches much further, across the [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]], [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] and, until twelve years ago, Tarûzel. Outside the empire the visible institution is the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]], the moneylenders' guild chartered under the [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]]. Its chapterhouses and member moneylenders handle the note traffic, hold the local reserves and present the public face of the system, and the Tarmex itself never visits these territories. Everyone knows the Danvar's chapter reserves are part of the Tarmex-backed pool, and nobody says so publicly.

This polite fiction is one of the most consequential pieces of imperial diplomacy. The Tarmex takes a 2% note-issuance fee on every note generated in the Danvar system, wherever the issuing moneylender keeps his chapter, so the imperial treasury draws a substantial revenue stream from territories the empire no longer governs. The Aurèldían crowns and the Byzarian League know it and tolerate it, because leaving the banking system would ruin their commercial life. Both sides have strong reasons not to disturb the arrangement, and so it holds.

## The Three Tiers of Paper

The Vylarian currency anchors at the **Solden** (gold), circulates in the **Ardin** (Ard, silver, 160 per Solden) and pays small transactions in the **Ogdel** (Bit, one-eighth of a Ard, 1,280 per Solden). Solden coin is rare, perhaps 200,000 in existence empire-wide, so large-value commerce moves in paper and not in coin, in three distinct forms. (The [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian Currency]] note holds the full coinage system, geographic acceptance, sample prices and the wage scale.)

| Paper                | Issued By                                 | Backed By                                                               | Honored Where                                                                        | Typical Use                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Retail notes**     | Individual member moneylender             | The issuing house's own reserves                                        | At the issuing house and at houses with reciprocal arrangements; locally circulating | Everyday large transactions in a single city or trading region                          |
| **Traveler's notes** | Member moneylender, drawn on Tarmex Orzan | Tarmex Orzan reserves (held locally by Danvar chapter where applicable) | At any Danvar-member moneylender across the commonwealth                             | Cross-territory travel; merchant working capital; remittance                            |
| **Master notes**     | Tarmex Orzan directly                     | Tarmex Orzan reserves                                                   | At any Tarmex chapter (imperial territory only) or any Danvar chapterhouse           | Inter-moneylender settlement; very large commercial transactions; imperial disbursement |

A common merchant uses retail notes for local commerce. A merchant traveling between cities, within one polity or across several, buys a traveler's note from his home moneylender, carries it and presents it for redemption at his destination. A great commercial transaction (a Vandrax's commission, a major land sale, a settlement between moneylenders) uses master notes drawn from the Tarmex directly.

## The Traveler's Note in Practice

The traveler's note is the Tarmex's most important commercial product, and continent-wide trade depends on it.

A customer goes to a member moneylender's office and asks for a note for a given amount. The moneylender prepares it: sealed paper, a registered serial, his own mark and the Danvar chapter's countersignature, and the customer's name or "to bearer" as the customer prefers. The Tarmex's issuance fee is **2% of the note's face value**. The moneylender adds his own arranging fee, typically 1–2%, and the customer pays the total. For a 100 Solden note the customer pays 103–104 Solden. The Tarmex's 2 Solden is forwarded to imperial accounts (through the Danvar chapter's clearing arrangement in non-imperial territory), and the moneylender keeps his 1–2 Solden.

The note travels with the customer, who presents it at any Danvar member moneylender in any city he reaches. That moneylender pays face value _minus a discount_ set by his own liquidity, the size of the note and the local market for redeeming notes. Discounts run from 1–3% in the major commercial cities to 15–25% at remote frontier postings. The redeeming moneylender then forwards the note to his Danvar chapter for clearing, the chapter reimburses him at face value, and the chapter clears the note through the wider Tarmex-backed pool: directly with the Tarmex in imperial territory, and through the Danvar's clearing channels back to Ôrzâra elsewhere.

The customer bears **location risk** but not **counterparty risk**. The note is good, because the Tarmex honors every valid note, but the customer must find a moneylender solvent enough to pay out today. A traveler who brings large notes into a small frontier town may be told that the moneylender cannot cover the note in cash and can offer only partial settlement in his own retail notes, which the traveler then converts at his next stop. That is one of the main reasons travelers carry a sheaf of notes in varied denominations and not one large note.

The Tarmex charges **no fee on redemption**. The moneylenders charge on both ends, a small markup on issuance and a larger discount on redemption, and that is their business model. Moving 100 Solden across the empire by traveler's note costs the customer from about 5% in the best case, between major cities, to about 27% in the worst, from a major city to the deep frontier. The moneylenders' share varies with location. The 2% to the imperial treasury is constant.

## The Noble and Crown Alternative

Gentry and local crowns have another option. A noble traveling without his home moneylender's correspondent in the destination city may present himself at the household of a peer family he knows. The family cashes his note within a day at a small discount, typically 1–3%, and redeems it through its own Danvar contact. The reciprocity is implicit. The same network runs between the Aurèldían crowns: a Tarvénian crown official on official business carries notes that he can cash on courtesy terms at the household treasuries of the other crowns.

Commoners cannot use this route, wealthy merchants and chartered moneylenders on personal business included. The social barrier is total, and a commoner who presents himself at an Aurelian house's gate is turned away without ceremony.

The **strongroom at Ôrzâra** holds the empire's largest single concentration of Solden bullion. The Imperial Quaestor, the Chief Censor of the Tarmex and the captain of the permanent imperial guard detail at the imperial palace jointly secure it. It has never been breached in three centuries. A panel audits its contents every year, with representatives of the imperial throne and the [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|Imperial Scribal Guild]] and, since the compromise reforms that followed the Tarûzelian war, one representative chosen by the **Reformist Faction** at court.

## The 2% Issuance Fee as Revenue Stream

The issuance fee is one of the throne's largest single revenue lines, comparable in scale to the imperial land tax. Every traveler's note issued anywhere in the commonwealth contributes 2% of its face value to the imperial treasury. A Tarvénian merchant family's working-capital cycle, an Aurelian house's commission payment and a Provenzian wedding-gift remittance all pay it, whatever the originating territory's political relationship with the empire.

That means **the empire extracts revenue from territories it no longer governs**, through a system those territories keep using because the alternative is worse. The Aurèldían crowns periodically propose changes, whether a sliding fee, a partial rebate to participating crowns or a renegotiated rate, and in every generation since the post-imperial settlement the Tarmex has refused to discuss any of them. The crowns acquiesce, because withdrawal would mean dismantling the banking infrastructure their commercial classes depend on, and no crown can survive that politically.

The fee is one reason the polite fiction holds. As long as the kingdoms can say they pay into "the Danvar" and not "the imperial treasury," they can bear the fee as a banker's charge and not as a tax. Imperial officials who try to make the fee's destination more visible, usually for domestic Vylarian political reasons, meet an immediate diplomatic protest from the kingdoms and quiet pressure from the Dukran's office to withdraw the claim.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-tolvan|Tolvan]]** is the primary source of tax revenue: the Tolvan collects and the Tarmex holds. Relations are correct and bureaucratic, and tension arises when the Tarmex's disbursement priorities, set by the Dukran and the Ôrzâs, collide with the Tolvan's revenue projections.
- **The [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]]** is a sibling cross-realm institution. It charters the [[affiliation-danvar|moneylenders' guild]] that handles the Tarmex's foreign operations, and the Tarmex supplies the credit backing that makes the Danvar's clearing system work. The two coordinate constantly, and the Tevrax and the Imperial Quaestor meet weekly during their tenures.
- **The [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]]** is the operational interface, the visible face of the Tarmex's banking system in non-imperial territory. Its chapter reserves belong to the wider Tarmex-backed pool and its moneylenders clear through Tarmex accounts. The relationship is described publicly as "honored guild standing," not subordination.
- **The Ôrzâs** is patron and ultimate authority. The Imperial Quaestor is one of perhaps six officeholders with direct standing access to the Ôrzâs's privy presence.
- **The Merâzûnî**, the Tarûzelian counterpart that the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezâkûnî]] runs, is formally unrecognized. The Tarmex does not honor Vezâkûnî paper and the Merâzûnî does not honor Tarmex paper. Cross-border arrangements run through the few Heliónite intermediary houses willing to operate in both systems.
- **The Aurèldían crowns and the Byzarian League** formally have no relationship with the Tarmex, though its revenue depends on their continued participation. Diplomatic communications about the banking system go through the Dukran's office and not the Tarmex, to keep up the fiction that the Tarmex has no foreign-relations role.

## Identifying Marks

The Tarmex's seal shows an eagle bearing a balance-scale and a wreath of laurel in its talons, with the imperial cipher above. It is struck into every master note, issued credential and official letter. Tarmex officers wear the imperial purple stole over working dress at formal sessions, and Station Magisters add a brass pin in the shape of the Tarmex eagle. The Imperial Quaestor wears the great gold collar of imperial finance, kept at Ôrzâra between holders.

The **traveler's note** itself is printed on distinctive watermarked paper. The watermark is the Tarmex eagle, visible only when the paper is held against the light. The issuing moneylender's seal is in colored wax and the Danvar chapter's countersignature in black wax, and the serial number is registered with both the moneylender and the Tarmex. The face value is spelled out in both Soldeni and Ard. Counterfeiting a note is a capital crime. The defenses are the watermarked paper, which is specially treated to expose known imitations, the registered serial system and the specific composition of the wax seals.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Counterfeit Wave.** A skilled forging operation has been turning out convincing counterfeit traveler's notes in three Aurèldían cities for the better part of a year. Detecting them means comparing each against the registered serials, which is slow. The Censors of the Tarmex are investigating quietly and the Censors of the Tevran, who audit the Danvar's guild compliance, less quietly. The Reformist Faction has begun suggesting that the Danvar houses themselves are involved, and the Imperial Quaestor is taking the suggestion with extreme seriousness.
- **The Frozen Note.** The Tarmex has suspended a major Ólvestrian moneylender house for political reasons, since its senior partners made the wrong court enemy. Notes that other houses have already cashed and forwarded to that house's Tarmex account cannot be redeemed for now. The cashing houses are exposed, receiving moneylenders are growing wary of any note from the suspended house's correspondents, and liquidity is tightening in a cascade across Aurèldía. The Imperial Quaestor must choose among lifting the suspension and losing the political point, holding it and risking a wider confidence crisis, or extending emergency clearing credit and pretending the suspension was a procedural error.
- **The Crown's Demand.** The Tarvénian crown has formally proposed renegotiating the 2% issuance fee, its first such proposal in two generations. The Provenzian and Élavendren crowns support it, the Aelwythan crown is undecided and the Byzarian League is watching. The Tarmex's standing answer is refusal. The Dukran is being asked whether the empire will face a coordinated boycott of the note system, and the answer will define imperial commercial policy for a generation.
- **The Strongroom Audit.** The annual audit of the strongroom falls in the third week of the spring quarter. This year's panel includes, through a procedural anomaly, a Reformist representative whom the Imperial Quaestor has been unable to block, reputed to command anomaly-detection techniques developed in private Vedyaran banking practice. If the audit finds what the Quaestor's enemies expect, the institutional consequences are hard to forecast.
- **The Hidden Hand.** A run of unusually large traveler's notes from one Heliónite moneylender house suggests that the house is channeling capital for foreign powers (the Vezâkûnî, perhaps, or something farther afield) across imperial territory under deniable cover. Each note is legitimate and the pattern is the anomaly. The Imperial Quaestor holds the analytical evidence, and the political question is what to do with it.
- **The Disbursement Refusal.** A senior Bandren has asked the Tarmex for an extraordinary disbursement to fund a campaign the Dukran has authorized and the Ôrzâs has not yet formally approved. Tarmex procedure requires _joint_ authorization. If the Imperial Quaestor refuses, the campaign is delayed by weeks. If he approves, he sets a procedural precedent that the **Conservative Faction** would treat as a serious breach of imperial prerogative. The campaign's window is closing.
