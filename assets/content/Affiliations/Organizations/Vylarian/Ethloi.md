You will sit where his clients sit, you will shout when they shout, and you will leave before the Irons' clubs come down from the top tiers. If a man in gray speaks to you on the way home, you are a client of this house, and you will say so."

## Overview

The Ethloi, the Golds, are the smaller and richer of the two racing factions of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]. Their name is the plural of _ethlon_ (gold) in [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari]], and gold is the color of the highest of the four noble estates, the _ethlen_ (Gilded). Their teams race against the [[affiliation-zargoi|Zargoi]], the Irons, at [[place-rathaunamelvar|The Racecourse]].

The Golds are a faction of patrons and clients. A board of patrons from the Gilded houses and the old horse order pays for the stables, the horses and the drivers, and the patrons' clients fill the Golds' stands. The Golds' crowd comes down from the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] and the [[place-gildedqrtr|Gilded Quarter]]: clients, stewards, shopkeepers who sell to the great houses, and the household staff of men who keep a box above the finishing line. It is a smaller crowd than the Irons', and a better dressed one, and it has fewer men who have stood in a battle line.

The Golds' teams are the best in the city. The patrons buy their horses from the same breeders the legions buy cavalry remounts from, and the faction's stables are kept by men of the _gulmalon_ (horse order), the old equestrian estate whose families have known horses for longer than they have known money. A Gold team that loses is a scandal on the Heights for a week.

## The Stables and the Stands

The Golds' stables stand beyond the [Clearance]{.name data-type="place/feature"} like every stable in Orzara, in a walled yard with its own training track among the garden-villas upriver of the city, well away from the Irons' yard. On a race day the Master of the Stables brings the teams across the line on the faction's license and through the avenues to the Racecourse, by a longer and grander route than the Irons take, so that the Heights can watch them pass.

At the Racecourse the Golds hold the lower tiers on the finishing straight and the patrons' boxes above them. The Golds' stand-clubs are fewer than the Irons' and their dues are higher, and their Brothers are as ready to fight in the lanes after the races as the Irons' are, though they do it less often and with better knives.

## The Present {#present}

The Golds lean to the claimant the court expects to be named. Their patrons are men of the Gilded houses, and a Gilded house wants an orderly succession and an Orzas who owes it something. The board has agreed that the Golds will cheer for the court's man at the accession, if he is named, and that their stand-clubs will keep the Heights quiet on the night the Orzas dies.

The board wants the **accession games contract** as much as the Master of the Irons does, and for a different reason: the faction that runs the accession games stands beside the new Orzas at his first public appearance, and the patrons mean to stand there. The board also wants to keep the concession of the stalls and the cookshops around the Racecourse Circle, which its patrons have held for a generation and which the Master of the Irons has asked the throne to give him.

The rivalry with the Irons is hot. The Golds won the last great race, at the feast of the last divine Orzas, and the Irons' stand-clubs have come looking for the Golds' clubs in the lanes below the Racecourse on every race night since.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The remounts.** A Gold patron's new team was bought from a legion breeder's yard, and a cavalry prefect says the horses were meant for his wing. He wants a party to prove it before the next race.
- **The box.** A patron of the Golds has died, and two houses claim his seat on the board and his box above the finishing line.

# The Truth of the Golds {#truth .secret}

The Golds' patrons owe the Irons. Their sons bet with the Irons' book, because its odds are better and its Keeper is discreet, and several of the sons who now sit in the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] have their names in it. The patrons do not know the full sum. The Master of the Irons does, and he has begun to ask the sons, one at a time and very politely, how they mean to vote on the concession.

The Golds' last great win was bought. A senator of a Gilded house paid the Master of the Irons to lose it, and the board does not know.
