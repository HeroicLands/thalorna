A tutor of Thordimalon, sitting down with a child whose talent has just been reported and entered on the register, says two things first: "You are not in trouble, and you are not a prisoner; you are on the list, which means nobody will come looking for you now. And you will never teach what we teach you to anyone who is not one of us—not your sister, not your mother, not your best friend, not one small trick. Everything else I tell you this year is detail."

Those two sentences are the Thordimalon (the Arcane Order). It is the dominant arcane guild of the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] and one of the most powerful institutions in the [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]], and it holds a monopoly on the practice, research and teaching of arcane lore throughout the regions under its authority. Only members of the Order are sanctioned to practice magic; everyone else is subject to investigation, sanction and, if necessary, elimination.

The Order is older than the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] it serves. The Vylarian Republic chartered it in 73 BF, and when the Republic became an Empire in Year 1 of the Vylarian reckoning the Order had held its charter for seventy-three years. Outsiders dispute the date of the founding; the Order does not. Its headquarters is [[place-alyssa|Alessa]], and nearly eight centuries after the charter it is still, on paper, the College of Arcane Philosophy within the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]]. Both the paper and the history behind it matter, so the rest of this note takes them in order: how the Order came to exist, how it is built, how it enforces its monopoly, and how it stands with the court it serves.

## The Age Before the Order

Before the Order, magic was practiced in many forms across western Ankaris, and nobody governed it. Mage-kings and mage-warlords rose with disturbing regularity, arcanists who seized temporal power and used magic as a weapon of conquest and coercion. Alchemical disasters poisoned rivers and blighted farmland. Summoning experiments tore holes in the fabric of reality. Seer-cults manipulated succession crises. Rogue necromancers raised armies of the dead. Whole communities were destroyed or transformed by experiments that went wrong, and at its worst a single mage-warlord commanding enough power could threaten the existence of a kingdom.

The [[place-helionis|Helionite]] city-states held the most sophisticated scholarship in the west, in every domain of philosophical inquiry and not only the arcane. The Epistémion (the Academy of Knowledge), a federation of scholars covering metaphysics, ethics, logic, natural philosophy, political thought and the arcane arts, had been the crown of Helionite civilization since about 400 BF (see [[lore-panepistfnd|The Founding of the Epistémion]]). Its College of Arcane Philosophy had built a rich body of theory, including the elemental classification and oppositional forces that became the [[affiliation-hexhodai|Zemor Hodhis]]. But the Epistémion (the Academy) was an intellectual society, not an enforcement body. It kept its standards through peer reputation and scholarly consensus, and it could not stop an individual mage, or a whole city-state, from turning that knowledge into a weapon.

### The Wars of Vylaria and Helionis

The Vylarian Republic, by 400 BF the dominant military and economic power of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], turned on [[place-helionis|Helionis]] in 335 BF and took the last of the city-states in 312 BF (see [[lore-helionscnq|The Conquest of Helionis]]). The conquest should have been straightforward. The legions were the finest in western Ankaris, and the Helionite poleis were fractured and outmatched in conventional terms. But the city-states had mages, and they used them.

The wars were the most devastating magical conflict in western Ankarian history. The city-states loosed sorceries that shattered formations, poisoned the land, called storms and earthquakes, and killed on a scale no conventional weapon could match. Mage-generals became the deciding factor in battle, and some became mage-warlords commanding forces their own city-states could not fully control. The Epistémion had theorized for generations about the ethical limits of arcane power, and it could do nothing to stop its own members from turning their knowledge to war.

The legions, for all their discipline, were being slaughtered by powers they could not fight with sword and shield. The [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (Senate) had two choices: retreat from Helionis, or find a weapon that could match magic with magic.

They found the dragons.

How the Republic secured the dragons' aid is in no account of the war. The records of the period, which the Order took over from the [Kandral]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, are incomplete, and the Senate's archives from those decades have suffered convenient losses. What the accounts do agree on is what certain dragons do: they destabilize magic in their presence. Arcane forces become unpredictable and dangerous near them. Spells misfire, wards collapse, enchantments unravel, and a caster whose working is denied its shape takes the energy back, often fatally.

The **[Dragon Riders]{.name data-type="affiliation"}**, warriors bonded to these dragons, became the Republic's decisive weapon. A Rider circling above a battlefield turned the enemy's greatest strength into its greatest weakness. Mage-generals who had shattered legions with a word saw their spells detonate in their own hands; shields that had turned a thousand arrows dissolved. The Helionite mages had built their whole strategy on magical superiority, and they were broken by its negation. Helionis fell, one city-state after another, over twenty-three years.

The victory cost the Republic heavily in lives, in treasure and in devastated land. The Senate had seen what free magic could do in war and was determined never to see it again. The Epistémion had produced brilliant theory and no restraint. The Republic wanted what the Epistémion had never been: an institution with the power to compel obedience.

## The Founding

The Order was made in stages, across two and a third centuries.

In 312 BF, the year the last Helionite city fell, the Senate reached into the Epistémion, took out the College of Arcane Philosophy, and placed it under a magistracy, the **Kandral**. The Epistémion's own phrase for it is a vivisection. There was no negotiation: the Republic had just used dragons to destroy the mages who took the field against it in western Ankaris, and the Senate had no patience for debate about academic freedom. The Kandral held the conquered Helionite masters—some on the register and lent like the rest, and a few on a roll of their own that was never lent and always guarded—and, in time, every arcanist born inside the Republic, on a register kept by an _usos_ (prefect) who changed with the year's magistracies. It had a register and a garrison and no doctrine, and its registered mages were lent out to whoever the Senate owed.

The Helionite scholars, humbled by the catastrophe their own members had helped cause, supplied the philosophical architecture of what came next: the classification of magic into schools, the idea of structured training through progressive ranks, the conviction that arcane practice could be systematized and controlled. The enforcement was wholly Vylarian: legal authority, military backing, and the memory of the dragons that had ended the war. The institution carries the marks of both parents, the sophistication of one and the iron discipline of the other.

In 75 BF the Senate ordered every registered practitioner in the Republic to bring their working-books to one place in the Alessan country, to be copied into a single register. That was [[lore-recensio|The Therona of Alessa]]. In the fourth month an unchanneled release destroyed the books and killed most of the Republic's senior arcanists. A tract of good Alessan land is bare to this day and is fenced at the Order's charge.

In 73 BF the surviving masters drafted their own terms and the Senate took them. That instrument is [[lore-kandrol|The Kandrol]], the charter of the Thordimalon and the act the Order reckons its foundation from. The Order accepts the monopoly and its enforcement, the registration of every talent, and war service on demand. In exchange it holds its own property, elects its own head, tries its own members in its own court, and answers to the state as a body and never as individuals. The clauses on property and on answering as a body were drafted by Helionite masters, and the Order has never published which. The same instrument constitutes the **[Sekran]{.name data-type="affiliation"}**, the Order's enforcement arm.

The remaining colleges of the Epistémion (ethics, logic, metaphysics, natural philosophy and the rest) stayed in place, diminished and shaken. The Order is technically the College of Arcane Philosophy within the Epistémion to this day, and it likes the arrangement, because it gives the Order a claim to authority over all knowledge, not only the arcane. In practice the Order dwarfs every other college combined, and the Epistémion has become something close to a university system run by its military intelligence department.

The Kandrol set out four principles that hold today:

Monopoly
: Only members of the Order may practice, research or teach arcane lore. All other practice is illegal.

Registration
: Every child who shows arcane talent must be reported to the Order and evaluated for enrollment.

Regulation
: The Order sets standards for arcane practice, keeps lists of prohibited magics, and controls the trade in arcane materials and texts.

Accountability
: The Order answers to the state (first the Senate, now the Orzas, as the Vylarians call their emperor), and the state grants it legal authority and institutional protection in return.

Many independent practitioners refused to submit. Some fled beyond Vylarian control and others went underground, and the early decades of the Order ([[lore-huntingyears|The Hunting Years]]) were taken up with bitter campaigns to enforce the monopoly: hunting down renegades, destroying rival traditions, and using the Sekran as the instrument of arcane compliance. The memory of the Helionite wars gave the enforcers their argument: _remember what happened the last time mages were free to do as they pleased._

### The Dragons

The Dragon Riders stood as a corps for more than two centuries after the last Helionite city fell. Around 45 BF the Republic, on the Order's counsel, did not renew its standing arrangement with the dragons, the Riders were disbanded, and the Eyrie of [[place-arvel|Arvel]] was sealed. The reason usually given is that the property that made the dragons invaluable in war, their destabilization of magic, made them intolerable in peace: the Order could not work as a research and regulatory body beside creatures that made spells misfire and experiments end in disaster.

The account that survives in the history notes is different. It holds that the Republic took the dragons' dead at Theradon and offered their return as the price of the dragons' help, and that the corps was kept on a lease renewed year by year until the Republic stopped renewing it ([[lore-dragondead|The Dragon Dead at Theradon]], [[lore-thebargain|The Bargain]] and [[lore-ridersdisb|The Lease Refused]] give it, and rest on a single source). The Republic's chancery and the Order's records say nothing of it, and a Senate archive that should hold the terms has lost the decades in question. Within a generation of the Order's founding the Dragon Riders were gone, and the Republic's relationship with dragons, and later the Empire's, had become a matter of silence.

Whether the dragons remember what was done, and whether they hold the successors of the Republic to account, are questions the Order would prefer not to hear asked. A scholar who pursues them is discouraged, and in some places endangered.

The Order outlasted the Republic's change into the Empire and helped to bring it about: its support was a large part of what legitimized the first Emperor's seizure of power. The Vylarian reckoning counts from that moment, with Year 1 the founding of the Vylarian Empire and earlier years dated Before the Founding (BF). The Order reckons its own founding from the Kandrol of 73 BF. Scholars outside the Order dispute the date, because the Order's records from the period are incomplete.

## Structure

### The Ranks

The Order sorts its members into five ranks, and a sixth place, that of the _zetmarendos_, stands alone above them. Each rank has its own rights, duties and limits.

- **Kosthemoi** (sing. _Kosthemos_)—Acolytes. The newly inducted, often children or adolescents identified as having arcane talent. They are evaluated, given basic instruction and brought into the Order's culture. They have no rights within the Order beyond instruction, and they may not practice magic unsupervised. Many never advance; those without enough talent or temperament are redirected into administrative or support roles.

- **Galethoi** (sing. _Galethos_)—Apprentices. Those whose talent warrants formal training. Each is assigned to a _marendos_ (master) as mentor, and the education combines arcane theory, practical instruction and, just as important, indoctrination in the Order's institutional values. A _galethos_ (student) may practice magic only under direct supervision. The apprenticeship usually lasts five to ten years; gifted students move faster and mediocre ones may languish far longer.

- **Thermoi** (sing. _Therman_)—Journeymen. Qualified practitioners who have passed the Order's examinations and are licensed to practice independently. They are the backbone of day-to-day operations: they staff the regional chapters, carry out assignments, conduct research under direction, and provide the magical services that kingdoms and merchants pay handsomely for. A _therman_ (adept) has a voice in chapter meetings and no vote in governance.

- **Marendoi** (sing. _Marendos_)—Masters. The governing rank. A _marendos_ has shown mastery of a discipline and the political skill to survive the Order's internal politics. Every _marendos_ has a vote in the chapter's council, and every chapter is governed by its council of _marendoi_ (masters). Seniority counts for far more than the vote: a _marendos_ who has held the rank for decades, controls key resources and has trained a generation of _galethoi_ (students) carries much more weight than one newly raised. The informal gradations of influence among the _marendoi_ are as complex and unwritten as the politics of a royal court.

- **Holmarendoi** (sing. _Holmarendos_)—Senior Masters. The highest rank below the _zetmarendos_ (archmaster), and the Order's cardinals. Each holds a seat on the **[Verdan Maldor]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}**, the Order's supreme governing body. Smaller or less important chapters may have none, while the great chapters of the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]] and [[place-helionis|Helionis]] send several. The existing Verdan appoints each new _holmarendos_ (prelate), which makes elevation an intensely political process. A _holmarendos_ usually leads a chapter, though some prefer to wield influence from the Verdan without the burden of local administration, and some chapters are led by senior _marendoi_ who lack the connections or the ambition to claim a seat.

### The Verdan Maldor

The Verdan Maldor (the Great Council) is made up of the _holmarendoi_ (prelates) drawn from chapters across western Ankaris. It sets policy, settles disputes between chapters and colleges, manages the Order's resources, and pronounces judgment on questions of arcane law that exceed any single chapter's authority.

The _zetmarendos_ leads the Verdan, and holds the single most powerful position in the arcane world. The _holmarendoi_ elect the _zetmarendos_ for life, though "life" has occasionally been shortened by political rivals. The _zetmarendos_ presides over the Verdan, speaks for the Order to foreign powers, and may overrule individual chapters on matters that concern the whole Order. Wise _zetmarendoi_ use that power sparingly.

### Chapter Governance

Each regional chapter is governed by its council of _marendoi_, one vote each. The Order is not a democracy. Formal votes are rare, and most decisions come out of a deliberation in which every _marendos_ must be heard and some voices carry far more weight than others. Seniority, reputation, control of resources, networks of former students and alliances with powerful _holmarendoi_ settle most questions long before a vote is called.

Most chapters are led by a _holmarendos_, who is both the chapter's internal authority and its representative on the Verdan Maldor. A chapter without one is governed by a senior _marendos_, often one angling for elevation and using the chapter as a stepping stone. The alliances, feuds and slow accumulation of favors owed and debts called in are, in a word, arcane. Outsiders find the process incomprehensible, and insiders find it consuming.

### The Zemor Hodhis

The Order teaches and sorts its members by the [[affiliation-hexhodai|Zemor Hodhis]], the Six Ways, a classification of all magical phenomena into six schools, the _rheza_ (currents), arranged on a hexagonal diagram called the Thizaron (the Seal). That note sets out the six _rheza_, the three oppositions that the Thizaron draws between them, and [[affiliation-kentra|Kentra]], the mastery of all six. Within the Order it is doctrine: a _kosthemos_ (initiate) is taught it as the structure of magic itself, and the Order's politics run along its lines.

Two consequences matter here. Alliances across fellowships form most easily between neighboring _rheza_ on the Thizaron, so a _marendos_ seeking allies on a council looks first to the fellowships that flank their own. And the Order's promotions favor the politician over the transcendent: the practitioners who learn to work across all six schools, the Kentra, ought to be the natural _holmarendoi_, but the _holmarendoi_ are likelier to be gifted politicians than gifted mages, and many a Kentra mage has spent a career as a quietly respected _marendos_ while less talented and more ambitious colleagues rose to the Verdan.

### The Zulethalar (Fellowships)

Each of the six _rheza_ has a corresponding **Zulen** (pl. _Zulethalar_), a fellowship of practitioners who share it. The Zulethalar (fellowships) work something like religious orders within a church: they cut across the formal hierarchy of chapters and colleges, binding together mages who share a philosophical orientation wherever they are stationed and whatever practical discipline they study.

Zulen [[affiliation-pyrethos|Pirethos]]
: The Fellowship of Fire. Aggressive, ambitious and politically influential. Fire mages are drawn to combat magic and hold a disproportionate share of the Sekran.

Zulen [[affiliation-hydalis|Hidalis]]
: The Fellowship of Water. Patient, adaptable, and associated with healing and divination. Water mages often serve as mediators in internal disputes.

Zulen [[affiliation-zepharis|Zefaris]]
: The Fellowship of Air. Subtle, cerebral, and drawn to illusion, communication and the magic of the unseen. It is the smallest fellowship and is heavily represented among the Order's intelligence operatives.

Zulen [[affiliation-physera|Fisera]]
: The Fellowship of Earth. Practical, conservative and deeply rooted in tradition. Earth mages dominate alchemy, herblore and the crafting of magical materials, and the fellowship is the largest by membership.

Zulen [[affiliation-sideros|Sideris]]
: The Fellowship of Metal. Precise and disciplined, associated with binding, warding and the imposition of arcane order. Metal mages have strong ties to the [College of Enchantment]{.name data-type="affiliation"}.

Zulen [[affiliation-pneumenos|Neumenos]]
: The Fellowship of Spirit. Mystical, intense and sometimes unsettling: summoners, seers and practitioners who deal with entities beyond the material world. The other fellowships regard it warily, and the Sekran supervises it most closely.

Kentra mages belong to no Zulen (fellowship), or rather have outgrown the one they began in. Some keep honorary ties to their original fellowship; others are regarded with awe and suspicion by former colleagues who can no longer follow how they think. There is no formal Zulen Kentra, though Kentra mages tend to find each other and form loose networks. The Order has debated whether to formalize a fellowship for them, and the Kentra mages have always resisted: organizing a group defined by transcending categories into a category of its own misses the point.

A mage's Zulen is a powerful axis of identity within the Order, often more important to them than chapter or college. Mages of one Zulen share a way of perceiving magic that makes for deep mutual understanding. The rivalries between Zulethalar whose _rheza_ stand opposite each other on the Thizaron are ancient, bitter and personal, and they show up in everything from academic debates to political alliances to the assignment of apprentices.

### Zulen Assignment

Every _kosthemos_ entering the Order is evaluated to determine an elemental affinity, and the evaluation is not a free choice. The Order's examiners decide which school a student will follow, on a combination of arcane testing, temperamental assessment and, though the Order does not admit it, institutional need. In a year with too few Hidalis candidates and too many Pirethos, borderline students may be nudged toward Water whatever they prefer.

Reassignment is rare and treated as extraordinary. A mage who has spent years learning to perceive magic through one school cannot simply switch, because the cognitive habits run too deep. The few who transfer successfully are usually met with mild suspicion by their new Zulen and lingering resentment by the one they left.

### The Colleges

The Order also organizes its practical disciplines into colleges, a structure that complements the Zulethalar. A Zulen reflects how a mage perceives magic, and a college reflects what the mage does with it.

The [College of Thaumaturgy]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: general magical practice; the largest and most influential college

The [College of Alchemy]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: transmutation, potion-craft and material science

The [College of Divination]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: prophecy, scrying and the interpretation of signs

Each college keeps its own curriculum, its own internal hierarchy and its own political agenda. The rivalries between colleges are intense and sometimes bitter, and all of them answer to the Verdan Maldor.

A mage thus holds three overlapping affiliations: a **Chapter** (geographic, where they are stationed), a **College** (practical, what discipline they study) and a **Zulen** (philosophical, how they perceive the arcane). The three axes of loyalty make the Order's internal politics extraordinarily complex. A Pirethos mage in the College of Alchemy at the Helionis chapter may have more in common with a Pirethos mage of the College of Thaumaturgy in Alessa than with the Hidalis mage beside them at chapter meetings.

### The Thordimthilandon

Every member of the Order, from the lowliest _kosthemos_ to the _zetmarendos_, is bound by the **[Thordimthilandon]{.name data-type="lore/law"}**, the Order's written code of conduct. The code is extensive and covers everything from the storage of arcane materials to the protocols for messages between chapters, but its rules come down to two principles, which the tutor of the opening gives a new _kosthemos_ in the same breath:

**Do not be conspicuous. Do not bring attention or controversy upon the Order.**

A member who draws public notice to the Order's work, whether by a spectacular display, a political indiscretion or simply being too visible, is a liability. The Order's power depends partly on mystique, and mystique depends on discretion. A mage who frightens the populace, antagonizes a local lord, or shows powers that make people ask whether the Order is too dangerous to tolerate threatens the institution itself.

**You may never teach magic to anyone outside the Order.**

This is the code's most absolute prohibition. The monopoly rests on controlling the knowledge of magic as well as its practice. A member who teaches an outsider, even a single cantrip, even a family member, even from the best of motives, faces the harshest sanctions the Order can impose. The Order enforces the rule with paranoid rigor, because the monopoly is only as strong as its control over arcane education.

The practical result is that magic is rare in the daily life of most people. The average farmer, merchant or nobleman has never seen real magic performed. They know the Order exists, they know mages are real, and they may have heard stories. Magic happens behind the walls of chapter houses, in private consultations with wealthy patrons, in sealed laboratories and guarded libraries. It does not happen in the street, in the marketplace, or on the battlefield except in the direst extremity. Most people live their whole lives without seeing a spell cast.

The invisibility is deliberate. The Order learned from the catastrophes of the age before its founding that visible magic breeds fear and fear breeds hostility. Better to be the eccentric academics in the walled compound on the edge of town than the sorcerers the mob comes for with torches.

### The Chapter Houses

The chapter houses are formally [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]] institutions, not Order ones, and the distinction is legal as well as cosmetic: the Kandrol chartered the Order inside the Epistémion, and the right to the door belongs to the Epistémion. The sign above the door bears the Epistémion seal. The scholars within teach logic, language, natural philosophy and law alongside arcane subjects, and to ordinary people a chapter house is a house of learning staffed by scholars of every discipline. Most people who deal with one never meet anything overtly magical. The Order controls every chapter house through its hierarchy, and the scholarly face is one of its most effective instruments of influence.

A typical chapter house is a walled compound, sometimes grand and sometimes modest, with libraries, laboratories, lecture halls, workshops, living quarters and extensive archives. The architecture varies by region (austere stone in Vylaria, elegant courtyards in Helionis, fortified compounds in the [[affiliation-provncmktr|Province of Moktur]]) and the function is the same everywhere: study, research and quiet power.

Four things keep a chapter house running:

- **Money.** Endowments gathered over centuries, fees for magical services, income from properties and investments, and above all patronage. Wealthy individuals, noble families, merchant houses and governments support their local chapter in exchange for access. A patron who funds a library wing or endows a research position earns the right to request favors (a consultation, a divination, an enchantment) that the Order provides discreetly and at its own pace. The system is never a crude transaction. It runs on a fiction of scholarly generosity and grateful reciprocity, and everyone involved understands the exchange.
- **Clerks.** _Galethoi_ and _thermoi_ (adepts) are often employed or loaned as scribes, mathematicians, legal scholars, cartographers, translators and advisors in fields that have nothing to do with magic. A provincial court that cannot afford a full _marendos_, or does not want one, may still rely on Order-trained clerks to keep its accounts, draft its contracts and interpret its laws. The service buys more than goodwill: it embeds the Order in the administration of every community it touches, gives its junior members practical experience and the chapter an income, and places eyes and ears in every institution that matters.
- **Schools.** Many chapter houses are the finest schools in their region, teaching mathematics, law, rhetoric, history, natural philosophy and languages to the children of patrons and local elites, with no arcane content at all. This is perhaps the Order's most far-reaching influence, because it decides what gets taught: the legal principles its _thermoi_ instill in a generation of provincial magistrates, the histories its scholars write, the mathematical and philosophical frameworks it makes foundational. A lord educated at the local chapter house thinks in categories the Order defined, remembers history the way the Order tells it and reasons about law the way the Order taught him, without ever learning a spell. That is what happens when one institution controls the infrastructure of knowledge.
- **Knowledge of the powerful.** The _marendoi_ know the secrets of the powerful, since divination is an effective intelligence tool, hold debts of gratitude from patrons they have served, and keep up relations with every significant power in their region. They do not rule, which would be conspicuous. They advise, they know and they remember. A lord who crosses the local chapter may find that his court astrologer has urgent business elsewhere, that the ward on his treasury has lapsed, or that the Order's healers are regrettably unavailable when his heir falls ill.

### Regional Chapters

The Order keeps regional chapters in every significant city and territory within its sphere, and their strength varies enormously.

[[affiliation-provinclys|Alessa]]
: The Order's headquarters, at maximum strength and influence. The Verdan Maldor meets here.

[[place-helionis|Helionis]]
: Deeply rooted, as the intellectual birthplace of the Order, and among the most prestigious chapters, with the finest libraries and scholars. Local philosophical-magical traditions have negotiated autonomy, and the Sekran treads carefully. The chapter's oldest teaching line runs to Theradon, not Thirenae.

[[affiliation-provincvld|Province of Salkalon]] and [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]]
: Solid chapters with reliable imperial backing.

[[affiliation-provncmktr|Moktur]]
: A limited presence. The frontier military _therkoi_ (governors) tolerate the Order and do not prioritize its agenda.

[[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]]
: Present, but under royal oversight. Mages answer to the Provenzian crown as well as to the Order.

[[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]
: Active and politically constrained. The merchant guilds see the Order as a tool of Vylarian influence and have restricted its activities.

[[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia Region]]
: A minimal presence. A handful of chapters in major cities work cautiously in a culture deeply hostile to institutional magic.

[[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]
: A substantial urban presence centered on the **[University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"}**, the largest Epistémion campus outside Vylaria, with several Zulen chapterhouses in residence. The Order has considerable influence in the capital and the larger towns, rivaling or exceeding the [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Circle]] and [[affiliation-groveconcl|The Grove Council]]. Outside the cities its reach is fiercely contested by traditional institutions and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]], and agents who probe the fae-touched interior do so at real personal risk.

[[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]] and the Nordmen kingdoms
: No presence. The Nordmen have their own velvur traditions and no interest in southern magical bureaucracy.

[[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]]
: Small but growing. The League's commercial interests create demand for Order services.

### Beyond Western Ankaris

The Order has tried to extend its influence across the rest of Thalorna with mixed results. It presents these efforts as partnerships, offering expertise, magical services and the prestige of association with the most powerful arcane institution in the west. In practice it is looking for footholds from which to expand its monopoly, and most foreign powers see that clearly.

[[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]
: The Order has no presence in Au'Khelathu and no prospect of one. Its agents visit the delta ports as foreign scholars, agents of the Gar-Au's court shadow them while they are there, and the few who have tried to recruit Khelathi mages have been expelled. The Khelathi priestly-magical establishment is older, more deeply rooted and more powerful within its own borders than the Order, and it has no intention of ceding authority. The Order chafes and has no leverage to change the terms.

[[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]]
: The Order is all but forbidden. Its members are confined to diplomatic enclaves, essentially embassy compounds, and are forbidden on pain of death to practice magic outside the walls. Tantsou's own arcane traditions are sophisticated and ancient, and the imperial court sees the Order as a foreign intelligence service in scholarly robes. The Order keeps its enclaves mainly to gather intelligence and as a token of diplomatic presence.

[[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]]
: A minimal presence. The desert peoples have their own traditions and no central authority for the Order to negotiate with. Individual agents sometimes travel the caravan routes at their own risk.

[[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]]
: One licensed factor at the river mouth below [[place-chandrapur2|Chendrapur]], and nothing else. There is no chapter house, no school and no Order right to examine anybody. The subcontinent's own traditions are mature and licensed by the Varnakan schools themselves. The political fragmentation the Order would ordinarily work through is no help, because what it would need is a place in a licensing structure that has no vacancy, and no ruler's favor can supply one.

[[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]]
: No presence. The island is too remote and too self-sufficient to have drawn serious Order attention, and its one scholar, at [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]], studies there on his own account.

[[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]]
: No presence. The Matriarchy has its own magical traditions and no interest in western institutions.

## The Sekran

The Sekran, the Arcane Inquisition, is the Order's enforcement arm, the division that makes sure no one practices magic outside the Order's authority. Its members, the **sekeroi**, are investigators, hunters and, when necessary, executioners. The Kandrol constituted it a clause before the Order itself, and its first commission, to establish what happened at Alessa and who did it, has never been closed.

A _sekeros_'s notary, writing up a search for the chapter's file, explains how the work is meant to look: "The _sekeros_ (inquirer) can compel testimony, search property, seize arcane materials and detain a suspect. In Vylaria that rests on imperial law, and I cite the statute. Further out we rest on the _sekeros_'s own standing, on frightening people, and on hired hands, and I cite nothing, because there is nothing to cite. What goes in the file is the outcome, and the best outcome is the quietest one: a name on the register."

### Methods

The Sekran chooses its method by the severity of the offense:

Recruitment
: The preferred outcome. Most unsanctioned practitioners are simply enrolled in the Order, often on restrictive terms. This is especially common with children and adolescents who have manifested talent without anyone reporting them.

Confiscation
: Arcane materials, texts and artifacts held by unauthorized persons are seized and either destroyed or added to the Order's archives.

Restriction
: Practitioners who cannot or will not be enrolled may be placed under binding oaths or magical constraints that limit their abilities.

Imprisonment
: Persistent offenders and those judged dangerous are held in Order facilities, magical prisons kept at several places across western Ankaris.

Elimination
: In extreme cases the Sekran is authorized to kill: rogue mages who threaten public safety, practitioners of categorically forbidden arts, and fugitives who resist everything else.

### Reputation

The Sekran is feared, respected and resented in roughly equal measure. In Vylaria and its core territories the _sekeroi_ (inquirers) are seen as a necessary protection against the chaos unregulated magic would bring, and official history keeps the age before the Order alive as a cautionary tale. Further from the center of Order power the _sekeroi_ are often seen as Vylarian enforcers imposing foreign authority, and their investigations can provoke as much resistance as cooperation.

The Sekran also runs its own network of agents, informants and sympathizers across western Ankaris, who report signs of unsanctioned magic. The network overlaps with the Vylarian Empire's own intelligence apparatus without being part of it, and the two cooperate while each guards its sources and methods.

## Character

The Order is ancient, conservative and institutional. It thinks in decades and centuries rather than days and months. Internal deliberation can take years, and the Order's decision-making is famously slow. Its factions argue endlessly over doctrine, jurisdiction, the interpretation of arcane theory and the right response to every conceivable threat.

Once the Order decides and sets itself in motion, it is a juggernaut, patient and relentless, with resources most individuals and some nations cannot match. It does not forget, and it does not forgive defiance of its authority. A Sekran investigation may lie dormant for years and be reopened when new intelligence surfaces or political conditions change.

The conservatism is the Order's greatest strength and its greatest weakness. Deep institutional memory and a cautious approach have carried it through wars, dynastic changes and the rise and fall of nations. The same conservatism makes it slow to adapt to new magical discoveries, new political realities and new threats.

The shrewdest minds within the Order have begun to see, though they would never say it aloud, that the Order may outlast the Empire that created it. Its chapters, colleges and practitioners span borders the legions can no longer defend. Its authority rests on more than imperial decree: on the universal need for magical services and the universal fear of magical chaos. Kings, princes and guild-masters who would never bow to a Vylarian emperor still send their talented children to Order academies, call on Order mages to ward their castles, and accept Order arbitration in arcane disputes. The institution has outgrown its patron.

## Relationship with the Vylarian Court

The Order and the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian]] state have been intertwined since the Order's founding. Each needs the other, uses the other and does not entirely trust the other.

### The Order as Imperial Instrument

The Order is enormously valuable to the Empire, and the court knows it. Through the Order, Vylaria projects power across northwestern Ankaris, even where the legions have no presence and the Orzas's writ has no legal force. A ruler under Order sanction can be crippled: court mages withdrawn, magical defenses stripped, arcane services cut off. The threat of censure is a diplomatic weapon the Empire uses with precision, and more than one foreign prince has found that defying Vylarian interests means losing the most powerful magical institution on the continent.

The Order supplies battlefield magic, intelligence through divination and arcane defenses for critical installations. It also lends credibility. Its endorsement legitimizes a ruler or a policy, and its disapproval can destabilize either. In return the Empire grants it legal authority, institutional protection, tax exemptions, and the political and military muscle to enforce the monopoly where the Order's own power is insufficient.

### Espionage and Counter-Espionage

Neither side simply commands the other. The Order has its own agenda, factions and secrets, and the court knows it. _Kurthoi_ (senators), generals and court officials cultivate informants among the _marendoi_, bribe junior mages and plant agents in the Order's administration. Everyone at court wants to know what the Order is planning before the Order announces it.

The Order is not naive about this. Senior _sekeroi_ and imperial spymasters watch one another, trade favors and betray one another on purpose. It is a shadow game played beneath the surface of official cooperation.

### The Decline Problem, and the Opportunity

The Empire's decline has strained the arrangement to its limits. As the Empire weakens, the Order's ability to project power through imperial channels shrinks. Former provinces such as [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]], [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia Region]] and [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] no longer accept Vylarian authority, and the Order's chapters there must negotiate their own terms with local powers.

For some factions within the Order this is also an opportunity. The decline forces the Order to build direct relationships with the successor states, relationships that no longer pass through the Vylarian court. In Provenzia the Order works under royal oversight, and the Provenzian crown has come to depend on its services. In Taruzel the merchant guilds restrict the Order and also hire its mages. Even in Tarvénia, where the culture is deeply hostile to institutional magic, local lords quietly send their gifted children to Order academies rather than leave the talent untrained.

The Order is becoming a cross-realm institution whose authority derives from no single state but from its monopoly on essential services. The Vylarian reckoning still counts from the founding of the Empire in lands that no longer obey the Orzas, and in the same way the Order's standards, training, certifications and prohibitions are becoming the shared framework of arcane practice across western Ankaris, a framework that will outlast the Vylarian throne.

The Order argues the point internally. Traditionalists hold to the imperial relationship, fearing that without military backing the Order would face challenges it could not survive. Reformers argue that it should cultivate its role as a pan-continental institution, the one organization every kingdom needs and none can replace. The most ambitious of them talk of a future in which the Order shapes the political order itself, crowning and uncrowning rulers by granting or withdrawing arcane sanction. The debate is among the most consequential in western Ankaris, and it is unresolved.

## The Order and the Tamzir

The Order's pursuit of the fugitive siblings aboard [[affiliation-thetamzir|the Tamzir]] shows the Order's methods at their most relentless. The sister, a damaged arcane prodigy, was the subject of Order experimentation. Her escape is a security breach, because she knows things the Order would prefer to keep secret, and an affront to its authority, because she is an extremely powerful unsanctioned practitioner beyond its control.

The Order hunts the siblings through its preferred channels: Vylarian imperial agents, [[affiliation-auricompct|guild]] contacts in Taruzelian ports, Sekran investigators and hired bounty hunters. Its approach is patient and methodical. It does not need to catch the Tamzir today; it needs only to make sure the crew can never stop running.
