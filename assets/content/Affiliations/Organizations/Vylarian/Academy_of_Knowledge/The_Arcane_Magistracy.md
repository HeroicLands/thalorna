The archive of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]] (the Thordimalon) keeps the register of the Arcane Magistracy (the Kandral) in a row of cedar boxes, one to each year from 312 to 75 BF. Each year opens with the name of that year's _usos_ (prefect), a senator, and runs on through every arcanist the Republic held: name, place of birth, current, the date entered, and a column of loans, each one a name of a house or a general and a sum. A Thordimalon archivist showing the register to a new _galethos_ (student) turns first to a page near the end, where one arcanist is lent to three men in the same year. "Two of them were at war with each other," he says. "That is what the Order was made to stop."

## What It Was

The Kandral was the magistracy the [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} made in 312 BF, the year the last city of [[place-helionis|Helionis]] fell (see [[lore-helionscnq|The Conquest of Helionis]]). [[affiliation-seldan|The Vilérian Senate]] (the Seldan) took the College of Arcane Philosophy out of [[affiliation-panepistmm|The Academy]] (the Epistémion) and set it under the Kandral, and the Kandral held the conquered Helionite masters and, in time, every arcanist born in the Republic. It had a register and a garrison and no doctrine. It taught nothing, published nothing and decided nothing. Its arcanists were the state's property, lent out by the year, and the loan was the whole of its work.

The _usos_ changed every year with the Republic's other magistracies, and nobody served long enough to own the office. In the Republic's last century, as its politics turned violent, the register became a weapon. Factions borrowed arcanists from one another's prefects, and a name on the roll was a thing that could be sold.

## The Register and the Kept Roll

Most of the Kandral's arcanists stand on the main register, lent and returned year after year. A few stand on a roll of their own, the **kept roll**, which carries no loans at all. The masters on it were never lent and were always under guard, and the register says who guarded them and nothing about why.

## The End of the Magistracy

In 75 BF the _usos_ of the year ordered every registered arcanist in the Republic to bring their working-books to one place in the Alessan country, to be copied into a single register the Senate could hold. That was [[lore-recensio|The Therona of Alessa]], and in its fourth month an unchanneled release killed most of the Republic's senior arcanists and burned the books. Two years later the surviving masters drafted [[lore-kandrol|The Kandrol]], the charter that made the Thordimalon, and the Senate accepted it. The Kandral ended there. Its register, its records and everything it held passed onto the Order's books and off the state's.

## What Survives of It

The register is the Order's now, and so is the habit it taught. A name on the register is still the best outcome a hearing of [[affiliation-sekran|The Arcane Inquisition]] (the Sekran) can file, and the Order's own register of every talent in the Empire begins, page for page, where the Kandral's stops. The Senate's own archive of the same decades has gaps, and the Order's copy of the register is the fuller record of the two.

## What It Is Fighting About {#present}

The Kandral has not sat since 75 BF, and nobody fights over it. Its register survives, and the Order owns it. The cedar boxes stand in the Order's archive, a new student is shown the page where one arcanist was lent to three men in a year, and the Senate's own archive of the same decades has gaps, so the Order holds the fuller record of the Republic's last arcane century. While the Order argues whether it should crown and uncrown rulers, the register is its record of the century when the state lent its arcanists to whichever faction paid.

# The Last Year {#truth .secret}

The page for 75 BF lists the kept roll as it stood at the Therona. None of the masters on the kept roll appears on the roll of the Therona's dead, and none appears on the Order's first register two years later. Their names are ruled through with no word in the margin.
