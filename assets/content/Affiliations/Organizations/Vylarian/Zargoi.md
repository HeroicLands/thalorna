A captain of one of the Irons' stand-clubs gives a new Brother of the Stand his ring and his rules on the same evening: "Wear it to the Racecourse and wear it home. Pay your dues the day after the dole, before the rent-man comes. If the club calls, come. If you are knifed in the club's quarrel, the club buries you and keeps your widow in bread for a year. And never bet against the gray, because the Master keeps the book and he will know."

## Overview

The Zargoi, the Irons, are the larger of the two racing factions of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]], and the faction of the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]]. Their name is the plural of _zargon_ (iron) in [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari]], and their color is the gray of plain iron, worn as a ring on a cord or a band on the sleeve. They race against the [[affiliation-ethloi|Ethloi]], the Golds, at [[place-rathaunamelvar|The Racecourse]], and the rivalry that begins on the track ends in brawls in the lanes on most race nights.

A racing faction is several things at once. It is the owner of the teams that race in its color, their stables, their horses and their drivers. It is a crowd that sits together in the stands and shouts together, and that crowd is the loudest voice the common citizens of Orzara have. It is a burial club and a mutual fund, because a Brother of the Stand who dies in his club's quarrel is buried by it. And it is a street army, because the stand-clubs fight the Golds' clubs in the lanes and anyone else their master asks them to.

The Irons draw their crowd from the veterans' stairs, the cookshops and the porters of the Wharves. A legion's stair is often an Iron stair to a man, and the old soldiers who sit together in the Racecourse sit in the same order they stood in the dole queue that morning.

## The Stables Beyond the Line

No draft animal is kept inside the thrima, and the Irons' horses are no exception. The faction's stables stand in the outskirts beyond the [Clearance]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, beside the gate-yard nearest the Racecourse, in a walled yard of their own where the teams are trained on a track of beaten earth. On a race day the Master of the Stables brings the teams across the line by the gate-road under the eye of the [[affiliation-citywatchorz|City Watch]], each team on a license that costs the faction a fortune a year, and walks them through the lanes to the Racecourse with a guard of the stand-clubs around them. The sight of the Irons' teams coming up through Nerval at dawn is the start of every race day in the Warrens.

## The Book

The Irons' Master owns the faction's betting books, and the books are the faction's real wealth. Every race day the Keeper of the Book takes wagers in the stands and in the taverns of the Warrens, at odds he sets himself, on the Irons' teams and on the Golds'. The books are licensed by nobody and closed by nobody. A wager the bettor cannot pay is written into the book as a debt, and the Keeper's men collect it.

## The Stand-Clubs and the Street

The Irons' crowd is organized into **stand-clubs**, each holding its own block of the Racecourse's seats and each with its own captain, its own tavern in the Warrens and its own dues. A club's Brothers sit together, shout together and leave together, and when they meet a club of the Golds in a lane after the races they fight together. The captains answer to the Master, and the Master can turn the clubs out into the streets on any day he chooses.

The clubs are the gangs of the Warrens in all but name. They keep their own lanes, take a share from the cookshops that sell on race days, and settle their quarrels with the Golds' clubs by knife as often as by race. A man the clubs want gone is beaten on a race night in the crush of the crowd, and the Watch counts it as one more brawl.

## The Present {#present}

The Master of the Irons wants the **accession games contract**. When the old Orzas dies, the new one gives games at his accession, and the faction that supplies the teams, the drivers and the spectacle for those games takes the throne's silver for a season and the city's gratitude for a reign. The Master means it to be the Irons. He also wants a concession of the stalls and the cookshops around the Racecourse Circle, which the Golds' patrons have held for a generation.

The Irons lean to no claimant. The Master sells the crowd. He has let it be known that the Irons will cheer whichever claimant is named, if that claimant's household promises the games contract, and that they will be in the streets on the night the Orzas dies whatever happens. Every house that expects to need the streets that night has sent someone to drink with the captains, and the Master has received them all.

The rivalry with the Golds is as hot as it has been in years. The Golds won the last great race at the feast of the last divine Orzas, and the Irons' stand-clubs have fought the Golds' in the lanes below the Racecourse every race night since. The [[affiliation-citywatchorz|City Watch]] stands between them and arrests the men it can reach.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The son's debt.** A senator's household pays a party to recover a page of the Irons' book before the senator learns what his son wrote in it.
- **The teams at dawn.** The Irons' teams cross the Clearance at first light on race day, and somebody means them not to reach the Racecourse.
- **The captain's price.** A claimant's agent wants the captains of the Irons' biggest stand-clubs bought before the Master can sell them to someone else.

# The Truth of the Irons {#truth .secret}

The book does not stop at the Warrens. Sons of senatorial houses come down from the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] to bet with the Irons because the Irons' odds are better and their book is discreet, and a son who loses more than his allowance writes his name in it. The Master holds those debts, and some of the sons now sit on their fathers' benches in the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]].

The Master of the Irons fixed a race for a senator. Three seasons ago a _kurthos_ (senator) of a Gilded house asked, in a letter in his own hand, for the Golds' team to win the great race on his patron's feast day, and paid for it. The Master told his First Driver to lose by a length, and the senator's friends won heavily on the Golds. The Master kept the letter. It is the most dangerous paper he owns, because it can ruin the senator and the Master both: a race fixed on a divine Orzas's feast day cheats every man who bet on it, and the senator's friends are not the only ones who did. Each of them knows the other has reason to keep it quiet, and each has begun to wonder whether the succession changes that.
