Watch a clerk at a chapterhouse counter take in a traveler's note and you see the guild's whole trade. He has under a minute to decide to believe in a slip of sealed paper. Say he works at the Ôrzâra chapter, and he checks three things from habit: the wax (red for a retail note, blue for a traveler's note, gold for a master note), the serial against the chapter's register, and the moneylender's mark above the chapter's countersignature. "People think we lend money," he tells the apprentice at the next desk. "We don't, mostly. We tell strangers whom to trust. The lending is what we do in the afternoons."

## Overview

The Danvar, "the Bankers' College," is the moneylenders' guild chartered under the [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]], and the visible banking institution of the post-imperial commonwealth. On paper it is one of the thirty-six trade guilds the Tevran charters, alongside the smiths, the scribes, the cartographers and the rest. In practice it is by some distance the largest and most consequential of them. Its member houses run the banking system that most large-value commerce in the commonwealth depends on, and its chapterhouses outside the empire are the working front end of the [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]]'s central-bank functions. Its senior partners carry political weight, and their presence at court rivals the senior nobility's.

The guild works in every member territory of the Tevran Maldor: [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], the five [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]] and [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]]. Its charter under the Tevran Maldor is two and a half centuries old and draws on older moneylender associations that predate the imperial consolidation of banking. Its chief officer is the **Danvor Maldor** ("Great Banker"), elected from the senior partners of the great houses.

The guild's position rests on a **polite fiction**. Inside Vylaria the Tarmex Orzan keeps direct offices, and the Danvar is visibly what it is: a guild of moneylenders who clear through the imperial treasury. Outside the empire, in the Aurèldían kingdoms and Byzaría, the Tarmex keeps no presence, and the chapterhouses are the entire visible face of the system. Say a Provenzian merchant carries a traveler's note from his home moneylender to a Byzarian receiving moneylender. He deals only with Danvar members at both ends, the chapter clears the note through Danvar channels back to Ôrzâra, and the imperial treasury sits at the back of the chain, drawing its 2% issuance fee. The Provenzian crown's official correspondence calls the whole transaction "Danvar-honored." Every party endorses the fiction tacitly, and it matters. (The [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]] note describes the structure behind it.)

The guild survives because it supplies what the small post-imperial polities cannot supply themselves, a continent-spanning banking system. No Aurèldían kingdom is big enough to build credible international banking alone. The Byzarian League's merchant houses lack the reserves one by one, and even Byzaría as a whole could not match the depth of credit the Danvar offers. By taking part, the kingdoms get banking infrastructure, mutual recognition of moneylenders' credentials, and the ability to move large sums across borders without carrying bullion. Generation after generation they judge the 2% fee flowing quietly to imperial coffers worth paying.

## Structure

The guild has three tiers.

### The Great Houses

About twenty-five **great houses** dominate the guild. They are the moneylender families whose reserves, chapter ties and political connections put them at the top of the trade, and each operates in several cities. The **House of Calventian** keeps offices in Ôrzâra, [[place-olvestria|Ólvestria]], Provènzia, [[place-beravel|Béravel]] and Byzaría. The **House of Maximian Tertian** has a similar footprint. The **House of Verrius** concentrates on the Vylarian commercial cities. The **House of Therion** is Heliónite in origin and has grown continent-spanning. The **House of Aldovardian** is Tarvénian, which makes it unusual, since most great houses began in Vylaria or Heliónis and expanded outward.

Each great house holds standing accounts with the Tarmex Orzan (or with the Danvar's chapter clearing arrangement in non-imperial territory) on a scale no smaller house can approach. The great houses issue the master notes drawn directly on Tarmex accounts, redeem the large traveler's notes, lend to the Aurèldían crowns and take the guild's political positions. Their senior partners sit on the **Council of the Danvori**, the guild's governing body, which meets quarterly at Ôrzâra.

### The Lesser Houses

Several hundred **lesser houses** work at smaller scales, typically in one or two cities with modest reserves and a regional clientele. A lesser house in a provincial Aurèldían town might serve a few hundred regular depositors, issue retail notes for local circulation, cash traveler's notes for visiting merchants within its means, and keep a clearing account with one or two great houses to settle balances between banks. For most commoners and minor nobles outside the major commercial capitals, a lesser house is what banking means.

An existing member of standing sponsors a lesser house for admission, and the **Censors of the Danvar**, auditors appointed by the Council, examine its reserves, its books and its partners' character. The examination is rigorous and many candidate houses fail it. Those that pass may take part in chapter clearing and issue their own retail notes under guild standing.

### The Individual Moneylenders

Below the lesser houses sit the **individual moneylenders**: small operators, often family businesses of one or two principals, who take modest deposits and offer small-scale credit. They are licensed practitioners under the guild's broader umbrella and not chapter-clearing members. They cannot issue notes drawn on the Tarmex and can operate only within their own reserves. Most work in a single town or district, and several thousand of them are scattered across the commonwealth. They are the guild's largest tier by count and its smallest by reserves, and they handle the everyday small-credit business the greater houses leave to them.

## The Chapterhouses

The guild keeps a **chapterhouse** in every member city of consequence, typically a substantial building near the commercial center. Each holds the chapter's clearing accounts, the local register of member moneylenders, the chapter's seal and, above all, a secure strongroom of the chapter's pooled bullion reserves. In imperial territory the chapter strongrooms answer to the Tarmex's strongroom at Ôrzâra. In non-imperial territory they hold reserves that belong to the broader Tarmex-backed pool but are administered locally.

An **Danvel** administers each chapterhouse: a senior moneylender, typically from one of the lesser houses, elected by the local member moneylenders for a five-year term. The Parthan manages the strongroom jointly with two appointed Censors, authorizes the admission of new members, countersigns master notes drawn against chapter reserves and represents the chapter at the Council of the Danvori.

The largest chapterhouses stand at Ôrzâra, Ólvestria, Byzaría, [[place-aelissium|Aelissium]], Béravel, Tarvénia (the city) and the principal Heliónite ports. Each is a substantial institution with a permanent staff in the dozens: clerks, auditors, security details and the household needed to host visiting members of standing.

## Governance

The Council of the Danvori is the guild's standing body. It meets quarterly at Ôrzâra and consists of the senior partners of the great houses plus a small number of elected representatives from the lesser houses. It decides guild policy on admission standards, member discipline, chapter operations and the guild's relations with the Tarmex Orzan and the Tevran Maldor.

The Council elects the Danvor Maldor for a ten-year term, renewable once. The present Danvor Maldor is **Lucia Calventiana**, senior partner of the House of Calventian, in her eighth year and presumed eligible for one renewal. Lucia is regarded as one of the most politically capable Danvori Maldor in the guild's history. Her tenure has steadied the guild through the decade since the Tarûzelian secession, and she is credited with winning several substantial concessions from the Tarmex Orzan's leadership.

Twelve Censors of the Danvar report to the Council. They conduct admission examinations, audit member houses' books, investigate complaints and prosecute disciplinary matters before the Council. Their authority within the guild is substantial: a Censor's adverse finding takes a two-thirds Council vote to override, and overrides are rare. They are distinct from the [[affiliation-tevran|Censors of the Tevran]], who audit guild charter compliance across all trades, this one included, and from the [[affiliation-tarmex|Censors of the Tarmex]], who audit the imperial treasury's accounts.

## Discipline and Sanction

A member can be disciplined on two parallel tracks.

On the **guild charter track**, run by the Censors of the Danvar and the Council, a moneylender can be suspended from membership for misconduct, fraud, insolvency or violation of standing protocols. Suspension is severe: the moneylender cannot issue notes, cannot clear through the chapter system and cannot legally call himself a member. In effect his business ends.

On the **clearing track**, run by the Tarmex Orzan, a moneylender's clearing rights against the Tarmex can be suspended. That does not formally end his guild membership, but it cuts him off from the master-note system and the wider credit pool and produces nearly the same consequences.

The two sanctions are usually invoked together but are formally independent, and the Council, the Censors of the Tevran and the Tarmex's leadership keep them so deliberately, because no single body can then destroy a member house by coordinated political action. A moneylender suspended by the guild but keeping Tarmex clearing rights has, in theory, the means to go on operating informally. One suspended by the Tarmex but keeping guild standing can go on with retail banking on his own reserves. Either path is constrained and neither is absolute ruin. Only the combination of guild expulsion and Tarmex suspension brings full institutional death.

By frequency, the most common sanction is a **temporary suspension of clearing rights** by the Tarmex for narrow procedural failures: late monthly clearing reports, discrepancies in serial registration, slow replies to Censor inquiries. These suspensions last days to weeks, embarrass the house and are usually lifted once the failure is mended. The most consequential is **joint expulsion-and-suspension** for fraud or treasonable conduct, such as selling reserves to foreign powers, falsifying notes or sustained fraud against depositors. It has been imposed perhaps a dozen times in the guild's history, and every time it has ended the house.

## Notable Houses and Officers

- **House of Calventian**—the largest of the great houses, founded in Ôrzâra three centuries ago to bank the rebuilding of the Restoration ([[lore-arzanasedict|The Edicts of Arzánas]]) and led by the Calventiani family across nine generations. Its senior partner is Danvor Maldor Lucia Calventiana, the guild's chief officer.
- **House of Maximian Tertian**—the second-largest great house, of Helionite-Vylarian origin and politically aligned with the **Reformist Faction** at court. **Septimus Maximian Tertian** leads it.
- **House of Verrius**—Vylarian and conservative, with strong ties to the Aurelian estate and, historically, to the Dukran's office.
- **House of Therion**—of Heliónite origin and now continent-spanning, the principal Heliónite great house, which specializes in cross-territory clearing.
- **House of Aldovardian**—Tarvénian, and unusual as a great house of Aurèldían rather than Vylarian-Helionite origin. It won its position by aggressive expansion in the late post-imperial settlement, and **Danvor Marcus Aldovardian** leads it, regarded by his Vylarian peers with formal correctness and informal suspicion.
- **Septimus Maximian Tertian**—senior partner of his house, widely seen as the most politically capable Danvor of his generation after Lucia Calventiana and a likely successor when her tenure ends.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]]** is the operational counterpart and ultimate backing. Publicly the relationship is "guild standing," not subordination. The Danvar's reserves belong to the broader Tarmex-backed pool, its clearing flows through Tarmex accounts, and its senior leaders coordinate closely and quietly with the Imperial Quaestor.
- **The [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]]** is the chartering authority. The Danvar holds the charter that lets it operate as a guild and occupies one of the thirty-six trade-seats on the **Council of the Tevran**, held ex officio by the Danvor Maldor. It is subject to the Censors of the Tevran's broader oversight.
- **The post-imperial crowns and the Byzarian League** are major customers and political counterparties. The crowns hold accounts at great houses, borrow heavily against future revenue and negotiate quietly with the guild's leaders over the terms of their banking.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]]** are a perennial concern. The [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]'s reach into the legitimate banking system is the guild's standing internal problem. Several Censor investigations run continuously, and the Reformist Faction criticizes the Council's discipline of compromised members as too lenient.
- **The Merâzûnî**, the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezâkûnî]]'s counterpart in Tarûzel, is formally unrecognized. The Merâzûnî's moneylenders run a parallel system, and Danvar members may not accept Merâzûnî paper directly. A small number of Heliónite member houses act as quiet cross-border intermediaries, which the guild's leaders deplore officially and tolerate unofficially.

## Identifying Marks

A member moneylender's seal is registered with both the local chapter and the Council at Ôrzâra, and it is impressed in colored wax on every note issued: red for retail notes, blue for traveler's notes and gold for master notes. Member moneylenders wear a silver pin at the throat in the form of a balance-scale, and senior partners of great houses wear the same pin in gold. Phêzûmûsândoi wear an additional brass medallion of office. The Danvor Maldor wears the great gold pectoral of the guild, kept at the Ôrzâra chapter between holders. The Censors of the Danvar wear black mantles with the silver Censor's mark, distinct in pattern from the Censors of the Tevran's mantle though similar in color.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Failing Great House.** One of the great houses, long established and deeply networked, is privately insolvent and has been hiding it behind creative clearing arrangements that the Censors of the Danvar are about to uncover. A great house's failure would shock the system. Rescuing it would take Tarmex intervention on a scale that would publicly acknowledge the polite fiction. The Danvor Maldor must decide which she prefers.
- **The Aurèldían Petition.** The Tarvénian, Provenzian and Élavendren crowns have formally petitioned the Council for representation, a seat for the senior partner of a great house from non-imperial territory. Granting it would formally recognize the kingdoms' stake in the guild's governance. The Vylarian and Heliónite great houses are divided, and the imperial throne's position is neutral in public and hostile in private.
- **The Compromised Censor.** A Censor of the Danvar's recent findings have favored, as a pattern, member houses with particular court connections. The Danvor Maldor's investigation is preliminary but suggestive. The Censor is the daughter of an Argentean family with both guild and court ties, so removing her would create political complications and keeping her in office would create different ones.
- **The Heliónite Intermediary.** A Heliónite great house has been confirmed as the principal cross-border intermediary between the Danvar and the Vezâkûnî's Merâzûnî, against standing guild policy. The volume of intermediated paper is now large enough that public exposure could damage the guild's standing with the Tarmex Orzan and the throne. A quiet sanction is possible but would need the Danvor Maldor's discretion, and a formal sanction would force the question into the open.
- **The Counterfeit Crisis.** The Censors of the Danvar, working with the Tarmex's own Censors, have uncovered a sustained counterfeiting operation that has put perhaps several thousand forged traveler's notes into circulation across Aurèldía. The forgeries are good enough to have escaped detection for months. The Council is debating whether to honor them on first presentation, which would preserve confidence in the note system, or refuse them, which would limit the guild's losses.
- **The New Charter.** The Tevran Maldor's Censors of the Tevran have begun a routine review of the Danvar's guild charter, scheduled for the coming year. Such reviews are usually procedural, but this one has drawn the attention of the Reformist Faction, and a sub-committee with Reformist members will conduct it. The Council is preparing carefully, the great houses are coordinating their testimony, and the Danvor Maldor hopes the review ends before the next Council elections.
