## The Curia Vylaria

_Vylarian: Curia Vylaria – "the Vylarian Court," the Palace of the Senate_

- **Type:** Imperial deliberative assembly and high court; seat of the patrician order
- **Seat:** The Curia Vylaria, within [[place-urbsaquiln|Urbs Aquilion]], the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]]
- **Membership:** The senatorial order – the senior houses of the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|Aurelian and Argentean estates]]
- **Answerable to:** The reigning **Augustar**

### Overview

The **Curia Vylaria** is at once a building and the body that fills it: the great Palace of the Senate within the Inner City, and the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|patrician]] assembly that gathers there. Beneath its bronze doors and within its tiered, semicircular chamber, the great families of the Empire convene to debate, to posture, to conspire, and – in theory – to advise the Augustar. The senatorial order is drawn from the senior estates: the founding **Aurelii** and the established **Argentei**, the houses whose names carry weight enough to claim a seat beneath the dome. To sit in the Curia is to stand among the highest of the Empire's nobility, and the order guards its dignity jealously even in the ages when its power is least.

### The Chamber

The Curia's great bronze doors open on a tiered semicircular chamber, banked so that every member faces the floor and the speakers' space at its focus. Here the rhetoric of the patrician class is made – the formal oratory, the cultivated insults, the alliances signaled by who sits beside whom and who rises to answer whom. Much of what passes is theater, and the senators know it; but theater in the Curia is a form of politics in its own right, and a speech well aimed, or a silence well kept, can shift the standing of a house or the fortunes of a faction. The chamber's traditions are ancient and elaborate, and a newly elevated house learns quickly that the forms of the Curia are themselves a language one must master to be heard.

### Power and the Throne

The Curia's authority is real but conditional, and its history is a long oscillation. In principle the Senate advises the Augustar, lends its weight to imperial acts, and speaks for the great houses of the realm. In practice its power waxes and wanes with the strength of whoever wears the imperial diadem. A strong Augustar reduces the Curia to a chorus – consulted for form, flattered, and overruled at will. A weak or contested one finds it suddenly formidable, for in the absence of a commanding throne the assembled great families are the Empire's center of gravity, and a succession dispute is decided as much in the Curia's lobbies as on the walls of the [[place-castraprtr|Castra Praetoria]]. The throne and the Senate are thus locked in a permanent, shifting negotiation, and reading which way it currently tilts is among the first things any player at court must learn.

### The Senate as High Court

Beyond its deliberative role, the Curia Vylaria is the **highest court for senatorial persons**. The civic [[affiliation-curiaurbis|courts of Magnápolis]] have no authority over the nobility, the Senate, or the crown; the throne's own [[affiliation-curiaurbis|imperial tribunals]] in the Officia Imperii take up causes touching the great – but for senatorial persons in the gravest matters, judgment may rise higher still, to the Curia itself, where a house is tried by its peers. To be arraigned before the Senate is the most serious proceeding a noble can face short of the Augustar's direct judgment, and the political stakes of such a trial are rarely smaller than the legal ones.

### Relations

- **The Augustar** – patron, counterweight, and master. The relationship defines the Curia's power in any given reign; the Senate advises a strong throne and constrains a weak one.
- **The [[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curia Urbis]]** – the civic government of the city below, many of whose Curators are drawn from the senatorial order; the gravest senatorial causes rise from the imperial tribunals to the Curia Vylaria.
- **The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|estates of the nobility]]** – the Aurelii and Argentei who fill its seats; the Senate is the political theater in which their rivalries are conducted.
- **The [[affiliation-pratrgstrm|The Praetar Augustarum]]** – the armed factor in any succession the Senate's lobbies debate; senators court the guard, and the guard watches the Senate.
- **The court factions** – the Reformist and Conservative blocs that run through every imperial institution contend on the Curia's floor as fiercely as anywhere in the Empire.

### Plot Hooks

- **The Weak Diadem.** A contested or failing succession has, for the first time in a generation, made the Curia genuinely powerful. Houses long resigned to chorus-work are rediscovering their voices; the question of whom the Senate will recognize has become the question of the Empire.
- **The Trial of a House.** A senatorial house has been arraigned before the Curia in a cause that is as much political as legal. The verdict will be decided by alliances on the floor, not evidence; every great family is calculating what it owes, what it is owed, and which way safety lies.
- **The Inconvenient Speech.** A senator is preparing to say aloud, beneath the dome, something the throne would much rather went unspoken. Suppressing him risks proving his point; letting him speak risks the chamber taking up his cause.
