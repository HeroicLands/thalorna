## The Curia Vylaria

An usher of the Curia can tell you how a day's business will go before the first speaker rises, and he does it by watching where the senators sit. "See who has taken the bench beside whom. See which of them has left a gap. By the time the speaker is on his feet, the vote is half cast, and the speech is for the record and for the galleries. If you are called to the floor and nobody sits near you, you will be heard politely and you will lose."

The **Curia Vylaria** (_Vylarian: "the Vylarian Court"_) is at once a building and the body that fills it: the Palace of the Senate within [[place-urbsaquiln|Urbs Aquilion]], the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]], and the patrician assembly that gathers there. Beneath its bronze doors and within its tiered, semicircular chamber, the great families of the empire convene to debate, to posture, to conspire and, in theory, to advise the Augustar, the emperor. The senatorial order is drawn from the senior estates: the founding **Aurelii** and the established **Argentei**, the houses whose names carry weight enough to claim a seat beneath the dome. The order guards its dignity jealously even in the ages when its power is least.

- **Type:** Imperial deliberative assembly and high court; seat of the patrician order
- **Seat:** The Curia Vylaria, within Urbs Aquilion, the Inner City of Magnápolis
- **Membership:** The senatorial order, the senior houses of the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|Aurelian and Argentean estates]]
- **Answerable to:** The reigning Augustar

## The Chamber

The great bronze doors open on a tiered semicircular chamber, banked so that every member faces the floor and the speakers' space at its focus. Here the rhetoric of the patrician class is made: the formal oratory, the cultivated insults, and the alliances signaled by who sits beside whom and who rises to answer whom.

Much of what passes is theater, and the senators know it. Theater in the Curia is a form of politics in its own right, and a speech well aimed, or a silence well kept, can shift the standing of a house or the fortunes of a faction. The chamber's traditions are ancient and elaborate, and a newly elevated house learns quickly that the forms of the Curia are a language it must master to be heard. A senator of an established Argentean house, seated by standing, holds a voice beneath the dome. A senator of a founding Aurelian house, traceable to the empire's first century, has a name that carries weight before he rises, and the chamber's ancient forms are his family's custom.

## Power and the Throne

The Curia's authority is real but conditional, and its history is a long oscillation. In principle the Senate advises the Augustar, lends its weight to imperial acts, and speaks for the great houses. In practice its power waxes and wanes with the strength of whoever wears the imperial diadem.

A strong Augustar reduces the Curia to a chorus, consulted for form, flattered and overruled at will. A weak or contested one finds it suddenly formidable, because without a commanding throne the assembled great families are the empire's center of gravity, and a succession dispute is decided as much in the Curia's lobbies as on the walls of the [[place-castraprtr|Castra Praetoria]]. The throne and the Senate are locked in a permanent, shifting negotiation, and reading which way it currently tilts is among the first things any player at court must learn.

## The Senate as High Court

Beyond its deliberative role, the Curia Vylaria is the **highest court for senatorial persons**. The civic [[affiliation-curiaurbis|courts of Magnápolis]] have no authority over the nobility, the Senate or the crown, and the throne's own [[affiliation-curiaurbis|imperial tribunals]] in the **Officia Imperii** take up causes that touch the great. For senatorial persons in the gravest matters, judgment may rise higher still, to the Curia itself, where a house is tried by its peers. To be arraigned before the Senate is the most serious proceeding a noble can face short of the Augustar's direct judgment, and the political stakes of such a trial are rarely smaller than the legal ones.

## Relations

- **The Augustar**: patron, counterweight and master. The relationship defines the Curia's power in any given reign; the Senate advises a strong throne and constrains a weak one.
- **The [[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curia Urbis]]**: the civic government of the city below, many of whose Curators are drawn from the senatorial order. The gravest senatorial causes rise from the imperial tribunals to the Curia Vylaria.
- **The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|estates of the nobility]]**: the Aurelii and Argentei who fill its seats. The Senate is the political theater in which their rivalries are conducted.
- **The [[affiliation-pratrgstrm|Praetar Augustarum]]**: the armed factor in any succession the Senate's lobbies debate. Senators court the guard, and the guard watches the Senate.
- **The court factions**: the Reformist and Conservative blocs that run through every imperial institution contend on the Curia's floor as fiercely as anywhere in the empire.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Weak Diadem.** A contested succession has made the Curia powerful for the first time in a generation. Houses long resigned to chorus-work are rediscovering their voices, and the question of whom the Senate will recognize has become the question of the empire.
- **The Trial of a House.** A senatorial house has been arraigned before the Curia in a cause that is as much political as legal. Alliances on the floor, not evidence, will decide the verdict, and every great family is calculating what it owes, what it is owed, and which way safety lies.
- **The Inconvenient Speech.** A senator is preparing to say beneath the dome what the throne would much rather went unspoken. Suppressing him risks proving his point, and letting him speak risks the chamber taking up his cause.
