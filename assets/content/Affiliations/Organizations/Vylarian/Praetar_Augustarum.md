## Overview

A junior of the Praetar Augustarum, put on the Castra's wall for his first night watch, is told the one thing the guard never says aloud: "Look at the palace. You are guarding it. Now look at us. Everyone in that palace knows which of those two things could end the other. Stand straight, and remember who feeds you."

The **Praetar Augustarum** (_Vylarian: "the Guard of the Augustars"_) are the imperial guard: the personal bodyguard of the Augustar, the emperor, and the keepers of order and justice within the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]] itself. They stand apart from the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|line legions]] in every way that matters. They recruit on their own authority, promote through their own ranks, answer to no Dux Bellorum or Magnar but to the throne directly, and are quartered not in a frontier castra but in the [[place-castraprtr|Castra Praetoria]] on the palace hill, the one fortress inside the **Inner Wall**. They are elite, lavishly equipped and intensely political: the praetorian muscle of the court, and the instrument through which the Augustar makes his grip on his own capital physical.

- **Type:** Imperial military institution; household guard and Inner City garrison
- **Region of operation:** [[place-urbsaquiln|Urbs Aquilion]], the Inner City of Magnápolis, and the imperial person wherever he travels
- **Seat:** The Castra Praetoria, on the palace hill within the Inner Wall
- **Answerable to:** The reigning Augustar alone

Their charge is twofold: the imperial person, and the peace of the Inner City. Within Urbs Aquilion they are guard, garrison and magistrate's arm together. They ward the [[place-palatimgnm|Palatium Magnum]], stand the gates and walls of the citadel, and keep order among the senators, priests, magistrates and great families who dwell or do business there. Their writ stops, sharply, at the Inner Wall. The vast city beyond is not theirs to police. That work belongs to the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|City Watch]], and the division is deliberate.

## The Body That Makes and Unmakes

The central fact of the Praetar Augustarum, the one that shapes every reign, is that the body best placed to guard an Augustar is also the body best placed to unmake one. The guard holds the palace, the citadel and the armed initiative at the very heart of the empire. If it withdraws its protection at the wrong moment, or extends it to the wrong claimant, it can end a reign in a single night without a legion ever marching.

Every reigning Augustar therefore courts the guard's loyalty with deliberate care. He pays donatives on accession and at intervals after. He grants privileges and prestige. He watches the officers who command the watches and could, between them, decide a succession. A throne that neglects the guard is living on borrowed time, and a throne that buys it too nakedly teaches the guard the price of its own loyalty, which is its own danger. The management of the Praetar Augustarum is one of the permanent arts of Vylarian rulership, and the histories are full of Augustars who mastered it and Augustars who did not.

## Recruitment and Standing

The guard recruits and promotes apart from the legions, and service in it is a privilege rather than a posting. The line army runs on a dual-track structure, with purchased noble commissions over a meritocratic commoner corps. The Praetar Augustarum is its own closed world, drawing picked men into a force that is better paid, better armored and far closer to power than any frontier legion. A transfer into the guard is a mark of favor. A command within it carries real political weight, because its senior officers stand within reach of the imperial presence and the succession itself.

That closeness to power is also the institution's hazard. The elite standing that makes the guard formidable makes it proud, factional and acutely conscious of its own importance, and a guard that believes itself the maker of emperors is never wholly safe to keep.

## Relations

- **The Augustar**: patron, charge and sole master. The guard answers to the throne directly and to no intermediate command. The relationship is one of mutual dependence and mutual wariness.
- **The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|line legions and the Dux Bellorum]]**: a separate world. The guard takes no orders from the war command and fights no frontier campaigns. The legions eye the praetorians' privileges with a mixture of envy and distrust.
- **The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|City Watch]]**: the guard's opposite number below the hill. The guard keeps the Inner City and the Watch keeps the rest. The praetorians look down on the vigiles as glorified street-wardens, and the Watch returns the contempt with the resentment of men who do the unglamorous work of policing a million people.
- **The [[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curia Urbis]] and the civic courts**: they govern the city beyond the wall. The guard's jurisdiction inside the Inner City sits beside theirs, and above it.
- **The court factions**: all of them court the guard. Its loyalty, real or purchased, is among the most consequential variables in any contest over the imperial succession.

## Identifying Marks

A praetorian in full harness is unmistakable, and meant to be. The guard wears armor far richer than any line legion's, burnished and gilded and worked with the imperial Aquila, and it is meant to be seen on the walls and at the palace gates as a standing display of the throne's wealth and reach. Their standards and liveries set them visibly apart from the field army.

The captaincy of the permanent palace detail is among the most trusted postings in the empire. Its holder shares with the Quaestor Imperii and the Censor Maximus of the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium]] the securing of the imperial strongroom.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Donative Withheld.** A new Augustar, pressed for funds, has delayed the customary accession donative. The Castra reads the delay as either weakness or insult, and more than one party is quietly sounding out the officers who command the watches.
- **The Wrong Gate.** On a night of crisis, the question of which way the gates of the Castra open will decide a succession. A handful of watch-captains hold the answer, and rival claimants and their agents are courting, threatening and bribing each of them.
- **The Praetorian's Reach.** A senior guard officer has begun extending the guard's keeping of order within the Inner City into matters that properly belong to the imperial tribunals: arrests, detentions, quiet disappearances, all under color of protecting the throne. The civic courts and the affected great houses are alarmed. The Augustar finds the overreach useful and is in no hurry to curb it.
