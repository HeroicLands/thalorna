_Vylarian: Collegium Argentariorum—"the Bankers' College" or "the Moneylenders' Guild"_

- **Type:** Chartered trade guild under the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]; effective banking institution of the post-imperial commonwealth
- **Region:** Every member territory of the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]—[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], the five [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]], and [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]]
- **Founded:** Chartered under the Magnum Collegium two and a half centuries ago, drawing on older moneylender associations that predated the imperial banking consolidation
- **Senior officer:** The **Argentarius Magnar** ("Great Banker"), elected from the senior partners of the great houses

## Overview

The Collegium Argentariorum is, on paper, one of the thirty-six trade guilds chartered under the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]—the bankers' and moneylenders' professional association, alongside the smiths, the scribes, the cartographers, and the rest. In practice it is by some distance the largest and most consequential of them. Its member houses operate the banking system on which most large-value commerce in the post-imperial commonwealth depends; its chapterhouses outside the empire serve as the operational front-end of the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]'s central-bank functions; its senior partners are politically weighty figures whose presence at court rivals the senior nobility.

The guild's structural position is best understood through the **polite fiction** that defines the commonwealth's banking architecture. Inside Vylaria and Heliónis, the Aerarium Imperii operates direct offices, and the Argentariorum is visibly what it is: a guild of moneylenders who clear through the imperial treasury. Outside the empire—in the Aurèldían kingdoms and in Byzaría—the Aerarium does not maintain direct presence, and the Argentariorum's chapterhouses serve as the entire visible face of the banking system. A Provenzian merchant who carries a traveler's note from his home moneylender to a Byzarian receiving moneylender deals only with Argentariorum members at both ends; the chapter clears the note through Argentariorum channels back to Magnápolis; the imperial treasury sits at the back of the chain, drawing its 2% issuance fee, but the Provenzian crown's official correspondence describes the entire transaction as "Collegium Argentariorum-honored." This fiction is universal, consequential, and tacitly endorsed by every party. (See the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]] note for the deeper structure.)

The Argentariorum's continued existence and influence rest on the fact that **it provides something the small post-imperial polities could not provide themselves**: a continent-spanning banking system. Each Aurèldían kingdom on its own is too small to build credible international banking; the Byzarian League's merchant houses individually lack the reserves; even Byzaría as a whole could not match the depth of credit the Argentariorum provides. By participating in the guild, the kingdoms get access to banking infrastructure, mutual recognition of moneylenders' credentials, and the practical ability to move large sums across borders without physical bullion transport. The cost—the 2% issuance fee flowing quietly to imperial coffers—is, generation after generation, judged worth paying.

## Structure

The Argentariorum is organized in three tiers.

### The Great Houses

Perhaps twenty-five **great houses** dominate the guild. These are the moneylender families whose reserves, chapter affiliations, and political connections place them at the apex of the trade. Each great house operates across multiple cities—the **House of Calventian** maintains offices in Magnápolis, Vénustria, Provenzal, Béravel, and Byzaría; the **House of Maximian Tertian** has a similar geographic footprint; the **House of Verrius** focuses on Vylarian commercial cities; the **House of Therion** is Heliónite in origin but has become continent-spanning; the **House of Aldovardian** is Tarvénian and unusual in that respect (most great houses originated in Vylaria or Heliónis and expanded outward).

Each great house holds standing accounts with the Aerarium Imperii (or with the Argentariorum's chapter clearing arrangement in non-imperial territory) on a scale that smaller houses cannot approach. The great houses are the principal issuers of master notes drawn directly on Aerarium accounts; the principal redeemers of large traveler's notes; the principal counterparties for Aurèldían crown borrowing; and the principal political actors when the guild has a position to take.

The senior partners of the great houses sit on the **Council of the Argentarii**—the guild's governing body—and meet quarterly at Magnápolis.

### The Lesser Houses

Several hundred **lesser houses** operate at smaller scales—typically one or two cities, modest reserves, regional clientele. A lesser house in a provincial Aurèldían town might serve perhaps a few hundred regular depositors, issue retail notes for local circulation, cash traveler's notes for visiting merchants (within its capacity), and maintain a clearing account with one or two great houses to settle interbank balances. The lesser houses are the everyday banking experience for most commoners and minor nobility outside the major commercial capitals.

Lesser houses are admitted to the guild by sponsorship of an existing member of standing and by examination of their reserves, books, and partners' character. The examination is conducted by the **Censores Argentariorum**—auditors appointed by the Council—and is rigorous. Many candidate houses fail; those that pass become eligible to participate in the chapter clearing system and to issue their own retail notes under guild standing.

### The Individual Moneylenders

Below the lesser houses sit the **individual moneylenders**—small operators, often family businesses of one or two principals, accepting modest deposits and offering small-scale credit. These are not chapter-clearing members; they are licensed practitioners under the guild's broader umbrella. They cannot issue notes drawn on the Aerarium; they can only operate within their own reserves. Most operate in a single town or district. There are several thousand across the commonwealth.

The individual moneylenders constitute the guild's largest membership tier by count but its smallest by reserves. They handle the everyday small-credit business that the lesser and great houses prefer to leave to them.

## The Chapterhouses

The Argentariorum maintains **chapterhouses** in every member city of consequence—typically a substantial building near the city's commercial center, holding the chapter's clearing accounts, the local register of member moneylenders, the chapter's seal, and (crucially) a secure strongroom of the chapter's pooled bullion reserves. In imperial territory these chapter strongrooms are subordinate to the Aerarium's strongroom at Magnápolis; in non-imperial territory they hold reserves that are part of the broader Aerarium-backed pool but are administered locally.

Each chapterhouse is administered by an **Argentarius Praepar**—a senior moneylender, typically of one of the lesser houses, elected by the local member moneylenders for a five-year term. The Praepar's authority is significant: he manages the chapter strongroom (jointly with two appointed Censores), he authorizes admissions of new members, he countersigns master notes drawn against chapter reserves, and he represents the chapter at the Council of the Argentarii.

The largest chapterhouses—at Magnápolis, Vénustria, Byzaría, Aelissium, Béravel, Tarvenia (the city), and the principal Heliónite ports—are substantial institutions with permanent staff numbering in the dozens, including clerks, auditors, security details, and the household needed to host visiting members of standing.

## Governance

The **Council of the Argentarii** is the guild's standing body. It meets quarterly at Magnápolis, comprises the senior partners of the great houses plus a small number of elected representatives from the lesser houses, and decides guild policy on matters of admission standards, member discipline, chapter operations, and the guild's relationships with the Aerarium Imperii and the Magnum Collegium.

The **Argentarius Magnar**—the guild's chief officer—is elected by the Council for a ten-year term, renewable once. The current Argentarius Magnar is **Lucia Calventiana**, senior partner of the House of Calventian, in her eighth year and presumed eligible for a single renewal. Lucia is regarded as one of the most politically capable Argentarii Magnar of the guild's history; her continued tenure has stabilized the guild through the post-Haradian-secession decade and is credited with negotiating several substantial concessions from the Aerarium Imperii's leadership.

The **Censores Argentariorum**—the guild's discipline officers—number twelve and report to the Council. They conduct admission examinations, audit member houses' books, investigate complaints, and prosecute disciplinary matters before the Council. Their authority within the guild is substantial; the Council's standing protocol is that a Censor's adverse finding requires a two-thirds Council vote to override, and such overrides are rare. The Censores Argentariorum are distinct from the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Censores Collegiorum]] (who audit guild charter compliance across all trades, including this guild's standing) and from the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Censores Aerarii]] (who audit the imperial treasury's accounts).

## Discipline and Sanction

Member discipline operates on two parallel tracks:

The **guild charter track**—administered by the Censores Argentariorum and the Council—can suspend a moneylender from guild membership for misconduct, fraud, insolvency, or violation of standing protocols. Suspension is severe: a suspended moneylender cannot issue notes, cannot clear through the chapter system, cannot legally describe himself as a member of the guild. In effect his business ends.

The **clearing track**—administered by the Aerarium Imperii—can suspend a moneylender's clearing rights against the Aerarium, which functionally cuts off his access to the master-note system and to the broader credit pool. This sanction does not formally end his guild membership but produces nearly identical practical consequences.

The two sanctions are usually invoked together but are formally independent—a feature that the Council, the Censores Collegiorum, and the Aerarium's leadership maintain deliberately, because it prevents any single body from unilaterally destroying a member house through coordinated political action. A moneylender suspended by the guild but retaining Aerarium clearing rights has, in theory, the means to continue operating informally; a moneylender suspended by the Aerarium but retaining guild standing can continue retail banking on his own reserves. Either path is constrained but neither is absolute ruin. Only the combination—guild expulsion and Aerarium suspension—produces full institutional death.

The most common sanction, by frequency, is **temporary suspension of clearing rights** by the Aerarium for relatively narrow procedural failures (late filing of monthly clearing reports, discrepancies in serial registration, slow response to Censor inquiries). These suspensions typically last days to weeks, embarrass the affected house, and are usually lifted on remediation. The most consequential sanction is **joint expulsion-and-suspension** for fraud or treasonable conduct (selling reserves to foreign powers, falsifying notes, sustained fraud against depositors), which has been imposed perhaps a dozen times in the guild's history and has every time ended the affected house.

## Notable Houses and Officers

- **House of Calventian**—largest of the great houses; founded in Magnápolis three centuries ago; led by the Calventiani family across nine generations; current senior partner is **Argentarius Magnar Lucia Calventiana**, the guild's chief officer.
- **House of Maximian Tertian**—second-largest great house; Helionite-Vylarian origin; politically aligned with the Reformist Faction at court; led by **Septimus Maximian Tertian**.
- **House of Verrius**—Vylarian; conservative; strong relations with the Aurelian estate and historically with the Dux Bellorum's office.
- **House of Therion**—Heliónite origin, now continent-spanning; specializes in cross-territory clearing and is the principal Heliónite great house.
- **House of Aldovardian**—Tarvénian; unusual in being a great house of Aurèldían rather than Vylarian-Helionite origin; founded its position through aggressive expansion in the late post-imperial settlement period; led by **Argentarius Marcus Aldovardian**, regarded with formal correctness and informal suspicion by his Vylarian peers.
- **Septimus Maximian Tertian**—senior partner of his house; widely regarded as the most politically capable Argentarius of his generation after Lucia Calventiana; thought to be a likely successor when her tenure ends.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]**—operational counterpart and ultimate backing. The relationship is described publicly as one of "guild standing" rather than subordination; the Argentariorum's reserves are part of the broader Aerarium-backed pool, the guild's clearing flows through Aerarium accounts, and the guild's senior leadership coordinates closely (and quietly) with the Quaestor Imperii.
- **The [[affiliation-magnumclgm|The Magnum Collegium]]**—chartering authority; the Argentariorum holds the Magnum Collegium charter that lets it operate as a guild, occupies one of the thirty-six trade-seats on the Curia Collegiorum (held ex officio by the Argentarius Magnar), and is subject to the Censores Collegiorum's broader oversight.
- **The post-imperial crowns and the Byzarian League**—major customers and political counterparties. The crowns hold accounts at great houses, borrow against future revenue (often substantially), and negotiate quietly with the Argentariorum's leadership over the terms of their banking relationships.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]]**—perennial concern. The [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]'s reach into the legitimate banking system is the guild's standing internal problem; several Censor investigations are continuously in progress; the Council's discipline of compromised members has been criticized by the Reformist Faction as too lenient.
- **The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s Bayt al-Khazînah**—formally unrecognized counterpart in Harad. The Bayt's moneylenders operate a parallel system; the Argentariorum's members are forbidden from accepting Bayt paper directly. A small number of Heliónite member houses operate as quiet cross-border intermediaries; the guild's leadership officially deplores this and unofficially tolerates it.

## Identifying Marks

A member moneylender's seal is registered with both the local chapter and the Council at Magnápolis; the seal is impressed in colored wax on every note issued (red for retail notes, blue for traveler's notes, gold for master notes). Member moneylenders wear a silver pin at the throat in the form of a balance-scale; senior partners of great houses wear the same pin in gold. Argentarii Praepares wear an additional brass medallion of office; the Argentarius Magnar wears the great gold pectoral of the guild, kept at the Magnápolis chapter between holders. Censores Argentariorum wear black mantles with the silver Censor's mark—distinct in pattern from the Censores Collegiorum's mantle, though the colors are similar.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Failing Great House.** One of the great houses—long-established, deeply networked—is privately insolvent and has been concealing it through creative clearing arrangements that the Censores Argentariorum are about to uncover. Failure of a great house would shock the system; rescue would require Aerarium intervention on a scale that would publicly acknowledge the polite fiction. The Argentarius Magnar must decide which to prefer.
- **The Aurèldían Petition.** The Tarvénian, Provenzian, and Élavendrean crowns have formally petitioned the Council of the Argentarii for representation on the Council—a seat for a non-imperial-territory great house's senior partner, alongside the existing seats. The proposal would, if granted, formally recognize the kingdoms' stake in the guild's governance. The Vylarian and Helionite great houses are divided; the imperial throne's position is officially neutral and unofficially hostile.
- **The Compromised Censor.** A Censor Argentariorum's recent disciplinary findings have, in pattern, favored member houses with particular court connections. The Argentarius Magnar's investigation is preliminary but suggestive. The Censor is the daughter of an Argentean family with both Argentariorum and court connections; her removal would create political complications, and her continuation in office would create different ones.
- **The Heliónite Intermediary.** A Heliónite great house has been confirmed as the principal cross-border intermediary between the Argentariorum and the Sôd-Naqîrîn's Bayt al-Khazînah, in violation of standing guild policy. The volume of intermediated paper is now substantial enough that public exposure could damage the guild's standing with the Aerarium Imperii and the imperial throne. Quiet sanction is possible but would require the Argentarius Magnar's discretion; formal sanction would force the question into the open.
- **The Counterfeit Crisis.** The Censores Argentariorum have, in coordination with the Aerarium's own Censores, uncovered a sustained counterfeiting operation that has produced perhaps several thousand forged traveler's notes circulating across Aurèldía. The forgeries are good enough to have escaped detection for months. The political question—which the Council is debating—is whether to honor the forgeries on first presentation (to preserve confidence in the note system) or to refuse them (to limit the guild's losses).
- **The New Charter.** The Magnum Collegium's Censores Collegiorum have begun a routine review of the Argentariorum's guild charter, scheduled for the coming year. Such reviews are usually procedural; this one has, by procedural anomaly, attracted Reformist Faction attention, and a sub-committee with Reformist representation will conduct the review. The Council of the Argentarii is preparing carefully; the great houses are coordinating their testimony; the Argentarius Magnar is hoping the review is concluded before the next Council elections.
