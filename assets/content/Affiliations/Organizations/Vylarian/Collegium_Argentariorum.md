Watch a clerk at a chapterhouse counter take in a traveler's note and you see the guild's whole trade. He has under a minute to decide to believe in a slip of sealed paper. Say he works at the Magnápolis chapter, and he checks three things from habit: the wax (red for a retail note, blue for a traveler's note, gold for a master note), the serial against the chapter's register, and the moneylender's mark above the chapter's countersignature. "People think we lend money," he tells the apprentice at the next desk. "We don't, mostly. We tell strangers whom to trust. The lending is what we do in the afternoons."

## Overview

The Collegium Argentariorum, "the Bankers' College," is the moneylenders' guild chartered under the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]], and the visible banking institution of the post-imperial commonwealth. On paper it is one of the thirty-six trade guilds the Collegium charters, alongside the smiths, the scribes, the cartographers and the rest. In practice it is by some distance the largest and most consequential of them. Its member houses run the banking system that most large-value commerce in the commonwealth depends on, and its chapterhouses outside the empire are the working front end of the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]]'s central-bank functions. Its senior partners carry political weight, and their presence at court rivals the senior nobility's.

The guild works in every member territory of the Magnum Collegium: [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], the five [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]] and [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]]. Its charter under the Magnum Collegium is two and a half centuries old and draws on older moneylender associations that predate the imperial consolidation of banking. Its chief officer is the **Argentarius Magnar** ("Great Banker"), elected from the senior partners of the great houses.

The guild's position rests on a **polite fiction**. Inside Vylaria and Heliónis the Aerarium Imperii keeps direct offices, and the Argentariorum is visibly what it is: a guild of moneylenders who clear through the imperial treasury. Outside the empire, in the Aurèldían kingdoms and Byzaría, the Aerarium keeps no presence, and the chapterhouses are the entire visible face of the system. Say a Provenzian merchant carries a traveler's note from his home moneylender to a Byzarian receiving moneylender. He deals only with Argentariorum members at both ends, the chapter clears the note through Argentariorum channels back to Magnápolis, and the imperial treasury sits at the back of the chain, drawing its 2% issuance fee. The Provenzian crown's official correspondence calls the whole transaction "Collegium Argentariorum-honored." Every party endorses the fiction tacitly, and it matters. (The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] note describes the structure behind it.)

The guild survives because it supplies what the small post-imperial polities cannot supply themselves, a continent-spanning banking system. No Aurèldían kingdom is big enough to build credible international banking alone. The Byzarian League's merchant houses lack the reserves one by one, and even Byzaría as a whole could not match the depth of credit the Argentariorum offers. By taking part, the kingdoms get banking infrastructure, mutual recognition of moneylenders' credentials, and the ability to move large sums across borders without carrying bullion. Generation after generation they judge the 2% fee flowing quietly to imperial coffers worth paying.

## Structure

The guild has three tiers.

### The Great Houses

About twenty-five **great houses** dominate the guild. They are the moneylender families whose reserves, chapter ties and political connections put them at the top of the trade, and each operates in several cities. The **House of Calventian** keeps offices in Magnápolis, [[place-olvestria|Ólvestria]], Provènzia, [[place-beravel|Béravel]] and Byzaría. The **House of Maximian Tertian** has a similar footprint. The **House of Verrius** concentrates on the Vylarian commercial cities. The **House of Therion** is Heliónite in origin and has grown continent-spanning. The **House of Aldovardian** is Tarvénian, which makes it unusual, since most great houses began in Vylaria or Heliónis and expanded outward.

Each great house holds standing accounts with the Aerarium Imperii (or with the Argentariorum's chapter clearing arrangement in non-imperial territory) on a scale no smaller house can approach. The great houses issue the master notes drawn directly on Aerarium accounts, redeem the large traveler's notes, lend to the Aurèldían crowns and take the guild's political positions. Their senior partners sit on the **Council of the Argentarii**, the guild's governing body, which meets quarterly at Magnápolis.

### The Lesser Houses

Several hundred **lesser houses** work at smaller scales, typically in one or two cities with modest reserves and a regional clientele. A lesser house in a provincial Aurèldían town might serve a few hundred regular depositors, issue retail notes for local circulation, cash traveler's notes for visiting merchants within its means, and keep a clearing account with one or two great houses to settle balances between banks. For most commoners and minor nobles outside the major commercial capitals, a lesser house is what banking means.

An existing member of standing sponsors a lesser house for admission, and the **Censores Argentariorum**, auditors appointed by the Council, examine its reserves, its books and its partners' character. The examination is rigorous and many candidate houses fail it. Those that pass may take part in chapter clearing and issue their own retail notes under guild standing.

### The Individual Moneylenders

Below the lesser houses sit the **individual moneylenders**: small operators, often family businesses of one or two principals, who take modest deposits and offer small-scale credit. They are licensed practitioners under the guild's broader umbrella and not chapter-clearing members. They cannot issue notes drawn on the Aerarium and can operate only within their own reserves. Most work in a single town or district, and several thousand of them are scattered across the commonwealth. They are the guild's largest tier by count and its smallest by reserves, and they handle the everyday small-credit business the greater houses leave to them.

## The Chapterhouses

The guild keeps a **chapterhouse** in every member city of consequence, typically a substantial building near the commercial center. Each holds the chapter's clearing accounts, the local register of member moneylenders, the chapter's seal and, above all, a secure strongroom of the chapter's pooled bullion reserves. In imperial territory the chapter strongrooms answer to the Aerarium's strongroom at Magnápolis. In non-imperial territory they hold reserves that belong to the broader Aerarium-backed pool but are administered locally.

An **Argentarius Praepar** administers each chapterhouse: a senior moneylender, typically from one of the lesser houses, elected by the local member moneylenders for a five-year term. The Praepar manages the strongroom jointly with two appointed Censores, authorizes the admission of new members, countersigns master notes drawn against chapter reserves and represents the chapter at the Council of the Argentarii.

The largest chapterhouses stand at Magnápolis, Ólvestria, Byzaría, [[place-aelissium|Aelissium]], Béravel, Tarvénia (the city) and the principal Heliónite ports. Each is a substantial institution with a permanent staff in the dozens: clerks, auditors, security details and the household needed to host visiting members of standing.

## Governance

The Council of the Argentarii is the guild's standing body. It meets quarterly at Magnápolis and consists of the senior partners of the great houses plus a small number of elected representatives from the lesser houses. It decides guild policy on admission standards, member discipline, chapter operations and the guild's relations with the Aerarium Imperii and the Magnum Collegium.

The Council elects the Argentarius Magnar for a ten-year term, renewable once. The present Argentarius Magnar is **Lucia Calventiana**, senior partner of the House of Calventian, in her eighth year and presumed eligible for one renewal. Lucia is regarded as one of the most politically capable Argentarii Magnar in the guild's history. Her tenure has steadied the guild through the decade since the Haradian secession, and she is credited with winning several substantial concessions from the Aerarium Imperii's leadership.

Twelve Censores Argentariorum report to the Council. They conduct admission examinations, audit member houses' books, investigate complaints and prosecute disciplinary matters before the Council. Their authority within the guild is substantial: a Censor's adverse finding takes a two-thirds Council vote to override, and overrides are rare. They are distinct from the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Censores Collegiorum]], who audit guild charter compliance across all trades, this one included, and from the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Censores Aerarii]], who audit the imperial treasury's accounts.

## Discipline and Sanction

A member can be disciplined on two parallel tracks.

On the **guild charter track**, run by the Censores Argentariorum and the Council, a moneylender can be suspended from membership for misconduct, fraud, insolvency or violation of standing protocols. Suspension is severe: the moneylender cannot issue notes, cannot clear through the chapter system and cannot legally call himself a member. In effect his business ends.

On the **clearing track**, run by the Aerarium Imperii, a moneylender's clearing rights against the Aerarium can be suspended. That does not formally end his guild membership, but it cuts him off from the master-note system and the wider credit pool and produces nearly the same consequences.

The two sanctions are usually invoked together but are formally independent, and the Council, the Censores Collegiorum and the Aerarium's leadership keep them so deliberately, because no single body can then destroy a member house by coordinated political action. A moneylender suspended by the guild but keeping Aerarium clearing rights has, in theory, the means to go on operating informally. One suspended by the Aerarium but keeping guild standing can go on with retail banking on his own reserves. Either path is constrained and neither is absolute ruin. Only the combination of guild expulsion and Aerarium suspension brings full institutional death.

By frequency, the most common sanction is a **temporary suspension of clearing rights** by the Aerarium for narrow procedural failures: late monthly clearing reports, discrepancies in serial registration, slow replies to Censor inquiries. These suspensions last days to weeks, embarrass the house and are usually lifted once the failure is mended. The most consequential is **joint expulsion-and-suspension** for fraud or treasonable conduct, such as selling reserves to foreign powers, falsifying notes or sustained fraud against depositors. It has been imposed perhaps a dozen times in the guild's history, and every time it has ended the house.

## Notable Houses and Officers

- **House of Calventian**—the largest of the great houses, founded in Magnápolis three centuries ago and led by the Calventiani family across nine generations. Its senior partner is Argentarius Magnar Lucia Calventiana, the guild's chief officer.
- **House of Maximian Tertian**—the second-largest great house, of Helionite-Vylarian origin and politically aligned with the **Reformist Faction** at court. **Septimus Maximian Tertian** leads it.
- **House of Verrius**—Vylarian and conservative, with strong ties to the Aurelian estate and, historically, to the Dux Bellorum's office.
- **House of Therion**—of Heliónite origin and now continent-spanning, the principal Heliónite great house, which specializes in cross-territory clearing.
- **House of Aldovardian**—Tarvénian, and unusual as a great house of Aurèldían rather than Vylarian-Helionite origin. It won its position by aggressive expansion in the late post-imperial settlement, and **Argentarius Marcus Aldovardian** leads it, regarded by his Vylarian peers with formal correctness and informal suspicion.
- **Septimus Maximian Tertian**—senior partner of his house, widely seen as the most politically capable Argentarius of his generation after Lucia Calventiana and a likely successor when her tenure ends.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]]** is the operational counterpart and ultimate backing. Publicly the relationship is "guild standing," not subordination. The Argentariorum's reserves belong to the broader Aerarium-backed pool, its clearing flows through Aerarium accounts, and its senior leaders coordinate closely and quietly with the Quaestor Imperii.
- **The [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]]** is the chartering authority. The Argentariorum holds the charter that lets it operate as a guild and occupies one of the thirty-six trade-seats on the **Curia Collegiorum**, held ex officio by the Argentarius Magnar. It is subject to the Censores Collegiorum's broader oversight.
- **The post-imperial crowns and the Byzarian League** are major customers and political counterparties. The crowns hold accounts at great houses, borrow heavily against future revenue and negotiate quietly with the guild's leaders over the terms of their banking.
- **The [[affiliation-crmsyndctsthlrn|Crime Syndicates of Thalorna]]** are a perennial concern. The [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|Crimson Ledger]]'s reach into the legitimate banking system is the guild's standing internal problem. Several Censor investigations run continuously, and the Reformist Faction criticizes the Council's discipline of compromised members as too lenient.
- **The Bayt al-Khazînah**, the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s counterpart in Harad, is formally unrecognized. The Bayt's moneylenders run a parallel system, and Argentariorum members may not accept Bayt paper directly. A small number of Heliónite member houses act as quiet cross-border intermediaries, which the guild's leaders deplore officially and tolerate unofficially.

## Identifying Marks

A member moneylender's seal is registered with both the local chapter and the Council at Magnápolis, and it is impressed in colored wax on every note issued: red for retail notes, blue for traveler's notes and gold for master notes. Member moneylenders wear a silver pin at the throat in the form of a balance-scale, and senior partners of great houses wear the same pin in gold. Argentarii Praepares wear an additional brass medallion of office. The Argentarius Magnar wears the great gold pectoral of the guild, kept at the Magnápolis chapter between holders. The Censores Argentariorum wear black mantles with the silver Censor's mark, distinct in pattern from the Censores Collegiorum's mantle though similar in color.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Failing Great House.** One of the great houses, long established and deeply networked, is privately insolvent and has been hiding it behind creative clearing arrangements that the Censores Argentariorum are about to uncover. A great house's failure would shock the system. Rescuing it would take Aerarium intervention on a scale that would publicly acknowledge the polite fiction. The Argentarius Magnar must decide which she prefers.
- **The Aurèldían Petition.** The Tarvénian, Provenzian and Élavendren crowns have formally petitioned the Council for representation, a seat for the senior partner of a great house from non-imperial territory. Granting it would formally recognize the kingdoms' stake in the guild's governance. The Vylarian and Heliónite great houses are divided, and the imperial throne's position is neutral in public and hostile in private.
- **The Compromised Censor.** A Censor Argentariorum's recent findings have favored, as a pattern, member houses with particular court connections. The Argentarius Magnar's investigation is preliminary but suggestive. The Censor is the daughter of an Argentean family with both guild and court ties, so removing her would create political complications and keeping her in office would create different ones.
- **The Heliónite Intermediary.** A Heliónite great house has been confirmed as the principal cross-border intermediary between the Argentariorum and the Sôd-Naqîrîn's Bayt al-Khazînah, against standing guild policy. The volume of intermediated paper is now large enough that public exposure could damage the guild's standing with the Aerarium Imperii and the throne. A quiet sanction is possible but would need the Argentarius Magnar's discretion, and a formal sanction would force the question into the open.
- **The Counterfeit Crisis.** The Censores Argentariorum, working with the Aerarium's own Censores, have uncovered a sustained counterfeiting operation that has put perhaps several thousand forged traveler's notes into circulation across Aurèldía. The forgeries are good enough to have escaped detection for months. The Council is debating whether to honor them on first presentation, which would preserve confidence in the note system, or refuse them, which would limit the guild's losses.
- **The New Charter.** The Magnum Collegium's Censores Collegiorum have begun a routine review of the Argentariorum's guild charter, scheduled for the coming year. Such reviews are usually procedural, but this one has drawn the attention of the Reformist Faction, and a sub-committee with Reformist members will conduct it. The Council is preparing carefully, the great houses are coordinating their testimony, and the Argentarius Magnar hopes the review ends before the next Council elections.
