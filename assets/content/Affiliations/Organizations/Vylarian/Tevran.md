Say a Tarvénian cooper unrolls his master's certificate on a Bizarian counter, far from the cooperage that trained him. Nobody asks which king he serves or whether the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] has quarreled with [[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Tarvénia]] this year. The clerk checks the seal at the foot of the page, finds it on the register, and the cooper is a master in [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]]. The seal is the Tevran Maldor’s, and the clerk's trust in it is the reason the Tevran exists.

## Overview

The Tevran Maldor is the "guild of guilds": a mandatory federation of thirty-six chartered trade-categories, to which every chartered guild in [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], the five [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldian territories]] and [[place-heladrgn|Hellád]] belongs, and to which [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] belonged until its secession. Its [Council of the Tevran]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} sits at [[place-orzara|Orzara]], the imperial capital. [Arzanas]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the second Orzas of the Restoration house, founded it by edict at the close of the western conquests, three centuries ago ([[lore-arzanasedict|The Edicts of Arzanas]]).

The Tevran is the most consequential civil institution the Vylarian Empire ever exported. The empire met a problem as it absorbed older municipal traditions: every conquered city had its own trade guilds, developed over centuries, and none of them answered to anyone in Orzara. Abolishing them would have collapsed the conquered economies. The empire instead required every guild to roll up into one chartered umbrella body answerable to the throne, and made membership the legal precondition for any guild to operate, set standards or train apprentices inside imperial territory.

That umbrella has become the lattice through which the trades of half of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] are organized. A chartered guild in any member territory holds its standing by Tevran recognition. A master's seal is honored across the empire and beyond, because the Tevran keeps the register. An apprentice certified in one city is good in another, because the Tevran has standardized the qualification. A guild that loses recognition loses, in any practical sense, its existence: its masters can no longer legally seal work, its apprentices can no longer be examined, its members lose their portability across realms, and its protection under imperial and municipal law evaporates.

The Tevran does not run individual guilds. It does not set the cooper's measure or the goldsmith's assay; those stay the business of the cooper's guild and the goldsmith's guild. It sits above them. It charters them, recognizes their standards or refuses to, settles disputes between guilds over which trade may claim which work, disciplines guildmasters whose conduct has fallen below the standing it requires, and speaks for the trades as a whole to the commonwealth's governments.

One of the thirty-six trade-categories is [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], the moneylenders' guild, whose chapterhouses across the commonwealth run the banking and note-clearing system behind most large-value commerce. The Danvar is also the visible interface for [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]]’s imperial central-bank functions outside imperial territory, and the reserves it holds belong to the wider Tarmeks-backed pool. Chartering the Danvar is therefore one of the Tevran's most consequential powers: in theory, revoking its charter would collapse the banking system. In practice neither the Tevran nor the throne would tolerate the step, but the formal authority sits with the Tevran all the same.

The Tevran no longer serves the empire as an instrument of imperial governance in the territories it spans. The Aureldian territories, Bizaria and [[place-helionis|Helionis]] have each, in their own ways, withdrawn from imperial political authority over the past two centuries; only Vylaria proper remains under direct imperial governance today, and Helionis, a province on paper with its own magistrates, is practically independent. The Tevran has kept operating across all of them because the smaller polities gain more from taking part than they would from leaving. A Tarvénian master's credentials are recognized in [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia]], Bizaria, Helionis and across Vylaria because the Tevran supplies the framework of mutual recognition, and a Tarvénian government that walked out would lose that access at a stroke. The Aureldian territories put up with the imperial vocabulary, the Orzara leadership and the quietly imperial-backed banking system, because the alternative is to dismantle their commercial infrastructure overnight.

That is the post-imperial commonwealth's central bargain: the Tevran persists because the Aureldian territories cannot afford to leave it, and the empire no longer needs to enforce it.

## Geographic Reach

### Member Territories

- **Vylaria proper.** The Tevran's home territory. Every guild in all four imperial provinces is a member, and the system works at its most integrated here.
- **The Aureldian territories**—[[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Tarvénia]], [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provenzia]], [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Kingdom of Élavendre]], [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] and [[affiliation-calypsa|Kalipsa]]. The Tevran was imposed here during the western conquests. Most pre-existing Aureldian municipal guilds were absorbed and kept their old internal forms beneath the Tevran charter; the rest were reorganized on the Vylarian model. The five governments chafe at the arrangement and periodically test the empire's willingness to enforce it, though none has yet attempted formal withdrawal. By long custom the Aureldian guilds hold themselves a little apart from the Vylarian core: their masters sit in the Council but rarely lead it, and the Tevran's procedures observe Aureldian holidays alongside the imperial calendar.
- **Hellád**—[[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]] and [[place-helionis|Helionis]]. Both came into the system by different routes: Bizaria by formal conquest, and Helionis, whose cities had kept their own councils since the conquest, through the slower pressure of treaty and trade on its city-states. The Bizarian guilds are full members in the Vylarian style. The Heliónite guilds answer to the Tevran, but the city-states keep unusual autonomy in how the rollup is administered.

### Former Member

- **Taruzel.** The Confederation of Taruzelian States was a Tevran member territory for over a century, brought under the Tevran's charter in the generation after its founding. Its violent break with Vylaria twelve years ago removed it from the rollup. The Taruzelian guilds were not dismantled, but the merchant princes who led the rebellion had no wish to keep any Vylarian forms. They set up their own body, the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] ("Council of the Guild-Wardens"), in the Taruzelian tongue and under Taruzelian officer titles, and framed it as a restoration of pre-conquest Taruzelian compact traditions. The Tevran's official position is that the Vezakuni does not legitimately exist. The Council in Orzara still addresses its correspondence to [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] to "Tevran Maldor Taruzel," a name the Vezakuni refuses and the Taruzelian council cites publicly as evidence of imperial bad faith. Taruzelian masters trained in the Tevran era keep personal credentials valid in the Vylarian system. Masters trained or raised under the Vezakuni hold no valid credentials in Vylarian territory at all.

### Influenced but Independent

The [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]]
: [[place-bethuargn|Bethua Region]], [[place-okharisrgn|Okharis Region]] and [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]]. None was ever a Vylarian province and none is a Tevran member, yet all three have guild structures that visibly borrow from the Vylarian model: multi-tier ranks, formal examinations, registered seals and standing inter-guild councils. The Khelathi houses ([[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]], [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] and the others) differ in religious framing, but their administrative procedures are plainly Tevran-derived, and so are those of [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|the Bethuan trade-houses]]. Where trades from the region meet the Tevran, in trade negotiations, embassies or a master traveling abroad, the institutions deal with one another as peers.

### Untouched

The regions south of [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|the Fertile crescent]], and the far north and west
: the Nordlands, Vristwald, Vedyara, Khazryn, Dunhara, Ki'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, Kalihara and the Zerathian regions. The Tevran has no formal presence in any of them, and the occasional Vylarian merchant is the nearest thing to an institutional footprint. [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Vedyara's Merchant Collective]] runs a parallel federation, and for two generations the Tevran has tried without success to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with it.

## Structure

The Tevran's governing structure mirrors the empire's.

Tevraks
: the chair, "First of the College." There is one, appointed by imperial edict for a ten-year term, renewable. He holds the imperial seal of office and presides over the Council. The current Tevraks is [Marcus Velerianus]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a former Vylarian _tarkelos_ (treasury officer) of substantial political weight, who has served eight years and is presumed to be in line for renewal.

The Council of the Tevran
: the standing council, with thirty-six seats, one for each major recognized trade-category (smiths, scribes, cartographers, builders, vintners, drapers, navigators and the rest). A _kelros_ (guild master), elected by the trade's senior masters in conclave, holds each seat for five years. The Council meets in continuous session at Orzara, with formal full convocations four times a year and standing committees that work between them.

The Rozimoi of the Tevran
: the discipline arm, twelve appointed officers who conduct inspections, investigate charter violations and prosecute disciplinary cases before the Council. The _rozimoi_ (auditors) are widely feared and not always loved. The senior _rozimos_ is the **Holrozimos**, presently [Lucia Praetoriana]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, whose reputation for procedural rigor approaches the pathological.

The Tarkelos of the Tevran
: the treasurer, who manages the Tevran's modest common fund, built from member-guild dues, ceremonial revenues and the Tevran's own commercial holdings. The office is administrative and limited. The [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]] and ultimately the [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmeks Orzan]] handle the commonwealth's much larger banking functions.

The Therkalenoi
: twelve provincial deputies: one per Vylarian province, one per Aureldian territory and two for Hellád (one each for Bizaria and Helionis). They administer the Tevran's operations in their regions and report to the Tevraks.

## Authority and Teeth

The Tevran's powers are real and exercised.

- **Charter.** No guild operates legally in a member territory without a Tevran charter. Unchartered "guilds" exist in every region, but they lack standing: they cannot enforce membership requirements on practitioners of the trade, cannot certify apprentices, and cannot bring complaints before any imperial or municipal court as a guild. A candidate guild applies for a charter, and the Council may revoke it on the _rozimoi_' petition.
- **Standards.** The Tevran does not write the cooper's measures. It does require that whatever measures the cooper's guild writes be registered, mutually intelligible with other regions' coopers and, where they touch imperial procurement, compatible with the imperial standard. The mechanism is procedural: guilds file their standards with the Tevran, the _rozimoi_ or other guilds challenge them, and the Council rules on the challenges.
- **Discipline.** The _rozimoi_ can bring a guildmaster before the Council on charges of misconduct that have escaped the master's own guild's disciplinary processes. The Council may censure him (a formal mark in his registered file), suspend him (a temporary inability to seal work) or revoke his seal for good. Revocation is rare and crushing.
- **Inter-guild jurisdiction.** When two guilds dispute which has the rightful claim to a trade or category of work, such as the perennial fight between the woodworkers and the timberwrights or between the scribes and the heralds, the Council rules, and the ruling binds every member territory.
- **Foreign recognition.** The Tevran negotiates trade-recognition agreements with guild systems outside its membership. A Vedyaran merchant in Vylarian territory whose home Collective has such an agreement carries reciprocal recognition; one whose Collective has none does not.

The teeth are not theoretical. In living memory the Tevran has revoked the charters of two minor guilds, one in Provenzia and one in Helionis, for a sustained pattern of credential fraud, and both guilds collapsed. The _rozimoi_ have brought dozens of individual masters to disciplinary hearing in the last decade. Most cases ended in censure or suspension rather than revocation, but the proceedings are public and formal, and they ruin the master's reputation whatever the outcome.

## The Mercenaries and the Compact

The mercenaries' seat, the [Mercenaries' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, is the strangest of the great seats, because the trade it charters is organized violence. Its logic is the same as every other guild's: recognition that travels. A mercenary company answerable to a noble, a crown or a city is legitimate only within that patron's jurisdiction, and the moment it carries arms across a border its standing evaporates and it becomes indistinguishable from a brigand band. A company holding the guild's charter is recognized everywhere the Tevran runs, across Vylaria, the five Aureldian territories, Bizaria and Helionis, exactly as a master smith's seal is honored from realm to realm. The **Thramezgartos** (master of the sellsword companies) vouches for each company's standing and answers for its conduct, and the _rozimoi_ discipline captains who disgrace it. A serious company that takes work across borders therefore holds two things: a charter for portable standing and a specific commission for the job at hand. The masterless company that holds neither is the outlaw band the whole arrangement exists to contain.

Because the charter says nothing about whom a company may serve, two member companies taking contracts on opposite sides of the same war is unremarkable. A Provenzian crown and a Tarvénian one come to blows and each hires chartered companies, guild-brothers who meet in the field as enemies this season and may drink together as friends the next. The guild does not forbid this, because forbidding it would destroy the trade. It regulates it.

Every captain who takes the charter swears the [Compact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} (_zaralon_), the conventions of war the companies hold among themselves. Some of its protections are owed to everyone, in every fight, whatever the enemy. No one is tortured, ever. The surrender of a man who lays down his arms is taken and not answered with the knife. Non-combatants (the farmer, the pilgrim, the merchant, the surgeon) are spared the worst of war regardless of which side they stand near, or none. Heralds and men under a flag of parley are inviolable. These are not the laws of any one crown. They are the trade's own usages, registered with the Council and enforced by it, and a captain who breaks them answers to the _rozimoi_ like any guildmaster who has fouled his craft: by censure, by suspension and, for the worst offenses, by revocation, which strips his company back to a single jurisdiction at best and to outright outlawry at worst.

Above that floor sits a further courtesy owed only to the _recognized_. To a guild company, or to the regular forces of a recognized polity, a captor extends the full usage of the prisoner of war: quarter honored, decent confinement, and ransom or exchange and parole under fixed terms. An enemy who is neither guild nor recognized lawful combatant, such as a masterless band, frontier brigands or a cult under arms on its own account, is handled as a _criminal_. He is still not tortured, his surrender is still taken and his life is not forfeit where he stands, but he is not ransomed or exchanged like a soldier either. He is given over to the law, to trial and to whatever judgment it decrees. The distinction runs both ways. The company a captain fights this year he may stand beside the next, the guild-brother he ransoms honorably will ransom him in turn, and a company that earns a name for treachery finds its own surrenders refused and the Council's patience short.

Everything turns on the word _recognized_, and the word does enormous and deliberately ambiguous work. The same [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame fighters]] are prisoners of war if they march as a recognized contingent of an opposing army, and criminals if they are taken raiding on their own account. They are identical men with an identical creed and opposite fates, decided wholly by whether some authority will own them as lawful combatants. That makes recognition something to be granted, withheld, manufactured and denied. A crown may "recognize" fanatics or irregulars to clothe them in a soldier's protections. A captor with a ransom to dodge or an example to make may withhold recognition and hang soldiers as bandits. The Tevran, the crowns and the companies all have reasons to push the line one way or the other. The humane floor holds throughout, but the gulf between an honored prisoner and a condemned criminal is a political judgment in a legal robe, and that is exactly where the law of war goes gray.

## The Danvar and the Banking System

The Tevran's tie to the commonwealth's banking system runs through one chartered guild, the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]], the moneylenders' guild and one of the thirty-six trade-categories. Its chapterhouses operate the visible banking infrastructure across all member territories, its member moneylenders issue retail notes and arrange traveler's notes, and its great houses (the [House of Calventian]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, the [House of Maximian Tertian]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, the [House of Verrius]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, the [House of Therion]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"} and several others) are the commonwealth's principal credit institutions.

The Danvar's chapter reserves belong to the wider [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmeks Orzan]]-backed pool, its clearing settles through the Tarmeks's accounts, and its master notes are drawn on Tarmeks credit. In imperial territory (Vylaria proper) the tie is overt: the Tarmeks runs its own direct offices beside the Danvar's chapters. In non-imperial territory (the Aureldian territories and Bizaria) the Tarmeks does not appear, the chapterhouses are the entire visible interface, and everyone maintains the polite fiction that the banking system is a guild operation and not an imperial one. The Tevran's chartering authority over the Danvar is what keeps that fiction tenable, because the Aureldian territories take part in a "guild," not in a "branch of the imperial treasury."

The Tevran is therefore a standing patron of the banking system, not its operator. It charters the Danvar, audits its compliance as a chartered guild through the _rozimoi_, and could in principle revoke its charter for cause. It has never done so, and doing so would set off an institutional crisis that neither the Tevran nor the throne would willingly invite. The Danvar's own Council, under the Danvor Maldor's chair, conducts the banking operations, manages the chapter reserves, issues credit and coordinates with the Tarmeks Orzan's leadership. The Tevraks and the Danvor Maldor coordinate closely on guild-political matters, but they do not share decisions about the day-to-day running of the banking system.

The Tevran keeps no treasury beyond the modest funds it needs to run its administrative offices, support the _rozimoi_' investigations and maintain the Council Hall at Orzara. Member-guild dues pay for the institution. The Tevran does not borrow, does not lend and holds no reserves on a scale that would make it a financial actor in its own right.

### Charter and Sanction

The banking system holds together because the Tevran's power to charter is kept separate from the Tarmeks's power to clear. Either institution can sanction a moneylender house in trouble. The _rozimoi_ of the Tevran can recommend suspending the house's guild standing, which in extreme cases leads to revoking the Danvar's charter and more commonly to the house's expulsion from membership while the guild's charter stands. The Tarmeks Orzan can suspend the house's clearing rights against imperial reserves, which functionally ends its access to the master-note system. Neither institution can use its sanction against the other's interests alone, and the most consequential actions need both to agree. That separation keeps either court politics or guild politics from capturing the banking system by itself, and the Tevran's leaders and the Tarmeks's Imperial _Tarkelos_ both treat it as essential.

## Relations

- **The imperial throne** is patron and ultimate authority. The relationship is symbiotic: the throne grants the Tevran its charter and confirms the Tevraks, and the Tevran administers the empire's trade life on the throne's behalf.
- **The [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|Imperial Scribal Guild]]** is the largest single member guild and one of the weightiest. Its High Hand sits on the Council ex officio.
- **The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]** is a sibling imperial institution, and the two keep a careful protocol of mutual non-interference. The Order regulates magical practice and the Tevran regulates trade. A joint Council-Order panel handles cases that touch both: artisans whose work involves magical materials, alchemy-adjacent practices, the rare smith whose tempering verges on the supernatural. The arrangement works because both institutions consider themselves senior and neither wishes to test the question.
- **The [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]]** is formally not recognized. The Council in Orzara holds the doctrinal position that the Taruzelian institution is illegitimate and goes on addressing it by the Vylari name "Tevran Maldor Taruzel," which the Taruzelians treat as a calculated insult and several Vylarian officers privately admit has become more counterproductive than useful. Informally the Vezakuni is a constant counterparty, because Taruzelian masters, contracts and credentials keep flowing through trade networks that ignore the political break. The Council's informal position is that the Vezakuni exists and the arrangement will eventually have to be addressed; the public position is more rigid and changes more slowly.
- **The Aureldian governments** are a chronic source of friction. The Tevran's authority crosses their borders, and several governments periodically test its limits.
- **The non-member guild traditions** are treated as foreign counterparties: the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]], [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|the Skalds' Circle]] of the Nordlands and other northern bodies, the [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] and the Bethuan trade-houses. Standing trade-recognition agreements exist with some (the Skalds' Circle has reciprocal hospitality recognition, and certain Khelathi houses have credential equivalence trade by trade). Others, the Merchant Collective above all, have refused every overture.

## Identifying Marks

The Tevran's seal shows a wreathed open book over a crossed measuring-rule and quill, with the imperial cipher above. It is struck into every charter document, disciplinary ruling and inter-guild jurisdictional decision, and it is the visible mark of the institution's authority across member territories.

The Tevraks wears the great gold pectoral of office, kept at the Council between holders. The _rozimoi_ wear black mantles with the silver _rozimos_'s-mark, and a _rozimos_ in mantle entering a guildhouse brings on an immediate quiet. Council members wear the deep blue stole of the Council at formal sessions and their own guilds' marks outside session.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Charter Revocation Hearing.** The _rozimoi_ have brought charges against a major Aureldian guild whose charter is at risk, and its home government is loudly committed to its defense. It is the most politically charged case in a generation, and the Council's ruling will set the practical limits of the Tevran's authority over the Aureldian crowns for the next quarter century.
- **The Taruzelian Master.** A Taruzelian master who held Tevran credentials at the secession has presented himself at a Vylarian provincial chapter, seeking a new commission. His pre-secession credentials are technically valid, and the Vezakuni's later confirmations of his standing are not. The _therkalen_ (provincial deputy) must decide whether to honor the original credentials, refuse them because a foreign body confirmed him afterward, or refer the matter to the Council for a precedent.
- **The Captured Rozimos.** A _rozimos_ on inspection in the Aureldian countryside has been quietly detained by a local baron who objects to the inspection's target, a guild the baron controls. The detention is not public, the _rozimos_ is unharmed, and the Tevran's response will tell the baron, and everyone watching, what they can expect to get away with.
- **The Tevraks's Succession.** Marcus Velerianus's renewal is in doubt. The imperial court is divided between factions that favor his continuing and factions that want a successor more amenable to recent imperial proposals for tightening the Tevran's discipline of conquered-territory guilds. The contest runs through proxies on the Council, whispered representations at court and the senior _rozimoi_' choice of which cases to bring forward in the months before the renewal vote.
- **The Order Brushfire.** A run of artisanal incidents (a smith's blade that may have been improperly tempered, an alchemist's product that may have been improperly registered, a cartographer whose maps may show features no natural surveying could have captured) has set off overlapping Tevran and Order investigations whose officers work at cross-purposes. The Tevraks and the _zetmarendos_ (archmaster) of the Order are due to meet, and the outcome will set the Order-Tevran boundary for a generation.
- **The Danvar Charter Review.** The _rozimoi_ have begun a routine compliance review of the Danvar's guild charter. Such reviews are usually procedural, but this one has drawn the attention of the [Reformist Faction]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, and a sub-committee with Reformist members will conduct it. An adverse finding could in principle justify revoking the charter, though no one believes the revocation would be allowed to happen. The review's political outcomes may be large regardless.
- **The Insulted Crown.** A senior Vylarian _therkalen_, on a routine inspection of the Tarvénian chapter, said publicly that the Aureldian governments are "honorary participants" and not full members of the Tevran. The remark was widely reported. The Tarvénian crown demands a formal disavowal and the Tevraks is preparing one, while the [Conservative Faction]{.name data-type="affiliation"} at the Vylarian court regards the disavowal as a humiliating concession. The _therkalen_'s career hangs on how the Tevraks handles it.
- **The Withdrawn Recognition.** On a _rozimos_'s adverse finding, an Aureldian guild's seal-recognition has been temporarily suspended, so its masters can no longer seal work that other member territories will honor. The affected government has protested vigorously. The suspension is procedurally proper and politically explosive, and the Tevraks must decide whether to back the _rozimos_, override the finding or refer the matter to the full Council for political cover.
