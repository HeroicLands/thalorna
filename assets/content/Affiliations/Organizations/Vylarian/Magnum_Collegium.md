_Vylarian: Magnum Collegium Collegiorum—"The Great Guild of the Guilds"_

- **Type:** Imperial umbrella institution; mandatory federation of chartered guilds
- **Region:** [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], the five Aurèldían kingdoms ([[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]], [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]], [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], [[affiliation-calypsa|Calypsa]]), and Hellád ([[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]]). Formerly Harad until the recent secession.
- **Seat:** The **Curia Collegiorum** at [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]], the imperial capital
- **Founded:** By edict of the second Vylarian emperor at the close of the western conquests, three centuries ago

## Overview

The Magnum Collegium is the single most consequential civil institution the Vylarian Empire ever exported. It was created—and, more importantly, _imposed_ during the western conquests—as the answer to a problem the empire encountered as it absorbed older municipal traditions: how to regulate, standardize, and ultimately control the patchwork of trade guilds that each conquered city had developed independently over centuries. The empire's solution was not to abolish the existing guilds (which would have collapsed the conquered economies) but to require every one of them to roll up into a single chartered umbrella body answerable to the imperial throne. Membership in the Magnum Collegium became the legal precondition for any guild to operate, set standards, or train apprentices within imperial territory.

The Collegium has become the lattice through which the trades of half of Ankaris are organized. A chartered guild in any member territory holds its standing by Collegium recognition; its master's seal is honored across the empire and beyond because the Collegium maintains the register; an apprentice's certification in one city is good in another because the Collegium has standardized the qualification. A guild that loses Collegium recognition loses, in any practical sense, its existence: its masters can no longer legally seal work, its apprentices can no longer be examined, its members lose the cross-realm portability that membership confers, and its protections under imperial and municipal law evaporate.

The Collegium does not run individual guilds. It does not set the cooper's measure or the goldsmith's assay—those remain the cooper's guild's and the goldsmith's guild's business. What it does is sit above them: it charters them, recognizes their standards (or refuses), adjudicates inter-guild jurisdictional disputes (which trade gets to claim which work), disciplines guildmasters whose conduct has fallen below the standing the Collegium considers essential, and acts as the commonwealth's interlocutor with the trades as a whole.

Among the thirty-six trade-categories the Collegium charters is the **[[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]]**—the moneylenders' guild, whose chapterhouses across the commonwealth operate the banking and note-clearing infrastructure that most large-value commerce depends on. The Argentariorum is, in turn, the visible interface for the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|imperial treasury]]'s central-bank functions in non-imperial territory; the reserves the Argentariorum holds are part of the broader Aerarium-backed pool. The Collegium's chartering authority over the Argentariorum is therefore one of its most consequential single powers: revocation of the Argentariorum's charter would (in theory) collapse the banking system. The reality is more nuanced—neither the Collegium nor the imperial throne would tolerate such a step—but the formal authority sits with the Collegium nonetheless.

Crucially, the Collegium is no longer an instrument of imperial governance in the territories it spans. The Aurèldían kingdoms, Byzaría, and Heliónis have all in their various ways withdrawn from imperial political authority over the past two centuries; only Vylaria and Heliónis remain under direct imperial governance today. The Magnum Collegium, however, has continued to operate across all member territories—_because the smaller polities benefit from participation more than they would benefit from withdrawal_. A Tarvénian guild master's credentials are recognized in Provènzia, Byzaría, Heliónis, and across Vylaria because the Collegium provides the mutual-recognition framework; a Tarvénian crown that walked out of the Collegium would lose that access in a single stroke. The kingdoms tolerate the imperial-flavored institutional vocabulary, the Magnápolis-centered leadership, the quietly imperial-backed banking system the Argentariorum provides—because the alternative is to dismantle their commercial infrastructure overnight.

This is the post-imperial commonwealth's central institutional logic: the Collegium persists not because the empire enforces it but because the kingdoms cannot afford to leave it.

## Geographic Reach

### Member Territories (Mandatory Rollup)

- **Vylaria proper.** The Collegium's home territory; all guilds in all four imperial provinces are members; the system functions at its most integrated here.
- **The Aurèldían kingdoms**—Tarvénia, Provènzia, Élavendre, Aelwyth, Calypsa. The Collegium was imposed during the western conquests three centuries ago. Pre-existing Aurèldían municipal guilds were either absorbed (most of them, retaining their old internal forms beneath the Collegium charter) or reorganized into the Vylarian model. The five Aurèldían crowns chafe at the arrangement and periodically test the empire's willingness to enforce it, but no Aurèldían crown has yet attempted formal withdrawal. The Aurèldían guilds, by long custom, hold themselves a little apart from the Vylarian core—their masters sit in the Curia but rarely lead it; the Collegium's procedures observe Aurèldían holidays alongside the imperial calendar.
- **Hellád**—Byzaría and Heliónis. Both incorporated into the system through different routes: Byzaría through formal conquest, Heliónis through the slower mechanism of treaty and economic pressure on the city-states. The Byzarian guilds are full members in the Vylarian style; the Heliónite city-states maintain a particular sub-arrangement in which their guilds answer to the Collegium but the city-states themselves retain unusual autonomy in how the rollup is administered.

### Former Member (Recent Secession)

- **Harad.** The Confederation of Haradian States was a Collegium member territory for over a century, having been incorporated during the empire's eastern expansion. The violent break with Vylaria twelve years ago removed Harad from the rollup. The Haradian guilds were not dismantled, but the merchant princes who had led the rebellion had no intention of preserving any Vylarian forms; they constituted their own institution—the **[[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]** ("Council of the Guild-Wardens")—in the haradi-language, under Haradian officer titles, framed as a restoration of pre-conquest Haradian compact traditions rather than as any kind of continuation of imperial work. The Vylarian Collegium's official position is that the Sôd does not legitimately exist; correspondence from the Curia at Magnápolis to Tamavar continues, twelve years later, to be addressed to "Magnum Collegium Haradicum," a name the Sôd refuses and which the Haradian council publicly cites as evidence of imperial bad faith. Haradian masters trained in the Collegium era retain personal credentials valid in the Vylarian system; masters trained or elevated under the Sôd do not, in Vylarian territory, hold valid credentials at all.

### Influenced but Independent

- **The Northern Fertile Region**—[[place-bethuargn|Bethûa]], [[place-okharisrgn|Okháris]], [[place-takheperurgn|Ta'Kheperu]]. None were ever conquered by Vylaria; none are Collegium members. All three have, however, developed guild structures that visibly borrow from the Vylarian model—multi-tier ranks, formal examinations, registered seals, standing inter-guild councils. The Ta'Kheperan houses ([[affiliation-permesnu|Per'Mesnu]], [[affiliation-pernuw|Per'Nuw]], and the others) are nominally distinct institutions in religious framing but their administrative procedures are unmistakably Collegium-derived. The Bethûan matriarchal trade-houses likewise. Where members of Northern Fertile Region trades come into contact with the Collegium—through trade negotiations, embassies, occasional masters traveling abroad—the institutions deal with one another as peers, not as parent-and-subordinate.

### Untouched

- **The Nordlands, Vrystwald, Vedyara, Khazryn, Dunhara, K'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, Kalihara, Xerathian regions south of [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|the Fertile crescent]].** No formal Collegium presence; no influence on guild structure beyond the occasional Vylarian merchant whose presence is the closest thing to an institutional footprint. Vedyara's [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] in particular operates a parallel federation that the Collegium has, for two generations, attempted to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with—without success.

## Structure

The Collegium's governing structure mirrors the empire's:

- **Princeps Collegii**—the chair; "First of the College"; one position; appointed by imperial edict for a ten-year term, renewable. Holds the imperial seal of office and presides over the Curia. The current Princeps is **Marcus Velerianus**, a former Vylarian Quaestor of substantial political weight; he has served eight years and is presumed to be in line for renewal.
- **The Curia Collegiorum**—the standing council; thirty-six seats, one per major recognized trade-category (smiths, scribes, cartographers, builders, vintners, drapers, navigators, etc.). Each seat is held by a Master Magister elected by the relevant trades' senior masters in conclave; terms are five years.
- **The Censores Collegiorum**—the discipline arm; twelve appointed officers; conduct inspections, investigate charter violations, prosecute disciplinary cases before the Curia. The Censors are widely feared and not always loved. The senior Censor is the **Censor Maximus**, presently **Lucia Praetoriana**, a woman whose reputation for procedural rigor approaches the pathological.
- **The Quaestor Collegii**—the treasurer; manages the Collegium's modest common fund (built from member guild dues, ceremonial revenues, and the Collegium's own commercial holdings). The Quaestor Collegii's role is administrative and limited; the much larger banking functions of the commonwealth are handled by the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]] and ultimately the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]], not by this office.
- **The Magistri Provinciarum**—provincial deputies; one per Vylarian province, one per Aurèldían kingdom, two for Hellád (one for Byzaría, one for Heliónis). Twelve in total. They administer the Collegium's operations in their regions and report to the Princeps.

The Curia meets in continuous session at Magnápolis, with formal full convocations four times a year and standing committees that work between convocations.

## Authority and Teeth

The Collegium's powers are real and exercised:

- **Charter.** No guild operates legally in a member territory without Collegium charter. Unchartered "guilds" exist (every region has them) but lack standing, cannot enforce membership requirements on trade practitioners, cannot certify apprentices, and cannot bring complaints before any imperial or municipal court as a guild. Charter is granted on application by a candidate guild and may be revoked by the Curia on petition of the Censores.
- **Standards.** The Collegium does not write the cooper's measures; it does require that whatever measures the cooper's guild writes be registered, mutually intelligible with other regions' coopers, and (where they touch imperial procurement) compatible with imperial standard. The mechanism is procedural—guild standards are filed with the Collegium and challenged by the Censores or by other guilds; the Curia rules on challenges.
- **Discipline.** The Censores can bring a guildmaster before the Curia on charges of misconduct that have escaped the master's home guild's own disciplinary processes. The Curia's rulings can include censure (a formal mark in the master's registered file), suspension (temporary inability to seal work), or revocation (permanent loss of the master's seal). Revocation is rare and crushing.
- **Inter-Guild Jurisdiction.** When two guilds dispute which has the rightful claim to a particular trade or work-category—the perennial fight between (e.g.) the woodworkers and the timberwrights, or between the scribes and the heralds—the Curia rules. The ruling is binding throughout member territories.
- **Foreign Recognition.** The Collegium negotiates trade-recognition agreements with non-member guild systems. A Vedyaran merchant traveling in Vylarian territory whose home Collective has a Collegium agreement carries reciprocal recognition; a Vedyaran whose Collective has no agreement does not.

The teeth are not theoretical. The Collegium has, in living memory, revoked the charters of two minor guilds (one in Provènzia, one in Heliónis) for sustained pattern of credential fraud; both guilds collapsed. The Censores have brought dozens of individual masters to disciplinary hearing in the last decade; most cases ended in censure or suspension rather than revocation, but the proceedings themselves are public, formal, and ruinous to the master's reputation regardless of outcome.

## The Mercenaries and the Compact

The mercenaries' seat—the **Collegium Mercennariorum**—is the strangest of the great seats, for the trade it charters is organized violence. Its logic, however, is identical to every other guild's: portable, cross-realm recognition. A mercenary company answerable to a noble, a crown, or a city is legitimate only within that patron's jurisdiction; let it carry its arms across a border and its standing evaporates, leaving it indistinguishable from a brigand band. A company that holds the guild's charter, by contrast, is recognized everywhere the Magnum Collegium runs—across Vylaria, the five Aurèldían kingdoms, Byzaría, and Heliónis alike—exactly as a master smith's seal is honored from realm to realm. The **Magister Mercennariorum** vouches for its standing and answers for its conduct, and the Censores Collegiorum discipline captains who disgrace it. A serious company that takes work across borders therefore holds _both_ a charter, for portable standing, and a specific commission, for the job at hand; the masterless company that holds neither is the outlaw band the whole arrangement exists to contain.

Because the charter confers a standing rather than a leash on whom a company may serve, it is both recognized and entirely unremarkable that two member companies should take contracts on opposite sides of the same war. A Provènzian crown and a Tarvénian one come to blows, and each hires chartered companies—guild-brothers who will meet in the field as enemies this season and, very possibly, drink together as friends the next. The guild does not forbid this. To forbid it would be to destroy the trade. What the guild does instead is **regulate** it.

Every captain who takes the charter swears the **Compact** (_Pactum Mercennariorum_)—the conventions of war the companies hold among themselves. Some of its protections are owed to _everyone_, in every fight, whatever the enemy: no torture, ever; the surrender of a man who lays down his arms is taken rather than answered with the knife; and non-combatants—the farmer, the pilgrim, the merchant, the surgeon—are spared the worst of war's appetite regardless of which side they stand near, or none. Heralds and men under flag of parley are inviolable. These are not the laws of any one crown; they are the trade's own usages, registered with the Curia and enforced by it, and a captain who breaks them answers to the Censores like any guildmaster who has fouled his craft—by censure, by suspension, and for the worst offenses by revocation, which strips his company back to a single jurisdiction at best and to outright outlawry at worst.

Above that floor sits a further courtesy owed only to the _recognized_. To a guild company, or to the regular forces of a recognized polity, a captor extends the full usage of the prisoner of war—quarter honored, decent confinement, ransom or exchange and parole under fixed terms. An enemy who is neither guild nor recognized lawful combatant—a masterless band, frontier brigands, a cult under arms on its own account—is handled instead as a _criminal_: still not tortured, his surrender still taken, his life not forfeit where he stands, but not ransomed or exchanged like a soldier either. He is given over to the law, to trial, and to whatever judgment it decrees. The distinction is reciprocal as much as principled: the company a captain fights this year he may stand beside the next, the guild-brother he ransoms honorably will ransom him in turn, and the company that earns a name for treachery finds its own surrenders refused and the Curia's patience short.

Everything, then, turns on the word _recognized_, and the word is doing enormous and deliberately ambiguous work. The same [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] fighters are prisoners of war if they march as a recognized contingent of an opposing army, and criminals if they are taken raiding on their own account—identical men, identical creed, opposite fates, decided wholly by whether some authority will own them as lawful combatants. That makes recognition a thing to be granted, withheld, manufactured, and denied: a crown may "recognize" fanatics or irregulars to clothe them in a soldier's protections; a captor with a ransom to dodge or an example to make may withhold recognition and hang soldiers as bandits; the Collegium, the crowns, and the companies all have their reasons to push the line one way or the other. The humane floor holds across all of it—but the gulf between an honored prisoner and a condemned criminal is, in the end, a political judgment wearing a legal robe, and that is exactly where the law of war goes gray.

## The Argentariorum and the Banking System

The Collegium's relationship to the commonwealth's banking system runs through one specific chartered guild: the **[[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]]**—the moneylenders' guild, one of the thirty-six trade-categories the Collegium recognizes. The Argentariorum's chapterhouses operate the visible banking infrastructure across all member territories; its member moneylenders issue retail notes and arrange traveler's notes; its great houses (the House of Calventian, the House of Maximian Tertian, the House of Verrius, the House of Therion, and several others) are the principal credit institutions of the commonwealth.

The Argentariorum's chapter reserves are part of the broader [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]-backed pool; its clearing system ultimately settles through the Aerarium's accounts; its master notes are drawn on Aerarium credit. In imperial territory (Vylaria and Heliónis) this relationship is overt—the Aerarium operates its own direct offices alongside the Argentariorum's chapters. In non-imperial territory (the Aurèldían kingdoms and Byzaría) the Aerarium does not directly appear; the Argentariorum's chapterhouses serve as the entire visible interface, and the polite fiction is universally maintained that the banking system is a guild operation rather than an imperial one. The Magnum Collegium's chartering authority over the Argentariorum is what makes that fiction tenable: the kingdoms participate in a "guild," not in a "branch of the imperial treasury."

The Collegium's actual relationship to the banking system is therefore one of _standing patronage_ rather than direct operation. The Magnum Collegium charters the Argentariorum, audits its compliance as a chartered guild (through the Censores Collegiorum), and can in principle revoke its charter for cause—a power that has never been exercised and the exercise of which would precipitate immediate institutional crisis on a scale neither the Collegium nor the imperial throne would willingly invite. The Argentariorum's actual banking operations, the management of its chapter reserves, the issuance of credit, and the coordination with the Aerarium Imperii's leadership are all conducted by the Argentariorum's own Council under the Argentarius Magnar's chair. The Collegium's Princeps and the Argentarius Magnar coordinate closely on guild-political matters; they do not, in practice, share decisions about the banking system's day-to-day operation.

The Collegium does not maintain a treasury of its own beyond the modest funds needed to operate its administrative offices, support the Censores Collegiorum's investigative work, and maintain the Curia Hall at Magnápolis. Member-guild dues fund the institution; the Collegium does not borrow, does not lend, and does not hold reserves on a scale that would make it a financial actor in its own right.

### Charter and Sanction

The constitutional check that keeps the banking system functioning rests on the _separation_ of the Collegium's chartering authority from the Aerarium's clearing authority. A moneylender house in trouble can be sanctioned by either institution: the Censores Collegiorum can recommend suspension of the house's guild standing (which would, in extreme cases, lead to revocation of the Argentariorum's charter or, more commonly, to the house's expulsion from guild membership while the Argentariorum's overall charter persists); the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]] can suspend the house's clearing rights against imperial reserves (which would functionally end its access to the master-note system). Neither sanction can be wielded unilaterally by either institution against the other's interests; coordination is required for the most consequential actions. This separation is what prevents the banking system from being captured by either court politics or guild politics in isolation—a feature the Collegium's leadership and the Aerarium's Quaestor Imperii both consider essential.

## Relations

- **The Imperial Throne**—patron and ultimate authority. The relationship is symbiotic; the Throne grants the Collegium its charter and confirms the Princeps, the Collegium administers the trade life of the empire on the Throne's behalf.
- **The [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]]**—the largest single member guild and one of the most politically weighty; the Scribal Guild's High Hand sits on the Curia ex officio.
- **The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]**—sibling imperial institution; the Ordo and the Collegium maintain a careful protocol of mutual non-interference. The Ordo regulates magical practice; the Collegium regulates trade. Cases that touch both (artisans whose work involves magical materials, alchemy-adjacent practices, the rare smith whose tempering procedures verge on the supernatural) are handled by joint Curia-Ordo panel. The arrangement works because both institutions consider themselves senior and neither wishes to test the question.
- **The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]**—formally not recognized. The Curia at Magnápolis maintains the doctrinal position that the Haradian institution is illegitimate and continues to address its correspondence by the Latinized name "Magnum Collegium Haradicum"—a usage the Haradians treat as a calculated insult and which several Vylarian officers privately concede has become more counterproductive than useful. Informally the Sôd is a constant counterparty as Haradian masters, contracts, and credentials continue to flow through trade networks that ignore the political break. The Curia's informal position is that the Sôd exists and that the arrangement will eventually have to be addressed; the public position is more rigid and changes more slowly.
- **The Aurèldían Crowns**—chronic friction. The Collegium's authority crosses the crowns' borders, and several crowns periodically test the limits.
- **The non-member guild traditions**—the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]], the Nordlands' [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] and other northern bodies, the [[affiliation-permesnu|Ta'Kheperan houses]], the Bethûan trade-houses—treated as foreign counterparties. Standing trade-recognition agreements exist with some (the Skalds' Circle has reciprocal hospitality recognition; certain Ta'Kheperan houses have credential equivalence on a per-trade basis); others (notably the Merchant Collective) have refused all overtures.

## Identifying Marks

The Collegium's seal—a wreathed open book over crossed measuring-rule and quill, with the imperial cipher above—is struck into every charter document, every disciplinary ruling, every inter-guild jurisdictional decision. The seal's impression is the visible mark of the institution's authority and is widely recognized across member territories.

Princeps Collegii wears the great gold pectoral of office, kept at the Curia between holders. The Censores wear black mantles with the silver censor's-mark; this dress is the visible marker of disciplinary authority, and a Censor in mantle entering a guildhouse is an event that produces immediate quiet. Curia members wear the deep blue stole of the Curia at formal sessions; outside session they wear their own guilds' marks.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Charter Revocation Hearing.** The Censores have brought charges against a major Aurèldían guild whose charter is at risk of revocation; the guild's home crown is loudly committed to its defense; the case is the most politically charged in a generation. The Curia's ruling will define the practical limits of the Collegium's authority over the Aurèldían crowns for the next quarter century.
- **The Haradian Master.** A Haradian master who held Collegium credentials at the time of the secession has presented himself at a Vylarian provincial chapter, seeking to take a new commission. His pre-secession credentials are technically valid; the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s subsequent confirmations of his standing are not. The Magister Provinciarum must rule on whether to honor the original credentials, refuse them on grounds of subsequent foreign confirmation, or refer the matter to the Curia for precedent-setting.
- **The Captured Censor.** A Censor on inspection in the Aurèldían countryside has been quietly detained by a local baron who objects to the inspection's intended target (a guild the baron personally controls). The detention has not been made public; the Censor has not been harmed; the Collegium's response will define what the baron, and others watching, can in future expect to get away with.
- **The Princeps's Succession.** Marcus Velerianus's renewal is in question; the imperial court is divided between factions favoring his continuation and factions favoring a successor more amenable to recent imperial proposals for tightening the Collegium's discipline of conquered-territory guilds. The contest is being conducted through proxies on the Curia, through whispered representations at the imperial court, and through the senior Censors' selection of which cases to bring forward in the months before the renewal vote.
- **The Ordo Brushfire.** A series of artisanal incidents—a smith's blade that may have been improperly tempered, an alchemist's product that may have been improperly registered, a cartographer whose maps may exhibit features no purely natural surveying could have captured—has produced overlapping Collegium and Ordo investigations whose officers are at cross-purposes. The Princeps Collegii and the Archmagister of the Ordo are scheduled to meet; the outcome will set the Ordo-Collegium boundary for the next generation.
- **The Argentariorum Charter Review.** The Censores Collegiorum have begun a routine compliance review of the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]]'s guild charter. Such reviews are usually procedural; this one has, by procedural anomaly, attracted Reformist Faction attention, and a sub-committee with Reformist representation will conduct the review. Anyone familiar with the political stakes understands that an adverse finding could in principle justify revocation of the Argentariorum's charter—though no one believes such revocation would actually be permitted to occur. The review's _political_ outcomes may be substantial regardless.
- **The Insulted Crown.** A senior Vylarian Magistar Provinciarum, during a routine inspection visit to the Tarvénian Collegium chapter, made a public statement implying that the Aurèldían crowns are "honorary participants" rather than full members in the Collegium structure. The remark was widely reported. The Tarvénian crown has demanded a formal disavowal; the Princeps Collegii is preparing one; the Conservative Faction at the Vylarian court regards the disavowal as a humiliating concession. The Magistar in question's career hangs on how the Princeps handles the matter.
- **The Withdrawn Recognition.** A particular Aurèldían guild's seal-recognition has, on a Censor's adverse finding, been temporarily suspended—meaning Aurèldían masters of that guild can no longer seal work that other member territories will honor. The crown of the affected kingdom has protested vigorously; the suspension is procedurally proper but politically explosive; the Princeps must decide whether to support the Censor, override the finding, or refer the matter to the full Curia for political cover.
