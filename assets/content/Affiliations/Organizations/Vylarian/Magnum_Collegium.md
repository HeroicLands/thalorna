Say a Tarvénian cooper unrolls his master's certificate on a Byzarian counter, far from the cooperage that trained him. Nobody asks which king he serves or whether the Byzarian League has quarreled with Tarvénia this year. The clerk checks the seal at the foot of the page, finds it on the register, and the cooper is a master in Byzaría. The seal is the Magnum Collegium's, and the clerk's trust in it is the reason the Collegium exists.

## Overview

The Magnum Collegium is the "guild of guilds": a mandatory federation of thirty-six chartered trade-categories, to which every chartered guild in [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], the five [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]] and [[place-heladrgn|Hellád]] belongs, and to which Harad belonged until its secession. Its **Curia Collegiorum** sits at [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]], the imperial capital. The second Augustar (the Vylarian emperor) founded it by edict at the close of the western conquests, three centuries ago.

The Collegium is the most consequential civil institution the Vylarian Empire ever exported. The empire met a problem as it absorbed older municipal traditions: every conquered city had its own trade guilds, developed over centuries, and none of them answered to anyone in Magnápolis. Abolishing them would have collapsed the conquered economies. The empire instead required every guild to roll up into one chartered umbrella body answerable to the throne, and made membership the legal precondition for any guild to operate, set standards or train apprentices inside imperial territory.

That umbrella has become the lattice through which the trades of half of Ankaris are organized. A chartered guild in any member territory holds its standing by Collegium recognition. A master's seal is honored across the empire and beyond, because the Collegium keeps the register. An apprentice certified in one city is good in another, because the Collegium has standardized the qualification. A guild that loses recognition loses, in any practical sense, its existence: its masters can no longer legally seal work, its apprentices can no longer be examined, its members lose their portability across realms, and its protection under imperial and municipal law evaporates.

The Collegium does not run individual guilds. It does not set the cooper's measure or the goldsmith's assay; those stay the business of the cooper's guild and the goldsmith's guild. It sits above them. It charters them, recognizes their standards or refuses to, settles disputes between guilds over which trade may claim which work, disciplines guildmasters whose conduct has fallen below the standing it requires, and speaks for the trades as a whole to the commonwealth's governments.

One of the thirty-six trade-categories is the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]], the moneylenders' guild, whose chapterhouses across the commonwealth run the banking and note-clearing system behind most large-value commerce. The Argentariorum is also the visible interface for the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|imperial treasury's]] central-bank functions outside imperial territory, and the reserves it holds belong to the wider Aerarium-backed pool. Chartering the Argentariorum is therefore one of the Collegium's most consequential powers: in theory, revoking its charter would collapse the banking system. In practice neither the Collegium nor the throne would tolerate the step, but the formal authority sits with the Collegium all the same.

The Collegium no longer serves the empire as an instrument of imperial governance in the territories it spans. The Aurèldían kingdoms, Byzaría and Heliónis have each, in their own ways, withdrawn from imperial political authority over the past two centuries; only Vylaria and Heliónis remain under direct imperial governance today. The Collegium has kept operating across all of them because the smaller polities gain more from taking part than they would from leaving. A Tarvénian master's credentials are recognized in Provènzia, Byzaría, Heliónis and across Vylaria because the Collegium supplies the framework of mutual recognition, and a Tarvénian crown that walked out would lose that access at a stroke. The kingdoms put up with the imperial vocabulary, the Magnápolis leadership and the quietly imperial-backed banking system, because the alternative is to dismantle their commercial infrastructure overnight.

That is the post-imperial commonwealth's central bargain: the Collegium persists because the kingdoms cannot afford to leave it, and the empire no longer needs to enforce it.

## Geographic Reach

### Member Territories

- **Vylaria proper.** The Collegium's home territory. Every guild in all four imperial provinces is a member, and the system works at its most integrated here.
- **The Aurèldían kingdoms**—[[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]], [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]], [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] and [[affiliation-calypsa|Calypsa]]. The Collegium was imposed here during the western conquests. Most pre-existing Aurèldían municipal guilds were absorbed and kept their old internal forms beneath the Collegium charter; the rest were reorganized on the Vylarian model. The five crowns chafe at the arrangement and periodically test the empire's willingness to enforce it, though none has yet attempted formal withdrawal. By long custom the Aurèldían guilds hold themselves a little apart from the Vylarian core: their masters sit in the Curia but rarely lead it, and the Collegium's procedures observe Aurèldían holidays alongside the imperial calendar.
- **Hellád**—[[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]]. Both came into the system by different routes: Byzaría by formal conquest, and Heliónis through the slower pressure of treaty and trade on its city-states. The Byzarian guilds are full members in the Vylarian style. The Heliónite guilds answer to the Collegium, but the city-states keep unusual autonomy in how the rollup is administered.

### Former Member

- **Harad.** The Confederation of Haradian States was a Collegium member territory for over a century, incorporated during the empire's eastern expansion. Its violent break with Vylaria twelve years ago removed it from the rollup. The Haradian guilds were not dismantled, but the merchant princes who led the rebellion had no wish to keep any Vylarian forms. They set up their own body, the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]] ("Council of the Guild-Wardens"), in the Haradian tongue and under Haradian officer titles, and framed it as a restoration of pre-conquest Haradian compact traditions. The Collegium's official position is that the Sôd does not legitimately exist. The Curia in Magnápolis still addresses its correspondence to [[place-tamavar2|Tamavar]] to "Magnum Collegium Haradicum," a name the Sôd refuses and the Haradian council cites publicly as evidence of imperial bad faith. Haradian masters trained in the Collegium era keep personal credentials valid in the Vylarian system. Masters trained or raised under the Sôd hold no valid credentials in Vylarian territory at all.

### Influenced but Independent

- **The Northern Fertile Region**—[[place-bethuargn|Bethûa]], [[place-okharisrgn|Okháris]] and [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu Region]]. None was ever a Vylarian province and none is a Collegium member, yet all three have guild structures that visibly borrow from the Vylarian model: multi-tier ranks, formal examinations, registered seals and standing inter-guild councils. The Khelâthi houses ([[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]], [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] and the others) differ in religious framing, but their administrative procedures are plainly Collegium-derived, and so are those of the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Bethûan trade-houses]]. Where trades from the region meet the Collegium, in trade negotiations, embassies or a master traveling abroad, the institutions deal with one another as peers.

### Untouched

- **The regions south of [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|the Fertile crescent]], and the far north and west:** the Nordlands, Vrystwald, Vedyara, Khazryn, Dunhara, K'ich'chik, Iseron, Aurionis, Kalihara and the Xerathian regions. The Collegium has no formal presence in any of them, and the occasional Vylarian merchant is the nearest thing to an institutional footprint. Vedyara's [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] runs a parallel federation, and for two generations the Collegium has tried without success to negotiate trade-recognition agreements with it.

## Structure

The Collegium's governing structure mirrors the empire's.

- **Princeps Collegii**—the chair, "First of the College." There is one, appointed by imperial edict for a ten-year term, renewable. He holds the imperial seal of office and presides over the Curia. The current Princeps is **Marcus Velerianus**, a former Vylarian Quaestor of substantial political weight, who has served eight years and is presumed to be in line for renewal.
- **The Curia Collegiorum**—the standing council, with thirty-six seats, one for each major recognized trade-category (smiths, scribes, cartographers, builders, vintners, drapers, navigators and the rest). A Master Magister, elected by the trade's senior masters in conclave, holds each seat for five years. The Curia meets in continuous session at Magnápolis, with formal full convocations four times a year and standing committees that work between them.
- **The Censores Collegiorum**—the discipline arm, twelve appointed officers who conduct inspections, investigate charter violations and prosecute disciplinary cases before the Curia. The Censors are widely feared and not always loved. The senior Censor is the **Censor Maximus**, presently **Lucia Praetoriana**, whose reputation for procedural rigor approaches the pathological.
- **The Quaestor Collegii**—the treasurer, who manages the Collegium's modest common fund, built from member-guild dues, ceremonial revenues and the Collegium's own commercial holdings. The office is administrative and limited. The [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]] and ultimately the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] handle the commonwealth's much larger banking functions.
- **The Magistri Provinciarum**—twelve provincial deputies: one per Vylarian province, one per Aurèldían kingdom and two for Hellád (one each for Byzaría and Heliónis). They administer the Collegium's operations in their regions and report to the Princeps.

More than twenty carry real weight, because the trade is strategic, the guild is enormous, or its work brushes the business of the throne, the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo]] or the banks. Those seats decide the Curia's votes and fill its committees. The rest belong to the ordinary settled trades, whose masters guard their measures jealously but whose seats rarely speak to any matter beyond their own craft. The seats of consequence are:

## Authority and Teeth

The Collegium's powers are real and exercised.

- **Charter.** No guild operates legally in a member territory without a Collegium charter. Unchartered "guilds" exist in every region, but they lack standing: they cannot enforce membership requirements on practitioners of the trade, cannot certify apprentices, and cannot bring complaints before any imperial or municipal court as a guild. A candidate guild applies for a charter, and the Curia may revoke it on the Censores' petition.
- **Standards.** The Collegium does not write the cooper's measures. It does require that whatever measures the cooper's guild writes be registered, mutually intelligible with other regions' coopers and, where they touch imperial procurement, compatible with the imperial standard. The mechanism is procedural: guilds file their standards with the Collegium, the Censores or other guilds challenge them, and the Curia rules on the challenges.
- **Discipline.** The Censores can bring a guildmaster before the Curia on charges of misconduct that have escaped the master's own guild's disciplinary processes. The Curia may censure him (a formal mark in his registered file), suspend him (a temporary inability to seal work) or revoke his seal for good. Revocation is rare and crushing.
- **Inter-guild jurisdiction.** When two guilds dispute which has the rightful claim to a trade or category of work, such as the perennial fight between the woodworkers and the timberwrights or between the scribes and the heralds, the Curia rules, and the ruling binds every member territory.
- **Foreign recognition.** The Collegium negotiates trade-recognition agreements with guild systems outside its membership. A Vedyaran merchant in Vylarian territory whose home Collective has such an agreement carries reciprocal recognition; one whose Collective has none does not.

The teeth are not theoretical. In living memory the Collegium has revoked the charters of two minor guilds, one in Provènzia and one in Heliónis, for a sustained pattern of credential fraud, and both guilds collapsed. The Censores have brought dozens of individual masters to disciplinary hearing in the last decade. Most cases ended in censure or suspension rather than revocation, but the proceedings are public and formal, and they ruin the master's reputation whatever the outcome.

## The Mercenaries and the Compact

The mercenaries' seat, the Collegium Mercennariorum, is the strangest of the great seats, because the trade it charters is organized violence. Its logic is the same as every other guild's: recognition that travels. A mercenary company answerable to a noble, a crown or a city is legitimate only within that patron's jurisdiction, and the moment it carries arms across a border its standing evaporates and it becomes indistinguishable from a brigand band. A company holding the guild's charter is recognized everywhere the Collegium runs, across Vylaria, the five Aurèldían kingdoms, Byzaría and Heliónis, exactly as a master smith's seal is honored from realm to realm. The **Magister Mercennariorum** vouches for each company's standing and answers for its conduct, and the Censores discipline captains who disgrace it. A serious company that takes work across borders therefore holds two things: a charter for portable standing and a specific commission for the job at hand. The masterless company that holds neither is the outlaw band the whole arrangement exists to contain.

Because the charter says nothing about whom a company may serve, two member companies taking contracts on opposite sides of the same war is unremarkable. A Provenzian crown and a Tarvénian one come to blows and each hires chartered companies, guild-brothers who meet in the field as enemies this season and may drink together as friends the next. The guild does not forbid this, because forbidding it would destroy the trade. It regulates it.

Every captain who takes the charter swears the Compact (_Pactum Mercennariorum_), the conventions of war the companies hold among themselves. Some of its protections are owed to everyone, in every fight, whatever the enemy. No one is tortured, ever. The surrender of a man who lays down his arms is taken and not answered with the knife. Non-combatants (the farmer, the pilgrim, the merchant, the surgeon) are spared the worst of war regardless of which side they stand near, or none. Heralds and men under a flag of parley are inviolable. These are not the laws of any one crown. They are the trade's own usages, registered with the Curia and enforced by it, and a captain who breaks them answers to the Censores like any guildmaster who has fouled his craft: by censure, by suspension and, for the worst offenses, by revocation, which strips his company back to a single jurisdiction at best and to outright outlawry at worst.

Above that floor sits a further courtesy owed only to the _recognized_. To a guild company, or to the regular forces of a recognized polity, a captor extends the full usage of the prisoner of war: quarter honored, decent confinement, and ransom or exchange and parole under fixed terms. An enemy who is neither guild nor recognized lawful combatant, such as a masterless band, frontier brigands or a cult under arms on its own account, is handled as a _criminal_. He is still not tortured, his surrender is still taken and his life is not forfeit where he stands, but he is not ransomed or exchanged like a soldier either. He is given over to the law, to trial and to whatever judgment it decrees. The distinction runs both ways. The company a captain fights this year he may stand beside the next, the guild-brother he ransoms honorably will ransom him in turn, and a company that earns a name for treachery finds its own surrenders refused and the Curia's patience short.

Everything turns on the word _recognized_, and the word does enormous and deliberately ambiguous work. The same [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] fighters are prisoners of war if they march as a recognized contingent of an opposing army, and criminals if they are taken raiding on their own account. They are identical men with an identical creed and opposite fates, decided wholly by whether some authority will own them as lawful combatants. That makes recognition something to be granted, withheld, manufactured and denied. A crown may "recognize" fanatics or irregulars to clothe them in a soldier's protections. A captor with a ransom to dodge or an example to make may withhold recognition and hang soldiers as bandits. The Collegium, the crowns and the companies all have reasons to push the line one way or the other. The humane floor holds throughout, but the gulf between an honored prisoner and a condemned criminal is a political judgment in a legal robe, and that is exactly where the law of war goes gray.

## The Argentariorum and the Banking System

The Collegium's tie to the commonwealth's banking system runs through one chartered guild, the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]], the moneylenders' guild and one of the thirty-six trade-categories. Its chapterhouses operate the visible banking infrastructure across all member territories, its member moneylenders issue retail notes and arrange traveler's notes, and its great houses (the **House of Calventian**, the **House of Maximian Tertian**, the **House of Verrius**, the **House of Therion** and several others) are the commonwealth's principal credit institutions.

The Argentariorum's chapter reserves belong to the wider [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]]-backed pool, its clearing settles through the Aerarium's accounts, and its master notes are drawn on Aerarium credit. In imperial territory (Vylaria and Heliónis) the tie is overt: the Aerarium runs its own direct offices beside the Argentariorum's chapters. In non-imperial territory (the Aurèldían kingdoms and Byzaría) the Aerarium does not appear, the chapterhouses are the entire visible interface, and everyone maintains the polite fiction that the banking system is a guild operation and not an imperial one. The Collegium's chartering authority over the Argentariorum is what keeps that fiction tenable, because the kingdoms take part in a "guild," not in a "branch of the imperial treasury."

The Collegium is therefore a standing patron of the banking system, not its operator. It charters the Argentariorum, audits its compliance as a chartered guild through the Censores, and could in principle revoke its charter for cause. It has never done so, and doing so would set off an institutional crisis that neither the Collegium nor the throne would willingly invite. The Argentariorum's own Council, under the Argentarius Magnar's chair, conducts the banking operations, manages the chapter reserves, issues credit and coordinates with the Aerarium Imperii's leadership. The Princeps and the Argentarius Magnar coordinate closely on guild-political matters, but they do not share decisions about the day-to-day running of the banking system.

The Collegium keeps no treasury beyond the modest funds it needs to run its administrative offices, support the Censores' investigations and maintain the Curia Hall at Magnápolis. Member-guild dues pay for the institution. The Collegium does not borrow, does not lend and holds no reserves on a scale that would make it a financial actor in its own right.

### Charter and Sanction

The banking system holds together because the Collegium's power to charter is kept separate from the Aerarium's power to clear. Either institution can sanction a moneylender house in trouble. The Censores Collegiorum can recommend suspending the house's guild standing, which in extreme cases leads to revoking the Argentariorum's charter and more commonly to the house's expulsion from membership while the guild's charter stands. The Aerarium Imperii can suspend the house's clearing rights against imperial reserves, which functionally ends its access to the master-note system. Neither institution can use its sanction against the other's interests alone, and the most consequential actions need both to agree. That separation keeps either court politics or guild politics from capturing the banking system by itself, and the Collegium's leaders and the Aerarium's Quaestor Imperii both treat it as essential.

## Relations

- **The imperial throne** is patron and ultimate authority. The relationship is symbiotic: the throne grants the Collegium its charter and confirms the Princeps, and the Collegium administers the empire's trade life on the throne's behalf.
- **The [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|Imperial Scribal Guild]]** is the largest single member guild and one of the weightiest. Its High Hand sits on the Curia ex officio.
- **The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]** is a sibling imperial institution, and the two keep a careful protocol of mutual non-interference. The Ordo regulates magical practice and the Collegium regulates trade. A joint Curia-Ordo panel handles cases that touch both: artisans whose work involves magical materials, alchemy-adjacent practices, the rare smith whose tempering verges on the supernatural. The arrangement works because both institutions consider themselves senior and neither wishes to test the question.
- **The [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]** is formally not recognized. The Curia in Magnápolis holds the doctrinal position that the Haradian institution is illegitimate and goes on addressing it by the Vylari name "Magnum Collegium Haradicum," which the Haradians treat as a calculated insult and several Vylarian officers privately admit has become more counterproductive than useful. Informally the Sôd is a constant counterparty, because Haradian masters, contracts and credentials keep flowing through trade networks that ignore the political break. The Curia's informal position is that the Sôd exists and the arrangement will eventually have to be addressed; the public position is more rigid and changes more slowly.
- **The Aurèldían crowns** are a chronic source of friction. The Collegium's authority crosses their borders, and several crowns periodically test its limits.
- **The non-member guild traditions** are treated as foreign counterparties: the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]], the Nordlands' [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] and other northern bodies, the [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] and the Bethûan trade-houses. Standing trade-recognition agreements exist with some (the Skalds' Circle has reciprocal hospitality recognition, and certain Khelâthi houses have credential equivalence trade by trade). Others, the Merchant Collective above all, have refused every overture.

## Identifying Marks

The Collegium's seal shows a wreathed open book over a crossed measuring-rule and quill, with the imperial cipher above. It is struck into every charter document, disciplinary ruling and inter-guild jurisdictional decision, and it is the visible mark of the institution's authority across member territories.

The Princeps wears the great gold pectoral of office, kept at the Curia between holders. The Censores wear black mantles with the silver censor's-mark, and a Censor in mantle entering a guildhouse brings on an immediate quiet. Curia members wear the deep blue stole of the Curia at formal sessions and their own guilds' marks outside session.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Charter Revocation Hearing.** The Censores have brought charges against a major Aurèldían guild whose charter is at risk, and its home crown is loudly committed to its defense. It is the most politically charged case in a generation, and the Curia's ruling will set the practical limits of the Collegium's authority over the Aurèldían crowns for the next quarter century.
- **The Haradian Master.** A Haradian master who held Collegium credentials at the secession has presented himself at a Vylarian provincial chapter, seeking a new commission. His pre-secession credentials are technically valid, and the Sôd-Naqîrîn's later confirmations of his standing are not. The Magister Provinciarum must decide whether to honor the original credentials, refuse them because a foreign body confirmed him afterward, or refer the matter to the Curia for a precedent.
- **The Captured Censor.** A Censor on inspection in the Aurèldían countryside has been quietly detained by a local baron who objects to the inspection's target, a guild the baron controls. The detention is not public, the Censor is unharmed, and the Collegium's response will tell the baron, and everyone watching, what they can expect to get away with.
- **The Princeps's Succession.** Marcus Velerianus's renewal is in doubt. The imperial court is divided between factions that favor his continuing and factions that want a successor more amenable to recent imperial proposals for tightening the Collegium's discipline of conquered-territory guilds. The contest runs through proxies on the Curia, whispered representations at court and the senior Censors' choice of which cases to bring forward in the months before the renewal vote.
- **The Ordo Brushfire.** A run of artisanal incidents (a smith's blade that may have been improperly tempered, an alchemist's product that may have been improperly registered, a cartographer whose maps may show features no natural surveying could have captured) has set off overlapping Collegium and Ordo investigations whose officers work at cross-purposes. The Princeps and the Archmagister of the Ordo are due to meet, and the outcome will set the Ordo-Collegium boundary for a generation.
- **The Argentariorum Charter Review.** The Censores have begun a routine compliance review of the Collegium Argentariorum's guild charter. Such reviews are usually procedural, but this one has drawn the attention of the **Reformist Faction**, and a sub-committee with Reformist members will conduct it. An adverse finding could in principle justify revoking the charter, though no one believes the revocation would be allowed to happen. The review's political outcomes may be large regardless.
- **The Insulted Crown.** A senior Vylarian Magister Provinciarum, on a routine inspection of the Tarvénian chapter, said publicly that the Aurèldían crowns are "honorary participants" and not full members of the Collegium. The remark was widely reported. The Tarvénian crown demands a formal disavowal and the Princeps is preparing one, while the **Conservative Faction** at the Vylarian court regards the disavowal as a humiliating concession. The Magistar's career hangs on how the Princeps handles it.
- **The Withdrawn Recognition.** On a Censor's adverse finding, an Aurèldían guild's seal-recognition has been temporarily suspended, so its masters can no longer seal work that other member territories will honor. The affected crown has protested vigorously. The suspension is procedurally proper and politically explosive, and the Princeps must decide whether to back the Censor, override the finding or refer the matter to the full Curia for political cover.
