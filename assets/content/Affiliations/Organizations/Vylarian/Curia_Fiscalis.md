_Vylarian: Curia Fiscalis—"the Fiscal Court"_

- **Type:** Imperial state institution; taxation administration
- **Region of operation:** [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]]—the territories currently subject to imperial governance
- **Seat:** The Fiscal Hall, within the **Officia Imperii** in the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]]
- **Founded:** Constituted in the founding-era reforms of the second Augustar, three centuries ago; reorganized into its present form roughly a century later

## Overview

The Curia Fiscalis is the imperial institution that administers Vylarian taxation. It sets the empire-wide rates on land, customs, mining, inheritance, and the various commercial transactions the imperial tax base draws on. It awards the territorial contracts under which the **Publicarii**—the empire's private tax-farmers—actually collect from individual taxpayers. It audits those Publicarii through its own discipline arm. It hears taxpayer appeals when assessments are disputed. It produces the annual revenue projections on which the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]] bases its disbursement planning.

The Curia's scope is geographically narrow but fiscally substantial. Its writ runs only in territories under direct imperial governance—Vylaria proper and Heliónis—because outside those territories, the post-imperial commonwealth's polities set their own tax rates and collect their own revenues. A Tarvénian merchant pays no imperial tax to the Curia Fiscalis; he pays his Tarvénian crown's land tax and his city's gate tolls and the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium]]'s 2% issuance fee on any note he buys, but the Curia has no authority over him. Inside Vylaria and Heliónis, however, the Curia's authority is comprehensive: every land assessment, every customs duty, every imperial sales tax flows from its rate-setting and its contract awards.

The institution is widely understood inside the empire as politically powerful and individually unpopular. Its officeholders are drawn substantially from the Argentean and Aurelian estates—providing the senior nobility a stake in the tax system and a check on its excesses—but the actual collection work, conducted by the Publicarii, is unredeemably hated by the common population. The Reformist Faction at court argues periodically that the Publicarii system should be replaced by salaried imperial collectors; the Conservative Faction blocks the reform; the system continues largely unchanged, generation after generation.

## The Three-Tier Tax Structure (Imperial Scope Only)

Inside Vylaria and Heliónis, three tiers of taxation apply to every taxpayer simultaneously. The Curia sets and collects only the first tier; the other two are administered by provincial and municipal authorities.

| Tier           | Set By                                                         | Collected By                                                                | Flows To                    | Examples                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Imperial**   | Curia Fiscalis at Magnápolis, with Augustar's seal             | Publicarii (contracted tax-farmers)                                         | Aerarium Imperii            | Land tax (uniform rate on assessed value); customs and frontier duties; mining royalties; inheritance tax above threshold; imperial sales tax on commission sales and other categories |
| **Provincial** | Provincial governor in Vylaria; city-state council in Heliónis | Provincial officials, mostly salaried; some Publicarii on smaller contracts | Provincial / civic treasury | Road tolls; provincial market overrides; special provincial assessments                                                                                                                |
| **Local**      | Municipal council, town magistrate, or village headman         | Municipal officials directly                                                | Municipal treasury          | Gate tolls; market stall dues; local property tax; public works levies; festival levies                                                                                                |

A Vylarian merchant in a provincial capital pays imperial land tax to the Publicar who holds the regional contract; pays his province's market override to the provincial collection office; pays his city's gate toll every time he brings goods through the walls. Three streams, three apparatuses, three ultimate recipients. The Curia Fiscalis has no claim on what the other two collect.

Outside the empire, only the lower two tiers apply. An Aurèldían merchant pays his crown's tax (the kingdom-tier equivalent of the provincial tier) and his city's tax (local tier), with no imperial layer above. He still pays the Aerarium's 2% issuance fee on traveler's notes—but that fee is collected through the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]] banking system, not by the Curia, and is not classified as taxation in any post-imperial polity's official accounts.

## The Publicarii System

The Curia does not collect taxes directly. The actual work of going city by city and town by town extracting tribute is contracted out to **Publicarii** (sing. **Publicar**)—private tax-farmers, almost without exception drawn from the wealthier commoner and Adlect-noble classes, who bid on collection contracts for specific territories and revenue categories.

The contract mechanism works as follows. The Curia's **Procurares Provinciarum** (one per province) prepare annual estimates of expected revenue from each territorial category—the land tax on the upper Vylarian river valley, the customs duties at the port of Mercavia, the mining royalties of the eastern silver lodes. These estimates are published. Eligible Publicarii bid against the estimates, offering to pay the Curia a specified sum in advance for the right to collect during the contract period. The Publicar with the winning bid pays his bid amount into the Aerarium Imperii through the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|The Collegium Argentariorum]]'s clearing system; he then collects taxes in his territory for the contract period and keeps every Argo he can collect above his bid.

The system's central feature—and its central abuse—is that **the Publicar's profit is exactly the margin between his bid and his actual collection**. A Publicar who collected only what was theoretically due would earn no profit; he would have paid his bid and recovered exactly his bid in collection, with no surplus. To earn the substantial returns that make Publicarius contracts worth bidding for, a Publicar must extract more than the theoretical assessment—through aggressive assessment of marginal properties, through harsh enforcement of small omissions, through penalty fees and seizure threats, through whatever means his contract permits and the local population will not actually riot over. The Publicarii are not lawless; the Curia's standing rules limit what they can do; the **Censores Fiscales** investigate the worst excesses. But within the rules there is substantial room for compounding pressure, and Publicarii compound pressure for a living.

The Publicar class has become wealthy, despised, and politically influential. The richest Publicarii operate substantial households, hire small private guard forces (to protect collection convoys and their own homes from popular hostility), and have, over the last century, increasingly acquired Adlect noble standing through purchases of imperial favor. Their grandsons and great-grandsons sit as Aerean and even Argentean members of the senior estates; the social stigma washes off slowly across generations but never fully disappears. A Senior noble whose ancestors three generations back were Publicarii is still occasionally referred to as "Publicar-spawn" in private noble correspondence.

## Structure

The Curia is organized in four working bodies.

### The Council of the Fiscal Court

The standing governing body. Sixteen seats, drawn from the senior Argentean and Aurelian estates by imperial appointment for ten-year terms, renewable once. The Council sets imperial tax rates (subject to Augustar approval), approves Publicarius contract terms, hears appeals against Procurar decisions, and decides matters of standing fiscal policy.

The Council meets monthly at the Fiscal Hall in Magnápolis and holds formal sessions quarterly with the Dux Bellorum, the Quaestor Imperii, and (twice a year) the Augustar himself.

### The Praetar Fiscalis

The Council's chair and the Curia's chief officer. Appointed by the Augustar for a ten-year term, renewable. The current Praetar Fiscalis is **Quintus Aurelii Marsenian**, an Aurelian in his sixties whose tenure has been notable for cautious moderation—refusing to push contentious reforms but also refusing to permit the worst Publicarius abuses to escalate beyond a manageable level. He is widely regarded by both Reformist and Conservative factions as the least objectionable Praetar Fiscalis they have any prospect of getting.

### The Procurares Provinciarum

The provincial officers; eight in total—one for each of the four Vylarian provinces, plus four for the principal Heliónite city-states. They administer the Curia's operations in their territories, prepare the annual revenue estimates, manage the Publicarius contract awards, and supervise local Censores Fiscales. They report to the Council monthly and to the Praetar Fiscalis directly on urgent matters.

The Procurares' role is one of the more politically consequential mid-level postings in the empire. A Procurar can quietly favor or disfavor particular Publicarius bidders, can adjust estimates to make contracts more or less profitable, and can call for Censor investigations selectively. Their personal wealth—accumulated through the Procurar's traditional cut on contract awards, parallel to the Legar's cut on commission confirmations in the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|imperial military]]—is substantial.

### The Censores Fiscales

The discipline arm; twenty officers in total, appointed by the Council and reporting both to the Council and to the Praetar Fiscalis directly. The Censores Fiscales investigate Publicarius abuses, audit Publicarius collection books, prosecute serious offenses before the Council, and (most consequentially) recommend Publicarii for permanent disqualification from future contracts.

The Censores Fiscales are distinct from the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Censores Collegiorum]] (who audit guild compliance) and from the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Censores Aerarii]] (who audit the imperial treasury). The three Censor bodies coordinate informally; their formal jurisdictions are kept separate to prevent any one body from accumulating concentrated investigative authority.

The senior officer is the **Censor Maximus Fisci**—currently **Sextia Marcania**, a famously rigorous Aerean officer whose elevation to the position was opposed by the Publicarius class and pushed through by the Reformist Faction at court. Sextia's tenure has been the most active in two generations; her disciplinary case-rate is roughly double that of her predecessors.

## Imperial Tax Categories

The Curia administers the following principal taxes within imperial territory:

- **Land Tax** (_tributum agrorum_)—annual assessment on agricultural land at uniform rate on assessed value, modified by quality and use; the empire's single largest revenue source.
- **Customs Duties** (_portoria_)—on goods crossing imperial frontiers and (at lower rates) provincial boundaries within the empire; the second-largest revenue source, particularly significant on the lucrative trade routes through Heliónis.
- **Mining Royalties** (_tributum metallorum_)—on extraction from imperial-claim mines; substantial in the silver-rich eastern Vylarian provinces.
- **Inheritance Tax** (_tributum hereditatium_)—on estate transfers above threshold; falls primarily on the senior estates and is one of the Reformist Faction's preferred levers for slowly reducing inherited wealth.
- **Imperial Sales Tax** (_centesima rerum venalium_)—on certain commercial transactions, most notably the military commission sales (40–55% of which flows to the imperial treasury per the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|standing schedule]]), real estate transfers above threshold, and major commercial contracts.
- **Manumission Tax**—on the granting of citizenship to non-citizens; small in aggregate but politically significant.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]**—direct counterpart. The Curia collects; the Aerarium holds. Coordination is constant; tensions arise when the Curia's revenue projections fall short of the Aerarium's disbursement requirements.
- **The Augustar and the Dux Bellorum**—patrons and ultimate authorities. The Praetar Fiscalis is appointed and dismissed at imperial discretion; Council seats turn over at the Augustar's pleasure within procedural limits.
- **The provincial governors and the Heliónite city-state councils**—operational counterparts in the lower tax tiers. The relationship is correct and bureaucratic, with occasional disputes over jurisdictional boundaries (a tax category may be claimed by either tier).
- **The Reformist Faction at court**—natural patron of the Censores Fiscales' more aggressive prosecutions; pushes periodically for Publicarius reform.
- **The Conservative Faction**—natural protector of the Publicarii system; comprises the Publicarius class itself, the families whose wealth derives from Publicarius contracts, and the broader Aurelian and Argentean estates whose political influence depends on the current arrangement.
- **The Publicarii themselves**—the Curia's contractors, its political problem, and its most visible public face. The relationship is structurally adversarial (the Censores Fiscales discipline them) and operationally interdependent (the Procurares could not collect without them).

## Identifying Marks

Curia officers wear the deep imperial-purple stole over working dress at formal sessions, with the Curia's eagle-and-balance sigil at the breast. Procurares Provinciarum wear an additional brass medallion of their province. Censores Fiscales wear black mantles with the gold sword-and-balance mark (the sword for enforcement, the balance for assessment)—distinguishable from the Censores Collegiorum's silver-marked mantle. The Praetar Fiscalis wears the great gold collar of office at imperial ceremonies.

A Publicarius's collection authority is attested by a **brass collection-tessera** stamped with his contract number, the territorial designation, the contract period dates, and the Procurar's countermark. Tesserae are presented to taxpayers as proof of collection authority and are essential for any forcible seizure under the Publicarius's contract powers. Counterfeiting a tessera is a capital crime under both Curia and imperial law.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Reform Petition.** The Reformist Faction has, after a year of preparation, formally introduced a Council proposal to replace the Publicarius system with salaried imperial collectors in two specific Vylarian provinces—a pilot program that, if it succeeds, would justify wider implementation. The Conservative Faction is mobilizing the Publicarius class and the major Adlect houses against. Praetar Fiscalis Quintus Aurelii Marsenian is signaling cautious openness to the pilot, which is itself a substantial shift. The vote will be close.
- **The Censor's Cases.** Censor Maximus Sextia Marcania has assembled simultaneous discipline cases against eight major Publicarii operating in different Vylarian provinces—the largest coordinated prosecution in two generations. The Publicarii's families are coordinating a counter-attack through court connections; the Praetar Fiscalis is being pressed to slow the prosecution; Sextia is refusing to slow. Resolution will either entrench her position or end her career.
- **The Foreign-Patron Publicar.** A Publicar holding a substantial Heliónite customs-contract has, on Censor investigation, been found to be quietly receiving payments from a Sôd-Naqîrîn-affiliated trading interest—payments calibrated to ensure that certain Haradian goods pass through customs at favorable assessment rates. The arrangement is technically not treasonable (the Publicar is collecting what the contract requires) but is unambiguously corrupt. Prosecution would expose a diplomatic embarrassment; non-prosecution would, if it leaks, be a Reformist Faction propaganda windfall.
- **The Assessment Riot.** A particularly aggressive Publicar in the upper Vylarian river valley has produced (his methods) a peasant uprising large enough to threaten provincial order. The provincial governor has called for legions; the Praetar Fiscalis has called for the Publicar's removal; the Augustar's standing position is that order must be restored before reforms are discussed; the Reformist Faction is making the obvious political case in every drawing room in Magnápolis. The Publicar's family is influential; his contract is large; the resolution will be politically substantial.
- **The Procurar's Margin.** A Procurar Provinciarum has, on internal Curia investigation, been found to have been quietly enlarging his contract-cut over several years through manipulation of revenue estimates. The pattern is suggestive but not conclusive; the Procurar's defense is that estimates necessarily reflect judgment and his judgments have erred on the favorable side without bad intent. The Praetar Fiscalis must rule on whether to proceed with formal charges, accept the Procurar's resignation quietly, or close the investigation.
- **The Vanishing Tessera.** A brass collection-tessera has been stolen from a Publicar's office in a Helionite port. Counterfeit tesserae using the stolen original's design have begun circulating; collections under their authority have begun. The Procurar of the affected territory has alerted the Censores Fiscales, but identifying the counterfeiter without finding the original is difficult. The Publicar whose tessera was stolen is being investigated; he may be the counterfeiter himself, or he may be the victim of someone who wished him implicated. Resolution within the contract period is essential.
