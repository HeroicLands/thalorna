A Curia assessor on circuit has a speech he gives the first time he rides into a village behind the tax-farmer's mule. Say it goes like this: "The Publicar will be pleasant tonight and exact tomorrow. He paid the Curia for the right to collect here before he saw a single field, and every Argo he squeezes out above his bid is his own. I am here to see that he squeezes by the rules. Keep your tally-sticks, keep your receipts, and when he shows his brass tessera, look at the contract number before you look at his face."

## Overview

The Curia Fiscalis, the Fiscal Court, administers Vylarian taxation. It sets the empire-wide rates on land, customs, mining, inheritance and the commercial transactions the imperial tax base draws on. It awards the territorial contracts under which the **Publicarii**, the empire's private tax-farmers, collect from individual taxpayers. It audits those Publicarii through its own discipline arm, hears taxpayer appeals when an assessment is disputed, and produces the annual revenue projections on which the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] plans its disbursements. Its seat is the **Fiscal Hall**, within the **Officia Imperii** in the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]]. The second Augustar (the Vylarian emperor) constituted it in his founding-era reforms three centuries ago, and it took its present form roughly a century later.

Its scope is geographically narrow and fiscally large. Its writ runs only in territories under direct imperial governance, [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], because outside them the post-imperial commonwealth's polities set their own rates and collect their own revenues. A Tarvénian merchant pays the Curia nothing. He pays his Tarvénian crown's land tax, his city's gate tolls and the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium]]'s 2% issuance fee on any note he buys, and the Curia has no authority over him. Inside Vylaria and Heliónis its authority is comprehensive: every land assessment, customs duty and imperial sales tax flows from its rate-setting and its contract awards.

Inside the empire the Curia is politically powerful and individually unpopular. The senior nobility staff it, drawing its officeholders largely from the Argentean and Aurelian estates, which gives them a stake in the tax system and a check on its excesses. The collection itself is the Publicarii's work, and the common population hates them without reserve. The [[affiliation-lethunu2|Lethunu]] at court periodically argues for replacing the Publicarii with salaried imperial collectors, the **Conservative Faction** blocks the reform, and the system goes on unchanged generation after generation.

## The Three-Tier Tax Structure (Imperial Scope Only)

Inside Vylaria and Heliónis, three tiers of taxation apply to every taxpayer at once. The Curia sets and collects only the first tier. Provincial and municipal authorities administer the other two.

| Tier           | Set By                                                         | Collected By                                                                | Flows To                    | Examples                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Imperial**   | Curia Fiscalis at Magnápolis, with Augustar's seal             | Publicarii (contracted tax-farmers)                                         | Aerarium Imperii            | Land tax (uniform rate on assessed value); customs and frontier duties; mining royalties; inheritance tax above threshold; imperial sales tax on commission sales and other categories |
| **Provincial** | Provincial governor in Vylaria; city-state council in Heliónis | Provincial officials, mostly salaried; some Publicarii on smaller contracts | Provincial / civic treasury | Road tolls; provincial market overrides; special provincial assessments                                                                                                                |
| **Local**      | Municipal council, town magistrate, or village headman         | Municipal officials directly                                                | Municipal treasury          | Gate tolls; market stall dues; local property tax; public works levies; festival levies                                                                                                |

A Vylarian merchant in a provincial capital pays imperial land tax to the Publicar who holds the regional contract, his province's market override to the provincial collection office, and his city's gate toll every time he brings goods through the walls. That is three streams, three apparatuses and three ultimate recipients, and the Curia Fiscalis has no claim on what the other two collect.

Outside the empire only the lower two tiers apply. An Aurèldían merchant pays his crown's tax (the kingdom-tier equivalent of the provincial tier) and his city's tax, with no imperial layer above. He still pays the Aerarium's 2% issuance fee on traveler's notes, but the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]]'s banking system collects it and not the Curia, and no post-imperial polity classifies it as taxation in its official accounts.

## The Publicarii System

The Curia does not collect taxes directly. The work of going city by city and town by town is contracted out to **Publicarii** (sing. **Publicar**), private tax-farmers who are almost without exception wealthier commoners and Adlect nobles. They bid on collection contracts for specific territories and revenue categories.

The contract works like this. The Curia's **Procurares Provinciarum** prepare annual estimates of expected revenue from each territorial category: the land tax on the upper Vylarian river valley, the customs duties at the port of [[place-mercavia|Mercavia]], the mining royalties of the eastern silver lodes. The estimates are published, and eligible Publicarii bid against them, offering to pay the Curia a stated sum in advance for the right to collect during the contract period. The winning Publicar pays his bid into the Aerarium Imperii through the Collegium Argentariorum's clearing system. Then he collects in his territory for the contract period and keeps every Argo he can collect above his bid.

The system's central feature, and its central abuse, is that **a Publicar's profit is exactly the margin between his bid and his actual collection**. A Publicar who collected only what was theoretically due would earn nothing: he would have paid his bid and recovered exactly his bid. To make the returns that justify bidding, he must extract more than the assessment. He does it by assessing marginal properties aggressively, by enforcing small omissions harshly, with penalty fees and threats of seizure, and by whatever else his contract allows and the local population will not riot over. The Publicarii are not lawless. The Curia's standing rules limit them and the **Censores Fiscales** investigate the worst excesses. Within the rules, however, there is plenty of room to keep adding pressure, and the Publicarii do so for a living.

The Publicar class has become wealthy, despised and politically influential. The richest Publicarii keep substantial households, hire small private guard forces to protect collection convoys and their own homes from popular hostility, and over the last century have increasingly bought imperial favor and with it Adlect noble standing. Their grandsons and great-grandsons sit as Aerean and even Argentean members of the senior estates. The stigma washes out slowly across generations and never entirely. A senior noble whose ancestors three generations back were Publicarii is still sometimes called "Publicar-spawn" in private noble correspondence.

## Structure

The Curia works through four bodies.

### The Council of the Fiscal Court

The Council is the standing governing body, with sixteen seats drawn from the senior Argentean and Aurelian estates by imperial appointment for ten-year terms, renewable once. It sets imperial tax rates (subject to the Augustar's approval), approves Publicarius contract terms, hears appeals against Procurar decisions and decides standing fiscal policy. It meets monthly at the Fiscal Hall and holds formal sessions quarterly with the Dux Bellorum, the Quaestor Imperii and, twice a year, the Augustar himself.

### The Praetar Fiscalis

The Praetar Fiscalis chairs the Council and is the Curia's chief officer. The Augustar appoints him for a ten-year term, renewable, and dismisses him at imperial discretion. The current Praetar Fiscalis is **Quintus Aurelii Marsenian**, an Aurelian in his sixties whose tenure has been marked by cautious moderation: he declines to push contentious reforms and declines equally to let the worst Publicarius abuses escalate past a manageable level. Both court factions, Reformist and Conservative, see him as the least objectionable Praetar Fiscalis they have any prospect of getting.

### The Procurares Provinciarum

Eight provincial officers run the Curia's operations in their territories: one for each of the four Vylarian provinces and four for the principal Heliónite city-states. They prepare the annual revenue estimates, manage the Publicarius contract awards and supervise the local Censores Fiscales. They report to the Council monthly and to the Praetar Fiscalis directly on urgent matters.

A Procurar holds one of the more politically consequential mid-level postings in the empire. He can quietly favor or disfavor particular bidders, adjust estimates to make contracts more or less profitable, and call for Censor investigations selectively. His personal wealth, built on the Procurar's traditional cut of contract awards, is substantial. It parallels the Legar's cut of commission confirmations in the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|imperial military]].

### The Censores Fiscales

Twenty Censores Fiscales form the discipline arm. The Council appoints them, and they report both to the Council and to the Praetar Fiscalis directly. They investigate Publicarius abuses, audit collection books, prosecute serious offenses before the Council and, most consequentially, recommend Publicarii for permanent disqualification from future contracts.

They are distinct from the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Censores Collegiorum]], who audit guild compliance, and from the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Censores Aerarii]], who audit the imperial treasury. The three Censor bodies coordinate informally, and their formal jurisdictions are kept separate so that no one body can gather concentrated investigative authority.

The senior officer is the Censor Maximus Fisci, currently **Sextia Marcania**, a famously rigorous Aerean officer whose elevation the Publicarius class opposed and the **Reformist Faction** pushed through. Her tenure has been the most active in two generations, and her disciplinary case-rate is roughly double her predecessors'.

## Imperial Tax Categories

The Curia administers these principal taxes within imperial territory:

- **Land Tax** (_tributum agrorum_)—an annual assessment on agricultural land at a uniform rate on assessed value, modified by quality and use. It is the empire's single largest revenue source.
- **Customs Duties** (_portoria_)—levied on goods crossing imperial frontiers and, at lower rates, provincial boundaries within the empire. They are the second-largest revenue source, and particularly significant on the lucrative trade routes through Heliónis.
- **Mining Royalties** (_tributum metallorum_)—on extraction from imperial-claim mines, and substantial in the silver-rich eastern Vylarian provinces.
- **Inheritance Tax** (_tributum hereditatium_)—on estate transfers above a threshold. It falls mainly on the senior estates and is one of the Reformist Faction's preferred levers for slowly reducing inherited wealth.
- **Imperial Sales Tax** (_centesima rerum venalium_)—on certain commercial transactions, most notably military commission sales (40–55% of which flows to the imperial treasury under the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|standing schedule]]), real estate transfers above a threshold and major commercial contracts.
- **Manumission Tax**—on the granting of citizenship to a resident. It is small in aggregate and politically significant.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]]** is the direct counterpart: the Curia collects and the Aerarium holds. Coordination is constant, and tension arises when the Curia's revenue projections fall short of the Aerarium's disbursement requirements.
- **The Augustar and the Dux Bellorum** are patrons and ultimate authorities. The Praetar Fiscalis serves at imperial discretion, and Council seats turn over at the Augustar's pleasure within procedural limits.
- **The provincial governors and the Heliónite city-state councils** are operational counterparts in the lower tax tiers. The relationship is correct and bureaucratic, with occasional disputes over jurisdictional boundaries, since either tier may claim a tax category.
- **The Lethunu** at court are the natural patrons of the Censores Fiscales' more aggressive prosecutions, and they push periodically for Publicarius reform.
- **[[affiliation-qetharpalu2|The Qethar'palu]]** are the natural protectors of the Publicarii system. They comprise the Publicarius class itself, the families whose wealth comes from Publicarius contracts and the broader Aurelian and Argentean estates whose political influence depends on the current arrangement.
- **The Publicarii themselves** are the Curia's contractors, its political problem and its most visible public face. The relationship is structurally adversarial, since the Censores Fiscales discipline them, and operationally interdependent, since the Procurares could not collect without them.

## Identifying Marks

Curia officers wear the deep imperial-purple stole over working dress at formal sessions, with the Curia's eagle-and-balance sigil at the breast. Procurares Provinciarum add a brass medallion of their province. Censores Fiscales wear black mantles with the gold sword-and-balance mark (the sword for enforcement, the balance for assessment), distinguishable from the silver-marked mantle of the Censores Collegiorum. The Praetar Fiscalis wears the great gold collar of office at imperial ceremonies.

A Publicarius's collection authority is attested by a **brass collection-tessera**, stamped with his contract number, the territorial designation, the dates of the contract period and the Procurar's countermark. He shows it to taxpayers as proof of his authority, and it is essential for any forcible seizure under his contract powers. Counterfeiting a tessera is a capital crime under both Curia and imperial law.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Reform Petition.** After a year of preparation, the Lethunu has formally put a Council proposal to replace the Publicarius system with salaried imperial collectors in two Vylarian provinces, a pilot that would justify wider rollout if it succeeds. The Qethar'palu is mobilizing the Publicarius class and the major Adlect houses against it. Praetar Fiscalis Quintus Aurelii Marsenian is signaling cautious openness to the pilot, itself a substantial shift. The vote will be close.
- **The Censor's Cases.** Censor Maximus Sextia Marcania has assembled simultaneous discipline cases against eight major Publicarii in different Vylarian provinces, the largest coordinated prosecution in two generations. Their families are mounting a counter-attack through court connections, and the Praetar Fiscalis is being pressed to slow the prosecution. Sextia refuses to slow. The outcome will entrench her position or end her career.
- **The Foreign-Patron Publicar.** On Censor investigation, a Publicar holding a large Heliónite customs contract has been found quietly receiving payments from a trading interest tied to the Sôd-Naqîrîn, payments calibrated to make certain Haradian goods pass customs at favorable assessments. The arrangement is technically not treasonable, since the Publicar collects what the contract requires, but it is plainly corrupt. Prosecuting would expose a diplomatic embarrassment, and not prosecuting would, if it leaks, hand the Reformist Faction a propaganda windfall.
- **The Assessment Riot.** An especially aggressive Publicar in the upper Vylarian river valley has provoked, by his methods, a peasant uprising large enough to threaten provincial order. The provincial governor has called for legions, the Praetar Fiscalis has called for the Publicar's removal, and the Augustar's standing position is that order must be restored before reforms are discussed. The Reformist Faction is making the obvious political case in every drawing room in Magnápolis. The Publicar's family is influential and his contract is large.
- **The Procurar's Margin.** Internal investigation has found that a Procurar Provinciarum has been quietly enlarging his contract-cut over several years by manipulating revenue estimates. The pattern is suggestive and not conclusive, and the Procurar's defense is that estimates reflect judgment and his have erred on the favorable side without bad intent. The Praetar Fiscalis must rule on whether to bring formal charges, accept the Procurar's resignation quietly or close the investigation.
- **The Vanishing Tessera.** Someone has stolen a brass collection-tessera from a Publicar's office in a Heliónite port. Counterfeit tesserae copying the stolen original's design have begun to circulate, and collections have begun under their authority. The Procurar of the affected territory has alerted the Censores Fiscales, but identifying the counterfeiter without finding the original is hard. The Publicar whose tessera was stolen is under investigation: he may be the counterfeiter himself, or the victim of someone who wanted him implicated. Resolution within the contract period is essential.
