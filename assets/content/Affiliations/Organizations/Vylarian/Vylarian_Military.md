Say a discharged veteran, drinking in a Watch-house tavern in [[place-orzara|Orzara]], explains the army to a nephew who leaves for the recruiting station in the morning. "There are two armies in every legion, lad, and they share a mess hall. One bought its rank and one earned it. You will serve in the one that earned it, and your first lesson is whom to listen to: the officer who outranks you, or the centurion who knows."

That is the Vylarian military in a sentence, and the rest of this note fills in the arrangement.

## At a Glance

The legions are the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]’s standing army. They operate in [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper, the territory under direct imperial governance, and not in [[place-helionis|Helionis]], whose cities keep their own forces. They do _not_ freely operate in the [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia Region]] or the [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]], which have their own armies, nor in [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] since the secession, nor anywhere else outside imperial territory except by invitation or hire.

The reigning **Orzas** (the Vylarian emperor) is the supreme commander. Operational command belongs to the **Dukran** at Orzara, who presides over the war council of the **Sodari Orzas**.

## Overview

The Vylarian military is a legion-based army built on the founding principle that the empire is defined by its borders and the borders are defined by the legions that hold them. Every Vylarian schoolchild learns the four-word formula that opens the imperial military manual: _Zel orkaima thalentor uthrime_, "Beneath the eagle stand the borders." The legions made the empire and, until very recently, kept the territories its founders conquered.

What sets the army apart, and what shapes nearly every story it appears in, is its **dual-track structure**. Two parallel rank hierarchies meet at a single, carefully controlled point. The **noble track** holds every formal officer rank from Kadel to Kamdor. A family buys each commission, with the price adjusted by its political standing, and military competence is welcome but not required. The **commoner track** holds every NCO and enlisted rank from Yunar to Hundar Maldor, and demonstrated capability alone fills it: no coin changes hands at any promotion. The two tracks share quarters, campaigns and casualty lists, and none of their qualifying criteria.

The arrangement is the institution's open secret. The army's real operational capability rests in the meritocratic track, while the noble track supplies political legitimacy, court patronage and the families' continued investment in the imperial military. Any officer who has served long enough understands that the purchased-commission system buys aristocratic consent for a parallel meritocratic structure that the aristocracy could dismantle and does not, because dismantling it would mean losing every war the empire fought next. The Vylarians have lost wars under the system, most recently and disastrously the war that ended in the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Taruzelian secession]] twelve years ago, but they have won most of them.

The army's reach has shrunk a great deal over the empire's history. Two centuries ago the legions garrisoned and policed territory from the [[place-elavendre|Élavendren]] border to the Taruzelian islands, and today they operate only in Vylaria proper. The Aureldian kingdoms keep their own armies (knightly orders, royal retinues, town militias), the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] keeps its own city militias and a small standing force, and Taruzel has built its own navy and land forces since the secession. Aureldian and Bizarian nobles can still buy commissions in the Vylarian legions, and a few provincial nobles of _ankren_ (Silver) standing do so to gain imperial credentials. The reverse is rare, because Vylarian nobles seldom serve in non-imperial armies. The **[Reformist Faction]{.name data-type="affiliation"}** at court counts the legions' reduced geographic scope among its standing concerns, and the **[Conservative Faction]{.name data-type="affiliation"}** regards the empire's reduced military reach as the price of stability with the post-imperial commonwealth.

## Unit Hierarchy

The organization runs from theater-level formations down to four-man fire teams, and the arithmetic is consistent: four men to a _Kelan_, two _Kelani_ to a _Masal_, ten _Masali_ to a _Hundal_, six _Hundali_ to a _Parthal_, ten _Parthali_ to a _Vandan_. Everything above the _Vandan_ is an operational formation and not an administrative one.

| Unit        | Gloss                                             | Strength        | Subunits                    | Commander |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------- | --------- |
| **Kamdol**  | "the Great Host"—a theater force                  | 30,000–100,000+ | 2–6 _Vexilia_               | Kamdor    |
| **Bandel**  | "the Banner"—a field army for a specific campaign | 10,000–30,000   | 2–6 _Vandani_               | Bandren   |
| **Vandan**  | Legion—the basic strategic formation              | ~5,000          | 10 _Parthali_ + auxiliaries | Vandraks  |
| **Parthal** | Cohort-equivalent—the basic tactical formation    | ~480            | 6 _Hundali_                 | Parthan   |
| **Hundal**  | Century-equivalent—the basic administrative unit  | ~80             | 10 _Masali_                 | Hundar    |
| **Masal**   | Squad / mess group                                | 8               | 2 _Kelani_                  | Masar     |
| **Kelan**   | Fire team—the smallest tactical maneuver element  | 4               | —                           | Kelor     |

The **Kelan** as the basic tactical element is a Vylarian doctrinal innovation that distinguishes the imperial army from earlier Aureldian and pre-conquest Taruzelian forces. A four-man team can carry out fire-and-movement on its own, and two teams in a _Masal_ support each other in alternating bounds. Field manuals credit the innovation to a long-dead military reformer, who may be real or invented, since the manuals disagree.

A young aristocrat buys a Kadel commission at fifteen, serves under his Vandraks's mentorship for two years, buys a Lusel commission at seventeen or eighteen and joins a legion as a junior officer. A common-born youth presents himself at a recruiting station, takes the legion's oath as a Yunar, completes a year of training to become a Vandor and begins the slow climb through demonstrated capability. He takes the oath no earlier than his fifteenth birthday and serves a full term of twenty years, and the commoner who completes it is discharged a **citizen of the Empire**, with the dole and the standing that status carries (see [[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vylarian Citizenship]]). The two careers run side by side through the same legion's barracks, mess halls and engagements. They meet formally only at the **Vallar** position.

## The Court Tier: Courtiers, Not Soldiers

The highest formal commands in the Vylarian military are openly political. Family standing, loyalty to the Orzas's faction and political reliability decide who holds them. Military experience is welcome but not required, and an experienced soldier as Dukran is often mistrusted at court precisely because he could use his actual command to threaten the throne.

| Rank            | Role                                                            | Reality                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Orzas**       | Emperor; nominal supreme commander                              | Almost never personally takes the field; military decisions filtered through the war council                                                                                                                                             |
| **Dukran**      | Chief War-Minister; presides over the imperial war council      | A court appointee; may or may not have ever held an operational command. The position rotates among the most politically powerful Holvethoi. Invariably an ethlen.                                                                       |
| **Sodar Orzas** | "Imperial Companion"—member of the war council; 4–8 at any time | Hold nominal command titles ("Sodar Orzas, Vandraks of the **Karsan Hilvan**"), draw the salary, attend imperial ceremonies, and may never visit their legions. Real operational command is delegated to the Lusar Maldor or the Vallar. |

When a real campaign is mounted, command in the field goes to a **Kamdor** (theater commander), who is also a noble but is usually chosen for his capacity to fight a war. The Kamdor reports formally to the Dukran and in practice acts with substantial autonomy. The two ranks coexist awkwardly: the Holvethoi hold nominal seniority and the Magnars hold the field. Sensible Holvethoi stay out of operational decisions, and insecure ones interfere, sometimes disastrously.

## The Imperial Guard and the City Watch

Two standing bodies sit apart from the field legions and the war command. They serve the throne and the capital and not the borders.

[[affiliation-sorvan|The Sorvan]] is the **imperial guard**: the Orzas's personal bodyguard and the keepers of order and justice within [[place-arvon|Arvon]] (the Inner City of Orzara). It garrisons the [[place-thaurzurgalis|Thaurzurgalis]] on the palace hill, recruits and promotes apart from the line legions, and answers to the Orzas alone. Elite, lavishly equipped and intensely political, it is the armed muscle of the court. The body best placed to guard an Orzas is also the body best placed to unmake one, and every reigning Orzas courts its loyalty with care. Its writ stops at the Inner Wall, and the rest of the capital is not the guard's to police.

The **City Watch** is a standing branch of the legions detailed permanently to the policing of Orzara. It is legionary in recruitment, discipline and equipment, but it is a city institution and not a field force. It takes no part in foreign campaigns. It patrols the wards, breaks riots, hunts the city's criminals and enforces the civic magistrates' rulings from its fortified watch-houses, one on or near each of the Twelve Circles. A general does not direct it day to day. The city's civic council, the [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Verdan Orzara]], does, through the _veramus_ (curator of the city) who holds the portfolio of order, an arrangement that deliberately keeps the capital's standing muscle answerable to the Orzas's civic appointees and not to any one commander. Lesser watches on the same model keep the peace in the empire's other great cities.

### The City Watch in Detail

The Watch is organized on the legionary pattern but bent to the city's shape. A **watch-cohort**, a _Parthal_ of four to five hundred men, is quartered in each ward's watch-house and broken into the _Hundali_ and patrols that walk its lanes, so that across the Twelve Circles the whole force comes to roughly a legion's strength. It is officered through the army's commoner track, with career centurions and veterans, many of them legionaries pensioned off the frontier into city service, which they count a soft billet or a comedown according to temperament. A **Nokaran**, the _zoralus_ (prefect of the watch), commands the whole and answers to the [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Veramus of Order]] on the Verdan Orzara.

By day the watchmen go lightly armed for the press of the streets: cudgel, short blade and no more than a leather jack, because a cohort in full harness cannot chase a cutpurse up a stepped lane. The same men can muster in proper legionary kit within the hour when a Circle erupts, and a riot is the one occasion when the Watch fights as soldiers and not as constables. Their charge is broad. They patrol and arrest, they stand the night-watch against fire in a timber-dense city (every watch-house keeps hand-engines, hooks and a cistern), they guard the granaries and gates, they supply the muscle behind the [[affiliation-aediles|Pelthralon's]] writs, and they are the first answer to disorder before the Inner City's guard need stir. By long tradition they are also thoroughly bribable. The Sorvan of the palace look down on them as glorified street-wardens, and the watchmen return the contempt with the resentment of men who do the actual, unglamorous work of keeping a million people from one another's throats.

## The Noble Junior Tier: Where the Lesson Is Learned

The system's tactical effectiveness depends on one fact: young noble officers below Lusar Maldor are _expected to learn from_ the senior centurions of their assigned units. The expectation is universal and unwritten, and institutional culture enforces it, not formal rule.

| Rank             | Age   | Position                                    | What He Actually Does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ---------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Kadel**        | 15–17 | Personal aide to a Vandraks or Lusar Maldor | Carries messages, attends officer councils as a silent observer, learns the legion's organization by watching.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Lusel**        | 17–22 | One of five junior tribunes per legion      | First real commissioned posting. Holds nominal command authority over an assigned area of the legion (a sectara, a logistics function, a training section). In practice, takes direction from the senior centurion of his assigned area on virtually all operational matters. |
| **Lusar**        | 22–28 | A tribune, second commission                | Has survived the Lusel years and learned the lessons. Now contributes; may command in the field with confidence.                                                                                                                                                              |
| **Lusar Maldor** | 28–35 | Senior tribune, 2IC of a legion             | Operational deputy to the Vandraks. Often the legion's effective day-to-day commander when the Vandraks is a courtier appointment.                                                                                                                                            |
| **Vandraks**     | 30+   | Legion commander                            | Frequently an ankren or ethlen appointment; political weight at least as important as tactical capacity. The legion's actual operational commander, but reliant on the Lusar Maldor and the Vallar for execution.                                                             |

The critical relationship is between the **Lusel**, an 18-year-old aristocrat who technically outranks every commoner in the legion, and the **Hundar Maldor**, a 50-year-old senior centurion who has held the legion's combat doctrine in his head for thirty years. The Lusel's commission says he is the Hundar Maldor's superior. Every unwritten rule of the Vylarian army says he should treat the Hundar Maldor's counsel as binding unless he has an extraordinarily good reason not to. An Lusel who issues operational orders against his Hundar Maldor's advice and gets the unit cut up gets no defense from his family's connections. He is quietly cashiered and sent home, and his family is informed that further military careers in their line should perhaps be reconsidered.

The same dynamic runs one rank lower. A **Kadel** is technically senior to every Hundar, every Masar and every legionary in the legion, and he is also fifteen years old and knows nothing. His informal subordination to the senior NCOs of his assigned area is total. The army treats a Kadel who behaves arrogantly toward a Hundar as having committed a serious breach of professional conduct, a breach not of _rank_ but of the unwritten code that holds the system together.

## The Bridge Rank: Vallar

The Vallar holds the most consequential rank in the Vylarian army's social architecture.

The **Vallar** ("Camp Prefect") is the third-in-command of a legion, ranking below the Vandraks and the Lusar Maldor. By long tradition he is a _promoted Hundar Maldor_, a career commoner who, after distinguished service as a legion's senior centurion, has been formally raised to officer status. The raising requires the Vandraks's recommendation and the relevant Kamdor's confirmation, and it is granted perhaps twice in a decade across the whole imperial army.

A Vallar has operational authority equal to the Lusar Maldor's and answers to no one in the legion except the Vandraks and the Lusar Maldor. He runs the camp, the logistics, the training and the discipline, and, when the Vandraks is a courtier appointment and the Lusar Maldor is busy with diplomacy, he runs the actual fighting. Every Hundar Maldor in the army holds him up as the possible end of his own career, and every young noble officer learns from him in his Lusel year. He is the institution's living proof that the two tracks are not, finally, separate: at the highest level, competence can cross.

Since the reform that followed the Taruzelian war, candidates for Vallar elevation must pass a formal examination set jointly by the Dukran and a panel of serving Magnars. The examination is rigorous and has cut the elevation rate substantially. The career NCO class takes this as a calculated insult, because the only path the reform tightened is the one path through which their own track has historically crossed into officer ranks. Noble commissions remain purchasable as before. The discontent on this point among the senior NCOs is real and ongoing.

## The Commoner Track: Pure Meritocracy

Demonstrated capability earns every NCO and enlisted rank. **No payment changes hands for any commoner promotion, ever.** This contrast with the noble track is the institution's most consequential structural feature: the army's real backbone is a parallel meritocratic system that runs unaffected by court politics, family standing or the commission market.

| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |

| **Antar**         | Status granted after sixteen years of service or by Vandraks's recognition for distinguished service | Extra pay, exemption from labor details, voice in tribunal proceedings |
| **Kelor**         | Recommended by Masar, confirmed by Hundar                                                            | Commands a 4-man fire team                                             |
| **Masar**         | Recommended by Hundar, confirmed by Parthan                                                          | Commands an 8-man squad                                                |
| **Nokar**         | Recommended by Hundar, confirmed by Parthan                                                          | Watch officer, 3IC of a century                                        |
| **Sekor**         | Recommended by Hundar, confirmed by Parthan                                                          | Deputy centurion, 2IC of a century                                     |
| **Hundar**        | Recommended by Parthan, confirmed by Vandraks                                                        | Commands a century                                                     |
| **Hundar Kerval** | Recommended by Vallar, confirmed by Vandraks                                                         | Senior centurion of a cohort                                           |
| **Hundar Maldor** | Recommended by Vallar, confirmed by Vandraks                                                         | Senior centurion of the entire legion                                  |

The "confirming officer's cut" that the Vandraks takes on noble commission confirmations does not exist anywhere in this track. A Hundar's promotion to Hundar Kerval is recommended by his Vallar, confirmed by his Vandraks and recorded by the legion's scribar, and that is the whole transaction. The Vandraks takes nothing. The career NCO class understands the distinction perfectly, and it is one reason their loyalty to the institution survives the irritation of the parallel commission system.

## The Four Estates of Vylarian Nobility

The imperial registry kept by [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|the Imperial Scribal Guild]] recognizes four formal estates of nobility. The registry is updated each year and published at the spring imperial address, and it is one of the most closely watched documents in the empire, because it records every elevation, demotion and adjustment of standing.

| Estate                                | Houses | Livery / Mark                                            | Mode of Address       | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------------------------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The ethlenoi** ("Gilded Houses")    | ~30    | Gold-embroidered livery; gilded cypher on seal-rings     | "Ethlen [Surname]"    | Founding-era families traceable to the empire's first century. Hold the senior court offices and the great inherited commissions. The Dukran is invariably an ethlen.                        |
| **The ankrenoi** ("Silver Houses")    | ~250   | Silver-embroidered livery; silver cypher                 | "Ankren [Surname]"    | Established noble families with sustained court presence over four or more generations. Most Sodari Orzas and most Legars come from this estate.                                             |
| **The thurdenoi** ("Bronze Houses")   | ~1,500 | Bronze-embroidered livery; bronze cypher                 | "Thurden [Surname]"   | The minor nobility—provincial gentry, established but politically modest. Provide the bulk of the empire's Subtribunars and Tribunars.                                                       |
| **The pelerenoi** ("Elevated Houses") | ~400   | No metal livery; distinctive cypher granted on elevation | "Pelerenos [Surname]" | Families elevated to noble standing within the last two generations through commerce, military distinction (commissioned Primars), or imperial favor. Pay full or premium commission prices. |

Movement between estates is rare but real. A _thurden_ (Bronze) house that produces three generations of distinguished imperial servants and accumulates substantial wealth may petition for _ankren_ elevation, for which the Dukran's seal is required and the imperial fee is substantial. Movement up to _ethlen_ (Gilded) standing has happened perhaps four times in two centuries. Movement _down_, the demotion of an estate for cause, has happened twice in living memory and counts as the most extreme civilian punishment short of attainder.

## Currency and the Commission System

The Vylarian currency anchors at the **Solden** (gold coin) but circulates mainly in the **Ardin** and **Ogdel** (Bit). One Solden equals 160 Ardin equals 1,280 Bits. The Solden is barely a circulating coin: perhaps 200,000 exist empire-wide, mostly in noble vaults, the imperial treasury and [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]]’s strongroom at Orzara. (The [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian Currency]] note holds the full coinage system, sample prices and the wage scale.)

Large-value commerce moves in **paper script** issued by [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], the moneylenders' guild, against the imperial treasury's reserves. (The Tarmeks Orzan note describes the three-tier note system of retail, traveler's and master notes.) Nearly every house settles nearly every commission in script, even at the largest scales. Physical Solden payment is reserved for the most prestigious transactions, where an _ethlen_ house wishes to make a public display of liquid wealth.

The commission price tables below are denominated in Soldeni as the unit of account, and in nearly every case sealed [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran script]] settles the payment.

### Where the Commission Payment Goes

A typical commission payment divides roughly as follows, though the proportions shift by reign and by court faction:

| Recipient                              | Typical Share | Notes                                                                             |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Imperial Treasury                      | 40–55%        | The publicly accountable share                                                    |
| Previous holder of the commission      | 15–25%        | If the commission was sold rather than vacated by death                           |
| Orzas's privy purse                    | 10–15%        | Personal imperial accounts; not auditable                                         |
| Dukran's discretionary fund            | 5–10%         | Used for court politics, including reduced-price commissions for favored families |
| Confirming officer's personal accounts | 5–10%         | The Vandraks who confirms an Lusel appointment in his legion takes a cut          |
| Specific funds                         | 5%            | Veterans' relief, augural college, miscellaneous                                  |

Most senior officers' personal wealth derives largely from the confirming-officer cuts they accumulate over a career. A long-serving Vandraks who has confirmed several dozen commissions can build a substantial fortune from that alone.

### The Two Promotion Routes

A noble holding a commission can rise to the next rank by one of two routes.

The **purchase route** is the default. A commission becomes available, the candidate's family advances the sum (almost always in script), the confirming officers process the paperwork and the commission is granted. The system does not formally require military competence and checks it only as far as the confirming officer cares to.

The **competence route** discounts the commission price by 60–85%. It applies when an officer below the next rank has performed a distinguishing service: a notable battlefield contribution, a successful logistic intervention, the recovery of a unit's standard from disaster or a diplomatic success on a frontier posting. The recommendation comes from the senior officer of the campaign and the Vallar, whose endorsement is widely considered the most credible signal of merit. The candidate still pays something, since even a heavily discounted commission is meaningful money, and his family's political standing still matters. A totally unconnected officer cannot be promoted by merit alone. The competence route is _the army's primary internal mechanism for recognizing actual military capability_, and every senior officer who is not personally a courtier either came up through it or knows people who did.

### Secondary Market Practices

**Selling out.** A noble may, with his Vandraks's permission, sell his commission to a successor of comparable family standing and leave the army. The sale is private, at a negotiated price. Many noble officers retire this way: a Lusar in his early thirties who has served his family's expected term and wants to attend to his estates may sell to a younger relative or a friendly family's son, and recover substantial capital.

**Commission heirlooms.** A few commissions belong, by long custom, to particular families. The Vandraks of the [Karsan Hilvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"} has been a **[Veroniani]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}** family commission for four generations, and **[Ovidian]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}**-line officers have held the Gelmalor of the **Tenth** for two centuries. These positions are not technically inalienable, but the political cost of breaking the tradition is high enough that emperors have rarely tried.

**Cashiering.** The removal of a commission for cause: cowardice in the field, criminal acts, gross insubordination or treasonable contact with foreign powers. The commission is forfeit with no refund, and the family's standing takes a generational blow.

**Commissioning a commoner.** This is the most political act in the system. The Orzas's personal seal grants a commoner, typically a distinguished Hundar Maldor or Vallar, _pelerenos_ (Elevated) estate standing _and_ an officer commission at the appropriate rank, usually Lusel and occasionally Lusar, at no cash cost. It has been done perhaps a dozen times in two centuries. Every commissioned commoner who survives founds a new noble line and is a permanent reminder that the system is, in theory, penetrable.

## The Post-Taruzelian Reform Crisis

The Taruzelian secession war was an institutional shock. The imperial forces lost engagements they should have won and a province they should have held, and in several cases they lost them because incompetent purchased commissions made decisions that experienced centurions would never have made. The Dukran's after-action report, leaked to the senatorial classes within months of its filing, produced the most serious challenge to the commission system in two centuries.

The Reformist Faction argued for compulsory military examinations as a precondition for any commission above Lusel. Sodar Orzas **[Valerius Sextian Octorius]{.name data-type="being/npc"}** led it in public, a man widely understood to have served his own commission years competently enough to have qualified by examination. In private a coalition of _ankren_ houses led it, whose sons had served distinguished merit-route careers in the Taruzelian campaign.

The Conservative Faction, which included most of the _ethlen_ estate, most of the great commission-holding families and every officeholder whose career depended on the system as it stood, blocked the reform. Dukran **[Marcus Tertian Veranius]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, the senior _ethlen_ of his generation, led it and architected the eventual compromise.

The compromise: candidates for **Vallar** elevation from the commoner track must now pass an examination set jointly by the Dukran and a panel of serving Magnars. Noble commissions remain purchasable on the previous basis, and no examination is required for any noble rank. The [Lethunu]{.name data-type="affiliation"} regards the compromise as a partial victory. The Conservative Faction regards it as the price of preserving the rest of the system. The army's career NCOs regard it as a calculated insult, since the only path the reform tightened is the one their own track has historically used to cross into officer ranks.

The reform debate continues. The Orzas's current position is officially that the matter is "under continuing review," and that position holds until the next major war exposes the system again or a generational shift at court changes the factional balance.

## Identifying Marks

Each rank's mark depends on its track and tier.

**Court tier.** The Dukran wears the great gold collar of office, kept at the imperial palace between holders. Holvethoi wear an _ethlen_-gold cipher pin on the right shoulder of formal dress.

**Noble field officers.** They wear estate livery on dress occasions: gold for _ethlen_, silver for _ankren_, bronze for _thurden_ and the personal cipher for _pelerenos_. Rank insignia is a metallic plate worn at the gorget: a stylized eagle for Kamdor, Bandren and Vandraks; crossed spears for Lusar Maldor; a single vertical spear for Lusar; a horizontal spear for Lusel; a half-spear for Kadel.

**Vallar.** He wears a unique combined mark: the estate livery of his commissioned grant, always _pelerenos_, the lowest noble livery, worn with the bronze cipher reserved for senior _thurden_ officials, and the gorget plate of the senior officer corps, crossed spears.

**Career NCOs and enlisted.** They wear no estate livery. Rank shows in transverse-crest helmet plumes (red horsehair for Hundar, white for Hundar Maldor, dyed red-and-white for Vallar) and in sleeve-stripes on the working tunic: one stripe for Kelor, two for Masar, three for Nokar, four for Sekor, five for Hundar, six for Hundar Kerval, seven for Hundar Maldor. The transverse crest is the most immediately legible rank indicator on a battlefield, and the sleeve stripes are the most legible in garrison.

The **orkaima** of a legion, the eagle standard its _orkaimos_ bears, is the most sacred physical object in the imperial military. A legion that loses its _orkaima_ is disbanded, and the loss is formally recorded in the imperial archives. A legion that recovers an _orkaima_ lost in an earlier defeat is honored permanently with a gold band added to its name: the **[Karsan Soldan Korvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"}** recovered the Eagle of the **[Pelsan Hilvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"}** after **[[lore-blackmarshbtl|the Battle of the Black Marsh]]**, two centuries ago. The _orkaima_ is a brass-and-gilt sculpture on a polearm. Its materials are not precious in themselves, but its consecration is total.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Examination Scandal.** A Reformist Faction member has documentary evidence that the senior examining Kamdor deliberately failed a recent Vallar examination, on the Conservative Faction's instruction, because the candidate was a Hundar Maldor with strong reformist sympathies. The evidence is authentic. Publishing it would force the Orzas to act, and suppressing it would need the Dukran's quiet intervention against the Reformists. The candidate's Vandraks was a Lusel-era classmate of the Talkor's son.
- **The Ethlen Kadel Crisis.** A fifteen-year-old Kadel of the Veroniani house, heir to an inherited Vandraks commission, has publicly insulted the Hundar Maldor of the Karsan Hilvan. The Hundar Maldor's Vandraks has formally asked for the Kadel's reassignment, the Kadel's family has rejected the request as insulting, and the Dukran is being asked to mediate. The Hundar Maldor is one of the army's most distinguished and the Kadel's family is among the empire's most senior, so the resolution will set a precedent for a generation of similar incidents.
- **The Compromised Commission.** A wealthy _pelerenos_ merchant has bought a Lusel commission for his son at very nearly the published price, so the family received almost no political discount. Investigation shows that the moneylender who issued the script for the purchase was secretly insolvent at the moment of the transaction, and the script was honored only because the Tevran Treasury extended credit it should not have. The script has now matured and must be settled. The moneylender has fled, the Treasury is exposed, and the new Lusel is in the field with the **[Zorman Ardan]{.name data-type="affiliation"}**.
- **The Sold-Out Commission.** A distinguished Lusar Maldor of a _thurden_ house has sold his commission to a wealthy _pelerenos_ family at a substantial premium and retired to his estates, after a campaign in which his Vandraks's recommendation should have won him a competence-route Vandraks promotion and he was passed over. The _thurden_'s friends in the army believe the Orzas personally blocked the promotion at the Dukran's request, and the _thurden_ himself says nothing. The _pelerenos_ family's son is now the Lusar Maldor, and the _thurden_'s friends are looking for the truth and for some appropriate response.
- **The Recovered Eagle.** A border patrol has found, in a frontier ruin cleared of the undead, what may be the lost _orkaima_ of the **[Doran Hilvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"}**, which by official record was destroyed in a campaign forty years ago ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]) but may only have been captured and hidden. Authenticating it will be politically explosive. The Twelfth was disbanded after the loss, and restoring it under a recovered standard would be a substantial imperial act. The families whose ancestors were cashiered in the disbandment will press their claims, and the families holding the commissions raised in the Twelfth's place will resist. Authentication rests on a single mark visible only to officers who served in the original legion, all of whom are dead.
- **The Orzas's Quiet Question.** In a private conversation with a trusted Kamdor, the Orzas has asked whether the commission system could be reformed without a civil war. The Kamdor's answer matters. Whether he reports it faithfully to the Dukran, faithfully to the Reformist Faction, faithfully to nobody, or selectively so as to provoke a court realignment will shape the next decade of imperial politics. The Kamdor is in the field, and he has two months before he must return to Orzara and report.
