- **Type:** Imperial military institution
- **Region of operation:** [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]] — the territories under direct imperial governance. The legions do _not_ freely operate in [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldía]] or [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]], which have their own armies; nor in Harad since the secession; nor anywhere else outside imperial territory except by invitation or hire.
- **Supreme commander:** The reigning **Augustar** (Emperor)
- **Operational command:** The **Dux Bellorum** at Magnápolis, presiding over the war council of **Comares Augustari**

## Overview

The Vylarian military is a legion-based imperial army modeled on the founding-era principle that the empire is defined by its borders and the borders are defined by the legions that hold them. Every Vylarian schoolchild learns the four-word formulation that opens the imperial military manual: _Sub Aquila stant fines_ — "Beneath the eagle stand the borders." It is the institution by which the empire was made and through which it has, until very recently, retained the territories its founders conquered.

What makes the Vylarian military distinctive — and what shapes nearly every plot it appears in — is its **dual-track structure**. The institution is built out of two parallel rank hierarchies that meet only at a single carefully-controlled point. The **noble track** holds every formal officer rank from Cadetar to Magnar; it is filled by purchase, with prices adjusted by political standing, and military competence is welcome but not required. The **commoner track** holds every NCO and enlisted rank from Tironar to Primar; it is filled by pure demonstrated capability, and not a single coin changes hands at any promotion. The two tracks share quarters, share campaigns, and share the casualty lists, but they share none of their qualifying criteria.

The arrangement is the institution's open secret. The army's actual operational capability rests in the meritocratic track; the noble track provides political legitimacy, court patronage, and the families' continued investment in the imperial military. Every officer who has served long enough to understand the institution understands that the purchased-commission system buys aristocratic consent for the existence of a parallel meritocratic structure that the aristocracy could in principle dismantle but in practice does not, because dismantling it would mean losing every war the empire next fought. The Vylarians have lost wars under the system — most recently and disastrously the war that ended in the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Haradian secession]] twelve years ago — but they have won most of them.

The military's effective scope has contracted substantially over the empire's history. Two centuries ago the Vylarian legions garrisoned and policed territory from the Drenavar Spine to the Haradian coast; today they operate only in Vylaria proper and Heliónis. The Aurèldían kingdoms maintain their own armies (knightly orders, royal retinues, town militias); the Byzarian League maintains its own city militias and small standing force; Harad has built its own navy and land forces since the secession. Aurèldían and Byzarian nobles can still purchase commissions in the Vylarian legions — and a small number of Argentean-equivalent provincial nobles do, seeking imperial credentials — but the reverse is rare; Vylarian nobles rarely serve in non-imperial armies. The legions' reduced geographic scope is one of the standing concerns of the Reformist Faction at court; the Conservative Faction regards the empire's reduced military reach as the price of stability with the post-imperial commonwealth.

## Unit Hierarchy

The military's organizational structure runs from theater-level formations down to four-person fire teams. The arithmetic is consistent: four men to a _Quatera_, two _Quaterae_ to a _Decanum_, ten _Decana_ to a _Centaria_, six _Centariae_ to a _Sectara_, ten _Sectarae_ to a _Legio_, with everything above the _Legio_ an operational rather than administrative formation.

| Unit         | Gloss                                               | Strength        | Subunits                    | Commander        |
| ------------ | --------------------------------------------------- | --------------- | --------------------------- | ---------------- |
| **Magnatum** | "the Great Host" — a theater force                  | 30,000–100,000+ | 2–6 _Vexilia_               | Magnar           |
| **Vexilium** | "the Banner" — a field army for a specific campaign | 10,000–30,000   | 2–6 _Legiones_              | Magistar Vexilii |
| **Legio**    | Legion — the basic strategic formation              | ~5,000          | 10 _Sectarae_ + auxiliaries | Legar            |
| **Sectara**  | Cohort-equivalent — the basic tactical formation    | ~480            | 6 _Centariae_               | Praepar          |
| **Centaria** | Century-equivalent — the basic administrative unit  | ~80             | 10 _Decana_                 | Centurius        |
| **Decanum**  | Squad / mess group                                  | 8               | 2 _Quaterae_                | Decar            |
| **Quatera**  | Fire team — the smallest tactical maneuver element  | 4               | —                           | Quaterar         |

The **Quatera** as the basic tactical element is a Vylarian doctrinal innovation that distinguishes the imperial army from earlier Aurèldían and pre-conquest Haradian forces. The principle is that a four-person team can independently execute fire-and-movement, with two teams in a _Decanum_ supporting one another in alternating bounds. Field manuals attribute the innovation to a long-dead military reformer (real or invented; the manuals disagree).

A young aristocrat purchases a Cadetar commission at fifteen, serves under his Legar's mentorship for two years, purchases a Subtribunar commission at seventeen or eighteen, and joins a legion as a junior officer. A common-born youth presents himself at a recruiting station, takes the legion's oath as a Tironar, completes a year of training to become a Legionar, and begins the slow climb upward through demonstrated capability. He takes the oath no earlier than his fifteenth birthday and serves a full term of twenty years; the commoner who serves it out is discharged a **citizen of the Empire**, with the dole and the standing that status carries (see [[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vylarian Citizenship]]). The two careers run in parallel through the same legion's barracks, mess halls, and engagements; they meet formally only at the **Praefar Castorum** position.

## The Court Tier: Courtiers, Not Soldiers

The highest formal commands in the Vylarian military are openly political. Selection criteria are family standing, loyalty to the Augustar's faction, and political reliability. Military experience is welcome but not required, and an experienced military Dux Bellorum is in fact often mistrusted at court precisely because he could conceivably use his actual command to threaten the throne.

| Rank                | Role                                                              | Reality                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Augustar**        | Emperor; nominal supreme commander                                | Almost never personally takes the field; military decisions filtered through the war council                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Dux Bellorum**    | Chief War-Minister; presides over the imperial war council        | A court appointee; may or may not have ever held an operational command. The position rotates among the most politically powerful Comares. Invariably an Aurelian.                                                                              |
| **Comar Augustari** | "Imperial Companion" — member of the war council; 4–8 at any time | Hold nominal command titles ("Comar Augustari, Legar of the Third Vesperine"), draw the salary, attend imperial ceremonies, and may never visit their legions. Real operational command is delegated to the senior tribune or the camp prefect. |

When a real campaign is mounted, operational command is given to a **Magnar** (theater commander) who is also a noble — but who is typically chosen for actual capacity to fight a war. The Magnar reports formally to the Dux Bellorum but in practice operates with substantial autonomy. The two ranks coexist awkwardly: the Comares hold nominal seniority, the Magnars hold the field. Sensible Comares stay out of operational decisions; insecure ones interfere, sometimes disastrously.

## The Imperial Guard and the City Watch

Two standing bodies sit apart from the field legions and the war command, dedicated not to the borders but to the throne and the capital.

The **Praetar Augustarum** are the **imperial guard** — the Augustar's personal bodyguard, and the keepers of order and justice within the Inner City of [[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]] itself. They garrison the **[[place-castraprtr|Castra Praetoria]]** on the palace hill, recruit and promote apart from the line legions, and answer to the Augustar alone. Elite, lavishly equipped, and intensely political, they are the praetorian muscle of the court: the body best placed to guard an Augustar is also the body best placed to unmake one, and every reigning Augustar courts their loyalty with care. Their writ stops at the Inner Wall — the rest of the capital is not theirs to police.

The **City Watch** is a standing branch of the legions detailed permanently to the policing of Magnápolis. Legionary in recruitment, discipline, and equipment, it is nonetheless a city institution rather than a field force: it takes no part in foreign campaigns, but patrols the wards, breaks the riots, hunts the city's criminals, and enforces the rulings of the civic magistrates from its fortified watch-houses — one on or near each of the city's Twelve Circles. Its everyday direction belongs not to a general but to the city's civic council, the **[[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curia Urbis]]**, through the curator who holds the portfolio of order — an arrangement that deliberately keeps the capital's standing muscle answerable to the Augustar's civic appointees rather than to any one commander. Lesser watches on the same model keep the peace in the empire's other great cities.

### The City Watch in Detail

The Watch is organized on the legionary pattern but bent to the city's shape: a **watch-cohort** (a _Sectara_ of some four to five hundred) is quartered in each ward's watch-house and broken into the _Centariae_ and patrols that walk its lanes, so that across the Twelve Circles the whole force runs to roughly a legion's strength. It is officered through the army's commoner track — career centurions and veterans, many of them legionaries pensioned off the frontier into city service, a posting they reckon a soft billet or a comedown by temperament — and commanded overall by a **Praefar Vigilum**, the Prefect of the Watch, who answers to the [[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curator of Order]] on the Curia Urbis.

Day to day the watchmen — the **vigiles** — go lightly armed for the press of the streets: cudgel, short blade, and no more than a leather jack, for a cohort in full harness cannot chase a cutpurse up a stepped lane. The same men can muster in proper legionary kit within the hour when a Circle erupts, and a riot is the one time the Watch fights as soldiers rather than constables. Their charge is broad — patrol and arrest, the night-watch against fire in a timber-dense city (every watch-house keeps hand-engines, hooks, and a cistern), the guarding of the granaries and gates, the muscle behind the Aediles' writs, and the first answer to disorder before ever the Inner City's guard need stir. They are also, by long tradition, thoroughly bribable; and the Praetar Augustarum of the palace look down on them as glorified street-wardens — a contempt the vigiles return with the resentment of men who do the actual, unglamorous work of keeping a million people from one another's throats.

## The Noble Junior Tier: Where the Lesson Is Learned

The system's tactical effectiveness depends on the fact that young noble officers below Tribunar Magnar are _expected to learn from_ the senior centurions of their assigned units. The expectation is universal, unwritten, and enforced by institutional culture rather than by formal rule.

| Rank                | Age   | Position                                    | What He Actually Does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| ------------------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cadetar**         | 15–17 | Personal aide to a Legar or Tribunar Magnar | Carries messages, attends officer councils as a silent observer, learns the legion's organization by watching.                                                                                                                                                                |
| **Subtribunar**     | 17–22 | One of five junior tribunes per legion      | First real commissioned posting. Holds nominal command authority over an assigned area of the legion (a sectara, a logistics function, a training section). In practice, takes direction from the senior centurion of his assigned area on virtually all operational matters. |
| **Tribunar**        | 22–28 | Tribune, second commission                  | Has survived the Subtribunar years and learned the lessons. Now genuinely contributes; may command in the field with confidence.                                                                                                                                              |
| **Tribunar Magnar** | 28–35 | Senior tribune, 2IC of a legion             | Operational deputy to the Legar. Often the legion's effective day-to-day commander when the Legar is a courtier appointment.                                                                                                                                                  |
| **Legar**           | 30+   | Legion commander                            | Frequently an Argentean or Aurelian appointment; political weight at least as important as tactical capacity. The legion's actual operational commander, but reliant on the Tribunar Magnar and the Praefar Castorum for execution.                                           |

The critical relationship is between the **Subtribunar** (18-year-old aristocrat, technically outranks every commoner in the legion) and the **Primar** (50-year-old senior centurion, has held the legion's combat doctrine in his head for thirty years). The Subtribunar's commission says he is the Primar's superior. Every unwritten rule of the Vylarian army says he should treat the Primar's counsel as binding unless he has an extraordinarily good reason to do otherwise. A Subtribunar who issues operational orders contrary to his Primar's advice and gets the unit cut up is _not_ defended by his family's connections; he is quietly cashiered, sent home, and the family is informed that further military careers in their line should perhaps be reconsidered.

The same dynamic operates one rank lower. A **Cadetar** is technically senior to every Centurius, every Decar, every legionary in the legion. He is also fifteen years old and knows nothing. His informal subordination to the senior NCOs in his assigned area is total. The army's culture treats a Cadetar who behaves arrogantly toward a Centurius as having committed a serious breach of professional conduct — not a breach of _rank_, but a breach of the unwritten code that holds the system together.

## The Bridge Rank: Praefar Castorum

This is the single most consequential rank in the Vylarian army's social architecture.

**Praefar Castorum** ("Camp Prefect") is the third-in-command of a legion, ranking below the Legar and the Tribunar Magnar. He is, by long tradition, a _promoted Primar_ — a career commoner who, after exceptional service as senior centurion of a legion, has been formally elevated to officer status. The elevation requires recommendation from the Legar and confirmation from the relevant Magnar; it is granted perhaps twice in a decade across the entire imperial army.

A Praefar Castorum carries operational authority equivalent to the senior tribunes but answers to no one in the legion except the Legar and the Tribunar Magnar. He runs the camp, the logistics, the training, the discipline, and — when the Legar is a courtier appointment and the Tribunar Magnar is busy with diplomacy — the actual fighting. He is the example every Primar in the army holds up as the possible end of his career, and the example every young noble officer learns from in his Subtribunar year. He is the institution's living proof that the two tracks are not, finally, separate: at the highest level, competence can cross.

Since the post-Haradian-war reform, candidates for Praefar Castorum elevation must pass a formal examination set jointly by the Dux Bellorum and a panel of serving Magnars. The examination is genuinely rigorous and substantially reduces the elevation rate. The career NCO class regards this as a calculated insult precisely because of the contrast: the only path the reform tightened is the one path through which their own track has historically crossed into officer ranks. Noble commissions remain purchasable as before. The discontent on this point inside the senior NCO ranks is real and ongoing.

## The Commoner Track: Pure Meritocracy

Every NCO and enlisted rank is awarded for demonstrated capability. **No payment changes hands for any commoner promotion, ever.** The contrast with the noble track is the institution's most consequential structural feature: the army's actual operational backbone rests on a parallel meritocratic system that runs unaffected by court politics, family standing, or the commission market.

| **Decar**            | Recommended by Centurius, confirmed by Praepar                                                      | Commands an 8-man squad                                                |
| **Tesserar**         | Recommended by Centurius, confirmed by Praepar                                                      | Watch officer, 3IC of a century                                        |
| **Optar**            | Recommended by Centurius, confirmed by Praepar                                                      | Deputy centurion, 2IC of a century                                     |
| **Centurius**        | Recommended by Praepar, confirmed by Legar                                                          | Commands a century                                                     |

The "confirming officer's cut" that the Legar takes on noble commission confirmations does not exist anywhere in this track. A Centurius's promotion to Primar Sectaris is recommended by his Praefar Castorum, confirmed by his Legar, and recorded by the legion's scribar — that is the entire transaction. The Legar takes nothing. The career NCO class understands this distinction perfectly, and it is one of the reasons their loyalty to the institution survives the irritation of the parallel commission system.

## The Four Estates of Vylarian Nobility

The imperial registry maintained by the [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]] recognizes four formal estates of nobility. The annual registry update — published at the spring imperial address — is one of the most politically watched documents in the empire; elevations, demotions, and standing-grade adjustments are all recorded there.

| Estate                              | Houses | Livery / Mark                                            | Mode of Address       | Notes                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ----------------------------------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The Aurelii** ("Gilded Houses")   | ~30    | Gold-embroidered livery; gilded cypher on seal-rings     | "Aurelian [Surname]"  | Founding-era families traceable to the empire's first century. Hold the senior court offices and the great inherited commissions. The Dux Bellorum is invariably an Aurelian.                |
| **The Argentei** ("Silver Houses")  | ~250   | Silver-embroidered livery; silver cypher                 | "Argentean [Surname]" | Established noble families with sustained court presence over four or more generations. Most Comares Augustari and most Legars come from this estate.                                        |
| **The Aerei** ("Bronze Houses")     | ~1,500 | Bronze-embroidered livery; bronze cypher                 | "Aerean [Surname]"    | The minor nobility — provincial gentry, established but politically modest. Provide the bulk of the empire's Subtribunars and Tribunars.                                                     |
| **The Adlecti** ("Elevated Houses") | ~400   | No metal livery; distinctive cypher granted on elevation | "Adlect [Surname]"    | Families elevated to noble standing within the last two generations through commerce, military distinction (commissioned Primars), or imperial favor. Pay full or premium commission prices. |

Movement between estates is rare but real. An Aerean house that produces three generations of distinguished imperial servants and accumulates substantial wealth may petition for Argentean elevation; the Dux Bellorum's seal is required and the imperial fee is substantial. Movement up to Aurelian standing has occurred perhaps four times in two centuries. Movement _down_ — demotion of an estate for cause — has occurred twice in living memory and is regarded as the most extreme civilian punishment short of attainder.

## Currency and the Commission System

The Vylarian currency anchors at the **Aurion** (gold coin) but circulates principally in the **Argentus** (Argo) and **Octus** (Bit). One Aurion equals 160 Argo equals 1,280 Bits. The Aurion is barely a circulating coin — perhaps 200,000 in existence empire-wide, mostly held in noble vaults, the imperial treasury, and the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]'s strongroom at Magnápolis. (See the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian Currency]] reference for the full coinage system, sample prices, and wage scale.)

Large-value commerce moves not in physical Aurion coin but in **paper script** issued by the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|moneylenders' guild]] against the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|imperial treasury]]'s reserves. (See the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]] note for the structure of the three-tier note system — retail, traveler's, and master notes.) Commission payments — even at the largest scales — are settled in script for nearly all houses and nearly all commissions; physical Aurion payment is reserved for the most prestigious transactions where an Aurelian house wishes to make a public display of liquid wealth.

The commission price tables below are denominated in Aurions as the unit of account; actual payment in nearly every case is by sealed Collegium script.

| Recipient                              | Typical Share | Notes                                                                             |

### The Two Promotion Routes

A noble holding a commission can rise to the next rank by one of two routes.

The **purchase route** is the default. The commission becomes available; the candidate's family advances the appropriate sum (in script, almost always); the confirming officers process the paperwork; the commission is granted. Military competence is not formally required and is checked only insofar as the confirming officer cares to check it.

The **competence route** discounts the commission price by 60–85%. It is invoked when an officer below the next rank has performed a distinguishing service — a notable battlefield contribution, a successful logistic intervention, the recovery of a unit's standard from disaster, a diplomatic success on a frontier posting. The recommendation comes from the senior officer of the campaign and the Praefar Castorum, whose endorsement is widely considered the most credible signal of genuine merit. The candidate still pays something — even a heavily discounted commission is meaningful money — and his family's political standing still matters; a totally unconnected officer cannot be promoted by merit alone. The competence route is _the army's primary internal mechanism for recognizing actual military capability_. Every senior officer who is not personally a courtier has either come up through it or knows people who did.

### Secondary Market Practices

**Selling out.** A noble may, with his Legar's permission, sell his commission to a successor of comparable family standing and leave the army. The transaction is a private sale at a negotiated price. This is how many noble officers retire: a Tribunar in his early thirties who has served his family's expected term and now wishes to attend to estates may sell his commission to a younger relative or a friendly family's son, recovering substantial capital.

**Commission heirlooms.** A small number of commissions are, by long custom, considered to belong to particular families. The Legar of the Third Vesperine has been a Veroniani family commission for four generations; the Praefar Alariae of the Tenth has been held by Ovidian-line officers for two centuries. These positions are not technically inalienable, but the political cost of breaking the tradition is high enough that emperors have rarely tried.

**Cashiering.** Removal of commission for cause — cowardice in the field, criminal acts, gross insubordination, treasonable contact with foreign powers. The commission is forfeit with no refund; the family's standing takes a generational blow.

**Commissioning a commoner.** The single most political act in the system. A commoner — typically a distinguished Primar or Praefar Castorum — is formally granted Adlect estate standing _and_ an officer commission at the appropriate rank (usually Subtribunar, occasionally Tribunar) at no cash cost. The act requires the Augustar's personal seal and has been used perhaps a dozen times in two centuries. Every commissioned commoner who survives becomes the founder of a new noble line and a permanent reminder that the system is theoretically penetrable.

## The Post-Haradian Reform Crisis

The Haradian secession war was an institutional shock. The imperial forces lost engagements they should have won, lost a province they should have held, and in several cases lost them because incompetent purchased commissions made decisions that experienced centurions would never have made. The Dux Bellorum's after-action report — leaked to the senatorial classes within months of its filing — produced the most serious challenge to the commission system in two centuries.

A **Reformist Faction** at court argued for compulsory military examinations as a precondition for any commission above Subtribunar. They were led publicly by Comar Augustari **Valerius Sextian Octorius**, a man widely understood to have served his own commission years with sufficient competence to have qualified by examination, and privately by a coalition of Argentean houses whose sons had served distinguished merit-route careers in the Haradian campaign.

A **Conservative Faction** — comprising most of the Aurelian estate, most of the great commission-holding families, and the entire body of officeholders whose careers depended on the system as-is — blocked the reform. They were led by Dux Bellorum **Marcus Tertian Veranius**, the senior Aurelian of his generation and the architect of the eventual compromise.

The compromise: candidates for **Praefar Castorum** elevation from the commoner track must now pass an examination set jointly by the Dux Bellorum and a panel of serving Magnars. Noble commissions remain purchasable on the previous basis, and no examination is required for any noble rank. The Reformist Faction regards the compromise as a partial victory; the Conservative Faction regards it as the price of preserving the rest of the system; the army's career NCOs regard it as a calculated insult, since the only path the reform has tightened is the one path through which their own track has historically crossed into officer ranks.

The reform debate continues. The Augustar's current position is officially that the matter is "under continuing review." The position will hold until the next major war exposes the system again or until a generational shift at court changes the factional balance. (For the broader politics surrounding the Reformist-Conservative split, see the forthcoming court-politics note.)

## Identifying Marks

Each rank's identifying mark depends on the track and tier:

**Court tier** — Dux Bellorum wears the great gold collar of office, kept at the imperial palace between holders; Comares wear an Aurelian-gold cypher pin on the right shoulder of formal dress.

**Noble field officers** — wear estate-livery on dress occasions (gold for Aurelian, silver for Argentean, bronze for Aerean, the personal cypher for Adlect). Rank insignia is a metallic plate worn at the gorget: a stylized eagle for Magnar/Magistar Vexilii/Legar; crossed spears for Tribunar Magnar; a single vertical spear for Tribunar; a horizontal spear for Subtribunar; a half-spear for Cadetar.

**Praefar Castorum** — wears a unique combined mark: the estate-livery of his commissioned grant (always Adlect, the lowest noble livery, but worn with the bronze cypher reserved for senior Aerean officials) and the gorget plate of the senior officer corps (crossed spears).

**Career NCOs and enlisted** — wear no estate livery. Rank is marked by transverse-crest helmet plumes (red horsehair for Centurius, white for Primar, dyed-red-and-white for Praefar Castorum) and by sleeve-stripes on the working tunic (one stripe for Quaterar, two for Decar, three for Tesserar, four for Optar, five for Centurius, six for Primar Sectaris, seven for Primar). The transverse crest is the most immediately legible rank indicator on a battlefield; the sleeve stripes the most legible in garrison.

The **Aquila** of a legion — the eagle standard borne by its Aquilar — is the most sacred physical object in the imperial military. A legion that loses its Aquila is disbanded with the loss formally recorded in imperial archives; a legion that recovers an Aquila lost in previous defeat is honored permanently with a gold band added to its name (the "Tertia Aurelia Coronata" recovered the Eagle of the Fourth Vesperine after the Battle of the Black Marsh, two centuries ago). The Aquila itself is a brass-and-gilt sculpture mounted on a polearm; the materials are not precious in themselves, but the consecration is total.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Examination Scandal.** A Reformist faction member has produced documentary evidence that a recent Praefar Castorum examination was deliberately failed by the senior examining Magnar, on instruction from the Conservative Faction, because the candidate was a Primar of strong reformist sympathies. The evidence is genuine. Publication would force the Augustar to act. Suppression would require the Dux Bellorum's quiet intervention against the Reformists. The candidate's Legar is a Subtribunar-era classmate of the Praetar's son.
- **The Aurelian Cadetar Crisis.** A fifteen-year-old Cadetar of the Veroniani house — heir to an inherited Legar commission — has publicly insulted the Primar of the Third Vesperine. The Primar's Legar has formally requested the Cadetar's reassignment; the Cadetar's family has rejected the request as insulting; the Dux Bellorum is being asked to mediate. The Primar is one of the army's most distinguished, the Cadetar's family is among the empire's most senior, and the resolution will set precedent for an entire generation of similar incidents.
- **The Compromised Commission.** A wealthy Adlect merchant has purchased a Subtribunar commission for his son at very nearly the published price — meaning the family received almost no political discount. Investigation reveals that the moneylender who issued the script for the purchase was, at the moment of the transaction, secretly insolvent; the script was honored only because the Collegium Treasury extended credit it should not have. The script has now matured and must be settled. The moneylender is in flight; the Treasury is exposed; the new Subtribunar is in the field with the Eighth Argentine.
- **The Sold-Out Commission.** A distinguished Tribunar Magnar of an Aerean house has sold his commission to a wealthy Adlect family at a substantial premium, retiring to his estates after a campaign in which his Legar's recommendation should have produced a competence-route Legar promotion that he was passed over for. The Aerean's friends in the army believe the Augustar intervened personally to block the promotion at the Dux Bellorum's request; the Aerean himself will say nothing. The Adlect family's son is now the Tribunar Magnar; the Aerean's friends are looking for the truth and for some appropriate response.
- **The Recovered Eagle.** A border patrol has found, in a frontier ruin cleared of the undead, what may be the lost Aquila of the Twelfth Vesperine — destroyed (by official record) in a campaign forty years ago, but possibly only captured and hidden. Authentication will be politically explosive: the Twelfth was disbanded after the loss; restoring it under a recovered standard would be a substantial imperial act; the families whose ancestors were cashiered in the disbandment will press their claims; the families currently holding the commissions raised in the Twelfth's place will resist. The Aquila's authentication rests on a single mark visible only to officers who served in the original legion, all of whom are now dead.
- **The Augustar's Quiet Question.** The Augustar has, in a private conversation with a trusted Magnar, asked whether the commission system could be reformed without a civil war. The Magnar's answer matters. Whether it is faithfully reported to the Dux Bellorum, faithfully reported to the Reformist Faction, faithfully reported to nobody, or selectively reported in such a way as to provoke a court realignment will define the next decade of imperial politics. The Magnar is in the field, and he has two months before he must return to Magnápolis and report.
