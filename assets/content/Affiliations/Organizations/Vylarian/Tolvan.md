A Tolvan assessor on circuit has a speech he gives the first time he rides into a village behind the tax-farmer's mule. Say it goes like this: "The Tolar will be pleasant tonight and exact tomorrow. He paid the Tolvan for the right to collect here before he saw a single field, and every Ardin he squeezes out above his bid is his own. I am here to see that he squeezes by the rules. Keep your tally-sticks, keep your receipts, and when he shows his brass tessera, look at the contract number before you look at his face."

## Overview

The Tolvan, the Fiscal Court, administers Vylarian taxation. It sets the empire-wide rates on land, customs, mining, inheritance and the commercial transactions the imperial tax base draws on. It awards the territorial contracts under which the **Tolari**, the empire's private tax-farmers, collect from individual taxpayers. It audits those Tolari through its own discipline arm, hears taxpayer appeals when an assessment is disputed, and produces the annual revenue projections on which the [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]] plans its disbursements. Its seat is the **Fiscal Hall**, within the **Imperial Offices** in the Inner City of [[place-orzara|Ôrzâra]]. Arzánas, the second Ôrzâs of the Restoration house, constituted it in his reforms three centuries ago ([[lore-arzanasedict|The Edicts of Arzánas]]), and it took its present form roughly a century later.

Its scope is geographically narrow and fiscally large. Its writ runs only in the territory under direct imperial governance, [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] proper, because outside them the post-imperial commonwealth's polities set their own rates and collect their own revenues. A Tarvénian merchant pays the Tolvan nothing. He pays his Tarvénian crown's land tax, his city's gate tolls and the [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex]]'s 2% issuance fee on any note he buys, and the Tolvan has no authority over him. Inside Vylaria its authority is comprehensive; its claim on [[place-helionis|Heliónis]] stands on paper and the cities do not pay: every land assessment, customs duty and imperial sales tax flows from its rate-setting and its contract awards.

Inside the empire the Tolvan is politically powerful and individually unpopular. The senior nobility staff it, drawing its officeholders largely from the _ankhrâzênôs_ (Silver) and _elthêzênôs_ (Gilded) estates, which gives them a stake in the tax system and a check on its excesses. The collection itself is the Tolari's work, and the common population hates them without reserve. The [[affiliation-lethunu2|Lethunu]] at court periodically argues for replacing the Tolari with salaried imperial collectors, the **Conservative Faction** blocks the reform, and the system goes on unchanged generation after generation.

## The Three-Tier Tax Structure (Imperial Scope Only)

Inside Vylaria, three tiers of taxation apply to every taxpayer at once. The Tolvan sets and collects only the first tier. Provincial and municipal authorities administer the other two.

| Tier           | Set By                                                         | Collected By                                                            | Flows To                    | Examples                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Imperial**   | Tolvan at Ôrzâra, with Ôrzâs's seal                            | Tolari (contracted tax-farmers)                                         | Tarmex Orzan                | Land tax (uniform rate on assessed value); customs and frontier duties; mining royalties; inheritance tax above threshold; imperial sales tax on commission sales and other categories |
| **Provincial** | Provincial thêrkhôs in Vylaria; city-state council in Heliónis | Provincial officials, mostly salaried; some Tolari on smaller contracts | Provincial / civic treasury | Road tolls; provincial market overrides; special provincial assessments                                                                                                                |
| **Local**      | Municipal council, town magistrate, or village headman         | Municipal officials directly                                            | Municipal treasury          | Gate tolls; market stall dues; local property tax; public works levies; festival levies                                                                                                |

A Vylarian merchant in a provincial capital pays imperial land tax to the Tolar who holds the regional contract, his province's market override to the provincial collection office, and his city's gate toll every time he brings goods through the walls. That is three streams, three apparatuses and three ultimate recipients, and the Tolvan has no claim on what the other two collect.

Outside the empire only the lower two tiers apply. An Aurèldían merchant pays his crown's tax (the kingdom-tier equivalent of the provincial tier) and his city's tax, with no imperial layer above. He still pays the Tarmex's 2% issuance fee on traveler's notes, but the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]]'s banking system collects it and not the Tolvan, and no post-imperial polity classifies it as taxation in its official accounts.

## The Tolari System

The Tolvan does not collect taxes directly. The work of going city by city and town by town is contracted out to **Tolari** (sing. **Tolar**), private tax-farmers who are almost without exception wealthier commoners and _pêlêrênôs_ (Elevated) nobles. They bid on collection contracts for specific territories and revenue categories.

The contract works like this. The Tolvan's **provincial Karvani** prepare annual estimates of expected revenue from each territorial category: the land tax on the upper Vylarian river valley, the customs duties at the port of [[place-mercavia|Dêlâlâra]], the mining royalties of the eastern silver lodes. The estimates are published, and eligible Tolari bid against them, offering to pay the Tolvan a stated sum in advance for the right to collect during the contract period. The winning Tolar pays his bid into the Tarmex Orzan through the Danvar's clearing system. Then he collects in his territory for the contract period and keeps every Ardin he can collect above his bid.

The system's central feature, and its central abuse, is that **a Tolar's profit is exactly the margin between his bid and his actual collection**. A Tolar who collected only what was theoretically due would earn nothing: he would have paid his bid and recovered exactly his bid. To make the returns that justify bidding, he must extract more than the assessment. He does it by assessing marginal properties aggressively, by enforcing small omissions harshly, with penalty fees and threats of seizure, and by whatever else his contract allows and the local population will not riot over. The Tolari are not lawless. The Tolvan's standing rules limit them and the **Rôzimândoi of the Tolvan** investigate the worst excesses. Within the rules, however, there is plenty of room to keep adding pressure, and the Tolari do so for a living.

The Tolar class has become wealthy, despised and politically influential. The richest Tolari keep substantial households, hire small private guard forces to protect collection convoys and their own homes from popular hostility, and over the last century have increasingly bought imperial favor and with it _pêlêrênôs_ noble standing. Their grandsons and great-grandsons sit as _thûrdimênôs_ (Bronze) and even _ankhrâzênôs_ members of the senior estates. The stigma washes out slowly across generations and never entirely. A senior noble whose ancestors three generations back were Tolari is still sometimes called "Tolar-spawn" in private noble correspondence.

## Structure

The Tolvan works through four bodies.

### The Council of the Fiscal Court

The Council is the standing governing body, with sixteen seats drawn from the senior _ankhrâzênôs_ and _elthêzênôs_ estates by imperial appointment for ten-year terms, renewable once. It sets imperial tax rates (subject to the Ôrzâs's approval), approves Tolar contract terms, hears appeals against Karvan decisions and decides standing fiscal policy. It meets monthly at the Fiscal Hall and holds formal sessions quarterly with the Dukran, the Imperial _Târkhêlândôs_ (treasury officer) and, twice a year, the Ôrzâs himself.

### The Talkor of the Tolvan

The Talkor of the Tolvan chairs the Council and is the Tolvan's chief officer. The Ôrzâs appoints him for a ten-year term, renewable, and dismisses him at imperial discretion. The current Talkor of the Tolvan is **Vêldâs Ôrthêmarândis**, an _elthêzênôs_ in his sixties whose tenure has been marked by cautious moderation: he declines to push contentious reforms and declines equally to let the worst Tolar abuses escalate past a manageable level. Both court factions, Reformist and Conservative, see him as the least objectionable Talkor of the Tolvan they have any prospect of getting.

### The Provincial Karvani

Eight provincial officers run the Tolvan's operations in their territories: one for each of the four Vylarian provinces and four for the principal Heliónite city-states. They prepare the annual revenue estimates, manage the Tolar contract awards and supervise the local _rôzimândoi_ (auditors) of the Tolvan. They report to the Council monthly and to the Talkor of the Tolvan directly on urgent matters.

A Karvan holds one of the more politically consequential mid-level postings in the empire. He can quietly favor or disfavor particular bidders, adjust estimates to make contracts more or less profitable, and call for _rôzimândôs_ investigations selectively. His personal wealth, built on the Karvan's traditional cut of contract awards, is substantial. It parallels the Vandrax's cut of commission confirmations in the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|imperial military]].

### The Rôzimândoi of the Tolvan

Twenty _rôzimândoi_ of the Tolvan form the discipline arm. The Council appoints them, and they report both to the Council and to the Talkor of the Tolvan directly. They investigate Tolar abuses, audit collection books, prosecute serious offenses before the Council and, most consequentially, recommend Tolari for permanent disqualification from future contracts.

They are distinct from the [[affiliation-tevran|Rôzimândoi of the Tevran]], who audit guild compliance, and from the [[affiliation-tarmex|Rôzimândoi of the Tarmex]], who audit the imperial treasury. The three _rôzimândôs_ bodies coordinate informally, and their formal jurisdictions are kept separate so that no one body can gather concentrated investigative authority.

The senior officer is the _hôlrôzimândôs_ (chief auditor) of the Tolvan, currently **Sextia Marcania**, a famously rigorous _thûrdimênôs_ officer whose elevation the Tolar class opposed and the **Reformist Faction** pushed through. Her tenure has been the most active in two generations, and her disciplinary case-rate is roughly double her predecessors'.

## Imperial Tax Categories

The Tolvan administers these principal taxes within imperial territory:

- **Land Tax** (_tolgar_)—an annual assessment on agricultural land at a uniform rate on assessed value, modified by quality and use. It is the empire's single largest revenue source.
- **Customs Duties** (_tolsen_)—levied on goods crossing imperial frontiers and, at lower rates, provincial boundaries within the empire. They are the second-largest revenue source, and particularly significant on the lucrative trade routes through Heliónis.
- **Mining Royalties** (_tolbor_)—on extraction from imperial-claim mines, and substantial in the silver-rich eastern Vylarian provinces.
- **Inheritance Tax** (_tolmen_)—on estate transfers above a threshold. It falls mainly on the senior estates and is one of the Reformist Faction's preferred levers for slowly reducing inherited wealth.
- **Imperial Sales Tax** (_tolvek_)—on certain commercial transactions, most notably military commission sales (40–55% of which flows to the imperial treasury under the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|standing schedule]]), real estate transfers above a threshold and major commercial contracts.
- **Manumission Tax**—on the granting of citizenship to a resident. It is small in aggregate and politically significant.

## Relations

- **The [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]]** is the direct counterpart: the Tolvan collects and the Tarmex holds. Coordination is constant, and tension arises when the Tolvan's revenue projections fall short of the Tarmex's disbursement requirements.
- **The Ôrzâs and the Dukran** are patrons and ultimate authorities. The Talkor of the Tolvan serves at imperial discretion, and Council seats turn over at the Ôrzâs's pleasure within procedural limits.
- **The provincial thêrkhoi and the Heliónite city-state councils** are operational counterparts in the lower tax tiers. The relationship is correct and bureaucratic, with occasional disputes over jurisdictional boundaries, since either tier may claim a tax category.
- **The Lethunu** at court are the natural patrons of the _rôzimândoi_ of the Tolvan' more aggressive prosecutions, and they push periodically for Tolar reform.
- **[[affiliation-qetharpalu2|The Qethar'palu]]** are the natural protectors of the Tolari system. They comprise the Tolar class itself, the families whose wealth comes from Tolar contracts and the broader _elthêzênôs_ and _ankhrâzênôs_ estates whose political influence depends on the current arrangement.
- **The Tolari themselves** are the Tolvan's contractors, its political problem and its most visible public face. The relationship is structurally adversarial, since the _rôzimândoi_ of the Tolvan discipline them, and operationally interdependent, since the Karvani could not collect without them.

## Identifying Marks

Tolvan officers wear the deep imperial-purple stole over working dress at formal sessions, with the Tolvan's eagle-and-balance sigil at the breast. Provincial Karvani add a brass medallion of their province. _Rôzimândoi_ of the Tolvan wear black mantles with the gold sword-and-balance mark (the sword for enforcement, the balance for assessment), distinguishable from the silver-marked mantle of the _rôzimândoi_ of the Tevran. The Talkor of the Tolvan wears the great gold collar of office at imperial ceremonies.

A Tolar's collection authority is attested by a **brass collection-tessera**, stamped with his contract number, the territorial designation, the dates of the contract period and the Karvan's countermark. He shows it to taxpayers as proof of his authority, and it is essential for any forcible seizure under his contract powers. Counterfeiting a tessera is a capital crime under both Tolvan and imperial law.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Reform Petition.** After a year of preparation, the Lethunu has formally put a Council proposal to replace the Tolar system with salaried imperial collectors in two Vylarian provinces, a pilot that would justify wider rollout if it succeeds. The Qethar'palu is mobilizing the Tolar class and the major _pêlêrênôs_ houses against it. Talkor of the Tolvan Vêldâs Ôrthêmarândis is signaling cautious openness to the pilot, itself a substantial shift. The vote will be close.
- **The Rôzimândôs's Cases.** _Hôlrôzimândôs_ Sextia Marcania has assembled simultaneous discipline cases against eight major Tolari in different Vylarian provinces, the largest coordinated prosecution in two generations. Their families are mounting a counter-attack through court connections, and the Talkor of the Tolvan is being pressed to slow the prosecution. Sextia refuses to slow. The outcome will entrench her position or end her career.
- **The Foreign-Patron Tolar.** On _rôzimândôs_ investigation, a Tolar holding a large Heliónite customs contract has been found quietly receiving payments from a trading interest tied to the Vezâkûnî, payments calibrated to make certain Tarûzelian goods pass customs at favorable assessments. The arrangement is technically not treasonable, since the Tolar collects what the contract requires, but it is plainly corrupt. Prosecuting would expose a diplomatic embarrassment, and not prosecuting would, if it leaks, hand the Reformist Faction a propaganda windfall.
- **The Assessment Riot.** An especially aggressive Tolar in the upper Vylarian river valley has provoked, by his methods, a peasant uprising large enough to threaten provincial order. The provincial _thêrkhôs_ (governor) has called for legions, the Talkor of the Tolvan has called for the Tolar's removal, and the Ôrzâs's standing position is that order must be restored before reforms are discussed. The Reformist Faction is making the obvious political case in every drawing room in Ôrzâra. The Tolar's family is influential and his contract is large.
- **The Karvan's Margin.** Internal investigation has found that a provincial Karvan has been quietly enlarging his contract-cut over several years by manipulating revenue estimates. The pattern is suggestive and not conclusive, and the Karvan's defense is that estimates reflect judgment and his have erred on the favorable side without bad intent. The Talkor of the Tolvan must rule on whether to bring formal charges, accept the Karvan's resignation quietly or close the investigation.
- **The Vanishing Tessera.** Someone has stolen a brass collection-tessera from a Tolar's office in a Heliónite port. Counterfeit tesserae copying the stolen original's design have begun to circulate, and collections have begun under their authority. The Karvan of the affected territory has alerted the _rôzimândoi_ of the Tolvan, but identifying the counterfeiter without finding the original is hard. The Tolar whose tessera was stolen is under investigation: he may be the counterfeiter himself, or the victim of someone who wanted him implicated. Resolution within the contract period is essential.
