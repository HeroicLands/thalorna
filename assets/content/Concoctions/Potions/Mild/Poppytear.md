A pale, cream-colored liquid with a slight viscosity, swirling gently when tilted despite its stillness. The aroma is sweet and cloyingly familiar—poppy fields in late summer, hay warming in the sun. When tasted, it coats the tongue with a syrupy numbness that spreads slowly across the mouth and throat. Herbalists prize this as a mercy for the wounded, a small mercy for those who would otherwise lie screaming through their darkest hours.

Within ten minutes of swallowing, the imbiber's perception of pain begins to fade like a distant memory. The body remains, but its suffering grows muted, almost unreal. One receives a bonus to Shock Rolls equal to the preparation's Success Value, and receives half that amount as a penalty to Move, Agility, and Agility-based skills. The effects persist for roughly four hours as the potion's potency gradually diminishes. Distant pain—remembered wounds, old griefs—also seems to lose its edge under this draft's influence.

Physicians and battlefield surgeons keep vials of this potion close at hand, though many cultures restrict its sale by law. The dependency it can foster is well-known and feared.
