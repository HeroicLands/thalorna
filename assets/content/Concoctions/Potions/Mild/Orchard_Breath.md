This pale golden liquid catches the eye like honey strained through silk, its surface reflecting candlelight with a subtle opalescence. The fragrance is immediately apparent even when the stopper is firmly in place, a pleasant floral note with hints of citrus and the green freshness of herbs picked in early morning. When applied to the skin as a perfume, the scent blooms outward in waves, attractive and present without being overwhelming—the kind of fragrance that makes those nearby lean in slightly, drawn by something pleasant they cannot quite name.

For four hours following application, the wearer benefits from a SV bonus to Charm tests that depend on attraction and persuasion. The scent itself does no magic, but it enhances the wearer's natural presence, making them seem more vivid and memorable, easier to like and easier to trust. Conversations flow more smoothly; offers seem more reasonable; the wearer's smile seems warmer. The aromatic is particularly effective for merchants, advocates, and those seeking favorable negotiations where genuine personal connection proves advantageous. The effect is subtle enough that most do not consciously recognize it, attributing their improved mood to the wearer's natural charisma rather than to perfumed chemistry.

Mild aromatics are staples of both personal grooming and courtly preparation. They are affordable enough for apprentices and small merchants to wear regularly, yet refined enough for nobility to dab on before formal functions. Their accessibility and gentle efficacy make them perhaps the most commonly purchased of all apothecary concoctions in civilized lands.
