Clear as water with a faintly greenish tint, this emetic has a smell like spoiled herbs and acidic fruit—the kind of aroma that makes the stomach anticipate trouble before the liquid even touches the tongue. On the palate it is bitter and almost metallic, with a texture that coats the mouth with the sensation of coming sickness. Those who know the preparation by reputation often drink it down quickly, in a single gulp, wanting to have it over with rather than prolonging the unpleasant taste. The vial is usually small and easily carried, as doses are measured in sips rather than drafts.

Approximately one to six rounds after swallowing, the mild emetic's effects manifest with uncomfortable clarity—the drinker finds themselves making a Shock Roll against Shock Index 7, but with a penalty equivalent to the preparation's SV. The body convulses in distress; the stomach empties violently; the world spins momentarily before settling back into uncomfortable clarity. Those who have suffered intentional or accidental poisoning find that the mild emetic's effects, while unpleasant, allow the body to expel potentially dangerous substances before they can cause serious harm. It is a rough and undignified cure, but an effective one.

Mild emetics are carried by physicians who treat poisoning cases, by nobles who fear assassination, and by herbalists dealing with rural populations where bad food and contaminated water are hazards of daily life. The potion is more humbling than dangerous; the worst it does is leave the sufferer weak and shaken, but the body is cleaner for it.
