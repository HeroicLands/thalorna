Pale green as spring leaves, this poultice preparation has the texture of thin mustard and smells of fresh herbs mixed with rendered animal fat and alum powder. When applied to an injury, the preparation cools the surrounding flesh, drawing heat from inflammation and fever alike. The patient experiences an immediate sensation of relief, as though a hand has lifted slightly from pressing down on their wound. The paste dries quickly, forming a protective seal that discourages infection while allowing the injury to breathe.

Applied as a poultice to a single injury, the mild curative grants an SV bonus to both the Healing and Infection Rolls throughout the injury's recovery period. The preparation works quietly and without drama, simply supporting the body's natural healing impulses. A gash that might take weeks to close becomes one that shows visible improvement within days; a puncture that might become infected becomes one that stays clean and closes properly. The mild curative is the standard preparation used by healers in ordinary practice, the one that occupies shelf space in every apothecary, and the one that most common people have had pressed onto some childhood scrape or childhood wound.

Mild curatives are inexpensive and reliable, and for routine injuries they are often preferred to more potent preparations simply because they are sufficient to the task. A merchant caravan might carry dozens of them; a village healer applies it as casually as a baker might apply flour. Its accessibility and dependability make it the foundation of competent field medicine.
