This legendary preparation is nearly transparent, a barely-golden distillation of the apothecary's art, held in a vial of such fine craftsmanship that the glass seems to glow from within. The scent is subtle – neither the burned-herb reek of milder antivenins nor any mere chemical tang, but rather the crystalline clarity of pure efficacy, faint as the smell of starlight. A single droplet costs what a laborer earns in a week, and herbalists guard their recipes for the great preparation with the jealousy of dragons guarding gold.

The moment the great antivenin passes the lips, its quadrupled potency – four times the Success Value – seizes the venom's progress with the force of an iron hand. A single round of combat passes – perhaps ten heartbeats – and already the darkening flesh begins to pale toward health again, the tremors cease entirely, and the victim draws breath as though an anvil has lifted from their chest. This is the preparation that snatches someone back from the threshold of death in the space of a breath, the one that turns a certain funeral into a probable recovery. It is the antivenin of legends, the one that transforms a desperate situation into a mere crisis.

The great antivenin is the province of richly-endowed temple houses, royal treasuries, and adventuring companies of significant renown. Its rarity ensures that its very presence at a table or in a satchel signals serious business – and serious resources. Few herbalists ever prepare it; fewer still succeed in crafting a batch worth keeping.
