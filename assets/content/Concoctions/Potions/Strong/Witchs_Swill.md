Murky yellow-brown, this concentrate has the viscosity of thin oil and smells like the distilled essence of everything repugnant – bile, rotten fruit, and chemicals that burn the nostrils. Even uncorking the vial makes the stomach flex in anticipation of violence. The taste is almost impossibly bitter, accompanied by a burning sensation that leaves the mouth raw and sensitive. Those who must drink the strong emetic typically steel themselves beforehand, knowing that the next few minutes will be among the most physically unpleasant of their lives. Drinking it straight is an act of will; it is sometimes disguised in wine or ale to make it slightly more tolerable.

Within one to six rounds of ingestion, the strong emetic forces a Shock Roll against Shock Index 7 with a doubled penalty – SV ×2 applied against the victim's resilience. The body does not merely empty; it convulses with profound distress. Drinkers report that it feels as though the stomach is attempting to expel not just its contents but the drinker themselves. Recovery from the immediate effects takes time – weakness, shakiness, and a powerful thirst characterize the aftermath. However, the body is thoroughly purged of any ingested poison or toxin, and poisons that might otherwise have been lethal become merely unpleasant memories and a terrible afternoon.

Strong emetics are deployed in cases where swift action against serious poisoning is critical – instances where the victim swallowed a deadly substance less than a minute ago. Their terrible efficacy is reserved for genuine emergencies, not routine stomach disturbance. An apothecary carrying strong emetics is signaling that they treat desperate cases.
