Brilliant amber-gold in color, this concentrated antivenin catches the light like liquid honey strained through cloth of gold, its surface catching lamplight with an almost oily sheen. The preparation smells sharply of distilled vinegar and crushed asps' teeth, potent enough to clear the sinuses at first whiff. A single sip burns the throat, but the sensation is almost welcome – the body recognizes immediately that it has received medicine of consequence. The vial is smaller than its mild counterpart, each drop more costly and more precious.

Minutes after ingestion, the strong antivenin's power floods the bloodstream with urgent force. Within a single minute, the venom's killing progress falters as the preparation's doubled potency – an SV multiplier of two – grips the Course Roll and wrestles the poison toward stalemate. Veins that were darkening arrest their discoloration; tremors that threatened paralysis become mere quivers; the desperate patient can draw a breath that feels clean and unburned. Veteran herbalists often reserve this strength for cases where time is truly of the essence: a companion bitten at dusk and racing the sun, or a merchant whose lifeblood is seeping away with each hour's passage.

The strong antivenin carries a steep price, marking its user as someone with access to wealth or the patronage of a substantial settlement. Adventurers and scouts who traverse truly dangerous territories often carry one or two vials, insurance against the scales and fangs of the wild.
