## Overview

Every party that goes south through the gate of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]] is going to Shelem Ish'bal, the Land of the Dead Jaguar, and most of them do not come back. It is the waste that fills the southern end of the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]], from the threshold of [[place-qapteltzaklrgn|Q'aptel Tsa'kul Region]] to the frozen sea at the bottom of the world. No one rules it, no one counts its people, and no road crosses it. The [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]] keeps its gate and keeps a cord of everyone who passes, and that is the whole of the civilized world's hold on it.

The waste is a proving ground. Warriors and priests go into it to show their worth before the gods, treasure-seekers go for the ruins and the strange materials that come out of it, and the desperate go because the north holds nothing for them. Those who come back are changed, and the pyramid-cities raise them high or keep them at the edges, and seldom anywhere between.

## The Land

At its kindest the waste is a hot desert of gravel, creosote and dry lakes, with mountains standing pale on every horizon and water a week apart. That is the best of it. The rest is worse, and not all of it is dry: there are forests where the trees are sick and the air is thick with spores, and swamps where the water is sweet and drives men mad. Returners who have seen both say that a party in the forest or the swamp prays for the desert.

The returners' accounts knotted at the gate divide the waste into five countries. The boundaries are the priests' and the returners', and nothing on the ground marks them.

| Country                               | Lies                                | What it is                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| [[place-ashcountry\|The Ash Country]] | Directly south of the gate          | Ash terraces, geysers, black glass and burning ground, below a line of dead and living mountains |
| [[place-thethirst\|The Thirst]]       | South-west, along the western coast | The kindest country, a hot desert of gravel and dry lakes where distances lie                    |
| [[place-sickforest\|The Sick Forest]] | East, beyond the Ash Country        | A rotting forest of house-sized fungi, glowing valleys and spores                                |
| [[place-deadwater\|The Dead Water]]   | South-east, along the great rivers  | Swamp, reed and mud, fevers and the sweet water                                                  |
| [[place-longwinter\|The Long Winter]] | The far south                       | Ice, snow and knife-edged wind under a winter that does not end                                  |

Most parties see the Ash Country and the edge of the Thirst and nothing more. The Long Winter is months beyond the gate, and the cords hold few accounts from anyone who has stood in it.

## The Sky and the Distances

The southern sky is seldom the color of a sky. From the gate's towers it shows a sickly yellow by day and a bruised purple when storms stand on the horizon, and inside the waste it goes further: a green noon, a white night with no moon in it, a red that lasts for days. Storms gather on the southern horizon without end. In the Thirst there is a basin, [[place-thebruise|the Bruise]], where the sky is purple every hour of every day.

Distance in the waste is not to be trusted. A mountain that stands a day away stands a day away for a week, and two parties that walked the same ground come back to the gate disagreeing by ten days about how long it took. Returners steer by the sun, by counting their steps and by the knots on a cord paid out behind them, and never by the look of a landmark. Below the threshold [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]] computes no day-table at all, and the gate's own tables mark most of their days as answers the house will not vouch for.

## Ways to Die

The waste has more ways to kill than any traveler can guard against at once:

- **Thirst.** Some countries hold no water at all, and a party carries what it drinks or dies.
- **Water.** Most of what water there is is poison. A drinker from an unknown source often goes mad or wastes away over weeks.
- **Cold.** In the Long Winter, and on some nights in every country, breath freezes solid and the air cuts like a blade.
- **Fire.** Geysers of scalding water and steam break from the ground without warning, and in the Ash Country fire comes up out of the earth itself.
- **Spores.** Fungi the size of houses release spores that bring madness or death to whoever breathes them.
- **The ground.** Sinkholes, crusts over boiling mud, ash that will not bear a man's weight and ash-storms that bury a man where he stands.
- **The beasts.** [[being-ashcairn|Kulikok]] with armor plates the size of a man's head, [[being-onemore|Shush'ikul]] that hunt together, [[being-jarkeeper|Ne'lumal]] that are neither snake nor lizard, and things stranger still, part flesh and part something else.
- **The places.** The waste holds thin spots where the world behaves as it should not, and houses and courts that set their own rules and take their own prices.

The plants are as bad. Thorny shrubs with spines that glow faintly in the dark grow wherever there is water, and whole valleys are carpeted with luminous fungi that light the night a sickly green.

## What Comes Out

What a returner carries home sells in the northern markets for prices that bankrupt lords: crystals that glow with their own light, metals that never rust, and organic matter that no healer or scholar can name. Every piece is priced by its rarity and by the blood it cost, and the blood cost is enormous. The treasure-seekers go for the ruins said to lie buried beneath ice and ash, which rumor fills with cities of fantastic architecture, devices whose purposes are forgotten, and wealth in forms that no longer exist.

## Who Goes In, and What They Swear

A warrior bound south by oath swears to reach a place, and the gate priests ask him at his return whether he reached it. The southern cities rank the oaths by distance:

1. **The foot of [[place-ashstair|the Ash Stair]]**, the first day's descent. Many reach it, and a southern lord who names it as the place of his oath is not much honored.
2. **A house of [[place-sevenhouses|the Seven Houses]]**, or all seven in order, which is the oath of the boldest.
3. **[[place-mirrormountain|Mirror Mountain]]**, where warriors go to see their deaths.
4. **[[place-playinghouse|the Playing House]]**, where a challenger may play for a boon, a life or a name.
5. **[[place-xelemixbal2|the Dead Jaguar]]**, the place the waste is named for, which almost no one claims to have stood on.

Priests go under vows to see the south, treasure-seekers go for what they can carry, and men under a doom go because they have to. The Blood-Denied, whom [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] have barred from the calendar and the funerary rites, go out through the south gate on any morning they choose. Many of them hold that a death in the waste reaches the [Eternal Garden]{.name data-type="place/site"} of [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]] without any priest's help, and the dream of that death draws more of them south than any treasure.

## What Is Said of It

Every account of the waste's making is somebody's, and none is the land's own. The priests of the cities teach that the jaguar of a lost age's sun was thrown down in the south, and that the waste is its body, still cooling; [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] keeps other accounts besides, and does not teach them to the cities. Some priesthoods whisper that the gods turned their backs on this ground. Others call it a living punishment, a scar that refuses to heal.

The death-walkers of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] hold that there is no road shrine and no reciter past the gate, so a soul that dies in the waste faces the passage below unguided. So they recite every chamber of the underworld to the living, in a cell under the gate, on the night before a party leaves.

## The Coast

The waste has a long coast on both oceans and no harbor on either. No Ki'ich'chik vessel sails past the threshold, the coast offers no water and no anchorage, and the southern coast at [[place-iceshore|the Ice Shore]] is ice. Nothing lands on the waste from the sea, and nothing leaves it.

## The Country's Quarrel

No one lives in the waste to quarrel over it, and the bodies that send people into it quarrel in its place. [[affiliation-tsumulob|The Blood-Workers]] go south with the expeditions from their garrison at [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]], and beyond the cords their Bearer of the Tables reads days the Star House will not vouch for, and the band speaks its workings on them at its own risk. The Severers wait at the gate for every band's field ledger, and hold that a working spoken on a closed day is a closed-day working however far from home it was spoken. The Blood-Workers answer that a band in the waste works on its best reckoning or dies. The returners are the second quarrel. The gate gives each one a _tq'anil_, the southern cities raise him high or keep him at the edges, and the Severers look hardest at the returners who went out of the Blood-Denied camp with no priest to name the gods. The accounts the returners give at the gate belong to the gate, which shares them only with the worthy and the desperate, and every party bound south wants them.

## Hooks

- **A witness for the ledger.** The Examiner of Ledgers of the Severers wants an outsider beside a band of the Blood-Workers on its next march south, to see what its Bearer of the Tables reads each dawn and what the band speaks. He asks a party to join the band as hired porters. The band's captain knows what porters are for.
- **The lost ledger.** A band came back through the south gate with half its men and without its field ledger, which its Keeper carried and the Ash Country kept. The Captain of the Southern Garrison wants the ledger found and brought back before the Severers ask for it, and he hires a party with no reason to read it.
- **A name for the returned.** A woman of the Blood-Denied camp went out of the south gate alone and came back through it a season later, and the gate priests have knotted her account. No priest will give her a tq'anil, because the Severers say the sentence still stands on her. [Ts'ultel Ne']{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Ch'alish Tq'an of the gate, has not decided, and she asks a party that saw her come back to speak for her.
