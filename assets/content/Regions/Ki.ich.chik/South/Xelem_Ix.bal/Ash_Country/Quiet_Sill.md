## Overview

Four days below [[place-ashstair|the Ash Stair]], past [[place-boilingground|the Boiling Ground]], a party walking through [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]] comes to a road. It is not a road: it is a band of smooth gray as wide as a road, flush with the ground, that comes up out of the ash at one end and runs a mile before the ash closes over it again. The returners call it the Quiet Sill. Its name in the priests' tongue is Ish'lan Sh'anik, the quiet road.

The gray is one body without a seam or a crack, swelling and hollowing in long, true, shallow runs, like a riverbed with the water gone from it. It is the only clean thing in the Ash Country. Ash will not lie on it. It falls on the Sill as it falls everywhere, and by the next breath of wind it has slid off the edges, so that the gray is bare when the drifts on either side are deeper than a man.

## Walking It

The returners' accounts tell every party to find the Sill, and every party that can find it walks on it. It is the only sure footing in the ash: no crust over a boiling pool, no sinkhole, no drift to bury a sleeper. It is faintly warm through a boot. A party that sheltered on it through an ash-storm came through unburied, standing in a mile-long trench of clean gray between two walls of drift.

And it is quiet. A footstep on the Sill makes no sound at all, nor does a dropped pot or a stamped heel, though a voice carries on it as anywhere. Parties who have walked the half-day along it say the silence of their own feet is the thing they remember, and that they spoke less on the Sill than anywhere else in the waste.

Nothing marks it. Returners have swung axes at it, struck it with obsidian and steel, and lit fires on it, and it shows no scratch, no chip and no soot. Its edges are sharp against the ash, with nothing cut or scratched on the gray itself, and the boulders beside it are scratched over with the names of every party that has passed.

## The Death-Walkers' Warning

The death-walkers of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]], who recite the underworld to each party the night before it leaves the gate, add a warning about the Sill to their recitation. They hold that it is the roof of a road the dead walk below, and that its silence is the dead listening. They tell a party to say nothing on the Sill that it would not want carried down to [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]], and never to speak a companion's name there.

## Hooks

- **The cuttings.** A factor of [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]], with Ankarian silver behind him, holds a grant from the Q'aptelmah to whatever comes off the Quiet Sill, and pays by weight. Three crews he sent have come back with nothing. He is outfitting a fourth, with better steel, and wants a party to lead it.
- **The name.** A member of the party's company spoke a dead friend's name on the Sill, and the death-walkers at the gate will not recite for him again until he has gone back and unsaid it there.
- **The other party.** A party sheltering on the Sill in an ash-storm finds another party's camp already there, its fire laid and its packs tied, and nobody in it.

# The Truth of the Quiet Sill {#truth .secret}

Everything the returners say of the Sill is true: ash will not lie on it, the gray is warm, and a footstep on it is silent. It is gray and gives a little under a hand laid flat, so it is not dead.

What lies under the ash at its two ends cannot be established. Returners have dug at both ends to the depth of a man and found the gray still running on under the ash. Nobody has dug further, and the ash fills a pit in a day.

The factor's grant is worthless. No tool takes anything from the Sill, and no crew will ever bring back a piece of it. The death-walkers' teaching is theirs, and nothing on the Sill confirms or denies it.

> [!TIP]
> The Sill is a rest for a party that has had none since the gate. Let the silence of their feet unsettle them. The empty camp is whatever the GM needs it to be, but it is not the Sill's doing.
