## Overview

The Bruise is a broad, shallow basin in [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]], a few days' walk across, and over it the sky is purple. It is purple at noon and at midnight, the deep, mottled purple of a week-old bruise, darker at the center and yellowing toward the rim, and no sun or moon shows through it. Light comes from the whole sky at once. The priests of the gate call it H'ulal Ts'upal, the Ugly Sky, and the returners call it the Bruise.

## Inside the Rim

The basin's floor is ordinary desert, gravel and brush and a dry wash or two, and nothing about the ground warns a traveler that he has crossed the rim. The sky changes over him in a few steps. Then the other changes show:

- **Shadows point inward.** Every shadow in the Bruise falls toward the center of the basin, whatever the hour, and so a party that steers by shadows walks in a spiral.
- **Sound carries wrong.** A shout from a companion at a man's elbow is a whisper, and a word spoken at the center of the basin is heard plainly at the rim, a day's walk off.
- **Nothing heals.** A cut taken in the Bruise stays open and wet as long as the wounded man stays there, and closes in the ordinary way once he leaves.
- **Nothing grows.** Hair and nails do not grow inside the rim, and neither does the brush, which stands the same from one party's account to the next.

The returners' accounts say the basin is safe to cross for a party that is not bleeding. They do not camp in it, because a traveler who sleeps under the purple sky wakes not knowing how long he has slept, and because the voices of other parties come across the basin at night, very clear, saying ordinary things.

# The Truth of the Bruise {#truth .secret}

The behaviors the returners list are real and constant, and none of them harms a traveler directly. The danger of the Bruise is in its combinations: a party that loses its way by its shadows walks in toward the center while its wounds stay open, and the beasts of the Thirst follow blood.

> [!TIP]
> A character who sleeps in the Bruise wakes after one to six days, with that much water gone. The voices at night are other travelers' words, from the rim or from parties that crossed in other seasons, and they can be used to drop a rumor or a warning into a camp.
