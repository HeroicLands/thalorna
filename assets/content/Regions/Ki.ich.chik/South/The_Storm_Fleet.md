## The Storm Fleet

Under the temple of [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]] at [[place-tzalamne|Ts'alam Ne']] lies a harbor basin that no merchant enters. In it the war canoes of the temple lie in a row, long and painted, each with a feathered serpent along its prow and its tail coiled at the stern, and their crews sleep in the long sheds behind them. When a temple's cargo leaves the port, two or three of them go with it.

The **Storm Fleet**, _H'ulshok Ne'kal_ in Ki'i'chek, the storm on the sea, is the temple's fleet: the priestly navy of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]]'s great port. It escorts the cargoes the temples own along the coast, watches every cargo that comes into the harbor for the priesthood's levy, and is the only power on the coast that the Ne'kalmah, the city's lord of the sea, cannot count.

## The Shrines and the Day

The fleet sails the way every ship of Ts'alam Ne' sails, from one shore shrine of [[affiliation-xilanixlan|Faith of Shilan Ish'lan]] to the next, and never out of sight of one. Each dawn its **Steersman** watches for the day called at the nearest headland, and a canoe that cannot see a shrine at dawn has lost the day and puts in to the nearest beach to wait. The fleet sails only on days the temple's tables open to the storm god, and before the first voyage of each season the **Binder of the Coast** speaks the binding of storms over the coast on an opened day, with the temple's blood behind it.

The shore shrines carry the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]]'s slow post along the coast, and the fleet carries it between them: a temple canoe that passes a shrine takes on whatever the keeper has waiting for the next.

## Membership

The paddlers are mostly **Vowed Paddlers**: faithful of the storm god who have pledged a season at the paddle against a debt of blood, Debt-Bearers in [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]]'s ladder, and who go home when the season ends with the debt paid. Fishermen's sons vow seasons at the paddle the way inland boys vow pilgrimages. The **Temple Crewmen** are sworn to the temple for life and are the fleet's fighters. The Steersmen and Captains are ordained priests of the storm god's branch, the water-priests and serpent-keepers, and the **Captain of the Storm Fleet** is a Ch'alish of the branch, named by the temple's Ch'alish Tq'an.

The present Captain is [[being-kushiqaptel|K'ushi Q'aptel]], a quiet, heavy-shouldered priest who has steered the coast from end to end and who reads cords better than any Keeper of the Levy the temple has had.

A crewman who steals from a temple cargo, or lets one he was set to watch go unwatched, is **Put Ashore**: set on the nearest beach with nothing, and never taken aboard a temple canoe again.

## The Levy

The priesthood takes its share of every cargo that comes into Ts'alam Ne', and the **Keeper of the Sea Levy** takes it before the cargo is landed. The fleet's canoes meet the trading canoes in the harbor mouth, count what they carry against the cords, and take the share then and there. The Ne'kalmah's fleet pays the levy as every other cargo pays it, and it is the cargo the Keepers watch most closely.

## Rivals

**The Ne'kalmah.** [[being-chakulish|Ch'akul Ish]], the lord of the sea, keeps the city's fleet for the Ts'enmah and keeps its cords himself, and the inspectors of the capital count what he shows them. The Storm Fleet counts what it sees. The two fleets share a harbor and escort different cargoes, and the Captain of the Storm Fleet holds that the Ne'kalmah's own cords are not the only ones that count his ships.

**The road priests.** The fleet cannot sail without the shore shrines, and the shrines belong to the road priests. A keeper who is late to call the day holds a temple canoe on a beach, and the road branch knows it.

## What a Party Can Ask of Them

- **Passage under the temple.** A temple canoe takes pilgrims and the servants of temples along the coast, and a party in a temple's service sails under the storm god's protection, which no one on the coast willingly crosses.
- **An escort.** A cargo that pays a gift to the temple may sail with a temple canoe beside it. The canoe watches the cargo for the levy as closely as it guards it.
- **A season at the paddle.** A party member with a debt of blood to clear may vow a season as a Vowed Paddler.
- **A hidden cargo.** The Captain of the Storm Fleet pays well for proof of a cargo that came into the harbor without being counted, and pays best for proof of one of the Ne'kalmah's.
