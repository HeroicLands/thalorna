## The Steel Kin

The steel of [[place-nekiahkul|Ne' Ki'ahkul]] is gray with a faint ripple in it, like water over stones, and a blade of it bends and comes back where an obsidian edge would shatter. Every warrior of the south who can afford one carries it, and every warrior who cannot wants one. Six kin groups of the town know how it is made, and nobody else does.

The **Steel Kin**, _Kul'iqal Nalash_ in Ki'i'chek, the family of the steel, are those six kin groups: the iron and steel smiths of the town's lower half, who make the blades and breastplates of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]]'s warriors. The largest of the six is led by [[being-toshelneok|Toshel Ne'ok]], a small, scarred woman with no patience for visitors, whose kin group makes the breastplates of the realm's best warriors and who speaks for all six.

## Membership

A smith of the Steel Kin is born into one of the six, or marries into one. Each kin group owns its forges and teaches its own children, and the six marry mostly among themselves, so that most smiths of the lower half are cousins of most others. A husband or wife from outside learns the forge but not the last part of the making, which is taught only to a **Master Smith**, and a kin group's head decides who that is.

Every forge in the town is lit from the fire on the temple of [[lore-kayikchalixdty|K'ayik Ch'alish]]. When one goes out, the kin group's **Bearer of the Coal** carries a covered jar up the temple stair, pays the fire-keepers, and brings the coal down again. The guild's gravest sentence uses this. A smith who taught the steel to an outsider, or sold steel without his kin's mark, is a **Cold Forge**: the council asks the fire-keepers to give him no coal, they give him none, and no smith in the town lends him a light.

## Standing

In the town the Steel Kin stand high. A master smith is honored above most of the highborn in the street, and the town's hereditary lord, the Pel'ushmah, deals with the six heads as near equals, because the town's tribute is their steel. The capital's inspectors weigh each kin group's steel against the cords every year, and a kin group whose weight falls short pays the difference before it sells anything.

The steel beyond the weight is the kin group's own. It goes down to [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]] on the town's own porters' backs, each piece struck with the mark of the kin group that made it, and the merchants of the market town sell it on to every city of the south. A piece without a mark is steel that should have been in the Ts'enmah's weight.

## Rivals

**The upper half.** The gold and silver workers and the obsidian cutters of the upper town work for the temples, and they hold that their work stands first at the fire of K'ayik Ch'alish. The Steel Kin hold that the temple's fire lights more of their forges than of anyone's. The two halves quarrel over the order in which their Bearers climb the stair.

**The inspectors.** The inspectors who come up from the capital weigh, count and write, and they are never satisfied. The Speaker of the Six meets them at the edge of the town and walks them to each forge in turn.

**The merchants of Ne' P'ashel.** The market town's merchants want to buy at the forges, not at their own market after the porters have carried the steel down. The Steel Kin will not sell at the forges, and a buyer who climbs to them is usually turned away at the first door.

## What a Party Can Ask of Them

- **A blade or a breastplate made to measure.** A master smith makes one for a buyer he accepts, after his kin group's weight is met, for a price paid half before the first heat. It takes a season.
- **Marked steel.** A party buys steel at Ne' P'ashel, where every piece carries a kin group's mark and the price is the merchants' price.
- **Repair.** A smith of the six mends steel of the six's making, and he looks at the mark before he looks at the damage.
- **Unmarked steel.** A party that finds unmarked steel on sale anywhere in the south has found a theft from the Sun Lord's weight, and the Speaker of the Six pays to learn who sold it.

:::secret {title="The sixth kin"}
The smallest of the six kin groups is down to two old smiths, a brother and a sister, and neither has children. When they die, their part of the making dies with them unless they teach it, and the guild's rules let them teach it only to a husband or wife of their kin. The other five heads have each put forward a marriage. Toshel Ne'ok's candidate is her own grandson, and the other four have noticed.
:::
