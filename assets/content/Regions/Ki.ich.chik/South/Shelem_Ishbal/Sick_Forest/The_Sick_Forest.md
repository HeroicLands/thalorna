## Overview

The Sick Forest, Shikesh Ts'ik'al, lies east of [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]], a forest as broad as a realm in which every tree is dying and nothing quite dies. Returners who have been in both say they would cross the Ash Country twice before they would cross the Sick Forest once. It is wet where the rest of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]] is dry, warm where the rest is hot or freezing, and full of water nobody can drink.

## The Land

From the edge of the ash the forest looks like any lowland forest: a green wall of trees with a haze over it. Inside, the trees are wrong. Their bark is soft and comes away in the hand, their leaves are spotted gray and yellow, and every trunk is grown over with shelves and caps and threads of fungus. Between the trees stand fungi of their own, pale domes and stalks as tall as a house, and the floor is a deep, wet mat of rot that gives under the foot. The air is still and thick and smells sweet.

Water stands everywhere, in pools and channels and hollows, warm and brown and full of threads. The returners' accounts say none of it is fit to drink, and a party carries its water into the Sick Forest as it would into a desert.

Its landmarks are [[place-greenmoonlit|the Green Moonlight]], the valleys where the forest floor glows at night, and [[place-palecity|the Pale City]], plazas and a pyramid of stone wrapped in white fungus.

## Ways to Die

The spores kill most. The great fungi release them in clouds when they are touched, when the wind rises, or for no reason anyone can see, and a cloud of them looks like smoke hanging in the trees. A traveler who breathes them sickens within a day: some grow feverish and die in a week, coughing up gray threads, and others go mad, wander off the path and are found sitting quietly against a tree, with fungus already growing on their arms. The miasma cloths sold at the gate help a little, wetted and bound twice over the mouth and nose.

The forest's beasts are its own. Its packs are smaller and quieter than the ones in the ash, and they hunt from the trees. Things move through the rot that the returners describe as part animal and part fungus, slow, and very hard to kill. And the trees themselves fall without warning, rotted through, and bring down others with them.

## What the Returners Say

Few accounts at the gate come from the Sick Forest, and those that do are short. They agree that a party must keep moving, must never sleep under the great fungi, and must burn the clothes it wore in the forest before it reaches the gate. The gate's priests burn them for any returner who did not.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Sick Forest quarrels over what may leave it. The priests of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]] burn the clothes of every returner from the forest who did not burn them himself, and they burn with the clothes whatever of the forest the returner carries. The northern markets pay for organic matter that no healer or scholar can name, and the herbalists of [[place-chalpuuts|Ch'al Pu'uts]] pay most for anything that grew in the forest, so a returner from the Sick Forest comes up to the gate with something hidden on him more often than not. The gate holds that it is keeping the spores on the far side of its wall. The buyers hold that it is keeping the price for itself.

## Hooks

- **A cap for the herbalists.** A family of herbalists of Ch'al Pu'uts pays for one cap of the great fungi brought north whole and sealed in a jar. It asks a party bound south to bring one, and to get it past the burning at the gate.
- **The one who slipped by.** A returner from the forest came in through the south gate in the dark, before the priests could burn his clothes, and is somewhere in the court. The gate priests want him found before he reaches the flats, and he is coughing.
- **The man against the tree.** A returner left a companion sitting quietly against a tree in the forest, with fungus already growing on his arms. He asks a party to go back with him and bring the man out, or make certain he is dead.
