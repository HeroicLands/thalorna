## Overview

The Ash Country, K'aypukal (the ash-bed) in the priests' tongue, is the first country of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]] and the only one most parties ever see. It begins where the guides of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]] turn back, at the place the gate's towers drop below the horizon, and runs south for weeks along a line of mountains, some dead and some still breathing smoke. Gray ash lies over everything, a hand deep on the ridges and deeper than a man in the hollows, and under the ash is black glass, red rock and ground that is hot to the touch.

## The Land

A party comes down into the Ash Country by [[place-ashstair|the Ash Stair]], a day's descent of ash terraces from the high ground of the threshold. Below the Stair the land is a broken plain of ash and old lava flows, cut by canyons and dotted with cones. The mountains stand to the west, and at night the living ones glow red at their tops. The air smells of sulfur and hot iron, and it tastes of it too.

Water is rare and almost always bad. The pools that steam in the hollows are scalding and thick with minerals, and the springs that run cool are the ones the returners warn against most. Thorn shrubs grow at every seep, and their spines glow faintly at night, so a traveler who sees a line of small lights in the dark has found water, and should not drink it.

The country's landmarks are its dangers:

- **[[place-boilingground|The Boiling Ground]]**, a field of geysers and mud pots that open without warning, two days south of the Stair.
- **[[place-mirrormountain|Mirror Mountain]]**, a hill of standing slabs of obsidian where warriors go to see their deaths.
- **[[place-deadmountain|The Dead Mountain]]**, a cold cone among the hot ones, with a cavern at its foot where the [[being-oldfrdrg|Old Fire Dragon]] lairs.
- **[[place-quietsill|The Quiet Sill]]**, a mile of bare gray four days below the Stair, where ash will not settle and footsteps make no sound.
- **The first three of [[place-sevenhouses|the Seven Houses]]**, standing a few days apart along the line of the mountains.

## The Sky

The sky over the Ash Country is seldom blue. By day it is yellow or brown with ash, and when the wind drops it clears to a hard white. At night the cones light the undersides of the clouds red, and a storm on the southern horizon lights them purple. Ash falls like snow for a day at a time, and then a wind comes and lifts it all again.

## Ways to Die

The ash itself kills more than anything that lives in it. An ash-storm rises in an hour, fills the air so thickly that a man cannot see his hand, and lays down a drift that buries a sleeping party where it lies. The returners' rule is to keep walking through a storm, roped together, with cloth over the mouth, and never to lie down in it. Under the ash the ground is treacherous: a crust over a boiling pool looks like any other ash, and a sinkhole opens where a party stood the day before.

The beasts of the Ash Country are the waste's best known. Scorpion-things with plates the size of a man's head move across the ash at dusk, and packs of hairless dog-shaped things follow a party for days, waiting for the weakest to fall behind. The Old Fire Dragon hunts the whole country, and the glazed swathes it leaves across the ash, where fire has been breathed across it, run for miles.

## What the Returners Say

The accounts knotted at the gate agree on very little about the Ash Country, and on three things they always agree. The first is that a party that reaches the Boiling Ground has passed the worst of the ash, and most of the accounts end there. The second is that the dead mountain is to be left alone, unless a party has come to bring the dragon something. The third is that the ash keeps what it buries: returners speak of figures standing in the drifts below the Stair, still upright, still roped together, with the ash to their shoulders.

Some returners bring back black glass from Mirror Mountain and wear it at the throat, and in the southern cities a shard of it is the mark of a warrior who has seen his death. Others bring back crystals that glow from the canyons below the cones, which the gate's storehouses buy before they ever reach a northern market.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Ash Country quarrels over what comes out of it. The keepers of the storehouses at Shelem Ch'alish buy the crystals from the canyons below the cones at the gate's price before any northern market sees them, and the treasure-seekers who carried them out of the ash call that price theft. The merchants who back the treasure-seekers, in [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]] and the cities of the north, want the crystals carried past the gate, and the priesthood keeps the trade in what the waste yields restricted. The black glass of Mirror Mountain is the other quarrel. In the southern cities a shard of it at the throat is the mark of a warrior who has seen his death, and shards are sold in the camps outside the gate to men who never went farther than the foot of the Ash Stair.

## Hooks

- **Past the storehouses.** A factor of Ne' P'ashel has outfitted a party of treasure-seekers and wants whatever crystals they bring out of the canyons carried past the storehouses of Shelem Ch'alish and across the flats unseen. He asks the party to be the ones who carry them.
- **Bought glass.** A lord of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]] wears a shard of black glass at his throat and stands ahead of his rivals at every rite. A rival house is sure that he bought it at the gate and never passed the Ash Stair, and hires a party to find the man who sold it to him.
- **The roped figures.** A house of [[affiliation-kuxiixbal|K'ushi Ish'bal]] whose son went south with a roped party and was never knotted back has heard the returners speak of figures standing in the drifts below the Stair. It asks a party to go down the Stair, find the figures and bring back anything that names him.
