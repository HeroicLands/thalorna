## The Taking Societies

A warrior of Ish'ilankul's cities wears his captives. A man who has taken one has a red cord tied around his wrist with one knot in it, and he does not take it off to sleep. A man who has taken four wears a cloak of white cotton, and every captive he has taken is a small figure sewn into its border, so that an old warrior's cloak is crowded to the hem.

These are the two **Taking Societies** of the taking field at [[place-ishtalhun|Ishtal'hun]]: the **Red Cords**, _K'otsikbal_ in Ki'i'chek, and the **Patterned Cloaks**, _P'ilashbal_. Their members come from every city of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] and from noble houses far beyond it, and what admits a man to either is the same thing: a captive taken alive, unbroken, and presented to the Dedicator of Captives. A taker of the societies is a knight, a Ts'omilbal, raised for his captives, and the societies are where the knights of the plateau are made and judged.

## The Taking Field

The field below Ishtal'hun belongs to two bodies at once. [[affiliation-tsumulob|The Blood-Workers]] train their Takers there in the field workings, and the **Master of Ishtal'hun**, their officer, holds it for them. The societies train the youths of the wards and the younger sons of other cities there in the taking itself: to disarm a man, throw him and bind him without breaking him, with clubs of weighted wood and coils of rope. A boy enters the field from his ward's house of youths as a **Youth of the Field**, and he leaves it as a Red Cord on the day he brings a captive back from a hunt.

The two societies keep the law of the captive-hunt between them. Their Elders and the Master of Ishtal'hun sit together on the field after every war month, hear every claim against a taker, and cut the cord of any man who broke the law.

## The Law of the Captive-Hunt

The captive-hunt, the _ch'utik'at_, is war fought for captives, and its law is the societies' to keep.

**When.** A hunt is fought only in Shok'alal, the war month, and only on a day the city's tables open for war. Nobody takes a captive in the five door days at the year's end, and a captive taken on a closed day is sent home.

**Who may take.** Any free man under arms in a city's levy, from a ward member to a lord. A captive belongs to the man who first laid hands on him. When two men claim one captive, the Elders hear both and decide, and the man who loses the claim may not raise it again. A slave who takes a captive takes him for his lord.

**Who may be taken.** Only a man under arms on the field. A farmer at his field, a woman, a child, a priest, a runner of the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]] and a pilgrim on a white road may not be taken. A man who takes one of them is a Cut Cord, and the person he took goes home with a gift from his city.

**What follows the taking.** The taker brings his captive to the Dedicator of Captives of his city, vows over him, and the Dedicator assigns the captive to a coming rite. Until the Dedicator has assigned him, the captive's own house may ransom him, at a price the taker names, and a taker who refuses a fair ransom is held to have shamed his city. Once the captive is assigned to a rite, no ransom is heard.

**Killing.** A man who kills on the field where he could have taken is no taker. A captive killed is a working spilled, in the priests' words, and the societies hold it the meanest thing a warrior can do. A Red Cord who kills a man he could have bound is a Cut Cord.

### The Treaty-Hunt

Most captive-hunts between the cities of the plateau are treaty-hunts. Two Ts'enmah agree a hunt in advance: a field between their cities, marked at its corners with stones; the days of the war month on which it is fought; and the count of captives each side may take. Each Ts'enmah names a **Marshal of the Hunt**, a Patterned Cloak, and the two Marshals walk the field together on every day of the hunt and count the captives as they are bound. When a side has taken its count, its Marshal closes the field for it, and its warriors leave.

The treaty forbids five things. No side takes beyond its count, and a captive taken over the count is returned unharmed the same day. No warrior takes off the marked field. Nobody kills on a treaty field, and a man who kills there is given to the other side as a captive in the dead man's place. Nobody lays hands on a Marshal. And no side carries the hunt past the stones into the other city's fields or towns. A Ts'enmah whose warriors broke a treaty has no treaty-hunt offered him again until he has paid the other city for it, in captives of his own.

[[being-tshlishtmtsl|Toshel Ishtem Ts'ultel]], Ts'enmah of [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]], has taken eleven captives himself in the hunts against [[place-qokulqat|Q'o Kul'qat]], and wears the cloak to his court.

## Standing

A Patterned Cloak eats in his lord's hall and may hold a field from the Ts'enmah. A Red Cord is honored in his ward, carried high at its festivals, and listened to in its levy. The societies' Elders are received by every Sun Lord of the plateau, because every Sun Lord's war depends on his takers, and the Ts'enmah of Ishtal'hun lends the best of them to other cities as captains for their campaigns.

The Elder of the Patterned Cloaks is [[being-penqalneok|P'enqal Ne'ok]], a broad, gray warrior of Ishtal'hun's own wards, who has walked a treaty field as Marshal more often than anyone alive and whose cloak has no room left on its border.

## Rivals

**The Blood-Workers.** The order recruits its Takers from the hunts, and it takes the cleanest takers of the societies, who become priests and leave the societies' tables for good. The Elders call this the priesthood harvesting their field. The Master of Ishtal'hun calls it the field's purpose.

**Kill-war.** Captains who fight the peoples beyond the frontiers, who fight to kill, learn to kill back, and their men come home with no captives and no knots. The societies will not admit a man who learned his war there until he has taken a captive in a hunt, and they say so to the captains' faces.

**Each other.** A Red Cord with three knots wants his fourth, and in a hunt the Red Cords crowd forward to take before the Patterned Cloaks reach the man. The Marshals spend half of every treaty-hunt keeping the two societies off each other's captives.

## What a Party Can Ask of Them

- **Train.** A noble house of another city pays the societies to train its younger sons on the taking field, and a party with a noble patron may train there too, for a season, in the taking of men alive.
- **Hire a champion.** A side in a suit before [[affiliation-courtkeepers|the Keepers of the Court]] may hire up to three takers of the societies as its champions, and the Patterned Cloaks are the champions every side wants.
- **Walk a treaty field.** Two cities that distrust each other's Marshals sometimes ask an outsider to walk the field with them as a witness, and a party known to neither city may be asked.
- **Ransom a captive.** A house whose son was taken needs someone to reach the taker before the Dedicator assigns him, and to agree a price.
