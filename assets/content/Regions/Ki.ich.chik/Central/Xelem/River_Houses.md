# terran_analog: the merchant families of a river trade in feathers, hides and cacao, after the long-distance traders of Mesoamerica and the river towns of the Orinoco; the name joins p'ik'ul- (trade) to sh'ulum (a house)

## The River Houses

Every merchant family of [[place-tsalukul|Ts'alukul]] keeps a landing below its house, and when a canoe comes down from the forest the head of the family is standing on it. He does not send a kinsman. He looks at the hides, opens a bundle of feathers, cracks a cacao pod with his thumb, names a price and pays it there. A forest trader who sells to one landing comes back to that landing, and the families know which forest traders belong to whom.

The **River Houses**, _P'ik'ul Sh'ulum_ in Ki'i'chek, the houses of the trade, are the merchant families of the council towns of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]]. They buy what the jungle sells, jaguar hides, dried feathers, cacao and the medicinal plants of the deep forest, and they carry it downriver in their own dugouts and along the coast toward [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] and [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]]. Ts'alukul, the City of Rivers, has the most landings, and the greatest families are its families.

## Membership

A family is its head, its kin, its landing and its fleet. Its kin are born or marry into it, work its landing and its sheds, and share its profit. Its canoe-men are hired, paddle its dugouts and eat at its fire, and are not of its blood. A kinsman who shows judgment is made a **Factor**: sent upriver to buy from the forest peoples who keep their own leaders, or north to live in a market town and sell there. The head, the **P'ik'ulmah**, names his heir from among his kin, and the heir he names is usually the factor who sent home the best word.

A family's seat on its town council is held by its canoes and its trade. A family that loses its fleet, to a flood, a debt or a rival, loses its landing and its seat, and the families of the town do not lend it canoes to get them back.

## Standing

In Shelem the families rule. The heads of a town's families sit on its council with its priests, choose its speaker, the **Ts'omeshmah**, each year when the town renews its trade agreements with the north, and give the gifts by which the town's allegiance is renewed. Disputes between families are judged in the council house by the heads of the other families, and a family that will not accept the council's judgment finds no canoe-man in the town who will paddle for it.

In the north the families are traders, received in the courts of the Sun Lords as men with goods and news, and seated below every noble. Their factors in the northern markets are the first to hear what a court is buying, and they send it home by the Carried Road of the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]] and by their own canoes, whichever is faster on the day. A Sun Lord who wants to know what is happening in Shelem asks a factor, and a factor who wants a favor from a Sun Lord tells him.

## Rivals

**The merchant guild of Ish'ilankul.** The plateau's traders send caravans to Shelem under their Ch'eshmah, and they want to buy at the forest's edge, upriver of the landings. The families keep the rivers closed to them. A northern trader buys at a landing or does not buy, and a northern caravan that hires its own canoes upriver finds that no forest trader will sell to it twice.

**The Speaking Road.** The Road's relay stations on the rivers are paid by the families, which is how the Road reaches Shelem at all, and the families mean their payments to buy something. The Road's law says no runner may be delayed. The landings say the Road is very far from Kul'taq'an.

**Each other.** The families of a town compete for every forest trader, every buyer in the north and every seat on the council. This year the speaker of Ts'alukul is [[being-chakulneshlm|Ch'akul Ne' Shelem]], head of the family with the most dugouts on the river, and his rival on the council is [[being-nelushpuqem|Nelush Puq'em]], a narrow, quiet woman whose family has fewer dugouts and the best factor in [[place-tsamash|Tsa'mash]].

## What a Party Can Ask of Them

- **Passage.** A family's dugouts go down the rivers and along the coast to K'ankul every few days in the dry season, and a party that pays, or works a paddle, rides with the cargo.
- **A guide upriver.** The families' upriver factors know the forest peoples beyond the river towns, their leaders and their prices, and a family lends one to a party it means to profit from.
- **Goods.** Hides, feathers, cacao and the medicinal plants of the deep forest, at the landing price, which is the price the north never sees.
- **News.** What a family's factors have sent home from the northern courts, given in exchange for news of the same worth.

:::secret {title="The Tsa'mash bargain"}
Nelush Puq'em's factor in Tsa'mash has agreed with a trading house of the market there to sell it the family's feathers directly, by its own canoes and porters, at a price below every landing on the river. If the other families learn of it, the council of Ts'alukul will call it the opening of the river to the north and take her seat. If the bargain holds for a season, two other families have already said privately that they will make the same one.
:::
