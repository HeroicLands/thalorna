## Overview

Ts'alukul has no streets to speak of. It stands where four tributaries of [[place-hanaltzuma|the Misty River]] (Ha'nal Ts'uma) come together, on a scatter of raised mounds and banks between the channels, and its people go from one quarter to the next by canoe. In the dry season the channels are narrow and brown and the mounds stand high on banks of cracked mud. In the rains the river rises over the forest around the town until only the mounds and the treetops show, and the town becomes a set of islands in a moving lake. Ts'alukul, the City of Rivers, is a town of about 25,000 people in the [[place-xelemrgn|Shelem Region]], and the greatest of the river towns of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]].

## The Landings

Everything the jungle sells north passes through Ts'alukul. Canoes come down the four tributaries loaded with jaguar hides, bundles of dried feathers wrapped in leaves, sacks of cacao and sealed jars of medicinal plants, and the master merchants of the town buy them at the landings below their houses. Each merchant family keeps its own landing, its own sheds on stilts and its own fleet of long dugouts, and the family's head stands on the landing to meet the canoes from upriver himself. What the families buy goes on downriver in their own canoes, and then along the coast toward [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] and [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]].

The town is also the farthest station of the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]], the Speaking Road, on this river. Its runners are canoe-men, and the relay station stands on its own mound at the downstream end of the town with a drum hung outside it. A message from Ish'ilankul reaches it in three or four days, by the coastal road through K'ankul and then by river. Beyond Ts'alukul the Road does not go.

## The River Dolphins

Pink river dolphins live in the channels around the town, and the people of Ts'alukul will not harm them. The priests of the town teach that the dolphins guide canoes through bad water, and they read omens in which way a dolphin turns when it rises beside a boat. A canoe-man who sees one rise on his left before a journey waits a day.

## Who Governs

Ts'alukul is a council town. It is governed by a council of the heads of its merchant families, the P'ik'ulmah, and the priests of its temples, which meets in a long house on the largest mound. Each year, when the town renews its trade agreements and its gifts with the north, the council chooses a Ts'omeshmah, a speaker, who speaks for the town to the envoys from Ish'ilankul and K'ankul and holds the seat for that year alone. The speaker this year is [Ch'akul Ne' Shelem]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, head of the family with the most dugouts on the river, who has bargained for the town three times before. A merchant family holds its seat on the council by its canoes and its trade, and a family that loses its fleet loses its seat.

:::secret {title="The held runner"}
The runners of the relay station are paid by the merchant families. A master merchant who learns that a cord about prices or a buyer's need has come in from the north pays the station's keeper to hold the downstream runner for half a day, and in that half day his own canoes are on the water. The keeper shares the fee with two of his runners. No cord is opened, and the keeper calls the delay a rest for a tired runner. The Road's law forbids any delay to a runner, and the priests of the dispatch at [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]] do not know that it happens.
:::
