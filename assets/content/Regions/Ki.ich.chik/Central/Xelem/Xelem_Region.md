## Overview

Shelem Region is the land of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]], and lies within [[place-centralkiichchik|Central Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]].

Shelem, the "Land of Birds," is the eastern frontier of the central Ki'ich'chik heartland—a vast region of tropical rainforest, immense river systems, and pu'uts (sheer-walled, flat-topped mountains) that rise like islands from the jungle canopy. Where [[place-ixilankulrgn|Ish'ilankul]] is built on volcanic stone and [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul]] on limestone, Shelem is built on water. The mighty rivers that carve through its landscape—the [[place-hanaltzuma|Ha'nal Ts'uma]] and the [[place-hanalxilik|Ha'nal Shilik]], and a thousand smaller waterways—are more important than roads, more important than temples.

The quetzal bird—regarded as a manifestation of [[lore-tzenultqandty|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]], god of order, on the material plane—dwells in the cloud forests of Shelem in greater abundance than anywhere in Ki'ich'chik. Its feathers, along with those of other exotic birds (macaws, parrots, hummingbirds of remarkable colors), command prices that only the wealthiest nobles can afford. Jaguar hides, harvested from the apex predator that rules Shelem's jungle, are the mark of military rank and divine favor. Medicinal herbs—plants whose properties are understood through centuries of herbal lore—grow in Shelem's interior and are gathered by healers and herbalists whose knowledge is so jealously guarded that some have been protected by oath and blood oath to guard their secrets even unto death.

## Geography

Shelem encompasses the vast riverine plains and forested highlands of the eastern frontier. Two great rivers cross the region. The larger, the Ha'nal Ts'uma—the Misty River, named for the fog that lifts off its flooded forest at dawn—gathers so many tributaries that its network of waterways winds for thousands of kilometers through the landscape. Along it the forest floods each year and drains again, and between the flooded stands run navigable channels that join communities too far apart to reach on foot. The second, the Ha'nal Shilik or Blue River, lies to the east, where the plains begin to climb toward the highlands, and it carries the same traffic through country just as wild: canoes loaded with jaguar hides, dried feathers, cacao and sealed jars of medicinal plants, bound downriver for the markets of the north. Villages stand on the higher ground along both banks and take their fish, their water and their news from the current. These rivers are the primary highways; to navigate Shelem's interior is to navigate by water.

The landscape rises from the coastal lowlands and riverine plains eastward toward the highlands, where ancient plateaus and pu'uts create zones of isolated flora and fauna. These high plateaus are topped with vegetation found nowhere else in the known world, having evolved in isolation for millions of years. The flora and fauna of Shelem overall is notably distinct from that of the central heartland—the jaguar, the tapir, the caiman, the anaconda, various species of poison frogs, and bird species of remarkable diversity create an ecosystem that seems almost alien to the sensibilities of highland Ki'ich'chik visitors.

The climate is intensely hot and humid throughout the year, with heavy rainfall generating the great rivers' seasonal floods and maintaining the luxuriant vegetation. The seasonal flooding—which expands the navigable waterways and creates temporary islands—is a natural rhythm that has shaped Shelem settlement patterns since ancient times. Settlements are built on higher ground, and agricultural zones are arranged to exploit the flooding cycles rather than resist them. The jungle itself is regenerative and resilient, and the Ki'ich'chik inhabitants of Shelem have learned to harvest from it sustainably, taking what they need without depleting the extraordinary bounty of flora and fauna.

## City-States and Realms

The greatest of these include [[place-tsalukul|Ts'alukul]] (the "City of Rivers"), which straddles several tributaries and serves as a collecting point for jungle products; [[place-chalpuuts|Ch'al Pu'uts]] ("Beneath the Mesa"), located at the foot of a magnificent pu'uts and serving as a center for the most exotic trade goods; and several coastal settlements that connect to maritime trade networks.

## Trade

Shelem's trade is inseparable from its ecology. The great river systems are the trade routes, and the goods that flow along them are largely products of the jungle and the river. Jaguar hides and other precious furs are collected by hunters and exchanged through intermediary merchants to eventually reach the great markets of Ish'ilankul and K'ankul, where they command prices equivalent to entire estates. Feathers—particularly those of quetzals, macaws, and other colorful species—are gathered, dried, and prepared for shipment northward. The finest feathers for ceremonial use come from Shelem, and the merchant families that monopolize their collection have become fabulously wealthy.

Medicinal and psychoactive plants are harvested according to ancient protocols known only to initiated herbalists. The vision-vine, brewed for shamanic rituals throughout Shelem and increasingly sought by adventurous priests in the north, flows northward in carefully sealed containers. Other plant preparations—digestive aids, pain relievers, treatments for parasitic infections common in tropical regions—are traded in smaller quantities but generate steady income for the families and communities that gather them. The knowledge of these plants, closely guarded and transmitted through apprenticeship and initiation, is as valuable as the plants themselves.

Cacao grown in Shelem's highland regions is of exceptional quality, and the chocolate prepared from Shelem beans is prized by the wealthy and noble throughout the northern city-states. Exotic woods—hardwoods for tool-making, aromatic woods for incense and perfume production—are harvested from the jungle and shipped northward in logs or carved into finished items. Rubber, a substance wholly unknown in the central heartland until Shelem traders began importing it, is slowly becoming appreciated for waterproofing and other applications, though its true potential remains largely unexplored.

## The Country's Quarrel

Shelem quarrels with the north over who buys at the forest's edge. [[affiliation-riverhouses|The River Houses]], the merchant families who govern the river towns, buy what the jungle sells at their own landings and carry it north in their own dugouts, so the price the north pays is set on the landings. The plateau's traders want to buy upriver, where the forest traders come in, and the families keep the rivers closed to them: a northern caravan that hires its own canoes finds that no forest trader sells to it twice. The [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]] reaches the river towns only because the families pay its stations, and the families mean their payments to buy something. Shelem's allegiance to the north is renewed every year with its trade agreements, and every year the families decide what the north's goodwill is worth.

## Hooks

- **Past the landings.** A Ch'eshmah of the merchant guild of Ish'ilankul has brought a caravan to Ts'alukul and means to take it upriver past the landings, to buy feathers where the forest traders come in. He hires a party to find canoe-men who will paddle for him and forest traders who will sell to him, and the families of the town are watching whom he hires.
- **The cheaper feathers.** [[being-chakulneshlm|Ch'akul Ne' Shelem]], speaker of Ts'alukul, has heard that feathers of the river are being sold in [[place-tsamash|Tsa'mash]] below every landing's price. He asks a party to go north and learn whose feathers they are, and his rival on the council, [[being-nelushpuqem|Nelush Puq'em]], would pay well to know that he has asked.
- **The tired runner.** A Reader of the Cords at [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]] has noticed that cords about prices reach the river towns half a day later than they should. He asks a party to travel the river route beside the Speaking Road's runners and time every stage.
