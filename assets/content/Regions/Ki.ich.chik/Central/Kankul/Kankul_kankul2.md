## Overview

The first thing a traveler sees of K'ankul, across the flat green of the lowland jungle, is a round tower. It stands on a two-tiered platform at the western edge of the city, plastered white, with a domed top and a few small square windows high in its side, and at dusk a light shows in one of them. The tower is the sighting house of [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]], the college of reckoners that computes the day-tables for every city on the continent the cords reach, and K'ankul's standing among the realms rests on it. K'ankul is a city of about 120,000 people in the limestone lowlands of the [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul Region]], and the seat of the realm of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]], the intellectual heart of Ki'ich'chik civilization.

## The City

K'ankul is white. Its temples, its palaces and its causeways are built of the pale limestone that lies a hand's depth under the soil everywhere in the lowlands, and the plaster on them is burned from the same stone. The causeways run out from the center to the outlying temple groups as raised white roads, straight through the jungle, and the city's people walk them in the evening because they are cooler than the streets. Every building of the center is turned to the rising or setting point of the sun, the moon or the bright star [K'inish'ilan]{.name data-type="place/celestial"}, and a newcomer who walks the plaza at sunset on the right day sees a shadow climb a stair like a serpent.

The scribes' ward lies east of the plaza. Its courts are full of racks of bark paper drying in the sun, and its houses smell of the lime the paper is coated with and the soot of the black ink. K'ankul's books are written, painted and folded here, and they are prized throughout the known world. South of it lies the Street of the Kilns, where the potters live, and beyond that the salt-sellers' yards.

## The Pool of the City

At the northern end of the main causeway lies the city's great sacred pool, a round shaft in the limestone some sixty paces across, with sheer walls dropping to still green water far below. A small temple stands on its rim. The priesthood of K'ankul teaches that the sacred pools are the mouths of [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]], the underworld through which the sun travels each night, and the city's priests pour water into this one as offering, give special weight to watery sacrifice, and read omens in its reflections at dawn. Some priestly families hold a hereditary right to a lesser pool in the jungle around the city and sell its minerals, its cave pearls and its sealed water to pilgrims.

## The Star House

The Star House is the city's pride and the realm's protection. Its reckoners carry the four counts forward, its Table-Makers join them into one table for each city, and the Master of the Cords has each finished table knotted into _k'otsel_ (knotted cords) and handed to the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]], which brings a cord from [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] to K'ankul in two days. A city receives its days and never the arithmetic behind them, and the arithmetic stays in K'ankul. Every city of Ish'ilankul works on days computed here, and that is why the Sun Lords of the plateau treat K'ankul as a partner in the faith and not as a tributary.

The house is not the Ts'enmah's. It belongs to [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]], it answers to the Q'emul Tq'an at [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]], and it elects its own First. The Ts'enmah of K'ankul feeds its reckoners, guards its tower and takes the credit for its tables in every court he visits, and he has no say in what they contain.

## Who Governs

A Ts'enmah rules K'ankul by descent from his father or his brother, and names his heir, the Ts'enilbal, in a public rite while the heir is still a child. The present Ts'enmah is [[being-tsltlishkksh|Ts'ultel Ishkul K'ushi]], a thin, nearsighted man in his sixties who was a court reckoner before his brother died and who still checks his own Ts'obanal's figures. Kulmah hold the lesser seats in the jungle around the city for him, and hereditary Ha'nalmah govern its towns. The Ilkanmah, the lords of the shore, hold the realm's ports and keep the sea roads open, and through them K'ankul's ships trade by way of [[place-tzokilkul|the Walled City]] (Ts'okilkul), and [[place-tzililnetzik|the Island of Swallows]] (Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik).

The cities of K'ankul do not compete with armies. They compete in discoveries and architecture: a city that announces a new calculation or a new way of building becomes a center of study, and its Ts'enmah's stones record the discovery as his. K'ankul's own rivals in the reckoning are [[place-nechakalkul|Ne' Ch'akalkul]] and [[place-lishepikum|Lishep'ikum]].

## Making a Living

K'ankul trades in knowledge. Its scribes and mathematicians are paid extraordinary sums to travel to the temples of Ish'ilankul and keep their calendars, and its books, astronomical instruments and carved jade make it a trading power second only to Ish'ilankul. Its coastal salt pans produce the salt that is traded for the grain surpluses of the plateau, and the honey of its stingless bees travels north and south.

:::secret {title="The late cords"}
The Ts'enmah knows that the court of Ish'ilankul wants K'ankul's partnership reduced to plain tribute before the conclave of 722. He has asked the First of the Star House, privately, to let it be known that the tables for the new round may be late if K'ankul is pressed, and the First has refused, because the house's honesty is its whole value. The Master of the Cords, who knots the tables and hands them to the runners, is the Ts'enmah's sister's son. He has said nothing to the First, and he has told his uncle that a cord can be knotted slowly.
:::
