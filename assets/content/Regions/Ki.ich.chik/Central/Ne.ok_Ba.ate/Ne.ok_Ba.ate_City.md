## Overview

The road comes up out of the rainforest in switchbacks, climbs to a saddle between two volcanoes, and runs straight down the middle of the city. Ne'ok Ba'ate is built across it: its gates stand at either end of the saddle, its great plaza sits on the road at the top, and every load that crosses the isthmus by land is set down in that plaza at least once. A porter going north carries jaguar hides, macaw feathers and cacao, and a porter going south carries obsidian, fine textiles and priests. Ne'ok Ba'ate, which means "Watery Bridge", is a city of about 70,000 people on the volcanic spine of the [[place-neokbaatergn|Ne'ok Ba'ate Region]], the seat of the realm of [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], and the city that holds the bridge between the great northern realms and the wilder, less centralized lands of the south.

## The Crossing

The great plaza is a market and a customs-house at once. The northern half belongs to the traders coming from the north, the southern half to the traders coming from the south, and between them along the line of the road stand the Ts'enmah's weighing houses, where every load is weighed, its owner named and the city's share taken before it may cross to the other half. Merchants grow rich here on the difference between the two halves, and the city's own merchant families buy on one side and sell on the other without moving their goods more than a hundred paces.

The two volcanoes above the city smoke on most days. The western one is the higher, and on a clear morning its summit shows the gray line of the western sea. The eastern sea lies down the long road to the coast, where the anchorages of the realm's eastern shore are some of the finest on the continent, and its merchant canoes go farther south from there than any others. The far southern peoples meet Ki'ich'chik traders through Ne'ok Ba'ate's canoes more than any other way.

## Who Governs

A Ts'enmah rules Ne'ok Ba'ate by descent from his father or his brother, names his heir in a public rite while the heir is still a child, and is enthroned by the priests. The present Ts'enmah is [[being-ishkulnktsl|Ishkul Ne'ok Ts'ultel]], a tall, quick-tempered man of forty-five with a merchant's head for numbers. Kulmah hold the lesser seats of the volcanic valleys for him, hereditary Ha'nalmah govern his towns, and a Shok'almah is named to lead each campaign and set aside when it ends. His sister is married to the Ts'enmah of [[place-qokulqat|Q'o Kul'qat]], and his eldest daughter is promised to a son of the royal house of [[place-netsumul|Ne' Ts'umul]].

The Sun Lords of Ne'ok Ba'ate are known for their independence. Ishkul Ne'ok keeps the forms of [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]], the pyramids, the vigesimal count and the 260-day calendar, with a local flavor, and he resents the demands of tributary status. His city is too wealthy and too important to the trade of the north for [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] to conquer, and the terrain behind the coast is too broken for an invading army to hold. His Ch'umts'om, the envoy he keeps at the court of Ish'ilankul, speaks for him there and is answered as if he were the Ts'enmah himself, and only that envoy's skill and the marriages of the royal ladies keep the realm's autonomy.

Messages from Ish'ilankul reach the city in two to three days by the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]], whose way-stations are fewer and farther apart here than in the heartland. A Ts'enmah of Ne'ok Ba'ate who sets up runners of his own is reminded by the priesthood that no lord on the continent commands a runner, and his runners stop.

:::secret {title="The slow letters"}
Ishkul Ne'ok sends his most private letters by canoe. A painted fold of bark paper, sealed in a gourd, goes down the eastern road to the coast with one of his own household and out by sea to [[place-tzililnetzik|the Island of Swallows]] (Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik), where the war canoes of the island carry it on to his envoys and his allies. A letter by canoe takes four times as long as a cord on the Road, and no priest of the dispatch ever reads it. His letters this season go to the Ts'enmah of Q'o Kul'qat and of Ne' Ts'umul. They propose that the cities of the isthmus agree among themselves, before the conclave of 722, on what they will send to Ish'ilankul in the new round, and that each send it on its own day by its own envoy, as Q'o Kul'qat sends its jade.
:::
