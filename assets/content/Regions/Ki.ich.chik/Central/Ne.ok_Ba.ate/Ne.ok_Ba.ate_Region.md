## Overview

Ne'ok Ba'ate Region is the land of [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], and lies within [[place-centralkiichchik|Central Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]].

Ne'ok Ba'ate is the narrow isthmus that connects the great heartland of Ki'ich'chik civilization to the wilder, less centralized lands of the south. The name means "Watery Bridge" in the old language, and it is apt—a region of narrow continental shelf, volcanic peaks, and river valleys that forms a natural corridor for both the great trading roads and the ambitious armies of the northern city-states.

Ne'ok Ba'ate is also the gateway of merchants. Goods flowing northward from the southern jungles—jaguar hides, macaw feathers, cacao from the highland margins, rare woods and medicinal herbs—pass through Ne'ok Ba'ate on their way to the [[place-ixilankulrgn|Ish'ilankul Region]] and the [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul Region]]. Goods flowing southward—obsidian, fine textiles, ceremonial objects, and priests dispatched to extend the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani faith]]—originate from the great northern city-states. Thus, Ne'ok Ba'ate is simultaneously a province and a chokepoint, a place where merchants grow wealthy but also where the ambitions of great powers often collide.

## Geography

Ne'ok Ba'ate is a region of dramatic transitions. The broad volcanic highlands that characterize the interior descend sharply into tropical rainforest, creating zones of distinct elevation and climate within short distances. The western coast is rocky and violent, with few good harbors but with excellent fishing grounds. The eastern coast is far more hospitable, with sandy beaches, coral reefs, and numerous protected anchorages that have made Ne'ok Ba'ate a maritime power. The great rivers, with their numerous smaller tributaries, flow from the highlands toward the coasts, creating natural corridors for settlement and trade.

The terrain is complex and challenging. Highland valleys provide agricultural zones suitable for maize, beans, and cacao cultivation; their isolation has allowed some highland communities to maintain semi-independence from the great lowland city-states. The rainforest itself is incredibly dense and biodiverse, inhabited by jaguars, pumas, tapirs, and hundreds of bird species. The quetzal, that most sacred of birds, is native to these forests, and the rarest and most perfect plumes come from the deepest, most inaccessible zones. The jungle has proven impossible for any single power to fully control or govern, creating zones of relative freedom for refugees, outcasts, and those who prefer independence to tributary status.

The climate is hot and humid throughout the region, with heavy rainfall in the eastern areas facing the sea and a more pronounced dry season on the western slopes. Water—from mountain streams to jungle swamps to the narrow coastal plains—is abundant and shapes settlement patterns. The region's harbors, particularly those on the eastern coast, are among the finest natural anchorages in all Ki'ich'chik, capable of sheltering dozens of great merchant canoes and ensuring that maritime trade in Ne'ok Ba'ate rivals the importance of overland roads.

## City-States and Realms

Ne'ok Ba'ate's greatest city-states include [Ne' Ts'umul]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} ("Red Water"), a sprawling complex overlooking the great river where astronomical monuments rival those of K'ankul; [Q'o Kul'qat]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} (the "Twin Pyramid"), famous for its intricate carved monuments and its political independence; and [[place-tzililnetzik|Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik]], the Island of Swallows, an island trading port that controls a vast archipelago of small islands and maintains its own small but efficient navy.

## Trade

Ne'ok Ba'ate's prosperity rests on its position as a crossroads. Highland goods from [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]]—obsidian, fine textiles, and luxury ceramics—enter Ne'ok Ba'ate's merchant networks and are transported southward to the diverse cultures of the outer continent and to the distant markets of the southern ocean. Conversely, exotic goods from the southern jungles and coast—jaguar hides, macaw feathers, rare woods like mahogany and rosewood, and the finest cacao—pass through Ne'ok Ba'ate on their way to satisfy the appetites of Ish'ilankul and K'ankul's aristocracy.

The maritime trade is particularly significant. Ne'ok Ba'ate's merchants have established networks of trading posts along the coasts, and their merchant canoes venture far beyond the traditional bounds of Ki'ich'chik culture. It is Ne'ok Ba'ate traders who have made the first contacts with peoples far to the south and have begun to establish the trade relationships that are slowly bringing southern regions into the Ki'ich'chik commercial sphere. The ports of Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik and the other eastern-coast city-states handle an enormous volume of maritime trade, and the merchant families that control these ports have become immensely wealthy.
