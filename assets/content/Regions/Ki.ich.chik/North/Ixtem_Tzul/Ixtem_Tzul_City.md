## Overview

A southern trader who survives the desert crossing comes up a dry wash, sees a wall of stone rooms four stories high against the canyon cliff, and has arrived. The south calls this great house Ishtem Tsul, after the nation, and counts it as the seat of the [[affiliation-ixtemtzul|Mesa Towns]]; about 1,000 people live in it and in the smaller houses on the canyon floor around it. It is the southernmost great house on the roads of the Mesa Towns and the first on the routes out of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul]].

## The House

The great house is a single block of several hundred rooms, terraced down toward a walled plaza. Its storerooms hold the canyon's grain. Round ritual chambers are sunk into the plaza floor, entered by ladders through their roofs, and a straight road leaves the plaza northward over the mesa toward the canyons of the other great houses. Cages along the plaza wall hold scarlet macaws, kept for the feathers that the chamber elders wear in the rain dances.

## The Trade

The Keeper of the South Road lives here. Every trader and pilgrim out of the desert is brought to him, and he decides what goes north and at what price. Cacao, macaw feathers and copper bells go north from this house, and turquoise goes south. What the traders say of the south is carried north along the roads, and the house hears first when a southern expedition is on the way.
