## Overview

Ch'akul Tsa'kul Region is an uninhabited desert claimed by no nation, and lies within [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]].

Ch'akul Tsa'kul, the "Thirsty Land," is a vast expanse of extremely hot, deadly desert, salt flats, barren mesas, and scorching rock that forms a barrier between the northern peoples and Ki'ich'chik civilization to the south. It is uninhabited, and on its northern rim, where the desert climbs onto the mesas, the great houses of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] keep its northern gate.

The desert is a filter rather than a wall: no garrison holds it, and its heat and thirst let through only the few who come prepared. There are no residents, seasonal settlements, staffed oases, or maintained trading posts. Those who attempt to cross must carry their water and supplies; the few isolated water sources do not sustain settlement. Ancient ruins of structures suggest that in some previous age the land was more fertile, when rains fell more abundantly and rivers flowed where now only dry channels remain. Whether this transformation is permanent or cyclical remains unknown.

## Geography

Ch'akul Tsa'kul encompasses several distinct desert environments, each with its own character and hazards. The northern portions feature vast sand seas—dunes that shift with the wind and extend to the horizon in every direction. Travelers crossing these regions rely on astronomical navigation and memory of landmark formations; a dust storm can utterly disorient the unprepared, and rescue from a wandering caravan is virtually impossible. The sand itself is scorching hot during the day and frigid at night; temperatures can fluctuate by fifty degrees or more between noon and midnight.

Central and western portions of the desert feature rocky plateaus, mesas, and badlands where stone replaces sand. These lands are slightly more navigable than the sand seas—physical landmarks provide navigation markers—but the absence of vegetation and limited water sources makes them equally deadly. Scattered throughout this region are box canyons and arroyos that can flash-flood with sudden violence during the infrequent rains that do fall; travelers who shelter in such features without knowing weather patterns upstream have perished when torrents of water suddenly filled previously dry channels.

The southern and southeastern portions of Ch'akul Tsa'kul feature increasingly complex topography as the land transitions toward the mountains and highlands of southern Ki'ich'chik. Salt flats of otherworldly appearance—vast expanses of crystallized salt deposits—stretch across areas where ancient inland seas evaporated in a geological past beyond human memory. These salt flats are impassable for most travelers; the salt crust can cut feet and pack animal hooves, and the reflected heat from the white surface intensifies the scorching temperatures. The air above salt flats shimmers with mirages that can deceive unwary travelers into wandering away from established routes.

Water is the fundamental scarcity in Ch'akul Tsa'kul. A few isolated springs surface, but they are unreliable and support no settlement or staffed stopping place. Travelers who attempt the crossing must carry water and supplies, and the heat and distance make the journey deadly.

## Ancient Ruins and Lost Knowledge

Ruins stand all through Ch'akul Tsa'kul: walls half buried in the sand seas, terraces cut into mesa sides, mounds on the salt flats that turn out to be buildings, and lines in the gravel that show from a mesa top as the outlines of rooms. The priests of the south say they are older than the first of their own cities, and nobody in the north or the south claims them. Five are known well enough to find:

- **[[place-greenspring|The Green Spring]]**, a stone-lined sink in the western mesas with palms, fig trees and a pool of clear water at its bottom, the kindest water in the desert, where those who sleep beside it do not wake.
- **[[place-blackpyramid|The Black Pyramid]]**, a stepped pyramid of black stone at the head of the deepest canyon of the central badlands, its upper tiers run like wax, where the [[being-mtrfrdrg|Mature Fire Dragon]] keeps its hoard.
- **[[place-writtenwall|The Written Wall]]**, a mesa cliff in the western badlands cut from foot to rim with rows of signs that no scribe can read, every one of them a scorpion's den.
- **[[place-sleepingpyr|The Sleeping Pyramid]]**, a white, painted pyramid in the northern sand seas that the dunes bury and uncover, so that most years only its top shrine shows above the sand.
- **[[place-salthouse|The Salt House]]**, a terraced house of many rooms on a rock island in the southern salt flats, grown over with salt crystal, whose rooms keep cloth, baskets and seated dead.

Each is its own place, and none looks like another. What comes out of them sells in the markets at either end of the crossing: painted pots, cloth that has kept its color, carved stone, copies of the Written Wall's signs, and now and then crystals and pieces of metal that no smith can name. No guide lives in the desert, and the traders who know a ruin take a party to it only on their own crossing.

## Trade and Passage

Ch'akul Tsa'kul is not a trade region but a deadly barrier that a few travelers cross each year. Old routes and stone cairns mark the passages, but no one maintains them. A crossing takes several weeks and requires careful preparation: water, preserved food, and porters enough to carry both, with spares for those who fall.

No trading posts, way-stations, seasonal settlements or staffed oases operate in Ch'akul Tsa'kul. The isolated sources of water and the extreme heat do not sustain residents. Any crossing is an unsupported expedition.

The crossing limits north-south trade to a trickle, and only goods light and precious enough to justify the risk make it. Southern traders and pilgrims carry cacao, scarlet macaws and cast copper bells north, and bring turquoise back. The routes end in the north at the Turquoise People, whose great houses receive everyone who comes out of the desert and decide what goes on to the rest of the north. Now and then a southern expedition crosses with other aims, and the Turquoise People are the first to meet it.
