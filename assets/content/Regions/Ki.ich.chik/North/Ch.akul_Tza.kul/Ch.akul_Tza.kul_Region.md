## Overview

Ch'akul Tsa'kul Region is an uninhabited desert claimed by no nation, and lies within [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]].

Ch'akul Tsa'kul, the "Thirsty Land," is a vast expanse of extremely hot, deadly desert, salt flats, barren mesas, and scorching rock that forms a barrier between the northern peoples and Ki'ich'chik civilization to the south. It is uninhabited.

The desert stands as a natural boundary maintained by neither wall nor garrison, but by the sheer hostility of its environment. There are no residents, seasonal settlements, staffed oases, or maintained trading posts. Those who attempt to cross must carry their water and supplies; the few isolated water sources do not sustain settlement. Ancient ruins of structures suggest that in some previous age the land was more fertile, when rains fell more abundantly and rivers flowed where now only dry channels remain. Whether this transformation is permanent or cyclical remains unknown.

## Geography

Ch'akul Tsa'kul encompasses several distinct desert environments, each with its own character and hazards. The northern portions feature vast sand seas—dunes that shift with the wind and extend to the horizon in every direction. Travelers crossing these regions rely on astronomical navigation and memory of landmark formations; a dust storm can utterly disorient the unprepared, and rescue from a wandering caravan is virtually impossible. The sand itself is scorching hot during the day and frigid at night; temperatures can fluctuate by fifty degrees or more between noon and midnight.

Central and western portions of the desert feature rocky plateaus, mesas, and badlands where stone replaces sand. These lands are slightly more navigable than the sand seas—physical landmarks provide navigation markers—but the absence of vegetation and limited water sources makes them equally deadly. Scattered throughout this region are box canyons and arroyos that can flash-flood with sudden violence during the infrequent rains that do fall; travelers who shelter in such features without knowing weather patterns upstream have perished when torrents of water suddenly filled previously dry channels.

The southern and southeastern portions of Ch'akul Tsa'kul feature increasingly complex topography as the land transitions toward the mountains and highlands of southern Ki'ich'chik. Salt flats of otherworldly appearance—vast expanses of crystallized salt deposits—stretch across areas where ancient inland seas evaporated in a geological past beyond human memory. These salt flats are impassable for most travelers; the salt crust can cut feet and pack animal hooves, and the reflected heat from the white surface intensifies the scorching temperatures. The air above salt flats shimmers with mirages that can deceive unwary travelers into wandering away from established routes.

Water is the fundamental scarcity in Ch'akul Tsa'kul. A few isolated springs surface, but they are unreliable and support no settlement or staffed stopping place. Travelers who attempt the crossing must carry water and supplies, and the heat and distance make the journey deadly.

## Ancient Ruins and Lost Knowledge

Scattered across Ch'akul Tsa'kul are ruins of settlements and structures from an age previous to the current northern tribal peoples. These ruins—some stone-built with substantial architecture, others merely impressions in the earth—suggest that the region once supported permanent settlements and possibly civilization. Scholars debate whether these ruins represent indigenous cultures that have disappeared, or whether they are remnants of adherents of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]] or other southern peoples attempting to establish control during a previous age when the desert may have been less hostile.

Travelers who attempt the crossing may seek valuables or artifacts among the ruins. Some items recovered—certain crystals, carved stones, and metal objects of unknown purpose—are believed to possess spiritual or magical properties. Expeditions to the ruins are dangerous, and no resident guides or traders are based in the desert.

## Trade and Passage

Ch'akul Tsa'kul is not a trade region but a deadly barrier that some travelers attempt to cross. Old routes and stone cairns may mark a passage, but no one maintains them. A crossing takes several weeks and requires careful preparation—water supplies, preserved foods, and spare pack animals to replace those that may perish.

No trading posts, way-stations, seasonal settlements, or staffed oases operate in Ch'akul Tsa'kul. The isolated sources of water and extreme heat do not sustain residents. Any crossing is an unsupported expedition.

The crossing of Ch'akul Tsa'kul is arduous enough that it effectively limits the volume of north-south trade. Goods must be high-value to justify the transportation costs and risks associated with desert crossing.
