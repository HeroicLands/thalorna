## Overview

Northern Ki'ich'chik is the part of the continent that the southern city-states do not rule and barely know: everything north of the desert of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul]], from the lake country of the northeast to the cedar coast of the west. Five nations hold most of it, rivals with standing grievances who are bound by one agreement, the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]], to stand together when an outsider comes. Two smaller peoples on the western coast and in the far northwest stay outside it. About 3,500,000 people live in the north: the One Fire holds 1,500,000 of them and the Maple People 750,000, the Shell-Bead Towns 350,000, the Grass People 300,000, the Cedar People 200,000, the Turquoise People 150,000 and the Ice-Coast Hunters 50,000, while about 200,000 more live in boreal and other bands outside every polity.

The north is a land of copper and maize, longhouse and mound, buffalo jump and great house, and none of it is written. Its peoples keep their law, their treaties and their histories in memory, and they refuse the speech and the glyphs of the south.

## The Nations of the Compact

Each nation is a confederacy of many towns or bands that share a family of languages and a set of rites. None of them is a single state, and each has its own factions. Each is listed under the name these pages give it. The [[skill-kiichek|Ki'i'chek]] name beside it is the one the south's merchants gave, from the animals on its war-standards and camp-markings.

[[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]]
: Osetakona in their own tongue, the people of the maple, and Shik'ul Ch'akal in the south's, the Otter People. Eight nations of palisaded longhouse villages in the northeastern lakes and forests, joined in a league they call the Grove and governed by consensus councils whose speakers the clan mothers name and depose. The Maple People are the compact's diplomats and its memory, and they distrust kings on principle.

[[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]]
: Tokchi Aska in its own tongue, Ts'uma No'tun in the south's, the Serpent People. Mound cities in the valley of the great river, ruled by paramount chiefs who claim descent from the Sun, with seafaring provinces on the southern shell coasts and the old eastern ranges at its back. It is the richest and most populous nation, the most centralized, and the one the others trust least.

[[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]]
: Tenlaya in their own tongue, the people of the grass, and P'enqal Ch'akal in the south's, the Coyote People. Hunting bands that follow the herds on foot with dogs and travois, and farming villages of earth lodges along the rivers. Their hunt chiefs and war leaders hold power for a season, and warrior societies police the communal hunts.

[[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]]
: Teyumi in their own tongue, the people of the turquoise, and Ishtem Tsul in the south's, the Bear People. Towns of multistory stone great houses on the desert's northern rim, linked by straight roads and fed by dry farming and canals. They hold the northern end of every route across the desert, and know the south better than anyone else in the north.

[[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]
: Kelwanuk in their own tongue, the people of the cedar, and Ish'bal Ki'ahk in the south's, the Eagle People. Plank-house villages on the salmon coast of the far west, ranked into nobles, commoners and slaves, where no house rules another and every house sits in one order of seats, won and kept by giving. They are a sea power, a month's journey from the others, and they keep the compact for its prestige.

## Outside the Compact

The [Shell-Bead Towns]{.name data-type="lore/culture"} stand on the western coast south of the Cedar People and in the oak valleys behind it. They are dozens of independent towns, rich in fish and acorns, with plank canoes that cross to the offshore islands and a currency of drilled shell beads. Mountains and desert shelter them, so they see no need for the compact, and the Cedar People and the Turquoise People both trade with them and court them.

The [Ice-Coast Hunters]{.name data-type="lore/culture"} of the far northwest take seal, walrus and whale from skin boats along the frozen coasts. They are remote, self-sufficient and uninterested in the quarrels of the south.

## The Desert and the South

Ch'akul Tsa'kul is a filter, not a wall. Every year a few southern traders and pilgrims cross it, and now and then an expedition, and what they carry north is small, light and precious: cacao, scarlet macaw feathers and cast copper bells. In the north these are the goods of chiefs. A Turquoise People great house keeps live macaws for their feathers, a One Fire noble drinks cacao at a feast, and a chief of the Maple People may be buried with a copper bell that crossed the desert before he was born.

The Turquoise People keep the northern gate. They receive the southern traders, decide what goes on and at what price, and pass the goods along to the other nations. That makes them the north's best informed nation about the south, and the most suspected.

## Copper Against Steel

The north works copper. It comes out of the ground pure in the Maple People's lake country, and smiths hammer it cold into plates, blades, beads and the embossed sheets the One Fire's nobles wear. Nobody in the north smelts ore. The south fights in steel blades and iron-headed spears, and the richest southern warriors wear steel breastplates. Every northern chief knows the difference, and it is the plainest reason the compact exists: no one nation could meet a southern army alone.

## Three Threats

Three dangers press on the north, and each falls on one nation first.

- **The south.** Expeditions probing north across Ch'akul Tsa'kul reach the Turquoise People first and the One Fire after them.
- **The sea raiders.** [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] from the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] have crossed the eastern ocean in longships three times in twenty-one years, and the last time was a year ago. The Maple People watched two of the crossings from their coast and understand neither the men nor their purpose ([[lore-westrnvoyages|The Western Voyages]]).
- **The newcomers from the ice.** Kin of the Ice-Coast Hunters are moving east along the arctic coasts with better boats and harpoons than the peoples they find there, and those older peoples are pushed south toward the Maple People's northern edge.

Whether a danger to one nation is a danger to all is the question the compact argues every summer and has never settled.

## The Land Between

[[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]] is the wooded river frontier between the Maple People and the One Fire, which both claim and neither holds. Its trading posts are where the goods of the east change hands, and its raids are the oldest grievance the compact contains.

The [Red Quarry]{.name data-type="place/site"}, where the compact's council meets, lies on the prairie where the lands of the Grass People, the Maple People and the One Fire meet. No blood may be shed there.

North of the Maple People and the grasslands, the boreal forest runs out into tundra. It is sparse hunting country, held by small bands of caribou hunters at the edge of the Maple People's reach and the Ice-Coast Hunters' coast.
