## Overview

Ha'nal Shi'lan Region is the wooded river frontier between the [[affiliation-xikulchakl|Woodland League]] to the northeast and the [[affiliation-tzumanotun|River Kingdom]] to the southwest, and lies within [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]]. Both nations claim it and neither holds it.

Ha'nal Shi'lan, "the river lowlands" in Ki'ich'chik, is a country of fertile valleys, rich bottomlands and hardwood forest, drained by a great river that flows west from the old ranges to join the River Kingdom's river. It is good land, and its goodness is the trouble: the League wants it for village sites and hunting, and the River Kingdom wants it for maize and for the towns of its younger nobles.

## Geography

The valley floors are the prize. The rivers rise every spring with snowmelt and rain, spread across the bottomlands and leave fresh silt behind; for a few weeks the bottoms are shallow swamps full of waterfowl and fish, and through summer and autumn they are open meadow and the best maize ground in the north. Above them the land rises into forest of oak, hickory, walnut and ash, rich in game and nuts. The spring spawning runs draw people to the rivers from every direction.

The rivers are also the frontier's roads. A canoeist can travel from the League's lakes down through Ha'nal Shi'lan to the River Kingdom's heartland with only a few portages, and the League's canoes carry most of the trade that crosses it.

## The Frontier

Ha'nal Shi'lan is the oldest grievance in the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]]. The League's villages push down the valleys from the north, the River Kingdom's nobles push up them from the south, and each side's war parties burn the other's new villages and take captives. Each accuses the other of raiding under cover of pipe kinship, and the council at the quarry hears the same complaints every summer.

The fighting does not stop the trade. Copper, furs and dried fish come down from the League, and shell, engraved copper and fine pottery come up from the River Kingdom; Grass People traders bring hides and robes from the west. The trading posts on the rivers, the largest of them the settlement that gives the region its name, are where the goods of the east change hands.
