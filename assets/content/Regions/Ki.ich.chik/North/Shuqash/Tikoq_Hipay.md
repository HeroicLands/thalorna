## Overview

Tikoq Hipay, the North Door, is the last town on the [[place-shuqhalaqrgn|Bead Coast]] before the empty shore that runs north to the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]. About 1,200 people live in it, on a bluff above a river mouth, behind a palisade of driftwood posts, the only wall on the coast of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]]. A watcher sits on the headland north of the town every day from spring to autumn, with a fire laid and a basket of wet seaweed beside it to make smoke.

## The Strand

Every spring, when the first Cedar trading canoes come south, the chief of Tikoq Hipay loads two of the town's plank canoes with beads gathered from the chiefs of the northern towns and sends them north. That cargo is the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, and the Cedar People's war canoes pass the coast towns by in return. The chief who sends it is [Hiluqkanan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Bright Sun, a great-grandson of [Hiluqchim]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the chief whose herald guided the war canoes into the Oak Valleys in the [Pointing]{.name data-type="lore/history"}. Hiluqkanan does not speak of his great-grandfather, and he counts the Strand to the bead every spring in front of the other chiefs' heralds.

## The Redeemer

The town's most famous man is [Sitiqnush]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Star Walking, a canoe master of the [Canoe Kin]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} who sails north three times a year to buy back captives from the Cedar People's villages. His canoe is the oldest on the beach and its seams are tarred over many times, and his crew are mostly young men of the town who paddle for nothing. Hiluqkanan praises him at every feast. The town notices that the chief has never paid toward a voyage.

## Trade

In autumn the Cedar People come to Tikoq Hipay to trade in peace, and their canoes are drawn up on the beach under the palisade. They bring copper, grease, cedar boxes and blankets, and they take beads, abalone shell and baskets. Captives taken in the summer are sometimes among the people who paddle their canoes, and Sitiqnush walks the beach looking at faces.
