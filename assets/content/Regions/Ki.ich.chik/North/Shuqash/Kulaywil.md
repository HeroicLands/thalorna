## Overview

A canoe coming over from the islands sees the smoke first, from a hundred cooking fires and the tar pots on the beach, and then the canoes themselves: forty or fifty plank canoes drawn up in rows above the tide line, their red-painted hulls shining with tar. This is Kulaywil, Canoe Place, the largest town of the [[place-shuqhalaqrgn|Bead Coast]], where [Shiyawkal]{.name data-type="being/npc"} sewed the first plank canoe. About 4,000 people live here, at the mouth of a creek on the channel shore of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]], the Shell-Bead People, with the islands on the horizon to the south and the gold hills and the mountains at their backs.

## The Beach and the Yard

The beach is the town's workshop. At its east end stands the house of the [Canoe Kin]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, a long reed house with a plank floor, where the [Paddle of Shiyawkal]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} hangs above the hearth and every son of the brotherhood takes his oath. In front of it is the yard, where two or three canoes are always under construction: planks split from driftwood logs drying in the sun, master sewers fitting the bow planks behind a screen of reed mats, apprentices drilling seam holes, and the tar pots smoking. Nobody outside the brotherhood walks behind the screen.

Every canoe that lands here pays the chief a bead in ten of its cargo, and the chief's counter stands on the beach to count it. Kulaywil sees more cargo than any other beach on the coast, and its chief is the richest on the coast for that reason.

## The Chief and the Sky Elder

The chief is [Shuqkanan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Bright Bead, a woman of about fifty whose own marriage and her five daughters' marriages tie Kulaywil to six other towns. Her storehouse beside her house holds more beads than some islands, and her midwinter feasts feed the chiefs of half the coast. She has never joined a quarrel between towns, and every quarrel on the coast comes to her house sooner or later.

On a rise behind the town stands the astronomer-priests' house, a round reed house with a sunken floor, and beside it a sacred ground of packed earth where the sun pole is raised at midwinter. The Sky Elder, [Tiyupliq]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Clear Sky, lives there and keeps the [Measure of Kulaywil]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the cord every town's strands are checked against, wrapped in otter skin in a basket sealed with tar.

## The Holding of the Sun

At midwinter the chiefs of many towns paddle or walk to Kulaywil for the [Holding of the Sun]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. For four days the astronomer-priests sing at the sun pole, the chiefs feast in Shuqkanan's house, the counters line up before the Sky Elder with their measuring cords, and quarrels between towns are heard. On the fourth morning the whole town climbs the rise before dawn to watch the sun come up over the mountains a little farther north than the day before.
