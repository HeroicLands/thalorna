## Overview

The Oak Valleys (_tulish lumuq_) are the inland country of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]], the Shell-Bead People, behind the coastal mountains of [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]]. They are oak savanna on rolling foothills, around a great flat valley of reed marshes, lakes and rivers that runs north and south for weeks of walking, with a wall of snow mountains on its eastern side. About 120,000 people live in them, in hundreds of villages of a few dozen to a few hundred people, and they live on acorns.

The valley people call themselves _tulishash_, the oak people. They speak the same tongue as the coast, name their children by the same sky and pay the same astronomer-priests, and the coast calls them _kowiqash_, acorn people, in a tone they notice.

## Geography

The foothills are the heart of it. Valley oaks and blue oaks stand apart from each other in grass that is green in winter and gold by summer, and a grown oak drops several baskets of acorns in a good autumn. Below the foothills the valley floor is flat and wet: reed marshes, shallow lakes that spread in spring and shrink in summer, and slow rivers lined with willow. Waterfowl come down from the north in winter in flocks that darken the sky, and elk and antelope graze the grassland. Above the foothills on the east rise the snow mountains, crossed only in late summer, beyond which the land falls to desert.

The rivers of the valley run north to a great bay that opens to the sea well north of the Bead Coast. Raiders come into the valley that way, up the rivers from the bay in the Cedar People's war canoes.

## Acorns and Baskets

Every village lives by the oaks. In autumn the whole village gathers acorns into burden baskets and stores them in granaries (_tiquy_), woven bins on legs that keep out the mice and the damp. Through the year the women shell the acorns, pound them to meal in hollows worn into the bedrock (_qopol_), and leach the bitterness out of the meal with water poured through a bed of sand. They cook the meal in baskets, dropping hot stones into the water, and the baskets hold water without leaking. A valley woman's baskets are the finest made in the north, coiled so tight that they hold water, patterned in black and red, and the coast buys every one it can.

## Villages

A village stands where a stream leaves the foothills, with its domed houses, its granaries, a sweathouse and a dancing ground, and a flat rock nearby pitted with dozens of mortar holes. Few villages have a chief of the coast kind, rich in beads; most are led by a headman whose standing comes from his family's oaks. The largest town is [[place-tulishwil|Tulishwil]], Oak Place, where the coast families come over the pass every autumn for the [Acorn Gathering]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. In the canyon above it is [Tishal Hapuq]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the Painted Cave, the holiest of the astronomer-priests' caves.

## Watch Fires

The Oak Valleys pay no Northern Strand and have no watchers on headlands. Since the [Pointing]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, when a coast chief's herald guided the Cedar People's war canoes up the rivers, the villages have kept watch fires on the ridges above the rivers. When war canoes are seen coming up from the bay, the fires are lit one after another down the valley, and the villages carry their children and their seed acorns into the hills. The fires were first set by [Kowiqnaq]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Acorn Seed, a basket maker of a village near Tulishwil, and the valleys still light them in her name.
