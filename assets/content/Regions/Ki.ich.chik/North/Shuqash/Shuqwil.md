## Overview

The first thing a visitor to Shuqwil hears is the drills: the whir and tick of pump drills in every doorway, from dawn until the light fails. Shuqwil, Bead Place, is the largest town on [Shuq Siwaq]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, Bead Island, a morning's paddle across the channel from [[place-kulaywil|Kulaywil]], and the place where most of the beads of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]] are made. About 2,000 people live here, in domed reed houses on a terrace above a cove, among mounds of broken shell as high as the houses.

## The Drilling

Nearly every household drills. The men dive and gather the small olive snails in the kelp of the cove; the women and the children break the shells, grind the disks on sandstone slabs, drill them with stone points on pump drills, and string them. The drill points are chipped on the beach from a hard stone that comes from the island's west end, and a driller wears out several in a day. A good driller makes a strand a day, and a household with three drillers lives well.

The town's counter is [Ayulchim]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Moon Rising, whose strands are the ones the island measures against. Every strand that leaves Shuqwil passes her doorway, and she cuts and restrings any that is short before it goes. Mainland traders pay a little more for a strand from Shuqwil, and they pay it because of her.

## Water and Wood

The island has little water and almost no wood. Shuqwil's water comes from one spring in a ravine above the town, and its households take turns there by a count the counter keeps. Its wood, its acorns and much of its meat come over from the mainland by canoe, paid in beads. A Shuqwil family that drills well eats as well as any on the coast. A winter of storms that keeps the canoes ashore for a month leaves the town hungry.

## The Young Chief

The chief is [Shiyawliq]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Clear Sea, who was nine when her father died in a coughing sickness. Ayulchim held the town's beads for her for three winters and counted them back to her at her first feast. Shiyawliq is fifteen now, and the island's canoe masters, who would like a larger share of the cargoes, test her every spring.
