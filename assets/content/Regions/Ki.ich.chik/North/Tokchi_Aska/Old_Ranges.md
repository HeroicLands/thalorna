## Overview

The Old Ranges (_poktasko hoshalo_) are the forested ridges east of the [[place-hokpargn|River Valley]], in the [[place-tokchiaskargn|Tokchi Aska Region]]. About 330,000 people live in them, in hill towns strung along the valley floors between the ridges, each with its own small river, its own fields and its own mound. They are part of [[affiliation-tokchiaska|Tokchi Aska]], the One Fire, and the heartland has never been sure how firmly.

The ranges are the country of the rival fire. When the Second Gathering scattered, it was the hill towns that first lit their hearths from a Sun of their own, and the largest of them still carries the name that came of it: [[place-tokchihinch|Tokchi Hinchaka]], the Other Fire.

## Geography

The ridges run northeast to southwest in long parallel lines, worn low and round and forested to their crests with oak, hickory, chestnut and tulip poplar. Between them lie narrow valleys with clear, fast streams, and the bottomland along each stream is just wide enough for a town's fields. A valley holds one town and its hamlets, and the next town is a day's walk away over a ridge. Deer, turkey and bear are plentiful, and in a good autumn the chestnuts fall thickly enough to fatten the hogs of the forest and the households that gather them.

The ranges give the nation what the valley lacks. Their streams cut through beds of glittering mica, which the hill towns' craftsmen split into thin sheets and cut into hands, falcons and coiled serpents for the Suns. Salt springs in the western valleys are boiled down in shallow clay pans, and the salt goes down to the river in baskets. Soapstone from the eastern slopes is carved into pipes and bowls. Copper comes in from the north through [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], and the hill towns' smiths have their own way of embossing it, with a heavier line than the heartland's.

## The Hill Towns

A hill town is smaller than a valley town, a few thousand people at most, and closer to its people. Its Mound Chief knows every household by name, its clan mothers carry more weight in its councils, and its warriors answer the town's drum before they answer a summons from [[place-hayopokta|Hayo Pokta]]. The ridges make every town hard to reach and easy to lose: a Coal Bearer from the great mound needs four days to cross them, and a Great Sun who wants to punish a hill town has to march an army up a valley it knows better than he does.

The hill towns take the coal at every New Fire and send their tribute in salt, mica, skins and chestnuts. They also remember that they were a nation of their own for three lifetimes during the Scattering, and that their rival Sun, [Apiyo]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Eastern Sun, fed them better than Hayo Pokta had.

## What Moves Through

The ranges are crossed along their valleys, never over the ridges when a stream will do. Salt, mica and soapstone go west down the streams to the great river. Copper and furs come south from Ha'nal Shi'lan along the northern valleys, and war parties of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]] come the same way. South and east, the valleys fall away into the swamps of the [[place-wikopihirgn|Dark Water]], and a few traders go down through them toward the eastern ocean, which the One Fire does not sail.
