## Overview

From the river the first thing a traveler sees of Hayo Pokta is the great mound: a flat-topped hill of earth higher than any tree on the floodplain, with the Great Sun's house on its summit and the smoke of the temple fire rising beside it. The name means the Sun's Mound. It is the seat of [[affiliation-tokchiaska|Tokchi Aska]], the One Fire, and the largest town in the north, with about 15,000 people on the floodplain of the great river. The south calls it Ts'uma No'tun, after the nation.

The town is young by the standards of its people. The Great Sun [Tamoska]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Gatherer founded it five Great Suns ago on a site no earlier Sun had used, and the great mound was raised in his lifetime, by the towns that had just taken his coal. The [[lore-tokchiliclt|Tokchili]] point out that the mound of the Second Gathering, a day's paddle downriver, took four lifetimes to build and is now empty.

## The Town

The great mound faces a plaza of beaten earth large enough to hold the whole town at the New Fire. Lesser mounds stand round it: platforms for the houses of the Nobles, the temple where the Keepers of the Fire tend the fire that never goes out and keep the bones of the dead Suns, and conical mounds over the noble dead. A palisade of logs with square bastions encloses the center. Beyond it the commoners' houses, square, wattle-walled and thatched, spread across the floodplain in clan yards, among the fields and the granaries, and the craft quarters where copper is embossed and shell engraved work for the Suns.

The rolling-stone yards run along the plaza's south side. In the Stone Season the champions of every town come to play before the Great Sun, the wagering empties purses across the town, and more marriages and alliances are made at the yard's edge than in any council.

## Work on the Mound

The great mound is never finished. Every morning in the dry season the work crews of the town's clans go up its ramp with baskets of earth, singing, to raise the summit, mend the slopes the rain cut and build out the flanks where the town's dead are buried high. Each household owes its baskets, and the Keepers keep the count. A visitor who wants to understand the town is told to watch the crews: a full ramp means the Great Sun is strong.

## The Seat

The Great Sun governs from the great mound, and every Noble of the heartland comes to Hayo Pokta to bring tribute, watch the game and be seen. The Shell Chiefs of the coast come for the New Fire, stand beside a Keeper while he says the Shell Promise is kept, and leave as soon as they decently can.

The present Great Sun, [Opahiko]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is old, and the town knows it. When a Great Sun dies he is buried in the great mound with his attendants, and the quarrel over who goes with him opens again. Every household in Hayo Pokta has an opinion on who the Sun Mother will name to follow him, and on whether the shell towns will still take the coal afterward.

:::secret {title="Who the Shell Chiefs are talking to"}

Two Shell Chiefs have already sent canoes down the gulf coast, farther than any heartland captain knows, and one came back with a cast copper bell of southern make that did not come through the Mesa Towns. If Opahiko's funeral breaks the Shell Promise, the coast intends to deal with the south on its own account. If it does not, the Shell Chiefs mean to sell the route to the Great Sun's heir for a lighter tribute.

:::
