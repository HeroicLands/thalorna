## Overview

The Dark Water (_wiko pihipo_) is the swamp country that lies below the [[place-poktaskorgn|Old Ranges]] to the south and east, in the [[place-tokchiaskargn|Tokchi Aska Region]]. It is the emptiest country of [[affiliation-tokchiaska|Tokchi Aska]], the One Fire: about 50,000 people live along its margins and almost nobody in its interior. The heartland thinks of it as the place where the nation's fire runs out, and the swamp towns agree, with a different tone.

Most of what the heartland believes about the swamp comes from the swamp towns' carvers, who cut coiled serpents on every post and paddle they make and say little about why.

## Geography

The ranges come down to a flat, wet lowland, and the rivers that leave the hills slow, widen and turn black with the tannin of the leaves that steep in them. Bald cypress and tupelo stand in the water with their knees around them and gray moss hanging from them, over a floor of saw palm, peat and floating mats of weed. The largest of the swamps is [Ne' P'uqan]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Black Water, a trackless expanse many days across, with a dark lake at its heart that the swamp towns say has no bottom.

The swamp is rich. Its channels hold fish, turtles and alligators, its islands of dry ground hold deer and wild hogs, and in winter the waterfowl crowd it. It is also the hunting ground of the [[being-margrush|Margrush]], which lies up beside the channels and takes what comes to the water, and the swamp towns' scouts read margrush sign before anything else. The towns keep one rule for the [[being-vdsrpnt|Voidserpent]] when it passes through the channels: get out of its way and let it go.

## The Swamp-Edge Towns

The towns stand on the dry ridges where the swamp meets the higher ground, small and poor by heartland measure, with low mounds and plain palisades. They farm a little, hunt and fish a great deal, and trade what the swamp gives: margrush hides for shields and sandals that do not rot, alligator teeth, cypress wood, and the carved posts and masks their craftsmen make. The heartland's priests buy the carvings and argue over what the coiled serpents on them mean.

The largest swamp-edge town is [[place-ishochi|Ishochi]], Ash, and it is the town the rest of the nation talks about. A household whose fire has been put out, which no town in the nation may give a coal, can go to Ishochi, and Ishochi takes it in.

## What Moves Through

Very little crosses the Dark Water. A few traders go down the black-water rivers from the Old Ranges to the eastern ocean shore for shell and the holly leaves of the black drink, and come back with tales of the sea. The swamp towns' canoes know hidden channels from one river to the next, and they sell that knowledge to nobody. Fugitives come the other way, from the ranges and the valley into the swamp, and the towns ask them no questions until the next New Fire.
