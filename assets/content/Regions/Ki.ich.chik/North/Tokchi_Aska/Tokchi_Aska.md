## Overview

Tokchi Aska, the One Fire, is the nation of the mound towns of the great river valley and the shell towns of the gulf coast: about 1,500,000 people, the richest and most populous nation of the north and the closest thing it has to a peer of the southern city-states. Its people, the [[lore-tokchiliclt|Tokchili]], name it for the fire on the great mound at [[place-hayopokta|Hayo Pokta]], from which every household in the nation relights its hearth each midsummer. The south calls it Ts'uma No'tun and its people the Serpent People, from the coiled serpents engraved on their shell gorgets. It holds the [[place-tokchiaskargn|Tokchi Aska Region]].

The heartland lies in the valley of the great river, where the floodplain yields a surplus of maize that feeds towns of thousands. Each town is built round a plaza and the earthen mounds raised on its edges: flat-topped platforms for the houses of the Nobles and the temples, and conical mounds over the dead. The surplus feeds the copper-workers who emboss the falcon dancer on plates, the engravers of shell gorgets, the potters and the Keepers of the Fire. To the east the heartland is backed by the old forested ranges, and to the south, where the land runs out into marsh and gulf, lie the shell towns of the coast, whose people never farmed.

The One Fire looks like a kingdom and works like a gathering. A town belongs to it because it takes the Great Sun's coal at the New Fire, and a town that refuses the coal has left. Under a strong Great Sun the towns gather in; under a weak one they light their fires from a rival, and the nation comes apart. The Tokchili have lived through two such Scatterings. The present Gathering, the third, has lasted five Great Suns.

## Governance

**The Great Sun** keeps the fire. The Hayo Hasko, chosen from the Suns, lives in the house on the summit of the great mound at Hayo Pokta and is never seen to hurry, to eat in public or to touch the ground outside his own house without a mat beneath him. He names the Mound Chiefs and the war chiefs, settles disputes between towns, keeps the great granary that feeds the nation in a bad year, and leads the New Fire. His authority is exactly as large as the number of towns that take his coal.

**The Sun Mother** chooses him. The Hayo Hanna, the eldest woman of the Suns, names each Great Sun from among her sons and her sisters' sons, and her choice is final. Rank passes through the mother, so a Sun woman's children are Suns and a Sun man's children are Nobles; the Sun Mother's line is the nation's line, and she is the most powerful person on the mound who never holds a war club.

**The Keepers of the Fire** conduct the rites. The Tokchi Hapilo are a hereditary priesthood of Noble rank who feed the fire on the great mound, keep the temple where the bones of the dead Suns lie, recite the line of the Suns at each New Fire and conduct every funeral. The Great Sun cannot hold the New Fire without them, and a Great Sun who quarrels with the Keepers finds the omens bad.

**The Mound Chiefs** govern the towns. A Poktimala holds a town from the house on its mound, collects its maize and its baskets of earth, judges between its clans, leads its warriors and opens its granary in a bad year. The clan mothers of the town settle what they can before it reaches him.

**The Shell Chiefs** govern the coast. The Koyo Imala of each shell town collects tribute from the smaller coastal towns around it and sends part of it up the river to Hayo Pokta, in shell, dried fish and canoes and never in people. That last condition is the [Shell Promise]{.name data-type="lore/law"}, made by the Sun Mother [Hoyani]{.name data-type="being/npc"} at the start of the present Gathering, and the Shell Chiefs recite it at every New Fire before they take the coal.

## The Funerals

When a Sun dies, others die with him. The Keepers teach that a Sun goes up to the sky with the sun and must go attended, and that the households of those who walk with him are raised: a _tiwilo_'s sister's children become Honored for three generations. Most attendants are volunteers, old retainers and wives for whom it is the best gift they can leave their kin. Some are captives, who are not asked.

The nation argues over the practice and has always argued. At the end of the Second Gathering the Great Sun [Lapali]{.name data-type="being/npc"} was buried with a hundred and eight, among them fifty-three young women sent up the river as tribute from the coast. The shell towns stopped paying, the eastern towns lit their fires from a rival, and within a generation the Second Gathering had scattered. The Tokchili call it the [Funeral of the Hundred]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, and it is the crime the nation carries.

The present Great Sun, [Opahiko]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is old. The Keepers want him buried with a full following. The Shell Chiefs say that one coastal death, even a volunteer's, breaks the Shell Promise. The Sun Mother [Shimaya]{.name data-type="being/npc"} has not yet named his heir, and both sides are waiting to see whom she chooses.

## Public Life

The rolling-stone game is the nation's spectacle and much of its politics. Two players run down a long yard of beaten clay, one bowls a polished stone disk ahead of them, and each throws a pole at where he thinks it will stop. Whole towns gather to watch, and wagers run from a basket of maize to a copper plate to a man's own freedom. Nobles settle rivalries through their champions, and the Sakko Hapilo, the Keeper of the Yard who holds the wagers, holds a share of each town's power. In the Stone Season after the harvest the towns send their champions to Hayo Pokta to play before the Great Sun, and a Mound Chief whose town loses badly comes home weaker.

Before a war the Falcon Chief, the Kiyo Imala, leads the falcon dancers through the plaza in copper-plated headdresses and falcon-feather cloaks, dancing the bird-man who strikes down the enemies of the sun. The same figure is embossed on the copper plates the Suns are buried with.

## Neighbors

The other nations see the One Fire as rich, arrogant and expansionist, and the One Fire sees them as poor. It contests [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], the wooded river frontier to the northeast, with the [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]], who tell the Suns at every council that hereditary rule is what their own councils exist to prevent. Its western towns suffer the raids of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]] and trade with them in the same season.

In the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]] the One Fire sends Nobles of the Sun's house as its delegates. It brings the compact what no other nation can: population, a granary that can feed an army, and the coast towns' canoes, which are the north's only navy on the gulf. Of all the compact's nations it is the one most tempted to make a private bargain with the south, and the others watch it for that.

## Commerce and Currency

The One Fire has no money. Wealth is maize in a granary, copper on a Sun, shell on a Noble's chest and the labor a Mound Chief can call on. Tribute goes up the river to Hayo Pokta in baskets of maize and down it again as gifts, feasts and seed in bad years; a Great Sun who keeps more than he gives back is the beginning of a Scattering.

The heartland's craftsmen work copper brought from the Maple People's lake country, in trade through Ha'nal Shi'lan and in plunder, into plates, gorgets and blades. Their shell comes up the river from the coast. Southern goods reach the One Fire across the desert through the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]], a little cacao and a few macaw feathers and copper bells at a high price, and its Suns prize them above anything made at home and resent the price. Some coastal captains claim to have traded with people far down the gulf coast who had southern goods, and the Great Sun's court would like to know whether the voyage can be made both ways.
