## Overview

The Tikupilarit are the Ice-Edge People, the sea hunters of the far northwestern coast, about 50,000 people in some two hundred villages on the capes and islands of the [[place-tikumanikrgn|Ice Coast]] and along the river channels of the [[place-taluqnapiqrgn|Lake Tundra]] behind it. They take their name from the ice edge (_tikupila_), where the ice fast to the shore meets open water and the spring hunt is made. Their ways are set out in the [[lore-tikupilaritclt|Tikupilarit]] culture note, and their tongue is the [[skill-tikupilaritlng|Tikupilarit Language]]. The south has no name for them and has never met them.

They live by the sea. Seal is the daily food, walrus the spring and autumn harvest, and in the north the great whale the wealth of a village for a year; the rivers add salmon and whitefish in summer, and the tundra birds, berries and caribou. They hunt from kayaks and from open skin boats with harpoons whose heads turn sideways in the wound, and they live through the winter on oil, dried meat and frozen fish in houses dug half into the ground.

## Who Rules

The men's house rules, and nothing rules the men's houses. A village is a few dozen to a few hundred people around one men's house (_kaniaq_), with the women's sod houses (_pagik_) around it, a row of drying racks, and kayaks on racks above the dogs' reach. The men's house is the council, the court and the school. The elders sit on the back bench, and one of them, the speaker (_kalitiq_), opens and closes every council. A matter is talked over until the men agree. A provider (_nalitiq_), whose catch feeds other houses, is heard before a hunter, and a hunter before a youth, but nobody gives an order, and a man who will not agree may simply go hunting.

The speaker is chosen by the men's house, never inherited. He is usually the oldest man still sharp, and he keeps the village's memory of its names, gifts and feuds and speaks for it to other villages. The present speaker of [[place-kavrumiq|Kavrumiq]] is heard across the whole coast, and his word on the Taking is the one Whale Point resents.

The shaman (_aklutiq_), a man or a woman, holds the other half of a village's authority. The shaman speaks for the animals and the dead, and the speaker for the living. When the two disagree, the elders decide what the village does, and the shaman decides what the animals want.

The women decide the shares. A hunter brings his catch to his wife's house, and she cuts it and sends the portions to the houses owed them, the widows and orphans first. A provider's standing is made by his catch and kept by his wife's cutting.

## Boat-Holders

A skin boat (_kalviaq_) costs the hides of six walrus or bearded seals, sewn by the women of a whole house, and a crew of eight to paddle it. The man who owns one is a boat-holder (_kalviaqtiq_), and he outfits the crew, feeds them in the hunting season, and divides the catch by fixed shares: the harpooner first, the steersman second, the paddlers after, the boat-holder last and largest. In the south a boat-holder is a respected man. On the northern coast, at [[place-imlakkagaq|Imlak Kagaq]] and the whaling villages around it, where a crew can take a great whale in the spring leads, a boat-holder is the richest man in his village, and his wife, who gives the whale its drink and leads the sharing of it, is its richest woman. The whaling villages are where the coast's talk is least equal, and where the East People came from.

## Between Villages

The villages of one river or one stretch of coast are a people: they speak the same way, marry each other, and hold their feasts together. The Ice-Edge People are about a dozen such peoples, named for their rivers and capes. They fought each other in the wars that ended with the Messenger Feast, and they still feud.

The [Messenger Feast]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} is how villages deal with each other. In the dark of the year a men's house sends two messengers (_malutiq_) to another village with an invitation and a list of things it wants: a sled, a wolf skin, a bow, a bride for a widower. A month later the guest village comes with the things on the list and a list of its own, and the hosts feed them for days and give them what they asked. The next winter the roles turn. Trade, marriage, the settlement of feuds and the news of the coast all travel by it. A village that refuses an invitation has declared a feud, and a village that gives less than it was given is sung about for years.

A killing between villages is settled in the men's houses. The two speakers meet, the killer's kin give gifts, and the next child born to the killer's family is named for the dead man and raised as one of his kin's. A family that refuses the name may take blood instead.

## Outsiders

The Ice-Edge People deal with outsiders as little as they can.

- **The Cedar People.** The [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]] trade at [[place-simimanik|Simi Manik]], Trading Shore, the southernmost village, every midsummer: ivory, furs, gut parkas and seal oil for cedar boxes, eulachon grease and now and then a sheet of copper. Every few years their war canoes come north for slaves instead, and Trading Shore keeps a watch and a set of bone armor for them.
- **The forest hunters.** Up the rivers of the Lake Tundra the land rises into spruce, and the hunters of the forest come down in spring with caribou hides and wolverine fur to trade for oil and dried fish at [[place-taqvamiq|Taqvamiq]] and the other river villages. The two sides do not marry and speak through signs.
- **The East People.** The kin who have gone east from Whale Point along the frozen coast (_mapirit_) are Ice-Edge People still, and a crew of them that comes home is a guest in any men's house. They now hold the northern coast for a summer's travel east.
- **Those Who Give Way.** The older people of the eastern tundra (_kipirit_), whom the East People are pushing south, are known on the home coast only from the East People's stories.
- **The compact.** The coast has heard of the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]] from the Cedar People, as a gathering of chiefs a summer's journey to the south and east. It has no dealings with it, and the compact's pipe gives no one any standing here.

## War

The Ice-Edge People fight with sinew-backed bows, spears and knives of stone and copper, and in the old wars they wore armor (_salaq_) of bone and ivory plates. They have fought no war between peoples in living memory. Feuds between families still end in killings, and a killing still ends in a name or in blood. Their danger to the compact is not war. It is the East People, who outhunt every people they meet with skin boats, dogs and the toggling harpoon, and whose advance pushes Those Who Give Way into the hunting country at the edge of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]].
