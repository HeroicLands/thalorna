## Overview

Kavrumiq, Walrus Place, stands on a cape in the middle of the [[place-tikumanikrgn|Ice Coast]], above a gravel beach where walrus haul out in hundreds every spring and autumn. About 600 people live in it, the largest village of the [[affiliation-tikupilarit|Tikupilarit]]. Its men's house, dug into the beach ridge at the root of the cape, is the greatest on the coast: forty paces around, roofed with driftwood logs a man's thigh thick, with room on its benches for every man and boy of the village and their guests from three others.

## The Walrus Beach

The walrus are the village. In spring the hunters go out to the ice edge for them in crews of three kayaks, and in autumn the herds haul out on the beach below the cape, and the men take them there with harpoons and lances, a few at a time, so that the herd does not leave. Nobody shouts on the beach in autumn and no dog is let loose, because a frightened herd goes elsewhere and does not come back for years. The village's oil, hide, ivory and meat come from that beach, and its wealth in the Messenger Feast with them.

## The Men's House

The Messenger Feast began here, in the coast's telling, when [Piqanmalu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sun Running, walked unarmed into an enemy men's house with an invitation. His harpoon hangs from the roof of the men's house of Kavrumiq above the speaker's place, and it is the only object on the coast older than living memory that nobody may touch.

The speaker of the men's house is the oldest man of the village and the most listened to on the coast. It was he who said, at the Bladder Feast four winters ago, that the Taking was a gift taken and not answered, and Whale Point has not sent its bladders to Kavrumiq's ice hole since. The provider [Kavrukuvla]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Walrus Hunting, sits nearest the elders, and the orphans of the village eat from his catch first.

## Trade

Kavrumiq hosts more Messenger Feasts than any other village, and guests come to it from the river villages and the islands. It gives walrus hide for boat covers, oil and ivory, and takes dried fish, caribou skins, wood and bird skins in return.
