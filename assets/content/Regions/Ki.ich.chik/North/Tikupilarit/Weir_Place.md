## Overview

Taqvamiq, Weir Place, stands on a high bank of a channel of the northern river of the [[place-taluqnapiqrgn|Lake Tundra]], a few days' paddle from the sea. About 300 people live in it. Below the village three weirs (_taqva_) of driftwood stakes and woven willow cross the channel, each with a basket trap at its gap, and in summer the salmon fill them faster than the women can cut and hang the fish on the racks along the bank.

## The River Year

The village follows the river. In spring the seals come up from the sea after the fish, and the men hunt them in the channels from kayaks; the birds arrive, and the children gather eggs. In summer the families go to fish camps along the river and work the weirs. In autumn the whitefish run, and the village comes back together before the ice. In winter the men fish through the ice and trap blackfish in the ponds, and the village holds its feasts.

## The Woman of the Shares

[Taluqivi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Lake Giving Drink, the oldest woman of the village, has given the drink of water to every seal brought into Taqvamiq for fifty years, and taught every girl of the village to cut and to sew. She is remembered for the hungry winter, eighteen years ago, when the fish failed and she took charge of every cache in the village and divided them so that every house had the same. The coast names her when it says what a woman should be, and the Feast of the Dead at Taqvamiq is the best attended on the river, because the families who were fed that winter come back to honor her dead.

## Trade

In spring, when the river opens, the forest hunters come down from the spruce country upriver with caribou hides, wolverine and marten fur and rolls of birch bark, and camp across the channel. The trade is done with signs over a fire on the bank: oil and dried fish for hides and fur. The forest hunters do not enter the village, and nobody of Taqvamiq goes up the river past the first spruce.
