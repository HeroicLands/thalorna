## Overview

Simi Manik, Trading Shore, is the last village on the [[place-tikumanikrgn|Ice Coast]] before the glaciers and fjords of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Cedar People's country]]. About 250 people live in it, on a sand beach in the lee of a headland, the only beach for days in either direction where a Cedar war canoe can land in a swell. A watcher sits on the headland every day from the breakup of the ice to the first snow.

## The Midsummer Trade

Every midsummer the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]] come north in a few large canoes and land on the beach under the headland. They bring bent cedar boxes, eulachon grease in boxes, cedar-bark mats and now and then a sheet of hammered copper, and they take walrus ivory, fox and wolverine furs, gut parkas, seal oil and carved bone. The trade lasts three days. Neither side sleeps in the other's camp, and the bargaining is done with signs and a few dozen words each has learned of the other's tongue. Much of what the Cedar People bring goes north up the coast at the Messenger Feasts, and a cedar box is a gift any provider is glad of.

## The Watch

Every few years the canoes that come north carry warriors instead of goods, and they land at night to take slaves. Trading Shore is the only village on the coast that still keeps armor (_salaq_) of bone and ivory plates in use, and every man of the village owns a set and a sinew-backed bow. When the watcher sees canoes that do not come to the beach in daylight, the women and children go inland across the tundra to a camp among the lakes, and the men put on the armor. The last raid took nine people, a dozen winters ago. Two of them were ransomed at the next midsummer trade for ivory, and the village still speaks of the other seven by name, and has not given those names to any child.
