## Overview

The Ice Coast (_tiku manik_) is the shore of the [[affiliation-tikupilarit|Tikupilarit]], the Ice-Edge People, on the far northwestern coast of [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]], beyond the last fjords of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Kelwanuk Region]]. It runs north from the glaciers where the Cedar People's country ends, along a low coast of capes, spits and lagoons facing a shallow western sea, to a point where the land turns east along the northern ocean. About 32,000 people live on it in some hundred and twenty villages, nearly all of them on capes, spits and islands where the sea comes close.

## Geography

The sea is the country. It is shallow and grey, and every autumn it freezes from the shore outward. By midwinter the ice lies fast to the coast for a day's walk out, and beyond that the pack ice grinds and drifts, opening and closing in long leads of black water. The line between the two is the ice edge (_tikupila_), where seals and walrus come up to breathe and haul out. In spring the hunters go out to it on foot, dragging their kayaks on sleds, and when the fast ice breaks up the walrus come north through the strait on the floes in herds of hundreds. By midsummer the sea is open from shore to sky, and the kayaks and skin boats have it all.

The land behind the shore is low tundra: moss, sedge and dwarf willow over frozen ground, with no tree within sight of the sea. Driftwood, carried down the rivers from the forests far inland and thrown up on the beaches by the storms, is the only timber on the coast, and every house, kayak frame and harpoon shaft is made of it. A village with a good drift beach is rich in a way the others are not. In the south, where the coast meets the Cedar People's country, mountains and glaciers come down to the sea; in the north, the hills of a long peninsula rise behind the capes.

Offshore lie a few islands. Most are low and windy. One is a single rock rising sheer from the sea, with [[place-napamiq|Napamiq]], Cliff Place, built up its southern face. Walrus haul out on its beaches in thousands every autumn.

The winters are long and dark, with storms off the pack that drive snow sideways for days. In midwinter the sun at Whale Point does not rise. The summers are cool, foggy and bright, and the sun hardly sets.

## Villages

A village is a men's house dug half into a beach ridge, with the women's sod houses around it, drying racks for fish and meat, kayaks on racks above the dogs' reach, and a cemetery of driftwood boxes on the tundra behind. Most hold a hundred to three hundred people. The largest is [[place-kavrumiq|Kavrumiq]], Walrus Place, on a cape in the middle of the coast, where the coast's greatest men's house stands and the Messenger Feast began. [[place-imlakkagaq|Imlak Kagaq]], Whale Point, at the northern tip, is the richest, and the crews of the East People set out from it. [[place-simimanik|Simi Manik]], Trading Shore, is the southernmost, where the Cedar People come to trade.

North of Whale Point the coast turns east along the northern ocean, and that coast belongs to the East People, the kin who have gone east along the ice. The home coast knows it only from the crews that come back.

## Trade

The coast trades with itself at the Messenger Feast: oil, ivory and walrus hide from the capes go to the river villages, and dried fish, caribou skins and wood from up the rivers come back. Whale Point sends whale bone and baleen south, and Napamiq's ivory carving goes everywhere. At midsummer the Cedar People bring cedar boxes, eulachon grease and copper to Trading Shore and take ivory, furs, gut parkas and oil. Almost nothing from farther south reaches the coast.
