## Overview

A canoe coming in from the sea sees the village before it sees the beach: a long row of cedar plank houses above the tide line, each with a crest pole before its door carved with the beasts of the lineage that lives there, and the tallest pole of all before the house of the High Chief. The south calls the place Ish'bal Ki'ahk, after the nation, and counts it as the seat of the [[affiliation-ixbalkiahk|Cedar Kingdom]]. About 3,000 people live here, at the mouth of a salmon river on a fjord of the far western coast, where cedar forest runs from the water to a spine of snow-crowned volcanic peaks.

## The Houses

Each house is a single great room of split cedar planks on a frame of carved posts, home to a noble lineage, its commoners and its slaves, with the chief's place at the back, farthest from the door. The paramount house is the largest. When the High Chief gives a feast, its doors stand open for days, the beach fills with the canoes of the other houses, and the gifts are piled where every guest can count them.

## The Year

The salmon run is the pulse of the village's year. In late summer the whole village works at the river, taking, splitting, drying and smoking fish enough to last until spring. In winter the houses turn to feasting and to the masked dance dramas, and the war canoes are drawn up under cover until the raiding season.
