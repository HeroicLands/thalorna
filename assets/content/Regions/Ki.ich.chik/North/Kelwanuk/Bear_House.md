## The Bear House

The village of [[place-lamekis|Bear Beach]] is the only one on the Cedar People's coast that needs two rows of houses, and at the end of the double row stands the Bear House's great house, its crest pole topped by a bear holding a copper. Every spring two plank canoes come up the empty shore from the south, low in the water with beads, and run in over the bar of the South River to its beach. Every summer the house's war canoes come back up the same river with captives.

The **Bear House**, the _lamekanuk_, is the noble house of Lamekis, the southernmost village of the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]], on a sandspit behind the river bar at the end of the [[place-yumisrgn|Outer Beaches]]. Its lineage has grown with its wealth until the whole village is its people, and its chief gives the greatest feasts on the coast.

## Membership

The house holds its chief and his kin, its nobles with their names and seats, its commoners and its slaves, as every house does, and its branches fill the double row behind the great house. The chief is [[being-yaldukita|Yaldukita]], Gives Wealth, the third of his house to hold its highest name and the first to make it matter. His Keeper of the Count is his mother's sister, [Tsamalkalu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Counts the Salmon, who has kept the house's count for thirty years and has never been wrong in public.

## Standing

The Bear House receives the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} for the whole coast: the beads the chiefs of the northern [[affiliation-shuqash|Shell-Bead People]] towns send each spring so that the Cedar war canoes leave their towns alone. The house gives the Strand away at its feasts, a strand to each guest in the order of seats, and every house that takes one owes the Bear House a greater gift and has agreed to leave the Shell-Bead towns alone. Its war canoes go up the rivers of the great bay south of the river mouth instead, into the oak valleys, where nobody pays, and it sells the captives on its beach to any house that can pay, in blankets, grease and copper.

The coast is divided about the house. The northern houses say that beads paid to keep raiders away are a price, not wealth, and the Bear House answers that every copper on the coast was paid for by someone. It has given two feasts in four years that the [[affiliation-eaglehouse|Eagle House]] has not answered, and this winter it gives a third. If the Eagle House cannot answer it, the coast seats the Bear House first.

## Rivals

**The Eagle House.** The house that sits first has answered every challenge for four generations of chiefs, and it has not answered this one yet.

**The Shell-Bead towns.** They pay the Strand, and they buy back the captives the Bear House's canoes take from the valleys behind them. The canoe master [[being-sitiqnush|Sitiqnush]] of the [[affiliation-canoekin|Canoe Kin]] comes north three times a year to buy them back, pays exactly and never argues, and the Bear House treats him as an honored guest and sells to him at its own price.

**The northern houses.** The houses of the inlets hold that a gift made of another people's fear is no gift, and the Keeper of the Count of the Orca House, [[being-kalunukwas|Kalunukwas]], has said that she will count what is given at the feast and nothing about where it came from.

## What a Party Can Ask of It

- **A captive.** The beach at Lamekis sells slaves to any house that can pay, and it sells captives back to their own people at the house's price.
- **Paid witness.** The house pays witnesses (_nilkwamis_) to see and remember its feasts, and this winter's feast needs more than any before it.
- **War canoes.** The house buys its war canoes from the Thunderbird House of [[place-gwemlasis|Thunderbird Beach]], and a party that can carry word or wealth between the two beaches is welcome at both.
- **The river.** In danger the people of Lamekis take to their canoes and go up the South River, and they know it a mile wide and far inland.
