# terran_analog: the outer coast of Washington and northern Oregon, from Cape Flattery to the Columbia mouth: Makah whaling villages such as Ozette, and the Chinookan towns at the mouth of the Columbia, the great slave and trade market of the coast
---

## Overview

South of the inlets the coast turns its face to the open sea. The Outer Beaches (_tsawis yumis_, the seaward beaches) are the southern half of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Kelwanuk Region]]: long sand beaches between rocky headlands, with the surf of the whole ocean breaking on them, and the broad mouth of the [South River]{.name data-type="place/feature"} at the southern end. About 80,000 [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]] live here. This is the coast that hunts whales, the coast that receives the beads of the south, and since the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} began, the coast that grows richer every year.

## Geography

The mountains stand back from the sea here, behind a strip of rainforest and lagoon, and the rivers come out of them short and fast onto the beaches. The villages stand behind the beaches in the lee of headlands, where a canoe can be run up the sand between the waves. The sea is rougher than in the inlets and the weather worse, and the villages lose canoes and men to it every winter.

Whales pass close inshore every spring on their way north and every autumn on their way south. The Orca House of [[place-malkisis|Malkisis]] and the houses near it hunt them from canoes with harpoons of mussel shell and floats of sealskin, and a whale towed home feeds a village for a month and gives oil for a year.

At the southern end the South River comes out of the mountains, wide enough that a man on one bank cannot see the people on the other. Far upstream, beyond the mountains, the river drops over the great falls where the Grass People fish. At its mouth, on a sandspit behind the bar, stands [[place-lamekis|Lamekis]], Bear Beach. South of the river mouth the shore is empty for many days' paddle, until the first walled town of the [Shell-Bead People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}.

## Villages

The Outer Beaches hold some forty villages. Two matter to the whole coast.

- [[place-malkisis|Malkisis]], Orca Beach, about 2,400 people, on a whaling beach in the north of the country: the Orca House, which the coast remembers for the Killing Feast and for the ruling that ended it.
- [[place-lamekis|Lamekis]], Bear Beach, about 1,500, at the mouth of the South River: the Bear House, which receives the Northern Strand and sells captives up the coast.

## What Moves Through It

Beads come up from the south. Every spring the chiefs of the northern Shell-Bead towns send two canoes of drilled shell beads to Lamekis, and the Bear House gives them away along the coast at its feasts. Captives come up too, taken by the war canoes in the oak valleys behind the Shell-Bead coast and sold at Lamekis to any house that can pay. Whale oil, dried halibut and canoes go south to the Shell-Bead People in autumn, when the Cedar People trade with them in peace.

Up the South River, the traders of the Outer Beaches go inland in summer to the falls, and come back with robes and dried roots.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Outer Beaches are where the slavery argument of the coast is loudest. The Orca House frees slaves at its feasts; the Bear House sells them at its beach. The two houses sit near each other in the order of seats, and the guests who eat at one in autumn eat at the other in winter. Neither chief speaks of the other's practice before guests.
