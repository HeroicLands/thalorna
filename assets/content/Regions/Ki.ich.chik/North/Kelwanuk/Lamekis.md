# terran_analog: the Chinookan towns at the mouth of the Columbia, the great slave and trade market of the Northwest Coast
---

## Overview

Every spring two plank canoes come up the empty shore from the south, low in the water, and run in over the bar of the [South River]{.name data-type="place/feature"} to the beach at Lamekis. They carry beads: the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, sent by the chiefs of the northern [Shell-Bead People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} so that the Cedar war canoes leave their towns alone. Lamekis, Bear Beach, is the southernmost village of the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]], on a sandspit behind the river bar at the southern end of the [[place-yumisrgn|Outer Beaches]], and about 1,500 people of the Bear House live here. Twenty years ago it was a minor village of a minor house. Now its chief gives the greatest feasts on the coast.

## The Strand and the Market

The Bear House receives the Strand for the whole coast, and gives it away at its feasts, a strand to each guest in the order of seats. Every house that takes a strand has accepted the Bear House's gift and owes it a greater one, and every house that takes one has agreed, in the way the coast agrees to anything, to leave the Shell-Bead towns alone. The war canoes go up the rivers from the great bay south of the river mouth instead, into the oak valleys, where nobody pays.

The captives come back to Lamekis. The Bear House sells them on its beach to any house that can pay, in blankets, grease and copper, and the slaves of half the houses of the coast passed through Lamekis. Three times a year a Shell-Bead canoe master, [Sitiqnush]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, comes north to buy captives back, and the Bear House sells to him at its own price. He pays exactly and never argues, and the Bear House treats him as an honored guest.

## Yaldukita

The chief of the Bear House is [Yaldukita]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Gives Wealth, the third of his house to hold its highest name and the first to make it matter. He has given two feasts in four years greater than anything the Eagle House of [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]] has answered, and this winter he gives a third. If the Eagle House cannot answer it, the coast seats the Bear House first.

The coast is divided about it. The northern houses say that beads paid to keep raiders away are a price, not wealth, and that a gift made of another people's fear is no gift. The Bear House answers that every copper on the coast was paid for by someone. The Keeper of the Count of the Orca House, [Kalunukwas]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has been asked whether she will count the Strand as the Bear House's gift, and has said she will count what is given at the feast, and nothing about where it came from.

Yaldukita's own Keeper of the Count is his mother's sister, [Tsamalkalu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Counts the Salmon, who has kept the Bear House's count for thirty years and has never been wrong in public.

:::secret {title="The Strand buys less"}

Tsamalkalu knows what Yaldukita has not admitted. There are more beads on the Shell-Bead coast every year, and each strand buys less: the grease, blankets and copper a strand fetched when the Strand began now take a strand and a half. The Bear House's feasts count the beads at the old worth, and its guests count them so too, but the debts those feasts create are coming back to Lamekis as gifts it must answer in grease and copper, at the new worth. The house is already further in debt than the coast knows. Yaldukita means to cover it with more captives, and he has told his war canoe captains that after this winter's feast they may raid the Shell-Bead coast towns themselves, Strand or no Strand. Tsamalkalu has not decided whether to stop him at the count, as the first Kalunukwas stopped the killing.

:::

## The Village

The houses stand in a double row on the sandspit, the only village on the coast that needs two rows, with the Bear House's great house at the end, its crest pole topped by a bear holding a copper. Behind the spit lie marshes and a lagoon full of wildfowl, and the river beyond is a mile wide. The village has no headland refuge; it has the river, and in danger its people take to the canoes and go upstream.
