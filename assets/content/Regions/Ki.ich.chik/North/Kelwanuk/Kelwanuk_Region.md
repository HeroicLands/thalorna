## Overview

Kelwanuk Region is the land of the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]], the Cedar People, and lies within [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]]. It is the far western coast of the north: a strip of fjords, islands and outer beaches between the open sea and a wall of snow-crowned volcanic peaks, with cedar and redwood forest running from the tide line up into the clouds. About 200,000 people live in it, nearly all of them in villages on the beaches, and the forest behind them is crossed by the rivers and little else.

## Geography

The coast is cut into fjords by ancient ice. In the north the fjords run far inland between cliffs, and the villages stand at their heads where the salmon rivers come down; in the south the coast opens into sandy outer beaches and broad river mouths, with islands offshore. The temperate rainforest is among the densest and most productive country in all of Ki'ich'chik, its canopy so thick that the forest floor lies in twilight at midday, and it rains for most of the winter. The coastal waters hold seal, sea otter, shellfish and the great whales, and every river of any size carries a salmon run each year.

The Cedar People live on the beaches and the rivers and leave the high country alone. Above the forest rises the mountain spine, a chain of volcanic peaks whose summits stand above the clouds, where eagles nest and the snow lies most of the year. The few passes through it are open only in high summer.

The Cedar People's land ends at the crest of the spine. East of the passes, where the cedar gives way to spruce and pine and the land falls toward the plateaus, the country belongs to the hunting bands of the Grass People in the north and to the Turquoise People in the south, and the Cedar People's traders go there only in summer, by the few passes they know.

## Countries

The coast falls into three countries.

- **The [[place-hapasrgn|Long Inlets]]**, about 90,000 people, the northern fjords, where the eulachon rivers run and the house that sits first lives.
- **The [[place-yumisrgn|Outer Beaches]]**, about 80,000, the southern surf coast and the mouth of the [South River]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, where the whalers live and the beads of the south come in.
- **The [[place-yatsalargn|Outer Islands]]**, about 30,000, the great islands across the strait, where the war canoes are built.

## Villages

A village is a single row of plank houses facing a beach, with the canoes drawn up before them and the crest poles standing at their doors. Most hold a few hundred people in four to ten houses, each house a lineage of its own. The largest is [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]], Eagle Beach, at the mouth of a salmon river on a northern fjord, where the house that sits first on the coast lives. [[place-malkisis|Malkisis]], Orca Beach, the village of the Orca House, stands on the outer coast to the south. The houses that matter most to the coast are these.

- [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]], Eagle Beach, about 3,000, in the Long Inlets: the Eagle House, which holds the first seat.
- [[place-malkisis|Malkisis]], Orca Beach, about 2,400, on the Outer Beaches: the Orca House, which keeps the count and frees slaves.
- [[place-gwemlasis|Gwemlasis]], Thunderbird Beach, about 1,800, on the Outer Islands: the Thunderbird House, the canoe builders.
- [[place-lamekis|Lamekis]], Bear Beach, about 1,500, at the mouth of the South River: the Bear House, which receives the beads of the south and challenges for the first seat.
- [[place-mukwasis|Mukwasis]], Wolf Beach, about 1,200, in the Long Inlets: the Wolf House, at the foot of the Grease Trail over the mountains.

Most villages keep a refuge on a steep headland nearby, walled with logs, where the people go when raiders come.

## Trade

The coast trades with itself first: grease, dried salmon, cedar boxes, blankets and canoes move between the houses, and much of it changes hands at giving feasts. To the south the Cedar People trade along the coast with the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shell-Bead People]] of the [[place-shuqhalaqrgn|Bead Coast]], and in summer their traders cross the passes to the Grass People and the Turquoise People, from whom a few southern goods reach the coast. The high passes close in winter, so inland trade is seasonal and its prices volatile.

To the north, beyond the last villages, the [Ice-Coast Hunters]{.name data-type="lore/culture"} trade ivory and the furs of animals unknown farther south, and bone carved in their own style, for cedar boxes and grease.
