## Overview

A canoe coming in from the sea sees the village before it sees the beach: a long row of cedar plank houses above the tide line, each with a crest pole before its door carved with the beings of the lineage that lives there, and the tallest pole of all, topped by an eagle with spread wings, before the house that sits first on the coast. This is Tsilkasis, Eagle Beach, where the first Tsilkas came down from the sky as an eagle and took off his mask. About 3,000 people live here, at the mouth of a salmon river at the head of a northern fjord of the [[place-hapasrgn|Long Inlets]], where cedar forest runs from the water to a spine of snow-crowned volcanic peaks.

The south counts Tsilkasis as the seat of the whole nation, because its traders met the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]] here first and named them the Eagle People after its poles. The Cedar People count it as one village among many, whose Eagle House happens to sit first, and which has sat lower before.

## The Houses

Twelve houses stand on the beach. Each is a single great room of split cedar planks on a frame of carved posts, home to a lineage, its commoners and its slaves, with the chief's place at the back, farthest from the door. The Eagle House is the largest, and its posts are the work of [Kwemlatsuma]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the master carver of the Raven House next door. When the Eagle House gives a feast, its doors stand open for days, the beach fills with the canoes of the other houses, and the gifts are piled where every guest can count them.

The chief of the Eagle House is the ninth [Lukwahakwa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who holds the first seat on the coast. The first to hold his name gave the [Killing Feast]{.name data-type="lore/history"} on this beach, and the people of Tsilkasis do not speak of it to guests.

## The Challenge

This winter the Eagle House must answer the Bear House of [[place-lamekis|Lamekis]], which has given two feasts in four years that the Eagle House has not matched, and gives a third this winter. Lukwahakwa has broken one copper already to answer the second, and his commoners have worked two summers at the weir and the rendering pits for the feast he has not yet given. His nephew [Tuklaskapala]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who holds the coast's seat at the compact's council, has told him that the Bear House's wealth is beads paid by frightened towns, and that the Eagle House should say so at its feast. Lukwahakwa has answered that the first chiefs of the Eagle House grew rich on captives too, and that a house that asks where its rival's wealth came from invites the same question about its own.

Every midsummer the Eagle House sends a few big canoes north past the glaciers to [Trading Shore]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, the southernmost village of the [Ice-Edge People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, with cedar boxes, grease and now and then a sheet of copper, and the ivory and furs they bring home go into the Eagle House's feasts.

## The Year

The salmon run is the pulse of the village's year. In late summer the whole village works at the weir across the river, taking, splitting, drying and smoking fish enough to last until spring. In autumn the houses give and attend feasts, and in winter they turn to the masked dances, and the war canoes are drawn up under cover until the raiding season. At the end of winter the eulachon crowd the river mouth, and the village renders their grease in pits along the shore.
