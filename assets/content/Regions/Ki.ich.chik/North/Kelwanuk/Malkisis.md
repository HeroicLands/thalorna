# terran_analog: a Makah whaling village of the outer Washington coast, such as Ozette, with Kwakwaka'wakw houses and feasts

## Overview

At the north end of the beach at Malkisis there is a stretch of sand, between two black rocks, where nobody draws up a canoe and children are told not to play. That is where the twenty died. Malkisis, Orca Beach, is the village of the Orca House, on a whaling beach of the [[place-yumisrgn|Outer Beaches]] where an orca walked out of the sea in the first days and took off its fin to become the first chief. About 2,400 [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]] live here, in eleven plank houses facing the surf, and it is the second village of the coast after [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]].

## The Whalers

Malkisis hunts whales. In spring, when the whales pass north close inshore, eight-man canoes go out from the beach with a harpooner in the bow, and a whale struck with a harpoon of mussel shell is held with sealskin floats until it tires, then killed with lances and towed home, sometimes over two days. The harpooner is always a noble of the Orca House, and he has spent the winter bathing in the sea at night, scrubbing with hemlock, and praying to the whale to give itself. The whole village cuts up the whale on the beach, and every house takes its share in the order of seats.

## The Count

The Orca House keeps the count. Its Keeper of the Count is [Kalunukwas]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the seventh to hold the name, who holds forty years of the coast's gifts and debts in her memory. The first Kalunukwas refused to count the twenty killed at the Killing Feast, and her ruling that a dead man is given to nobody is the law of every feast on the coast. Houses in dispute over an old debt send for her, pay her in blankets, and accept what she says, though many of them would rather not.

## The Freed

The chief of the Orca House, the fifth [Kwemulkita]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds the name of the chief who killed the twenty. At his last three feasts he has freed a slave before the guests: led a man or a woman out of the back of the house to the front, given them a blanket and a small name, and told the guests that the person was a gift. Kalunukwas counts each one aloud as a gift given. The guests have to decide whether to accept it, and the Eagle House's guests have twice walked out before the blankets, which means they do not.

The freed live in the Orca House as commoners. One of them, a woman taken from the valleys behind the Shell-Bead coast, has married a commoner of the house, and her children are the first on the coast born to a person who was once a slave and is not.

:::secret {title="Why the fifth Kwemulkita frees slaves"}

Kwemulkita frees slaves because Kalunukwas told him to, privately, five winters ago, and told him why. A gift that guests refuse cannot be answered, so the Eagle House cannot match it, and every freed slave the Eagle House refuses is a gift the coast sees the Orca House give and the Eagle House fail to receive. Kwemulkita believes he is shaming the Eagle House out of the first seat. Kalunukwas believes the first Kalunukwas was right. Neither has asked the other which it is.

:::

## The Village

The houses face the surf behind a bank of driftwood logs that breaks the winter waves. Behind them, in the edge of the forest, stand the grave houses of the Orca House's dead and a mortuary pole carved with an orca, on which the first Kwemulkita lies in his box. On the headland to the south is the refuge, walled with logs. The winter dance house stands apart from the others at the forest edge, and in the dark months the Keeper of the Masks of Malkisis, [Gunistalwa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a commoner, rules it; the [[lore-nakpulalas|Meeting]], the faith of the coast, tells of the boy he brought back last winter.
