## Overview

The Canal Plain (Kenuva Soko) is the hot lowland of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]], where two rivers come down out of the mountains onto a flat desert floor and the towns lead their water across it in canals dug by hand. It lies southwest of the mesa country of the [[place-teyumirgn|Teyumi Region]], on the edge of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul]], and about 50,000 people live on it, a third of the nation. In a plain where nothing grows without the canals, every field, every town and every quarrel follows a canal.

## Geography

The plain is flat, wide and very hot. Summer days are hotter than anywhere else the Turquoise People live, and winters are mild enough that a lowland farmer plants twice in a good year. The two rivers run all year, fed by snow on the mountains to the north and east, and swell in spring. Away from the rivers the land is desert: creosote, giant cactus, mesquite thickets along the dry washes, and bare stony slopes.

The canals change all of that. A main canal leaves a river at a head gate of stones and brush, runs for miles along the slope of the plain, and splits into branches and then into the ditches that water single fields. The longest canals on the plain run for most of a day's walk, as wide as a road and deeper than a man is tall. Along them, for as far as the water goes, the land is green with corn, cotton, beans, squash and tobacco.

## Towns and Water

The lowland towns are spread out along the canals, each a scatter of adobe household compounds around a great house on a platform mound. [[place-kenuvahosa|Kenuvahosa]], the Canal House, is the largest, at the head of the main canal of the northern river. Each town that holds a head gate has a canal chief (_kenuvamota_) of its great-house kin, who names the water turns of every household on his canal, and the towns downstream on the same canal take what he lets through.

That makes the plain quarrelsome in a dry year. When the rivers run low in early summer, the upstream towns take water first and the downstream towns wait, and the quarrels go to the chamber elders and then to the canyon feast, where the canyon towns, who have no canals, hear them with a patience the lowlanders find hard to bear. The lowlanders speak their tongue quickly, clipping the long vowels, and the canyon towns say that they plant twice a year because they cannot wait.

## Trade and Danger

The plain is the start of the western trail. Every summer a trading party goes west from the Canal House over the mountains and along a line of desert springs to the coast of the [Shell-Bead People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, carrying turquoise and cotton cloth and bringing back shell. The plain grows more cotton than the rest of the nation together, and its cloth goes up the roads to every great house.

The desert begins at the last canal. Southern traders who cross it usually bear east to [[place-kwatuhosa|Kwatuhosa]], but the plain is close to the desert's western routes, and a trader who comes up out of the sand at a canal town is fed like any other guest. A southern war party could come the same way. The plain's towns have signal fires on the rocky hills between the rivers, and their great houses stand on their mounds with the ladders ready to draw up, but the households of the plain live spread out along the canals, far from their mounds.
