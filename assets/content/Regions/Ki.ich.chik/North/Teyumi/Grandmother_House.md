# terran_analog: Pueblo Bonito in Chaco Canyon, the D-shaped great house of about 650 rooms with its great kiva, its walled plaza and the trash mound where pots were broken
---

## Overview

From the rim of the [[place-hosaoyalargn|Canyon of Houses]] the Grandmother House looks like a half-moon of stone laid on the canyon floor: a curved back wall five stories high against the north cliff, terraces stepping down inside it, and a straight wall closing the plaza on the south. Yaahosa, the Grandmother House, is the first great house of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Teyumi]], the Turquoise People, raised where [Teyuwesa]{.name data-type="being/npc"} stopped walking. It has some six hundred rooms. About 1,200 people live in it and in the small houses on the canyon floor below it, and at the canyon feast ten times that many camp along the wash.

Every town of the Turquoise People counts itself a daughter of this house, and the Grandmother House is the one place where the towns meet as one. It commands none of them.

## The House

The rooms at the back are the oldest, dark and low, and the dead of the house lie in them wrapped in cotton. The newer rooms in front hold the storerooms (_mehu_), more of them than in any other great house, because the pilgrims of every town bring corn to the feast and the house feeds the feast from it. The plaza holds a dozen chambers (_pelu_) and one great chamber, three times the width of the others, whose roof stands on four pillars of stacked stone. The chamber elders teach that the navel in its floor is the first one, through which the people came up into this world, and that every other chamber's navel leads down to the same place.

Outside the south wall lies the great mound, a long heap of earth and broken pottery higher than a man. At the end of every canyon feast the pilgrims climb it and break the pots they carried, and the mound has grown with every feast the house has held.

## The Sun Watcher's Notch

The sun watcher (_waimayo_) of the Grandmother House stands each morning at a marked stone on the roof of the back rooms and reads the sunrise against the canyon's south rim. On the morning of the sun-turning the sun comes up in a single notch in the rim, and the watcher calls the day from the roof. Every town reads its own sunrise against its own wall, but the canyon feast is held on the day the Grandmother House names, and runners (_ponuyo_) carry the count out along the roads so that the towns can start walking in time.

## The Rain Chief and the House Head

The house head (_hosamota_) is [Yalutseya]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Cloud clan, a heavy, quiet woman of sixty who has kept the storerooms for twenty years and swore her oath in the doorway of [[place-hepuhosa|Hepuhosa]] across the canyon. Her younger brother [Waimahalu]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is the rain chief (_lumamota_). At the feast he speaks first, and she decides who eats.

The two have stopped agreeing. Three summers have come up short of rain, and at last year's feast Waimahalu said aloud that the rain was being kept from the canyon, and that the bear doctors (_matsauyo_) should be asked to search the towns for the dry hearts responsible. No Dry Search has been held at the feast in Yalutseya's lifetime. She has said nothing in public, which in the canyon is itself a statement. This autumn's feast will decide whether the search goes ahead.

## The House in the Year of Shut Doors

The Grandmother House kept its ladders up through all nine years of the [Year of Shut Doors]{.name data-type="lore/history"}. The canyon remembers it as the house that held the most corn and opened none of it, and the rain chiefs of the house have answered for that at every feast since. The storerooms are full today because the pilgrims fill them, and the oath its house head swears is the oath that the other house across the canyon made necessary.
