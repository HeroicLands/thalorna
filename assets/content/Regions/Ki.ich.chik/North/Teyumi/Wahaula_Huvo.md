## Overview

The Cave Mesas (Wahaula Huvo) are the high northern country of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]]: broad mesas with juniper and piñon on their tops, cut by canyons so deep and narrow that a town on one rim can shout to the next and walk half a day to reach it. About 45,000 people live in them, and over the last generation more and more of them have moved their homes down off the mesa tops into the great alcoves weathered into the canyon walls. The mesas take their name from those alcoves. They are the wettest part of the [[place-teyumirgn|Teyumi Region]], and they are not at peace.

## Geography

The mesas stand higher than the [[place-hosaoyalargn|Canyon of Houses]] three days to the south, and the air on them is cooler and wetter. Snow lies on their tops through the winter, and its melt soaks the red soil deep enough for dry-farmed corn to grow in rows on the mesa tops and on terraces down the canyon sides. Springs seep from the base of the cliffs, and the alcoves form where they do: long, shallow caves in the pale sandstone, some big enough to roof a town.

On the eastern edge of the mesas a long ridge runs out toward the mountains, and at its end stands [[place-yaluhepu|Yaluhepu]], the Moon Door, the farthest north of the great houses. Beyond the ridge the mountains rise toward the snow peaks, hunting country that no town holds, crossed by the Grass People's bands and the Cedar People's traders.

## The Cliff Towns

A generation ago the towns of the Cave Mesas lived on the mesa tops beside their fields, in houses of a few dozen rooms around open chambers. Today most of them live in the alcoves. A cliff town is a block of stone rooms, towers and round chambers built under an overhang, with the cliff above it and a sheer drop below, reached by toeholds cut in the rock and by ladders that can be pulled up. The fields are still on the mesa tops, and the women carry the corn and the water down to the town every evening.

The cliff towns explain the move in different ways. Their elders say the alcoves are warmer in winter and nearer the springs. Their young men say plainly that the alcoves are easier to hold. Three short summers have left the mesa-top towns short of corn, and a hungry town does not stay hungry quietly: storerooms have been raided, a granary in a side canyon was burned last winter, and the bodies of two men of a cliff town were found at the foot of its own ladders. No raider has been caught. The cliff towns blame the Dry, the people put out of their towns as dry hearts, who are known to be living in the empty houses of the side canyons. Some families have left the mesas altogether and walked south to the Door House at [[place-hepuhosa|Hepuhosa]] to be fed.

## Neighbors and Trade

The Cave Mesas are the Turquoise People's frontier with the mountains and the plains. The bands of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]] come to the eastern mesas in autumn to trade meat and hides for corn and cloth, and a few of their young men winter in the cliff towns as guests. The mesas send their corn, their pottery and their black-on-white painted mugs south to the canyon on the roads, and the signal fire on the Moon Door can call the whole north to arms in an evening.
