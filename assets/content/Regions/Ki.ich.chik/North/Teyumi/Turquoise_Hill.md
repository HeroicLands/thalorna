## Overview

The hills north of the [[place-hosaoyalargn|Canyon of Houses]] are bare and pale, and their slopes are pocked with pits and heaps of broken rock as far as a person can see from the top of any one of them. Teyu Potama, Turquoise Hill, is the camp among the pits: about 400 people in a cluster of stone rooms around a single chamber, with as many again in the summer, when the work parties come. Every piece of turquoise (_teyu_) the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] wear, give to their dead and send south comes out of these hills.

## The Digging

The stone lies in thin blue veins in a soft white rock. The diggers break the rock with stone mauls and pry the veins out with picks of antler, in pits that run from a man's depth to ten times that, braced with juniper poles. Before a digger goes down a pit he leaves a pinch of cornmeal at its lip for the clouds, because turquoise is rain made stone and the hills are giving it up. A digger who finds a pale or greenish stone leaves it in the pit. Such a stone is called dry, and nobody wants it.

No town owns the hills. Each town sends a work party in its turn, and the rain chief of [[place-yaahosa|Yaahosa]] names the turns at the canyon feast. The stone a party digs goes to its own town, less a share left at the Grandmother House for the feast, the dead of the canyon and the south. Most of the stone is drilled and polished into beads in the canyon, though a few of the camp's families drill beads and do nothing else.

## The People of the Camp

The families who live at Turquoise Hill all year keep the pits braced and the work parties fed, and they know which pits are safe. They are a mixed lot: younger sons who married into the camp, widowers, and families who left a dry town and stayed. The camp has no great house, and its storerooms are filled from Yaahosa. The canyon holds its people to be a little strange, because the camp feeds anyone who picks up a maul and does not ask where he came from.

## The Quiet Pits

The deep pits on the eastern hill have gone quiet. A work party from a canyon town came up out of one at midsummer and would not go down again. They said they heard something moving below the lowest brace, and that the cornmeal at the lip was gone in the morning. The camp's people say a pit that deep always sounds like that. The canyon's rain chiefs have sent no party there since, and the stone from the eastern hill was the bluest in the hills.

:::secret {title="What is in the pits"}
People are in them. A band of the Dry, expelled from their towns in the last two short summers, has been living in the worked-out galleries that join the deep pits under the eastern hill, coming up at night for water and for the cornmeal at the pit mouths. Some of the camp's families know and leave food for them, because some of the camp's families were Dry themselves before the camp took them in without asking. The band numbers about thirty, and its oldest member was a chamber elder.
:::
