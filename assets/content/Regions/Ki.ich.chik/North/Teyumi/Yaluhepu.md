# terran_analog: Chimney Rock, the Chacoan outlier on a high mesa in southwestern Colorado whose great house looks between twin rock pillars at the moon's major standstill rising
---

## Overview

Two pillars of red rock stand on the end of a long ridge above the [[place-wahaulahuvorgn|Cave Mesas]], each as tall as a great house is high, and between them is a gap of sky. Once in a generation the moon rises in that gap. Yaluhepu, the Moon Door, is the small great house built on the ridge just below the pillars to watch for it: forty rooms and two chambers, reached by a stair cut into the ridge's spine, with about 300 people of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] living in small houses on the slopes below. It is the farthest outlier of the great houses to the north, and the highest.

## The Far Rising

The moon's rising wanders along the eastern horizon over the years, as the sun's does over a year, and at the far end of its wandering it rises farther north than at any other time. From the roof of the Moon Door that rising falls between the pillars. The towns call the year it happens the [Far Rising]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and every town adds a story or a wing to a great house in that year.

The watcher here is a moon watcher, an initiate who does for the moon what a sun watcher (_waimayo_) does for the sun, and the house's elders keep the count of moons since the last Far Rising in a song that adds a verse each winter. The moon has been rising closer to the gap every year. The watcher says it will rise inside it in two winters, or three.

That makes the Moon Door a house with an argument at its door. The Far Rising means building, and building means feeding the builders through a season. After three short summers, some rain chiefs say that a town that builds at the Far Rising will be answered with rain. Some house heads say there is not enough corn in the north to feed the builders, and that the Far Rising can be honored with a song.

## The Fire

On a ledge beside the eastern pillar the house keeps a signal fire ready, with a fire keeper (_kowayo_) always on the ridge to light it. A fire at the Moon Door can be seen from the rim of the [[place-hosaoyalargn|Canyon of Houses]] three days' walk to the south, and from every mesa in between. When the fire on the Moon Door burns, the cliff towns of the north draw up their ladders without waiting to be told why.

## The Moon Clan

Most of the families at the Moon Door are of the Moon clan, and the house's head and its rain chief are always of it. The clan says that it has watched the pillars since before there was a house, and every other clan in the north agrees, because no other clan wants to live on the ridge. The wind on it in winter is hard, the water is carried up from a spring an hour below, and the corn grows on terraces no wider than a bed.
