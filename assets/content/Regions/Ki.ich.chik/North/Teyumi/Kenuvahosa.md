# terran_analog: the Hohokam towns of the Salt and Gila rivers, such as Snaketown and Mesa Grande, with their platform mounds, ball courts and miles of canals

## Overview

The heat is the first thing a visitor from the uplands notices, and the water is the second. Kenuvahosa, the Canal House, stands on the bank of the great river of the [[place-kenuvasokorgn|Canal Plain]], where the main canal leaves it, and from its great house the canals spread out across the flat land like the veins of a leaf. It is the largest town of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]]: about 3,000 people in adobe houses strung along the canals for most of a morning's walk, among fields of corn, cotton, beans and squash that stay green through a summer that would burn the mesa fields brown.

## The Mound and the Houses

The great house here is not a terraced block of stone. It stands on a platform mound of earth and river cobbles, faced with plaster and as high as three men, with a walled compound of adobe rooms on top. The storerooms are inside the compound and the chambers are sunk into the mound's top. A ramp climbs one side of it, and a ladder is drawn up at the top of the ramp when the fires burn.

The rest of the town is low and spread out. Each household has a compound of adobe rooms around a yard, where the women grind corn in the shade and the men weave cotton. The canyon towns think the lowlanders live too far apart, and the lowlanders find the canyon houses crowded and cold.

## The Water

The canal chief (_kenuvamota_) of the Canal House is [Kenuvawesa]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Water clan, a lean man of fifty who knows every head gate on the plain by its stones. Every household on the main canal and its branches waters its fields by turns, and he names the turns at the spring planting, standing on the mound with the sun watcher. A household that takes water out of turn loses its next turn, and one that does it twice loses its share of the canal. The house head keeps the storerooms as in any great house, but on the plain the canal chief's word is the one a farmer fears.

Each spring, before the head gate is opened, the canal chief and the chamber elders go down to the river at dawn and throw turquoise into the water where the canal leaves it. They call this feeding the [Water Serpent]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}, the snake (_pohiwa_) that lives under the river and lets the water go.

## The Ball Court

North of the mound lies a long oval court sunk a man's height into the ground, with sloping earth banks on either side for the watchers. On feast days the young men of the town play a game there with a heavy ball of southern gum, striking it with their hips and never with their hands. The ball comes across the desert with the traders, and so does the game, by every account but the lowlanders' own. The canyon elders call it a southern thing. The lowlanders say their grandfathers played it, and that the canyon elders have never seen it played well.

## The Western Trail

Every summer a trading party leaves the Canal House for the west, over the mountains and along a trail of desert springs to the [Shell-Bead People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} of the coast. It carries turquoise and cotton cloth and comes back with shell beads and abalone shell, which the cloud dancers wear beside the macaw feathers. The party is the Canal House's own, and the lowland towns take their shell from it before the canyon sees any.

This past winter a southern trader came up out of the desert to the Canal House by a western route, and went no farther. He sold cacao and copper bells on the mound and went home with turquoise, and nobody sent him on to [[place-kwatuhosa|Kwatuhosa]] to see the Keeper of the South Road. The Keeper has sent a runner to ask why. Kenuvawesa says that a guest who comes to the door is fed, and that the south road is not the only road.
