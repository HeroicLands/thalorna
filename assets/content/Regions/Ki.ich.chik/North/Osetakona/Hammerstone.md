## Overview

Hammerstone is heard before it is seen. From the lake a canoe catches the sound of stone on copper from a mile out, a steady knocking from a dozen hearths at once, and then the village comes into sight on a rocky point of the [Copper Lake]{.name data-type="place/feature"}'s northern shore: a palisade of pine stakes, the smoke of forty fires and a beach lined with big lake canoes. The name (_kahnekwaroten_) means the place of hammers. About 1,500 people of the Copper People live here, and it is the largest village of the [[place-kahnionrgn|Western Lakes]] and the place where most of the north's copper is made into something.

## The Smiths

The copper of the Copper Lake comes out of the ground pure, and a smith of Hammerstone works it cold. He sets a lump on a flat anvil stone and beats it with a round hammerstone held in both hands, folding and beating it again, until it is a sheet, a bar or the rough shape of a blade. Then he grinds and polishes it with sand and water. Heating the copper in the fire softens it when it has grown hard under the hammer, and that is all the fire is for. A good smith (_tsikenhahen_) makes a copper awl in a morning, a knife in a day and a speaker's throat plate in a month.

Every longhouse of the village has a smith at its door in summer, and the beach below the palisade is littered with the round grey hammerstones that give the village its name, worn smooth and discarded. A smith's apprentice is his sister's son, and he learns first to choose a hammerstone.

## The Island

The richest copper lies on the long island out in the lake, a day's paddle in good weather. Each summer, after the Sap Feast and the planting, the men of Hammerstone cross to it in the big canoes and dig. Their pits are shallow and old, hundreds of them in rows along the ridges, and some were dug before anyone in the Grove can remember. The diggers light fires on the rock and throw cold water on it to crack it, and pry the copper out with stones and wooden wedges. They leave tobacco at each pit before they dig and come home before the autumn storms.

The Copper People hold that the copper belongs to the [Great Sturgeon]{.name data-type="lore/deity"} under the lake, and that the pure lumps are scales it has shed. The faith of the Grove, the [[lore-tiatorenhsa|Answering]], tells why.

## The Village

Inside the palisade stand thirty longhouses with the clan animals over their doors, and a council house where the Copper People's council sits. The fields are poor, on thin soil between the rocks, and the village buys most of its maize from the Birch People with copper. The canoe beach is the busiest place in the village from the thaw to the freeze: canoes come in from every nation of the Grove to trade for copper, and leave with it for the Falls Country and beyond.

The village's speaker on the great council is [Tsikenhakahtiron]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Holds Copper, of the Otter clan, a smith himself and the best maker of throat plates in the Grove. Half the speakers at the Kettle wear his work.

## What the Village Argues About

The River People want the copper trade with the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]] stopped, because the One Fire's warriors carry copper-headed clubs into their valleys. Hammerstone sells to the One Fire's traders every summer, by way of the Falls Country and the rivers south, and has refused three times at the great council to stop. Tsikenhakahtiron says that copper is the Copper People's maize and that a village does not stop planting because someone might steal its corn. The River People's speakers answer that it is their corn being stolen. His clan mother, [Kenrisatsiotie]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has not told him which answer she wants him to give at the next council, and the village knows she is thinking about it.
