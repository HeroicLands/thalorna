# terran_analog: the upper Great Lakes, Lake Superior's Keweenaw Peninsula and Isle Royale for the copper, and the forest-prairie edge of Wisconsin and Minnesota for the Birch People

## Overview

The Western Lakes (_kahnionten_) are the inland seas of the Grove, three lakes so large that a canoe on any of them loses the far shore by midmorning. Two nations of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]] live on them: the Copper People along the northern shores, who dig the copper the whole north wants, and the Birch People in the forest to the southwest, where the trees give out and the grassland begins. About 160,000 people live here, in the westernmost part of the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]], farther from the Kettle than anyone else in the Grove except the Pine People.

The Western Lakes are the Grove's wealth and its frontier at once. Every sheet of copper in the north starts on one of their beaches, and every raid from the grassland ends at one of their palisades.

## Geography

The westernmost lake is the [Copper Lake]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, cold, deep and clear enough to see a stone at the depth of four men. Its northern shore is bare rock, worn smooth by ice and cut by long ridges running into the water, and in the ridges the copper lies pure, in lumps and threads and sheets that a smith can beat into shape without fire. A long island in the lake holds the richest of it. The Copper People dig there each summer in shallow pits ringed with hammerstones, and leave the island before the autumn storms.

The lake is dangerous. Its storms come up in an hour, its water kills a swimmer with cold before he can drown, and its fogs hide the shore from a canoe a stone's throw out. The Copper People paddle it in big canoes with high ends, and they do not cross open water after the leaves turn.

South and east of the Copper Lake the land softens into forest of birch, maple and pine, with small lakes in every hollow and rivers running south. The Birch People live here. Toward the southwest the trees thin into oak openings and then into tallgrass, and the villages there build double palisades. The grass belongs to the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]], and the [Red Quarry]{.name data-type="place/site"} lies out on it, a few days' walk from the Birch People's last villages.

## The Villages

- [[place-kahnekwaroten|Hammerstone]], about 1,500 people, the chief village of the Copper People, on the Copper Lake's northern shore.
- [[place-kaniestraten|Birches]], about 1,000, a Birch People village on the edge of the grass and the home of the speaker [Skahniotiaro]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.

The Copper People keep seven villages and the Birch People eleven, and the summer camps of both are scattered along every shore.

## What Moves Through It

Copper goes east. It leaves Hammerstone as lumps, sheets and finished blades, crosses the lakes in the big canoes, comes over the portage at the falls into the [[place-kahsiotargn|Falls Country]] and goes on to every nation of the Grove and down the rivers to the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]]. Furs and wild rice go with it. Coming the other way are dried fish and shell from the eastern shore, and maize, which the Copper People grow badly on their thin soil and buy from the Birch People.

The Birch People's trade runs west as well. Every summer they meet the Grass People's bands at the forest's edge with maize, copper and birch canoes, and take robes, dried meat and red stone from the quarry in return. In a year when the bands raid instead, the same villages are the ones burned.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Copper People sell copper to the One Fire, which uses it for the plates and headdresses of its Suns and for the war clubs it carries into the River People's valleys. The River People have asked the great council three times to stop the trade, and three times the Copper People have refused to agree. A council of the Grove decides nothing until every nation agrees, so the copper still goes south, and the River People still bury dead killed under copper blades.
