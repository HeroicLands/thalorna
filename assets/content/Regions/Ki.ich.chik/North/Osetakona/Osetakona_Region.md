## Overview

Osetakona Region is the land of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]], the Maple People, and lies within [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]]. It is a country of lakes joined by rivers and portages, and of hardwood and pine forest, running from the inland seas of the west to the rocky shores of the eastern ocean. About 750,000 people live in it, in the palisaded villages of the eight nations of the Grove.

## Geography

Water is everywhere: lakes from small forest pools to inland seas hundreds of miles across, rivers broken by rapids that can swallow a canoe, and the portage trails that join one water to the next. A canoeist who knows the portages can travel from any lake to any other, and the Maple People know them all. The largest lakes lie along the western reach, so vast that they seem almost oceanic. On the shores of the westernmost, the [Copper Lake]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the copper lies in the ground pure enough to hammer.

The lakes drain east through the [Great Falls]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, where a river the width of a lake drops over a cliff with a roar that carries for a day's walk, and then down the [Great River]{.name data-type="place/feature"} to the eastern ocean. Every canoe that passes the falls is carried around them on the longest portage in the Grove. The Falls People hold the country around them, and the great council most often sits there.

The forests are mixed: pine and spruce in the north, maple, birch, oak and chestnut in the south. The villages stand in the southern forests, where the women clear fields for corn, beans and squash, and every spring the sugar maples are tapped for sap. Each village has its sugar bush, and the first boiling of the year is its Sap Feast. The forests also give game, nuts, berries and the medicinal plants that make the Maple People's healers wanted across the north.

A village lasts ten to twenty years in one place. Then its fields are worn out and its firewood is a long walk away, and it moves a few miles to a new clearing (_tahsiren_), burned and cut by the men the year before. The forest is full of old village sites: rings of rotted palisade posts, fields gone back to brush, and the low mound of a pit of bones that no one disturbs.

East, the land comes down to the ocean in rocky shores, river mouths and fishing coves. From that ocean twice in twenty-one years have come strange boats, and a year ago one of them burned [[place-tsiorhenten|Tsiorhenten]], a village of the Dawn People, which has since moved inland. North, the forest thins into the boreal country of the [[place-kawiskakrgn|Kawiskak Region]] and then tundra, held only by small bands of caribou hunters, and from there come the peoples the newcomers from the ice have driven south.

Between the Pine People and the Falls People lies the [Lake of Islands]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the old country of the Island People. Nobody has built a village on its islands since the Grove broke them, and the Pine People's canoes cross it without stopping.

## Countries

The Grove's land falls into five countries, each the home of one or more nations.

- **The [[place-kahsiotargn|Falls Country]]**, about 280,000 people, around the Great Falls and down the Great River: the Falls People, the Rapids People and the Beaver People, and the empty Lake of Islands.
- **The [[place-kahnionrgn|Western Lakes]]**, about 160,000, the inland seas of the west: the Copper People and the Birch People.
- **The [[place-tionhsekargn|Southern Rivers]]**, about 140,000, the valleys running south toward Ha'nal Shi'lan: the River People.
- **The [[place-tsiorhenrgn|Dawn Shore]]**, about 100,000, where the Great River meets the sea: the Dawn People.
- **The [[place-tsitarargn|Pine Country]]**, about 70,000, the cold northern edge: the Pine People.

## Villages

The Maple People build palisaded villages, never towns, and none holds more than about 2,000 people. The ones that matter most to the Grove are these.

- [[place-ostakaten|Ostakaten]], the Kettle, about 2,000, in the Falls Country, where the great council sits.
- [[place-tsinikasiwa|First Sugar]], about 1,800, the Rapids People's village beside the sugar bush where the Grove began.
- [[place-kahnekwaroten|Hammerstone]], about 1,500, the Copper People's village on the Copper Lake, where the north's copper is made.
- [[place-kaniestraten|Birches]], about 1,000, the Birch People's village on the edge of the grass.
- [[place-tsiorhenten|Tsiorhenten]], the Dawn Place, about 900, burned by the men from the water and moved inland.
- [[place-tsiontaha|New Clearing]], about 600, the River People's southernmost village, burned by the One Fire and rebuilt.

## Borders and Trade

To the west the forest gives way to the grassland of [[place-tenlayargn|P'enqal Ch'akal]], where the Birch People trade and fight with the Grass People. To the southwest lies [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], the wooded river frontier the Grove contests with the One Fire, and beyond it the old ranges of the [[place-tokchiaskargn|Tokchi Aska Region]].

The Maple People's canoes carry copper, furs, maple sugar and dried fish along the waterways to Ha'nal Shi'lan and on to the other nations. Their copper reaches every corner of the north, and the One Fire's engravers depend on it.
