## Overview

The Dawn Shore (_tsiorhen_) is the eastern edge of the Grove, where the [Great River]{.name data-type="place/feature"} widens until its banks are a day apart and then becomes the sea. The Dawn People live here, the easternmost nation of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]], about 100,000 people in the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]]. They see the sun come out of the ocean every morning, and they are the nation that met the men from the water, and since the raid on [[place-tsiorhenten|Tsiorhenten]] every council of the Grove has had them on its mind.

## Geography

Below the rapids of the [[place-kahsiotargn|Falls Country]] the Great River runs east between low wooded banks, wide and slow, and the tide reaches far up it: twice a day the water runs backward and the river smells of salt. The Dawn People's villages stand on the terraces above the river, among fields of maize and the best eel weirs in the north. Each autumn the eels run down to the sea, and a village's weirs can take enough in a month to feed it through the winter.

Where the river meets the sea the shore breaks into rocky headlands, coves, tidal flats and islands, with a cold sea beyond them. Seals haul out on the rocks, whales blow offshore in summer, and the flats give clams by the basket. The Dawn People's summer fishing camps stand in the coves, and in the summer half the nation lives on the shore.

Inland the country rises into rough hills of spruce and birch, poor for maize and good for moose. The Dawn People hunt there in winter, and since the raid they have built their newest villages there as well.

## The Villages

The Dawn People keep nine palisaded villages along the river and three on the shore.

- [[place-tsiorhenten|Tsiorhenten]], the Dawn Place, about 900 people, burned by the men from the water and moved inland a year early.

## What Moves Through It

The river carries the Dawn People's goods west: dried eel and fish, seal oil, clams, and the shells of the whelk and the clam from which the white and purple beads of the memory belts are cut. Every bead in every belt of the Grove began as a shell on this shore, and the Dawn People's bead makers are the best in the north. Copper and maize come east in return, by way of the Falls Country.

Nothing comes from the sea. Twice in twenty-one summers a strange boat has come out of it, and the second time the men in it burned a village and carried four people away. The Dawn People now keep watchers on the headlands from the thaw to the first snow, with signal fires laid ready, and a smoke on a headland empties the coves in an afternoon.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Dawn People want the Grove to treat the sea as a border. They have asked the great council for warriors to hold the coves and for the next Kettle to stand nearer the shore, so that the council can see what it is deciding about. The western nations, a month's paddle away, answer that one boat in twenty-one years is not a war. The Dawn People answer that the boat came back, and that the council will hear about the third one from them too. The Grove has carried the same question to the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]], and there it meets its own answer: three summers ago, when the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] sent the pipe against a southern war party, the Grove did not smoke.
