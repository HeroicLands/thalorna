# terran_analog: a late Iroquoian-style village set at the prairie-forest edge of Wisconsin and Minnesota, where Ojibwe and Dakota met to trade and fight
---

## Overview

From the palisade of Birches a watcher can see the forest end. To the east and north the birch and maple stand thick, and to the west and south they thin into oak openings and then stop, and beyond them the grass runs on to the sky. The village stands on a low ridge at that line, about 1,000 people of the Birch People behind a double palisade, at the western edge of the [[place-kahnionrgn|Western Lakes]]. Its name (_kaniestraten_) means the place of birches, for the white trees around it, whose bark the village peels for the best canoes in the Grove. It is also the village of [Skahniotiaro]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the speaker the western villages name when they say what a man should be.

## The Village

Inside the double palisade stand twenty-eight longhouses and a council house, with the clan animals carved over the doors. Outside lie the fields, the sugar bush and a long beach on a lake where the village builds canoes: bark peeled in one sheet from a single birch in early summer, sewn over cedar frames with spruce root and sealed with spruce gum. A canoe from Birches is wanted from the Copper Lake to the sea.

On the grass side of the palisade, a long bowshot out, stands a ring of posts with no roof. It is the trading ground. Every summer the Grass People's bands come in to it with robes, dried meat and red stone from the [Red Quarry]{.name data-type="place/site"}, and the village carries out maize, copper, canoes and sugar. Nobody from either side goes past the ring armed while the trading lasts.

## Skahniotiaro

The village's speaker was born among the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]] and taken in a mourning war at nine. An Elk clan mother of Birches, [Kaniestrakerens]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Birch Hears, adopted him in place of a son who had drowned, and six years ago she named him her clan's speaker. He speaks both tongues, and he has twice carried a compact pipe out across the grass to his birth people and twice brought home a truce. Kaniestrakerens is old now and sits by the fire, and the village's clan mothers ask her first.

Skahniotiaro goes to the Red Quarry each summer as one of the Grove's delegates, because the Red Quarry is a few days' walk from Birches and no speaker of the Grove talks to the Grass People better. He is the poorest man in the village and the best fed.

## The Raids

The Grass People raid Birches in some springs, when a band's young men want war names, and trade at the ring in the same summer. The village has learned to live with both. In a raid year the women work the fields with the men posted at the edge of the grass, the palisade gates are shut at dusk, and the summer's trading goes on as if nothing had happened, because both sides need it.

## What the Village Argues About

The village argues about Skahniotiaro, in the way the Grove argues about adopted men. The last raid, two springs ago, came from his birth band, and two of the village's young men died. He carried the pipe to the band that summer and brought home a truce and two of their sons as hostages, raised in the village as Elk kin. Half the village calls it the best answer anyone could have given. The other half asks why the raid came from his band, and why the truce was so easy to get. Kaniestrakerens has heard both, and has not taken his copper.
