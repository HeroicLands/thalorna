## Overview

A visitor to Ostakaten who wants an answer from the Maple People waits, sometimes for days, while the council talks. The village is the Kettle, the place where the great council of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]] sits: about 2,000 people of the Falls People behind a double palisade on a rise above a lake, half a day's walk from the [Great Falls]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. The name belongs to the council and not to the ground. Whichever village hosts the great council is the Kettle, and the south, which counts it as the Grove's seat, has learned that its seat moves.

## The Village

Inside the palisade stand some forty longhouses, each the home of one clan's line of women, their husbands and their children, with a fire for every two families down the middle and the clan's animal carved over the door. Between them stands the council house, the longest building in the village, with its fires down the middle and benches along both walls for the speakers. Outside lie the fields of corn, beans and squash, the sugar bush, and beyond them the forest the village has been cutting for firewood for twelve years. The canoe landing below the rise is never empty: canoes come in from the portages with copper, furs and dried fish, and go out again.

Outside the palisade, on the side away from the lake, lie the village's dead, in graves and on scaffolds, waiting.

## The Council

When the great council sits, after the sugaring each spring, the speakers of the eight nations fill the council house, and the clan mothers sit where they can hear. The rememberers open it by reciting the Founding and the agreements since, holding up each belt as they reach it, and that alone takes a day. Nothing is decided until everyone has spoken and been answered. A visitor who presses for a quick answer is told, politely, that the kettle has not boiled down.

The Falls People feed the whole council for as long as it sits, from the village's stores and from gifts the other nations bring. A long council empties the Kettle's storerooms, and the Falls People say so, which is one of the reasons councils end.

## The Move

The Kettle's fields are thinning and its firewood is a long carry, and the clan mothers have spent this spring walking the forest for the next site. When they have chosen it, the men cut and burn the clearing, and before the village moves it holds its Feast of Bones: twelve years of the Kettle's dead, among them two speakers of the great council, laid together in one pit with the best furs and copper the village owns. Speakers from every nation come to a Kettle's Feast of Bones, and the great council moves with the village.

Where the council sits matters. The Dawn People want the next Kettle closer to the sea, where the council can see what it is deciding about; the Falls People want it where it has always been.
