## Overview

The Pine Country (_tsitaraten_) is the northern edge of the Grove, a country of granite, pine and cold lakes where the maize ripens in some years and not in others. The Pine People hold it, about 70,000 people, the smallest and hardiest nation of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]], at the northern end of the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]]. Beyond them the forest runs north into spruce, muskeg and caribou country with no villages at all. The Pine People are the Grove's door to that country, and this year the people of that country are standing in it.

## Geography

The land is the bare bones of the earth: rounded hills of pink and grey rock scraped clean by ice, with a thin skin of soil, and a lake in every hollow. Pine stands everywhere on the ridges, white and red, so tall that the Grove's canoe makers come north for the paddles and the frames. The soil is thin and the summers short, and a Pine People village plants its maize on the few patches of deep soil along the rivers and hopes for a late frost.

The Pine People eat more fish and game than any other nation of the Grove. They fish the lakes through the ice in winter, hunt moose and deer in the hills, gather wild rice in the shallow bays, and trade furs south for the maize they cannot grow. Their furs are the best in the Grove, because the cold makes them thick.

The [Lake of Islands]{.name data-type="place/feature"} lies on their southern edge, between them and the [[place-kahsiotargn|Falls Country]], and the Pine People's canoes cross it without stopping.

## The Villages

The Pine People keep six palisaded villages, smaller than the southern nations' and farther apart. The largest is [Snow Place]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} (_kenrisaten_), about 900 people on a river below a falls, where the nation's council sits.

## The Bands

The hunting bands of the far woods have always come south in summer to trade furs and meat for maize at the Pine People's villages, and gone home before the snow. For three summers now some of them have not gone home. They say that newcomers from the ice have taken their coasts and their caribou crossings, and they are camped along the Pine People's rivers in birchbark lodges, a few dozen families at a time, hunting the Pine People's hills.

The Pine People's villages are divided over what to do with them. Some clan mothers want to adopt them, family by family, as the Grove has always taken in captives, and raise the names that sickness has left lying down. Some want to trade with them and keep them outside the palisade. The young men want to drive them off before they are too many. The bands, for their part, have not asked to be adopted, and do not want their names laid down.

The Pine People's speaker [Okwiahasterio]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Watches the Bear, has taken a band's spirit-doctor into his village, and the healers of the Grove call it witchcraft. The quarrel belongs to the [[lore-tiatorenhsa|Answering]], the faith of the Grove.

## What Moves Through It

Furs go south: beaver, marten, otter and moose hide, down the rivers to the Falls Country. Maize comes north, with copper blades, beads and pots. The bands bring caribou hides and the tools of the far north, and they have begun to bring news of the newcomers from the ice, which the Pine People's runners carry to the Kettle every spring.
