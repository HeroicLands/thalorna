## Overview

The Falls Country (_kahsiotaten_) is the heart of the Grove: the country around the [Great Falls]{.name data-type="place/feature"} and down the [Great River]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, where the two oldest nations of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]] live within a few days' paddle of each other. About 280,000 people live here, more than a third of the Maple People, in the villages of the Falls People, the Rapids People and the Beaver People, within the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]]. The great council has sat here, at one village or another, for most of eleven generations, and the other nations complain that the Grove's business is always decided where the Falls People can hear it.

A traveler hears the country before seeing it. The roar of the falls carries for a day's walk in still weather, and a mist stands over the gorge on the coldest mornings, visible from the ridges for miles.

## Geography

The country lies between two of the great lakes and along the river that drains them. The upper lake spills over a cliff of grey rock at the Great Falls, a drop of a hundred and seventy feet that no canoe survives, and the river runs on through a deep gorge to the lower lake. Every canoe going east or west is unloaded at the foot of the gorge and carried around the falls on the longest portage in the north, a hard day's carry up the escarpment with a canoe overhead. The Falls People feed whoever carries it, and their villages stand along it.

Below the lower lake the Great River leaves for the sea, broad, cold and full of islands. Its first stretch is broken by rapids, white water a mile long where the canoes are walked down on lines or carried, and the Rapids People live along them. Beyond the rapids the river runs on to the [[place-tsiorhenrgn|Dawn Shore]].

South of the falls lie long narrow lakes in rows, like the marks of fingers drawn through the country, with good soil between them and the best maple stands in the Grove. Most of the Falls People's villages are here. East of them the country sinks into marshes of cattail and wild rice, the Beaver People's country, where the villages stand on low ridges and the canoe is the only road for half the year.

North of the river mouth lies the [Lake of Islands]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, hundreds of rocky islands crowned with pine, with no village on any of them. The Island People lived here until the Grove broke them. The Falls People fish its channels in summer and leave by dark.

## The Villages

The Falls People hold a dozen palisaded villages and the Rapids People eight, each of a few hundred to two thousand people. Two stand out.

- [[place-ostakaten|Ostakaten]], the Kettle, about 2,000 people, half a day from the falls, where the great council sits.
- [[place-tsinikasiwa|First Sugar]], about 1,800, the chief village of the Rapids People, beside the sugar bush where the Grove began.

The Beaver People's villages are smaller and closer together, and their council meets on whichever ridge is driest that spring.

## What Moves Through It

Every road in the Grove runs through the Falls Country. Copper from the [[place-kahnionrgn|Western Lakes]] comes east over the portage, dried fish and sea shell come up from the Dawn Shore, and furs come down from the [[place-tsitarargn|Pine Country]]. The runners of the great council go out from the Kettle in every direction, and a runner who reaches the Falls People with news finds that someone else has usually brought it first. In the spring the speakers of the eight nations come in to council, and the portage is crowded with canoes going the same way.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Falls People host the council and feed it, and they say so. The other nations accept the hosting and resent the hearing: a council that always sits in the Falls Country always sits among the Falls People's clan mothers, who sit where they can hear. The Kettle is about to move, and the Dawn People want the next one nearer the sea. The Rapids People have offered First Sugar, between the two, and the clan mothers of the Falls People have not answered.
