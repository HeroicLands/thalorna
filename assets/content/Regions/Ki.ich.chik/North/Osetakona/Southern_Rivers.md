## Overview

The Southern Rivers (_tionhseka ohiatse_) are the River People's country: a broad upland of forested ridges south of the lakes, cut by rivers that all run south toward [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]] and the country of the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]]. About 140,000 people live here, the River People of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]], in the southwestern part of the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]]. It is the Grove's best farmland after the Falls Country, and its only border where the fighting never stops.

A River People village moves every ten to twenty years like any other, and for three generations it has moved south. Each new clearing is a little farther down a valley than the last, and the farthest are now within a few days' paddle of the One Fire's northern towns.

## Geography

The northern part of the country is a high plateau of oak, chestnut, hickory and maple, with deep soil in the valleys and the springs that feed the rivers. Each river cuts a steep valley as it runs south, and the valleys widen as they go, until the lowest are broad bottomlands with soil as good as any in the north. That bottomland is why the villages move south. A clan mother walking the forest for the next site looks downstream first.

Below the plateau the valleys open into Ha'nal Shi'lan, the wooded river country that the Grove and the One Fire both claim. Neither holds it. The River People farm its northern valleys and the One Fire's towns its southern ones, and the war parties of both cross the middle.

## The Villages

The River People keep fourteen villages, most of them double-palisaded, and the southernmost have bastions at the corners and watchers on the ridges.

- [[place-tsiontaha|New Clearing]], about 600 people, the farthest south, burned by the One Fire's raiders two autumns ago and rebuilt on the same site.

## What Moves Through It

In peace the rivers carry trade south: copper, furs and maple sugar going down to the One Fire's northern landing, and shell, pots and engraved gorgets coming up. The One Fire's towns have no maples, and they pay for the sugar in shell. In war the same rivers carry raiding parties both ways. Most years have both, the trade in summer under the pipe and the raids in autumn after the pipe kinship lapses.

## The Country's Quarrel

The River People accuse the One Fire of raiding their valleys under cover of pipe kinship, and say so at every council of the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]]. The One Fire answers that the River People's villages are being built on its land. Within the Grove the River People have a second grievance: the copper of the [[place-kahnionrgn|Western Lakes]] goes south to the One Fire and comes back as war clubs, and the Copper People will not agree to stop selling it. The River People's speakers raise the question at every great council, and every great council ends without agreeing.
