## Overview

Tsiorhenten means the Dawn Place, and it can no longer see the dawn. Until last summer the village stood on a terrace above a cove of the [[place-tsiorhenrgn|Dawn Shore]], facing the sea, where the sun came up out of the water every morning. Then a boat of men out of the sea fell on it, burned the longhouses nearest the shore, killed six people and carried four away. The clan mother [Tsiorhensiwen]{.name data-type="being/npc"} moved the village a day's walk inland, a year before its fields gave out, to a clearing among spruce hills with no view of the water. A village keeps its name when it moves, so the Dawn Place it still is. About 900 people of the Dawn People live in it, fewer than before the raid.

## The Village

The new village is raw. Its palisade is double, which no village of the Dawn Shore built before, and its stakes are still white where the bark came off. Inside stand twenty-two longhouses, three of them still roofed with hides because there was not time to peel enough bark, and the council house is half built. The fields are new clearings, full of stumps, and the first harvest was thin. The women walk back to the old fields by the shore to bring in what the raid left standing, and they go in groups, with the young men along.

The old village stands empty on its terrace above the cove, its burned longhouses black among the ones that did not burn. Nobody lives there, and nobody has torn it down. The dead of twelve years still lie in their graves outside its palisade, and the village owes them a Feast of Bones it has not yet held.

## The Watch

From the thaw to the first snow the Dawn Place keeps watchers on the headland above the old cove, two young men at a time with a signal fire laid ready. A smoke on the headland brings the village to its palisade and the shore camps inland. The watchers have lit the fire four times since the raid, each time for a whale.

## Tsiorhensiwen

The clan mother of the Loon clan is the village's first voice, and the Dawn People's council is still arguing about her. She refused a mourning war after the raid, because there was no one within reach who had done the village harm. She raised two of the six names onto children born in the winter, and left the other four lying down. The four who were carried off she has not counted as dead. Their names are held, neither lying down nor given, in a way no clan mother of the Grove has had to manage before, and their places at the fires stand empty.

The young men call her old and cold, and some of them want to take a canoe out along the coast to look for the men from the water. Their war leader, [Atoniatienha]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has agreed to wait one more year.

## What the Village Argues About

The Dawn Place has two quarrels. The first is the Feast of Bones: the old village's dead are still in the ground by the sea, and the clan mothers cannot agree whether to hold the feast at the old site, where the boat came, or carry the bones inland. The second is the four names, and whether a name can be held open forever.

:::secret {title="The belt over the sea"}

The men from the water took more than four people. They took the Dawn People's joining belt, the memory belt of white and purple beads that records the day the Dawn People came into the Grove, which the nation's rememberer kept in her longhouse at the old village. It is now on a shelf in a jarl's house across the ocean, among things nobody there can read.

Only Tsiorhensiwen and the rememberer, [Tsihkwakariote]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Loon clan, know it is gone. A nation whose joining belt is lost has, by the Grove's own reckoning, nothing to show for its place in the Grove, and the Falls People's clan mothers have never liked the Dawn People's claims. So the rememberer is stringing a new belt from memory, at night, in a closed longhouse, row by row, and means to carry it to the next great council as the old one. In a people that holds secrecy to be the root of evil, the two women are doing the one thing the Grove calls witchcraft. They have also, without knowing it, given the people across the sea a reason to come back: the belt is the most discussed thing in the jarl's house.

:::
