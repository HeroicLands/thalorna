## Overview

The Osetakona are the Maple People, a league of eight nations in the lakes and forests of the northeast, about 750,000 people in palisaded villages of bark longhouses across the [[place-osetakonargn|Osetakona Region]]. They take their name from the sugar maple (_oseta_), whose sap opens their year, and they picture their league as a sugar bush (_osetaten_), the Grove: many trees tapped for one sugar. The south calls them _Shik'ul Ch'akal_, after their great lakes, and its traders call them the Otter People, after the otters carved on their canoe prows. Their ways are set out in the [[lore-osetakonaclt|Osetakona]] culture note, and their tongue is the [[skill-osetakonalng|Osetakona Language]].

A village is a few hundred to two thousand people inside a palisade of sharpened stakes, in longhouses of bark over bent poles, each the home of one clan's line of women, their husbands and their children. Around it lie the fields where the women plant corn, beans and squash together in the same hills. Every ten to twenty years the soil and the firewood near a village give out, and the whole village moves to a new site, lays its dead together at the Feast of Bones, and builds again. The men hunt, fish, clear land and trade by canoe through the lakes and along the portages; the women own the houses and the fields.

## The Eight Nations

The Grove holds eight nations, each a cluster of villages that share a council. The **Falls People** (_kahsiotakona_) live around the [Great Falls]{.name data-type="place/feature"} between the largest lakes, and are the eldest nation and the usual hosts of the great council. The **Rapids People** (_tahiontokona_) downriver from them were the second, and their alliance with the Falls People is the Grove's founding. The **Copper People** (_tsikenhakona_) of the western lakes dig the copper. The **Dawn People** (_tsiorhenkona_) hold the eastern shore and the mouth of the [Great River]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. The **River People** (_ohiatsekona_) of the southwest face the One Fire across [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]]. The **Pine People** (_tsitarakona_) hold the north, the **Birch People** (_kaniestrakona_) the western forest at the edge of the grassland, and the **Beaver People** (_kahnatiokona_) the inland marshes. A ninth nation, the **Island People** (_tsiotienkona_), was broken and taken in seven generations ago.

The same six clans run through every nation: Otter, Loon, Elk, Sturgeon, Porcupine and Crane. A traveller of the Loon clan is kin in every Loon longhouse of the Grove, and is fed there as a sister's child.

## Who Governs

No one commands. Each clan's line is headed by a clan mother (_kontiesa_), chosen by its women from among the elders. She names the clan's speaker (_katirohen_) to a title her line holds, and she can take the title back, with the hammered copper plate he wears at his throat. She names the ceremony keepers (_otekwahen_) as well, and decides whether a captive is adopted.

The speakers govern by talking. A village council is its clans' speakers, a nation's council is speakers sent by its villages, and the great council of the Grove is the speakers of all eight nations, about sixty titles in all, at the Kettle, [[place-ostakaten|Ostakaten]]. Every council decides by agreement: nothing is done until everyone present has spoken, been answered and agreed, and the Maple People call a council a boiling (_rostahsa_), because talk boils down to one mind as sap boils down to sugar. The clan mothers sit where they can hear, and a speaker who agrees to something his clan mother has not agreed to does not keep his copper long. Runners (_takwarohen_) carry a council's word to the next council, and must say all of it.

The rememberers (_sakwentahen_) are the Grove's records. They keep its agreements in memory and in memory belts of white and purple shell beads, and they open every great council by reciting the Founding and the agreements since.

## War

The Grove's wars are small and personal. When deaths leave a clan's names lying down, a clan mother may ask for a mourning war: a raid to take captives who will raise the names. A war leader (_rotsikwen_) raises the party and leads it only while it is out. A captive brought home is adopted, and becomes a son, a daughter or a husband of the clan in full, or is put to death. Many of the Maple People were born elsewhere. A nation goes to war as a whole only when its council agrees, and the whole Grove only when the great council agrees.

## The Grove and the Compact

The Maple People are proud of their government and suspicious of everyone else's. They hold that a king is a witch with a council's power, and they say so to the One Fire's Suns at every council of the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]]. In the compact the Maple People are the diplomats and the memory: their rememberers open each summer council by reciting the agreements of past ones, and their runners carry more of the compact's business than any other nation's.

## Neighbours and Threats

The Grove contests [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], the river frontier to the southwest, with the One Fire, and each accuses the other of raiding there. The Grass People raid the Birch People's villages in the west. Two dangers fall on the Grove before anyone else. [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] from the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] have crossed the eastern ocean, and last summer they burned [Tsiorhenten]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, a Dawn People village, and carried four of its people away; the Maple People have no name for them. And in the north, hunting bands driven out of the far woods by newcomers from the ice are moving into the Pine People's country.

## Trade

The Grove's lake country is where the north's copper comes from. It lies in the ground pure on the shores of the [Copper Lake]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, and the Copper People's smiths hammer it cold into blades, awls and sheets that the other nations want. Canoes carry it with furs, maple sugar and dried fish along the waterways to Ha'nal Shi'lan and beyond. Southern goods reach the Grove last and fewest, by way of the Turquoise People and the One Fire.
