# terran_analog: the fortified Initial Coalescent and Plains Village sites of the lower Missouri in South Dakota, such as Crow Creek and Fay Tolton, with bastioned ditches and burned lodges
---

## Overview

A traveler coming up the river from the One Fire's country sees the Forks long before reaching it: a hump of black on a high terrace at the fork of two rivers, and as he comes closer, the black resolves into burned posts standing out of fallen earth, and behind them, still standing, the domes of the lodges that did not burn. Henu Shewan, the Forks, is the downstream village of the [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]] and the nearest of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]]'s villages to the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]]: about 1,100 people, where there were 1,500 a year ago, in some forty earth lodges inside two ditches and a palisade, with eighteen more lodges standing burned inside the same walls. Its fields lie in the angle between the rivers, and the women who work them do it with men watching from the bluffs.

## The Walls

The Forks has the deepest ditches on the river. The outer one is twice a man's depth, the inner one deeper, and the palisade between them is a double row of cottonwood posts with bastions on every corner, from which archers can shoot along the face of the wall. There is one gate, a winding passage between the ends of the ditches that a war party must walk through single file under the bastions. The village has deepened the ditches four times in living memory, and every time, the lodge owners (_moshenka_) have dug the earth themselves, with their daughters, because the men were away on the grass.

The walls held for a generation. Last winter, in the deep snow, a war party of the One Fire came up the frozen river at night, crossed both ditches on the snow drifted into them, and burned the lodges along the north wall before the village woke. They killed twenty-six people and took thirty-one captives, most of them women and children, and were gone down the ice by dawn.

## Why It Was Burned

The One Fire's war party did not come for the Forks. It came because the Coyote band (Kanweya) of the [[place-watotenlargn|Long Grass]] had raided two of the One Fire's western towns the autumn before, under their war leader [Kanwewiwe]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and brought home robes, copper and a dozen captives. The Coyote band was a hundred miles away on the winter grass. The Forks stands where it stands.

The village knows this, and says it. The lodge owners of the Forks have said at every autumn trading for twenty years that the bands make the names and the villages pay for them. This year they have done more than say it.

## The Refusal

[Shewantiyu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, River Heart, is the eldest lodge owner of the Forks, a thin, hard woman whose lodge was one of the eighteen that burned. Her youngest daughter is among the thirty-one captives. At the end of the winter she called the lodge owners together in the bundle lodge, and they agreed that when the bands come in this autumn, the Forks will open its cache pits to every band that comes, as the [Corn Pipe]{.name data-type="miscgear"} binds it to, except the Coyote band. They have sent word to the Coyote band that if it wants corn from the Forks, it can bring back the thirty-one first.

The village speaker (_hoyanka_) of the Forks, [Hetanwinte]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sun Gives, is a careful man who has bargained with the bands for fifteen years and has kin in three of them. He has said in council that a village which shuts its pits against a band under the Corn Pipe is doing what the [Burned Village]{.name data-type="place/site"} did, and the lodge owners have answered that the Burned Village shut its pits against the hungry, and the Coyote band is not hungry. Both have sent runners to the bundle keeper of [[place-tansheyawa|Tanshe Yawa]], who keeps the Corn Pipe, and he has not yet answered either.

## The Captives

The thirty-one are in the One Fire's western towns, and some of them are probably alive. The One Fire takes captives to adopt, to work and sometimes to kill at the funeral of a great man, and nobody at the Forks knows which of these the thirty-one have met. The Coyote band's own captives, taken from the same towns, are being raised in its lodges as its own children, and Kanwewiwe has said that they are Grass People now and not his to give.

A trade is possible. The One Fire's towns would ransom their own people for robes, and would sell back the captives from the Forks for more. Somebody would have to walk two weeks into the One Fire's country under a compact pipe to arrange it, and the Forks has nobody the One Fire would listen to.
