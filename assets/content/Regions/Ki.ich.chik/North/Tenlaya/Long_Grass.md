## Overview

In late summer the grass of the eastern plain stands higher than a man's head, and a band crossing it walks in single file down a trail it cannot see over, with a scout on every rise. The bands call it Wato Tenla, the Long Grass: the wet, rich eastern prairie of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Tenlaya]], the Grass People, between the [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]] and the forests of the east. About 80,000 people hunt it, in bands that are larger and richer than those of the west and that raid more than any others. The other nations of the north know the Grass People best from here, because this is the edge of the plains that faces them.

## The Land

The Long Grass is lower and wetter than the [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]], and it gets its rain in spring and early summer as well as in storms. The grass grows tall and thick and green until midsummer, then goes red and gold. Small lakes and sloughs lie in the hollows, full of ducks and geese, and the rivers wind through it in wooded valleys broader than any in the west. Elk come out of the woods to graze it, and the herds come into it in summer when the western grass is dry.

There are no cliffs. The bands of the Long Grass take the herds with fire and with pounds. In spring they burn the old grass, and the new green that comes up in the black draws the herds to it within a moon; in autumn they set fires in a ring around a herd and close it, and drive the animals into a pound of logs in a river valley. A fire set in the wrong wind runs for days, and every band of the Long Grass has a story of one that took a camp.

## The Red Quarry

In the north-east, where the grass meets the forest of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]] and the river country of the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]], a low ridge of hard pink rock breaks the prairie. Under it lies the soft red stone of the [Red Quarry]{.name data-type="place/site"}, where the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]] meets each summer and no blood may be shed. The bands of the Long Grass camp nearest to it and know its ground best, and they remember that [Kanwesawi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Coyote Runs, the war leader killed there by a One Fire noble, was one of their own. His band still keeps the robe he wore, with the hole in it.

## The Raid Trails

Raiding is the Long Grass's other harvest. Trails worn by war parties run east out of the grass to the One Fire's western towns, two weeks' walk on foot, and north-east to the villages of the [Birch People]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the western nation of the Maple People, who raid back and adopt the captives they take. A young man of the Long Grass makes his first coup on one of these trails, and a war leader (_yashenka_) who brings a party home rich gives it all away within a moon and raises another. The towns the bands raid do not strike back at the bands, which have already moved on. They strike back down the river at [[place-henushewan|Henu Shewan]], the Forks, and the other villages that cannot move.

The greatest raiding band of the Long Grass is the Coyote band (Kanweya), whose warriors paint coyotes on their shields. Southern traders who met them first named the whole nation after them, and the Grass People still find that funny. The Coyote band's war leader [Kanwewiwe]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Coyote Fights, is Kanwesawi's sister's son, and he has counted more coups on the One Fire than any man alive.

## Borders and Neighbors

West, the Long Grass ends at the Village River and its villages. East and south-east, the grass breaks into groves and then into the forest and the river country of the One Fire, whose western towns the bands raid and trade with in the same season. North-east, past the Red Quarry, it runs into the lake forests of the Maple People. North, it thins into the aspen parkland.

## What Moves Through It

Robes, hides and dried meat go east, to the One Fire's towns for shell beads, engraved shell, copper and fine pottery, and to the Maple People for corn, copper and maple sugar, in trade in summer and as plunder in autumn. Captives go both ways. Red stone comes down from the quarry as pipe bowls, carried by delegates and traders under the compact's peace.
