# terran_analog: the Knife River flint quarries and the Hidatsa villages at the mouth of the Knife River in North Dakota, with the Hidatsa practice of catching eagles by hand from covered pits

## Overview

The bluffs above the village look as if something has been digging in them for a thousand years, and something has. Their slopes are pocked with hundreds of pits, each a few paces across, with heaps of broken stone beside them, and in summer there are people in dozens of them, prying out nodules of dark brown stone and knocking flakes off them to see what is inside. Kopen Mokin, Flint Hill, is the upstream village of the [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]] and the northernmost village of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]]: about 900 people in some thirty earth lodges on a low terrace where a small river joins the great one, under the flint bluffs. The stone in its pits is the best on the plains, glassy and even, and an arrowhead or a knife of it is worth a robe on the [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]] and two at the One Fire's towns.

## The Pits

The flint lies in a layer of gravel just under the soil of the bluffs, in lumps from the size of a fist to the size of a head. A digger clears the soil, digs down to the layer with a bone hoe or a digging stick, and pries the lumps out. Most are cracked or flawed; one in ten is good. The good ones are knocked into rough blanks on the spot, and the blanks are carried down to the village to be finished over a winter fire.

The pits belong to the lodge owners (_moshenka_) of the village, each lodge to its own stretch of the bluff, and pass from mother to daughter with the lodge. A band that wants to dig asks the lodge owner whose ground it is, pays her in meat or robes, and digs. Nobody refuses a band, and nobody digs without asking. A band that digs on a lodge's ground unasked has its blanks taken off it at the foot of the bluff, and the villages tell that story with relish, because it happens every few summers and is always a young war leader in a hurry.

The lodge owner whose stretch of bluff is largest is [Loshiwinte]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Gives Corn, a broad, cheerful woman in her fifties who speaks for the pits at the autumn trading. She has quilled eagle feathers into the hair of three husbands and outlived two of them, and she is said to know the worth of a flint blank by its sound when she drops it.

## The Eagle Pits

On the ridges north of the village, high up where the wind blows, are the eagle pits: holes the length of a man, covered with a roof of poles and grass, with a dead rabbit tied on top. A man fasts and sweats, lies down in the pit before dawn, and waits, sometimes for days. When an eagle lands on the bait, he reaches up through the roof, seizes it by the legs with his bare hands, and pulls it in. The feathers go to men who have counted coups, who wear them, and the bird is sent back to the sky with tobacco and a song.

Eagle catching belongs to Flint Hill, and the men of the village teach it to no one else. Every society head on the plains wears feathers that passed through the village's hands.

## The North Trade

Flint Hill is the last village up the river. Above it the valley narrows, the grass thins into aspen parkland, and the northern bands go on toward the forest to trade with the [Spruce People]{.name data-type="lore/culture"}. When they come back down the river in autumn, they bring furs, birch-bark canoes, and copper from beyond the forest, and Flint Hill trades for them first. A birch-bark canoe from the north is the finest thing on the river, and the village keeps three of them for ferrying the lodge owners across.

## The Old Points

Last summer a lodge owner's daughter, digging deeper than anyone had dug on that stretch of bluff, broke into a pocket of stone below the flint layer and found eleven spear points laid together in red earth. Each is longer than a hand, thin as a leaf and fluted down both faces, made by a skill nobody on the plains has. The bundle keeper (_chekanka_) of the village has wrapped them in a new robe and keeps them in the bundle lodge, and says they belong to the people who lived on the plains before the herds came up out of the [Breathing Hole]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. A war leader of the Coyote band has offered forty robes for one of them, and the bundle keeper has said no.

:::secret {title="The points"}
The points are real, and very old: the work of hunters who lived on the plains long before the Grass People, and who hunted beasts larger than bison. Below the pocket where they lay, the gravel of the bluff holds the bones of one of those beasts, a great tusked animal, a pace below the deepest pit.
:::
