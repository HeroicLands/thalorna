## Overview

In autumn the bands walk in off the grass with their dogs dragging travois piled with dried meat and robes, set up their hide lodges on the flat below the palisade, and wait for the Corn Pipe. Tanshe Yawa, Bison Ford, is the largest village of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Tenlaya]], the Grass People: about 1,500 people in some sixty earth lodges on a terrace above the [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]], the great river of the plains, at the shallows where the herds cross it every spring and autumn. Its fields of corn, beans, squash and sunflowers lie on the bottomland below. No band or village takes orders from it, but more of the plains' trade passes through it than through any other place. The south counts it as the seat of the Grass People and calls it by the nation's name, _P'enqal Ch'akal_.

## The Village

The earth lodges stand close together inside a ditch as deep as a man is tall and a palisade of cottonwood posts, with a bastion at every corner. Each lodge is forty feet across, a dome of earth over four great posts and a ring of shorter ones, entered by a covered passage, with a smoke hole at the top where the old men sit on summer evenings. The lodges belong to the women who raised them, and the lodge owners (_moshenka_) sit in the village council beside the men. Under every lodge floor are cache pits (_okun_) of dried corn, sealed with grass and earth.

In the open space at the center of the village stands the bundle lodge, where the village's bundle keeper (_chekanka_) keeps the oldest bundle on the river, and beside it the post where the society on duty sets its shields during the trading. Outside the palisade, on a rise of the prairie upstream, is the ring of skulls, where the women of the village go to sit with their dead.

The herds come to the ford every spring and autumn, and the village takes what it can from them: drowned animals carried down by the spring ice, and calves cut off in the shallows by the village's young men. Every summer, once the corn is hoed, most of the village goes out to a hunting camp on the grass and lives like a band until the green corn.

## The Autumn Trading

The village chooses a village speaker (_hoyanka_) to deal with the bands, and the bargaining is the same every year and never easy: corn, beans, squash, sunflower cakes and tobacco against meat, fat, robes and hides. A society from the bands keeps the peace of the trading camp while it lasts, and sets its shields against the post in the village to show it. The trading opens with the [Corn Pipe]{.name data-type="miscgear"}, which the bundle keeper of Tanshe Yawa keeps, and which every village speaker and every band's society head smokes before any price is named. The pipe binds the village to open its cache pits to the hungry before it bargains, and the bands to come in peace.

The pipe's keeping is the reason the village is first among the villages. It was made after the Winter of the Ditch, when the bands burned a village of their own people downstream; the ruins of the [Burned Village]{.name data-type="place/site"} lie half a day's walk below the ford, its ditch still visible in the grass, and Tanshe Yawa deepened its own ditch and doubled its palisade the spring after. The two sides need each other and do not entirely trust each other, and the village keeps its palisade in repair.

## The Bundle Keeper's Answer

The bundle keeper of Tanshe Yawa is [Kohanipo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Mountain Sits, a small, bald old man who has kept the village's bundle and the Corn Pipe for thirty winters and has never once been seen in a hurry. Every village speaker on the river and every society head of the bands knows his lodge, and this summer runners from both sides of one quarrel have been in it.

Downstream at [[place-henushewan|Henu Shewan]], the Forks, the lodge owners have said that they will open their cache pits at this autumn's trading to every band except the Coyote band, whose raids brought the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]] down on them last winter. Their village speaker says that this is what the Burned Village did. The Coyote band says that it comes under the Corn Pipe like everyone else. Only Kohanipo can say what the pipe binds, because only he knows all its songs, and he has said that he will answer when the trading opens, with the pipe in his hands, and not before.

## What Comes In

The village trades on to the east and the southwest: robes and hides to the Maple People and the One Fire, and to the Turquoise People for cotton cloth, pottery and turquoise. Now and then a southern good comes up the trails from the Turquoise People, a macaw feather or a copper bell, and a war leader who gets one wears it until he gives it away.
