## Overview

Hold your hand in front of your mouth on a cold morning and look at your breath. The Grass People say that it is the same breath that comes out of the [Breathing Hole]{.name data-type="place/feature"} in winter, the same that moves the grass in the wind and comes out of a bison's nostrils in a cloud, and the same that rises out of a pipe as smoke. They call it Nipan Heshehin, Earth's Breath: the earth breathes, and everything alive is alive because it breathes with her. A thing with more of the breath in it than others is holy (_tunse_): a bundle, a pipe, an eagle, a herd, a person who has had a strong vision, a place like the Breathing Hole.

The [[affiliation-tenlaya|Tenlaya]] have no word for religion, and no priests. Earth's Breath is what everyone does: the first bite of meat set aside, the pipe offered to the sky, the earth and the four quarters, the sweat before anything that matters. Its keepers are people who know more songs than others, and none of them commands anyone. The everyday practice is set out in the [[lore-tenlayaclt|Tenlaya]] culture note. This note is about the powers, the people who deal with them, and the rites that belong to them.

## The Powers

The Grass People name few powers, and they pray to all of them through the pipe.

The Earth (_nipan_)
: is under everything, and breathes. In spring she breathes out, and the herds come up out of her through the Breathing Hole and spread across the grass; in autumn she breathes in. The people lived inside her in the dark before the herds found the way out, and the dead are laid on scaffolds above her so that she does not take them back too soon. She is never painted and never danced for. A woman who digs a cache pit, a lodge post hole or a grave puts tobacco into it first.

The Herd Mother (_tanshe kuhashi_)
: lives under the Breathing Hole and sends the herds up each spring. The herds are her children and a people of their own, and they give themselves to a people who share them. When they are wasted, left to rot, or hoarded by one lodge while another goes hungry, she calls them back under the earth, and they do not come out again until the people have mended what they did. Every band's bundle holds a bison skull painted for her, and every drive opens with a song to her.

The Sun (_hetan_)
: walks across the sky every day and sees everything done under it. He is the power of the summer gathering. The pledge dance is danced to him, around a cottonwood pole with its leaves still on, by men who made a vow to him in danger: to dance four days without food or water, facing him, until they fall. He keeps the promises made to him, and holds everyone else to theirs.

The Thunders (_kunton_)
: are great birds who live in the mountains to the west and fly over the plains in the summer storms. The beat of their wings is thunder, and the flash of their eyes is lightning. They bring the rain that makes the grass, and they give power in war. A person who dreams of the Thunders belongs to them, and must become a contrary.

The Corn Grandmother (_loshi sheki_)
: lives in the south and sends the waterfowl north each spring to tell the villages to plant. She gave the first seed corn to the women of the first villages in a dream. She is the villages' power more than the bands', and the village women keep her rites: the planting songs, the singing to the seed, and the green corn feast, when the first ears are boiled and offered to her before anyone eats.

Coyote (_kanwe_)
: is no power to pray to. He is the hero of a thousand stories, told in winter to children, in which he hides meat from his relatives, cheats at the hand game, boasts and lies, and loses everything every time. Every closed-hand story on the plains is a Coyote story. The Coyote band paints him on its shields because, it says, coyotes follow the herds and eat well, and the other bands say that this is the only Coyote story the Coyote band has ever understood.

The people also tell of their own great dead. [Loshitayu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Corn Dream, who carried the pipe to the river villages after the [Winter of the Ditch]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, is sung at every autumn trading. Nobody prays to her. She is remembered so that the peace she made is remembered.

## The Keepers

A bundle keeper (_chekanka_)
: holds a band's or a village's holy bundle and its songs. A bundle is not inherited. Its keeper chooses a successor, usually a nephew or a niece, who pays for it in robes, meat and years of service, and learns its songs one at a time over as many winters as the keeper thinks it needs. A bundle whose songs are lost is a bundle that does nothing, and a band that loses its keeper before the songs are passed on counts the loss as a death.

A doctor (_machonka_)
: is someone whose vision gave a power to heal. Doctors cure with songs, with roots and herbs, with the sweat lodge, and with the help of the animal that came in the vision. A doctor called to a sick person asks, first of all, what rule was broken: a weapon stepped over, a first bite not set aside, a soul bundle neglected, meat wasted. The sickness is the rule coming home. Doctors do not name anyone as the cause of a sickness, and a sick person is never blamed. The cure is to mend what was broken, by a gift, a feast or a sweat, and the family pays the doctor in robes.

A contrary (_kewitaka_)
: is someone who has dreamed of the Thunders. From the day of the dream a contrary says yes for no and no for yes, walks backward, sits facing away from the fire, shivers in summer and goes half-naked in snow, and washes in dust. Contraries are feared and laughed at in the same breath, and they are among the bravest fighters on the plains, because a contrary told to retreat charges. A contrary is freed by the Thunders themselves: in the first storm of a spring, he stands in the open under it, alone, and if the lightning spares him, he gives away everything he owns the next day and goes back to living forward.

Anyone may make a pledge, have a vision, or sing over the sick. A keeper is only someone who has done it longer and remembers more of the songs.

## The Rites

The great rites of the year, the summer gathering, the drive, the autumn trading and the winter dances that call the herds, are set out in the [[lore-tenlayaclt|Tenlaya]] culture note. Four others belong here.

Turning the Skull
: is done after every drive. When the meat is cut, the hunt chief (_yuchanka_) takes the skull of the first animal killed, paints it red, stuffs its eye holes and nostrils with sage, and sets it on a rise facing the direction the herd came from, so that the herd can see it and know that its children were well used. The skulls of a hundred drives stand along the lanes of the [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]].

Opening the Bundle
: is done before a drive, before the planting and before a war party leaves. The keeper sweats, unwraps the bundle on a robe in the bundle lodge or at the head of the drive, sings each thing's song in order, offers the pipe, and wraps it again. Nobody may pass between the bundle and the sun while it is open. A drive does not start against the keeper's word, and a war party that leaves without its bundle's blessing leaves without its society.

The First Fish
: is the rite of the western bands at [[place-hiyespito|Hiye Spito]], Falling Water, when the first salmon of the year is shared among the whole camp in tiny pieces, and its bones are put back in the river whole, with the head upstream. It is the western bands' Turning the Skull.

The Release
: ends the year of keeping a soul bundle (_nashe chekan_), when the family gives away everything it owns and the soul goes free onto the [Spirit Road]{.name data-type="lore"}. It is the greatest giveaway a family ever makes. A doctor sings the soul out of the lodge and through the door, and the family sweeps the floor after it.

## The Argument

The herds are thin. They have been thin in the west for three summers, and the bands of the Cairn Plain are hungry, while the herds have come in numbers into the [[place-watotenlargn|Long Grass]] in the east, where the raiding bands have eaten well. Earth's Breath gives one answer to why: the Herd Mother has called the herds back, because the people did something that called for it. The argument is over what that was.

The bundle keepers of the west say that it was waste. Last autumn at [[place-kuntontoshe|Kunton Toshe]], Thunder Cliff, more than a thousand animals went over the cliff in one run and half of them rotted, and [Mahintayu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sky Dream, the bundle keeper of the Cairn band, says the herds will not come back until the bands that drove there have given away a robe for every animal that spoiled. The villages say that it was the bands' closed hand, the raids that bring the One Fire down on the villages, and [[place-henushewan|Henu Shewan]], the Forks, now refuses the Coyote band at the trading. The bands of the Long Grass, whose herds are fat, say nothing and keep raiding.

Nobody on the plains has said, in council, that the raids themselves might be what the Herd Mother is answering. A few old doctors have said it to their nephews. It is the old argument over the raids, with the bison in it.

# The Breathing Hole {#truth .secret}

The Breathing Hole is the mouth of a cave that runs for miles under the hills, through passages of white crystal and honeycombed rock, down to a still black lake that nobody has seen in a lifetime. It breathes because the air inside it is always at one warmth: when the weather above is colder, the cave breathes out, and when it is warmer, the cave breathes in. A storm passing over the hills can make it gasp.

A day's crawl in, beyond the place where the bands' gifts stop, the walls of a dry chamber are covered with red handprints and painted beasts: bison, but larger than any on the plains, with long straight horns, and among them tusked animals the size of an earth lodge. The people who painted them are the same people whose flint points lie under the bluffs at [[place-kopenmokin|Kopen Mokin]]. Whether the Grass People's ancestors came up out of this cave is a matter of belief. Someone did come out of it once, and left their hands on the walls.

The herds are thin in the west because the summers there have been dry and the bands have driven too many, and they are fat in the east because the Long Grass has had rain and its bands burn the old grass every spring. The Herd Mother, if she is there at all, has not been asked.
