# terran_analog: Celilo Falls on the Columbia, the great fishing and trading place of the Plateau peoples, with its dip-net platforms, its first-salmon rite and its summer trade fair
---

## Overview

You hear the falls from half a day away, and smell them from a mile: water, wet rock and fish drying on a thousand racks. Hiye Spito, Falling Water, is where the great river of the [[place-potontayuntorgn|Root Fields]] drops over a ledge of black rock into a narrow gorge, and where the western bands of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]] gather every summer for the salmon. About 300 people winter in pit lodges along the bank. From early summer to the first frost, when the fish are running, about 3,000 camp here, and in the busiest weeks of the trading half as many again.

## The Fishing

The salmon come up the river from the sea in runs, one after another, from early summer into autumn, and below the falls they mass in the white water and leap. Out over the falls, on the rocks and the ledges, stand the fishing platforms: frames of poles lashed to the rock, reaching out over the current, from which a man with a dip net on a pole twice his height takes fish as fast as he can lift them. A good platform in a good run feeds a band for the winter.

The platforms belong to families, and pass from father to son. A family lets kin and guests fish from its platform, and anyone who asks is given a turn, but the first fish of each day goes to the family. The women of the camp clean the fish, split them and dry them on racks in the wind off the gorge, and pound the dried fish into a meal that keeps for years.

## The First Fish

Nobody fishes until the first salmon of the year is caught and eaten. When the scouts at the foot of the gorge send word that the fish are coming, the bundle keeper (_chekanka_) of the falls band catches one fish from the oldest platform, cooks it on a fire of driftwood, and shares it in tiny pieces among everyone in the camp. Its bones are put back in the river, whole and in order, with the head pointing upstream, so that it can go home and tell the others it was treated well. Then the fishing begins.

## The Trading

The falls are the great market of the west. The western bands bring dried salmon, salmon oil, roots and berries; bands from the [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]] come over the passes with robes, dried bison meat and red-painted rawhide cases; the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]'s traders come over the coast range with shell, dried sea fish, carved horn spoons and cedar boxes. People gamble for days at the hand game, singing to confuse the guesser, and lose robes, bows, lodge covers and, once in a while, a dog team. A trade at the falls is binding once both sides have eaten together.

The western bands' head at the falls is [Tashusawi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Wind Runs, the oldest man of the falls band, who owns the platform where the first fish is taken. He settles quarrels over platforms and keeps the peace of the trading, and his word holds because his band owns the best rocks on the river.

## The Captive

This summer the Cedar People's traders have brought a captive with them to sell: a girl of about twelve from the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shell-Bead People]] of the southern coast, taken in a raid and kept as a slave in a Cedar house. They want twenty robes for her. The Grass People take captives in war and adopt them as kin, but they do not buy and sell people, and a slave is a thing they have heard of and never seen.

The camp is divided. Some families want to buy her and adopt her, because a child bought out of a Cedar house is a child saved. Others say that a band that pays for a person teaches the Cedar People that the plains will pay, and that next summer the traders will bring ten. Tashusawi has not decided, and the traders are packing to go back over the passes before the snow.
