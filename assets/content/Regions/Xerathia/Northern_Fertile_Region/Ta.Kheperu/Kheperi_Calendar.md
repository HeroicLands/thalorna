The **Kheperi calendar** divides the year into three seasons of four thirty-day months each, following the river rather than the sun's angle: **Akhet** (Inundation), **Peret** (Emergence and Growing), and **Shemu** (Harvest). Five days outside any month close the year once the twelve are spent, so the count stays whole every year without a leap.

Kheperi astronomers are proud of the fit, and say so—the calendar tracks the solar year with a precision they hold unmatched anywhere in western Ankaris. It is not the calendar [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta'Kheperu]] uses to date a document, though. Everyday business runs on regnal years, counted afresh from Year 1 of each reigning Per-Aá, and history and theology run on the **Sep Tepy**, the First Occasion some 2,830 years gone. The month-and-season count here is the working year underneath both: what a farmer, a priest and a tax-scribe all mean when they say a season has turned.

## The Seasons

**Akhet**, the Inundation, opens the year. The river rises and floodwater covers the fields, so the farming population turns to the seasonal labor that raises the great monuments while the land itself is unworkable.

**Peret**, Emergence and Growing, follows as the water recedes. The silt it leaves behind is planted the moment it can be worked, and this is the season of the year's real agricultural labor.

**Shemu**, the Harvest, closes the twelve months. Crops come in before the next flood, and the five days that follow belong to no month at all—the year's own reckoning is finished before the new one begins.
