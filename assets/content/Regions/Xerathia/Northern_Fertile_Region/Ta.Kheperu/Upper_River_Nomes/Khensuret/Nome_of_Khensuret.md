## Overview

Khensuret is the great mortuary nome of the [[affiliation-uperivrnms|upper river]] — the place where [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]] tends its dead. The empire's largest pilgrimage flows here, for to be buried in the sacred ground of Khensuret — or merely to make the journey and lay an offering — is held to weigh favorably when the heart is set against the feather of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Má'át]]. The nome is not large or rich in the worldly sense, but in the economy of the afterlife it is the capital of the world. The land it holds is [[place-khensuretnome|Khensuret Nome]].

## Character

Khensuret is solemn, ancient, and wholly given over to death — not morbidly, for the Kheperi see death as renewal, but completely. The whole life of the nome organizes around the dead: the embalmers in their workshops on the west bank, the tomb-builders and quarrymen cutting and raising the houses of eternity, the funerary artists painting the walls that _are_ the afterlife made real, the coffin-makers and amulet-carvers and the keepers of the great rolls of mortuary spells. Above them all stand the Wer'Hekau of the mortuary temples, who command estates and labor-gangs rivaling the [[affiliation-empirtkhpr#titles-and-forms-of-address|Haty'a]]'s own — and the central political fact of the nome is the wary sharing of power between the hereditary nomarch and the priesthoods of the dead. Pilgrims throng the temple-city year-round; the festival-calendar is dense with rites for the departed; and the whole place lives, paradoxically, very well off of dying well.

## For the Worldbuilder

Khensuret is the empire's uncanny heart, and it plays like nowhere else — a whole society organized around death, with all the wealth, secrecy, and dread that implies. Run it as a place of sacred industry and buried danger: the embalmers' guild-secrets, the tomb-workers' closed brotherhood at [[place-setmaat|Set-Maat]], the rivalry between the nomarch and the mortuary priesthoods, the endless war between the tomb-builders who hide the dead and the tomb-robbers who plunder them. The funerary magic here is real and the curses guarding the tombs are not always idle superstition. Adventure runs from the mundane — pilgrim-fleecing, grave-goods fraud, stolen mortuary papyri — to the genuinely eerie: what walks the necropolis after dark, what the deepest tombs were built to keep in as much as out, and what a desperate or greedy party finds when it goes down into the houses of eternity. What is abundant here is ritual, stone, and the dead; what is scarce is levity, and any tomb that has not already been robbed at least once.

## Commerce and Currency

Khensuret uses the [[lore-tkhprcrncy|Ta'Kheperu currency system]]. Much of its wealth moves as temple-account and pilgrim-offering rather than open trade, banked through the mortuary temples' own treasuries and the [[affiliation-perhati|Pér-Háti]] houses that serve them; the grave-goods and amulet trades, by contrast, deal briskly in struck copper and silver. See [[lore-tkhprcrncy|Ta'Kheperu Currency]] for the full system.
