## Overview

Wasetkara Nome is the land of [[affiliation-capitalnom|The Capital Nome]] of the [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Empire of Ta'Kheperu]], and lies within the [[place-takheperurgn|Ta'Kheperu Region]].

Within its bounds lie the palace and the throne, the central bureaucracy, the greatest temples of the [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Kemetian Pantheon]], the [[affiliation-kenbetpat|Kenbet'Pat]] council, and—across the river on the western bank—the royal necropolis where the dynasties of three thousand years lie buried. With some two and a half million souls, it is the most populous nome in the empire and the densest concentration of power, wealth, and people in [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]].

## Character

The nome is a city-and-its-hinterland writ vast: the great metropolis of [[place-wasetkara|Wasetkara]] at its heart, a dense belt of temple-estates, workshop-towns, and market-villages around it, and the silent necropolis-cities of the dead on the desert margin opposite.

## Economy

The Capital Nome consumes more than it produces, and proudly so. It is fed by grain barged down from the [[affiliation-uperivrnms|upper river]] and the produce of its own dense hinterland; it is enriched by the tribute and taxes of forty nomes flowing into its treasuries; and it employs an immense population in the work of governing, worshipping, building, and burying. The great temple-complexes are vast economic enterprises in their own right, owning farmland across the nome and employing thousands. The mortuary industry alone—embalmers, tomb-builders, funerary artists, priests, and the grave-goods trades that serve the royal necropolis—sustains whole districts. And the construction of the monuments, carried on through every flood-season, is the largest public works program in the world.

## Notable Features

- The imperial palace and the throne of the Per-Aá, at the heart of [[place-wasetkara|Wasetkara]]
- The great temples—the cult of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Rā]] that defines official doctrine chief among them
- The royal necropolis on the western bank—pyramids and rock-cut tombs of three thousand years of dynasties

## Settlements

The nome's settlement pattern is a single great metropolis ringed by temple-towns, workshop-towns, and the necropolis-cities of the western bank.

**The metropolis:**

- **[[place-wasetkara|Wasetkara]]** (~450,000)—the imperial city, seat of the Per-Aá, and by far the largest city in the empire; palace, great temples, bureaus, and the polyglot crowd of a capital that draws in the whole realm.

**The bank of the dead and the temple-towns:**

- **[[place-tadjeser|Ta-Djeser]]** (~40,000)—"the Sacred Land," the necropolis-city on the western bank; the embalmers, tomb-builders, funerary artists, and mortuary priests who serve the royal dead, and the tomb-workers' settlements behind them.
- **[[place-perheka|Per-Heka]]** (~30,000)—a temple-estate town east of the capital; the granaries, workshops, and tenant-villages of one of the great cult-temples, a town that exists to feed and serve its god.

**The hinterland villages:** the dense farm-and-market country around the capital holds scores of villages of 500–3,000—among them Iunu-Heri, Per-Sekhem, Sma-Behdet, Khut-Aten, Iat-Nebes, Per-Wer, Sheny-Ta, Ankh-Tawy, Mer-Atef, and Hut-Repyt—each rendering grain, beer, and labor to the capital and the temples, and each within a half-day's barge of the great city's markets.
