## Iat-Tepy — the Sacred Island

Midstream in the great river, between the living city on the east bank and the necropolis on the west, rises **Iat-Tepy** — the First Mound, the holiest ground in all of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]]. The Kheperi hold it to be the very mound that first lifted from the waters of creation at the [[affiliation-empirtkhpr#the-kheperi-calendar-and-dating|Sep Tepy]], the place where the world began; and upon it stand the two poles of imperial power — the **Great Temple of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Rā]]** and the **palace of the Per-Aá** — the god and the god-king moored together in the middle of the waters. It is the one part of [[place-wasetkara|Wasetkara]] ringed by a true fortified wall, white-plastered limestone visible for leagues and blinding at noon, and the one part whose gates are watched and whose ground is forbidden to the common crowd. Priests, courtiers, scribes of the inner bureaus, the royal guard, and those summoned before the throne pass its gates; the rest of the half-million city know it only as a white-walled vision across the water, and as the place from which the sun-god and the Per-Aá together rule their days.

### The Great Temple of Rā

The Great Temple of Rā is the oldest and grandest sanctuary in the empire and the seat of its official doctrine — the temple against which every other measures itself. It is less a building than an accreted city of stone: court behind court, pylon behind pylon, each dynasty having raised its own gate, hall, or forest of painted columns before the last, so that to walk inward from the river quay is to walk backward through three thousand years toward the small, dark, ancient sanctuary at the heart, where the god's image dwells and only the Wer'Hekau and the Per-Aá may go. Obelisks tipped with electrum catch the first and last light; a vast rectangular **sacred lake** mirrors the pylons, its level rising and falling with the flood. The temple owns estates across the Capital Nome and beyond, employs thousands, schools the empire's finest priests, and keeps — in the adjoining precinct of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Thōth]] — master copies of the king-lists and the calendar.

Its **Wer'Hekau of Rā** is, after the Per-Aá, the most powerful figure in the realm: it is the priesthood of this temple that has crowned and uncrowned more dynasties than any army, and it was here that the **False Rā** was judged, bound, and given over to the curse (see [[place-tadjeser|Ta-Djeser]]). The symbiosis of temple and throne is also their rivalry — the priests validate the Per-Aá's divinity, and the Per-Aá endows the temple's wealth, but each knows the other can be its undoing, and the long quiet duel between them is the deepest current of Kheperi politics.

### The Palace of the Per-Aá

Across the island from the temple stands the palace — not one building but a walled quarter of its own: the great audience-halls where the Per-Aá receives the empire as religious ceremony, the inner courts and private apartments of the royal household, the women's quarters and the royal nursery, the kitchens, treasuries, and storehouses, and the bureaus of the **inner court** that serve the sovereign's person directly. Its gardens are the finest in the empire — walled groves and lotus-pools fed from the river, a green sanctuary within the white walls. Here the fiction of divine kingship is performed at its most total: the Per-Aá enters and leaves through sacred gates, eats ritually prepared food, sleeps behind guarded doors, and is never quite alone, for every hour of the god-king's life is itself a rite of state. The **[[affiliation-kenbetpat|Kenbet'Pat]]**, the council of the great nobles, convenes in its halls; the [[place-wasetkara#governance-and-order|Tjaty]] and the high officers attend the throne; and the court's endless intrigues — succession and regency, favor and disgrace, the rivalry of throne and priesthood — are all played out behind these walls.

### The Measure

At the island's upstream point, where the river first strikes the sacred ground, stands **the Measure** — the great nilometer of the capital: a graduated stone stair and shaft descending to the water, its walls cut with the mark of every cubit of the flood. Each year, as the inundation rises, the priests of Rā read the Measure daily and carry the count to the temple; and as the flood nears its crest the reading is **proclaimed to the city and the empire** as the single most important number of the year. For the height of the flood foretells the harvest, and the harvest sets the taxes: a full Measure means plenty and a heavy levy, a low one means dearth, and a failed one means famine. The reading is announced as a festival — and the falsifying of it is among the gravest crimes a Kheperi can name, which is precisely the crime the [[place-tadjeser|False Rā]] committed when he hid a failing flood to mask the famine his blasphemy had called down.

### Walls, Gates, and Causeways

Iat-Tepy is the only fortified place in Wasetkara. Its white limestone wall rings the whole island, and its few gates are watched day and night by the **royal guard** — a body apart from the city's Medjay, sworn to the Per-Aá's person and the holy ground. The island is reached three ways: by the **Bridge of the Coming-Forth** from the living city to the east, by the **Bridge of the Going-Down** toward the necropolis to the west — both carrying the [[place-wasetkara#the-suns-road-and-the-great-processions|Sun's Road]] across the water on causeways raised above the flood — and by sacred barge to the temple and palace quays. In the flood season, when the lesser approaches drown, the island stands most truly alone above the waters, and the daily traffic of priests and officials goes by boat. Few but the highest sleep on the island; most who serve it cross from the city at dawn and return at dusk, as the dead are served across the water, so that the holy ground is left at night to the god, the king, and the guard.

### For the Worldbuilder

Iat-Tepy is the stage for the empire's highest play and its most controlled space. Run it as the place where access _is_ power: to be admitted is to matter, to be barred is to be nothing, and the whole drama of the court turns on who may pass which gate. The intrigues here are those of throne and temple — succession and regency, the favor of the Per-Aá, the quiet war between the sovereign and the Wer'Hekau of Rā who can unmake him, the reading of the Measure and the fortunes that ride on it. It is a hard place to reach uninvited — the wall, the royal guard, the watched bridges, the open water — and a dangerous one to be caught in unsanctioned. For a party, the island is more often a goal, a summons, or a heist than a haunt: an audience to be won, a secret to be carried out of the inner bureaus, a poisoned succession to be survived, or the king-lists and the Measure's true readings to be reached past the priesthood that guards them.
