## Overview

Ta-Djeser — "the Sacred Land" — is the necropolis-city on the western bank opposite [[place-wasetkara|Wasetkara]], the city of the royal dead. Here, in the shadow of the pyramids and rock-cut tombs of the dynasties, live the embalmers, tomb-builders, funerary artists, and mortuary priests who tend the eternal houses of the Per-Aás and the high nobility, together with the tomb-workers' settlements and the priest-guarded mortuary temples that serve the cult of the royal dead. It is a place apart — solemn, walled, and watched — where the wealth buried in the tombs and the sanctity of the kings make for an uneasy mix of devotion, secrecy, and temptation. The mortuary priesthoods and the necropolis-guards hold the western bank against the tomb-robbers who have plagued it since the first dynasty, and the embalmers' and craftsmen's guilds guard their trades and their dangerous knowledge of where the dead and their treasures lie.

Ta-Djeser is less a city people live in than one they _work_ in. A standing corps tens of thousands strong keeps it running: the keeper-priests who perform the daily offerings and the ceaseless calendar of mortuary rites; the restoration guilds of masons, gilders, painters, and inlayers who reface and renew the royal tombs so the honored dead never fall to ruin; the embalmers and coffin-makers; and the necropolis-guard who patrol the walls and the tomb-mouths against robbers. A resident core lives on-site in the closed tomb-workers' villages, but most cross by ferry each dawn from the living city and home again at dusk, for it is held unlucky to sleep among the dead without cause.

Ta-Djeser proper is the resting-place of royalty alone — the Per-Aás and their kin, along the dynastic Ways. Everyone else in the capital, from the greatest noble to the poorest laborer, lies in the vast graded burial-fields of [[place-khautimnet|Khaut-Imnet]] that spread across the desert to either side; and it is there, among the gold-rich and lightly-guarded tombs of the nobility and the merchant class, that the robbing trade truly works, for the royal Ways behind their walls and guards are far harder game. The royal tombs trust to the same layered defence as any subject's — concealment, barriers, traps, curses, and the bound dead set to walk the dark — only deeper and dearer than any subject could buy, of which the living binding of the **False Rā** is the blackest extreme.

## The Death-Road and the Dynastic Ways

The western half of the great ceremonial axis — the **death-road** — runs straight out of the river-crossing and climbs onto the desert shelf, dead level and dead straight, the axial line from the Great Temple of [[affiliation-kemtnpnthn|Rā]] on [[place-wasetkara|Iat-Tepy]] to the midwinter-sunset horizon. By a law older than any living dynasty, nothing may stand in that line: the road itself runs clear and open down its whole length, and the monuments are ranked to either side of it, so that from the temple across the water the eye travels west between the tombs of the kings to the place where the sun dies. On the midwinter **Going-Down**, the setting sun rolls straight down the corridor of the royal dead.

The necropolis is a single strict rectangle, walled and sacred, whose long axis is the death-road. It holds the Ways only of the dynasties that ruled _from_ Wasetkara — a continuous row, with no commemorative gap for the houses that reigned from other capitals and built their tombs at other necropoli; those are simply not here. At intervals of roughly a thousand paces a transverse stone avenue crosses the death-road, throwing one arm to the north and one to the south: each is a single dynasty's **Way**, the road of its Per-Aás, along which their pyramids, rock-cut tombs, and the sepulchres of their royal kin are ranked. By old custom each new dynasty raises its Way farther out toward the sunset, so the row _tends_ to run from the oldest, nearest the river, to the newest, at the desert frontier.

The length of each Way records the length of its dynasty. A great, long-lived house — scores of Per-Aás across many generations — drives its crossing-road nearly from the northern wall to the southern, lined with pyramids and a hundred lesser tombs; a brief or broken dynasty leaves only a short stub off the spine, a single royal tomb or two and then bare ground. Read with the feet, the necropolis is a chronicle of dynastic fortune — the mighty and the ephemeral side by side, a long reign and a short one a thousand paces apart and unmistakable at a glance.

But the row is no fixed timeline, for its ground is contested. The near-river end — first in every procession's path, in full view of the temple across the water — is the most honored real estate in the empire, and the strongest Per-Aás have not been willing to wait their turn at the frontier for it. More than once a powerful house has decreed the relocation of its predecessors: the older dynasties' mummies and grave-goods lifted from the river-front and rehoused in new tombs built farther out, the honored ground cleared, and the reigning dynasty installed at the head of the road. To disturb the royal dead is no small thing, and each such Removal is remembered as either the height of glory or the height of hubris.

The current dynasty did exactly this. Two and a half centuries old, it spent its first generations at the traditional frontier until one of its Per-Aás, at the peak of the house's power, ordered the **Great Removal** — the ancient houses that held the river-front carried outward, and the current dynasty's Way raised in their place at the very head of the death-road. It is the showpiece of the necropolis, and still unfinished, lengthened with each royal burial: splendid in dressed white stone, gleaming with gold leaf and polished bronze, and inlaid throughout with the deep cobalt blue the Kheperi prize above every other colour. It is the first thing the eye meets on the death-road and the last the setting sun touches before the horizon — and it is meant to leave no doubt whose age this is.

Nothing of consequence here is allowed to fall to ruin. The restoration guilds keep the honored Ways immaculate, refacing and re-gilding them so continually that some of the "oldest" monuments are, on their surfaces, centuries younger than the kings within; decay is the mark of neglect, not age, and creeps in only out in the unwatched reaches where a dynasty's endowment has lapsed, its cult dwindled, or its line been forgotten — the same reaches where the tomb-robbers work. A house that falls in true disgrace usually leaves no monument at all: its tombs are cleared, its mummies destroyed, its stone quarried for reuse, its names struck from every surface, and its Way pulled up and the ground re-let to a worthier line. The one exception is the **cursed road** — a single Way that no decree dares remove, marked with a warning stela and abandoned to rot, its temples roofless and its tomb-mouths shunned, left deliberately to ruin because the curse upon it forbids the touch of any tool. It is the one broken, silent stretch in a necropolis otherwise kept gleaming, and the guard walk wide of it after dark.

That Way belonged to a house six hundred years gone, and its story is the one every Kheperi child is told. Its last Per-Aá — the dynasty stood barely a century — overreached the throne's oldest fiction and proclaimed himself Rā in the flesh, not in rite but in truth, seizing the great temple and its granaries and silencing the priests who named the blasphemy. Then the floods failed. Year upon year the river would not rise, and as famine hollowed the valley he hid it: falsifying the readings of the Measure, hoarding the temple grain, and putting to death any who so much as spoke the word _hunger_. When he was at last thrown down, the priesthood exacted a price the god himself might see — his wives and children ritually killed before his eyes, and the Per-Aá bound and embalmed _living_, sealed breathing into his tomb, his name and his house's struck from every wall, so that in the records he is only the **False Rā** — though the oldest servants of the necropolis still whisper the truth of it: Khâfirah, "the Bold," last Per-Aá of the **House of Ba'en'Rê**, the Soul of Rā, the line whose very name turned to judgement upon it. His dynasty's Way was given over to the god and pronounced cursed; the next flood came full and the famine broke. Six centuries on no tool has touched it and no foot walks it — it lies four crossing-roads back from the river-front, its walls climbed by dunes and its tombs drowned in sand, a silent gap of desert in the gleaming city of the dead, and proof, the priests say, that the heart of even a Per-Aá is weighed.

And there is a darker truth the records omit but the temple keeps. Khâfirah was no mere tyrant but one of the great sorcerers of his age — which is why his claim to be Rā in the flesh was not laughed down at once — and his living entombment was less a punishment than a binding. The priests of Rā did not merely kill him; they sealed his death-magic into the tomb with him and warded his whole Way to hold for all time, for the curse is not only shame but a lock. Should the seal ever be broken and the False Rā loosed, it is held, he would rise a [[being-tereb|ṭerebu]] — a sorcerer-king with his wits and his death-magic intact — and spread ruin across the empire, famine again and worse. So the warning stela is no idle superstition, and the necropolis-guard keep an order as old as the curse: any soul seen seeking the cursed road, for any reason, is killed where they stand, without question or appeal. And still they come — grave-robbers certain that six centuries of dust hide the richest unplundered tomb in the world, scholars who would test the wards, and now and again a cult that would not bind the False Rā but _wake_ him. The sand takes them, and the guard say nothing.

## Notable Features

- The death-road and the dynastic Ways — the necropolis as an edited record in stone, each dynasty's crossing-road as long as the dynasty itself
- The current dynasty's Way at the river-front — white stone, gold leaf, and cobalt inlay, the showpiece raised by the Great Removal
- The standing corps of keeper-priests, restoration guilds, and necropolis-guard that keeps the honored tombs immaculate
- The cursed road — the warded Way of the **False Rā**, the sorcerer-king embalmed alive and bound; by standing order, any who seek it are killed on sight, lest he rise a ṭerebu
- The mortuary temples of the royal dead, and the embalmers' and tomb-builders' settlements
