## Overview

The Okháris Region is the southern margin of the
[[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]]—the country held by
[[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]], where hot, rain-watered forest, river valleys
and grassland in the west dry eastward into savanna. It is the last settled country before the
[[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], beyond which no northern
civilization has established a lasting presence.

## Geography

The country is hot throughout and wettest in the west. There regular rains
keep the grasslands green and the rivers running, and forest follows the
watercourses and thickens southward until it meets the wall of the rainforest.
The western land is fertile without canals: barley, sorghum, flax, dates and
figs grow on rain and river water, and the open plains carry cattle and horses.
The rains lessen moving east, and by the border with
[[place-bethuargn|Bethûa]] the land is semi-arid savanna and herding country.
Three cities stand above the villages—Zarhánis, Kaljékor and Vuthráka—and the
region's western edge touches the wet-coastal strip that runs the length of the
northern shore.

## Peoples and Culture

Many Okhárics move with their cattle between seasonal grazings, while the
village tribes farm, fish, fell timber and work their crafts from fixed seats.
Where the Khelâthi and the Bethûans built their civilizations around canals,
the Okhárics built theirs around the herds and the rain-fed land. Religious life follows the three-flame cosmology of
[[affiliation-nkaruthar|Nkaru'thar]], whose creation-destruction-renewal triad
is expressed through the three temple-cities; those cities are the region's
religious, trade and diplomatic anchors, and the urban-rural compact between
them and the clans is the Three-Flame Settlement.

## Politics and Power

The region is the territory of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]]. Its village tribes
owe fealty to the crown, and their chieftains and elders attend the moots held
in the temple-cities. Its frontier clans to
the east pay tribute to [[place-bethuargn|Bethûan]] authorities in exchange for
grazing rights, and a Khelâthi attempt centuries ago to bring the eastern
temple-cities into a tributary relationship collapsed within a generation
([[lore-templetrib|the Tribute of the Temple-Cities]]).
