## Overview

The Matriarchy of Bethua is a society in which women hold political, religious, and military authority. Located in the [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]] of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]], west of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], Bethua is a prosperous agricultural state organized around a strict gender hierarchy that inverts the patriarchal norms common to most of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. The realm is governed by the **Two Pillars**—a sacred pillar of priestesses and a secular pillar of clan-matriarchs—joined at the summit by the Metrissa, who is at once Queen and High Priestess. The throne passes not by inheritance but by election within the female royal line. See Bethuan Matriarchy for the full structure, titles, and succession.

## Geography

Bethua lacks a great river, and its landscape is shaped accordingly. The **northern coast** along the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] is a lush wet-coastal strip where citrus, grape, olive, fig, and date orchards flourish on the sea-tempered climate. Bethuan coastal cities have been prosperous trading ports for as long as anyone cares to remember, and the coastal aristocracy—the oldest and wealthiest clans—live in sprawling villa estates among the orchards. Bethuan coastal wine, olive oil, and preserved fruits are premium exports throughout the [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]] and beyond.

The **interior** is another landscape entirely. Rainfall is sparse inland, and Bethuan agriculture is an engineering achievement—a vast network of stone aqueducts, tunnel qanats, cisterns, and canals distributes water from seasonal mountain streams and deep wells to the grain fields of the hinterland. Between the irrigated cultivated strips, the land rolls in semi-arid grasslands given over to pastoralism: sheep, cattle, and the fine Bethuan horses for which the kingdom's cavalry is known. Bethuan engineering and water-management rival even Khelathi standards, and the professional engineering corps—traditionally staffed by women drawn from the priestly caste—is one of the kingdom's most prestigious institutions. Without their work, the interior would be uninhabited steppe, and every Bethuan child learns this as a foundational fact of civic life.

Southern Bethua shades into drier borderlands where the matriarchal writ grows thin and the country is shared with pastoral nomadic peoples who pay tribute rather than submit to full governance.

## Character

Bethuan society is structured around clans led by matriarchs. Property, titles, and political authority pass from mother to daughter. Men hold no standing—not merely barred from governance, religion, and the command of armies, but unable in law to own property, inherit, or contract in their own name. A Bethuan man is the dependent and charge of a woman, **a kind of property**: valued, often cherished, and prized above all for his strength, skill, and beauty, but property nonetheless (a small population of outright male slaves marks the lowest rung). Men do the labor of the workshops, orchards, fleets, and fishing-boats, but as agents and dependents of the women who own them, never as owners themselves. Beneath this regard runs a permanent fear of revolt by the physically stronger sex: no man may bear weapon or armor on pain of death unless specifically authorized, martial training for men is forbidden and watched-for, and men are kept under constant, customary surveillance. See [[affiliation-mtrrchybth#the-place-of-men|the status of men]] for detail.

The [[skill-bethunlng|Bethuan Language]] is related to [[skill-helonclng|Helonic Language]] through ancient migration, and Bethuan scholars maintain ties with intellectuals of [[place-helionis|Helionis]]. The culture venerates the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], worshipped across much of the world, and Bethuans venerate gods of both sexes as freely as anyone—a warrior prays to [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]], a smith to the [[affiliation-sacredforge|Sacred Forge]], a sailor to [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]]. What is distinctively Bethuan is the **state cult**: the sacred authority of the matriarchy is held to descend through goddesses—[[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] (creation and beauty, the sovereign's own patron), [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]] (dreams and the unseen), and [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] (prosperity and pleasure)—and it is these three goddess-orders that anchor the realm's sacred pillar of government. The Metrissa rules as high priestess of a goddess; the gods men favor simply have no place on that bench.

Bethua keeps a large and highly regarded military. Its army is a professional warrior class, overwhelmingly female—the [Spear-Sisterhood]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} (the _Lonkhai_)—trained from youth in arms, tactics, and a unique lethal unarmed art, the [Charissa]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} ("the Grace"), an open-hand and kicking style that turns the women's prized agility into a weapon and lets a trained woman best a larger, stronger man. Fighting beside them is the realm's one cadre of armed men: the [Spadai]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, sworn eunuch-warriors trusted with steel precisely because, gelded and without House or heirs, they stand outside the male threat the matriarchy fears—the most honored men in Bethua and the sole exception to the prohibition on armed men. Above all, Bethua is a **naval power**: its fleet is renowned the length of Mídhalión, guarding the coast, escorting the trade convoys, and waging a deniable privateer war on Vylarian shipping as the maritime proxy of its Khelathi patron (see [[affiliation-bethuanflt|The Bethuan Fleet]]). Together—army, cavalry, and fleet—they defend the kingdom's borders against the nomadic peoples to the south and maintain Bethua's independence among its more powerful neighbors. See [[affiliation-mtrrchybth#the-martial-bridge-the-spear-sisterhood-and-the-fleet|the martial sphere]] for detail.

## History

Bethua began as a **Helonic colonial foundation**—a daughter-settlement of the seafaring Helonic peoples, planted from [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]] on the southern Vylarian coast about 650 BF and governed by a council in Pelagora across the sea for twelve centuries ([[lore-daughtercts|The Daughter-Cities]]). Its matriarchal traditions, its Aureldian faiths, and its Helonic-descended tongue all date to this deep colonial past. Independence came only comparatively recently, in a war of separation from its metropole, Pelagora, fought about 615 AF ([[lore-bethuasep|The Separation of Bethua]]), that Bethua won with the decisive backing of the Empire of Au'Khelathu—whose court saw in a free Bethua a useful check on Helonic and Vylarian reach along the southern sea, much as it would later midwife the secession of [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]. That alliance left a permanent mark: Bethua kept its Aureldian faiths and the Helonic core of its sacred and clan institutions, but its newer machinery—treasury, water-engineering corps, court accounts, currency—was rebuilt on Khelathi models and bears Khelathi names to this day. See [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethuan Matriarchy]] for how this history shaped the modern state.

## Commerce and Currency

Matriarchy of Bethua uses the [[lore-aukhlthcrncy|Au'Khelathu Currency]]—temple-attested weight-pieces of copper, silver, and gold in gezan and qelu units. Bethua has no native coinage; the Khelathi pieces serve as the standard medium of exchange throughout the kingdom. Credit and large-value commerce flow through [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|The Bethuan Trade-Houses]], whose Great Houses maintain correspondent accounts with specific [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapters and issue their own letters of credit redeemable through those accounts. See [[lore-aukhlthcrncy|Au'Khelathu Currency]] for the full monetary system and The Bethuan Trade-Houses for the Bethuan institutional arrangements.

## Relations {#relations}

### Au'Khelathu—the patron

Bethua's defining foreign relationship is with the Empire of Au'Khelathu, the great empire to the east that midwifed its independence. To the Khelathi court, Bethua is a **useful proxy**—a free, prosperous, Khelathi-aligned power planted on the southern shore of the Vylarian Sea, whose existence keeps Helonic and especially **Vylarian** ambitions in Mídhalión perpetually off balance. A strong Bethua divides Vylarian attention, contests Vylarian trade, and gives [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] a forward partner on the sea without the empire having to project naval power of its own—much as Taruzel serves Khelathi interests in the eastern sea. The Khelathi cultivate the relationship accordingly: the loan-built treasury and water-engineering corps, the Gar-Hálzi correspondent accounts that underwrite the Trade-Houses, and a steady current of quiet subsidy and diplomatic cover all flow from this calculation. The Bethuans, for their part, understand the arithmetic perfectly well and accept it as the price of a freedom they could not otherwise hold—a proud people who know the difference between a patron and a master, and intend to keep it.

### The ports and the foreign trader

Bethua is a trading nation, and its harbors are open—but the militant female-centrism of its society does not stop at the dock. A **foreign man** who comes to a Bethuan port to trade is suffered rather than welcomed: he is served, his coin is taken, his contracts are honored, but he is met throughout with a cool, faintly contemptuous condescension, expected to defer to the women he deals with and quietly reminded at every turn that he holds no standing in the country whatsoever. A **foreign woman** trading on her own account faces no such treatment—she is dealt with as a normal merchant, woman to woman, and may find Bethua one of the easier markets in Mídhalión to work.

The single exception is **Au'Khelathu**. An official representative of the Khelathi crown—envoy, factor, temple-agent, or naval officer—is received with marked **deference regardless of gender**, a male Khelathi emissary treated with a courtesy no other foreign man could command. The distinction is deliberate and political: it is the visible, daily acknowledgement of the patron relationship, and Bethuan port officials make a point of it precisely because everyone watching—Vylarian captains above all—understands exactly what it signals.

## The Sovereign: the Metrissa

At the apex of both pillars stands the **Metrissa** (_MEH-tree-sah_; "the Sacred Mother," from Helonic _meter_, "mother," with the elevated priestess-ending _-issa_ that Bethuan reserves for goddesses and high priestesses). The office fuses two authorities that elsewhere are held apart: she is the **Queen of Bethua**, the secular sovereign whose word is the law of the realm, and she is the **High Priestess of the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]]**, whose goddess is foremost among the goddesses in Bethuan devotion and through whom the realm's legitimacy is held to descend. She presides over the [Sacred College]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} and over the [Council of Houses]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} both; she alone sits where the two pillars meet.

Her full ceremonial style is **"the Metrissa, Mother of Bethua, Veiled Daughter of Lusinia, First of the College and First of the Houses."** The phrase _First of the College and First of the Houses_ is constitutionally exact: it names her headship of each pillar in turn, and Bethuan jurists hold that a Metrissa who lost the confidence of one council would be only half a sovereign.

The regalia of the office is the [Purple Veil]{.name data-type="miscgear"}—a mantle and face-veil dyed in the deep murex purple that has been Bethua's signature export and royal color since colonial antiquity. To "take the Veil" is to be raised to the throne; to "set down the Veil" is to abdicate or die. In common speech the Metrissa is simply **the Veiled Mother**, and her court is the [Court of the Veil]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}.

The reigning sovereign is Metrissa [Amithena]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [House Kervanion]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, raised to the Veil some years ago by election of the Sacred College and confirmed by the oracle of the [[affiliation-zunelas|Faith of Zunelas]]. A consecrated priestess of Lusinia before her elevation, she is reckoned a careful, conservative ruler who keeps the two pillars in balance by giving neither its way entirely—a politician of the long game, more inclined to outlast her rivals than to overrule them.

## The Sacred Pillar: the Sacred College

The sacred pillar is the **temple**—but a specific part of it. Bethuans venerate the full Aureldian pantheon, gods and goddesses alike, as freely as anyone in the world; what is particular to Bethua is that _political-religious authority_ is reserved to the cults of three **goddesses**, because the matriarchy's legitimacy is held to descend through the divine feminine. The consecrated priestesshood of these goddesses holds not merely religious authority but a co-equal share of governance, and three goddess-orders structure the entire pillar (the many other cults, male and female, are honored but hold no share of rule):

- The Order of Lusinia (creation, beauty, sovereignty)—the **state cult**, the order of the crown itself. The Metrissa is its supreme priestess; its temples are temples of the realm. Because the sovereign cannot run an order day to day, the Lúsinian cult is administered by her chief deputy, the **Prohiérissa** (see below).
- The Order of Zunelas (dreams, the unseen)—the **oracular order**, keepers of divination, dream-incubation, and the reading of omens that legitimize elections and ratify great decisions of state. It is here that Khelathi religious influence shows most: Aethérian practice has absorbed Khelathi techniques of divination and judgment, and Bethuan oracles speak of the "weighing" of a candidate's worth in language a Khelathi priest would recognize. The faith remains wholly Aureldian; the method has traveled.
- The Order of the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]] (prosperity, pleasure)—the order of fertility, festival, marriage, and the **blessing of commerce**. Its temples consecrate the contracts and voyages of the Trade-Houses, and its clergy are the most worldly and the best-endowed of the three.

The governing body of the pillar is the Sacred College (Bethuan: _the Hierossa_)—the assembled high priestesses of the three orders and of the great temples, sitting in council at the capital, [[place-bethura|Bethura]]. The College is the realm's spiritual authority, its highest religious court, and—decisively—its **electoral body**: it is the College that elects each new Metrissa.

### Title-ladder of the sacred pillar

- **Metrissa**—the sovereign, supreme priestess of Lusinia (see above).
- **Prohiérissa** ("First Sacred One")—deputy head of the Lúsinian state-cult and the Metrissa's right hand. Because she runs the crown's own order and stands nearest the throne, the Prohiérissa is almost always the strongest candidate at the next election; the role is the closest thing Bethua has to a crown-princess, though it confers no guaranteed succession.
- **Hiérissa** (pl. _Hiérissai_; "Sacred One")—a high priestess: the head of one of the three goddess-orders, or of one of the great temples. The Hiérissai are the members of the Sacred College and among the most powerful individuals in the realm.
- **Hiora** (pl. _Hiorai_; "sacred woman")—an ordained priestess, the working clergy of a temple: she conducts the rites, keeps the sanctuary, teaches, and tends the temple's lands and charities.
- **Parthena** (pl. _Parthenai_; "consecrated maiden")—a novice or initiate, a girl or young woman in the long temple education that precedes ordination.

### The engineer-priestesses: the Order of the Waters

A specialized sub-order sits within the sacred pillar and forms one of Bethua's proudest institutions: the [Order of the Waters]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} (the _Qanatissai_, "sisters of the qanat"), the corps of **engineer-priestesses** who build and maintain the aqueducts, tunnel-qanats, cisterns, and canals on which the dry interior wholly depends. Drawn traditionally from the priestly caste, they are sworn women, half clergy and half engineers, and every Bethuan child is taught that without their work the hinterland would be empty steppe.

The order's expertise—and its institutional vocabulary—is the most visibly [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] thing in Bethua, learned through the alliance that won the realm its freedom. The chief engineer of the realm is styled the **Izet-Mu** ("Overseer of the Waters"), a Khelathi loan-title feminized to Bethuan use, deliberately echoing the Gar-Hálzi "Overseer of the Account." She answers to the Metrissa directly and sits in the Sacred College by right of office.

## The Secular Pillar: the Council of Houses

The secular pillar is the **clans**. Bethuan society is organized into matriarchal **Houses**—extended kin-networks holding land, title, and political standing, inherited from mother to daughter. The head of a House is a **Dómissa** (pl. _Dómissai_; "House-Mother," from Helonic _domos_, "house"). The Dómissai are the secular aristocracy of Bethua: they hold the coastal villa-estates and the interior grain-lands, they sit in judgment over their clansfolk, and they fill the offices of civil government.

The governing body of the pillar is the Council of Houses (Bethuan: _the Sunedra_)—the assembled Dómissai of the recognized clans, sitting at Bethura. The Sunedra is the secular legislature and high court of the realm: it ratifies law, votes the levies and taxes, adjudicates disputes between Houses, and speaks for the temporal interests of the aristocracy against the temple.

> **Note on the Trade-Houses.** The Trade-Houses and their own [Council of Matriarchs]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} are a _third, de facto_ power—a commercial federation, not an organ of the state. But the spheres interlock at the level of persons: the matriarchs of the great trading clans (the _An'Bet_) are themselves Dómissai who sit in the Sunedra, and many sit in the Sacred College besides. A Bethuan grandee may be at once a Dómissa of the Council of Houses, a Hiérissa of the Sacred College, and the matriarch of a Great Trade-House. The institutions are distinct; the women who staff them are not.

### Title-ladder of the secular pillar

- **Dómissa** (pl. _Dómissai_; "House-Mother")—the matriarch of a clan or House; a member of the Council of Houses.
- **Hégissa** (pl. _Hégissai_; "she who leads," from Helonic _hegemon_)—a **provincial governess**, a Dómissa appointed by the Metrissa to administer one of Bethua's cantons (the coastal city-districts, the interior watershed-districts, and the southern marches). She holds the local courts, the levy, and the waterworks-quotas in the sovereign's name.
- **Izet-Halzi** ("Overseer of the Account")—the **state treasurer**, head of the royal fisc. The title is a direct **Khelathi loan**, and not by accident: Bethua keeps no native coinage, runs on Au'Khelathu Currency, and built its entire fiscal apparatus on the Khelathi model during the liberation. The treasury of Bethua speaks Khelathi to this day. (See [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|The Bethuan Trade-Houses]] for the correspondent-banking arrangements that connect the realm's commerce to the Gar-Hálzi.)
- **Grammatissa** (pl. _Grammatissai_; "scribe-woman")—the literate civil administrators and clerks who keep the rolls, draft the law, and run the offices beneath the Hégissai and the Izet-Halzi.

## The Martial Bridge: the Spear-Sisterhood and the Fleet {#the-martial-bridge-the-spear-sisterhood-and-the-fleet}

For all that it is a modest realm in size, Bethua keeps a **large and famously capable military**—a professional standing army, the fine Bethuan horse for which the realm's cavalry is renowned, and, above all, a **navy whose reputation runs the length of Mídhalión**. The fleet is the instrument of Bethua's outsized role on the Vylarian Sea: it guards the coast and the orchard-ports, escorts the Trade-Houses' convoys, and—to the lasting irritation of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]—contests Vylarian dominance of the southern sea on behalf of Bethua and its Khelathi patron. A Bethuan war-galley is a respected and unwelcome sight in waters Vylarian captains would prefer to call their own.

The army's backbone is the **Spear-Sisterhood** (the _Lonkhai_, from Helonic _lonche_, "spear"), the female warrior class, trained from youth in arms and tactics. Bethuan women train not only in weapons but in a **unique and lethal unarmed combat art—the Charissa** ("the Grace"), an austere open-hand and kicking style that turns the agility and grace for which Bethuan women are prized into a weapon in its own right. A Charissa adept can kill unarmed, disable a stronger opponent before he can close, and fights with a quickness that heavier, more powerful foes struggle to answer; the art is built precisely around the advantage in speed and agility a trained woman holds over a larger man, and it is one reason an unarmed Bethuan woman is never to be mistaken for a defenseless one. The Charissa is taught to every woman of the Lonkhai and widely among free Bethuan women besides, and its masters (_Charissai_) are figures of real prestige.

Alongside the women fights one cadre of men: the **Spadai**, the sworn eunuch-warriors (see [The Place of Men](#the-place-of-men) below), the only males in Bethua permitted arms and the most honored men in the realm.

Command of the whole host is a **bridging office** that belongs neither wholly to the temple nor wholly to the Houses:

- **Stratégissa** ("she-general")—the supreme war-leader of Bethua, appointed by the Metrissa and answerable to her. She commands the army—Lonkhai and Spadai alike—and, through the Naukrátissa, the fleet besides. She attends both the Sacred College and the Council of Houses when matters of war are in question, but votes in neither; her authority is the sovereign's, lent.
- **Naukrátissa** ("sea-mistress")—the admiral of the Bethuan fleet and the realm's chief naval commander, answering to the Stratégissa and the Metrissa. In a maritime power whose navy is its proudest arm, hers is among the most prestigious military commands in Bethua. (For the fleet and the privateer war it wages, see [[affiliation-bethuanflt|the Bethuan Fleet]].)
- **Lonkhissa** (pl. _Lonkhissai_)—an officer of the Spear-Sisterhood, commanding a company of the Lonkhai.
- **Spados** (pl. _Spadai_)—a eunuch-warrior of the sworn cadre; serves under the Stratégissa and her officers like any other arm of the host.

## The Place of Men {#the-place-of-men}

Men in Bethua hold no political, religious, or military standing—and, uniquely among the realms of the region, no standing as free persons in the full sense either. In law and custom a Bethuan man is the **dependent and the charge of a woman**: his mother, then the matriarch of his House, then the wife or mistress to whom he is bound. He **cannot own property, cannot inherit, cannot make a binding contract in his own name**, and cannot stand before a court except through the woman responsible for him. Men are, in plain terms, **a kind of property**—valued property, often cherished property, but property nonetheless. The harshest expression of this is the small population of outright **male slaves**, who are bought and sold; the ordinary free-born man stands above them not in right but only in regard.

What a man is prized for is his **person**—his strength, his skill, and his beauty. A handsome, well-made man is an ornament to a House and a mark of his mistress's standing; a gifted craftsman, husbandman, or seafarer is a genuine asset. Such a man may be courteously styled **kiris** ("master," from Helonic _kyrios_)—a title of real esteem that confers, pointedly, nothing: no property, no rights, no voice. The labor of Bethua's workshops, orchards, fishing-fleets, and trading-vessels is largely men's labor, but the workshops, orchards, fleets, and cargoes belong to women; the men who work them do so as agents and dependents, never as owners. A man does not trade in Bethua—he trades _for_ the woman who holds him.

Beneath the regard runs a constant, sober **fear**. The matriarchy never forgets that its men are, on average, the physically stronger sex and that they outnumber the warrior-women many times over; the nightmare of every Metrissa is the riot in the harbor-quarter or the rising in the grain-villages that turns the realm's own men against it. Bethuan order is therefore engineered to make such a rising impossible. **No man may possess a weapon or a piece of armor, on pain of death**, save the few specifically authorized; **martial training for men is forbidden and actively watched-for**, and a man who shows an unsanctioned aptitude for arms brings suspicion down on himself and on the House that holds him; and men are, simply, **watched**—in the wards, on the docks, in the fields—by custom, by the Houses, and by the Spear-Sisterhood, as a permanent and unremarkable fact of civic life. The velvet of Bethuan regard for its beautiful, valued men is wrapped tight around this iron.

### The Spadai—the eunuch-warriors

The single great exception to the prohibition on armed men is the **Spadai** (sing. _Spados_; "the gelded swords"), the sworn cadre of **eunuch-warriors** who fight alongside the women of the Spear-Sisterhood. They are the most honored men in Bethua—and they are trusted with steel precisely _because_ they have been set outside the male threat the matriarchy fears. A Spados has no House to raise, no sons to set up as patriarchs, no stake in any male revolt; taken and gelded young and bound by oath into the service of the realm, he is wholly the matriarchy's creature, his loyalty engineered to be total and his ambition surgically foreclosed. The Bethuans repay this with genuine reverence: the Spadai are praised in song and ceremony, quartered and equipped at the Houses' and the crown's expense, and held up as the ideal of what a man may become once he has surrendered the things that make men dangerous. They are a small, elite, and ferocious corps under the command of the Stratégissa—the one place in all of Bethuan life where a man may carry arms and be celebrated for it.

### Foreigners in the ports

The same order shapes how Bethua receives outsiders. A **foreign man** who trades in a Bethuan port is suffered rather than welcomed—served and dealt with fairly, his coin taken and his contracts honored, but met throughout with a cool condescension and expected to defer to the women he negotiates with. Part of the disdain is simple affront: a free man trading on his own account, owning his own goods, presumes as of right to do the very things no Bethuan man may do. A **foreign woman** trading on her own account faces none of it, and may find Bethua one of the easier markets in Mídhalión to work. The lone exception to the contempt shown foreign men is an **official representative of the Empire of Au'Khelathu**, received with marked deference _regardless of gender_—a daily, public acknowledgement of the patron-proxy relationship that everyone, the watching Vylarians most of all, is meant to notice. (See [[affiliation-mtrrchybth#relations|the realm's Relations]].)

## Succession: the Election of the Metrissa

The Veil passes by **election within the female line**—never by simple inheritance. When a Metrissa sets down the Veil, the **Sacred College** convenes as an electoral conclave and chooses her successor from among the **eligible women of the sacred royal line**: the consecrated priestesses of Lusinia who descend from the Metrissai of the past—daughters, nieces, granddaughters, and cousins of reigning and former sovereigns. Birth alone does not qualify a candidate; she must also be a priestess of the state cult, which is why ambitious royal kinswomen take the veil of Lusinia young.

The election proceeds in two movements. First the **College deliberates and votes**, the Hiérissai weighing the candidates' fitness, faction, and the balance of the two pillars. Then the chosen name is carried to the **oracle of Zunelas**, whose ratification—framed as the goddess's confirmation that the candidate's worth has been "weighed and found true"—makes the election sacred and final. In practice the oracle confirms the College's choice; in theory it could refuse, and the memory that it has refused, once or twice in the realm's history, gives the Aethérian order a quiet veto that everyone respects and no one tests lightly.

Because the office is elective, Bethua has no automatic heir and a permanent, low-grade succession politics. The **Prohiérissa** is the natural front-runner, but a strong faction of Dómissai in the Council of Houses can lift a rival; the Trade-Houses can fund one; the Aethérian oracle can sink one. A reigning Metrissa who wishes to shape her own succession does so not by naming an heir—she cannot—but by appointing a favored kinswoman as Prohiérissa and grooming the College's goodwill toward her. Whether that grooming holds once the Veil is set down is the great recurring drama of Bethuan high politics.

## The Two Pillars in Balance

The genius and the fragility of the Bethuan constitution is that **neither pillar can rule without the other**. The Sacred College consecrates the Metrissa and holds the legitimacy of the realm, but it does not hold the land, the grain, or the levies—the Houses do. The Council of Houses commands the realm's wealth and arms, but it cannot make a sovereign or bless a law without the temple. The Metrissa is the keystone that holds the arch, and a sovereign who governs well plays the two pillars against each other just enough to keep herself indispensable to both. A weak Metrissa finds the pillars leaning past her toward each other—the College and the Houses cutting their own bargains—and a realm so governed drifts toward either theocracy or oligarchy until a strong sovereign takes the Veil and pulls the keystone back into place.
