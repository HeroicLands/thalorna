The navy is the proudest arm of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethua]] and the instrument of its outsized place in the [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]]. A modest realm of three million souls has no business commanding the respect at sea that Bethua does—and yet a Bethuan war-galley is a known and unwelcome sight from the ports of [[place-helionis|Helionis]] to the archipelago of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]], and the realm's captains are reckoned among the most aggressive sailors on the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]]. The fleet is the reason a small southern-shore power can contest the trade of empires, and it is the sharpest edge of the bargain Bethua struck with the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]: where the Khelathi navy guards the southern shore but rarely sails north for strategic purposes, the Bethuan fleet carries Khelathi interest into the northern sea-lanes that [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]'s own ships will not enter.

## Place Among the Sea-Powers

The Vylarian Sea is contested by three great navies—the declining but still-formidable [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian fleet]], the modern and ascendant [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzelian fleet]], and the large but homebound Empire of Au'Khelathu navy (kept to the delta's mouths and coast since the reign of [Gez'Uzner I]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [[lore-seahouse|the Closing of the Sea House]])—and beneath them a tier of lesser powers. Among that second tier, **Bethua is the foremost and the most feared**. It cannot stand in a line-of-battle against Vylaria or Taruzel and does not try; its strength is in fast galleys, superb seamanship, intimate knowledge of the southern waters and islands, and a doctrine built not around fleet engagements but around **commerce-raiding, convoy-cutting, and the deniable private war**. A Heliónite squadron may be more skilled ship-for-ship, and Okhárian [[being-takorozanethar|Tak'oro Zanethar]] holds the far southwest, but no secondary power on the sea projects menace into the trade-lanes the way Bethua does.

## Character of the Fleet

Bethuan warships are **oared galleys** in the southern tradition—long, lean, and fast, built for the dash and the boarding-action rather than for weathering the open ocean, which suits a navy whose whole theater is the enclosed sea. They are built in the yards of the northern orchard-ports and crewed out of the same coastal cities whose villa-clans have grown rich on sea-trade since colonial antiquity. Bethuan sails are dyed, by long custom, in the realm's signature **murex purple**—the color of the [Purple Veil]{.name data-type="miscgear"}—so that a squadron under full sail is unmistakable, a deliberate piece of intimidation as much as a mark of pride. The same purple flies as the fleet's ensign; it is why the sailors of the northern shore speak of "the Veiled Sails" coming out of the south.

The fleet's home is the chain of fortified harbors along the northern Bethuan coast, the greatest of them at the maritime city of the [House Othris-Khanu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the principal Bethuan shipping clan, whose private capacity and the crown's are not always easy to tell apart. Naval stores—timber, pitch, cordage, sailcloth—are a strategic industry watched as closely as the waterworks of the interior, and a berth in the royal yards is a prized appointment.

### Who Mans the Ships

The crewing of a Bethuan warship is a study in the realm's whole social order. **Command and the fighting complement are women's work and the work of the gelded.** The captain is a woman; her officers are women; the marines—the boarding-fighters who decide a galley action—are drawn from the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth#the-martial-bridge-the-spear-sisterhood-and-the-fleet|Spear-Sisterhood]] and the eunuch [Spadai]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, and they fight with blade, with javelin, and with the lethal unarmed [Charissa]{.name data-type="skill/combat"} in the press of a boarding where a longer weapon is useless. A boarding-action against a Bethuan galley is a thing Vylarian sailors speak of with dread, precisely because the fighters who come over the rail are faster and deadlier in the crush than their size suggests.

The **labor of the ship, though, is men's**—the rowers at the oars, the deckhands in the rigging, the shipwrights and pitch-boys of the yards. They serve as everything men in Bethua serve as: as the agents and dependents and property of the women who own the vessels, free-born hands and outright slaves pulling the same oar. A Bethuan man may spend his life at sea and never own so much as the bench he rows from. The arrangement gives the fleet a particular hardness—its fighting class and its laboring class are divided not only by rank but by sex and by law—and it is one more reason the Charíssa-trained marines are watched as carefully aboard as the oar-slaves are: an armed and able man is never far from the women who command him, and never permitted to forget it.

## Command

The fleet answers, like all of Bethua's armed force, through the bridging command of the crown:

- The [[affiliation-mtrrchybth#the-martial-bridge-the-spear-sisterhood-and-the-fleet|Stratégissa]] holds supreme command of the whole host, army and fleet alike, as the Metrissa's lent authority.
- The **Naukrátissa** ("sea-mistress") is the admiral of the fleet and the realm's chief naval commander—in a maritime power whose navy is its proudest arm, one of the most prestigious commands in Bethua, and a frequent stepping-stone to the highest circles of both councils.
- Below her, each war-galley or squadron is commanded by a **Nauklera** (pl. _Naukléreai_; "ship-mistress," from Helonic _naukleros_), a captain who in the private and privateer service is also the owner of her vessel.

## The Purple Warrant: the Privateer War

Bethua's most consequential naval instrument is not its battle-fleet but its **licensed privateers**. The crown issues, under the Metrissa's seal and through the Naukrátissa's office, a commission of private war called the [Purple Warrant]{.name data-type="lore/law"}—a sealed authority for a private captain to arm her vessel, take enemy shipping as lawful prize, and bring her captures into a Bethuan port for condemnation and sale. A **warrant-captain** (a _Nauklera_ of the Purple) keeps the bulk of what she takes; the crown takes its share; the temples of [[affiliation-olvenia|Ólvenia]], who bless and register the prize-condemnations, take theirs. It is private enterprise and state policy fused into a single hull.

The Warrant is, in practice, aimed at **Vylaria**. The standing instruction is to prey on Vylarian and Vylarian-protected shipping—the grain and wine and steel of the northern trade—while leaving Khelathi, Taruzelian, and (carefully) neutral Heliónite and Bizarian bottoms alone. The strategic logic is the one Bethua lives by: every Vylarian cargo taken, every northern voyage that must now sail in convoy or pay for escort, every insurance rate that climbs in the Vylarian ports is a small weight laid on the empire's commerce and a small profit booked to a Bethuan House. The warrant-captains are the deniable arm of a deliberate policy of economic harassment—and the policy is not only Bethua's. It is the maritime face of the **Khelathi proxy strategy**: Galezkara cannot and will not send its own navy north to harry Vylarian trade, but a free Bethua with a fleet of purple-sailed privateers does the work just as well, and Au'Khelathu can disclaim all knowledge of it.

### The Deniability Dance

Everyone understands the arrangement and everyone maintains the fiction. When a warrant-captain takes a Vylarian merchantman, the Vylarian court protests to Bethua and, beyond Bethua, to Au'Khelathu; the Metrissa's court regrets the excesses of private captains over whom (it says) it holds imperfect control; the Khelathi court professes bafflement that anyone should connect a Zerathian neighbor's commercial disputes to the dignity of the Gar-Au. The same polite theater that surrounded Khelathi aid to the Taruzelian secession surrounds the Purple Warrant—and serves the same end: to weaken Vylaria's grip on the sea while keeping the harassment beneath the threshold that would justify a real war. A warrant can be quietly recalled when a captain becomes an embarrassment; it can be quietly multiplied when the court wishes to turn the pressure up.

## Vylarian Retaliation

Vylaria is the aggrieved party and the most dangerous, and its responses run the full range short of open war:

- **Convoy and escort.** The standard answer: Vylarian and allied merchantmen on the southern routes sail in escorted convoys, and the cost of that escort—paid in galleys, in time, in higher freight and insurance—is itself part of what the Warrant is designed to impose.
- **Anti-privateer sweeps.** The Vylarian admiralty, formidable still in its own waters, mounts periodic sweeps into the central narrows and toward the southern islands to burn out privateer havens and hunt warrant-captains. They catch some; the rest disperse into the islands and the Bethuan ports and wait, in the same rhythm by which the sea's endemic piracy ebbs and flows.
- **Hired hunters.** Vylaria and the northern merchant interests retain the prized Heliónite mercenary captains—the most skilled sailors on the sea—to hunt Bethuan privateers, turning skill against audacity. A Heliónite squadron in southern waters is the warrant-captains' most respected enemy.
- **Commercial reprisal.** Through [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]] and [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], Vylaria can squeeze the Bethuan exporting economy—withdrawing the standing arrangements that honor letters of credit of [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|the Bethuan Trade-Houses]] at the northern trading-ports, so that the Houses feel the cost of their captains' prizes in their own ledgers. This is the lever that divides the Bethuan interest against itself (see Plot Hooks).
- **Coastal reprisal raids.** When provoked far enough, Vylarian or Vylarian-hired squadrons strike the Bethuan orchard-ports directly—burning shipping, wrecking the murex-yards, carrying off what they can. These raids are costly and politically fraught, and the standing fear on the Bethuan side that a great raid might escalate is exactly the deterrent that keeps the Warrant from being pushed too far.
- **Diplomatic pressure on the patron.** The subtlest Vylarian play is to go over Bethua's head and offer Au'Khelathu inducements—trade concessions, a settlement of some other dispute—in exchange for reining in its proxy. Whether Galezkara takes the bait is the central uncertainty the whole arrangement turns on.

The equilibrium holds because no one wants it broken: Vylaria cannot project decisive force into the southern waters without stripping its home waters; Bethua and Au'Khelathu do not want the Vylarian battle-fleet roused south in earnest; and so the war stays small, deniable, and permanent—a slow bleed on Vylarian commerce that everyone has an interest in keeping below the boil.

## Plot Hooks

- **The Captain Who Went Too Far.** A celebrated warrant-captain has taken a ship she should not have—a neutral Heliónite bottom or a bottom of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]], or a vessel carrying a personage of rank—and turned a tolerable nuisance into a diplomatic crisis. The Metrissa's court must decide whether to disavow and surrender her (placating the injured power and the watching Vylarians) or to shield her (and own the consequences). She, meanwhile, has her own ideas, a fast ship, and a hold full of evidence about who really issues the Warrants.
- **The Squeeze.** Vylaria has persuaded enough of the Danvar to stop honoring the letters of credit of a major Bethuan House, and the House is bleeding. Its matriarch—who has profited handsomely from the privateers—now wants the Warrant reined in to save her ledgers, while the Houses still flush with prize-money want it pressed harder. The [Council of Houses]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} is split, and the quarrel is really about whether Bethua's future lies with its Khelathi patron or its Vylarian customers.
- **The Reprisal.** A Vylarian squadron—or Heliónite hunters in Vylarian pay—has burned a Bethuan orchard-port and its yards in answer for a season of lost cargoes. The realm is enraged and the hawks want a fleet action; the Metrissa knows a fleet action is exactly what Bethua cannot win. Someone must thread the needle between honor and ruin—perhaps a deniable answer-in-kind, perhaps a quiet appeal to the patron whose policy started the whole cycle.
- **The Patron's Price.** Word reaches Bethua that Vylarian envoys have made Au'Khelathu a serious offer to call off its proxy. If Galezkara accepts, the Warrants stop, the prize-money dries up, and Bethua is suddenly alone with the enemy it spent a generation provoking. The court must find out whether the rumor is true and what, if anything, can be done to keep the patron in the game—or whether the time has come to be less of a proxy and more of a power.
- **The Oar-Slave's Knife.** A warrant-galley returns to port with a story that does not add up: the captain dead, the prize lost, and an oar-deck of men who will not meet a questioner's eye. A rising at sea—the very nightmare the matriarchy is built to prevent—has happened on one ship, and the question of how, and whether it can happen again, and who among the surviving men can be allowed to live to tell of it, lands on the desk of the Naukrátissa and the watchers of the nearest House.
