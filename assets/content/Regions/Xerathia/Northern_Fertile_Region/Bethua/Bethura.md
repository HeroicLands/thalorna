## Overview

> "Off the ship, are you? Then you have seen our quays and none of our government. The orchard-ports grow the wine and press the oil. Bethûra decides what they owe for it, and whose paper they may be paid in."

That is roughly how a factor of one of the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Trade-Houses]] puts it to a foreign trader who has just stepped onto the harbor-front, and it is a fair summary. Bethûra is the capital of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethûa]] and the city where the realm is governed: some thirty-five thousand people in the [[place-bethuargn|Bethûa Region]] on Xerathia's northern shore. Both pillars of the Bethûan state sit here—the **Sacred College** of the high priestesses and the **Council of Houses** of the clan-matriarchs—and the Mêtríssa, Queen and High Priestess, presides over each. The Trade-Houses keep the principal hall of their federation in the same city.

Bethûra stands on a bay of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], and a ship bound for the capital enters the calm water of the bay with the whole city in front of it: the quays and bonded warehouses of the harbor-front, which every Bethûan calls the _en-meret_ (quay), and above them the temple roofs and the halls of the Houses climbing the slope. The city sits in the sea-tempered coastal strip, among the same orchards of citrus, olive and vine as the other ports. Behind the orchards the rain falls away, and the country becomes the semi-arid grassland of the interior, which grows only what water is brought to it. The quays of the bay and the coast roads to the other orchard-ports are what the **Court of the Veil** watches most closely.

## The Water

A factor teaching a newcomer usually starts with the water, because every Bethûan child learns it before anything else: without the engineer-priestesses, the interior would be empty steppe. Say you ride inland from the bay in the dry months. On one side of a stone channel the wheat stands green; on the other the grass is the color of straw, and sheep, cattle and the fine Bethûan horses graze it. The line between the two is where the water stops.

That water reaches the fields, and the capital's own fountains, through aqueducts, tunnel qanats, cisterns and canals, and the women who build and keep them are the **Order of the Waters** (_Qanatíssai_, "sisters of the qanat"), sworn priestesses who are half clergy and half engineers. Inside the irrigated strips the farms grow wheat, barley, millet and legumes; between the strips, the open grassland is pasture. The upkeep of the works employs tens of thousands of people across the interior year-round.

The chief of the order is the **Izet-Mû** (Overseer of the Waters). She answers to the Mêtríssa directly and sits in the Sacred College by right of office, so the woman who keeps the capital's water also has a voice in choosing its queen. Her title is Khelâthi, borrowed during the alliance with the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] that won Bethûa its freedom, and so is the treasurer's, the **Izet-Halzi** (Overseer of the Account). The titles of Bethûra's temples and Houses are Helonic; the titles of its water and its money are Khelâthi.

## The Seat of the Two Pillars

The **Sacred College** (_the Hieróssa_) is the assembly of the Hiérissai, the high priestesses of the three goddess-orders of Lúsinía, Aethería and Ólvenía and of the great temples. In Bethûra it sits as the realm's spiritual authority and its highest religious court. When a Mêtríssa sets down the Veil, the College meets as a conclave and elects her successor from the priestesses of the royal line, and the oracle of Aethería confirms the choice.

The **Council of Houses** (_the Sünédra_) is the assembly of the Dómissai, the matriarchs of the recognized clans. In Bethûra it ratifies law, votes the levies and taxes, judges disputes between Houses, and speaks for the land-holding aristocracy against the temple. The College holds the realm's legitimacy; the Houses hold its land, grain and levies. Neither can govern without the other.

The Mêtríssa sits where the two meet. Her regalia is the **Purple Veil**, a mantle and face-veil of murex purple, and her court is the Court of the Veil. The reigning sovereign is **Mêtríssa Amîthéna of House Thamîrîon**, a former priestess of Lúsinía and a careful, conservative ruler who keeps the two pillars in balance by giving neither its way entirely. The Stratégissa, the supreme war-leader, attends both councils when war is in question and votes in neither.

A factor's practical lesson is that the same women turn up on every bench. Suppose your contract is caught in a quarrel between two trading Houses. The Council of Houses judges disputes between Houses as the realm's high court; the Trade-Houses' own council settles commercial disputes between its members. Many of the matriarchs who sit in one sit in the other, and some sit in the Sacred College besides, so the choice of bench is mostly a choice of which office the same grandee is wearing that day.

## The Trade-Houses' Hall

Twice a year the **Council of Matriarchs**—the matriarchs of the Great Houses, the _An'Bet_—meets at the federation's principal hall in Bethûra. There are perhaps a dozen Great Houses, among them **House Khalath-Mara**, the oldest and most prestigious, whose ancestral seat is at [[place-ashira|Ashíra]] on the coast; **House Bera-Sehel**, horse-breeders and suppliers to armies; and **House Othris-Khanu**, the principal shipping House. Each session the council chooses a **Speaker of the Council** by consensus from the matriarchs present. She presides, frames the questions and announces the rulings, and the office rotates; the federation has no standing chief, and its members prefer it that way. Its senior voice by age and precedence is **Matriarch Aselia of House Khalath-Mara**, who has held her seat for thirty-five years.

The council sets the protocols by which the Houses honor each other's letters of credit, settles disputes between Houses, and admits or expels members. That first duty is the one a visitor meets. Bethûa strikes no coin of its own, so every purse in the city, from a Dómissa's to a porter's, holds Khelâthi weight-pieces of copper, silver and gold. Large sums move as letters of credit: sealed documents bearing a House's seal, a value in gezan, a payee or "to bearer," and a serial mark.

The factor's warning about them is short. A Great House's letter is good at any other Great House. A Lesser House's letter is good only at Houses that have agreed to honor it, and a Junior House's is rarely taken outside the city that issued it. If you are paid in paper, ask which Houses will honor it before you leave Bethûra.

## Men, Strangers and the Law

The second warning is the one foreigners need most. In Bethûa a man holds no property, no inheritance and no contract in his own name, and the realm watches its men—in the wards, on the docks, in the fields—as a plain fact of civic life. No man may carry a weapon or armor on pain of death unless he is specifically authorized. The one body of men permitted arms is the **Spádai**, the sworn eunuch-warriors who serve beside the **Spear-Sisterhood** (_Lonkhai_) and are the most honored men in the realm. The factor's advice to a foreign man who arrives wearing a sword is blunt: "The law is written for our men. Do not make the Spear-Sisters decide whether it is written for you."

A foreign man who comes to trade is served, his coin taken and his contracts honored, but he is met with a cool condescension and expected to defer to the women he deals with. A foreign woman trading on her own account is dealt with woman to woman, as any merchant would be. The one exception is an official representative of Aû'Khelâthu—envoy, factor, temple-agent or naval officer—who is received with marked deference whatever his or her sex. Bethûan officials make a point of that courtesy, because it shows everyone watching who the realm's patron is.

Do not mistake an unarmed Bethûan woman for a defenseless one. The **Charíssa** ("the Grace"), an open-hand and kicking art built on speed, is taught to every woman of the Spear-Sisterhood and widely among free women besides.

## The Houses of the City

Not every House of Bethûra holds great estates. The city's lettered Houses supply the clerks a realm of rolls and qanats depends on. **House Safîrôn**, a middling House of Bethûra, has served the realm's offices for generations, and [[being-rshrhssfrn|Rashîra of House Safîrôn]] rose from it to become a senior Grammatíssa (scribe) of the Naukrátissa's office and a keeper of the sealed rolls of the **Purple Warrant**, the crown's commission for privateers. She has since fled south with one of those rolls. House Safîrôn renounced her before the Court of the Veil and struck her from its rolls, because in Bethûa a House that shelters or even mourns a traitor to the crown invites ruin on every woman of its line.

**House Marut** is a prosperous Lesser House of the Trade-Houses whose trade runs from the orchard-ports across the Vylarian Sea to Aû'Khelâthu. Its heir-presumptive, [[being-laylamarut|Layla Marut]], makes her home in Bethûra and means to see her House rise to a Great House on terms she sets herself. She spends less time at the counting-house than her mother intended. House Marut once hired the thief-taker [[being-zahirmlkht|Zahira Malkhet]] to run down a caravan thief, Layla rode along to "learn the trade-roads," and she has ridden with Zahira since.

Zahira works out of Bethûra, hiring her tracking, her blade and her Charíssa to the Houses, the Trade-Houses, the cantonal governesses and the agents of the capital itself. In the circles that know of such work she is called the **Hound of the Veil**.
