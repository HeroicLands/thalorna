At Khelunu a Keeper of the King-Lists lays a thumbnail beside a line of the list and says to a new copyist: "Read me this one." The copyist reads: _the throne passed to the one who stood beside it._ "Now read the line above it." The copyist reads that a child of three floods had gone to the West in the night. "Now you can read the list," the Keeper says. "That is the line the present house is built on."

## The Child and the Regent

The house of **Qelt'Gultau** took the throne in 2559 ST (449 AF), and its first Gar-Aû, Quz'el'Retha I, reigned eighteen years. Quz'el'Retha II followed her and reigned four. When he went to the West in 2579 ST (469 AF), **Quz'el'Retha III** was crowned a child of three floods, and **Zu'Mequ** stood beside him.

That is the list's formula for a regent. A child of three floods cannot hold the sceptre or speak the rites, and the head of a named house stands beside him to do both. The list enters the two names together for the child's reign, as it enters them in every reign of this kind, and the Keepers read the pair as a warning, because a regent of a named house stands closer to the mound than anyone else in the valley.

## The Night

In 2580 ST (470 AF), in the child's second year, the child went to the West in the night. The list enters the manner of a death in no reign, and it enters none here. It enters the line above the crowning and the crowning below it, in the same year, and the line that carries them both is the one the copyist read: **the throne passed to the one who stood beside it.**

The priesthood of Uqa'â crowned **Meqes** of Zu'Mequ on the mound at [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] in the same year. The house of Zu'Mequ was not one of the great houses of the delta or the upper river. Its head had been a regent for two years, and the priesthood had the choice of crowning him or of raising a house of its own, as it had raised the houses before him. It crowned him. The list does not say why, and the Keepers say that the list is right not to.

He kept his own name as his throne name. A throne name is chosen at the crowning, a name element joined to a god's house-form, and almost every Gar-Aû before him had worn one that told the valley what was expected of him. Meqes wore his given name, and the house wore it after him.

## The Name

Every Gar-Aû of the house has been crowned Meqes, and the list numbers them as it numbers any name, by bearers across the whole length of the list. The first was Meqes I, who reigned fourteen years. The sixteenth is the Gar-Aû who reigns now, [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]]. The name makes a contract or a tax roll easy to date within the house and impossible to date without a numeral, and every clerk in the bureaus learns the numerals before the signs.

## What It Left

- **The name and the house.** The reigning house, [[lore-akhhsmeqes|the longest of the Near Count]], descends from a regent who was crowned in a child's second year.
- **The dread of children's reigns.** The house has two children's reigns of its own in the list, and the court keeps a regent's loyalty in mind.
- **The formula.** The line the copyist reads is the most worn line in the list, and it is on this reign that every later copyist learns it.
- **The Way.** The house raised its Way at the traditional frontier of the necropolis at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]], and its sixth Gar-Aû carried it to the river-front ([[lore-greatremovl|the Great Removal]]).
