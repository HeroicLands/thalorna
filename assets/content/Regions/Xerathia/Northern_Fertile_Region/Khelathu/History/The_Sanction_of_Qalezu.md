A stranger at the gate of any Khelathi counting-house is asked the same question before he is asked his business: "Which hand were you taught?" The question dates from a queen.

## The Paperwork That Outgrew the Priesthood

By the generation after the sea house the empire's records had outrun its scribes. The delta weighed and taxed every cargo, the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapters kept the temple accounts, and the king-list had begun to give months. All of it was written in the sacred hand: hieroglyphs, each drawn with care, taught over years by the priests of [[affiliation-rethsaar|Reth'Sa'ar]], and fit for a tomb inscription and ruinous for a daybook.

The bureaus of [[place-garmedjatozo|Gar-Medjatozo]] solved the problem in practice before anyone allowed it in law. Their clerks reduced the hieroglyphs to the consonants a daybook needs, took each consonant from a hieroglyph whose word begins with it, and wrote the twenty-four signs apart with a reed on papyrus at the speed of a counting. The result was an alphabet that another clerk could learn in a season and a priest could not like.

## The Queen

[Gith'el'Retha I]{.name data-type="being/npc"} ("daughter in knowledge") was crowned in 2025 ST (86 BF) as the first Gar-Au of [Amqelu'Gatau]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}. She is one of the empire's ablest queens, and she did the thing a Gar-Au does best: she made a custom a law. About 2030 ST (81 BF) she sanctioned the clerks' alphabet, Qalezu, as the hand of the bureaus, the tax rolls and the courts.

The Reth'Sa'ar priesthood did not oppose it. The sacred hand was the god's, and the temples kept it whole for the tomb, for the sacred text and for the rite. What the queen's decree took from them was the daybook.

## Two Hands

After the decree a Khelathi scribe learned two things where his predecessors had learned one. The sacred hand was the prestige register: slow, exact, cut into stone, and kept for what lasts. [[skill-qalzscrscrpt|Qalezu]] was the working register: quick, plain, abbreviated, and the hand in which most of the empire's business was written. Every educated Khelathi read the first or the second, and a stranger's schooling was measured by which.

## What It Left

- **The question at the gate.** "Which hand were you taught?" sorts every foreigner and every clerk.
- **The counting-house and the tax roll.** Both are written in Qalezu, and the forgers work in it too.
- **A queen the empire boasts of.** Gith'el'Retha I is one of the rulers the Khelathi cite when they say their queens were their ablest.
- **A division of literacy.** The temples read the sacred hand and the bureaus read Qalezu, and the schools of [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]] teach both.
