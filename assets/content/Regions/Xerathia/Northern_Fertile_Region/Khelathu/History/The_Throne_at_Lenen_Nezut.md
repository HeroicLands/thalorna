A priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] at Lenen-Nezut shows a visitor the long forecourt of the chief temple and says: "Count the paces from the ram's door to the altar. That is the length of a throne room." The town is not large, and the forecourt is built for a court that is long gone. It was a **capital** once, and this is how.

## The Crowning

In 1462 ST (649 BF) the throne of Aû'Khelâthu was in dispute between the river's two ends. Houses ruled and fell at [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] in this stretch of the Middle Count, and the upriver selatu, whose granaries fed the capital, had grain enough to carry a claim of their own. In that year a house of the upper river, which the list calls **Geze'el'Anlaghu**, had its head crowned in his own city, in the temple of the ram at [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], and he took the throne name **Zab'el'Psaqa I**. The priesthood of Uqa'â, which has deposed and raised more Gar-Aûu than any army, performed the rite.

He was not crowned on the mound at Yath-Telgu, and the list enters him all the same. It records him as it records any Gar-Aû, with his years and the formula his end was entered under, and that entry is the strongest evidence that the priesthood meant the crowning to hold.

## Sixty-nine Years

Three Gar-Aûu of the house reigned from Lenen-Nezut: Zab'el'Psaqa I, a second whose reign fills the middle of the stretch, and **Zab'el'Psaqa III**, crowned in 1519 ST. For sixty-nine years the king-list counts the line as the legitimate one. Meanwhile the delta and the mound were held by claimants who were crowned and who ruled, and the list gives them no reign at all. The list's silence is the plainest case in the whole Middle Count of the list choosing whom to count.

The house did not leave its dead to the capital. The three Gar-Aûu were buried at home, in tombs on the desert edge above the town, and the row of Ways at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]] holds nothing of them. The house laid out its tombs and their short crossing-road in imitation of the necropolis across the river from the capital ([[place-lnzways|the Lenen-Nezut Ways]]).

## The End of It

The line ended in the field. In 1531 ST (580 BF) Zab'el'Psaqa III was killed at Iqeru in a war with the house that ruled the delta, and the upriver throne did not outlast him ([[lore-twothrones|the War of the Two Thrones]]).

## What It Left

- **A capital-sized town.** Lenen-Nezut holds a temple forecourt, a scribal bureau and a set of granaries far larger than a selat seat needs.
- **The Halzi'a's house.** The house of Geze'el'Anlaghu was kept as the Halzi'a of Lenen-Nezut after its Gar-Aûu fell, and its descendants hold the selat now. A claim of royal descent sits awkwardly beside the question of the next succession.
- **The Lenen-Nezut Ways.** The three royal tombs stand on the desert edge above the town, kept by the Halzi'a's house and not by the crown.
- **A precedent.** The upper river crowned a Gar-Aû in its own city once, and the priesthood has not forgotten that it can.
- **The pride.** The town "remembers" its throne because the house that rules it does.
