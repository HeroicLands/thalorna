"The wells are the forts," a drover of the Marches tells a young hand who has asked why every ruin on the long road sits beside water, "and the forts are whoever holds the wells this year." The Khelâthi who built them called the chain **Khuqet-Zalu**, the southern desert-march, and the name has outlasted every garrison.

## How It Was Made

The sun-house built the chain for one purpose: a road across the dry country from the empire to the eastern temple-cities of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]], along which tribute was to come back ([[lore-templetrib|the Tribute of the Temple-Cities]]). The South Marches have no river, so the army dug and deepened a well at each stage and walled a fort around it, a day's march from the next: mud-brick towers, a cistern, a barracks and a store.

The tribute came east for about a generation. When the sun-house closed in 1822 ST (289 BF) the court at Galezkara called the garrisons home, and the forts were left standing in a country nobody in the valley wished to hold.

## What Is There Now

The mud-brick is broken and the barracks roofs have fallen, but the cisterns were built to hold water and some of them still do. That alone makes the chain valuable. A fort with a standing cistern in the middle of the Marches decides which caravans can cross and which clan's herds can drink, and the strongest of the free companies hold the best of them.

Most towers are reduced to a wall-stump and a stair. The best-preserved forts keep a gatehouse, a courtyard well and a vaulted store at the back, which the companies have fitted with doors of their own. Khelâthi letters cut over the gates give the stage's name and the year of its building in the sacred hand, and they are the only writing in that country.

## Who Goes There

- **The Marches' free companies** use the forts as lairs, and the most feared of them is [[affiliation-jcklsthmrchs|the Jackals of the South Marches]], whose warlord [[being-yasurajckl|Yâsûra]] winters in the stages nearest Bethûa.
- **Caravans** that cannot avoid the Marches pay the company that holds the well, or pay a second company to escort them past.
- **Bethûan patrols** look at the forts from a distance and keep a list of which are held.
- **Scholars** come for the Khelâthi inscriptions, which date the chain more exactly than the king-list does.

## Hooks

- **The unclaimed treasury.** One fort's sealed store still holds the tribute of a generation of Okháric carriers, which nobody ever came east to collect. The company that holds the fort does not know what the vault contains.
- **A well for a job.** The Jackals offer a party the use of a well and its cistern for a season, in exchange for a task the party is not told in advance.
- **The gate inscriptions.** A scholar of Khelunu wants squeezes of the stage-inscriptions, which date the chain to the year, and the company that holds the best-preserved fort wants to be paid for the visit.
