Shelu is the third season of the Khelâthi year, four months of low water in which the river's work gives way to the people's own. The harvest is taken in and the granaries are filled and weighed, and the ground dries hard enough to build on and to march on in the same months. Whatever the empire does beyond its own borders — a campaign, an embassy, a long caravan — it does in Shelu, because the roads the rest of the year makes impassable are, for these four months, the easiest they will ever be.
