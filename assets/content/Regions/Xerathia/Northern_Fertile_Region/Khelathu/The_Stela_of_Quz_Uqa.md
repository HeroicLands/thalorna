A stone stands at the upstream end of [[place-zelhalzi|Zel-Halzi]], the Unmeasured Field, on the west-bank floodplain below [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], at the point where the road leaves the field's edge to bend round it. It is the **Stela of Quz'Uqa**, _Qedlet elu Quz'Uqa_, a _qedlet_ of the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] royal kind: a dated heading, the throne name in its chain of titles, the deeds in the old formulas with the verb first, the plunder counted at a gate, and the enemy set under the king's feet. Quz'Uqa, "strong of the sun-lord," is the name the priesthood of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] gave the chief of the Tha'Ulgau when it crowned him on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] in 1612 ST, the year his horsemen came down out of the western hills and destroyed the last army of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] on this ground ([[lore-thaulgau|the Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]). He ordered the stone in the Season of Harvest of that year, his first, with the dead of the battle still lying on the silt around it, and the scribes of Lenen-Nezut cut it for him in the sacred hand, which he could not read. It belongs to the age the Keepers call [[lore-akhdivriver|the Divided River]], and it is the one monument of the hill house that stands where it was set.

The stone is as tall as a man above the ground and was twice that when it was raised; the flood that covered the dead in the next [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]] has come back twelve hundred times since, and the lower half is under the silt. The scribe who cut it was **Rethur ez Lenen-Nezut**, of the town's bureau. The rendering gives the stone from the top down as it reads now. It marks a space the cutter left empty with a dash, a line the weather or the silt has taken with `[…]`, and the later hands where they stand.

Each [[lore-geletssn|Gelet]], on the night of the rout, a priest of [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]] from Lenen-Nezut stands at the foot of the stone with a lamp and holds the Reading for the unnamed. He reads the columns from the top, the heading, the titles, the deeds and the count, and then the rows: at each row he reads the formula, and at the five rows that carry a name he reads the name. The surveyors of Lenen-Nezut walk every field of the selat that season and do not walk this one; a rod staked on it overnight is found moved in the morning, and they do not stake one twice. The road bends round the field at the stone, and the villages on its edge shut their doors on the field's side after dark. The five names came from [[place-iqu|Iqu]], [[place-gau|Gau]], [[place-tjegu|Tjegu]], [[place-zelqar|Zel-Qar]] and [[place-iqeru|Iqeru]], brought by families who could prove that a man of theirs had marched with the army of Galezkara and had not come home, and each was cut, by a different hand, after a Reading at the stone closed on it; the newest is scratched in [[lore-peopleshand|the People's Hand]], which did not exist when the stone was cut. The Halzi'a house of Tha'Ulgau, the conquerors' kin, settled on the western pastures of [[place-zuqeztunome|Zu-Qeztu]] when the hill house closed ([[lore-hillhouse|the Closing of the Hill House]]), rides in from [[place-zileti|Zileti]] once a year with an offering and no escort and sets it at the edge of the field below the stone. Nobody has dug for the lower half.
