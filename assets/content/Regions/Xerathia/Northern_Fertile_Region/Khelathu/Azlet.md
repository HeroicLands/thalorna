**Azlet** is the **Inundation**, the first of the year's three seasons and the reason the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] have a year at all. For four months the river climbs over the black land until only the old tells and the stone of the temples stand clear of the brown water, and every kind of work that needs dry ground waits for it to go.

"Ask a farmer what he does in Azlet and he will say he waits," a priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] tells a pilgrim at **the Measure**, the flood-gauge on [[place-yathtelgu|the Sacred Island]]. "Ask him what he is owed and you will get a better answer, because that is settled here. Watch where the water stands on the wall at the crest. That mark sets the harvest, and the harvest sets his tax." The priests read it daily as the flood rises, and the reading at the crest is proclaimed to the city and the empire as the most important number of the year.

The waiting is not idle. A drowned field owes labor to the temples and the works, so the hands that plant in [[lore-geletssn|Gelet]] spend Azlet raising monuments, floating stone to the desert edge on flood-barges and rebuilding the mudbrick quarters. The low quarters of the capital empty as the water comes up, their people moving in with kin or into the temple precincts. The weeks of the crest are counted an unhealthy season, because the flood fouls the wells. What the water leaves behind is what the empire eats.
