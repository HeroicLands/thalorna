A herder of [[place-zuqeztunome|Zu-Qeztu]] drives his sheep past a row of low green swellings in the pasture and does not let them graze the tops. "They are not ours," he says, and that is all he says. They are the **Horse Barrows**, and what lies in them is the oldest thing in the western country that is not the grass.

## How They Were Made

The Tha'Ulgau came out of the hills with their horses and buried their dead with them. The first of them to die, the warriors and chiefs who rode down with the nomad chief before his crowning ([[lore-thaulgau|the Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]), were laid in earth mounds in the grazing country of the west, unembalmed and unwrapped, each with the horse or horses that had carried him. That is the hill way. The Khelâthi had no part in it and asked no questions. When the hill house closed, its kin were settled on the same pastures ([[lore-hillhouse|the Closing of the Hill House]]), and the barrows came inside the empire's border with them.

## What Is There

A dozen mounds, perhaps more, in a line along a low rise a day's ride west of [[place-zileti|Zileti]]. They are grassed over and a man's height at the highest, and from the ground they look like ground. The pasture around them is the best in the district, and the herders keep the stock off them.

No ward is cut on any of them. A Khelâthi tomb carries its protection in its glyphs and its stone, and these carry none. Nobody knows what the hill way did in place of a seal.

## How the Khelâthi Treat Them

As unclean. A body with no embalmer, no tomb and no name read aloud has no continuation by Khelâthi doctrine, and the barrows hold a good many. The villages do not cross them, and the priests of [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]] do not bless them. They are not a blood-field, because the dead in them were buried by their own people and not left on the ground. The Khelâthi keep away from them because the dead are foreign and unwrapped.

The house of **Tha'Ulgau** does not leave them alone. Its Halzi'a rides out once a year with a few retainers and no priest, and the herders find the grass at the foot of the nearest mound pressed down in the morning.

## Who Goes

- **The Tha'Ulgau house**, in secret, because the priesthood does not know what the house still visits.
- **Robbers**, who find a rich grave unprotected in the Khelâthi way and do not ask what protects it instead.
- **Scholars of the conquest**, who want to know whether the first Gar-Aû of the hill house lies here or in the necropolis, and what his people buried with him.
- **Herders**, who keep their stock off the tops and say nothing.

## Hooks

- **The opened mound.** Robbers dig into a barrow and let out something the hill-people bound with a horse instead of a seal. The herders see it first, and the Halzi'a's house wants the robbers found before the priesthood learns what its lord visits.
- **The empty barrow.** A scholar from [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]] opens the end mound with the Halzi'a's permission and finds a grave with the horse and no man in it.
- **The pressed grass.** A new herder follows the Halzi'a out one morning and sees what the house leaves at the foot of the mound.
