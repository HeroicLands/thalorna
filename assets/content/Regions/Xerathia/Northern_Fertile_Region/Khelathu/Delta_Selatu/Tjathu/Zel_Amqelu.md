"That pylon was the temple door," a salt-cutter of [[place-tjathu|Tjathu]] says, pointing across the flat at a stump of stone in the reeds, "and everything else you can see is the town, under the mud." The town was **Zel-Amqelu**, and it has not had a roof since the day the sea-raiders landed.

## How It Was Made

Zel-Amqelu was a trading port on one of the silt-islands where the river's mouths spread into the sea. In 1960 ST (151 BF) the raiders of [[lore-searaiders|the Sea-Raiders' Throne]] landed within sight of it and burned it the same day. The port was not rebuilt. Its channel shifted soon after, the island silted to the mainland side, and the trade that had used its quays went to the ports whose water stayed deep.

The place is not a blood-field. The town burned and its dead were burned with it, and the raiders buried their own. Its dead were received, and the town's rites were said for them.

## What Is There Now

A broad reed-grown mound stands in the flats of the [[place-tjathuselat|Selat of Tjathu]]. The burned temple's pylon still stands out of the mound, with the lower courses of the temple walls round it, and the old quay line shows as a pale seam in the reeds at low water. Everything else is silt over ash.

A smugglers' landing lies behind the mound, where a creek still reaches the water. Salt-cutters work the pans on its edges. A customs patrol of the weigh-houses ([[place-amqelmiglet|Amqel-Miglet]]) calls at the landing at irregular intervals and always finds nothing, which is why the smugglers keep using it.

## What the Temple Court Holds

The raiders buried their own dead in the temple court, and they did it in a manner no Khelâthi recognizes. The graves are shallow, aligned to something other than the river, and furnished with goods of a make the delta's craftsmen cannot place. Nobody has dug them since the Halzi'a's officials closed the court and left the pylon standing as a mark.

## Who Goes There

- **Smugglers** use the landing behind the mound, and know every channel the patrol does not.
- **Salt-cutters** work the pans along its edge and guard the paths to them.
- **The customs patrol** of the weigh-houses, which would like the landing closed without a fight.
- **Scholars** who want the raiders' origin, which the Khelâthi record does not hold, and who have come to dig in the temple court.

## Hooks

- **The graves.** A scholar offers to pay a party to guard a quiet dig in the temple court. The goods in the graves would name the raiders' country, and the Halzi'a's officials do not want them named.
- **The ring at the landing.** A smuggling ring uses the ruin as a store, and the customs-house wants it closed without bloodshed. The ring's leader is a salt-cutter with a cousin in the weigh-house.
- **The pylon.** Someone has been cutting new marks into the pylon's lower courses at night, in a hand that is not Khelâthi.
