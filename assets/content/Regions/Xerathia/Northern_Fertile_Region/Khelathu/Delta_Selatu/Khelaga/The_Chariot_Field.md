"We do not moor on that bank," a boatman at the water-stair of [[place-khelaga|Khelaga]] tells a passenger, and says no more until the town is behind them. The bank is the western shore of the western channel, a short way below the apex where the river first divides, and the ground behind it is the **Chariot Field**.

It is a _zelalgit_ (a thin blood-field, "the Devourer's place"), the third in Khelâthi memory and the only one of the three that kills. The Khelâthi call the ground made by a battle left unread a _zelqezat_, and a thin one takes the Devourer's name because the things that come out of it hunt the living as they hunt the dead.

## How It Was Made

In 2487 ST (377 AF) the chariot corps met a Vylarian army on the flat ground below the town ([[lore-vylconquest|the Vylarian Conquest]]). The corps broke against the legions' line and its dead lay where they fell, the sons of the military aristocracy with their commander and their king, because the army held the field and the families were not let through to read the names. The Vylarians buried their own in a pit beside them without the rite that places a Vylarian in the keeping of Sélmoros. Both sets of dead went unreceived.

## What Is There

The field is a **recurrence**, and its kind is on the water. The ground where the corps stood is reed and mud-bank now, and nobody farms it. At dusk on the anniversary the western channel below the field runs, for an hour, a little apart from itself. Boatmen see a second bank, lit as if by lamps, and a line of shapes standing on it. A boat that follows it comes out of the reeds at dawn in the same reach it left, and never in any other.

The field is **thin**. On the anniversary and on any night when the water stands, hunters cross into the world in a semi-material form, and they act on it. They are seen close to, they take hold, and they kill; a body found at the edge in the morning is something the town has seen. Nothing living crosses into the field bodily, however it is led.

The chariot families keep a vigil of lamps on the water each year, set afloat at dusk along the western channel for their dead. The Vylarian pit lies on the same bank, under a mound the reeds have taken. Veterans of the corps take [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]]'s purification for the battle's stain before they marry.

A Reading held at the edge for the unnamed quiets the field for the night it is held. Whether the Vylarian dead can be quieted by a rite of their own, with the Khelâthi dead left as they are, is a question the court has never been asked in earnest.

## Who Goes

- **The chariot families,** with their lamps, once a year.
- **Vylarian envoys,** asking leave to visit their own dead, a request the court treats as a lever.
- **Necromancers,** who find a thin field of unreceived soldiers an attraction.
- **Boat-crews** who misjudge the hour and moor on the wrong bank.

## Hooks

- **The Vylarian request.** An embassy wants its dead raised for an honorable burial, and the court wants the price to be Bethûa. The priests of Sélmoros who would place the dead in the god's keeping would quiet one bank, and not the other.
- **The second bank.** A chariot heir followed the second bank and came back at dawn in the same reach, changed, and will not say who stood on it.
- **The lamp-lit shapes.** A family of the corps swears it saw its son's name written on the water, a name the Reading never entered.
