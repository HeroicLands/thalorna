West of [[place-zumeleshrvr|The Zumelesh]], where the black land stops and the cliffs of the high desert stand up out of the sand, a canal runs straight across the margin into a bay of the rock. At its end stands **Lut-Ithnet**, the western estate of the house of [Zu'Lemetu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}: a walled house of whitewashed brick, a bronze water-gate on a stone basin, and behind it a row of tomb doors cut into the cliff face. Boatmen on the river call it the **House of Sealed Mouths**, and they say it under their breath.

## What It Is

Lut-Ithnet is a funerary estate, the kind of house a great family endows so that its dead are prepared, laid in their own ground and fed for as long as the endowment lasts. Zu'Lemetu built it when the house was rich. The dead came upriver by boat to the basin, were carried through the water-gate into the preparation hall and spent their seventy days there in washing-salt and linen, and were walled into the cliff galleries with their tablets beside them. A household of embalmers, bondsmen and offering-priests lived in the house to do the work, and the estate's fields at the canal's edge fed them and the offering tables both.

The house dwindled, as houses do. The last _Zabet'lutu_ of the line, [Zabegulu]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, died at the estate, and her heirs live downriver and have never seen it. Since then the estate has answered to its steward, the _lemzabu_ [Gezuhati]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, who has served it since before the old mistress was born and keeps its accounts in a careful hand.

## What Lies There

- **The basin and the water-gate.** A stone basin at the canal's end, a stair down to it, and a bronze gate that opens on the preparation hall. The basin is dry to the stones. Drain mouths open in the basin wall, each big enough for a crawling man, with a dark crust on their lips.

:::secret {title="The drains"}
The drains run from the gutters of the preparation hall, under the basin, to the canal. They are tight crawl-ways, and they are the one way into the hall that does not pass through the house. The crust on their lips is old resin and natron washed down from the tables.
:::

- **The gatehouse.** The steward receives visitors at the bronze gate in the house wall, standing in the shade of the gatehouse, and a tablet waits on a stand there for the receipt the house asks of anyone who brings it business.

:::secret {title="The receipt at the gate"}
The receipt is one short line in the people's hand beneath a block in the sacred hand. The line reads as an acknowledgment, and it is a witness's attestation. The block above it is a release of the steward in the old mistress's name, so a visitor who reads only the people's hand and signs has witnessed a release he never saw made.

> [!TIP]
> Present the receipt as the dullest thing at the gate. The steward holds the tablet out with the reed pen already inked, and it costs him nothing if a character who reads the sacred hand looks closer: he says the receipt goes beneath the old mistress's last account, as the house closes its affairs, which is true.

:::

- **The upper house.** Guest rooms over the hall, the steward's office, the record room with its shelves of tablets in clay jars, the kitchen, the servants' quarters and an offering chapel facing the cliff. Along the passage from the record room is a door with a new bronze lock, on the room where the dead of the house once waited for the hall below.
- **The preparation hall.** A long, low hall below the house, cut back into the rock, with natron beds, embalming tables, drains that run out under the basin to the canal, and bronze partitions on tracks that close the hall into bays. A light-shaft opens to the sky above the main table. Its bronze shutter is new.

:::secret {title="The light-shaft"}
The shutter is bolted from below. Between mid-morning and mid-afternoon, opened, it drops a shaft of sun across the main table, and the steward cannot cross it without great cost. Out of those hours it shows only stars.
:::

- **The cliff galleries.** The house's dead in their niches, row above row, with the endowment's offering table at the foot of each gallery. The offering tables are bare and dusty.
- **The vault.** A sealed room in the rock behind the hall, under the seal of Zu'Lemetu, where the house keeps what the endowment owns.

## What Moves There Now

The river hears that the estate is in mourning and that its servants have taken a vow of silence for the old mistress, and sewed their own lips at her burial, as the old families are said to do. Boatmen who put in at the basin in the last few years say that the servants who came down to the stair did not open their mouths, and that their lips were stitched. Nobody who said so went back to look.

The steward sent a man downriver for a relief boat when the water dropped, by his own account. No relief boat has come up the canal.

## Who Knows of It, and What They Say

- **River boatmen** know the canal and know the estate is a bad place to put in. The canal has failed this season, and they are not sorry.
- **The temple scribes of the [[affiliation-wethur|Faith of Weth'ur]]** hold copies of the house's older burial records and have had no new tablets from the estate in years. They want the originals before the heirs go to law.
- **The heirs of Zu'Lemetu**, a cousin line in a river town, have never visited. They have a claim on the estate and no proof of it, because the proof is in the record room.
- **The herders of the western margin** graze nowhere near the cliff bay. They say the estate's lamps burn all night, every night.

## Reaching It

The canal leaves the river by a head-gate and runs most of a day by boat across the margin to the basin. In flood it carries barges; at low water it carries a reed skiff with care. This season the canal has failed, and its water is going into the mud. The head-gate at the river is closed, and the mud packed against it carries the estate's seal. A traveler on foot follows the canal bank across open desert, with no shade and no water between the river and the house.

:::secret {title="The head-gate"}
The steward had the head-gate closed and sealed. The canal failed because he wanted it to: nobody leaves the estate by water, and nobody comes. The estate's seal-cord, on the steward's belt, breaks the seal, and water comes down the channel in a day.
:::

# What Is Really There {#truth .secret}

**The steward.** Gezuhati kept the endowment for forty years and spent it, and let the offerings to the house's dead lapse one by one, which in the valley is a default against creditors who cannot chase it. Every dead member of Zu'Lemetu is his creditor. When a physician told him he was dying, the old mistress entered the finding in her own hand, and he saw what his Reading would hear: any one of the dead, or any servant who had seen the books, could stand and say no. He did not die. The estate's embalmer, [Lizulu]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, a Lekhau of [[affiliation-garithnetau|Gar-Ithnetáu]], prepared him through the rite that makes a [[being-tereb|tereb]], and he went into it willing. He keeps to shade, does not eat, and feeds by drawing out the life of the living.

**The household.** Then he silenced the creditors. Zabegulu was the first. Lizulu was the last who could have spoken for the house. One by one the household died in the preparation hall and rose again as [[being-damut|damutu]], and each had its lips sewn shut with resin-soaked cord before it rose. The stitches are the working: Lizulu bound the dead to answer their master _while the mouth is closed_, and the words are cut into the edge of the main table in the sacred hand. Cut a dead mouth open and the binding fails. The dead one screams with no breath behind it, says the refusal from the litany of the Reading, _no_, and falls. Seven of the dead attend the steward in the upper house, clean and dressed, with linen wrapping under their tunics. The rest lie bound on the shelves and beds of the hall, every mouth sewn, Zabegulu on the main table with her eyes open, and Lizulu on a shelf near the light-shaft, never woken.

**The records.** In the record room Gezuhati enters each death in the sacred hand and, beside it, a release in the people's hand: _I require no more of the steward._ A release closes an entry only when it is declared before witnesses, and every witness line beneath the household's releases stands empty, because a witness must be a living person and the steward has killed everyone with a tie to the house. The last release in the newest jar is for the man he says he sent downriver, and that man's death is entered a month before the day the steward names for his setting out. An earlier tablet in the old mistress's hand records the physician's finding that the steward was dying, and no death follows it.

**Who the visitors are for.** Strangers with no tie to the house are the proper witnesses to a release. Gezuhati needs living names on the witness lines, and once he has them every account the household holds against him closes. He takes names wherever he can get them: from the receipt at the gate, from a request to witness set out with a reed pen, and from any letter a visitor carries that names its bearers. A temple letter serves best, because a temple's agents witness as the temple. A name copied from a letter onto a witness line is a false entry, and in the valley a false entry damns its witness along with its maker. A witness who signed and a witness whose name was copied stand alike: each is on the releases, and each needs to disown the witnessing to be clear of it.

**Why the mouths come last.** A witness who can still speak can unsay. A release whose witness declares it false is no release, and the declaration counts where the parties to the entry can hear it, which means aloud in the preparation hall, before the dead of the house. So the steward keeps his witnesses alive and comfortable for as long as he can feed on them, and closes their mouths only at the end, with the same cord and needle as the household's, so that what rises can never disown anything. A witness who walks away instead leaves the releases standing: every entry against the steward closed, and a false entry in the witness's own account at the Weighing.

**The ground.** The preparation hall is his Hallowed Ground. At its far end is a stone niche, freshly cut, with a lamp in it and his own name above it, and he lies there between mid-morning and mid-afternoon, slow and dulled by the hour. Destroyed anywhere else, he gathers again in the niche after a season or a year; destroyed in the hall, he is ended, and the risen dead fall where they stand. In the gatehouse wall, behind a false offering niche, is a provision store kept full of water and dried food for the household's living, which is to say for his own use of them. The vault behind the hall holds the burial goods held back for heirs and the true accounts of the endowment in Zabegulu's hand, which identify the rightful heirs and show what the steward spent.

**The living.** Two of the household are alive. [Thakete]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a young bondswoman, hides among the wrapped bodies on the shelves of the locked room off the record-room passage, unsewn, gray-faced and thin, fed on until she tires crossing a room. Her brother [Wenlegir]{.name data-type="being/npc"} lies in the salt of the hall, alive, and the records open an entry in his name with its closing day already set.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The vow of silence.** False. Nobody in the valley sews their own lips in mourning; the stitches are the binding, and they went in before each servant rose.
- **The relief boat.** There is none. The man the steward names as its messenger is dead and sewn.
- **The failing canal.** The steward's work, at the head-gate.
- **The lamps that burn all night.** True. The dead stand at the foot of the stair all night with their faces to the cliff, as if listening for something in the galleries, and the lamps burn whether anyone fills them or not. Lizulu's working took hold more firmly than the rite allows, and nobody knows why.

> [!WARNING]
> Never let the steward lie once he has dropped his courtesy at night. He tells a direct question the truth, because by then he believes nothing the visitors know can change his accounts. The only thing he will not volunteer is the rule that a witness can disown in the hall.
