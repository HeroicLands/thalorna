West of the [[place-zumeleshrvr|Zumélesh]], where the black land stops and the cliffs of the high desert stand up out of the sand, a canal runs straight across the margin into a bay of the rock. At its end stands **Lut-Ithnet**, the western estate of the house of **Zu'Lemetu**: a walled house of whitewashed brick, a bronze water-gate on a stone basin, and behind it a row of tomb doors cut into the cliff face. Boatmen on the river call it the **House of Sealed Mouths**, and they say it under their breath.

## What It Is

Lut-Ithnet is a funerary estate, the kind of house a great family endows so that its dead are prepared, laid in their own ground and fed for as long as the endowment lasts. Zu'Lemetu built it when the house was rich. The dead came upriver by boat to the basin, were carried through the water-gate into the preparation hall and spent their seventy days there in washing-salt and linen, and were walled into the cliff galleries with their tablets beside them. A household of embalmers, bondsmen and offering-priests lived in the house to do the work, and the estate's fields at the canal's edge fed them and the offering tables both.

The house dwindled, as houses do. The last _Zabet'lutu_ of the line, **Zabegulu**, died at the estate, and her heirs live downriver and have never seen it. Since then the estate has answered to its steward, the _lemzabu_ **Gezuhatî**, who has served it since before the old mistress was born and keeps its accounts in a careful hand.

## What Lies There

- **The basin and the water-gate.** A stone basin at the canal's end, a stair down to it, and a bronze gate that opens on the preparation hall. The basin is dry to the stones.
- **The upper house.** Guest rooms, the steward's office, the record room with its shelves of tablets in clay jars, the kitchen, the servants' quarters and an offering chapel facing the cliff.
- **The preparation hall.** A long, low hall below the house, cut back into the rock, with natron beds, embalming tables, drains that run out under the basin to the canal, and bronze partitions that close the hall into bays. A light-shaft, shuttered in bronze, opens to the sky above the main table.
- **The cliff galleries.** The house's dead in their niches, row above row, with the endowment's offering table at the foot of each gallery.
- **The vault.** A sealed room in the rock behind the hall, where the house keeps what the endowment owns.

## What Moves There Now

The river hears that the estate is in mourning and that its servants have taken a vow of silence for the old mistress. Boatmen who put in at the basin in the last few years say that the servants who came down to the stair did not open their mouths, and that their lips were stitched. Nobody who said so went back to look.

The truth is the steward. Gezuhatî kept the endowment for forty years and spent it, and let the offerings to the house's dead lapse. Every dead member of Zu'Lemetu is his creditor, and every servant who saw the accounts is a witness. When a physician told him he was dying, he knew what his Reading would hear. He did not die. He is a [[being-tereb|tereb]] now, and the estate is his Hallowed Ground. His household are [[being-damut|damutu]], bound to him through the stitches in their mouths, and the burial records in his office show each of them releasing him from everything he owed.

## Who Knows of It, and What They Say

- **River boatmen** know the canal and know the estate is a bad place to put in. The canal has failed this season, and they are not sorry.
- **The temple scribes of [[lore-wethurdty|Weth'ûr]]** hold copies of the house's older burial records and have had no new tablets from the estate in years. They want the originals before the heirs go to law.
- **The heirs of Zu'Lemetu**, a cousin line in a river town, have never visited. They have a claim on the estate and no proof of it, because the proof is in the record room.
- **The herders of the western margin** graze nowhere near the cliff bay. They say the estate's lamps burn all night, every night.

## Reaching It

The canal leaves the river by a head-gate and runs most of a day by boat across the margin to the basin. In flood it carries barges; at low water it carries a reed skiff with care. This season the canal has failed, and its water is going into the mud. A traveler on foot follows the canal bank across open desert, with no shade and no water between the river and the house.
