# terran_analog: "The Book of the Dead and the Coffin Texts in a cheap late copy: a royal text with the owner's name written in, gatekeepers who must be named, the figure-spell, the water-spell, the sun's night passage on foot. Departs: the spells stop at the hall door, the account goes down with the body, and the price is on the roll."
---

The burial clubs of the delta buy a roll of road-spells for each member they bury, and the club's reader says it over the body before the wrapping is tied, spell by spell in the order the road keeps, from the waking in the dark to the door of the hall where the accounts are opened. [[affiliation-garithnetau|Gar-Ithnetáu]], the House of the Westward Road, holds the spells and sells the copies, and the price is set by what is copied in. This roll was copied in the twelfth year of [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]], in the age of [[lore-akhhsmeqes|the House of Meqes]], for **Lemat ez Khelaga**, a fowler's widow of the water-stair at [[place-khelaga|Khelaga]], and **the fowlers' club of the water-stair** paid for it. The House says the old lines in it are from the tablets of the Great Land, the oldest writing in the valley, which the temple of [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]] at [[place-tjegu|Tjegu]] keeps above [[place-firsttablets|the Ground of the First Tablets]] at [[place-zugernome|Zu-Ger]]; the colophon says the same and names no king. The schools read the core as the work of [[lore-akhfirsttab|the First Tablets]], the age that fixed the sacred hand ([[lore-sacredhand|the Fixing of the Sacred Hand]]), and this copy carries it in the people's hand, so that a club's reader can read it.

The copyist wrote Lemat's name wherever the old text said _the Gar-Aû_, and once did not. The rubrics, which stand in red on the roll, say what each spell is for, when it is said and over what. The keepers' names at the gates are left blank, because the club did not pay for them, and the roll ends at the door, because what is said inside is not written. The road it follows is the night half of the sun's circuit ([[place-zulaten|the Night Road]]), behind [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] and on foot, and the one the god fights there is not named on the roll. The rendering keeps the roll's order, its blanks, its silence and its one slip.

: The Head of the Roll {#head}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The spells of the westward road, as they stand on the tablets of the Great Land.
For Lemat ez Khelaga, who has gone to the West.
They are said over her in the order the road keeps: the waking first, the door last.
None is left out. None is said twice.
What the road asks, the roll answers. What the roll does not answer, the road does not ask.
```

Said over the body after the wash and before the wrapping is tied, not after. This is the third course, a wash, a wrapping and a prayer, and the prayer is this roll. The reader stands at the head and not at the feet. He says her name as the temple holds it, the whole of it, as often as the roll gives it; a name that stops short is not said over the dead, and nothing that stops short is written here.

: The Waking {#waking}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
It is dark, and I am awake.
I am awake in the dark, and I know my name.
My name is Lemat ez Khelaga.
A priest gave it me at the water-stair. The temple holds it. It was read at my Reading.
I am entered. I am not struck.
Whoever calls Lemat ez Khelaga, I answer.
Whoever calls another name, I do not turn my head.
The dark calls no name. The dark has no name to call with.
I know my name in the dark, and the dark does not know it.
I have my two hands, and they are mine. I have my two feet, and they are mine.
I have my eyes. I have my ears. I have my mouth, and my name is in it.
My _reth_ is set down. My _qelt_ is wrapped. Both were seen to; both were paid for.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga. I have said it. I say it again.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga.
I get up.
```

Said as the last knot is tied, and not before; a knot that is not yet tied is not a knot. The reader puts his finger on the knot while he says it.

: The Linen {#linen}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
This linen is mine. It was bought for me, and the buying is written.
It is not the first course, and it is not the second. It is the third, and it is paid.
It goes round me once. It goes round me twice. The knot is at the breast, over the copper.
The knot is tied. I did not tie it, and I do not untie it.
Nobody takes it off me on the road. The road does not ask for it.
I am wrapped. I was not left unwrapped. The club saw to it, and the club is paid up.
A member of the club goes to the West wrapped, and I am a member, and I paid my share every month, in the bad year too.
This linen is mine.
```

Said with the reader's hand flat on the roll. It is said once, and the reader reads her name off the roll as he says it, and does not say it from memory.

: The Name on the Roll {#name}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
This roll is addressed to me.
The name on it is my name. It is Lemat ez Khelaga.
A roll with another name on it would carry another, and I would walk and arrive nowhere.
The name on it is mine. It was looked at. It is right.
Wherever the roll says a name, it says mine.
Wherever it says _I_, I am the one who says it.
The road reads the name and not the face. The name is right.
I go where the roll goes. The roll goes where my name goes.
```

Said at the mouth of the pit, or at the door of the chamber if the club has bought one, before the block is set. The reader faces west. The household stands behind him and says nothing; a word from the household here is a call, and she will turn for it.

: The Going Out {#goingout}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The block is set on me, and I go out by it.
I go out by the door as the god goes out by the west.
I go out westward. The valley is behind me. The river is behind me.
The ones who wrapped me are behind me. They have been paid.
The ones who wept are behind me. They will eat tonight, in the hall, on the club's bread.
I do not look back for them. The road is in front.
The road is the god's road. It is the night half of it.
I walk the god's road, and the god is not beside me.
He is ahead. I see where he has been.
Where the god has been, the ground is a road.
Where the god has not been, there is no ground.
I keep to where the god has been.
I do not run. The road is long, and the god does not run on it.
The block is set. I go out. I go out.
```

Four gates stand on the road, and each has a keeper, and each keeper has a name. The names are not copied for one who did not pay for them. The club's reader says the spell as far as the blank and stops, and a Lekhau of the House says the name into it; if no Lekhau has been hired, the blank stands, and the dead supplies it herself or does not. The reader does not guess. A wrong name opens nothing, and the keeper hears it.

Said over the figures, of whatever stuff they are made. The reader holds each one up as he speaks to it and sets it down at her feet when he has done. If the club has paid for one figure only, the first part is said and the second is left out; a figure that is not there is not spoken to, and a figure spoken to that is not there answers from somewhere else.

: The Two That Answer {#zaglu}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
You, the first _zaglu_. You are mine.
You were bought for me, and you were paid for, and the paying is written.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga, and you are mine.
When the dead are called to the canals in the West,
when they are called to carry silt, to cut the reed, to raise the dyke, to bail the field,
when my name is called for the work, you answer.
You say, _Here. I am hers. I do her share._
You carry the basket. You carry it on the left shoulder and then on the right.
You do not put it down until it is counted.
When I am called, you go. When you go, I stay.

You, the second _zaglu_. You are mine.
You were bought for me, and you were paid for, and the paying is written.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga, and you are mine.
When the dead are called to the canals in the West,
when they are called to the net, to the reed-boat, to the long pole in the mud,
when my name is called for the work, you answer.
You say, _Here. I am hers. I do her share._
You stand in the water to the knee. You stand in it to the waist.
You do not come out until the count is made.
When I am called, you go. When you go, I stay.

Two of you, and one of me.
Two shares you do, and no share I do.
You are made, and what is made does not tire, and does not ask for beer.
You do not answer for another. If another calls you, you are deaf.
You do not answer in my voice. You answer in your own, and you say whose you are.
You owe me the work, and you will pay it, and I am owed by you.
```

Said with the reader's right hand wet. The jar stands at her right side; if the club bought no jar, the hand is wet all the same, and the words are said over the hand. Water is not an entry, and the spell asks nobody for it.

: Water on the Road {#water}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The road is dry. The road is the dry land, and no flood has ever come up it.
There is a river below as there is a river above.
It goes and it comes, below as above.
I come down to it. I go into it to the ankle.
I drank from the river above at the water-stair, and I paid nothing, and nobody asked me.
I drink from the river below, and I pay nothing, and nobody asks me.
Water is not entered. Water is owed to no one. Water is not on the tablets.
The crocodile lies on the bank below as he lies on the bank above.
I pass him as I passed him above: upwind of him, and quiet, and never between him and the water.
He does not lift his head for me. He has his own affairs, and they are not mine.
I drink. I fill the jar. I go on.
The jar is at my right side. It is full. It stays full.
I do not drink it dry on the first stretch. The road is long, and I know it is long.
```

Said facing west, with the reader's arm out along the road. It is said once. A reader who says it twice has told her to turn round.

: Not Losing the Way {#way}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The road is one road. The day is half of it. The night is half of it.
I am on the night half, and I do not look for the day half. It is the same road.
I do not turn to the left. There is no ground on the left.
I do not turn to the right. There is no ground on the right.
I do not turn round. Behind me the gates are shut.
The hawk stands at the turning. He does not take me. He shows me the road.
The road goes down. Then it goes along. Then it goes up.
I know the three. I have said them. Down, along, up.
Where the god has been, the ground is a road. I keep to it.
A voice from the left is not a voice. A voice from the right is not a voice.
A voice from behind is a voice, and I do not answer it, because I have gone out.
I do not lose the way. The way is the god's, and he has never lost it.
```

Said with the reader's back to the household, and the household hears it. It is the last spell in which her name is said toward the household; after it the reader says her name toward the west only.

: That She Not Turn {#turn}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
If my name is called from behind me, it is my house that calls it.
They have my name because I gave it them, to say at the shrine, on the days, with the beer poured.
I did not give it them to say on the road.
A name said on the road is a call, and the dead turn for a call, and the one who turns has left the track.
I do not turn. I have gone out. The block is set.
Let them say it at the shrine. I hear it there. It is written on my side there.
Let them not say it here.
If they say it here, they say it to my back, and my back does not answer.
I walk on. My name is in front of me, on the roll, and I follow it.
```

Said at an even pace, with nothing done. The reader does not shorten it, and the household does not leave to see to the bread.

: The Going Along {#along}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The road goes along.
It went down, and now it goes along, and it goes along for a long time.
Nothing is on the left. Nothing is on the right. The track is in front, and it is warm.
I walk. The dead of my village walk. The dead of the next village walk.
The dead of the upper selatu walk, and the dead of the delta walk, and they do not speak to each other.
A scribe walks with his reed behind his ear, and he does not write with it here.
A tiller walks with nothing. A Halzi'a walks with his titles, and nobody says them.
There is no sun. There is where the god has been.
The jar is at my right side. I do not drink.
My copper is on my breast. I do not spend.
My tablets are under my right hand. I do not read them. They were read.
I count my steps, and I lose the count, and I begin again, and that is the going along.
The road goes along. I go along it. Nothing is asked of me here.
Nothing is asked of me here, and I ask nothing.
The road goes along.
```

Said over the piece on her breast. If the household put in two, it is said twice, and the second time the reader says _the second_ where the roll says _my copper_. A piece that was clipped is not put in; the reader tries it on the stone before he begins.

: The Copper on the Breast {#copper}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
This is my copper. It is on my breast. It is one _qelu_ by the temple's weight.
It is weighed. It is not clipped. It was tried on the stone, and it rang.
With it I buy my first bread in the West.
I do not beg my first bread. I buy it, and I am given my change, and I count it.
I buy bread, and I buy beer, and I sit down, because I have walked.
After the first bread I work, and the two that answer work for me, and I eat what is mine.
What I eat after the first bread, I have earned, and nobody gave it me.
This is my copper. Let no hand lift it. A hand that lifts it makes me poor.
It is small. It is enough. I am Lemat ez Khelaga, and it is mine.
```

Said over the tablets of her account, which go into the pit under her right hand. The reader does not read the entries here. They were read at the Reading, and nothing is read twice.

: The Account That Goes Down {#account}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
These are my tablets. They were read. The household heard them, and the creditors, and the scribe.
What I owed is closed. It was closed before I was wrapped, and the closing is written.
What I am owed is open, and it goes down with me, and it goes on paying.
The bread I gave in the bad year is written. Let it be found.
The net I lent and was not given back is written. Let it be found.
The kindness that was not returned is written, and I did not call it in. Let it come to me in the West.
Gold runs out in the West. The tablets go on paying.
Let no hand lift them. A hand that lifts the tablets unmakes me.
They are under my right hand, and I keep them there.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga. I closed well. I go down owed.
```

Said at the mouth of the pit when the body is in it and before it is filled. The reader says the names the household gives him, and no other; a name the household does not give is not said, however well the reader knew the man.

: The Dead of the House {#house}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The dead of my house are in the West before me.
They know my name. They heard it at the shrine, on the days, with the beer poured.
I said their names on the days. I poured. I did not let it lapse.
What I poured is written on their side, and they are glad of it, and they owe me for it.
Let them come to the door and know my face.
Let the one who went before me come to the door.
    _\[Here the reader says the names the household gives him.\]_
I said your names. You hear them. I am Lemat ez Khelaga, of your house.
Come to the door. Know my face. Say my name back to me.
```

Said when the filling begins. The reader names the year as the temple names it, and the dead of the year are addressed together, and none by name.

: The Dead of the Year {#year}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
I go down with the dead of my year, and we go down together.
The old who went down in their beds, with the household round them.
The child who went down before the naming, with nothing to carry.
The man the crocodile took at the reed-heads, who was not found, and was entered anyway.
The woman who went down in childbed, and the child with her, two in one pit.
The one who went down owed, and the one who went down square, and the one who went down short.
The one who was entered by his house, and the one who was entered by his club, and the one the temple entered because nobody else would.
The boatman who went down at the bar, and the brewer who went down in her yard, and the bondsman who went down on the estate and was entered under its name.
We go down together. We do not ask each other how we stand.
How I stand is on my tablets, and the tablets are under my hand.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga, of the year that is named over me, and I go down with my year.
```

Said while the pit is filled, until the filling is done. If the filling is done before the spell, the reader finishes the spell; if the spell is done before the filling, the reader waits, and says nothing, and does not begin the old lines until the last basket is in.

: At the Foot of the Stair {#stair}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
We stand at the foot of the stair, and we wait for the god.
Above us it is still day. We hear nothing of the day. The day is not ours.
We wait as the household waits at the embalmers' door, and nobody comes out to tell us.
The stair is dark. Then the top of the stair is warm.
Then the top of the stair is light, and the light comes down one step.
It comes down one step, and another, and the stair is a road.
Nobody on the stair speaks. The ones who are not entered are not on the stair, and there is nobody to speak to.
I stand with the dead of my year. My jar is full. My copper is on my breast. My tablets are under my hand.
The light is on the step above me. It is on my step. It goes past.
I have waited. The waiting is done. I fall in.
```

The old lines begin here. They are said as they stand, and nothing is put in and nothing is taken out, and the reader does not explain them. The god's name is said with its pause in it. A reader who runs it together has addressed nobody, and the dead has fallen in behind nobody.

: Falling In Behind the God {#fallingin}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
I am the one who stood upon the mound.
I am the one who stood upon the mound, and the valley was many, and it was one.
My deeds are the god's.
The god goes down in the west. He goes down on foot.
He has no boat. He has his fire, and his fire is himself.
He goes down as a man goes down the water-stair, one foot and the other foot.
The dead who are entered stand at the foot of the stair.
The dead who are not entered are not there. They are not called. They are nowhere.
He passes. He does not look at us. He has the night to cross.
Lemat ez Khelaga falls in behind him.
Lemat ez Khelaga falls in behind him with the dead of her village and the dead of her year.
We walk in his track. His track is warm. The ground on either side is not ground.
We do not see him. We see where he has been.
I say his name. I say Uqa'â. I keep the stop in it.
The stop is his. I do not take it from him. I did not take it from him in the valley.
Lemat ez Khelaga is behind the god, and the god is before.
I am the one who stood upon the mound.
```

This is the middle of the night, and it is the middle of the roll. The reader does not hurry it. The name of the one the god fights is not written here, and it is not to be supplied, by anyone, for any price.

: On the Bank {#bank}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
We come to the water in the middle of the night.
The god goes down to the water, and the dead stop on the bank.
The dead stop on the bank. They do not go into the water. The water is not theirs tonight.
He is in the water. He is in it to the waist. His fire is on the water.
The long one lifts his head.
He lifts his head out of the water, and his coils are from bank to bank.
He opens his mouth. His mouth is the dark. The dark is his mouth.
He would swallow the god, and the morning in the god, and the road, and us on the bank.
I do not say his name. It is not written here.
I say the god's name. I say Uqa'â. I keep the stop in it.
On the bank the dead say the god's name, and they stop in the middle of it, all of them.
The bank is quiet in the stop. The long one hears the quiet, and it is not for him.
The god strikes. He strikes with the fire that is himself.
He strikes, and the long one is thrown down.
He strikes, and the long one is thrown down, and the water closes over him.
He is thrown down. He is not destroyed. He will lift his head tomorrow.
Tomorrow is the god's affair. Tonight he is thrown down.
I am on the bank. I am not in his mouth.
I am not swallowed with the dark.
I am not swallowed with the dark.
The Gar-Aû stood on the bank and was not swallowed.
The Gar-Aû saw the god strike, and the Gar-Aû said the god's name with the stop in it.
The fire of the god was in the Gar-Aû. It went down with the god, and it came up with him.
The Gar-Aû goes on behind the god, and the dead of his valley go on behind the Gar-Aû.
    _\[In another hand: say it as it stands.\]_
I am not swallowed with the dark.
The god comes up out of the water. He goes on. We fall in behind him.
I am the one who stood upon the mound.
I am not swallowed with the dark.
```

Said with the reader standing, and the household may stand now. The reader keeps his arm along the road until the last line, and lets it fall on the last line.

: The Road Goes Up {#up}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
The road goes up. I said it would. Down, along, up.
The track is warm, and it goes up.
Behind the god the dead go up, and I go up with them, Lemat ez Khelaga with the dead of her year.
The ground on the right is ground now. The ground on the left is ground now.
There is a smell of silt. There is the sound of a dyke being cut, and a count being called.
The two that answer have gone ahead to the count. I hear my name called, and I hear _Here_.
There is bread somewhere. I have the copper for it.
There is a door.
It is the door of the hall where the accounts are opened.
The god does not stop at it. He has the morning to make. He goes on along the road, and the road goes on with him.
The dead turn in at the door, and I turn in with them.
I stand at the door. My tablets are under my hand. My copper is on my breast. My jar is full.
I am Lemat ez Khelaga. I am entered. I closed well. I am owed.
The door is opened for one who is entered.
I go in.
```

What is said within is not written. Here the spells end. The reader rolls the roll and puts it into the pit at her left hand, and the pit is filled, and the club goes to the hall to eat.

: The Colophon {#colophon}

```poetry {form=lekhunet lang=en}
Copied for Lemat ez Khelaga, fowler's widow, of the water-stair at Khelaga,
in Year 12 of Gar-Aû Meqes XVI, the first month of Shelu,
from the roll of the House, which is from the tablets of the Great Land.
Paid for by the fowlers' club of the water-stair: eight _qelu_ of copper, weighed at the temple, and the weighing is written.
The names of the four keepers: two _gezan_ more. Not paid. Not copied.
A jar: one _qelu_. Paid. Two figures of clay: one _qelu_ the pair. Paid.
Copied by **Lenni**, Lekhau of the House, in the house of the embalmers at Khelaga, in one day, in the people's hand, in good order.
Taken from her hand, it is still hers, and the hand that took it is written.
```
