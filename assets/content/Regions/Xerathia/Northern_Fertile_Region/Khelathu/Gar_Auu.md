**Gar-Aû** is the title of the divine sovereign of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]], and Gar-Aûu, its collective plural, names the whole line of them taken together, from the first to the one who sits now. The throne is the first thing a scribe learns to write, because everything else is dated by it.

"Head the page with the year of the reign, and the reign is the name," a clerk in the bureaus tells a boy on his first morning at the desk. "Year 12 of Gar-Aû [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]]. When she dies the next sheet reads Year 1 of whoever follows, and the old sheets stay as they were. Write it plainly and do not argue about who is a god while you are writing it."

The Gar-Aû is addressed as divine. In practice nobody believes the Gar-Aû is a god, everyone knows that nobody believes it, and the fiction is kept anyway, because the throne's authority, the priesthood's standing and the people's sense of cosmic order all rest on it. Legions of priests attend the Gar-Aû, who passes through sacred gates, eats ritually prepared food and conducts affairs of state as ceremony. The Gar-Aû's word is law, and no Gar-Aû governs well without the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]]. Queens have reigned as Gar-Aû in their own right, and several of the ablest have.

As nominal supreme priest of every god, the Gar-Aû holds a claim over every temple. Every high priest serves at the throne's pleasure, and a Gar-Aû who wished to replace one would have doctrinal grounds. Few have tried and fewer have succeeded; the high priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] has deposed or raised more Gar-Aûu than any army has.

One dynasty tried to make the fiction true. The last Gar-Aû of [[affiliation-garzinelrelu|Gar-Zin'el'Rêlu]] proclaimed himself Uqa'â in the flesh, and his name was struck from every wall; the records know him only as [[being-falseuqaa|the False Uqa'â]].

## The King-List

The temple king-lists, compiled by the priesthood of [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'âr]], count the Gar-Aûu back to the [[lore-qettelgu|Qet Telgu]] in unbroken sequence. The master copy lies in the god's precinct beside the Great Temple on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], in the charge of the **Keeper of the King-Lists**, and a second copy is kept at [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]]. It holds two hundred and sixty-two reigns in fifty-nine houses, from the first Gar-Aû to Meqes XVI.

Every reign stands under its throne name, the name the Gar-Aû was crowned under, and the throne name is what every contract and tax roll of the reign carries. A throne name joins a name element to a god's house-form—_Amqel'Uqa_, "beloved of the sun-lord"; _Gez'el'Qar_, "enduring in order"—and the [[skill-khelathlng|Khelâthi Language]] note sets out how one is built. Names are used again, and the list numbers their bearers across its whole length, so a numeral counts every Gar-Aû who has worn the name, in any house.

A conqueror is entered like anyone else. Hill-nomads from the western margins, sea-raiders out of the north and a [[lore-vylarianclt|Vylarian]] army have each taken the throne; each time the priesthood crowned the new master under a throne name, entered his line as a house, and waited. The list gives the foreign houses Khelâthi names and keeps no other.

## Reading the List

"Sit, and do not copy anything yet," the Keeper tells a newly ordained copyist on his first morning in the precinct. "You will copy this list for forty years. First learn to read it, because it does not say what it means, and the house has never once corrected it."

The list is written in formulas, and a reader who knows them can read a great deal that the list never states.

| The list says                                           | It records                                                   | Read it as                                                       |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _He stood upon the mound_                               | the crowning on Yath-Telgu                                   | Year 1 of the reign                                              |
| _He went to the West in his ninth year_                 | a death, in the ninth regnal year                            | the commonest entry, and the only one that says nothing more     |
| _He went to the West in the night_                      | a death the house does not describe                          | an end that was not natural, more often than not                 |
| _His year was not completed_                            | a reign that ended before its first flood                    | a reign of months; look at who was crowned next                  |
| _A child of five floods; **Gul'Zetu** stood beside him_ | a Gar-Aû too young to rule, with a regent of the named house | a short reign coming                                             |
| _The throne passed to the one who stood beside it_      | the regent's crowning                                        | the child's death, entered a line above it                       |
| _The house is closed_                                   | the end of a dynasty                                         | the house's entry in the ledger, written shut                    |
| _The name is struck; the years stand_                   | a reign whose name was taken from the record as a sentence   | a reign that happened, counted and unnamed                       |
| _His deeds are the god's_                               | nothing at all                                               | the oldest entries, which give a name, a length and nothing else |

"The house enters a death and not its manner," the Keeper says. "So the list does not tell you who was murdered. It tells you which children were crowned, who stood beside them, and how often the one who stood beside a child was the next to stand upon the mound. Count those yourself. The list will not count them for you, and it is right not to."

What the copyist counts is the shape of the whole. Most houses close within fifteen to thirty years, and some within six months. Many of the shortest reigns are children's, and the regent who stood beside a dying child is crowned in the child's place so often that the formula for it is the most worn line in the list. The list calls none of it murder.

### The Keeper's Caution

"The farther back you read, the less you know," the Keeper says, "and the list is written in the same hand all the way down. That is the trap."

The list has three depths, and the Keepers themselves divide it so.

- **The First Tablets.** The first thousand years hold eleven Gar-Aûu in two houses, with reigns of 150, 120 and 110 years and nothing recorded of any of them but the name, the length and the formula _his deeds are the god's_. The lengths are round, and they add up end to end, as no real succession does: a real reign shares its first year with the last year of the reign before it, because the new Gar-Aû is crowned in the year the old one dies. The first houses lie in the burial-ground of [[place-zugernome|Zu-Ger]], the Great Land, which is why that selat is called royal.
- **The Middle Count.** The next twelve and a half centuries hold forty-four houses and two hundred and six reigns. From here the reigns overlap as real ones do and their lengths are plausible, but the Keepers hold the copy they have and not the tablets it was made from, and a name or a length in the Middle Count is a thing a careful scholar quotes as "the list gives."
- **The Near Count.** From the house the necropolis calls Gar-Zin'el'Rêlu to the present, the list gives every reign with its months, its crowning year in the count and the formula its end was entered under. This is the part a court, a temple or a Halzi'a cites, and the part the tables below give whole.

The foreign scholars who read the list at Khelunu count the First Tablets on their fingers and frown. The Keepers do not dispute their arithmetic. The tablets are the god's, and the house does not correct the god.

### Dating a Document

The regnal year turns with the calendar year, on the first day of [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]]. Year 1 of a reign runs from the crowning to the next flood, and Year 2 begins with it. So a reign of nine regnal years may be eight years long, and the year a Gar-Aû died is also Year 1 of the next.

To date a document, find its reign in the list and count: the year in the count from the Qet Telgu is the crowning year plus the regnal year, less one, and the western year is that figure less 2,110. For a year before the Founding, subtract the count from 2,111 instead, because the western reckoning has no year zero: 1006 ST is 1105 BF.

A lease of farmland in the Zu-Ger flood-basins is dated "Year 9 of Gar-Aû **Gez'el'Psaqa I**, the second month of Gelet." Gez'el'Psaqa I was crowned in 2535 of the count, so Year 9 is 2543, which is 433 AF in the western calendar. The same lease dated "Year 9 of Gez'el'Psaqa" with no numeral is a lease nobody can place until someone has checked which Gez'el'Psaqa was meant, and in a dispute that question is itself the dispute.

A document of the struck house carries the name the list no longer gives. The temples scraped the name from the copies they held and left the years, so a temple record reads "Year 6 of the struck one, the sixth reign of the struck house," which is 2370 of the count and 260 AF. A private contract that escaped the scraping still carries the name whole. It is valid as a contract and dangerous to own.

## The Near Count

The tables give each reign as the list enters it: the throne name, the crowning year in the count, the number of regnal years entered, the western year of the crowning, and the formulas the list enters it under. A year in the count is written ST, for _Selqur Qet Telgu_ (the year of the First Occasion).

### The Struck House

The house the necropolis calls Gar-Zin'el'Rêlu stood barely a century, from 2281 to 2378 of the count. The list keeps its six reigns as years under struck names: 31, 22, 4, 19, 13 and 14. The last of them is the False Uqa'â, crowned in 2365 and thrown down in 2378, which is 268 AF—four and a half centuries before the present Gar-Aû. The list gives no name for any of the six and no name for the house; it enters the house's close as _the house is closed, and its name with it_.

The Amralo'Methu are the Vylarian occupation. A Vylarian army at the height of [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]]'s reach took the throne in 377 AF, and the priesthood crowned its commander Zab'el'Qar—"lord in order," a name that told the valley what was expected of him. His son reigned after him as a Khelâthi, and when he went to the West in the night the house was closed and no Vylarian claimed it. The list gives the commander's throne name and nothing else, and no Khelâthi record gives the name he was born to.

### The Reigning House

The house of Zu'Mequ took the throne in 470 AF, when its head, **Meqes**, stood beside the child Quz'el'Retha III and was crowned in the child's second year. He kept his own given name as his throne name, and every Gar-Aû of the house since has been crowned Meqes. The house is two and a half centuries old, the longest-lived in the Near Count, and at the height of its power **Meqes VI** carried the older houses out from the river-front of the royal necropolis at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]] by the **Great Removal** and set the house's own Way at the head of the death-road.

The chartered guilds date themselves by this house: [[affiliation-garnuw|Gar'Nuw]] holds its charter from Meqes VII, the seventh Gar-Aû of the house, and [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] from Meqes XI, the eleventh. Meqes XVI is Meqes XV's daughter, crowned at thirty-four. Her nephew [[being-anlaghur|Anlaghur]] stands near enough the succession that the court asks the question seriously, and the house has two children's reigns in its own list, and the one it ended to take the throne, to remind it what a succession can cost.

Only the houses that ruled from Galezkara lie in the royal necropolis across the river from it. The First House and Azlet'Qûtu lie at Zu-Ger, the house of Lenen-Nezut in its own old capital, and the house of Magu-Athen in its own half-empty city, so the row of Ways at Zu-Gezer is the list with whole stretches missing.
