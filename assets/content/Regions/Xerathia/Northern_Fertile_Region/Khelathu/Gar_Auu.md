**Gar-Aû** is the title of the divine sovereign of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]], and **Gar-Aûu**, its collective plural, names the whole line of them taken together, from the first to the one who sits now. The throne is the first thing a scribe learns to write, because everything else is dated by it.

"Head the page with the year of the reign, and the reign is the name," a clerk in the bureaus tells a boy on his first morning at the desk. "Year 12 of Gar-Aû **Meqes XVI**. When he dies the next sheet reads Year 1 of whoever follows, and the old sheets stay as they were. Write it plainly and do not argue about who is a god while you are writing it."

The Gar-Aû is addressed as divine. In practice nobody believes the Gar-Aû is a god, everyone knows that nobody believes it, and the fiction is kept anyway, because the throne's authority, the priesthood's standing and the people's sense of cosmic order all rest on it. Legions of priests attend the Gar-Aû, who passes through sacred gates, eats ritually prepared food and conducts affairs of state as ceremony. The Gar-Aû's word is law, and no Gar-Aû governs well without the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]]. Queens have reigned as Gar-Aû in their own right, and several of the ablest have.

As nominal supreme priest of every god, the Gar-Aû holds a claim over every temple. Every high priest serves at the throne's pleasure, and a Gar-Aû who wished to replace one would have doctrinal grounds. Few have tried and fewer have succeeded; the high priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] has deposed or raised more Gar-Aûu than any army has.

One dynasty tried to make the fiction true. The last Gar-Aû of [[affiliation-garzinelrelu|Gar-Zin'el'Rêlu]] proclaimed himself Uqa'â in the flesh, and his name was struck from every wall; the records know him only as [[being-falseuqaa|the False Uqa'â]].

The temple king-lists, compiled by the priesthood of [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'âr]], count the Gar-Aûu back to the [[lore-qettelgu|Qet Telgu]] in unbroken sequence.
