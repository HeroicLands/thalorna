"Walk it in the order the sun does," a priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] tells a pilgrim at the eastern edge of the city, "and the city explains itself."

**Wal-Uqa'â**, the Sun's Road, is the one straight line on which [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] is hung. It begins at the eastern edge of the living city and runs in along the spine of the streets, and the named quarters either lie along it or branch off it. It crosses the Bridge of the Coming-Forth to the sacred island of [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], passes through the forecourts of the Great Temple of Uqa'â, and leaves the temple's western face by the Bridge of the Going-Down. Beyond the second channel it climbs onto the desert shelf and becomes the death-road of the necropolis of [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]. Carved sphinxes and ram-statues line much of its length.

The road is laid to the solstice line. At midsummer dawn the sun rises exactly down its eastern length, and at midwinter dusk it sets exactly down its western length into the necropolis. By law nothing may stand in the way of either view from the temple, and on the high festivals the image of Uqa'â is carried in procession down the avenue and across the water to the island.
