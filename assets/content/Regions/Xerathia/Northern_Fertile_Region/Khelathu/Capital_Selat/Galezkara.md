## Overview

Galezkara is the imperial city of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]]—seat of the Gar-Aû, capital of the [[affiliation-capitalselat|The Capital Selat]], and by far the largest city in the empire. For three thousand years it has been the center of the oldest civilization on Thalorna, and it wears the weight of that antiquity in stone: the immense temple-complexes that define the empire's official doctrine, the palace where the semi-divine Gar-Aû rules through ritual and ceremony, the bureaus and treasuries of the central bureaucracy, the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]] council-halls, and the scribal schools that staff the realm. Midstream, on a sacred island held to be the very mound where creation began, stand the palace and the Great Temple of [[affiliation-khelathpnthn|Khelâthi Pantheon]]; and across the water on the western desert shore stretch the necropoli—the pyramids and rock-cut tombs of dynasty upon dynasty. The living city, the god's island, and the city of the dead lie strung from east to west along the path of the sun.

To live in Galezkara is to live at the center of everything. Tribute and delegations flow in from forty selatu; ambassadors from [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] and [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]] keep houses near the palace; the festival-calendar fills the streets with processions and feasts; and the markets are the most crowded and polyglot in the interior. Some four hundred and fifty thousand souls live here, fed by grain barged down from the [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|The Upper River Selatu]] and the dense farm-and-temple country of the surrounding selat—a concentration of people, wealth, ritual, and power found nowhere else in Xerathia.

## Geography

### The Site and the Solar Axis

At Galezkara the great river widens and divides around a large midstream island, giving the capital a body in three parts: the **living city** on the eastern, dawn-side bank; the **Sacred Island** in the channel between; and the **necropolis** on the western, desert shore where the sun goes down. The arrangement is not accident but theology made into ground. The Khelâthi hold that the sun is born in the east, rides to its height at noon, and dies into the west each day, and that the island is the [[affiliation-empireakhlth#the-kheperi-calendar-and-dating|primeval mound]] that first rose from the waters at the Qet Telgu—so the city of the living, the god's island, and the city of the dead lie strung along the sun's own path, in the order of its daily journey.

Running through the whole of it, dead straight, is the **Sun's Road** (_Wal-Uqa'â_)—the great ceremonial thoroughfare and the axis the entire capital is hung upon. It runs from the eastern edge of the living city, in along the spine of the streets, across the **Bridge of the Coming-Forth** to the Sacred Island, straight through the forecourts of the Great Temple of Uqa'â, and out the temple's western face onto the **Bridge of the Going-Down**, which carries it across the second channel and up onto the desert shelf, where it becomes the single avenue of the necropolis. It is laid to the solstice line: at **midsummer dawn the sun rises exactly down the eastern road**—the longest day, the sun at its most triumphant, born along the road of the living—and at **midwinter dusk it sets exactly down the western road** into the necropolis, the shortest day, the sun at its weakest, dying along the road of the dead. The Temple of Uqa'â stands at the pivot of both. So sacred are these two sightlines that the **law of the empire forbids any structure to break the view from the temple to the horizon along either road**; the prohibition has stood through every dynasty, and a building that creeps into the sightline is pulled down without appeal, however great its owner.

### The Two Shores

The asymmetry of the two banks is itself a doctrine. The **living city** on the east is a rough half-circle, its straight edge along the river and its bulk swelling eastward, with a long spur spiking out along the Sun's Road toward the midsummer horizon. It is organic, accreted, unplanned—a thing **grown** over three thousand years. The **necropolis** opposite is the reverse in every way: far smaller, strictly rectangular, almost entirely of stone, laid out by decree along its single avenue—the death-road—with each dynasty's Gar-Aûu interred along their own crossing-road off it, ranked to north and south—by tradition oldest-nearest-the-river, though strong dynasties have reordered the row, and the current house holds the splendid, gold-and-cobalt river-front itself (see [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]). The living build however they please; the dead are housed by law and geometry, in an order meant never to change.

The two banks differ in their very substance, and this too is the local logic of the flood. Only the things meant to outlast death are built in stone—the temples, the tombs, the island, the great causeways—set on massive raised platforms that shrug off the water; everything the living actually inhabit is **mudbrick**, which slumps in the damp and is torn down and rebuilt every few generations atop its own rubble. The result is that the old quarters ride high on **tells** of their own accumulated wreckage, while the newer and poorer ground lies low; and the city has no single wall but a hundred of them, for every temple, granary, palace, and great house sits inside its own enclosure and the streets are only the crooked gaps left between.

## City Layout

### The Sacred Island

The island—_Yath-Telgu_, the First Mound—is the holiest ground in the empire and the only part of the capital ringed by a true fortified wall, white-plastered and visible for leagues. Within it stand the **Great Temple of Uqa'â**, an accretion of courts, pylons, and obelisks that a hundred reigns have each added to, and the **palace of the Gar-Aû**, with its gardens, audience-halls, and the bureaus of the inner court. Here too is **the Measure** (_the igelu-gauge_)—a graduated well and stair, tended by the priests of Uqa'â, by which the height of the rising flood is read each year; the reading predicts the harvest, sets the year's taxes, and is proclaimed to the city as the single most important number in the empire. The island is reached by the two great bridge-causeways of the Sun's Road, and by sacred barge. Its three great works—temple, palace, and Measure—are treated in full in [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]].

### The Living City {#the-living-city}

The east bank is a patchwork of named quarters, grown rather than planned and strung along or branching off the Sun's Road:

- **Qelt-Ge ("the Forefront")**—the monumental plaza at the head of the Bridge of the Coming-Forth, where the Sun's Road meets the river facing the island; the civic heart, where proclamations are read and the great crowds gather.
- **Zu-Leri ("the High City")**—the oldest and highest tell, crowned by the great **Temple of [[affiliation-khelathpnthn|Khelâthi Pantheon]]**, craft-god and patron of the capital, and the walled mansions of the priestly-noble houses. The deepest layers of the past lie under its streets.
- **Gar-Medjatozo ("the House of Records")**—the bureau district: the treasuries, the Genzet'Palu council-hall, the great scribal schools, and the archives where the empire's paperwork lives.
- **Amqel-Ger ("the Great Quay")**—the working riverfront downstream of Qelt-Ge: docks, warehouses, the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] banking-houses, the [[affiliation-zebequzut|Zebequ'Zut]] merchant halls, and the **foreign shore** where Haradian and Mídhalión factors keep their compounds.
- **Lut-Lemu ("the Place of Craftsmen")**—the artisan quarters: coppersmiths, potters, faience-makers, weavers, and woodworkers, loud and smoky and kept downstream and downwind.
- **The Wazulet ("the Pure Place")**—a liminal, faintly shunned quarter by the necropolis-ferry, where the working embalmers and coffin-makers cluster and the dead pass through on their way to the western shore.
- **Pa-Demi ("the Town")**—the sprawling mudbrick laborers' warrens on the low, flood-prone ground, dense and poor, built among and atop old ruins.
- [[place-garqethar|Gar-Qethar]]—abandoned palace-and-noble precincts of fallen dynasties: half-robbed, squatted, a maze of the past standing inside the living city.
- [[place-zelnut|The Zelnut]]—the great state and temple granaries, raised behind dikes, where the grain-dole is distributed.

### Gardens, Lakes, and Open Ground

Green space in Galezkara is sacred and mostly walled, but real and abundant. Each great temple holds a rectangular **sacred lake** and a planted **grove** of sycamore, persea, tamarisk, and date-palm around lotus pools, fed by groundwater that rises and falls with the flood; these are the city's green lungs, opened to the crowds on festival days. The palace keeps its own gardens on the island. And threading the High City is **the Drowned Way**—an old silted-up channel of the river, long dead, that the noble houses have made into a chain of pleasure-gardens and lotus-pools: a vanished arm of the river become a garden, the city's most prized and exclusive walk.

### Markets and Commerce {#markets-and-commerce}

Trade is done in the open—on quays, in squares, and in temple forecourts. **Amqel-Ger's riverfront** is the everyday market, a floating bazaar of cargo-boats and a shore lined with stalls. **Open market-squares** stand at the crossings of the districts—a grain-market by the Shenut, a cattle-market on the landward edge, a luxury-and-foreign market by the harbor. But the greatest commerce of the year rides on the **festival calendar**: when the temple precincts open their forecourts for the high feasts, the city fills, and more goods change hands in those days than in whole seasons between.

### The Sun's Road and the Great Processions {#the-suns-road-and-the-great-processions}

The Sun's Road is the armature of the monumental city and the stage of its grandest rites. Lined for much of its length with carved sphinxes and ram-statues, it links the river temples through Qelt-Ge and carries the god across the water on its two stone causeways. On the high festivals the image of Uqa'â is borne in procession down the avenue and across to the island; on the two solstice climaxes—the midsummer **Festival of the Coming-Forth**, when the dawn breaks straight down the eastern road, and the midwinter **Festival of the Going-Down**, when the sun sets straight into the necropolis—the whole capital turns out to watch the god born and the god die along his own road, the sightlines kept clear by a law older than memory.

## Flood Season

For one season each year—[[lore-azletssn|Azlet]], the Inundation—the river rises and remakes the city, and Galezkara is built to expect it. The flood is no sudden disaster but a slow swelling over weeks, heralded in advance by the stars and watched on the Measure; the whole calendar is organized around it. As the water rises, the channels around the island swell, the lower harbors submerge and shift, and the low quarters of **Pa-Demi** and the flood-margins go partly under—evacuated each year as a matter of routine, their people moving up to kin, to the temple precincts, or to reed-shelter on the high tells, and the damaged mudbrick repaired or rebuilt once the water falls. This is not a failure of the city but its design: mud is the impermanent material precisely because the floodplain demands one.

The stone city stands above it all. The temple-platforms, the granary-dikes, the great causeways, and the Sacred Island itself are raised clear of the highest flood, so that at the peak the old high quarters and the island stand as islands above a brown sea—and the rising water lapping the outer courts of the Great Temple of Uqa'â re-enacts the primeval mound alone above the waters of creation, the holiest sight of the year rather than a thing to be feared. The two bridge-causeways of the Sun's Road are built high above maximum flood on long ramped approaches, so the solstice processions cross **dry-shod above the inundation**, the god's road standing clear above a drowned world; lesser crossings are seasonal pontoon-bridges, floated out and drawn back with the water, and where they fail the city falls back on sacred ferrying.

Because the fields lie underwater and farm-labor is impossible, **flood season is building season**: the surplus labor of the selat rebuilds the mud city and raises the monuments, and the great blocks of stone for the tombs and temples are floated on flood-barges right up to the desert edge, which the water reaches only now. The necropolis itself never floods—it sits up on the high desert shelf beyond the water's reach, the death-road causeway climbing from the river onto dry rock—so that the dead lie forever above the inundation and the living forever below it, drowned and renewed each year. Over centuries the flood leaves its slower marks even on the stone: salt creeping up from the damp into the bases of the monuments, and the silt settling unevenly beneath them, cracking obelisks and tilting colossi that a hundred reigns then labor to shore up.

## Governance and Order {#governance-and-order}

Galezkara answers to three powers at once, and the friction between them is the city's daily politics. Above all sits the Gar-Aû, whose word is law and whose presence on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] makes the capital the one place in the empire where the throne governs directly rather than through a distant Halzi'a. Beneath the throne, the civil administration of the city—and of the empire it runs from here—falls to the **Zeglar**, the chief minister and supreme judge, who keeps his bureaus in [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Gar-Medjatozo]] and is, in all but name, the mayor of the capital as well as the second man in the realm. And cutting across both stand the **great temples**, for each major temple precinct is a near-sovereign enclave that owns its lands, feeds its own people, keeps its own guards and courts, and answers to its Thâz'Lekhau before it answers to the city—so that a fugitive who reaches temple ground, or a dispute that touches a god's estate, passes out of the Zeglar's hands entirely.

Order in the public city is kept by the **Meglay**, the standing city guard—distinct from the army, and distinct again from the necropolis-guard across the water. They walk the quarters and the quays, man the gates of the walled enclosures after dark, chase thieves, break brawls, and double as the fire-watch; their watch-posts stand in every district, with the largest by the markets and the harbor where trouble gathers. Justice runs in tiers beneath the Zeglar: the local **Genzet**, a magistrate's court in each quarter, handles market disputes, debts, and petty crime under the eye of [[affiliation-khelathpnthn|Khelâthi Pantheon]]; graver matters of blood and theft rise to the higher courts; and anything touching a temple, a noble house, or the crown leaves the civic system altogether. As everywhere, the law bends to the right name and the right purse—but in a city this watched, the wrong enemies are as dangerous as the wrong crimes.

## The People {#the-people}

The capital's near half-million sort into a steep pyramid. At the apex, a thin crust of priests, nobles, senior scribes, and court officials lives in the walled mansions of [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Zu-Leri]] and on the temple estates; beneath them a broad middling sort of artisans, traders, lesser scribes, and temple-servants; and at the base the vast mass of laborers, porters, boatmen, market-folk, and the poor of the low warrens. The one ladder between the layers is the **scribal school**—a clever child of any birth who masters the demotic hand can rise into the administration, and the capital draws the ambitious of the whole empire to try. Women hold property, trade, plead in the Genzet, and serve as priestesses here as everywhere in Aû'Khelâthu, and the capital has more women of independent means than anywhere else in the realm.

Galezkara is also the most foreign-touched place in the interior. [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Haradian]] and [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]] factors keep walled compounds along the harbor in [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Amqel-Ger]]; [[place-helionis|Helionite]] scholars lodge near the temple-libraries, among the few outsiders ever admitted to them; southern traders, steppe horse-dealers, and the endless tribute-delegations and pilgrims throng the markets in season. And set a little apart from all of them are the people of the [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Wabet]]—the embalmers, coffin-makers, and tomb-trade folk who cross each day to serve the dead at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]], and whom the rest of the city treats with the wary respect owed to those who handle corpses for a living.

## Water, Bread, and Fire

A city on the greatest river in the world still minds its water with care. The river runs thick with silt and fouler still where the city's drains meet it, so Galezkara drinks not from the stream but from **wells, cisterns, and settling-jars**, and the wealthy from spring-fed conduits; in the heat of low water the poor quarters thirst even as the river slides past their doors. The flood that renews the fields also fouls the wells when it crests, and the weeks of the inundation are reckoned an unhealthy season.

Bread comes from upstream: the grain of [[affiliation-selatnlghztn|The Selat of Anlagh-Zetûn]] and the upper river is barged down, stored in the great granaries of the [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Shenut]], and given out in part as a **dole** to the temple-dependents, the construction-gangs of the flood-season, and the registered poor. As in any capital, the dole is less charity than statecraft—a hungry Galezkara is a dangerous one, and whoever controls the granaries holds a lever on the peace of the city, a lesson written in stone across the water on the cursed road. Fire is the lesser hazard here than in the timber cities of the west, for mudbrick does not burn; but roofs, market-stalls, workshops, and above all the grain-dust of the granaries do, and a blaze in the crowded [[place-galezkara#the-living-city|Lut-Lemu]] craft-quarters can run from lane to lane before the Meglay's fire-gangs and the walled enclosures between districts pen it in.

## The Underworld

The signature crime of the capital is **tomb-robbery**, and it is no petty trade but an ancient, organized one with its roots in the necropolis itself—for the people best placed to plunder the dead are the very guilds and priesthoods sworn to tend them, and a corrupt keeper, a bribed guard, or a craftsman who never forgot a tomb's hidden door is worth more to a robbing-ring than any outside thief. The stolen yield—grave-gold melted and recast, amulets, funerary papyri, "recovered" stone—moves through fences in the markets and out through the harbor with the foreign factors, and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] money-changers ask few questions of old gold. Around it runs the ordinary underworld of any great port-capital: smuggling to dodge the temple and crown monopolies, the protection rackets of the dock-gangs, and the forgers who supply the courts with false deeds and the tombs with false provenances.

Beneath the common crime lurk the **forbidden cults**—the rites the temples have outlawed and driven underground, worship of the chaos-serpent [[affiliation-khelathpnthn|Khelâthi Pantheon]] foremost among them. The Meglay and the temple-inquisitors hunt them without rest, and most are harmless desperation; but the one the priesthood of Uqa'â fears in its bones is the small, hidden sect that does not merely defy the order of the dead but means to **break** it—the cult that seeks the cursed road across the water, and would wake the False Uqa'â (see [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]). That any such cult exists at all is a secret the temple keeps close, and the reason the kill-on-sight order on the cursed road has never once been relaxed.

## Notable Features

- **The Sacred Island (Yath-Telgu)**—the primeval mound, holding the palace, the Great Temple of Uqa'â, and the Measure (igelu-gauge), behind the empire's only true fortified wall
- **The Sun's Road (Wal-Uqa'â)**—the solstice-aligned ceremonial axis running through the whole city and across both bridges, its horizon-sightlines protected by ancient law
- **The two solstice festivals**—the midsummer Coming-Forth and the midwinter Going-Down, when the sun rises and sets straight down the god's road
- **The living city**—a grown, organic half-circle of mudbrick tells, walled enclosures, and named quarters
- **The necropolis**—the small, strict, stone rectangle on the western desert shelf, ranked along the death-road
- **The central bureaucracy and scribal schools**—the treasuries, the Genzet'Palu, and the empire's meritocratic engine
- [[place-waligelu|Wal-Igelu]]—a dead river-channel turned chain of noble pleasure-gardens
- **The Meglay and the Zeglar's courts**—the city guard and the tiered justice that keep order in a half-million capital
- **The tomb-robbing underworld**—organized plunder of the dead, the grave-goods black market, and the hunted cults
