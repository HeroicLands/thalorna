## Overview

**Galezkara** is built on one straight line. The living city on the east bank, the god's island in the river and the city of the dead on the western desert are strung along the path of the sun, and at midsummer dawn the sun rises exactly down the road that joins them. It is the imperial city of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]]—seat of the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Aû]], capital of the [[affiliation-capitalselat|Capital Selat]], and by far the largest city in the empire, with some 450,000 people.

"Stand here, at the foot of the bridge, and face the island," a young priest of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] tells a party of pilgrims at first light. "Behind you the city is born every morning. In front of you is the mound where the world began. Beyond it, across the second channel, is where the sun goes to die each evening, and where the kings went before it. Everything in Galezkara is in that order. Learn the order and you will never be lost here."

The mound at its heart is as old as the count of years, and for about eighteen centuries, since the court crossed to it in 1006 ST (1105 BF; [[lore-crossmound|the Crossing to the Mound]]), the city has been the seat of the oldest civilization in [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]]. It carries that age in stone: the temple complexes that define the empire's official doctrine; the palace where the Gar-Aû rules through ritual and ceremony; the bureaus and treasuries of the central administration; the council halls of the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]]; and the scribal schools that staff the realm. Midstream, on the island held to be the mound where creation began, stand the palace and the **Great Temple of Uqa'â**. Across the water on the western desert shore lie the necropolises, the pyramids and rock-cut tombs of every house that has ruled from the city; the first two houses, which ruled before the Crossing, lie upriver at [[place-zugernome|Zu-Ger]].

Tribute and delegations come in from thirty-nine selatu. Ambassadors from [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] and [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]] keep houses near the palace, the festival calendar fills the streets with processions and feasts, and the markets are the most crowded and polyglot in the interior. Grain barged down from the [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|Upper River Selatu]] and from the dense farm-and-temple country of the surrounding selat feeds it all. Nowhere else in [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]] holds so many people, so much wealth, ritual and power in one place.

## Geography

### The Site and the Solar Axis

At Galezkara the river widens and divides around a large island, and the city has three parts: the living city on the eastern, dawn-side bank; the [[place-yathtelgu|Sacred Island]] in the channel; and the necropolis on the western desert shore where the sun goes down. The arrangement is theology laid out on the ground. The [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] hold that the sun is born in the east, rides to its height at noon and dies into the west each day, and that the island is the [[lore-qettelgu|primeval mound]] that first rose from the waters at the [[lore-qettelgu|Qet Telgu]]. So the city of the living, the god's island and the city of the dead lie along the sun's own path, in the order of its daily journey.

The **Sun's Road** ([[place-waluqaa|Wal-Uqa'â]]) runs dead straight through all of it, and the whole capital is hung upon it. It starts at the eastern edge of the living city and runs in along the spine of the streets, across the **Bridge of the Coming-Forth** to the Sacred Island, straight through the forecourts of the Great Temple of Uqa'â, and out of the temple's western face onto the **Bridge of the Going-Down**. That bridge carries it over the second channel and up onto the desert shelf, where it becomes the single avenue of the necropolis.

The road is laid to the solstice line:

- At midsummer dawn the sun rises exactly down the eastern road—the longest day, the sun at its strongest, born along the road of the living.
- At midwinter dusk it sets exactly down the western road into the necropolis—the shortest day, the sun at its weakest, dying along the road of the dead.

The **Temple of Uqa'â** stands at the pivot of both. _The law of the empire forbids any structure to break the view from the temple to the horizon along either road._ The prohibition has stood through every dynasty, and a building that creeps into the sightline is pulled down without appeal, however great its owner.

### The Two Shores

The two banks are deliberately unlike each other, and the difference is itself a doctrine.

The living city on the east is a rough half-circle, its straight edge along the river and its bulk swelling eastward, with a long spur running out along the Sun's Road toward the midsummer horizon. It is accreted and unplanned, a thing grown over the eighteen centuries since the court came to the mound.

The necropolis opposite is its reverse in every way: far smaller, strictly rectangular, almost entirely stone, and laid out by decree along its single avenue, the death-road. Each dynasty's Gar-Aûu lie along their own crossing-road off it, ranked to north and south, by tradition the oldest nearest the river. Strong dynasties have reordered the row, and the reigning house holds the gold-and-cobalt river-front itself (see [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]). The living build however they please; the dead are housed by law and geometry, in an order meant never to change.

The banks differ in substance too, and the flood explains why. Only what is meant to outlast death is built in stone—the temples, the tombs, the island, the great causeways—on massive raised platforms that shrug off the water. Everything the living actually inhabit is mudbrick, which slumps in the damp and is torn down and rebuilt every few generations on top of its own rubble. The old quarters therefore ride high on tells (mounds) of their own accumulated wreckage, while the newer and poorer ground lies low. The city has no single wall. It has a hundred, because every temple, granary, palace and great house sits inside its own enclosure, and the streets are the crooked gaps left between them.

## City Layout

### The Sacred Island

The island, [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] (the [[place-yathtelgu|First Mound]]), is the holiest ground in the empire and the only part of the capital ringed by a true fortified wall, white-plastered and visible for leagues. Inside it stand the Great Temple of Uqa'â, a mass of courts, pylons and obelisks that a hundred reigns have each added to, and the palace of the Gar-Aû, with its gardens, audience halls and the bureaus of the inner court.

Here too is **the Measure**, the igelu-gauge: a graduated well and stair, kept by the priests of Uqa'â, where the height of the rising flood is read each year. The reading predicts the harvest and sets the year's taxes, and it is proclaimed to the city as the most important number in the empire. Two great bridge-causeways on the Sun's Road reach the island, and so does the sacred barge. The temple, the palace and the Measure are described in full in the Yath-Telgu note.

### The Living City {#the-living-city}

The east bank is a patchwork of named quarters, grown rather than planned, strung along the Sun's Road or branching off it:

- [[place-qeltge|Qelt-Ge]] ("the Forefront")—the monumental plaza at the head of the Bridge of the Coming-Forth, where the Sun's Road meets the river facing the island. It is the civic heart, where proclamations are read and the great crowds gather.
- [[place-zuleri|Zu-Leri]] ("the **High City**")—the oldest and highest tell, crowned by the great Temple of [[lore-psaqarudty|Psaq'âru]], craft-god and patron of the capital, and by the walled mansions of the priestly and noble houses. The deepest layers of the city's past lie under its streets.
- [[place-garmedjatozo|Gar-Medjatozo]] ("the **House of Records**")—the bureau district: the treasuries, the Genzet'Palu council hall, the great scribal schools, and the archives where the empire's paperwork lives.
- [[place-amqelger|Amqel-Ger]] ("the **Great Quay**")—the working riverfront downstream of Qelt-Ge, with its docks and warehouses, the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] banking houses, the [[affiliation-zebequzut|Zebequ'Zut]] merchant halls, and the foreign shore where [[lore-haradianclt|Haradian]] and [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] factors keep their compounds.
- [[place-lutlemu|Lut-Lemu]] ("the **Place of Craftsmen**")—the artisan quarters of coppersmiths, potters, faience-makers, weavers and woodworkers, loud and smoky and kept downstream and downwind.
- [[place-wazulet|The Wazulet]] ("the [[place-wazulet|Pure Place]]")—a faintly shunned quarter by the necropolis ferry, where the embalmers and coffin-makers live and the dead pass through on their way to the western shore.
- [[place-garqethar|Gar-Qethar]]—the sprawling mudbrick laborers' warrens on the low, flood-prone ground, dense and poor, built among and on top of old ruins.
- [[place-zelnut|The Zelnut]]—a quarter built among and over the tombs of fallen dynasties: half-robbed, squatted, a maze of the dead city standing inside the living one.

### Gardens, Lakes, and Open Ground

Green space in Galezkara is sacred and mostly walled, but there is a good deal of it. Each great temple keeps a rectangular sacred lake and a grove of sycamore, persea, tamarisk and date-palm around lotus pools, fed by groundwater that rises and falls with the flood. These are the city's lungs, opened to the crowds on festival days. The palace keeps its own gardens on the island.

Through the High City runs [[place-waligelu|Wal-Igelu]], **the Drowned Way**: an old channel of the river, silted up and long dead, that the noble houses have made into a chain of pleasure gardens and lotus pools. A vanished arm of the river has become the city's most prized and exclusive walk.

### Markets and Commerce {#markets-and-commerce}

Trade is done in the open, on quays, in squares and in temple forecourts. Amqel-Ger's riverfront is the everyday market, a floating bazaar of cargo boats against a shore lined with stalls. Open market squares stand where the districts meet: a grain market by the great granaries, a cattle market on the landward edge, and a market for luxuries and foreign goods by the harbor. The greatest commerce of the year rides on the festival calendar. When the temple precincts open their forecourts for the high feasts the city fills, and more goods change hands in those days than in whole seasons between them.

### The Sun's Road and the Great Processions {#the-suns-road-and-the-great-processions}

The Sun's Road is the frame of the monumental city and the stage of its grandest rites. Carved sphinxes and ram-statues line much of its length; it links the river temples through Qelt-Ge and carries the god across the water on its two stone causeways. On the high festivals the image of Uqa'â is carried in procession down the avenue and across to the island.

The year turns on two of those processions. At the midsummer **Festival of the Coming-Forth** the dawn breaks straight down the eastern road; at the midwinter **Festival of the Going-Down** the sun sets straight into the necropolis. The whole capital turns out to watch the god born and the god die along his own road, and the sightlines are kept clear by a law older than memory.

## Flood Season

For one season each year, [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]] (the Inundation), the river rises and remakes the city, and Galezkara is built to expect it. The flood is a slow swelling over weeks, foretold by the stars and watched on the Measure, and the whole calendar is arranged around it. As the water rises, the channels around the island swell, the lower harbors go under and shift, and the low quarters of Gar-Qethar and the flood margins are partly drowned. Their people move out every year as a matter of routine—up to kin, into the temple precincts, or into reed shelters on the high tells—and repair or rebuild their mudbrick once the water falls. Mud is the material of the living city because the floodplain demands an impermanent one.

The stone city stands above it all. The temple platforms, the granary dikes, the great causeways and the Sacred Island are raised clear of the highest flood, so that at the peak the old high quarters and the island stand like islands above a brown sea. The water lapping the outer courts of the Great Temple of Uqa'â re-enacts the primeval mound alone above the waters of creation, and the city treats it as the holiest sight of the year. The two bridge-causeways of the Sun's Road stand high above the greatest flood on long ramped approaches, so the solstice processions cross dry-shod above the inundation. Lesser crossings are pontoon bridges, floated out and drawn back with the water, and where those fail the city falls back on sacred ferrying.

_Flood season is building season._ The fields are under water and farm work is impossible, so the surplus labor of the selat rebuilds the mud city and raises the monuments, and the great stone blocks for tombs and temples are floated on flood-barges right up to the desert edge, which the water reaches only now. The necropolis never floods: it sits on the high desert shelf beyond the water's reach, with the death-road causeway climbing from the river onto dry rock, so that the dead lie forever above the inundation and the living forever below it. Over centuries the flood marks even the stone. Salt creeps up from the damp into the bases of the monuments, and silt settles unevenly beneath them, cracking obelisks and tilting colossi that a hundred reigns then labor to shore up.

## Governance and Order {#governance-and-order}

Galezkara answers to three powers at once, and the friction between them is the city's daily politics.

- The Gar-Aû sits above all, and the throne's presence on Yath-Telgu makes the capital the one place in the empire governed directly rather than through a distant Halzi'a.
- **The Zeglar**, chief minister and supreme judge, runs the civil administration of the city and of the empire through the ministry of [[affiliation-garzeglar|Gar-Zeglar]], from his bureaus in Gar-Medjatozo. He is in all but name the mayor of the capital, and the second man in the realm.
- The great temples cut across both. Each major temple precinct is a near-sovereign enclave that owns its lands, feeds its own people, keeps its own guards and courts, and answers to its high priest before it answers to the city. A fugitive who reaches temple ground, or a dispute that touches a god's estate, passes out of the Zeglar's hands entirely.

The **Meglay**, the standing city guard, keeps order in the public city. It is separate from the army and separate again from the necropolis guard across the water. Its men walk the quarters and the quays, guard the gates of the walled enclosures after dark, chase thieves, break up brawls and double as the fire-watch; their posts stand in every district, the largest by the markets and the harbor where trouble gathers.

Justice runs in tiers below the Zeglar. A magistrate's court, the local Genzet, sits in each quarter and hears market disputes, debts and petty crime under the eye of [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]]; graver matters of blood and theft rise to the higher courts; and anything touching a temple, a noble house or the crown leaves the civic system altogether. Here as everywhere the law bends to the right name and the right purse. In a city this closely watched, the wrong enemies are as dangerous as the wrong crimes.

## The People {#the-people}

The capital's near half-million sort into a steep pyramid. At the top a thin crust of priests, nobles, senior scribes and court officials lives in the walled mansions of Zu-Leri and on the temple estates. Below them is a broad middle of artisans, traders, lesser scribes and temple servants, and at the base the great mass of laborers, porters, boatmen, market folk and the poor of the low warrens. The one ladder between the layers is the scribal school: a clever child of any birth who masters [[skill-qalzscrscrpt|the people's hand]] can rise into the administration, and the capital draws the ambitious of the whole empire to try. Women hold property, trade, plead in the Genzet and serve as priestesses here as everywhere in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]], and the capital has more women of independent means than anywhere else in the realm.

Galezkara is also the most foreign-touched place in the interior. [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Haradian]] and [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] factors keep walled compounds along the harbor in Amqel-Ger. [[place-helionis|Helionite]] scholars lodge near the temple libraries, among the few outsiders ever admitted to them. Southern traders, horse-dealers from the western grazing country, and the endless tribute delegations and pilgrims throng the markets in season. A little apart from all of them live the people of the Wazulet—the embalmers, coffin-makers and tomb-trade folk who cross each day to serve the dead at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]], and whom the rest of the city treats with the wary respect owed to those who handle corpses for a living.

## Water, Bread, and Fire

A city on the greatest river in the world still minds its water. The river runs thick with silt, and fouler where the city's drains meet it, so Galezkara drinks from wells, cisterns and settling-jars, and the wealthy from spring-fed conduits. In the heat of low water the poor quarters go thirsty while the river slides past their doors. The flood that renews the fields fouls the wells when it crests, and the weeks of the inundation are counted an unhealthy season.

Bread comes from upstream. The grain of the [[affiliation-selatnlghztn|Selat of Anlagh-Zetûn]] and the upper river is barged down, stored in the city's great granaries, and given out in part as a dole to temple dependents, the flood-season work-gangs and the registered poor. As in any capital, the dole is statecraft as much as charity: a hungry Galezkara is a dangerous one, and whoever controls the granaries holds a lever on the city's peace—a lesson written in stone across the water on the cursed road.

Fire is a lesser hazard here than in the timber cities of the west, because mudbrick does not burn. Roofs, market stalls, workshops and above all the grain-dust of the granaries do, and a blaze in the crowded craft quarters of Lut-Lemu can run from lane to lane before the Meglay's fire-gangs and the walled enclosures between districts pen it in.

## The Underworld

The capital's signature crime is tomb robbery, an old and organized trade with its roots in the necropolis itself. The people best placed to plunder the dead are the guilds and priesthoods sworn to tend them, and a corrupt keeper, a bribed guard, or a craftsman who never forgot a tomb's hidden door is worth more to a robbing ring than any outside thief. The yield—grave-gold melted and recast, amulets, funerary papyri, "recovered" stone—moves through fences in the markets and out through the harbor with the foreign factors, and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] money-changers ask few questions of old gold. Around it runs the ordinary underworld of any great port capital: smugglers dodging the temple and crown monopolies, dock-gang protection rackets, and forgers who supply the courts with false deeds and the tombs with false provenances.

Below the common crime are the forbidden cults, the rites the temples have outlawed and driven underground, with the worship of the chaos-serpent [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'âthis]] foremost among them. The Meglay and the temple-inquisitors hunt them without rest, and most of what they find is harmless desperation. The one the priesthood of Uqa'â fears in its bones is the small, hidden sect that means to break the order of the dead outright: the cult that seeks the cursed road across the water and would wake the [[being-falseuqaa|False Uqa'â]] (see [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]). The temple keeps the existence of any such cult a close secret, and it is the reason the order to kill on sight on the cursed road has never once been relaxed.

## Notable Features

- [[place-yathtelgu|The Sacred Island (Yath-Telgu)]]—the primeval mound, holding the palace, the Great Temple of Uqa'â and the Measure, behind the empire's only true fortified wall
- [[place-waluqaa|The Sun's Road (Wal-Uqa'â)]]—the solstice-aligned axis running through the whole city and across both bridges, its horizon sightlines protected by ancient law
- The two solstice festivals—the midsummer **Coming-Forth** and the midwinter **Going-Down**, when the sun rises and sets straight down the god's road
- The living city—a grown half-circle of mudbrick tells, walled enclosures and named quarters
- The necropolis—the small, strict stone rectangle on the western desert shelf, ranked along the death-road
- The central bureaucracy and scribal schools—the treasuries, the Genzet'Palu, and the road up for a clever child of any birth
- [[place-waligelu|Wal-Igelu]]—the Drowned Way, a dead river channel turned into a chain of noble pleasure gardens
- The Meglay and the Zeglar's courts—the city guard and the tiered justice that keep order in a capital of half a million
- The tomb-robbing underworld—organized plunder of the dead, the black market in grave goods, and the hunted cults
