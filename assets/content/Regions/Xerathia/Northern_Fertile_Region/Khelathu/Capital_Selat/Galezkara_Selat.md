## Overview

"Count the barges on the grain quay and then count the mouths," a granary clerk tells a new tally-boy at the [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] docks. "There are two and a half million of them in this selat, and the barges have to keep up."

**Galezkara Selat** is the land of the [[affiliation-capitalselat|Capital Selat]] of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]], and it lies within the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu Region]]. It holds the palace and the throne, the central bureaucracy, the greatest temples of the [[affiliation-khelathpnthn|Khelâthi Pantheon]], the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]] council, and, across the river on the western bank, the royal necropolis where the dynasties of three thousand years lie buried. With some two and a half million people it is the most populous selat in the empire, and the densest concentration of power, wealth and people in [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]].

## Character

The selat is a city and its hinterland on a vast scale. The metropolis of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] sits at its heart, a dense belt of temple-estates, workshop-towns and market-villages surrounds it, and the silent necropolis-cities of the dead lie on the desert margin opposite.

## Economy

The Capital Selat consumes more than it produces, and is proud of it. Grain barged down from the [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|Upper River Selatu]] and the produce of its own dense hinterland feed it. The tribute and taxes of the other provinces fill its treasuries. An immense population earns its keep in the work of governing, worshipping, building and burying.

The great temple-complexes are economic enterprises in their own right, owning farmland across the selat and employing thousands. The mortuary industry, which takes in the embalmers, tomb-builders, funerary artists, priests and the grave-goods trades that serve the royal necropolis, sustains whole districts. The monuments, built on through every flood season, make up the largest public works program in the known world.

## Notable Features

- The imperial palace and the throne of the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Aû]], at the heart of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]
- The great temples—the cult of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] that defines official doctrine chief among them
- The royal necropolis on the western bank—pyramids and rock-cut tombs of three thousand years of dynasties

## Settlements

The selat's settlement pattern is a single great metropolis ringed by temple-towns, workshop-towns and the necropolis-cities of the western bank.

The metropolis:

- [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] (~450,000)—the imperial city and seat of the Gar-Aû, by far the largest city in the empire: palace, great temples, bureaus, and the polyglot crowd of a capital that draws in the whole realm.
- [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] (~4,000)—the sacred island in the river at its center, with the **Great Temple of Uqa'â** and the palace.

The bank of the dead and the temple-towns:

- [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]] (~40,000)—"the **Sacred Land**," the necropolis-city on the western bank: the embalmers, tomb-builders, funerary artists and mortuary priests who serve the royal dead, and the tomb-workers' settlements behind them.
- [[place-khelautithnt|Khelaut-Ithnet]]—the burial fields on either side of Zu-Gezer, where everyone below the throne is laid to rest.
- [[place-garlegulu|Gar-Legulu]] (~30,000)—a temple-estate town east of the capital: the granaries, workshops and tenant-villages of one of the great cult-temples, a town that exists to feed and serve its god.

The hinterland villages: the dense farm-and-market country around the capital holds scores of villages, many of 500–3,000 people. Among them are [[place-iuguleri|Iugu-Leri]], [[place-garzekhemu|Gar-Zekhemu]] (180), [[place-zmabelgen|Zma-Belgen]], [[place-khelutathen|Khelut-Athen]], [[place-yathzabes|Yath-Zabes]], [[place-garger|Gar-Ger]], [[place-sheluzu|Shelu-Zu]], [[place-anlaghthawu|Anlagh-Thawu]], [[place-amqelagefu|Amqel-Agefu]] and [[place-lutregyt|Lut-Regyt]]. Each renders grain, beer and labor to the capital and the temples, and each lies within a half-day's barge of the great city's markets.
