## Overview

Khelzuret is the great mortuary selat of the [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|Upper River Selatu]]—the place where [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] tends its dead. The empire's largest pilgrimage flows here, for to be buried in the sacred ground of Khelzuret—or merely to make the journey and lay an offering—is held to weigh favorably when the heart is set against the feather of [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]]. The selat is not large or rich in the worldly sense, but in the economy of the afterlife it is the capital of the world. The land it holds is [[place-khelzuretslt|Khelzuret Selat]].

## Character

Khelzuret is solemn, ancient, and wholly given over to death—not morbidly, for the Khelâthi see death as renewal, but completely. The whole life of the selat organizes around the dead: the embalmers in their workshops on the west bank, the tomb-builders and quarrymen cutting and raising the houses of eternity, the funerary artists painting the walls that _are_ the afterlife made real, the coffin-makers and amulet-carvers and the keepers of the great rolls of mortuary spells. Above them all stand the Thâz'Lekhau of the mortuary temples, who command estates and labor-gangs rivaling the [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]]'s own—and the central political fact of the selat is the wary sharing of power between the hereditary Halzi'a and the priesthoods of the dead. Pilgrims throng the temple-city year-round; the festival-calendar is dense with rites for the departed; and the whole place lives, paradoxically, very well off of dying well.

## For the Worldbuilder

Khelzuret is the empire's uncanny heart, and it plays like nowhere else—a whole society organized around death, with all the wealth, secrecy, and dread that implies. Run it as a place of sacred industry and buried danger: the embalmers' guild-secrets, the tomb-workers' closed brotherhood at [[place-zelqar|Zel-Qar]], the rivalry between the Halzi'a and the mortuary priesthoods, the endless war between the tomb-builders who hide the dead and the tomb-robbers who plunder them. The funerary magic here is real and the curses guarding the tombs are not always idle superstition. Adventure runs from the mundane—pilgrim-fleecing, grave-goods fraud, stolen mortuary papyri—to the genuinely eerie: what walks the necropolis after dark, what the deepest tombs were built to keep in as much as out, and what a desperate or greedy party finds when it goes down into the houses of eternity. What is abundant here is ritual, stone, and the dead; what is scarce is levity, and any tomb that has not already been robbed at least once.

## Commerce and Currency

Khelzuret uses the [[lore-aukhlthcrncy|Aû'Khelâthu Currency]]. Much of its wealth moves as temple-account and pilgrim-offering rather than open trade, banked through the mortuary temples' own treasuries and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] houses that serve them; the grave-goods and amulet trades, by contrast, deal briskly in struck copper and silver.
