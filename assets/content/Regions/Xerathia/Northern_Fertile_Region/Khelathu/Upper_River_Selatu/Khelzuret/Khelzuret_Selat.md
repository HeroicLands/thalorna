## Overview

"Keep to the path and touch nothing," the watch-priest of an endowed tomb tells a party of pilgrims at the western landing. "The ground above a tomb is mine to keep, and most of what I keep it from is hunger."

**Khelzuret Selat** is the land of the [[affiliation-selatkhelzrt|Selat of Khelzuret]], one of the [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|Upper River Selatu]] of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]], and lies within the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu Region]]. It is built around the temple-city of [[place-khelzuret|Khelzuret]] on the river's east bank and the vast necropolis that faces it across the water on the west, and it holds the empire's funerary civilization: its embalmers, its tomb-builders, its funerary artists, and the priesthoods of [[lore-wethurdty|Wethûr]] and [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]], lords of the passage into death.

## Character

The river divides the selat into the country of the living and the country of the dead. The east bank holds the temple-city, the pilgrim-road and the farm villages that feed it. Across the water, on the dry western shore, lie the necropolis, the embalmers' workshops, the tomb-builders' settlements and the cliffs the tomb-builders quarry.

Everything on the west bank is built against thieves, and the wealthy build in layers. A great tomb is hidden first, then barred, then trapped, then cursed and warded, and last of all, for those who can pay, guarded. Endowed watch-priests keep the ground above. In the deepest chambers the bound dead keep the post: zaglu-soldiers, the [[being-damut|ḍumaṭu]], that wake to a broken seal and do only what their binding wrote, and revenant-wardens, the [[being-tereb|ṭerebu]], that keep their wits and their station in the dark. The funerary magic here is real, and the curses on the tombs are not always idle. The war between the builders and the robbers has run for three thousand years with neither side winning, and it is fought hardest in this selat and in the burial fields of [[place-khelautithnt|Khelaut-Ithnet]].

## Economy

The mortuary industry is the economy. Embalming, tomb construction and decoration, the making of coffins, grave-goods, amulets and funerary papyri, and the pilgrimage trade employ the overwhelming majority of the selat's people and draw wealth from every corner of the empire, because the dead of distant selatu are sent here for proper preparation and the rich pay to be buried, or at least commemorated, in sacred ground. Around that core the selat farms enough flood-silt grain to feed itself, quarries the stone its tomb-builders need from the western cliffs, and runs the natron and resin trades the embalmers depend on. It exports the one service nobody can finally do without.

## Notable Features

- The temple-city of [[place-khelzuret|Khelzuret]] and its great temples of Wethûr and Hezmuîri
- The western-bank necropolis—the empire's most sacred burial ground
- The embalmers' workshops and the tomb-builders' settlements of the west bank
- The natron flats and resin-stores that supply the mummification trade
- The pilgrim-road and the festival of the dead that draws the whole empire

## Settlements

- [[place-khelzuret|Khelzuret]] (~90,000)—the selat capital and the empire's great temple-city of the dead: the mortuary temples, the pilgrim-markets, the embalmers' and coffin-makers' quarters, and the seat of the Halzi'a in uneasy proximity to the Thâz'Lekhau.
- [[place-zelqar|Zel-Qar]] (~6,000)—"the **Place of Truth**," the walled tomb-workers' settlement on the west bank: a closed company-town of master-craftsmen, draftsmen and stonecutters who cut and paint the great tombs, hereditary in their trades and proud of their secrets.
- The pilgrim and farm villages: small communities of 500–2,500 line the east bank and the pilgrim-road, among them [[place-garzekri|Gar-Zekri]], [[place-lutanpuqa|Lut-Anpuqa]], [[place-yathmulu|Yath-Mulu]] and [[place-zmagezer|Zma-Gezer]]. They lodge pilgrims, farm the river margin and serve the endless traffic of the dead and their mourners.
