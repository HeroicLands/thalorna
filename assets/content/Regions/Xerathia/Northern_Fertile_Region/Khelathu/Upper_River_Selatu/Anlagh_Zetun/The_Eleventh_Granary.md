"Ten you open and one you do not," a granary steward tells a new tally-clerk as they walk the row of domes. "Count them all the same, and when the sum will not close, do not look for the error in the eleventh."

The **Eleventh Granary** stands at the end of the granary row of [[place-anlaghzetun|Anlagh-Zetûn]], a dome like the others, with its door bricked and plastered over. It is a _zelqezat_ (a blood-field, "the place of a battle left unread"), the second in Khelâthi memory, and it is a withering one: the grain around it spoils.

## How It Was Made

In 2378 ST (268 AF) the Halzi'a of the selat broke the god-king's guard at the granary gates ([[lore-sealedgran|the Battle of the Sealed Granaries]]). Several thousand lay dead between the domes, the guard and the levies together, and the living had no grain to pay the embalmers. One granary had been emptied to feed the guard, and the dead of both sides were thrown into it and shut in, with no rite said and no name entered. The selat meant to open it when the rites could be paid for. It never has.

## What Is There

The field is a **withering, contained**. The kind comes out of what the dead died wanting, and they died wanting grain: in the granaries beside the eleventh the stored wheat spoils first, the dry domes breed rot and weevil, and the tallies never balance. The sickness does not spread past the granary row. The wheatland around the city is as rich as any in the basin.

Its thinness is **moderate**, and it follows hunger and not the season. In a full year it is a bricked dome with a bad name. In a lean year the wall sweats, and the guard on the row hears grain poured inside. Anyone who lingers meets the dead's one fixation, and sleepers within sight of the dome dream the gates: the crush, the hunger and the hands. Nothing on the field kills by its own hand. A clerk who works the row night after night can lose his sleep and his nerve, and the worst cases are scribes who hear a count that is not there.

## The Bread for the Gate

Each anniversary the selat throws bread over the wall of the Eleventh Granary, and the festival has its name from that: the **Bread for the Gate**. The Halzi'a's house of **Anlaghet'Zaru** keeps the granary shut by order, and the bread is the house's offering at the edge. The poor of [[place-amqelulegez|Amqelu-Legez]] eat what lands outside.

A Reading held for the unnamed at the edge quiets the field for the night it is held. It cannot end it, because no roll of the dead was kept and a Reading closes on a name.

## Who Goes

- **Auditors** whose numbers will not close, and who come to find what the row is hiding.
- **Thieves** who believe the god-king's grain is still inside.
- **The starving,** in a famine year, who have heard the dome is full.
- **The Halzi'a's wardens,** who walk the wall and keep the door bricked.

## Hooks

- **The hungry year.** A lean year presses a Halzi'a to open the Eleventh Granary for the grain he is sure it holds, and a party is hired either to open it or to keep it shut.
- **The extra granary.** A tally-scribe keeps finding a twelfth entry in the roll, and the extra granary has no door.
- **The name.** A family proves an ancestor lay in the dome and wants him read aloud, which is the one thing that might settle part of the field.
