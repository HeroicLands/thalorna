## Overview

"Count the domes from the gate," a granary steward tells a new tally-clerk. "Every one of them is full, or it is somebody's fault."

**Anlagh-Zetûn**, "the living storehouse," is the capital of the [[affiliation-selatnlghztn|Selat of Anlagh-Zetûn]], an inland city of about 80,000 set among the richest wheatland in the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]]. Its skyline is a forest of granary-domes, some held by the crown and some by the temples, and its markets trade wheat and barley by the boatload. Its busiest halls are not palaces but the survey-courts and tally-bureaus where the flood-fields are measured and the harvests assessed.

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] holds court here: a conservative, land-proud lord whose power rests on the bread he controls, and one of the empire's quiet kingmakers. Over the city presides the great temple of [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]], goddess of order and right measure, which suits a place whose whole life is survey, tally and the honest filling of stores.

Anlagh-Zetûn is not a glamorous city, and it is one of the most important in the empire. When the harvest fails elsewhere, its granaries decide who eats.

## Notable Features

- The great granaries that store the empire's reserve against famine
- The grain-markets and the survey-and-assize courts that measure the land
- The temple of Qe'âret, goddess of order and right measure
- The seat of the granary-Halzi'a, one of the empire's quiet kingmakers
