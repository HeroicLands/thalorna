"Mind the third step, it has been loose since my father's day," the steward of the Halzi'a's house says to a visitor at the foot of the scarp, and puts a hand under the visitor's elbow as though the visitor were old. The **Lenen-Nezut Ways** are three tombs on the desert edge above the town, and the steward's house is all that holds them up.

## The Ways

A single short road climbs from the cultivated edge to a shelf of pale rock above [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]]. It crosses the shelf once, and the three royal tombs of the house of **Geze'el'Anlaghu** stand along it, each cut into the rock of the scarp and fronted with a chapel of mud-brick. The road is laid out in imitation of a dynasty's Way at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]: a crossing-road off a spine, with the tombs of the Gar-Aûu ranked along it. There is no spine and no necropolis for it to cross; the Way is the whole of the place.

## How It Was Made

The three tombs are those of the Gar-Aûu who reigned from Lenen-Nezut for sixty-nine years and were buried at home, outside the row at the capital ([[lore-thronelnz|the Throne at Lenen-Nezut]]). The first was cut for Zab'el'Psaqa I, crowned in 1462 ST (649 BF), and the last holds Zab'el'Psaqa III, killed in the wheat at Iqeru in 1531 ST (580 BF) ([[lore-twothrones|the War of the Two Thrones]]). The road was meant to be the first of a long row, and it is the whole of it, because the house that built it lost its throne before it could lay a fourth tomb.

## What Is There Now

The crown keeps no endowment here. The mortuary priests of the capital's necropolis are paid to tend the Ways of the houses that ruled from Galezkara, and the Ways of Lenen-Nezut are not in their ledgers. The Halzi'a's house, descended from the three, keeps them, and keeps them as a house with a small estate can: a steward, a few families of tomb-servants, a mason hired for a season when the roof of a chapel comes down. The gilding on the outer doorways is gone, the lintel of the third tomb has cracked, and wind drifts sand into the second chapel faster than the servants sweep it. The wards are intact. The names are cut deep, and the house reads them aloud once a year.

## Who Goes

- **The house itself,** on its ancestors' feasts, with the Halzi'a at its head and a procession of the household behind him.
- **Agents from the capital,** who come to see whether the house is restoring its tombs, because a house that restores its tombs may be preparing a claim.
- **Robbers,** who find the Ways less guarded than any royal tomb in the empire and less rich than they hope, since the three Gar-Aûu were buried with what a selat could afford.
- **Scholars,** from the bureaus and from abroad, who ask to see where a Gar-Aû was laid outside the necropolis.

## Hooks

- **The quiet restoration.** In the year the court asks about the succession, the Halzi'a's house begins to repair the Ways, hiring masons from three selatu so that none of them knows the full extent. A crown inspector wants to know who ordered the work, and what it means.
- **The sealed chapel.** The second tomb's chapel has been closed since before the steward's grandfather, and the house will not say why. Robbers have started to ask in the town.
- **The fourth chamber.** An heir of the house, looking for proof of descent, finds the register of what was buried with the three. It lists a fourth chamber.
