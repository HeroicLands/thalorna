The surveyors of [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]] walk every field in the selat each spring, re-establishing the lines the flood has erased, and entering what they find. They do not walk **Zel-Halzi**. It is a stretch of the west-bank floodplain below the town, and it has no line in any roll: the **Unmeasured Field**, the first _zelqezat_ (a blood-field, "the place of a battle left unread") in Khelâthi memory.

## How It Was Made

In the Season of Emergence of 1612 ST (499 BF) the hill-nomads of the west destroyed the last army of Galezkara here, and the Gar-Aû Amqel'Uzner I died with it ([[lore-thaulgau|the Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]). The victors forbade the rites. Thousands of men lay unwrapped on the silt through the Season of Harvest, and the next flood covered them. Nobody entered their names, and nobody has found a way to enter them since.

## What It Is

Zel-Halzi is a field of **recurrence**, and a moderate one. The battle does not stay in the past. In the weeks after the flood goes down in Gelet, when the ground is wet and planted fields run to its edge, the army is heard on it: the scream of men at their last work, a column calling a road home, the sound of a line breaking. It is worst on the anniversary night of the rout, and the Reading held then quiets that night and no more.

By day it is a flat stretch of silt grown with thorn and rough grass. By night the visions come on anyone who lingers, and sleepers within sight of it dream the battle. A traveler who crosses it in the dark comes away with sights that do not leave at dawn, and a person who stays too long, or comes back night after night, can leave with sleep that will not come and a mind that hears the field in every quiet. The field kills nobody by its own hand. A survey rod staked on it overnight is found moved in the morning, and the surveyors do not stake one twice.

The hunters that gather where unwrapped dead lie do not cross on ground this thin. The dead are all that are on it: men looking for their names.

## How the Selat Treats It

Nobody farms it, and nobody owns it. Because it is entered in no roll, its render has been remitted for twelve centuries, and the remission is the only thing the crown has ever done about it. The road bends round, and the villages on its edge shut their doors on the field's side after dark.

Each Gelet a priest of [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]] from Lenen-Nezut stands at its edge and holds a Reading for the unnamed. A Reading closes on a name, and these have none, so the field is as it was the morning after. A family that can prove an ancestor lay here and bring his name to the edge is the one thing that settles any part of it. In twelve centuries that has happened a handful of times.

The Halzi'a house of **Tha'Ulgau**, the kin of the conquerors, settled on the western pastures in 1671 ST (440 BF), makes the yearly offering at the edge. Its ancestors owe it, and the house has kept the debt as it keeps its horses.

## Who Goes

- **The Tha'Ulgau house**, once a year, with an offering and no escort.
- **A priest of Hezmuîri** with a Reading, and the families who bring a name to the edge.
- **Necromancers**, who want the unclaimed souls. The circle of [[place-akhozamenlaa|Akhoza-Menlaa]] is the one the temples fear.
- **Robbers**, after the bronze of an army no one stripped.
- **Families** whose ancestor was a Galezkara soldier and never came home.

## Hooks

- **The boundary suit.** Two estates on either side of the field go to law over a line that only the field itself can settle. The court needs someone to measure it, and the only season in which it can be measured is Gelet, when it is worst.
- **The account.** A Tha'Ulgau heir cannot be confirmed in his seat until the house's debt at the field is closed with a name, and the name his house needs is one nobody in the west remembers.
- **The soul-hunters.** The Akhoza-Menlaa has been taking men from the edge of the field. The priest of Hezmuîri who holds the Reading has missed three in a year.
