The marsh fishermen of [[place-gadjumuluslt|Gadju-Mûlu]] pole their reed boats round a reach of open water and will not tie up in it. Under the surface, a hand or two below the keel, a wall runs for the length of three boats, and the face of it is carved. The fishermen call it nothing. The hunters of the [[affiliation-zeghetnelgu|Zeghet'Nelgu]] call it the **Drowned Stones**.

## What Is There

Stone walls lie beneath the water in the southern marsh, inside the marsh country the Zeghet'Nelgu hold by charter. They are half-submerged: at the lowest water of the year the tops of two stand above the mud, and in the flood they are a hand's depth down and visible only on a still, bright morning. The faces carry rows of symbols that are not Khelâthi. They are not [[skill-khelthzscrpt|the sacred hand]] or the people's hand or any script a scribe at [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]] can read, and no one in the temples has proposed what they say.

At night, lights move under the water along the lines of the walls. They are seen from a boat poled quietly and gone when it is poled nearer. Fishermen tell of them, the hunters have seen them, and the temples have not been told.

The country around is the hippopotamus marsh: reed beds, mud islands and channels that shift with every flood. Crocodiles and hippopotami are the day's danger, and they are enough. A boat that goes after the stones goes with a boat-steerer who knows the channels, or it does not come back.

## What the Khelâthi Say

Nothing, as a matter of doctrine. The temples hold that what cannot be counted is not history, and they do not date the stones or argue about their age. The stones are on no survey roll, because no one farms the marsh floor.

The hunters of the Zeghet'Nelgu are the ones who know the place, and their Steersman has told them to forget it. The hunt comes first.

## Who Goes

- **The Khetai hunters**, who pass it on the way to the herds and keep it off their routes.
- **Marsh fishermen**, who keep out of the reach and say something in the stones is driving the animals north.
- **A scholar** who wants proof that the valley had a history before the count, and who will pay a boatman more than the boat is worth.
- **A priest** who wants the carvings broken before anyone copies them.

## Hooks

- **The herd.** The hippopotamus herds have moved into the farm country, and the fishermen say something in the stones woke. The hunters want to know whether the herds went because of the wall or in spite of it.
- **The scholar's boat.** A scholar of the temple libraries hires a crew to take rubbings of the carvings at low water. The Khetai hunter who guides them has been told to bring the boat back empty.
- **The priest's order.** A priest of [[affiliation-rethsaar|Reth'Sa'âr]] asks the hunters to chip the symbols from the visible stones. The hunters can do it in a morning, and they do not know what else will notice.
