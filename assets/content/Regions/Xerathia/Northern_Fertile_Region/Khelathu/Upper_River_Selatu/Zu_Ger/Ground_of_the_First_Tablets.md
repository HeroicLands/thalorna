"Plow round the stones," a farmer of [[place-zugernome|Zu-Ger]] says to his son at the end of the furrow, "and do not ask what is under them." The stones stand in a row at the foot of the desert edge, and above them, on the shelf where the cultivated land gives out, lies the **Ground of the First Tablets**.

## The Ground

It is the burial-ground of the first eleven Gar-Aûu, the First House and **Azlet'Qûtu**, who ruled from the Great Land for a thousand years and are entered in the king-list by a name and a length ([[lore-akhfirsttab|the First Tablets]]). The houses that ruled from [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] lie in the necropolis at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]], and these do not. Their tombs were never moved, and when the court crossed to the mound in 1006 ST (1105 BF) the kings of the Great Land stayed where they lay ([[lore-crossmound|the Crossing to the Mound]]).

## What Is There

Low flat-topped tombs of mudbrick and rough stone, weathered nearly to the level of the shelf they stand on, in rows that follow the desert edge. The oldest are shapeless mounds. The more recent have a recognizable face, a door sealed with a block, and a lintel cut in the sacred hand. It is not a place for splendor. The oldest tombs hold little gold, because the age they belong to had little to bury, and the temple of [[affiliation-hezmuiri|Hezmuîri]] at [[place-tjegu|Tjegu]] keeps watch over the whole of it. The guard is thinner than the one at Zu-Gezer, and the age of the ground does most of the work.

Below the shelf run the marker stones, a long row of upright slabs worn smooth, and no one in Zu-Ger can say who set them or what they mark. The farmers plough round them as their fathers did and keep a handful of rules about them: no fire beside them, no digging within a plough's length, and no gleaning of the crop that grows at their feet.

## Who Goes

- **Pilgrims**, who come to be near the oldest kings.
- **Foreign scholars**, who come to count the First Tablets on their fingers and are shown as much of the ground as the temple of Hezmuîri decides.
- **Robbers**, who are after the tablets and not the metal. The inscribed tablets of the age are the oldest writing in the valley, and a dealer in Galezkara pays more for one than for a gold collar.
- **The temple of Hezmuîri**, which says the ground is the god's to keep and the Gar-Aû's to visit.

## Hooks

- **The extra chambers.** A scholar who has surveyed the ground finds more burial chambers than eleven Gar-Aûu need. The Keepers will not discuss it.
- **The stone with a name.** A farmer's plough turns up a marker stone carved with a name the king-list does not hold, and the farmer wants to know whether to bury it again.
- **The sealed tablet.** A robber sells a tablet in the capital that the temple at Tjegu says it never lost.

:::secret
For the GM, one answer the chambers can have: the round reigns of 150, 120 and 110 years cover more than eleven kings, and the tablets folded several into one name. The extra chambers are theirs. The Keepers will not discuss it because the god's list does not need correcting.
:::
