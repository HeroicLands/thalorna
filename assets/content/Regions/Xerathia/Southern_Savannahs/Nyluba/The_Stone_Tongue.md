The Nyáluba hold the account of **Mvura wa Kambezi** and the Stone Tongue in two forms, and keep the two apart. The first is the griots' recital of what the Bombwe griots entered into the record of the Great Indaba. It is short, it states which clan holds each claim, and it is either right or wrong; a [[skill-griot|griot]] recites it before people who already know it. The second is the story told at the fire. It belongs to no record, it may be told well or badly, and every teller tells it differently. The tradition's rule that a griot is not a storyteller is the line between them. Neither form says what the Stone Tongue is for.

: As the griots recite it {#recital}

> This is entered in the record of the Indaba. The griots of the baobab received it. It is recited as it was received.
>
> Mvura, of the Kambezi, spirit-speaker of the crocodile. Her line is held by the griot of the Kambezi. It is not recited here, because her line is not what was entered.
>
> In her initiation she walked into an Old Kraal, and she came out of it speaking the Stone Tongue.
>
> Of the Stone Tongue, this is entered: no living Nyáluban understands it. The elders of the Ngonzi say that it was the speech of the people who built in stone. The record holds that the Ngonzi elders say this. The record does not hold that it is so.
>
> The Ngonzi confirmed the report.
>
> Whose dead the stone-builders are is not entered here. The Ngonzi hold one answer and the griots of the baobab hold another, and the record holds only that the two differ.
>
> What the Stone Tongue is for is not entered.
>
> That is the whole of it. A griot who adds to it has not told it better. He has told it wrong.

: As it is told at the fire {#fireside}

> Nobody at this fire was there, so you get it the way I tell it, and the way I tell it is not the record. If you want the record, go and sit in front of a griot, and mind what you say while you are sitting there.
>
> You know the Old Kraals. No, you don't. None of us do, unless we are Ngonzi, and the Ngonzi don't let anyone in. Not a Mvuzi, not a Sengala, not a Bombwe with every clan's fathers in his mouth. Each Kraal has its guardian, and the guardian says no. Hold on to that; you'll want it in a moment.
>
> So picture one. Stone laid on stone with nothing between, curving round on itself like a cattle-pen that forgot to stop. Platforms raised up out of the grass where nobody stands. And inside are the builders. Not their bones. Them. The Ngonzi say so, and the rest of us say so too, because who argues with the people who live next door?
>
> Now picture a Kambezi girl. River-born, reed-canoe and slow water. And in her initiation she walks into one of those.
>
> How? I don't know. Nobody tells that part. If someone at another fire tells you how, ask him who told him.
>
> She walks in. She walks out. And when she opens her mouth, what comes out is not Kambezi. It is not anything. Not river-talk, not drum-talk, not any of the tongues the Sengala bring back from the north. The Stone Tongue. Nobody alive understands a word of it. Not one word.
>
> The Ngonzi elders said: that is how the builders spoke. Then they said nothing else, which is how the Ngonzi say most things.
>
> Here is the part I like. The Ngonzi say those builders are their own grandfathers' grandfathers. The baobab griots, if you push them, say they were somebody else's. And now a girl from the river comes out speaking their tongue. So whose grandfathers were they? Don't look at me. I only tell it.
>
> And the griots took it into the record. Think what that means. That part isn't a story any more. It is like a boundary, or a debt. It is so.
>
> She is the senior spirit-speaker of the Kambezi now. And what is it for, a tongue nobody speaks? Everybody asks that. Nobody will say. Mind, I don't say nobody knows. I say nobody will say.
>
> Somebody tells it better than I do. That is allowed. It is a story. The record is the record, and the record is shorter.
