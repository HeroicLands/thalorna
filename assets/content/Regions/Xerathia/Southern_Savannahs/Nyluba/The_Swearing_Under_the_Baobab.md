The Swearing Under the Baobab is the memory-song of the [Long Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"}'s swearing, held by the [[affiliation-nyalblion|Lion Totem]] and kept by the griot of the [Ruwozi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}. It is praise poetry in the griot's call and the hearers' answer: the griot names, and those gathered return the name. A memory-song is evidence as well as praise, so the hearers' answers also serve as a check, and the song is sung before people who already know it. The [[skill-griot|griot]] holds it to its received length, keeps [Nyoli Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s sentence whole, and says of the lion only that it was witness. Of the catastrophe that drove the five clans together, the song keeps what the Lobwezi griots hold and nothing more. In the text below, the indented lines in italics are the answer of those gathered.

: The Swearing Under the Baobab {#song}

Transcription of an oral memory-song.

::: {.verse form=praise lang=en work=the-swearing-under-the-baobab-1}
Listen, children of the five clans./
._We hear you, keeper._/
Listen, lion tooth and braided hair./
._We hear you._/
They placed it in the mouth. I carry it./
I carry it at its given length,/
no longer and no shorter./
._Carry it. We know it too._

In the past, nine generations,/
count them through the braid./
._Nine, never one fewer._/
In the lion's country, in the middle grass,/
a baobab stood, already old./
._It stood before the five._

Five clans arrived. They were no friends./
Each kept its cattle feud warm./
One clan was broken, then another,/
with the others watching./
._One clan, then another. The others watched._

What attacked them?/
._Worn smooth, worn smooth._/
The baobab's griots tell it this way:/
a host came south from the grass country's middle,/
taking cattle, taking women, taking lives,/
making no distinction among the three./
That host has gone. No cattle remained behind it./
._Worn smooth, and gone._

Who came from the river?/
._The crocodile came._/
[Kolembozi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, reed canoe and slow brown water,/
naming the crocodile by mother and grandmother.

Who came from the highlands?/
._The eagle came._/
[Ngonzi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, red escarpment and gold in river sand,/
alone among the five in working stone.

Who came from the edge of the trees?/
._The leopard came._/
[Sengala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} speaks many languages, knows the northern road,/
and tells less than it knows.

Who came from the still water?/
._The baobab's people came. The tree does not travel._/
Lobwezi, grain and well and slow speech,/
carrying the five clans' fathers in their mouths.

Who stood upon their own ground?/
._The lion stood._/
Ruwozi, cattle and challenge in the open,/
first of five: the tree was theirs to stand beneath.

Who spoke for the lion?/
._Nyoli Ruwozi._/
Speak the name. Give its meaning./
._Daughter of the Lion._/
Every senior woman of the lion now carries that name./
She carried it first.

Who watched?/
._The lion watched._/
The song says witness. It makes no other claim./
._Witness. No other claim._

Now the terms. Count on one hand./
._One hand, five fingers._/
First, the five raise no weapon against the five./
They lay old cattle feuds down and never take them up again./
._Laid down. Never taken up again._/
Second, a spear against one is a spear against five./
._Against one, against five._/
Third, share grass, rivers and rains./
Keep the others' customs with the spirits of those places./
._Shared, and properly kept._/
Fourth, the five return in person/
to speak it again after four dry seasons./
._Four dry seasons. Again and again._/
Fifth, the Pact cannot change unless all five paramounts/
say yes aloud before the Lubemo and the griots./
._All five, aloud._/
That is the whole hand./
._There is no sixth finger._

Some beneath the tree wanted more than a hand./
They wanted a house over the five./
Nyoli Ruwozi answered. Hear her complete words./
We are not making a kingdom./
We agree that we will not destroy one another./
Our daughters are free to decide the rest for themselves./
._The rest belongs to our daughters._

No king came out from under that tree./
No law came out except these five terms./
No cattle were counted for anyone but their owner./
._Only the five, and the word._

Nine generations. The five return./
Every one may say no. None has ever said it./
That makes the yes precious, worth carrying./
._None has ever said it._

I have carried it at its given length./
._No longer and no shorter._/
Who has confirmed it?/
._We have. We know it too._/
Now it passes to the next mouth,/
waiting for the next dry season./
Listen, children of the five clans./
._We hear you, keeper._
:::
