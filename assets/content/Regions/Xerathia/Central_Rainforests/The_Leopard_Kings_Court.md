Deep in the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], in a valley that the canopy closes over completely, there is a city that appears on no map. Its people call their ruler the **Leopard King**, and the forest peoples around them call the city the **Leopard King's Court**. It is a town of terraced stone and raised timber halls strung between giant trees, home to a few thousand people, and the largest settlement in the interior by a wide margin.

## What It Is

The Court sits where three rivers rise within a day's walk of each other. That is the first reason for its size: whoever holds the valley holds the ways between the rivers. The second reason is the King.

The Leopard King is said to take the leopard's shape. The Court's hunters say he walks the forest by night as a great spotted cat and returns at dawn as a man. The tribes who pay him tribute say that no one has seen the King and the leopard in the same place. Nothing in the surviving accounts shows the claim to be false, and the King does nothing to discourage it.

The claim leans on the old lore of the leopard. The Sengala of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba]] hold the [[lore-leopardsengalaspr|leopard-guide]] to be solitary, unseen and unbound to any ground, and say it enforces bargains struck under its notice. The Court says its bargains are struck under that notice. The Sengala do not confirm this and do not deny it. They hold that the guide is the guide of the Sengala and does not belong to any court.

## What Lies There

The Court has no wall. Its defence is the forest itself, the hunters who patrol it in leopard-skin cloaks, and a ring of watch-posts in the trees. The city is built on three terraces cut into the valley's side:

- **The Lower Terrace**, where the tribute is received: drying sheds, long storehouses, and the guest-lodges where delegations from the tribes wait their turn.
- **The Hall of Pelts**, the audience hall, with its roof-beams hung with leopard pelts. Each pelt marks a tribute-bearing people, and the arrangement shows which people stand highest.
- **The Throne Cave**, at the head of the valley, where the King sleeps by day and where no guest has been admitted.

The tribute it receives is hardwood, dyes, ivory, medicinal plants, bundled hides, smoked fish and river-salt. In return the Court gives protection from the other tribes and from what comes out of the deep forest. It also gives iron, which it acquires through the Sengala and distributes by measure.

## Who Knows of It, and What They Say

- **The tribute-paying peoples** speak of the King with fear and a grudging respect. The gifts are paid as an oath, and an oath-breaker is dealt with by the Spotted Guard.
- **The Sengala** trade at the margins of the Court's lands and take care to seem neither friend nor rival.
- **Okhárian river-folk** who speak Okháric know the leopard-guide as _Ndawelari Thakwaring_, the Hunter's Rival. They hear the claim of the Court and hold it a vanity: a man who calls himself the Hunter's Rival will meet the real one.
- **Northern traders** have heard that there is a king in the interior who is a cat, and treat it as a tavern story.

## Reaching It

No road runs to the Court. Tribute-bearers walk the trails from their own territories, and each people has its own approach, known only to its bearers and the Spotted Guard. A stranger must join a caravan, follow one, or be brought. The valley opens abruptly from the forest, with its first terrace visible only from the last hundred paces.
