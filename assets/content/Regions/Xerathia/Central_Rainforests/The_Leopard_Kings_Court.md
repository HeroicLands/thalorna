Deep in the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], in a valley that the canopy closes over completely, there is a seat that appears on no map. Its people call their leader the **Leopard King**, and the tribes around them call the seat the **Leopard King's Court**. It is a town of earth terraces and raised timber halls strung between giant trees, home to some four hundred people. That is a large tribe by the forest's measure.

## What It Is

The Court is one tribe, as every tribe in the forest is: a few bands that follow one leader. Nothing is above it and it rules nothing. Its leader is a sorcerer, and what sets the tribe apart is that its neighbours are afraid of him.

The Leopard King is said to take the leopard's shape. The Court's hunters say he walks the forest by night as a great spotted cat and returns at dawn as a man. The neighbouring tribes say that no one has seen the King and the leopard in the same place. Nothing in the surviving accounts shows the claim to be false, and the King does nothing to discourage it. The title is his own: every other tribe in the forest has a leader, and none has a king.

The claim leans on the old lore of the leopard. The Sengala of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba]] hold the [[lore-leopardsengalaspr|leopard-guide]] to be solitary, unseen and unbound to any ground, and say it enforces bargains struck under its notice. The Court says its bargains are struck under that notice. The Sengala do not confirm this and do not deny it. They hold that the guide is the guide of the Sengala and does not belong to any court.

The Court sits where three rivers rise within a day's walk of each other, and its hunters range the ways between them. The valley is the Court's own, and the neighbouring tribes keep out of it unless they come with a gift.

## What Lies There

The Court has no wall. Its defence is the forest itself, the hunters who patrol it in leopard-skin cloaks, and a ring of watch-posts in the trees. The seat is built on three terraces cut into the valley's side:

- **The Lower Terrace**, where the gifts are received: drying sheds, long storehouses, and the guest-lodges where delegations from the neighbouring tribes wait their turn.
- **The Hall of Pelts**, the audience hall, with its roof-beams hung with leopard pelts. Neighbours give the pelts in fear, and the hall holds more of them than the Court's own hunters could take.
- **The Throne Cave**, at the head of the valley, where the King sleeps by day and where no guest has been admitted.

The gifts are hardwood, dyes, ivory, medicinal plants, bundled hides, smoked fish and river-salt, brought in the hope that the King will leave the giver alone. The Court gives nothing back by right, though the King will sometimes send word of what comes out of the deep forest. It keeps iron, which it acquires through the Sengala in exchange for ivory and hides, and which it hands out by measure.

## Who Knows of It, and What They Say

- **The neighbouring tribes** speak of the King with fear. Each sends its gift on its own schedule, and a tribe that stops is visited by the Spotted Guard, the Court's own fighters.
- **The Sengala** trade at the margins of the Court's lands and take care to seem neither friend nor rival.
- **The Kubindo** say the King has done what the drowned court did, and expect the same end for him.
- **Okhárian river-folk** who speak Okharik know the leopard-guide as _Ndawelari Thakwaring_, the Hunter's Rival. They hear the claim of the Court and hold it a vanity: a man who calls himself the Hunter's Rival will meet the real one.
- **Northern traders** have heard that there is a king in the interior who is a cat, and treat it as a tavern story.

## Reaching It

No road runs to the Court. Gift-bearers walk the trails from their own territories, and each tribe has its own approach, known only to its bearers and the Spotted Guard. A stranger must join a caravan, follow one, or be brought. The valley opens abruptly from the forest, with its first terrace visible only from the last hundred paces.
