## Overview

Ne’ P’ashel, the "Water House of the Quetzal," stands at the critical junction where the highland rivers flowing from the mountain passes meet the larger river systems descending into the jungle lowlands. The city is the beating heart of trade and communication for the southern territories of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K’ich’chik]], a cosmopolitan hub where merchants from the highlands of the [[place-ixbaltzakurgn|Ish'bal Tsa'ku Region]], jungle dwellers, and traders from distant [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] meet and negotiate. The riverside setting gives Ne’ P’ashel a unique character—constantly alive with the sound of water, the cries of boatmen, and the bustle of commercial enterprise.

## Location and Geography

Ne’ P’ashel perches on a series of natural terraces overlooking the junction of three major rivers. The city spreads across both banks, connected by impressive stone causeways and wooden bridges engineered to withstand the seasonal flooding that swells the rivers during the rainy months. The central pyramid—dedicated to [[lore-xilanixlandty|Shilan Ish'lan]], god of voyages—dominates the skyline, its geometric perfection reflecting in the water below.

## Districts and Markets

The city is organized by function and ethnicity. The highland merchants cluster in the north-facing district near the upriver wharves, where goods from [[place-nekiahkul|Ne’ Ki’ahkul]] and the mountain cities arrive on llama-trains and are transferred to boats. The jungle merchants occupy the southern quarter, where canoes laden with cacao, exotic feathers, and rare medicines are loaded and unloaded under the watchful eyes of priestly assessors. The central district hosts the great marketplace where bulk goods are exchanged and where smaller merchants hawk their wares—textiles, dyes, preserved foods, and countless other commodities.

## People and Culture

Ne’ P’ashel’s population is ethnically and culturally mixed, drawing people from across K’ich’chik’s southern territories. Highlanders, jungle dwellers, and coastal merchants live side by side, often speaking three or four languages fluently. The city has the reputation of being relatively cosmopolitan, where a merchant’s skill and wealth matter more than bloodline or birth. However, the priesthood maintains strict authority—the great temples extract heavy tribute, and priestly assessors oversee all significant commerce.
