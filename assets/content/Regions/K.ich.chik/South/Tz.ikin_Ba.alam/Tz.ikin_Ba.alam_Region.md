## Overview

Tz'ikin Ba'alam Region is forsaken wastes that no one truly rules, watched over from its threshold by the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] priesthood, and lies within [[place-sthrnkchchk|Southern K'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik Continent]].

Tz'ikin Ba'alam – the Land of the Dead Jaguar – is the forsaken realm at the southern edge of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]]. It is a place of terror, legend, and desperate ambition. The earth here has been scourged by divine wrath, blasted and twisted into forms that defy nature and sense. Some priesthoods whisper that this was the site of a catastrophic failure in a previous age of the world, when the celestial order faltered and the gods themselves turned their back. Others claim the wastes are a living punishment, a scar in the world that refuses to heal.

The people of the northern realms speak of Tz'ikin Ba'alam with dread reverence. It is both graveyard and crucible – a place where warriors and priests travel to test their worth against impossible odds, knowing that most who venture into those wastes will never return. Those who do return changed forever, marked by knowledge and suffering that sets them apart from ordinary mortals. In the pyramid-cities, such survivors are regarded with a mixture of awe and fear, often elevated to positions of great authority or spiritual significance. The wastes call to the ambitious, the doomed, and the desperate – and in return, most of them feed the frozen earth.

## Geography

Tz'ikin Ba'alam is a realm of extremes and contradictions. The landscape itself seems to war against life. Great expanses of blasted rock and ash stretch toward horizons that shimmer with heat-haze or glow with sickly phosphorescence. In other regions, permanent winter has taken hold – vast fields of ice and snow, even in the warmer seasons, where the air itself becomes knife-like and breath freezes solid. Some travelers speak of regions where fire erupts from the earth itself, geysers of superheated water and steam that appear without warning.

The plant life is sparse, twisted, and often poisonous. Thorny shrubs with spines that glow faintly in darkness cling to life wherever water can be found. Fungal growths the size of houses spread across the ground in some regions, releasing spores that cause madness or death in those who breathe them. Entire valleys are carpeted with luminescent fungi that provide a sickly, greenish light at night.

The fauna is equally nightmarish. Apex predators – some species unique to these wastes – hunt with terrifying efficiency. Giant scorpion-things with armor-plating the size of a man's head stalk across the ash. Packs of twisted, hairless canine creatures hunt in coordinated groups. Massive reptilian things, neither snake nor lizard, move through the shadows. And there are things stranger still – creatures that seem to violate the natural order, part flesh and part something else entirely, as if the boundary between the animate and inanimate has grown thin here.

Water is scarce and often toxic. Those brave enough to drink from unknown sources in Tz'ikin Ba'alam often go mad or develop horrific wasting sickness. Some regions are completely devoid of water; travelers must carry heavy supplies or die of thirst.

## Maritime Powers

Tz'ikin Ba'alam has no maritime access and no territories to claim or defend in any traditional sense.

## Trade

Trade is essentially non-existent in Tz'ikin Ba'alam itself. The only significant economic activity is the looting of ancient ruins said to lie buried beneath ice and ash – remnants of a civilization that existed before the current age. Rumors speak of cities of fantastic architecture, of devices and artifacts whose purposes are forgotten, of wealth in forms that no longer exist in the modern world. A handful of obsessed explorers and treasure-seekers venture into these wastes, risking their lives (and usually losing them) in pursuit of legendary artifacts.

What truly flows through Tz'ikin Ba'alam is not trade, but transformation. The wastes are a crucible where the weak are burned away and the strong are tempered. It is this that draws the ambitious, the doomed, and the desperate – not commerce, but the promise of transcendence, of power, or of a glorious death that will grant passage to the Eternal Garden of [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|P'iqal Kul'qat]], the creator god.
