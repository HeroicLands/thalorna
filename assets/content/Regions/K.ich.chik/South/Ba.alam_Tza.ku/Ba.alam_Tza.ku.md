## Overview

Ba'alam Tza'ku holds the land of [[place-balamtzakurgn|Ba'alam Tza'ku Region]]. The region is home to some of the most powerful city-states in all of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]], rivals in grandeur and influence even to the great cities of [[affiliation-balamkul|Ix'ilankul]]. The name itself means "Jaguar's House" in the high speech, and the region's peoples have long revered the spotted predator as an emblem of divine power and earthly dominion.

The region stands as the uppermost reach of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]]'s southern expansion, fully integrated into the unified priesthood of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] and bound by the sacred _ix'lan ch'upul_ (white roads) that connect its greatest cities. The Ch'um Ix'lan relay network reaches Ba'alam Tza'ku in three to four days from [[affiliation-balamkul|Ix'ilankul]], routing through [[affiliation-kiikbaate|Ki'ik Ba'ate]] along the great southern white road; the priesthood maintains well-staffed way-stations along the highland road, ensuring that the K'ul Tq'an's decrees and intelligence from the heartland arrive with regularity. Here, the celestial calendar governs all—planting and harvest, pilgrimage and sacrifice. The great pyramid temples cast long shadows across terraced highlands, and blood spilled on their altars waters the cosmic order itself.

## Maritime Powers

These coastal powers are nominally part of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] unified priesthood, but they guard their maritime autonomy jealously, and the ocean itself remains a boundary that even the highest priest-kings approach with caution.

The river systems themselves are carefully controlled by the highland city-states. [[place-hakukul|Ha' K'ukul]] stands at the critical junction where mountain and jungle trade routes meet, and its rulers exact tribute and control from merchants passing through to the coast.

## Trade

Tribute flows constantly to the temples: the finest goods, the most precious offerings. The priesthood's control of trade is absolute; no major transaction occurs without priestly oversight and the extraction of religious levy.
