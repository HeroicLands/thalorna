## Overview

Before the crossing south, a party stops at **K'awiil Tza'kul**, and the temple's quartermaster sells it everything it will need. The seat of the Boundary Place, about 4,000 people, is a fortress-temple built to endure at the last edge of the ordered K'ich'chik world, where managed fields and pyramid-cities give way to toxic mineral flats, hissing geothermal vents and a sky that glows sickly yellow or bruised purple with distant storms. The settlements here exist as waypoints for the doomed and the desperate.

## The Price of Passage

The temple-fortresses sell passage itself: guides, provisions, and the priests' blessing. They hoard dried foods that survive the journey, cloths that protect against the toxic miasmas, and consecrated amulets. All potable water is carried in or dug from deep wells, since the one lake, a mineral-laden sheet in the north, is undrinkable. The priesthood profits immensely from the transit trade through heavy fees and tribute. Most of those who travel here are bound for the wastes by oath or doom, and the priests mostly ensure that the proper offerings are made before they leave.

## The Two Gods

The priesthood is intensely focused on two deities: [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|P'uqil Ch'aqun]], who commands the weather and the fury of storms, and [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Tz'uqil Ix'bal]], mistress of death. Blood flows freely on the altars in the hope of safe passage through what lies beyond.

## Reagents

Alchemists and mystics venture into the poisoned lands for crystalline formations ground into potent reagents, and for sulfurous compounds sold to alchemists in the northern cities. The cost in lives is enormous, and the priesthood keeps trade in them restricted.
