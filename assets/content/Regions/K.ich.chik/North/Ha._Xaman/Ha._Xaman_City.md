## Overview

K'ich'chik merchants who deal in **Ha' Xaman** begin by deciding which clan to pay. The seat of the Tz'okum Tz'aqalil, the Coyote People, is reckoned at about 1,500 people, in the endless grasslands at the center of the northern frontier, where the great herds of bison and elk follow routes worn smooth by centuries, and knowing where the herds are heading is the first skill a child learns. Of the five northern nations, the Tz'okum are the most immediately dangerous to K'ich'chik interests.

## Raiders and Traders

The Tz'okum are mounted warriors, and their tactical speed, signaled across miles with hand signs and calls, has defeated K'ich'chik armies built for jungle and highland war. Young Tz'okum compete for prestige through increasingly daring raids, and they raid across the Ch'akul Tza'kul barrier into K'ich'chik lands with alarming frequency. In Ha' Xaman, "tribute," "trade" and "plunder" are not always different things: some traders keep formal relationships with particular clans and pay for safe passage, an arrangement that works and has to be renegotiated constantly. The merchants put up with it for the herd products, and because the road north runs through Tz'okum land.

## What Comes Back

The wealth that comes in lets the Tz'okum buy obsidian weapons, feathered cloaks and dyed textiles, which mark the status of wealthy clans, and what they sell is hides, rendered fat, spectacular beadwork and decorated armor.
