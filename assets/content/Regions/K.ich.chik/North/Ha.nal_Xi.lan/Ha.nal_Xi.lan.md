## Overview

Ha'nal Xi'lan holds the land of [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Xi'lan Region]]. The rivers are too large and sprawling to defend entirely, the lands too rich to abandon, and the competing powers — the Tz'okum from the west, the Ha'ik from the north and east, and the Ch'aqun from the south — too evenly matched for any decisive victory.

Ha'nal Xi'lan exists in a state of perpetual conflict. Camps appear and vanish with the seasons as different parties establish temporary dominance. Violence between rival groups is constant, and alliances shift without warning. K'ich'chik knowledge of the region is fragmentary and hard-won, pieced together from the accounts of the handful of merchants bold or desperate enough to approach the borderlands. These merchants report that the contested nature of Ha'nal Xi'lan makes it the one place in the north where K'ich'chik traders are occasionally tolerated — not out of hospitality, but because the rival nations sometimes find it useful to acquire southern goods through a neutral party who can be expelled or killed if circumstances change. Any K'ich'chik presence in Ha'nal Xi'lan is precarious, temporary, and maintained only at the sufferance of whichever faction currently holds the ground.

## The Contested Borderlands

Ha'nal Xi'lan is not claimed by any single nation, yet all three adjacent peoples maintain permanent (if seasonal) presence. The Tz'okum maintain camps along the western margins, using Ha'nal Xi'lan as a waypoint for herds following migration routes and establishing trading posts at major river crossings. The Ha'ik dominate the waterways themselves, with canoe bases established at strategic locations and floating villages that move with seasonal fish runs. The Ch'aqun maintain hidden camps deep in the swampier southern reaches, emerging for trade and occasionally to conduct raids against rivals or to intimidate merchants.

The lack of central authority in Ha'nal Xi'lan creates opportunities for entrepreneurship and violence in equal measure. Ambitious individuals from any of the three nations sometimes attempt to expand their control, establishing fortified trading posts or attempting to enforce exclusive access to particularly productive territories. These attempts usually fail; the other two powers are quick to form temporary alliances to prevent any single nation from achieving hegemony. This enforced equality, while frustrating to leaders with expansionist ambitions, creates a kind of peace—not through any genuine resolution of conflicts, but through the constant threat of intervention by rival powers.

A small number of K'ich'chik merchant families have attempted to establish trading posts in the borderlands, but these ventures are extraordinarily dangerous. Posts are burned, looted, or simply surrounded and starved out when inter-tribal tensions shift or when the northern peoples decide that southern presence has become unwelcome. The few K'ich'chik merchants who maintain any foothold in Ha'nal Xi'lan do so by making themselves temporarily useful to one faction or another — and by maintaining the ability to flee on short notice. No K'ich'chik trading post in Ha'nal Xi'lan has survived more than a few seasons in the same location.

## Trade

The wealth that flows through Ha'nal Xi'lan draws K'ich'chik merchants despite the danger, but the mortality rate among those who trade in the borderlands is sobering. The northern peoples tolerate K'ich'chik traders only as long as they are immediately useful; a merchant who lingers, asks too many questions, or is perceived as favoring one faction quickly discovers the limits of that tolerance. K'ich'chik merchants who have attempted to play rival nations against one another, profiting from escalated tensions, have been made examples of — their fates serving as grim warnings to others who might forget that they are tolerated guests in a land that belongs to none of them.
