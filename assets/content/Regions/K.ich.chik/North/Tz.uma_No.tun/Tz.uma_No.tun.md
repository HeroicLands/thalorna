## Overview

Tz'uma No'tun holds the land of [[place-tzumanotunrgn|Tz'uma No'tun Region]]. K'ich'chik scouts call the people here the Ch'aqun Tz'aqalil ("Serpent People"), from the serpent-imagery that dominates the few artifacts recovered from border encounters and the rare survivors of failed expeditions into their territory. Their own name for themselves is unknown. Of all the northern nations, the Ch'aqun are the most feared—their reputation for mastery of poisons, ambush tactics, and strikes that come from darkness and vanish without trace has made K'ich'chik border commanders treat Tz'uma No'tun as effectively impassable.

K'ich'chik observers believe the Ch'aqun have developed a civilization more organized and centralized than the other northern nations, though evidence is fragmentary and largely inferred from the sophistication of their military operations. The swamplands that form the heart of their territory are simultaneously a refuge and a weapon; K'ich'chik expeditions that have entered the trackless wastes have found them disorienting and deadly, while the Ch'aqun clearly move through them with effortless mastery. No K'ich'chik scholar has ever been permitted into Ch'aqun lands and returned with a reliable account.

## The Ch'aqun Tz'aqalil

K'ich'chik scholars speculate, based on the coordinated nature of Ch'aqun military operations and the apparent specialization of their warriors, that the Serpent People maintain a more centralized hierarchy than the other northern nations. Some K'ich'chik accounts use the term "Ch'aqun Nah" ("Serpent Lords") for the leaders observed at rare border parley, though whether this is their actual title or a K'ich'chik invention is uncertain. What is clear from the outside is that whoever commands the Ch'aqun can marshal coordinated action across large distances—a capability that implies organizational sophistication unusual among the northern peoples and deeply concerning to K'ich'chik military planners.

The Ch'aqun are masters of swamp-craft and poison-working; their knowledge of which plants produce which toxins, how to extract and concentrate venom, and how to deliver poisons via dart, blade, or food is unmatched and carefully guarded. This specialized knowledge has made them formidable warriors and valuable allies (and dangerous enemies) in inter-tribal conflicts. The Ch'aqun are famous for their ability to defeat larger forces through ambush, poisoning of water supplies, and night attacks executed with coordinated precision. Their reputation as uncompromising in victory and ruthless in defense has made them cautious neighbors; the other northern nations are slow to aggress against the Ch'aqun.

The Ch'aqun have developed sophisticated traditions of mound-building—constructing massive earth mounds for ceremonial purposes, burials, and astronomical observation. These mounds are not permanent settlements but rather serve as gathering places for seasonal ceremonies and councils. The construction and maintenance of mounds represents significant labor investment, indicating a level of population coordination and planning unusual in the northern frontier. The mounds also serve a defensive purpose; they provide vantage points for observation and contain internal structures suitable for ritual and strategic communication.

## Trade

This middleman position generates considerable wealth and political influence; the ability to grant or withhold access to southern luxury goods gives Ch'aqun leadership substantial leverage in inter-tribal negotiations.

The Ch'aqun appear to maintain some presence in the contested [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Xi'lan]] borderlands, and rare transactions with K'ich'chik merchants occur there—always on Ch'aqun terms, always brief, and always through intermediaries who may or may not actually be Ch'aqun. The opaque nature of these trade networks makes it impossible for K'ich'chik merchants to predict resource flows, negotiate reliably, or establish lasting commercial relationships.
