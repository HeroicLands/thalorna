## Overview

Border commanders treat **Tz'uma No'tun** as effectively impassable. The seat of the Ch'aqun Tz'aqalil, the Serpent People, is reckoned at about 2,500 people, in the northeast of the frontier, where ancient forested mountains descend through piedmont into swamp, river delta and coastal wetland. Of all the northern nations, the Ch'aqun are the most feared: masters of poisons, ambush and strikes that come from darkness and leave no trace. No K'ich'chik scholar has been admitted to their lands and returned with a reliable account.

## What Is Inferred

K'ich'chik observers judge from the coordination of Ch'aqun military operations that the Serpent People are more organized and centralized than the other northern nations. Some accounts give their leaders the title "Ch'aqun Nah," Serpent Lords, though it may be a K'ich'chik invention. The Ch'aqun build great earth mounds, which serve as gathering places for seasonal ceremonies and councils, as burial places, as places for astronomical observation and as vantage points; they are not permanent settlements. The swamps are a refuge and a weapon, and the Ch'aqun move through them with effortless mastery.

## Trade

The Ch'aqun act as middlemen for southern goods in the north and carefully control access to the most valuable ones. They sell finished products only, never raw materials: poisons, herbal medicines, preserved foods and rare items of spiritual power. K'ich'chik physicians who have analyzed the medicines report extraordinary potency and have identified neither the source plants nor the preparation. Transactions with K'ich'chik merchants happen only in the contested borderlands, on Ch'aqun terms, briefly, and through intermediaries who may or may not be Ch'aqun.
