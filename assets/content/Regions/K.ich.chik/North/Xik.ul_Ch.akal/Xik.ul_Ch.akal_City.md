## Overview

A K'ich'chik trader who wants to deal with the Ha'ik waits, sometimes for days, while their representatives consult authorities he never sees. The seat of **Xik'ul Ch'akal**, the Water Path, is reckoned at about 2,000 people, in the lake-and-river country of the northeast, where the Ha'ik Tz'aqalil, the Otter People, control the waterways and the portage routes between them with quiet, absolute authority. K'ich'chik traders who navigate Ha'ik rivers without permission vanish.

## What the Scouts See

The region's abundance has let the Ha'ik settle more than most northern peoples. K'ich'chik scouts see seasonal villages of substantial longhouses that appear and disappear with the seasons, visible from a distance and approached at one's peril. The Ha'ik build birch-bark and log canoes that carry heavy cargo through lakes and rapids, and they maintain fish weirs, dams and sluices communally. The sugar maples of the southern part of their country supply a syrup and a sugar that fetch premium prices in the south.

## Trade

The Ha'ik tolerate trade only at designated border sites, on their own schedule and at their own prices, and they can afford to wait. Merchants who press for better terms are cut off. The sugar reaches the south through a chain of intermediaries because no southern merchant is admitted to the sugar camps, and the Ha'ik canoes, beadwork, quillwork and carved bird figurines that reach K'ich'chik collectors do so on non-negotiable terms.
