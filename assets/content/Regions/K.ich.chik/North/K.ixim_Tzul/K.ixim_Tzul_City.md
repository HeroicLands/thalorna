## Overview

At the border of **K'ixim Tzul** a K'ich'chik trader is turned back, firmly and without negotiation. The seat of the No'kin Tz'aqalil, the Bear People, is reckoned at about 1,000 people, in a vast territory of boreal forest, grassland and subarctic plateau between the east and west of the northern frontier. Even the border merchants who deal with the Xak'nal and the Tz'okum report almost no direct contact with the No'kin, who regard all outsiders, the other northern nations among them, with deep suspicion.

## What Reaches the South

What the south knows of the No'kin it knows from their goods. The hides that reach southern markets are leather of remarkable suppleness and strength, worked by masters who follow the caribou herds across hundreds of miles. The No'kin gather berries with systematic precision, dry them for food and trade, and know medicinal plants and poisons well enough that their healers are sought out by distant peoples and some of their compounds are guarded as trade secrets. They rely on the Xak'nal and the Tz'okum as intermediaries for southern goods, and the dependence rankles. The migrations of the herd come first, and trade routes second.
