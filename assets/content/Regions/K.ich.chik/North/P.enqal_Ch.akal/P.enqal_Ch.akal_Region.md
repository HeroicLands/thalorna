## Overview

P'enqal Ch'akal Region is the land of [[affiliation-penqalchakal|P'enqal Ch'akal]], and lies within [[place-nrthrnkchchk|Northern K'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik Continent]].

P'enqal Ch'akal—"Great Grassland" in K'ich'chik—is an endless sea of grass and sky where the horizon stretches beyond sight and the wind carries scents of a thousand miles.

## Geography

P'enqal Ch'akal is deceptively simple in appearance yet possessed of subtle complexities that have taken generations for the Ts'okum to master. The central plains form an immense plateau of grassland, interrupted by occasional river valleys and low-rolling hills. The grass grows thick in the north, where water is more reliable, and becomes increasingly sparse toward the south as [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]]'s contested river valleys approach. The landscape shifts with the seasons: spring brings floods and rapid greening, summer bakes the grass golden and sees the great herds at their most numerous, autumn triggers the migrations that drive Ts'okum society, and winter—while less brutal than Ishtem Tsul's extremes—still claims the unprepared.

The herds themselves are the defining feature of P'enqal Ch'akal's geography. The great bison and elk that migrate across the plains follow routes worn smooth by centuries of use, creating natural pathways visible from leagues away. These migration routes intersect with river valleys and seasonal water sources; the Ts'okum have mapped every such intersection with meticulous care and plan their hunting expeditions and tribal movements accordingly. To understand P'enqal Ch'akal is to understand the herds; knowing where the herds are and will be is the fundamental skill all Ts'okum children learn before they learn to craft a bow.

The western border of P'enqal Ch'akal touches the foothills and mountains of [[affiliation-ixtemtzul|Ishtem Tsul]], creating a transitional zone of broken prairie and scattered forest where No'kin and Ts'okum hunters sometimes encounter one another. The eastern borders are more diffuse, shading gradually into the eastern woodlands claimed by the [[affiliation-xikulchakl|Ha'ik Ts'omalil]]. The southern boundary is aggressively contested; P'enqal Ch'akal's grasslands edge into the river valleys and bottom-lands of [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], where the Ts'okum, Ha'ik, and [[affiliation-tzumanotun|Ch'akun]] peoples all maintain seasonal hunting camps and trading posts. These borderlands see the highest concentration of inter-tribal violence in the entire northern frontier.

## Trade

P'enqal Ch'akal's position at the center of the northern frontier makes it a natural crossroads for trade, though the Ts'okum's martial temperament and cultural emphasis on military virtue means they engage in trade more as a byproduct of territorial movement than from primary economic interest. Nevertheless, hides and rendered fats flow regularly southward from Ts'okum hunters, and herbal medicines and craft goods move north and east to supply communities that lack direct access to southern trade.
