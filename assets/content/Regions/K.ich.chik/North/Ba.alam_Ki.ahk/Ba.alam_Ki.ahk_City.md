## Overview

The border scouts' first rule about **Ba'alam Ki'ahk** is that the passes decide who comes. The seat of the Xak'nal Tz'aqalil, the Eagle People, is reckoned by K'ich'chik at about 3,000 people, in the westernmost expanse of the northern frontier, where cedar forest runs from a fjord coast to a spine of snow-crowned volcanic peaks. What the Xak'nal call the place, and what stands in it, the scouts do not report, and traders who venture inland without an invitation do not return.

## What the Scouts Know

The Xak'nal are master craftspeople, and a few of their works reach the south: cedar longhouses, totem poles that read as documents in wood, canoes carved from single logs, and copper shields hammered until they glow. They hunt salmon and whale with ritual discipline, and the salmon run is the pulse of their year. Their prestige appears to come from giving away: gift-exchange ceremonies establish rank and bind the nation together, and when an outsider threatens it they unite swiftly and ferociously.

## Trade

The Xak'nal hold a monopoly on the northern trade and keep the routes and contacts to themselves. Cedar products, whale oil and furs go south; obsidian, jade and featherwork go north. The high passes are closed in winter, so trade is seasonal, prices are volatile, and the Xak'nal traders who succeed grow wealthy and influential.
