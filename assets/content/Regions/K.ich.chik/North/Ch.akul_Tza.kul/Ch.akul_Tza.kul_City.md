## Overview

Water decides everything in **Ch'akul Tza'kul**, the Thirsty Land, and the region's central settlement, about 700 people, lives by that rule. The desert is a barrier of sand seas, mesas and salt flats between the tribal north and K'ich'chik civilization, held by neither wall nor garrison, and claimed by no nation. A caravan crossing it from the northern trading posts to the southern markets takes several weeks.

## Posts and Oases

The few permanent water sources are oases where underground springs surface, with small groves of date palms and acacia. Traders and scouts guard or monitor them and keep records of their condition. Most oases keep a tradition of neutrality and let travelers of any nation or origin pass, in exchange for a modest payment or trade goods. Merchants who establish posts or way-stations at the major oases gather wealth and influence, and most of the posts are seasonal: built, maintained for a season or several, and abandoned when conditions turn bad. Stone cairns mark the established routes between them.

## Guides and Ruins

A few hardy guides make a living combining knowledge of desert routes with learning about the ruins scattered across the desert, the remains of an older settlement. Shamans and scholars buy what comes out of those ruins, and the expeditions that go looking for it are costly and dangerous. Anyone crossing needs water, preserved food and spare pack animals for the ones that die, and the goods that justify the crossing are the high-value ones.
