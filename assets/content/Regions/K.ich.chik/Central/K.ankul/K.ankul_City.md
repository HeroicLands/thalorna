## Overview

The scribes of **K'ankul** will tell you that the sky is a book and that they are the only people who read it to the end. The seat of the realm of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] is a city of about 120,000 in the limestone lowlands of the east, and its realm is the intellectual heart of K'ich'chik civilization. Its priests spend decades perfecting astronomical tables carved in stone, and the calendar-keepers of [[affiliation-balamkul|Ix'ilankul]] depend on its astronomers and mathematicians. K'ankul texts, written and illuminated here, are prized throughout the known world.

## The Cenotes

The land looks flat from the surface, but underground rivers and sinkholes run beneath it, and the K'ankul priesthood teaches that the sinkholes are the mouths of the underworld through which the sun travels each night. The priests, the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Ch'ul'bal]] of K'ankul, pour water as offering at the cenotes, give special weight to watery sacrifice, and divine by reading reflections in the water. Some powerful priestly families keep a personal relationship with a particular cenote and treat it as a sacred being with its own preferences. The boundary between managing water and interpreting omens is intentionally blurred. Pilgrims come specifically for cenote-touched materials, and merchant families who have inherited the right to harvest a particular cenote sell its minerals, its cave pearls and its sealed waters.

## Who Governs

A K'inmah rules the realm, and the realm's cities treat the priesthood as a partner rather than a master. K'ankul's wealth and cultural prestige have earned it that standing, and the Speaking Road reaches the city from Ix'ilankul within two days. The K'ankul city-states do not compete with armies. They compete in discoveries and architecture: a city that announces a new calculation or technique becomes a center of study for scholars from across K'ich'chik.

## Making a Living

K'ankul trades in knowledge. Its scribes and mathematicians are paid extraordinary sums for their services, and its books, astronomical instruments and carved jade make it a trading power second only to Ix'ilankul. Its merchants control sea routes along the coast, and its ports, the Walled City and the Island of Swallows, handle salt fish, feathers, woods and resins. Coastal pans produce the salt that is traded for the grain surpluses of Ix'ilankul, and stingless-bee honey travels north and south.
