## Overview

In **Shelem**, the road is a river. The seat of the realm of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]], the Land of Birds, is a city of about 60,000 in the river-bound jungle on the eastern edge of the K'ich'chik heartland, where the [[place-hanaltzuma|Ha'nal Ts'uma]], the [[place-hanalxilik|Ha'nal Shilik]] and a thousand smaller waterways matter more than roads or temples, and pu'uts, the sheer-walled table mountains, rise from the canopy like islands. Where Ish'ilankul is built on volcanic stone and K'ankul on limestone, Shelem is built on water.

## What the North Needs

Shelem is the source of the luxuries without which no Ts'enmah of the north can claim true power. Quetzal feathers, from the birds that live in its cloud forests, and macaw and hummingbird plumage go north to adorn the nobility. Jaguar hides are the mark of military rank and divine favor. Herbalists gather medicinal and visionary plants under ancient protocols known only to the initiated, and cacao from the highland slopes is prized in the northern cities. A Ts'enmah who commands a steady supply of hides and feathers can outfit his warriors and honor his priests, so good relations with Shelem's merchant families are essential to political survival in [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] and [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]]. The merchant families know it, and they use it.

## Who Governs

Shelem is less urbanized and less strictly ruled by the Itsani hierarchy than the heartland, and many of its settlements are governed by councils of merchant families and priests, not by a single Ts'enmah. The major river settlements keep the forms of orthodoxy and send tribute to Ish'ilankul and K'ankul, but the realm's allegiances are renegotiated every year through trade agreements and gifts. The priesthood blends the Itsani cosmology with the veneration of local spirits: the river dolphins that guide canoes through dangerous water, the anacondas that bridge the world of humans and the underworld, the forest itself. The Speaking Road reaches Shelem only along the river routes, in three to four days from Ish'ilankul by way of K'ankul's coastal network.

## The Rhythm of the Floods

Seasonal floods widen the waterways and make temporary islands, so settlements stand on higher ground and fields are arranged to use the flood instead of resisting it.
