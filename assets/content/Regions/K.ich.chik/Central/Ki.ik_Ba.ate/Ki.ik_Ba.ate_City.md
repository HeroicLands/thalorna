## Overview

A merchant crossing **Ki'ik Ba'ate** going north carries jaguar hides, macaw feathers and cacao; going south he carries obsidian, fine textiles and priests. The name of the realm means "Watery Bridge," and its seat is the city that holds the bridge: about 70,000 people on a narrow volcanic isthmus, between the great northern realms and the wilder, less centralized lands of the south. Merchants grow rich here, and the ambitions of great powers collide here.

## Who Governs

A K'inmah governs, and Ki'ik Ba'ate's K'inmah are characteristically independent-minded. They keep the forms of [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] orthodoxy, pyramids, the vigesimal count and the 260-day ritual calendar, with a local flavor, and they chafe at the demands of tributary status while being too wealthy and too strategically important for the northern powers to conquer. Messages from [[affiliation-balamkul|Ix'ilankul]] arrive in two to three days along way-stations that are fewer and more widely spaced than in the heartland. When the K'inmah have tried to set up a messenger network of their own, the priesthood has reminded them who controls the Speaking Road.

Ix'ilankul has been tempted to take the isthmus's trade by force. Only diplomatic skill and strategic marriages have kept the realm's autonomy, and that balance is the main political tension of the region.

## The Isthmus

The coast is two coasts. The western shore is rocky and violent, with few harbors and excellent fishing. The eastern shore has sandy beaches, coral reefs and some of the finest natural anchorages in K'ich'chik, and its merchant canoes venture far beyond the bounds of K'ich'chik culture. It was Ki'ik Ba'ate traders who first made contact with the peoples of the far south. Behind the coast the terrain is too complex for an invading army to occupy and the sea routes cannot be entirely blockaded, which makes the realm more resilient than its size suggests, and the rainforest holds the quetzal, whose rarest plumes come from its deepest, least reachable zones.
