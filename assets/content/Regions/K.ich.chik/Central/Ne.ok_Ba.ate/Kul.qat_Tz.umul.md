## Overview

On the eastern slopes of [[place-neokbaatergn|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], where the rains come in off the sea and fall for half the year, a stepped platform rises out of the rainforest on the shoulder of an old volcano. Its stair was plastered red once, and the plaster still holds in long runs between the roots, so that the steps show red through the green from the valley below. The builders called it **Kul'qat Tz'umul**, the red pyramid, _kul'qat_ the sacred peak and _tz'umul_ red. The few who know the valley call it the Red Steps.

It is a binding temple of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|P'uqil Ch'aqun]]: walls far thicker than they need be, a sanctum anchored into the bedrock of the slope, and an interior that was once packed with offerings to keep the storm-serpent sated. The serpent-keepers lodged duplicate codices in its sealed chamber, as they do in every such temple, so that something of the count would survive the end of the age. When the city that kept it lost the road to it, the temple was shut, and the rainforest took the road.

## The Building

The builder-priests of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|P'iqal Kul'qat]] raised the temple to the serpent-keepers' specifications, as they raise every binding temple, and the specifications included things the serpent-keepers would not explain. Three of them are still there.

- **The barrier.** At the back of the pillared sanctuary a stone slab the height of two men hangs in a slot, held by a counterweight and a locking pin in a gallery inside the wall. Lowered and pinned, it closes the stair to the sealed chamber.
- **The shaft.** In the sanctuary floor a square shaft drops into the dark under a pivoting stone lid. Offerings went down it. The lid turns on a pin that a person in the gallery can draw, and a second gate in the pit beneath can be dropped to close the pit off from the chamber.
- **The paving.** The processional way that runs from the foot of the stair around the platform and down to a landing on the river is paved in fitted stone, now under a hand's depth of fern and leaf-mould. The builders paved it to the exact line the specification gave, and no wider.

## The Guardian

The builder-priests hold that the continent's [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]] are P'iqal Kul'qat's own work, the _Kul'ik P'iqal_, the Living Works of the Builder, and they record each one as they would a building: its design, its purpose and its failure. The serpent-keepers set one over this temple. The carvings in the sanctuary name it **Ch'al Kul'iq**, the strength beneath, and show it only in part: a weight, a reaching, a shape under a lowering stone.

What is known of it is what it does. It is heavy enough to crack a stair it crosses, and it moves with the long scrape of stone dragged on stone. It does not strike. It lies upon what it takes, and what it lies upon is crushed evenly, the bones broken small inside a skin that is not torn and armor that is barely marked. It can pass through a gap a hand high, and it takes what it catches into passages no person could follow it through. Lamp flames lean toward it before it arrives, as if the air were being drawn after it. It is drawn to blood spilled on stone. It keeps to the stone the builders laid, and it has never been seen off it.

The Warden of the Ruins at **Ne' Tz'umul** keeps the register of this temple, and the register records the guardian as failed, its offerings ended and its works presumed dead.

## Who Knows of It

- **The serpent-keepers of Ne' Tz'umul** know the temple holds duplicate codices and a seal-stone of their branch, and that the seal-stone has not been presented for inspection at the turning of the Tz'uqal Cycle since the temple was shut.
- **The Warden of the Ruins** keeps the register that calls the guardian dead.
- **The hunters of the valley** do not walk on old paving anywhere on the slope, and they say so as a rule of the forest rather than as a story.

## Reaching It

By the eastern trail from the highland valleys toward the coast, which climbs the volcano's shoulder above the river gorge. The trail passes below the temple and does not go to it; the red of the stair through the trees is the only sign. From the river a processional stair climbs to the platform from a sheltered landing, overgrown and still sound.
