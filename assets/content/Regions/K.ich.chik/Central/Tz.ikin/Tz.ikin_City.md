## Overview

In **Tz'ikin**, the road is a river. The seat of the realm of [[affiliation-tzikin|Tz'ikin]], the Land of Birds, is a city of about 60,000 in the river-bound jungle on the eastern edge of the K'ich'chik heartland, where the [[place-hanaltzuma|Ha'nal Tz'uma]], the [[place-hanalxilik|Ha'nal Xilik]] and a thousand smaller waterways matter more than roads or temples, and pu'utz, the sheer-walled table mountains, rise from the canopy like islands. Where Balamkul is built on volcanic stone and K'ankul on limestone, Tz'ikin is built on water.

## What the North Needs

Tz'ikin is the source of the luxuries without which no K'inmah of the north can claim true power. Quetzal feathers, from the birds that live in its cloud forests, and macaw and hummingbird plumage go north to adorn the nobility. Jaguar hides are the mark of military rank and divine favor. Herbalists gather medicinal and visionary plants under ancient protocols known only to the initiated, and cacao from the highland slopes is prized in the northern cities. A K'inmah who commands a steady supply of hides and feathers can outfit his warriors and honor his priests, so good relations with Tz'ikin's merchant families are essential to political survival in [[affiliation-balamkul|Balamkul]] and [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]]. The merchant families know it, and they use it.

## Who Governs

Tz'ikin is less urbanized and less strictly ruled by the Itzáni hierarchy than the heartland, and many of its settlements are governed by councils of merchant families and priests, not by a single K'inmah. The major river settlements keep the forms of orthodoxy and send tribute to Balamkul and K'ankul, but the realm's allegiances are renegotiated every year through trade agreements and gifts. The priesthood blends the Itzáni cosmology with the veneration of local spirits: the river dolphins that guide canoes through dangerous water, the anacondas that bridge the world of humans and the underworld, the forest itself. The Speaking Road reaches Tz'ikin only along the river routes, in three to four days from Balamkul by way of K'ankul's coastal network.

## The Rhythm of the Floods

Seasonal floods widen the waterways and make temporary islands, so settlements stand on higher ground and fields are arranged to use the flood instead of resisting it.
