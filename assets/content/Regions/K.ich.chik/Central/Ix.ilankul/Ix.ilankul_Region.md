## Overview

Ish'ilankul Region is the land of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]], and lies within [[place-cntrlkchchk|Central K'ich'chik]] on the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik Continent]].

A vast plateau region dominated by highland valleys and volcanic peaks, Ish'ilankul has served as the cradle of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani]] faith and the seat of the Ch'alish Tq'an, the supreme high priest who mediates between the celestial gods and mortal kingdoms. It is here that the grandest pyramid-temples rise from the earth, their summits crowned with observatories where the Ch'alishbal (ordained priests) calculate the movements of the heavens and chart the inexorable march of the vigesimal calendar. The very stones of Ish'ilankul are said to remember the age of creation, when P'iqal Kul'qat (god of creation) first sang the world into being.

The soil is rich, watered by seasonal rains and engineered canal systems, permitting populations far larger than those found in any other region of the central heartland. This abundance fuels both the ambition and the appetite for blood sacrifice, for the Ish'ilankul K'ich'chik believe that the more elaborate and numerous their offerings to the cosmic gods, the more securely they bind the sun to its path and keep the world from descending into chaos.

## Geography

The Ish'ilankul plateau rises between 1,200 and 2,400 meters above the coastal lowlands, a realm of dramatic altitude and volcanic drama. A north-south spine of volcanic peaks marks the region's eastern boundary, their slopes rich with obsidian and lined with cloud forest where the rarest medicinal plants grow. The volcanic calderas are both fertile and feared—their eruptions remembered in myth as moments when the gods themselves grew angry at the failures of human ritual. The central valleys between these peaks form natural fortresses and centers of population, where cities such as Kul'taq'an and Ishtal'hun sprawl across multiple hillsides, connected by _ish'lan ch'upul_ (white roads) of packed limestone that glow pale in the moonlight.

The climate is temperate at higher elevations, with distinct dry and wet seasons. The annual rains (the fifth through tenth months) feed the intensive agricultural terraces that cascade down the hillsides, supporting maize, beans, amaranth, and cacao cultivation. During the dry season (the eleventh month through the fourth), Ish'ilankul becomes parched, and great ceremonial gatherings are timed to culminate just before the rains return—the priests believe that human blood offered at the moment of cosmic transition strengthens the bonds that hold the rain-bringers at bay.

Water sources—sinkholes, mountain springs, and engineered reservoirs—hold sacred significance. Each major city maintains elaborate hydraulic systems, including aqueducts carved from living stone, cisterns carved deep into the earth, and canals lined with mortar that channel water to terraced gardens and distant fields. These waterworks are engineering marvels and the subject of intense priestly study, for water is the lifeblood of the realm and its control is the ultimate expression of Ts'enmah authority.

## City-States and Realms

The most powerful include Kul'taq'an (the "Seat of the Priests"), where the Ch'alish Tq'an traditionally resides and where the most elaborate astronomical observations are conducted; Ishtal'hun (the "Place of Obsidian Blood"), a center of ritual warfare and the training of elite captive-warriors; and Tsa'mash (the "Stone Heart"), a merchant republic famous for its great market plazas and its control of cacao-producing highlands to the south.

At the heart of Kul'taq'an lies [[place-tzomalchalix|Ts'omal Ch'alish]] (the "Sacred Foundation"), the holiest site in all of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]]—the precinct where P'iqal Kul'qat is believed to have first breathed the world into existence at the dawn of the First Age. The precinct is centered on an ancient volcanic vent from which warm, mineral-laden vapors still rise, regarded by the priesthood as the living breath of the creator god. Around this vent, the greatest pyramid in K'ich'chik rises—the Ts'ultel Ch'alish ("Sacred Mountain")—a monumental structure of nine ascending tiers that represents the nine layers of the heavens through which the sun climbs each day. Only the Ch'alish Tq'an and the most senior Ch'alishbal may ascend to the summit, where the vapors of creation mingle with the open sky and where the supreme rites of the Celestial Recurrence are performed. Every K'ich'chik person, from the highest Ts'enmah to the lowest bound laborer, is expected to make pilgrimage to Ts'omal Ch'alish at least once in their lifetime. The great festivals of the calendar year—particularly the **K'ayik Kul'al** ("the Living Flame") that marks the turning of the 52-year cycle—draw hundreds of thousands of pilgrims from every corner of the civilized world, transforming Kul'taq'an into a sea of humanity stretching to the horizon. To have stood in the shadow of the Ts'ultel Ch'alish and breathed the vapors of creation is to have touched the divine; to die without having made the journey is, in K'ich'chik theology, to face the gods with an incomplete soul.

## Trade

Ish'ilankul's central position on the plateau, combined with its enormous population and religious authority, makes it the premier trading hub of the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]] heartland. Obsidian from the volcanic slopes flows outward to [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]], [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], and [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]] in the form of ritual blades, mirrors, and ceremonial objects. Cacao, grown in the rich soils of the southern highlands, is processed and distributed by Ish'ilankul merchants who jealously guard the recipes for sacred preparations. Textiles dyed with cochineal and woven with elaborate geometric patterns bearing the seals of the Ts'enmah command prices that only nobles in the tributary states can afford.

In return, Ish'ilankul imports rare woods from the southern jungles, aromatic incense resins from the coasts, and salt harvested from coastal evaporation ponds. The great market plazas of Kul'taq'an and Tsa'mash operate on a rotating 20-day cycle synchronized with the vigesimal calendar, drawing merchants, tribute-bearers, and pilgrims from across the known world. The volume of trade is staggering—estimates suggest that thousands of transactions occur daily in the largest plazas—and the Ts'enmah grow wealthy on market fees and the taxation of merchant guilds.

The most prestigious trade good, however, is neither practical nor abundant: the feathers of the quetzal bird, whose iridescent green plumage adorns the headdresses of the highest nobles and whose plumes are among the few items considered more precious than jade or gold. The rarest quetzal plumes come from Shelem's deepest jungles, but Ish'ilankul's merchants have established trading networks that ensure a steady if limited supply, making the city-states custodians of one of the most sacred symbols of divine authority and rank.
