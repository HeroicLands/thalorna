## Overview {#overview}

Tveskavesh is a walled market town of about three and a half thousand people, held by the [[affiliation-prncdmzhtvsh|Princedom of Zhitavesh]] at the foot of the southern range. The grain road of the lowlands ends here: the wagons that bring the harvest up from the river unload, change their teams for the steeper road, and either climb the passes to the mining and market towns of [[place-helionis|Helionis]] and [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]] or sell to the buyers who wait in the square. Ore and iron come down the same road in the other direction. The town is small for its trade, because most of the people who make it rich are somewhere else for most of the year.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The road from the lowlands runs straight across flat fields for a day and then lifts, and the town sits at the point where the road begins to climb: a stone-footed wall with a gatehouse on the lowland side and a smaller one facing the pass. Inside the walls, the main street is a long yard of wagon-sheds, harness shops and team-stables, and the smell of horse and axle-grease is the town's smell. The range stands over the roofs, gray in winter and blue in summer, and the pass road can be seen as a pale line against it. Wagons in the street are always facing one way or the other, and which way is the first thing a visitor learns to read.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Tveskavesh lives by the change of teams. A wagon that comes up from the lowlands with a pair of horses cannot take the pass with them, so the town's stables rent teams of heavy horses and oxen by the trip, and the drovers who winter on the [[place-northrnmrch|Northern March]] of Bizaria bring their own. Around the stables grow the other trades: the wheelwrights and farriers, the inns, the granary where grain is held for a better price, and the money-changers who turn the grain-silver of the lowlands into the ore-silver of the south. The market is of regional reach, since it sets the price at which Velanthian grain leaves the land. The town grows little and buys its own food from the lowland villages.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The prince of Zhitavesh holds the town through a reeve of the pass market, who keeps the toll on every wagon that goes through the south gate and the order of the stables, and who answers to the captain of the retinue at the seat. The wall is held by a small detachment of the retinue. The pass roads above the town are patrolled by the riders of the [[affiliation-hstsvrt|Sevret Host]], whose muster town, [[place-tvsknv|Tveskanov]], lies close under the range, and the Host's riders take the toll at the upper pass if the prince's pay is late. The customary law of the market is simple: a bargain struck in the square is binding until the wagon has crossed the south gate.

## The Present {#present}

The prince of Zhitavesh wants to sell grain here that the port does not see. The merchant houses of the port know it and keep agents in the square who count the wagons that go south, and the grain-town houses, who sell at Tveskavesh because the price is better than on the river, resent being counted. The monasteries' grain, which the prince cannot audit, also goes south, and no one at the market will say whose it is. This year's late rain has made every sack more valuable, and the Sevret Host is owed its pay at harvest. A pass that closes to wagons, or a patrol that collects its own tolls, would stop the whole trade in a week.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The inns near the south gate serve the drovers and the wagon-masters and fill on the first dry days of spring. They are cheap, noisy and well supplied with news of the pass. The stable-yards rent a loft and a place by the fire to a party that hires its team there, and the reeve keeps a small guest room for travelers with a letter from the prince.

## History {#history}

The town grew from a stable at the foot of the range where the drovers' escorts that guarded the grain road changed horses; the escorts became the Sevret Host, and the stable became a market. The lowland princes walled it when the passes began to carry more grain south than the river carried to the port, and the wall has been rebuilt since.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- A wagon-master with a load of monastic grain needs an escort over the pass that does not report to the reeve.
- A port house's agent offers a fair price for a list of the wagons that crossed the south gate in the last month.
- A rider of the Sevret Host has not been paid, and the toll at the upper pass is rising.
