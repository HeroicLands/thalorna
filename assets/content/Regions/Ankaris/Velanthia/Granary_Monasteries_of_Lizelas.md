## Overview {#overview}

The granary monasteries are the houses of the [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]] in the Velanthian grain belt. Each is a large endowed house with its own fields, mills and barns, and each keeps a granary reserve against a bad year. The houses also keep the land's letters: their monks copy the customary law the princely houses share, teach the sons of the lowland halls to read, and write the ledgers a prince has no clerk for. The Velanthians call a granary and the monastery that keeps it by one word. The [[place-frrygrnry|Ferry Granary]], on the river crossing of the grain road, is one of the houses.

## Membership {#membership}

A monk works the land and keeps the hours of Lizelas, and enters the house by taking the vows of the harvest after a year of labor in its fields. A granary-keeper holds the keys to a barn and answers to the head of the house for every sack in it. The houses take in younger sons and daughters of the lowland halls and orphans of the frontier villages, and they take in the starving in a famine year ([[lore-leanyear|The Year of the Barns]]).

## Leaders {#leaders}

Each house is led by its own abbot or abbess, called a starbrat, chosen by the monks from among themselves. The heads of the houses do not obey a common superior, and they keep the feasts of [[affiliation-lizelas|the faith]] without answering to the Lizena or to any officer of its temple priesthood. They meet when the princes and the Hosts need a mediator both sides trust, and each of them speaks for the network as far as the other heads will let him or her. The starbrat of the great house at [[place-zhtvsh|Zhitavesh]], [[being-rdrbskvnch|Radora Boskvanich]], is the one both princes and Hosts send to first.

## What It Wants Now {#present}

The houses want their reserves left uncounted. The prince of the [[affiliation-prncdmzhtvsh|Princedom of Zhitavesh]] wants to know what they hold, since in a famine year he is the one the villages blame, and the abbots will not tell him. A house that opens its barn to a prince has shown its price, and a house that keeps it shut may be asked, in a bad year, why the barns are full while the villages go hungry. The late rain on the eastern fields has made the question urgent.
