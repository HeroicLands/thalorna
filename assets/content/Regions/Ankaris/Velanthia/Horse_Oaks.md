## Overview {#overview}

The Horse Oaks stand on a bluff above the river a day's ride upstream of [[place-brlvsh|Brelavesh]], where the forest-steppe comes down to the water: a stand of oaks so old that three people cannot circle one with their arms. The bark of the largest is cut with sun-wheels and with the long-necked beasts the villagers call river-horses, and the cuts are older than any shrine of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] in the country.

Nobody lives in the grove. The villages along the bluff keep it, and the people who come are those who bring a sheaf.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The grove belongs to the villages of the bluff in common, and no prince has granted it or taxed it. A headman of the nearest village keeps the paths and the fence, and a priest of the [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]] comes up from the river to bless the first sheaf. The priesthood has long since stopped trying to remove the older marks and sets a small wooden image of the goddess at the foot of the largest oak each harvest.

## Layers {#history}

The oaks were sacred before the river-princes had seats. The Velanthian houses of the river layered the goddess's iconography over the sun-wheels and river-horses and cut nothing out. The villagers cut the first sheaf of the harvest in the nearest field, lay it at the roots of the largest oak, and carry the first fruits to the goddess afterward.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The port wants the grove's oak. [[being-mlgryrgrch|Milogor Yarogorich]] of Brelavesh has bought mills upriver against the lowland prince's wishes, and a mill needs oak for its wheel and its sluice gates. The prince's mill-wrights have offered silver for the oldest trees, since no other stand on the river is as large. The villages of the bluff will sell windfall and nothing standing, and their headman says the grove is not Milogor's to sell. This year's late rain makes the first sheaf harder to cut, and the villages are not in a mood to argue about it.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbors}

The grove stands inside the territory of the prince of Brelavesh but far from his patrols, and a mill-wright who cuts without leave finds the villages at his back. The track from the river is steep and muddy after rain.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

A trodden ring beneath the largest oak, a wooden image of Lizelas wrapped against the weather, offerings of flax and honey cakes, and bark cut with wheels and horses. The villagers will explain the carvings to a guest who comes with a sheaf and say little to one who comes with an axe.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The mill-wright's price.** The prince's mill-wrights need a go-between the headman will hear, since the bluff will sell windfall and nothing standing.
- **The first sheaf.** The late rain has put off the cutting, and the headman wants the mill-wrights kept off the bluff until the sheaf is laid.
