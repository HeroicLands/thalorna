## Overview {#overview}

Zhitavesh is the seat of the [[affiliation-prncdmzhtvsh|Princedom of Zhitavesh]], a walled town of about eight thousand people on the river meadows of the lowland grain belt. Its prince's hall stands beside the princely granary, and the great granary monastery of the lowlands stands on the other side of the market square. The town buys nothing it can grow: the barns, mills and ferries of its own villages feed it, and the barges that load at its landing carry the surplus down to the port of [[place-brlvsh|Brelavesh]]. It is the largest grain market in Velanthia: more grain passes through its square in a harvest month than through any other place, and everything about the town is arranged around counting it.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The road in runs between stubble fields and mill-races, and the town shows first as a roofline of barns: long timber granaries with steep thatch, raised on stone footings against damp and rats. The wall is earth and timber over a stone base, with four gates, and the grain wagons use the river gate at the landing. Inside, the market square is the whole of the town's public life. The prince's hall, a timber house with a carved porch, faces the monastery's gate across it, and the granary of each stands behind its owner, so that a wagon crossing the square passes both. Every wagon is stopped at the gate and counted by a clerk who writes the load in a ledger. A stranger who carries no grain is waved through, and a stranger who carries any is asked whose it is.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Zhitavesh is a grain town. The prince takes his tithe in kind from the villages and stores it in the princely granary; the monastery holds a second reserve of its own beside it; the merchant houses buy what remains in the autumn and ship it down the river. The mills at the town's edge grind for the villages around it, and the ferries across the river charge a copper for a person and a silver for a loaded wagon. The town makes little else: sacks, barrels, wagons and the iron bands of their wheels, and the ledgers that the monastery's scribes sell to the merchant houses. Its market is of regional reach because every price of grain in the lowlands is read from the price on its square at harvest.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The prince, [Zhitovod Dobrovodich]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds the town as his seat and lives in the hall. The captain of the retinue commands the riders sworn to the hall and the garrison of the wall, the judge of the hall hears the disputes of the town by the customary law the princely houses share, and the keeper of the princely granary answers for the prince's stores and counts them against what the monastery holds. Order in the square is kept by the retinue's riders, and order in the barns is kept by the monks and by the granary-keepers' own keys. A theft of grain is the gravest charge the judge hears: the thief repays threefold from his own harvest, and a thief with no harvest works the debt off in the prince's fields.

## The Present {#present}

The late rain on the eastern fields has made the count the town's whole concern. The prince's hall wants to know what the monastery holds and has twice been refused entry to a barn; the monks answer that a barn opened to a prince is a barn priced. The Hosts of the south-eastern margin are paid from this granary at harvest, in grain and silver, and a short harvest leaves the prince unable to pay both them and the forts of the [[affiliation-prncdmbrvvsh|Princedom of Brevavesh]] that his grain also feeds. The merchant houses of the grain towns watch the same ledgers, since the price they will pay in the autumn depends on the number the hall will admit to.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The monastery keeps a guest barn for travelers and the poor, with straw, bread and a bowl of porridge, and asks only that a guest work an hour in the yard. The inns of the landing serve the bargemen and the merchant houses' factors: they are loud, dear in the autumn, and full of people who know the price of grain before anyone else. A visitor of rank is lodged at the prince's hall, and everything said at his table is heard by the retinue.

## History {#history}

The town grew where its first barn stood at a ferry, and the seat has been held by the same princely line since. After the third union of the steppe overran the eastern fringe about 548 AF ([[lore-thrdorqwnq|The Third Orghunoq]]), the grain villages of the heartland sent settlers to resettle the eastern fringe, and the town's granaries were raised to feed those settlers through the first years. The prince's barn and the monastery's barn have stood on either side of the square since.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- The keeper of the princely granary wants an outsider to count the monastery's barn, since no one the monks know may enter it, and he will pay in grain.
- A merchant house's factor needs a harvest escort down to the landing before the price is known and will not say why the timing matters.
- An ataman's rider arrives to ask the hall why the Host's grain is late, and the captain of the retinue wants him kept in the guest barn until harvest.
