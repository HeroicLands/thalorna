# terran_analog: "Moldova, Ukraine, southern Russia, and the Pontic steppe and forest-steppe—the great east-European grain belt and forest-steppe transitional zone, historically home to Scythians, Sarmatians, Cumans, Slavs, and Cossack-style frontier confederations."

Velanthia is the great transitional zone east of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] and [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]]—a forest-steppe belt and black-earth grain country between those forests and the open steppes of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] to the east. The kingdom of [[place-targud|Targud]] lies to the north, [[place-malagna|Malagna]] to the northwest, and the [[place-vylariargn|Viléria Region]], the [[place-heladrgn|Hellád Region]] and [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]] lie to the south. Mixed forest, river meadow, farmland, and horse-pasture interleave across the region.

What Velanthia _has_ is rivers, soil, and a particular knack for surviving between predators. Its great river systems—the western, central, and eastern groups—are the trade arteries that carry Nordmal furs and ironwork, Khazrin horses and steppe-goods, and the region's own staggering grain surplus to the roads and markets that take them on. Its black-earth belt produces some of the finest cereal yields on the continent. And its political culture has been shaped, generation after generation, by the awkward fact that the great Nordmal kingdoms to the northwest and, now and then, an orghunoq of the steppe tribes to the east have both considered Velanthia a strategic prize worth taking.

The Velanthians have not been taken. Not lastingly. The region has lost border districts more times than the chroniclers can count, has been raided and tributed and overrun in waves, and has emerged each time with its core river-kingdoms intact and its frontier confederations re-formed. The Velanthian explanation for this is partly geographic (no single conqueror controls enough of the region long enough to absorb it), partly cultural (the Velanthians fight defensively with extraordinary tenacity and are politically agile in ways their would-be conquerors rarely manage), and partly providential (every Velanthian church and shrine acknowledges, in its way, the patient endurance that has kept the region alive between hammer and anvil).

## Geography

Velanthia stretches from the eastern margin of the Nordlands and Vristwald forests to the western Khazrin frontier. The region is landlocked: no sea touches it on any side. [[place-targud|Targud]] lies to the north, [[place-malagna|Malagna]] and [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]] to the northwest, the Khazrin steppe to the east, and a mountain line along its southern rim divides it from [[place-vylariargn|Viléria]], [[place-helionis|Helionis]] and [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]]. It runs about a thousand miles from west to east and about half that from north to south.

The terrain transitions through three principal belts:

The **forest-steppe belt** in the north and west—mixed-deciduous and pine forest interspersed with open meadow and river-bottom farmland. This is the heartland of settled Velanthian agriculture: dense villages, ancient field systems, and the great river-kingdoms whose princely capitals sit at fortified river-junctions. The forest belt provides timber, fur, honey, and the protective cover that has made the heartland defensible against steppe-cavalry advances.

The **black-earth grain belt** across the center—the agricultural treasure of the region, a broad band of extraordinarily fertile black soil that produces wheat, rye, sunflower, and flax surpluses in quantities that have repeatedly drawn outside attention. The grain belt is more lightly forested than the north and runs south to the foot of the mountain line. It is studded with fortified market-towns, monasteries-and-shrines, and the country estates of the great river-princes.

The **steppe margin** in the east and southeast—the open grass-and-scrub country where Velanthia shades into the Khazrin proper. This is the country of the Velanthian frontier confederations: semi-settled, semi-mobile communities of horse-keeping farmer-warriors who have absorbed enough of the steppe-nomad lifeway to deal with their eastern neighbors on something like equal terms. Militarily competent, organized in their own distinctive Host-and-ataman pattern, and politically jealous of the river-prince states, whose pay they take and whose taxes they resist.

The **mountain line** on the south closes the grain belt. Its passes carry Velanthian grain south to the mining towns of Helionis's hinterland, [[place-khaddaria|Khaddaria]], and to the [[place-northrnmrch|Northern March]] of Bizaria, where Velanthian drovers winter, and they bring ore and iron back. Marauders come down from the mining country on the far side, and the [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] is strong there; it holds nothing inside Velanthia. The danger belongs to the passes, and the Hosts that patrol them meet it there. The outermost eastern reach, along the Khazrin frontier, is the most heavily steppe-influenced part of the region: open horse-country, oasis-and-river settlements, and a gradient of cultures that becomes difficult to distinguish from the tribes of the western Khazrin.

The climate is broadly continental: cold winters with significant snowfall, warm-to-hot summers, distinct spring and autumn seasons. Rainfall is reliable in the north and west, increasingly variable as one moves east and southeast. The river systems sustain the region's agriculture; failed rains in the grain belt produce regional hardship rather than collapse, since the rivers continue to bring down the snowmelt of the northern hills.

## Peoples and Culture

Velanthians are overwhelmingly human and are organized along a clear cultural gradient running east from the Nordlands border to the Khazrin frontier.

The **western and northern river-kingdoms** are the cultural heartland—settled, agricultural, urban around the river-junction capitals, with their own ancient liturgical-and-courtly language family that is unrelated to either Nordmal or Aureldian tongues. Their princely houses are old and intermarried; their towns are wooden-and-stone; their courts maintain elaborate ceremonial that mixes Aureldian courtly forms (absorbed via long contact with the western kingdoms) with older indigenous ritual that the priests have folded into the Aureldian pantheon over the centuries. The Velanthian river-courts are reputed to be the most ceremonious in eastern Ankaris.

The **central grain-belt towns** are more cosmopolitan than the river-courts—mercantile, polyglot, host to merchant communities from every neighbor. The grain trade pulls in buyers from [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión]] and the Helládian city-states and produces a class of Velanthian merchants whose wealth and external connections sometimes rival the river-princes' political authority. The grain-belt monastic establishments are also distinctive—large endowed houses that combine religious function with massive agricultural management and (less openly) the storage of regional grain reserves against bad years and bad neighbors.

The **eastern frontier confederations** are the most distinctive Velanthian population to outside eyes—the **Velanthian Hosts**, semi-settled communities of horse-keeping farmer-warriors organized around hereditary headmen called **atamans** (each Host is named for its founding ataman or its principal river: the [[affiliation-hsttrvl|Tarvál Host]], the [[affiliation-hstsvrt|Sevret Host]], the [[affiliation-hstkhrd|Khorad Host]], and several others). The Hosts maintain their own internal law, their own military organization, and a complicated tributary-and-alliance relationship with the river-prince kingdoms to the west and north. They are the practical defense of the eastern and southeastern frontier against tribal raiding from the steppe, and they are the primary mediators of trade and diplomacy with the western Khazrin tribes.

The **eastern oasis-and-river communities** along the Khazrin frontier have absorbed enough steppe culture, language, and intermarriage to be culturally hybrid; some of them speak Velanthian dialects with heavy [[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]] loanwords, others speak Sovidur with Velanthian agricultural vocabulary. The Velanthian coronation rolls list them as Velanthian; the nearest Khazrin tribes sometimes claim them as their own; in practice they answer to whichever side last had the better year.

## Religion

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] comprises the formal faiths across all Velanthian populations, though with a heavy local emphasis on the harvest-and-fertility deities (especially [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]]) and on the storm-and-protection aspects of the warrior gods. Velanthian shrines often layer Aureldian iconography over older indigenous symbols—sun-wheels, river-horses, sacred groves—that the priesthood has long since stopped trying to suppress.

The frontier Hosts maintain a more austere religious culture, with portable battle-shrines and traveling priests rather than fixed temples. Their religious practice has absorbed elements from the steppe peoples to the east—particularly an emphasis on sky-and-storm deities, oath-taking under the open sky, and the great seasonal gatherings where multiple Hosts assemble for joint religious observance and political consultation.

A scattered but persistent population of [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian worshippers]] exists in the eastern hybrid communities, descended from intermarriage with Tellumi merchant families and oasis-belt refugees. Their shrines are tolerated and sometimes patronized by the eastern river-princes, who find it diplomatically useful to be seen accepting both traditions.

## Politics and Power

Velanthia has no single sovereign. The region is governed jointly by:

The **river-prince kingdoms** of the heartland
: perhaps a dozen substantial princely states, plus several smaller ones, each ruled by a hereditary princely house and centered on a fortified river-junction capital. The major kingdoms are loosely confederated through dynastic intermarriage, occasional joint councils, and a shared body of Velanthian customary law that all the princely houses recognize as binding.

Three of the princely states are told in full: the [[affiliation-prncdmzhtvsh|Princedom of Zhitavesh]], which holds the grain lowlands and their granaries from its seat at [[place-zhtvsh|Zhitavesh]]; the [[affiliation-prncdmbrlvsh|Princedom of Brelavesh]], which holds the river-port and its merchant houses from [[place-brlvsh|Brelavesh]], the one city of the land; and the [[affiliation-prncdmbrvvsh|Princedom of Brevavesh]], which holds the frontier forts against the Grukar from [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]]. Together they hold about 1,500,000 of Velanthia's 4,000,000 people, and about 300,000 of those live in the country of the three Hosts. The other princely states hold the remaining 2,500,000 and are told here only in outline. Their capitals are towns.

The **Velanthian Hosts** of the eastern frontier
: formally subordinate to nearby river-princes, practically self-governing under their atamans and Host councils. Each Host serves a river-prince for pay, musters its own forces, makes its own peace with the steppe tribes across the frontier, and sometimes makes its own war. A Host wants autonomy, pay and plunder rights, and it can walk away from a prince who pays late or badly. The princes fear a Host changing sides more than any raid, because the frontier it guards then opens from the inside.

The **monastic establishments** of the grain belt
: the [[affiliation-grnrymnstrs|Granary Monasteries of Lizelas]], large endowed houses whose abbots wield genuine political and economic power, often as third-party mediators among the princely houses and the Hosts. Their barns hold the reserve that carries the land through a bad year, and the princes can neither count it nor claim it.

The **merchant houses** of the major grain-belt towns
: the [[affiliation-mrchnthsrvr|Merchant Houses of the Rivers]], wealthy, well-connected, and influential through grain credit and trade financing.

The three differ by their river and their trade, and each needs the others. Zhitavesh has the grain, and the monasteries that keep the reserve stand in its towns; it takes its tithe in kind. Brelavesh has the port, the merchant houses and the mint, and it takes its tolls in silver. Brevavesh has the stone forts on the edge of the Grukar country and no fields worth the plow, so the lowland prince's grain feeds its garrisons and the port prince's silver pays its wardens. Neither delivery is on time, and each prince knows what the others owe him, so their rivalry is constant and checked.

The river-princes pay for the frontier. A stone fort is raised at a prince's expense, its warden is appointed by him and paid from the port's silver, and the villages beneath it keep a watch of their own that rings a bell and burns a signal fire when a raiding party crosses the margin. The wardens are paid, tired and corruptible, and a late payment shows first in who is let through at night.

Coordination across these power centers happens through ad-hoc councils called when joint action is needed: a major steppe incursion, a Nordmal pressure-play in the west, a famine year, a particularly egregious dispute between major princely houses. The councils are occasional, not an office: they have no permanent existence and no executive authority; they advise, negotiate, and sometimes (when the threat is large enough) coordinate joint military response.

The system is fragile in theory and durable in practice. Foreign observers regularly predict Velanthian collapse; Velanthian centuries continue to accumulate.

## Current Situation {#present}

Velanthia has no sovereign and no capital, and every pressure on it lands on a different power. The river-princes face the Nordmal kingdoms to the north and northwest, the Hosts face the steppe tribes to the east, the monasteries hold the grain against a bad year, and the merchant houses lend against the next harvest. The councils meet only when a threat is large enough to force them, so the response to each pressure is whatever the nearest power can muster.

Each of the three named princes is short of what another holds. [Zhitovod Dobrovodich]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [[place-zhtvsh|Zhitavesh]] has the grain and cannot sell it upriver without the port noticing; this year's rain came late to the eastern fields, and a short harvest would leave him unable to pay his Hosts and to feed the forts together. [Milogor Yarogorich]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [[place-brlvsh|Brelavesh]] has the silver and the fewest soldiers, buys mills upriver against the lowland prince's wishes, and pays his Host more each year. [Krodogor Brevogorich]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]] has the forts and no grain: the lowland grain and the port silver both reach him late, and his wardens are owed months.

The Hosts keep their price in view of the prince who pays them. [Volkar Kodogorich]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the [[affiliation-hsttrvl|Tarvál Host]] lets the prince of Brelavesh hear what the steppe tribes offer him. [Sovidan Volketich]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the [[affiliation-hstsvrt|Sevret Host]] holds the passes, and his riders collect their own tolls there if the harvest pay is late. [Belukan Kelodov]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the [[affiliation-hstkhrd|Khorad Host]] has told the river communities to stop paying the [[affiliation-sqrtrtrb|Soqritur]], and each side watches which way he turns. The princes fear a Host changing sides more than any raid.

Raiding is constant on the western and northwestern frontier. Captives taken there are ransomed where a family or a prince will pay; those nobody ransoms go down Vristwald's rivers to Nordmen and to steppe traders. The northwestern reaches are watched from the princes' forts for the Grukar, whom no Velanthian bargains with, and fur hunters buy a night's passage from a tired warden to work the margins.

## Trade

Velanthia's economy is built on grain, furs, horses, and the trade-routes that move them. The rivers carry grain to the markets and to the passes, and the pass roads carry it south to Helionis and Bizaria and on to Midhalión, where Velanthian wheat fills the granaries of the Hellád and beyond. Furs from the northern forest belt travel the same rivers and roads. Horses bred in the eastern Host country are exported east to the Khazrin tribes (which prize Velanthian-bred crossbreeds) and west to the Aureldian heavy cavalry market.

In return, Velanthia imports Aureldian and Vilérian finished goods (textiles, glassware, fine metalwork, books), Midhalión luxuries (wine, olive oil, citrus when shipping permits), Helládian philosophical-and-medical literature, Khazrin silver and salt, and the small steady trickle of distant Tantsou silk and porcelain that reaches the eastern grain-belt through Khazrin intermediaries.

The merchant houses of the grain-belt towns are the principal financial intermediaries; the river-princes maintain the mints; the monastic establishments hold the grain reserves; the Hosts collect tolls on the eastern caravan routes. The whole system runs on overlapping authority, hand-to-mouth in any given year, and has never produced the great unified mercantile institutions of Midhalión—but it sustains a population of perhaps four million people in a region that no single power has ever managed to govern.

## Defense

Velanthian defense rests on three pillars: the river-prince levies of the heartland, the Velanthian Hosts of the eastern frontier, and the difficulty of the terrain itself. The forest belt is hostile to steppe cavalry; the great rivers are defensible obstacles when properly garrisoned; the Hosts are world-class light cavalry on their home ground.

Major external threats have come from two directions: the western Nordmal kingdoms (occasionally), and the great steppe orghunoqs (rarely, but with far worse effect). The Nordmal incursions tend to be limited frontier wars that resolve into renegotiated borders within a generation. The steppe incursions are more dangerous: when an orghun forces the tribes of the central Khazrin into a great orghunoq, Velanthia is one of its first targets, and the region's eastern fringe has been overrun multiple times in recorded history. The pattern, well-understood by all parties, is that the heartland holds, the frontier is bled, the orghunoq eventually fragments, and Velanthian settlers slowly recolonize the lost ground over the next two generations.

## History {#history}

The recorded history of Velanthia is a series of overrun frontiers and recovered ground. The Hosts took shape about 470 AF and retook the fringe the steppe had overrun ([[lore-hostsforpay|The Hosts Take Pay]]). About 548 AF the third great union of the steppe ([[lore-thrdorqwnq|The Third Orghunoq]]) overran the region's eastern fringe. About 590 AF a river-prince raised a fort on the Thornwald frontier against the Grukar ([[lore-princesfort|The Prince's Fort]]). About 625 to 631 AF the jarls of Malagna and Norgaad pressed the northwestern frontier, and the Hosts marched west for pay ([[lore-northernwar|The Border War with the Jarls]]). In 641 AF the rains failed and the granary monasteries fed the villages from barns the princes could not count ([[lore-leanyear|The Year of the Barns]]). From 660 AF captives taken on the Velanthian frontier went down Vristwald's rivers to Nordmen buyers ([[lore-thrallrivers|The Thrall Rivers]]). In 697 AF the Tarvál Host took the port's pay in place of the lowland prince's, and the princes have feared a Host changing sides since ([[lore-tarvalturn|The Tarvál Host Changes Sides]]).

## Relations with Neighboring Regions

To the **north** lies [[place-targud|Targud]], and to the **northwest** lie [[place-malagna|Malagna]], [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]] and the rest of the Nordlands. Relations are old, complicated, and generally tense—Nordmal princes consider Velanthia a frontier prize within reach of whichever kingdom is strongest; Velanthian princes consider the Nordmal a perpetual problem requiring careful management. Trade is substantial despite the friction; intermarriage among the western princely houses and the eastern Nordmal jarls is common.

The [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm Region]] country, where Grukar nests are especially prevalent, overlies the northwestern part of Velanthia and the eastern edge of Vristwald. No line is drawn around it. Velanthia is raided from it, avoids it and watches it, and no Velanthian makes a bargain with a nest. The river-princes pay for stone forts along the frontier, held by wardens who are paid, tired and corruptible, and the villages beneath the forts ring bells and burn signal fires when a raiding party passes.

To the **west** lies Vristwald—the Varokh forest country between Velanthia and Aureldia. The Vristwald-Velanthian frontier is the most permeable border in the region: the Varokh tribes and the Velanthian river-prince and grain-belt populations have intermarried, traded, raided one another, and shared frontier garrisons for many generations. The Velanthian river-princes consider them useful auxiliaries and unreliable subjects in approximately equal measure.

To the **east** lies the vast Khazrin. The relationship is the central strategic fact of Velanthian existence. Across a long, fluid frontier, Velanthian Hosts and Khazrin tribes conduct a continuous low-level commerce of trade, raid, alliance, and intermarriage. When a great orghun rises in the central Khazrin, the relationship turns dangerous; in the long stretches between, it is workable.

To the **south**, beyond the mountain line, lie Viléria, the Hellád, Helionis and Bizaria. Velanthian grain feeds their cities and mining towns; finished goods and scholarship flow north. Farther south, beyond the Hellád, lie the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] on the eastern shore of the Vilérian Sea and the broader [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]].
