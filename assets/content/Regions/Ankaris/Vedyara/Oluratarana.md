**Olūratarana**, the ford of the restless dead, is a wide gravel crossing of the upper [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadī]] above the fields of [[place-rajapurjnpd|Rājapur]], and the oldest _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] that anyone can date. About 300 BF a king of Mahānadī destroyed a rising of the upper-river villages here and forbade the pyre to the dead ([[lore-unburnford|the Unburned Ford]]). Some thousands lay through a monsoon with no conch, no lamp and no fire, and the way behind them never closed.

## The Two Banks

The ford is two places. The east bank is grazed and ordinary, and the village above it waters its cattle there. That bank is the half the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]]'s founder burned about 290 BF ([[lore-kindlford|the Kindling at the Ford]]), and it has been quiet since.

The west bank is a mile of tall grass, and it is a silence. A lamp lit on it gutters out within a breath. Cooking-fires will not draw, and a drover who tries to make tea on it gives up. The quiet is heavy enough that cattle driven across drink fast and leave, and no bird nests in the grass. In the low-water months bones come up out of the gravel. The village above puts them back in the river and will not touch fire to them, on the reasoning that fire is the one thing the field has always refused.

## After Dark

By day the west bank is uneasy and no worse. After dark it is a different place. Travelers who have crossed at night tell of men moaning in the grass with no one there, of figures standing in the shallows looking upstream, and of a cold that comes up through the feet and carries the dead's despair with it into the chest. A sleeper within sight of the west bank meets the dead in dream and wakes remembering them.

The field breaks people who stay. Some who camped on the west bank in the monsoon were found the next morning walking upstream with no memory of the night. A man who comes back to it night after night stops sleeping anywhere, and the upper river knows two or three families who have kept a relative indoors for the rest of his life after a season of that.

So the upper river keeps its rules. Nobody crosses after sunset. A drover who must cross waters his beasts on the east bank first and drives them over at noon, and the saying he is given is the one every Vedyari district gives: "Cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls."

## Who Comes

- **The Agnī-panthā**, once in every twelve years, for the Night of the Kindled Road at the autumn equinox: every Flame-priest within reach lights his vessel into one fire on the east bank.
- **Scholars of the six kings** from Rājapur, who want to stand where the chronicle's first count against the line was made.
- **Drovers** who need the crossing, and who keep to the rules.

The [[affiliation-thresholdkeepers|Threshold-keepers]] do not come. The dead here are a war's, and nothing on the field may be asked of them.

## Hooks

- **The fire will not catch.** The rendezvous year comes round and the common fire will not light on the east bank either. Either the half-settlement is failing, or someone has done something to the ground, and the sect wants to know which before the next twelve years begin.
- **The king's fire.** A Rājapuri scholar wants a party to carry a lamp lit at [[place-sandstonealtr|the sandstone altar]] across the west bank by night, to test whether the kings' own fire will take where the dead are the kings' victims.
- **The drowned man.** A trader drowned at the ford last season, and his heirs want the Threshold-keepers to ask him where the money went. The keepers refuse twice over: the question is one of trade, and the ground is a war's.
