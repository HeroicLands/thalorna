The **Great Bazaar** occupies the whole of the tenth district of [[place-chandrapur2|Chandrapur]], between the river stair and the customs house. It is a roofed ground rather than a building: a forest of stone piers carrying a tile roof over about nine acres, open on every side, with the assay hall, the weighing floor and the crown's counting rooms built solid at the northern end.

Rough stone is sold here and nowhere else in the city. A parcel is assayed by the crown's assayers before it may be offered, the assay is recorded, and the record is what a buyer is actually buying; a stone sold on a private assay is a stone nobody will take in payment afterwards. The Nine Houses buy here, bid against each other here, and go home to their own quarters to cut what they have bought.

## Why It Is the Crown's

The Bazaar is the tenth district because the Houses could never have agreed whose it was. A market ground inside any one quarter would put that House's **Kulina** in judgement over the others' trade, and none of them would sit still for it. The crown holds it instead, takes the market dues and the assay fee, and is not a bidder.

That is the arrangement, and the arrangement is why there is still a Mahārāja in Chandrapur. A crown that could not tax the quarters can tax what passes between them, and everything passes between them here.

## The Open Floor

The Bazaar's second rule is that a trade done under the roof is done in the open, in the hearing of anyone standing there. Prices are cried. Weights are the crown's and are checked in public at the turn of every season. A foreign factor with no house, no quarter and no charter may buy on the floor exactly as a Kulina may, which is unusual in Vedyara and is the largest single reason the foreign trade comes to this city rather than to some other.

What follows from that is that the Bazaar is the best place in southern Vedyara to hear something. Every Kulina keeps a man there who buys nothing.
