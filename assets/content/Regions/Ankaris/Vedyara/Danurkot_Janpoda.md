The **Danurkot Janpoda** trains the archers of every kingdom in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], and it will train a student of any station, the lowest included. It is the federation of the villages of the upper [[place-sarvedarivr|Sarveda valley]], governed jointly through the great hall of **[[place-dnrktemple|The Mahajaya Temple at Danurkot]]** inside [[place-bowfort|the Bow-Fort]] at [[place-danurkot|Danurkot]], the "Bow-Fort" the janpoda takes its name from. The janpoda holds the land of [[place-danurkotjnpd|Danurkot Janpoda]].

By any measure but one, Danurkot is ordinary. It is one of perhaps eighty janpodas of the upper Sarveda, in a **Sarveda circuit** of more than a thousand, which is the reckoning that matters at the [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, where a janpoda is counted with the others that drink the same river and not with the whole of the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahva-Sangra]]. It is neither large nor wealthy by Vedyari standards. What it has, and what it is known for across the region, is the **Bow**: four ancient archery academies whose graduates have staffed the elite missile companies of every major Vedyari kingdom for fifteen centuries.

At the [Festival of the Drawn Bow]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} each spring, an instructor of the [[place-highdraw|High Draw]] lines up the year's new students in the temple court and gives them the first lesson before anyone touches a stave. "Look at the man beside you. He may be a **Sentija**'s son and he may be a cultivator's, and inside these walls it changes nothing about how either of you is taught. Outside them, the people who hire you will care a great deal. Learn to shoot first. The rest you will learn on the road."

## The Four Academies

Each academy keeps its own master, its own hereditary teaching lineages and its own practices, and each has a hall of its own in the bow-fort:

| Academy                                   | Teaches                                   | Its graduates                                                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[place-highdraw\|The High Draw]]         | The great longbow; the oldest of the four | Heavy infantry support in every Vedyari kingdom                                  |
| [[place-swifthand\|The Swift Hand]]       | Archery from horseback                    | Sought by every kingdom that fields cavalry                                      |
| [[place-patienteye\|The Patient Eye]]     | Accuracy at extreme range                 | Scouts, hunters, fortress sharpshooters, and the personal bowmen of the nobility |
| [[place-hiddenstring\|The Hidden String]] | Ambush and concealment                    | Scouts and raiders, hard to recruit and bound by a vow of selectivity            |

The academies cooperate loosely within the janpoda and compete fiercely for students, for prestige and for the reserved seats they hold between them on the sabhra. [[place-hiddenstring|The Hidden String]]'s vow is the one a hirer must know about in advance: it refuses any contract it considers dishonorable.

## The Sabhra

The sabhra has forty-two members, convened by the senior priest of the Mahajaya temple. The constituent villages are represented in proportion to their size, and the masters of the four academies hold reserved seats as **Dhanuracharyas**. The sabhra meets monthly in the campaign season and every second month for the rest of the year, and a major decision needs two-thirds of it. By long convention the four masters sit together at every formal session and answer together on any question of war, and their joint word settles the janpoda's foreign policy.

## Society

Danurkot keeps the ordinary Vedyari order of stations, with the Sentija households, who bear arms by right and owe service in the host, unusually numerous and unusually wealthy for a janpoda. About one Danurkot in eight is Sentija, against perhaps one in twenty in an ordinary janpoda. Their money comes from three places: the fees aspiring archers bring from across Vedyara, the standing retainers paid by patron kingdoms whose officers come out of the academies, and the donations that men the academies trained make to the Mahajaya temple.

The other stations stand in ordinary proportions. The **Danhija** households handle the considerable trade in arms, since Danurkot bows and arrows are sold across Vedyara, together with the provisioning, lodging and equipment-keeping the academies need. The **Ritrija** households sit in and around the temple, and the senior priest convenes the sabhra. The villages supply the bow itself: bamboo staves from [[place-veniban|Veniban]], horn and sinew from [[place-vishanagram|Vishanagram]], reed shafts from [[place-shiraban|Shiraban]], wax for the bindings from [[place-madupada|Madupada]].

Danurkot allows more social mobility than most Vedyari polities. The academies accept students of any station, the lowest included, and within the janpoda a graduate takes certain Sentija prerogatives whatever his birth: the right to bear arms, the right to hold land in his own name, and the right to speak in the sabhra on military matters. Orthodox opinion finds the custom scandalous. The sabhra holds that the academies would not be what they are without it.

## Religion

The Mahajaya temple at Danurkot is the religious heart of the janpoda and one of the major [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] temples of inland Vedyara. In the tradition of the [[affiliation-vrnkpnthn|Varnuka Pantheon]], [[lore-mahajayadty|Mahajaya]] is the preserver-goddess, mistress of order, of prosperity and of the keeping of dorma, and she suits a janpoda whose identity is disciplined martial training in the service of legitimate authority.

The temple keeps the standard Mahajaya cycle of daily, weekly, seasonal and annual rites and adds three observances of its own:

- The Festival of the Drawn Bow, at the spring equinox, when each year's incoming students present themselves at the temple for blessing.
- **The [Festival of the Returning]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}**, in autumn, when graduates back from service abroad ritually re-enter the janpoda and are welcomed home. It was first kept for the graduates who answered the masters' recall in [[lore-hghlndwar|the Highland War]].
- **The [Watch of the Bow-String]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}**, the night before any major engagement involving Danurkot graduates anywhere in Vedyara, when the temple's bowmen-priests keep vigil for their own.

Shrines to the other Varnuka gods stand throughout the villages. [[place-vyalsrctmp|The temple of Vyalendra]] at the Sarveda source-spring is a popular pilgrimage for those seeking children, and the [[affiliation-rasikara|Rasikara]] shrine in the cremation-ground outside the town receives the dead.

## Economy

The janpoda is moderately wealthy by janpoda standards and not rich by a city-state's. Most of its wealth is held by the temple and the Sentija lineages, and the cultivating villages live as well as any in inland Vedyara and no better.

## Defense

Danurkot is defended far better than a janpoda of its size usually is. The four academies keep their own training-corps, which field eight hundred elite archers between them on short notice, and the villages can muster another two thousand fighting men of the Sentija households. The bow-fort stands on megalithic foundations that no siege technique short of sustained heavy magic will breach, and in extremity it shelters the whole janpoda.

Danurkot has not been seriously threatened in three centuries. Its reputation is its first defense, since no neighboring kingdom wants to learn what eight hundred elite archers cost on their own ground, and every Danurkot engagement in recent memory has been fought by academy graduates serving abroad in the wars of patron kingdoms. The last threat was [[lore-hghlndwar|the Highland War]], about 415, stopped at [[place-sharamukha|Sharamukha]].

## Relations

Danurkot keeps formal patron-client relations with three neighboring kingdoms, small mountain kingdoms whose names change as their dynasties rise and fall. It keeps informal supplier relations with most of the major Vedyari city-states, [[affiliation-chendrapur|Chendrapur]] and the inland branches of the great coastal merchant houses among them. Its closest tie is to [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyalendra]], which has taken a Danurkot graduate as commander of its city watch for as long as anyone can remember; the two keep up a continuous correspondence on military and commercial matters.

Within the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahva-Sangra]], Danurkot's standing is high. The senior priest of the Mahajaya temple is one of the senior voices at every Mahva-Melka, and the four academy masters are routinely consulted on military questions between janpodas.

## Commerce and Currency

Danurkot holds one of the five seats in the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]], and the bow trade and the academies' retainers run through [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]]'s letters as readily as through coin. Like every janpoda it strikes no coin of its own. See [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]] for the system and [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] for the institution.

## Outside the Ladder

A person placed outside the tarona, by transgression or by birth, is excluded from the wells, the temples and the courts.
