Tāmravana (910) is the smelting village, a mile below the panning ground on the same stream. It takes its name from the copper that was worked here before the iron was, and the copper is long finished.

The iron comes from a poor seam on the back slope and would not be worth carrying anywhere else. What makes the work is the charcoal and the knowledge, and the bloom sells as far as the eastern coast. The village burns its own charcoal in the slope forest under a cutting-rotation the sabhā sets.

The jewelers of Lower Suvarnagiri want the same wood, and the charcoal-burners of Vanapāda cut it for them. The quarrel over the rotation comes to the sabhā about once in five years and is settled the same way each time.
