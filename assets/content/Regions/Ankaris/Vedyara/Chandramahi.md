The **Chandramahī** rises at [[place-chandrprbh|Chandraprabhava]], under [[place-suryashkhr|Sūryaśikhara]], and runs some twelve hundred miles south and west to a delta on the southern sea. It is the shortest of the four great rivers and the richest. The country it crosses is the gem country, and the city at its mouth has spent four centuries making sure nothing else is.

Boats work the river from the delta up to the last of the terraces below the highland kingdom. Above that it is a mountain water and carries nothing but log-rafts.

## What the River Does

It floods on snowmelt first and monsoon second. That makes it the most predictable of the four and the least forgiving when it is wrong. A warm spring on [[place-sthrnwall|the Southern Wall]] puts the rise three weeks early. The janapadas of the middle valley plant by the temple almanac and not by the water, because the water has fooled them.

Its lower valley carries the gem-road, the traffic of worked stone, gold and finished metal coming down out of the highlands to the coast. That traffic is why the river has a fleet on it, a customs regime along it, and a permanent argument about both.

## Circuits

The river is one of the four pilgrim-circuits by which the janapadas are seated at the great assembly. A janapada of the Chandramahī circuit speaks at the gathering through the circuit's speaker, unless it is one of the forty that hold a seat in their own right. Which circuit a border janapada belongs to is a question that has gone to litigation, in one case for two hundred and ten years.
