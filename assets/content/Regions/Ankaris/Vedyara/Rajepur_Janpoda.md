# terran_analog: "Medieval South Indian temple-republic that emerged from the ruins of a failed kingdom—a Chola-era brahmadeya village federation centered on a temple complex built atop or alongside an abandoned royal capital, governed by an assembly that explicitly preserves the memory of the displaced dynasty"

The **Rajepur Janpoda** dissolved its own kingdom, and it has opened every session of its assembly since by reciting the kings it ended. It is the federation of the villages of the [[place-mahanodi|Mahanodi floodplain]] in the central plains, one of the larger and wealthier janpodas of the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahva-Sangra]], and it took its name and its temple-seat from the ruins of a royal capital. Every Vedyari course in political philosophy teaches it as the classical case of a janpoda that _replaced_ a kingdom where the others grew up beside one. The janpoda holds the land of [[place-rajepurjnpd|Rajepur Janpoda]].

The "King's-Town" of the name is a memory. There has been no king here for nine hundred years and more.

Visiting scholars are met at the gate of [[place-rajavalilib|the Rajavali Library]] by one of the **Memory-Keeper**'s junior scribes, and the first thing the scribe tells them is a warning. "If you have come for the [Day of the Dissolution]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, you will get nothing done on it. Nobody sells, nobody works, nobody opens a book but the one being read aloud. Come the day after, and the whole town will want to tell you why."

## The Fall of the Kingdom

[[place-rajepur|Rajepur]] was the later capital of [[lore-mhndkngdm|the Kingdom of Mahanodi]], a middling Vedyari kingdom of the early classical period. Its ruling dynasty traced its descent from a heroic ancestor of legendary virtue, as Vedyari dynasties generally do. The chronicles give it some four centuries of flourishing. For the last two and a half of them it ruled from Rajepur, where its kings founded their capital in the year of [[lore-stndrdmdstn|the standardization at Madustan]]. Across the four centuries it dominated the upper Mahanodi valley, fielded a respectable army, kept extensive temple-patronage, and produced several monarchs the classical chronicles recount as exemplary.

It collapsed from within, in the standard Vedyari narrative of kingly decline. The Memory-Keepers recite the last six monarchs as a procession of progressively worse rulers: a dilettante, a cruel man, an incompetent, a paranoiac, a child-tyrant, and a drunkard who let his own household guards loot the granaries while a famine ran in the villages around him. Tradition holds that the last king was poisoned by his own cook, who could no longer endure the suffering of his neighbors. The cruel man is the king the chronicle names [Dandabira]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and his slaughter at [[lore-unburnford|the Unburned Ford]] is the first count in the indictment; the upper-river villages send no delegate to any assembly that sits here on its anniversary.

The people sought no other king. They went to the senior priest of the great [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyalendra]] temple, which stood beside the royal palace on centuries of dynastic patronage, and asked him to convene a sabhra.

The sabhra met for forty days. It declared the kingdom dissolved and the dynastic line ended, with full honors and ceremonial acknowledgment of what the line had done in its better generations, and it reorganized the former kingdom as a federation of villages governed jointly through the temple. Every clause of the decree decided what to keep and what to unmake:

- The palace was demolished, and its stones went to enlarge the temple. Its cellars lie open at the north end of the town as [[place-palacecellar|the Palace Cellars]].
- The royal granaries became common stores.
- The royal army was disbanded, and its men were given parcels of land and taken into the cultivating and artisan households of the new janpoda.
- The council-chamber was kept, and the record in it was kept whole.

The classical chronicles put this in the first year of [[lore-agecopyists|the Age of Copyists]] (1 AC) in [[lore-mdstncnt|the Madustan count]], 241 BF in the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr#the-vylarian-calendar|Vylarian reckoning]], some nine hundred and sixty years ago. Rajepur has governed itself by the same arrangement ever since, with adjustments at the margins.

[[lore-fortydays|The Forty Days]] records the sabhra's answer:

: They Asked for No King {#forty-days-excerpt}

> They asked for no king.
>
> _Gloss._ The last four words are the whole of the matter, and the chronicler wrote them without comment.

## The Memory-Keeper

Rajepur's government carries one office no other janpoda has, the Memory-Keeper (Smrtibhara). A single family has held it in continuous succession since the dissolution, and its duties are four:

- **The genealogy.** The Memory-Keeper keeps the complete genealogy of the displaced royal line, before the dissolution and notionally after it. The last king left no surviving descendants, so the genealogy records who would have inherited had the line continued, and each generation a new "what would have been" name is entered.
- **The recitation.** At the opening of every formal session of the sabhra, the Memory-Keeper recites the dynastic history in full, from the founding ancestor through the dissolution, and concludes with the formal acknowledgment that the kingdom is no more and the janpoda governs in its place.
- **The precedents.** The Memory-Keeper advises the sabhra where the precedents of the old kingdom bear on a present decision. The office has no vote and no authority over policy; it is consultative and ceremonial.
- **The library.** The Memory-Keeper keeps [[place-rajavalilib|the Rajavali Library]], the archive of every chronicle, treaty and legal text of the kingdom-period and of every sabhra session since the dissolution. It is housed in the old royal council-chamber, now an annex of the temple complex.

Each session's [[lore-rcitkngsmhnd|Recitation of the Kings of Mahanodi]] closes on the janpoda itself:

: The Acknowledgment {#recitation-excerpt}

```poetry {form=recitation lang=en}
The Mahanodi kingdom is no more;
  it ended with its due.
The janpoda governs in its place,
  and does so by your leave.
```

The Memory-Keepers are one of the most respected scholarly families in the inland part of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]]. Historians from across the region consult their library, their authority on Vedyari political history has no rival, and their ceremonial role in the sabhra is taken with great seriousness. The present Memory-Keeper, [Ramavahan Smrtibhara]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is a quiet scholar in his late fifties and the author of the most respected modern commentary on the dissolution.

Visiting scholars ask why a dynasty that cannot return is kept in memory at all. The Rajapuri answer is that the dissolution was a _judgment_: the kingdom failed because it deserved to fail, and the recitation is the standing reminder of why kingship is dangerous. The sabhra governs by consent of the constituent villages, and the recitation reminds it that any government, its own included, can fail and be dissolved by the process that ended the Kingdom of Mahanodi.

## The Sabhra and the Temple

The sabhra has thirty-six members, convened by the senior priest of the great Vyalendra temple. Each constituent village sends its representatives, and the principal artisan, merchant and scholar shrenis of the central town hold seats. The Memory-Keeper sits with it by long tradition and opens every formal session.

The great **Vyalendra temple** at [[place-rajepur|Rajepur]] is the religious and political heart of the janpoda. In Varnuka teaching, [[lore-vyalendradty|Vyalendra]] is the creator of the [[affiliation-vrnkpnthn|Varnuka pantheon]], the world's first light, the shaper from whom all forms emerge. The temple's main hall holds [[place-sandstonealtr|the Sandstone Altar]], the original altar of Vyalendra, a sandstone block cut at the founding of the dynastic capital twelve centuries ago, and the great hall in which the sabhra meets. The outer courts hold the four-story granary, still the community store; the Rajavali Library; the school open to the children of every constituent village; and the great hostel that lodges the pilgrims who come from across central Vedyara at the spring festival.

By long custom the senior priest of the temple convenes the sabhra. [Karunamoya Podma-Hasta]{.name data-type="being/npc"} holds both offices. She is a woman of about sixty, the third woman to hold the position in nine centuries, and a respected theologian of the Vyalendra tradition. She has presided over the sabhra for fourteen years, and with the Memory-Keeper she is the senior ceremonial figure of the polity.

## Society

Rajepur keeps the ordinary Vedyari order of stations with one Rajapuri specialty: an unusually large and influential body of **scholar families**, descended in part from the educated court functionaries of the old kingdom and absorbed into the new order at the dissolution. They live in and around the temple complex and staff the school, the library and the sabhra's secretariat, and Rajepur's share of Vedyari classical scholarship is out of all proportion to its size.

The Danaja households handle the river trade up and down the Mahanodi, the considerable manuscript trade out of the temple library included. The Senaja households are few and over the centuries have largely merged into the artisan households. Rajepur has kept no military tradition since the dissolution and has needed none.

## Religion and the Day of the Dissolution

Beyond the great Vyalendra temple, Rajepur keeps shrines to the other Varnuka gods throughout its villages. The two that matter most are the [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] shrine at the upstream village of [[place-khandapur|Khandapur]], where farmers bless the irrigation works at planting, and the [[affiliation-rasikara|Rasikara]] cremation-ground at the downstream village of [[place-muktoshvara|Muktoshvara]], which receives the dead of every constituent village. The standard Vedyari religious calendar is kept with full Rajapuri ceremony. The great event of the year is the [Spring Festival of Vyalendra]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, which draws perhaps fifteen thousand visitors from neighboring janpodas and city-states.

The **Day of the Dissolution** falls each year on the anniversary of the decree, and it is Rajepur's alone. No business is done and no labor performed. The whole population gathers in the temple precincts to hear the Memory-Keeper recite the full dynastic history and the decree from beginning to end, which takes most of the day. At its close every adult Rajapuri drinks a cup of Mahanodi water blessed by the senior priest and renews the janpoda's compact by it. Children attend and do not drink until their fifteenth year, when they are formally enrolled as citizens. Other janpodas keep founding festivals of their own, and none carries the weight of this one.

## Economy

Rajepur is moderately wealthy by janpoda standards. The temple holds a great deal and holds it as community wealth in the ordinary janpoda way, and its senior priest lives no better than any scholar household in the town. A modest endowment of common funds, built from centuries of pilgrim donations and managed by the temple, pays for famine relief, irrigation works, the school and the occasional substantial contribution to the [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}.

The manuscript trade is the town's steadiest income, and the sugar the janpoda is known for is cut and boiled at [[place-gudagram|Gudagram]] and shipped from the town.

## Defense

Rajepur has had no army since the dissolution. Its villages can muster perhaps a thousand fighting men at need, cultivators and artisans. Its answer to a military threat is threefold: an appeal to its patron city-state, [[affiliation-chendrapur|Chendrapur]], under a formal protection arrangement nearly two centuries old; an appeal to the Mahva-Sangra for inter-janapada disapproval of the aggressor; and the Mahanodi floodplain itself, which is hard ground for a sustained campaign in the wet season. The arrangement dates from the season a kingdom up the river held the head-gate at Khandapur ([[lore-rjprprotct|Rajepur Under Protection]]).

The janpoda has not been seriously threatened in living memory. The last armed conflict on Rajapuri ground was a bandit-clearing carried out jointly with two neighboring janpodas eighty-three years ago.

## Famous Figures

Karunamoya Podma-Hasta
: senior priest of the Vyalendra temple and convenor of the sabhra, about sixty, the third woman to hold the office in nine centuries.

Ramavahan Smrtibhara
: the Memory-Keeper, in his late fifties, author of the standard modern commentary on the dissolution.

[[being-anrjhrshkr|Anuraja Harshakar]]
: a Rajapuri-born scholar resident in [[place-chendrapur2|Chendrapur]], who corresponds with several of the major Vedyari kingdoms on the merits of further dissolutions on the Rajapuri model. He is controversial and well received in some janpodas. The kingdoms call him a dangerous radical and have asked Chendrapur to expel him; Chendrapur has declined.

## Commerce and Currency

Rajepur holds one of the five seats in the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]], so its sabhra has a voice in the [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking system]] that [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective]] runs. Like every janpoda it strikes no coin of its own; its river traders deal in the silver chendra [[place-moonhouse|the Moon House]] strikes at Chendrapur and in whatever copper comes up the river. See [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]] for the system and [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] for the institution.

## Outside the Ladder

A person placed outside the tarona, by transgression or by birth, is excluded from the wells, the temples and the courts.
