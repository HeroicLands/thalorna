**The Silenced Hall** stands in the hill country below the conch-door road, on the way up to [[place-sangrafort|the Sangra-fort]], and it is the whole of what is left of a janpoda. [Tirnugram]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, the village of the bell, refused the conch-door adventurer about 320 AF, and he shut its sabhra in this hall and fired the roof ([[lore-silenthall|the Silenced Hall]]). The [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} of 320 mourned the janpoda on the roll, divided its land, and had the doors bricked up.

## What Is There

A temple hall of dressed stone, longer than most janpoda halls, roofless since the night it burned. The doors are bricked to the lintel with the Melka's brick, plainer and redder than the hall's stone. The bronze bell that gave the janpoda its name, a _tirnu_, still hangs in the gate on its iron bar, green with five centuries of rain. Nobody rings it. The hill villages that took [Tirnugram]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}'s land have let the fields round the hall gho back to scrub, and the path to the gate is kept open only by the priests who use it.

## Not a Blood-field

The hall was its own pyre. The sabhra burned with fire, the dead passed, and the ground is not a _shorukshetra_ (blood-field). What burned with them was the janpoda's record: its survey, its boundary register and its genealogies, kept in the temple as every janpoda keeps them.

That does not make the hall a pleasant place at night. The hill people keep away from it after dark, because a building where a whole assembly died in one fire is a sad and heavy place, and because of the bell.

## Who Comes

- **Priests of the [[place-bharavarivr|Bharava circuit]]** on their way up to a turn at the Sangra-fort, who stop to light a lamp at the bricked door and gho on.
- **Descendants of Tirnugram's lineages**, scattered through the neighboring janpodas, who come on the anniversary and say the names of their families at the gate.

## Hooks

- **The petition.** A descendant of Tirnugram's lineages is preparing a petition to the next Melka, at [[place-sangema|Sangema]], asking that Tirnugram be recognized as newly formed. Recognition would undo a four-century division of its land, and the janpodas that hold the land want the petitioner discredited before the assembly meets.
- **The bell.** A priest of the Sangra-fort, coming down late, heard the bell ring once in the night. Nobody else heard it, the bar has not moved, and the bell's clapper was taken out by the Melka's order five centuries ago.
- **The survey.** The petition fails without proof of the janpoda's old boundaries, and every copy burned in the hall. A rumor puts a second copy in a temple on the Tantsou side of the conch-door, where the adventurer came from.
