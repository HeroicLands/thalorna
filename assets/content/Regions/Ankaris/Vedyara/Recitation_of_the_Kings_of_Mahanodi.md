The Memory-Keeper of the [[affiliation-rajeprjnpd|Rajepur Janpoda]] speaks this recitation at the opening of every formal session of the sabhra, in the great hall of the [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyalendra]] temple at [[place-rajepur|Rajepur]], and speaks it again in the temple precinct on the [Day of the Dissolution]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, together with the dissolution decree. In full it runs from the founding ancestor of [[lore-mhndkngdm|the Kingdom of Mahanodi]] through every king of the line to the dissolution, and it closes on the formal acknowledgment that the kingdom is no more and the janpoda governs in its place. Its six worsening kings are the standard Vedyari narrative of kingly decline, and it carries the poisoning by the king's cook as tradition, not as record. The keeper holds no vote, and the recitation takes no side in the business the sabhra turns to after it.

The verse is codified, like the rest of Vedyari devotional making: couplets of a long line of ten syllables answered by a short line of six, with fixed openings and a fixed close. The text here keeps the opening, the six, the forty days and the close. The line between the ancestor and the six is recited at its place in every session and is marked where it falls.

The verses are in couplets of ten and six syllables.
