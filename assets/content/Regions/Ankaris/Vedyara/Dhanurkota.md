**Danurkota** (2,000) is fullest in the month before the spring equinox, when aspirants from every part of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] and from three kingdoms beyond it climb the slope to [[place-bowfort|the bow-fort]] and every lodging-house in the town fills. It is the capital of the [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Danurkota Janapoda]] and the bow-fort the janapoda is named for. It stands on a low hill where [[place-sarvadarivr|the Sarveda]] bends west round an outcrop of red rock, at the point the river comes out of the northern hills into the plain.

The fort holds the [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] temple, the four academy halls, the sabhra chamber and the granary. The town proper spreads down the slope below the walls and along the riverbank. In extremity the walls take the whole janapoda, and the granary is stocked against that year by year.

## What the Town Does

More than half the town works for the academies. There are lodging-houses for aspirants, sixty or seventy of them, graded by what a family can pay. There are bowyers, fletchers, string-makers, leather-workers and armorers. There are the provisioners who feed several hundred resident students and their servants, and the carters who bring the food in.

A quarter of the aspirants are sent home inside the year. The town takes their fees either way.

## The Cremation-Ground

The [[affiliation-rasikara|Rasikara]] shrine stands outside the walls on the downstream side, where the ground is poor and the wind carries away from the town. The dead of the town and of the nearer villages are burned there.

Its priests are of the janapoda and are not of the Mahajaya temple. The two establishments deal with each other at arm's length and have done so for a very long time.
