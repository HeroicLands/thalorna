Dhanurkota (2,000) is the capital of the [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota Janapada]] and the bow-fort the janapada is named for. It stands on a low hill where the Sarvada bends west round an outcrop of red rock, at the point the river comes out of the northern hills into the plain.

The fort holds the Mahájaya temple, the four academy halls, the sabhā chamber and the granary. The town proper spreads down the slope below the walls and along the riverbank. In extremity the walls take the whole janapada, and the granary is stocked against that year by year.

## What the Town Does

More than half the town works for the academies. There are lodging-houses for aspirants, sixty or seventy of them, graded by what a family can pay. There are bowyers, fletchers, string-makers, leather-workers and armorers. There are the provisioners who feed several hundred resident students and their servants, and the carters who bring the food in.

Aspirants come up in the month before the spring equinox, from every part of Vedyara and from three kingdoms beyond it. A quarter of them are sent home inside the year. The town takes their fees either way.

## The Cremation-Ground

The Rásikara shrine stands outside the walls on the downstream side, where the ground is poor and the wind carries away from the town. The dead of the town and of the nearer villages are burned there.

Its priests are of the janapada and are not of the Mahájaya temple. The two establishments deal with each other at arm's length and have done so for a very long time.
