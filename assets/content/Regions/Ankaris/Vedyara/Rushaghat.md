**Rushaghat** (620), the dust landing, is a river-town without a river. It stands on its old levee in the floodplain of [[place-mahanodi|the Mahanodi]], a half-day's walk inland from the water, and its broad stone ghat-steps gho down eleven courses to a field of millet. The river moved away from it in one of the three changes of course the record never dated, and the town stayed where it was.

## The Stairs

The ghats are the finest stonework in the village and the best kept. They run the length of the old waterfront, three stairs wide, with a temple of [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] at the head of the central one and the platforms where the boats tied up still set with their iron rings.

The village uses them every day for something. The lowest platforms are threshing floors, swept and plastered with dung before the harvest, and the millet is beaten out on the stone where cargo used to be landed. The middle courses are the market on the fourth day of the week, with sellers on one step and buyers on the step below. The top step is where the sabhra's delegate stands to read out what the janpoda has decided, because that is where a voice carries to the whole village. Children learn to count on the courses.

Below the stairs the old channel runs off in both directions as a long crescent of reed-bed and black soil, and the field in front of the steps is the best ground the village has. A cultivator here plows a riverbed.

## What the Town Keeps From the River

- **The boat-shreni.** Rushaghat still has a shrena of boatmen, nineteen households strong, and no boats. The shrena keeps its hall, its register and its seat in the village assembly, and it sends its sons to the river at [[place-rajepur|Rajepur]] to work the boats there. Once a year it carries a boat it keeps in its hall down the ghat-steps and sets it on the field, and the priest blesses it as though it floated.
- **The river-stair rites.** Funerals still gho down to the lowest step. The dead are carried to the water's place, and a pot of water fetched from the Mahanodi that morning is poured on the stone before the procession turns back up the steps to the pyre.
- **The flood mark.** A line cut in the temple wall marks the highest water the town ever saw. Every year in the rains the dead channel fills a hand's depth or two, and every year somebody walks down to see whether the river is coming back.

## What a Party Finds Here

The village is quiet, prosperous and welcoming, and it asks visitors the same question first: "Did you come up from the water?" A traveler who did is given the stranger's place on the top step and the best of the evening.

## Hooks

- **The channel fills.** This year the dead channel has filled to the third step and is still rising, and the river has not done that in living memory. If the Mahanodi is moving back, Rushaghat becomes a river-town again and the village downstream of it loses its water. The boat-shreni wants someone to gho upriver and find where the bank is failing before the sabhra hears of it from the downstream village.
- **The ring that holds.** A cultivator plowing the old riverbed has turned up a mooring post with a bronze plate on it, cut with the seal of a [[lore-mhndkngdm|Mahanodi king]] and a date in the old count. [[place-rajavalilib|The Rajavali Library]]'s copyists want it, because it could date the move the record never dated.
- **The shrena's boat.** The boat the shrena carries down the steps each year is older than the move and has never touched water since. A buyer from the coast has offered the shrena more than the village's harvest for it, and the shrena's eldest refuses to say why it will not sell.
