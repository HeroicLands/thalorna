Hiranyadhārā (980) lies two miles above Middle Suvarnagiri on the richest of the mountain's streams, and is where most of the gold is won. Nine of the twenty-eight hereditary panning families live here, which is more than any other village holds.

The panning ground is a half-mile of gravel bar and pool. It is worked from the first clear water after the rains until the stream drops in the spring, and a family takes perhaps forty ounces out of it in a good year. Every family's take goes down to the weighing-station the same day. The village keeps no gold of its own and has no strong-room in it.

A sabhā guard of twenty men is quartered at the head of the village the year round. They are there for the road rather than for the water, and everybody in the village knows the difference.
