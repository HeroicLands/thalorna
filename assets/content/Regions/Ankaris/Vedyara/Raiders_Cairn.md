The **Raiders' Cairn** stands in a narrow gorge below the mountain road of [[place-suvarnagirijnpd|Suvarnagiri]], a half-day down from [[place-girimarga|Girimārga]], where the road drops to the stream and crosses it on stepping stones. About 80 BF the horse of [[affiliation-bharyastan|Bharyastān]] caught the raiders who had taken a month's gold off the weighing-station, and killed most of them on the gravel below the crossing ([[lore-lastgldrd|the Last Gold Raid]]). The cairn marks the place.

## The Stones

The cairn began as the stones piled on the raiders' bodies, because the king would not carry them up to the janapada or down to their kingdom and the gorge has no ground to dig. It is now a shoulder-high mound of river stone the length of a cart. Every spring the tribute party that rides up from Bharyastān to fetch the year's gold stops at the crossing, and each rider adds a stone going up. Nobody adds one coming down, because coming down they carry the gold, and the custom says the gold does not stop at the place it was once taken.

The raiders were burned by nobody. They were a few dozen, and their kingdom took no interest in them. The gorge is a cold place and a quiet one, and the tribute escort does not camp in it, but it is a grave and not a field.

## Hooks

- **The marked stone.** A stone from the cairn has turned up in a [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] counting-house at Chandrapur, with a weight mark cut on its underside in the old style of the weighing-station. Someone is tracing the raid's gold, which the record says was all returned to the grain.
- **The tribute party.** This year's escort is short a rider, and the captain offers a place to anyone who can sit a horse. The ride up is easy. The talk round the fire is about who in Bharyastān's court wants the treaty to lapse at the next succession.
