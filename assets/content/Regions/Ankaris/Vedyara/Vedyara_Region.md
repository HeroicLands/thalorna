# terran_analog: Indian subcontinent
---

Start with the [[doc-vedyaraadventurersguide|Vedyara Adventurer's Guide]] for an introduction to the people and places of the subcontinent.

Vedyara is one civilization of a hundred and ten million people shut behind a wall of ice, and it has never been one state. It projects southeast from the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] into three seas, and the world's highest mountains close it off to the north. Everything that matters here comes down off that wall: the four great rivers, the snowmelt that floods them, the gods who live on the peaks, and the trade of the six crossings. An outsider remembers Vedyara for its gems, its cloth and its learning, and for the monsoon that decides when any of it can move.

A factor of the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] who has worked every road on the subcontinent puts it to a newcomer this way: "Learn three things before you learn a single price. Which river you are on, because that tells you whose assembly speaks for the ground. Which calendar the court follows, because they differ by a day and a day can cost you a season. And what the monsoon is doing, because it closes the sea for four months and nobody here will apologize to you for it."

## The Wall and the Six Crossings

The [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] run the whole northern edge of Vedyara, and no realm claims them. Their southern face is [[place-sthrnwall|the Southern Wall]], an escarpment of some fifteen hundred miles that holds the highest peaks in Thalorna, [[place-suryashkhr|Sūryashikhara]] first among them. North of the range's central and western reaches lies the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]]; north of [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]], where the range turns east, lies [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]].

Only six crossings are recognized along the entire range, and the routes and safe seasons of each are known only to the mountain guides:

- [[place-meghadvara|Meghadvāra]], the storm-door, the westernmost crossing, shut from below by the monsoon.
- [[place-gudesroad|The Guides' Road]], between the two western doors, with no fortress, toll, shrine or blessing.
- [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]], the greatest of the passes, under the fortress-capital of [[place-suryagarha|Sūryāgarha]], forking beyond the throat for the Khazryn and for Tānvür.
- [[place-tamradvara|Tāmradvāra]], the copper-door, up [[place-slvrgorges|the Silver Gorges]] to the Khazryn's eastern marches.
- [[place-jnanadvara|Jñānadvāra]], the scholars' door, the long crossing of the Eastern Reach to Tānvür's western frontier.
- [[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvāra]], the conch-door, the easternmost and worst, watched from [[place-sanghafort|the Sangha-fort]].

Above the last [[place-pssshrines|Pass-Shrine]] the roads belong to the guide-peoples: the [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] on the western and central crossings and the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] on the eastern two. No Vedyari court governs there, and a guide-mother's refusal ends a caravan's day whatever its papers say.

At the western end the wall comes apart into the dry foothills of [[place-wstrndscnt|the Western Descent]]. [[place-marchroad|The march road]] goes round that end into [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]], and it is the one way into Vedyara that crosses no pass.

## Rivers, Plateau and Forest

Four great rivers leave the ice at four glacier-springs, and the Varnaka faith holds each spring to be the literal source its river flowed from at the beginning of time. The springs are pilgrimages in their own right, and half the subcontinent drinks off this face of the range.

| River                             | Source                                | Reaches the sea      | Known for                                                       |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[place-chandrmahi\|Chandramahī]] | [[place-chandrprbh\|Chandraprabhava]] | A delta on the south | The gem-road, and the city-state of Chandrapur at its mouth     |
| [[place-sarvadarivr\|Sarvada]]    | [[place-sarvaprbhv\|Sarvaprabhava]]   | The east coast       | The bow-fort janapada upstream and the copyist towns below      |
| [[place-mahanadi\|Mahānadi]]      | [[place-mahaprbhva\|Mahāprabhava]]    | The east coast       | The longest and most populous river, and the twelve-yearly Mela |
| [[place-bharavarivr\|Bhārava]]    | [[place-bhrvprbhav\|Bhāravaprabhava]] | The east coast       | The gold highlands, the temple forests and the pilgrim road     |

The valleys hold wide fertile floodplains, and the Chandramahī delta carries two rice crops a year. The Mahānadi has changed its channel four times within written record and drowned a royal capital doing it, so the villages of the central plain sit on old levees and on mounds raised over generations.

Between the rivers lie forests and the high, semi-arid tableland of [[place-vandhyabhumi|Vandhyabhūmi]], where wells are property and the ground is not, and where the ruined capital of [[place-madhavendra|Mādhavendra]] stands on open pasture. The lower Bhārava runs through [[place-bharavavana|Bhāravavana]], six hundred miles of tropical forest held in temple estates. Below the Eastern Reach lies [[place-forestnoroad|the Forest of No Road]], which no janapada has ever sent an assembly into.

## Three Coasts

The eastern shore faces the warm [[place-suryasamdra|Sūrya-samudra]], the sea the sun rises from; three of the four rivers reach the sea on this coast, and the ports stand at their mouths. The southern shore faces the [[place-meghsamdra|Megha-samudra]], the sea the monsoon comes from, and takes the heaviest rain in Thalorna. Both coasts are humid and monsoon-washed. The shorter western shore faces the [[place-sandhysmdr|Sandhyā-samudra]], the sea of evening; it is much drier, no great river reaches it, and it has no deep natural harbor anywhere along its length.

Offshore to the southeast, the [[place-suryaislnds|Sūrya-samudra Islands]] hold small kingdoms of their own and the Vedyari merchant colonies.

## Climate

A wet season and a dry season govern the region. The monsoon builds over the southern sea and comes ashore within a few days, falling hardest on the southern and eastern coasts, and the interior plateau stays comparatively dry. While the rain lasts the eastern sea is closed to shipping for four months, and no hull that can be got up a river stays outside.

The highest Grazian summits stay glaciated and snow-covered all year; the lower slopes take seasonal snow. Snowmelt and monsoon runoff together feed the great rivers. A warm spring on the wall puts them up three weeks early and drowns the seed of whoever planted by the old reckoning, and the dispute between the priests who sight the year and the reckoners who compute it is, at bottom, an argument about that snow.

## Who Holds the Ground

No ruler speaks for Vedyara. Most of its people live in janapadas, small temple-republics of a few villages governed by a sabhā (assembly) in the hall of their central temple, and the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahā-Sangha]] that joins them convenes once in twelve years and does not govern. Beside them stand the kingdoms and city-states, most of them larger than any single janapada, and a few bodies that fit neither form:

Three janapadas set the pattern for the rest: [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]], which dissolved its own kingdom; [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]], which holds a gold mountain under a constitution; and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]], which trains the archers of every kingdom. Five polities share the [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran banking system]] through the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]], and the Merchant Collective's letters of credit move money between all of them.

## Peoples and Beasts

Vedyara is overwhelmingly human, and nearly all of it is [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]]: one language, one classical learning, one faith in the [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka]] gods, and one order of stations read from a mark on the wrist. The guide-peoples above the Pass-Shrines stand outside that order entirely. The gathering peoples of the Bhārava forest work under temple licence and are counted by nobody. Enclaves of [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] are rumored in the Forest of No Road and have never been confirmed.

The [[being-vdyrnrhn|Vedyaran rhinoceros]] keeps to the marshes and tall grass of the floodplains. [[being-vdyrnelphnt|Vedyaran elephants]] serve as temple mounts and war beasts.
