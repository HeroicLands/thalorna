Start with the [[doc-vedyaraadventurersguide|Vedyara Adventurer's Guide]] for an introduction to the people and places of the subcontinent.

Vedyara projects southeast from [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. The unaligned [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]], the highest range in the world, rise along its northern edge above broad river valleys, a dry interior plateau, deep forests, and three distinct coasts. The [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] lies north of the range's central and western reaches; [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]] lies north of its eastern reach. At the western end the mountains descend into foothills, and the open land route to [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] runs around that end of the wall.

## Terrain and Waters

The northern foothills rise toward snow-covered slopes and the Grazian passes. Only six crossings are recognized along the entire range; their precise routes and safe seasons are known only to local mountain guides. South of the mountains, the Chandramahī, Sarvada, Mahānadi, and Bhārava river systems water wide plains before reaching the coast. Their valleys contain fertile floodplains; between them lie forests and the high, semi-arid tableland of [[place-vandhyabhumi|Vandhyabhūmi]]. The lower [[place-bharavavana|Bhāravavana]] is a long reach of tropical forest along the Bhārava.

The eastern shore faces the warm [[place-suryasamdra|Sūrya-samudra]], and the southern shore the [[place-meghsamdra|Megha-samudra]]. Both are humid and monsoon-washed. The shorter western shore faces the [[place-sandhysmdr|Sandhyā-samudra]]; it is much drier, with no great river reaching its coast. The southern and eastern shores receive the heaviest seasonal rain.

The [[being-vdyrnrhn|Vedyaran rhinoceros]] favors the marshes and tall grass of the floodplains. [[being-vdyrnelphnt|Vedyaran elephants]] serve as temple mounts and war beasts. Their notes give travelers and GMs a closer look at the largest animals of this country.

## Climate

A wet season and a dry season govern the region. Monsoon rain falls hardest on the coasts, while the interior plateau remains comparatively dry. The highest Grazian summits to the north remain glaciated and snow covered year round, while lower slopes receive seasonal snow. Snowmelt and monsoon runoff together feed the great rivers, whose level changes shape the floodplains below.
