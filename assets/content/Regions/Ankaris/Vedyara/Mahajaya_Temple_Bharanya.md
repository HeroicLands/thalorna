The great **Mahajaya temple** at [[place-bharanya|Bharanya]] stands on the oldest of the town's mounds, above the highest water of the lower [[place-mahanadi|Mahanodi]]. It is one of the three largest pilgrim-temples of inland [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] and the largest establishment of [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] outside [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnageri]].

Mahajaya is the preserver-goddess of the [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnuka pantheon]], mistress of order, of prosperity and of the keeping of dorma. A river that moves its bed and drowns towns is exactly the condition her observance addresses, and the lower Mahanodi is the most devoted stretch of water in the subcontinent.

## The Council of Three

The temple's senior priest sits on the **Council of Three** with the senior priests of [[place-sangama|Sangema]] at the [[place-melaground|Melra ground]] and of the [[place-suryatempl|Surva temple]] at the source of [[place-chandrmahi|the Chandramahi]]. A joint pronouncement of the three carries near-universal weight in the confederation; Sangema's note says what the council does and what its members say it is not.

Bharanya's is the voice on that council with no assembly behind it. Sangema convenes the Melra and the Surva temple sights the year; this temple governs nothing, holds no roll and speaks for no circuit, and what it has is a million devotees on one river.

## The Observance

The temple keeps the standard Mahajaya devotional cycle and adds the flood-rites, which are kept twice: once when the river begins to rise and once when it has gone down and the reach can be walked again. The second is the greater of the two. The priests read out what the water took, village by village, and the reading is a legal record as well as a rite.

Pilgrims come the year round and in their thousands after the fall of the water. The temple feeds them from its own granary for three days apiece, which is the outer limit of its charity and is enforced by men at the gate with a tally.
