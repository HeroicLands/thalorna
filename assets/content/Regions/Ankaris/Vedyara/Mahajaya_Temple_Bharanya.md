The great **Mahájaya temple** at [[place-bharanya|Bharanya]] stands on the oldest of the town's mounds, above the highest water of the lower [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadi]]. It is one of the three largest pilgrim-temples of inland Vedyara and the largest establishment of [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahájaya]] outside [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]].

Mahájaya is the preserver-goddess of the [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka pantheon]], mistress of order, of prosperity and of the keeping of dharma. A river that moves its bed and drowns towns is exactly the condition her observance addresses, and the lower Mahānadi is the most devoted stretch of water in the subcontinent.

## The Council of Three

The temple's senior priest is one of the three whose consultations are called the Council of Three, with the senior priests of Sangama at the Mela ground and of the Sūrya temple at the source of [[place-chandrmahi|the Chandramahī]]. A joint pronouncement of the three on a matter before the confederation carries near-universal weight. It works as an executive committee, and the priests deny that it is one.

Bharanya's is the voice on that council with no assembly behind it. Sangama convenes the Mela and the Sūrya temple sights the year; this temple governs nothing, holds no roll and speaks for no circuit, and what it has is a million devotees on one river.

## The Observance

The temple keeps the standard Mahájaya devotional cycle and adds the flood-rites, which are kept twice: once when the river begins to rise and once when it has gone down and the reach can be walked again. The second is the greater of the two. The priests read out what the water took, village by village, and the reading is a legal record as well as a rite.

Pilgrims come the year round and in their thousands after the fall of the water. The temple feeds them from its own granary for three days apiece, which is the outer limit of its charity and is enforced by men at the gate with a tally.
