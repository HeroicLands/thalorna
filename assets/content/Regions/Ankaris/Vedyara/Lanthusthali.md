The march companies of [[affiliation-bhumipala|Bhūmipāla]] call it **Lanthusthalī**, the banner ground, and they say the word in daylight. After dark they call it nothing. It is the fan of stony ground where the foothills of [[place-wstrndscnt|the Western Descent]] open onto [[place-marchroad|the march road]], a day and a half west of [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]], and it is the thinnest _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]]: a dread, and a _ghūrakshetra_ (cursed field).

## How It Was Made

In the spring of 315 AF the march kingdom of **Marupāla** met [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]] here and died with its last king ([[lore-marupalafl|the Fall of Marupāla]]). Two sets of dead lie on the ground unburned, the march's and the steppe's, and neither was received by its own rite.

## The Dread

The overrun host's panic is still on the field. It comes on anyone who lingers, by day as well as by night: the certainty that the hills behind are full of horsemen, that the line has gone, that there is nowhere to run. It does not feel like a field's. It feels like one's own fear, and that is how it kills. Horses bolt from it, and a caravan that must pass in daylight passes at a trot with its animals blindfolded.

## The Curse

Lanthusthalī is thin enough that things come through it. On any night of the year, and worst in the dry season when the wind comes off the Descent, the hunters that gather where the dead were never received cross onto the field in a form half made of the place: shapes that are seen close to, that take hold of a man, and that leave him dead at the edge of the stones in the morning. The companies have lost riders to it in living memory. The [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri]] dream-readers at Ashvapada log the dreams of everyone who slept within sight of it, and some of those dreams are not Vedyari.

The march lives by three rules for it:

- **The road goes round.** The march road bends south of the field and costs half a day for it.
- **The road is barred at dusk.** A company post at each end of the bend shuts a chain across the road at sunset, and a caravan that arrives late camps where it is.
- **The edge is marked.** A line of whitewashed stones runs round the field, and a herder who sees a beast cross it lets the beast go.

New men in the companies are told the names of riders who went past the stones after dark, and where each was found.

## Who Comes

- **The march companies**, who hold the posts at each end of the bend. They also herd [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhari]] raiders onto the field when they can, a practice old enough to have a name and disreputable enough to be denied.
- **Khazryn travelers** from [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|the steppe]], who leave offerings at the stones for their own dead on the far side of them.
- **Nobody else** who knows what it is.

## Hooks

- **The caravan on the stones.** A company captain is accused at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandhyāpur]] of driving a caravan onto the field at dusk so that the field would leave its goods for him to collect. The witnesses are dead, the drivers who lived are not sane, and the court needs someone to go and see what is lying past the stones.
- **The khanate's dead.** A Khazryn noble house asks leave to bring its own rites to the field for the steppe dead. Settling half the field might quiet it or might wake it. The Bhūmipāla court must decide whether ending the curse is worth losing its most useful weapon against raiders, and the companies have made their view plain.
- **The rider who came back.** A company rider missing past the stones for three nights walks into the post at dawn and will not say where he has been. The dream-readers at Ashvapada want him brought to them before he sleeps.
