The march companies of [[affiliation-bhumipala|Bhumipala]] call it **Lantustali**, the banner ground, and they say the word in daylight. After dark they call it nothing. It is the fan of stony ground where the foothills of [[place-wstrndscnt|The Western Descent]] open onto [[place-marchroad|The March Road]], a day and a half west of [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]], and it is the thinnest _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]]: a dread, and a _ghurakshetra_ (cursed field).

## How It Was Made

In the spring of 315 AF the march kingdom of **[Marupala]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}** met [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|The Storm of the Hundred Banners]] here and died with its last king ([[lore-marupalafl|The Fall of Marupala]]). Two sets of dead lie on the ground unburned, the march's and the steppe's, and neither was received by its own rite.

## The Dread

The overrun host's panic is still on the field. It comes on anyone who lingers, by day as well as by night: the certainty that the hills behind are full of horsemen, that the line has gone, that there is nowhere to run. It does not feel like a field's. It feels like one's own fear, and that is how it kills. Horses bolt from it, and a caravan that must pass in daylight passes at a trot with its animals blindfolded.

## The Curse

Lantustali is thin enough that things come through it. On any night of the year, and worst in the dry season when the wind comes off the Descent, the hunters that gather where the dead were never received cross onto the field in a form half made of the place: shapes that are seen close to, that take hold of a man, and that leave him dead at the edge of the stones in the morning. The companies have lost riders to it in living memory. The [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasari-sameja]] dream-readers at Ashvapada log the dreams of everyone who slept within sight of it, and some of those dreams are not Vedyari.

The march lives by three rules for it:

- **The road goes round.** The march road bends south of the field and costs half a day for it.
- **The road is barred at dusk.** A company post at each end of the bend shuts a chain across the road at sunset, and a caravan that arrives late camps where it is.
- **The edge is marked.** A line of whitewashed stones runs round the field, and a herder who sees a beast cross it lets the beast gho.

New men in the companies are told the names of riders who went past the stones after dark, and where each was found. Every Vedyari on the march knows the saying that goes with the rules: _cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls._ The companies also say that what crosses the field will not come through a line of burning grass while it burns, and that it gathers round any pyre lit on the ground. Nobody on the march says why.

The march holds that whatever lies past the stones belongs to whoever brings it out, since no owner ever comes back for it.

## The Ground

The field is a long slope of stones and dry grass falling from the foothills toward the road, cut by shallow gullies that run with water for a few days in the rains and are dry the rest of the year. A herders' track leaves the road short of the eastern chain post and runs north into the foothills; the guides at Ashvapada say it goes round the field's northern side and saves half a day.

:::secret {title="The herders' track"}
The track does not gho round the field. It runs onto it from the north and across it toward the road, and just short of the southern stones it crosses a stony gully that breaks wheels. It is the road the company's guide brings caravans in by, and the road its collectors use at dawn.
:::

Most of the whitewashed stones are old, crusted with lichen and sunk into the ground. On the southern side, where the field comes nearest the road, a long stretch of them stands loose on the surface, freshly whitewashed, with no bedding round their feet. The march finds its dead along that stretch in the morning more often than anywhere else on the ring.

:::secret {title="The southern stones"}
The southern stretch is a false line. A company has lifted the old stones there and set them again well inside the ring, so that the ring bulges inward in a long bite. The true edge runs on beyond the bite, through a cleft in a rock spur. A caravan that runs for the road stops at the false line, thinks itself past the stones, and dies there in the night, where the march expects to find its dead.

> [!TIP]
> Describe the loose stones honestly to anyone who looks at their feet, and say nothing about the curve of the ring. The bite cannot be seen from the ground; it shows only from height.

:::

On a rise in the southern part of the field stands a squat stone shrine with a dead tree beside it, older than the field's ruin. It is roofless at one end and has a flat stone roof at the other, and the stumps of banner poles stand in sockets along its parapet. On the southern side of the ring a rock spur runs down toward the road, and two lines of the old stones climb to a narrow cleft in it and stop on either side.

## Who Comes

- **The march companies**, who hold the posts at each end of the bend. They also herd [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhari]] raiders onto the field when they can, a practice old enough to have a name and disreputable enough to be denied.
- **Khazryn travelers** from the steppe of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]], who leave offerings at the stones for their own dead on the far side of them: cups, knotted cloths and bowls. They leave them at the cleft and along the old stones, and the company men at the eastern post say the Khazryn never stop at the southern stretch.
- **Nobody else** who knows what it is.

The drivers at Ashvapada say that a caravan of three wagons belonging to a cloth factor, **[Pallivata]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, went onto the field last dry season and nobody came out of it, and that its guide came back alone.

## Hooks

- **The caravan on the stones.** A company captain is accused at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandyapur]] of driving a caravan onto the field at dusk so that the field would leave its goods for him to collect. The witnesses are dead, the drivers who lived are not sane, and the court needs someone to gho and see what is lying past the stones.
- **The orkunoq's dead.** A Khazryn noble house asks leave to bring its own rites to the field for the steppe dead. Settling half the field might quiet it or might wake it. The Bhumipala court must decide whether ending the curse is worth losing its most useful weapon against raiders, and the companies have made their view plain.
- **The rider who came back.** A company rider missing past the stones for three nights walks into the post at dawn and will not say where he has been. The dream-readers at Ashvapada want him brought to them before he sleeps.

[[scenario-lastwagonadv|The Last Wagon on the Banner Ground]] gives the first of these as a five-scene adventure.

# The Truth of Lantustali {#truth .secret}

**The racket.** The company that holds the Ashvapada stage is a season in arrears, and its captain, **[Krtiraya]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, collects what he is owed from the traffic. He sells a caravan its escort and recommends a guide, and the guide, **[Karasha]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, a lean, cheerful man with a good horse, takes the wagons off the road short of the eastern chain post by the herders' track. He times the crossing so that a wheel breaks in the gully near sunset, and rides out north by the way he came, which by daylight he can do. The caravan runs for the road, stops at the false line and dies there. At first light four of the captain's riders and Karasha come in by the herders' track at a trot, with their mules blindfolded against the dread, take the goods and leave the dead at the stones. They enter what they bring out as the field's leavings, and the march's own custom makes it theirs. Men who take the goods of a dead caravan off the field are salvagers before the law; men who take them from a living owner are robbers, and that difference is why the court needs a witness who comes out alive.

**The shrine and the ring.** The shrine on the rise is a wayside shrine of the old march, where Marupala's host set its banners on the morning of the battle. From its roof, under the stars, the whitewashed stones catch what light there is, and the whole ring shows as a pale broken loop on the dark slope, with the bite on its southern side and the two lines of old stones climbing to the cleft beyond it. It is the one place on the field from which the false line can be read. The cleft is the nearest point of the true edge to the shrine, and it is wide enough for two people side by side. Anyone who stands on the far side of it feels the panic lift.

**What crosses.** The hunters are the predators of the unreceived dead, crossing in a form half made of the field: too much of each is the color of the dry ground and the dead grass, and the face is a shape the dark has made out of where a face should be. They come close, take hold and drag a person off into the dark, and they come thickest round anyone who is bleeding. Steel and fire hurt them, and neither hurts them the way it hurts a man. They speak in the voices of people the hearer knows, and of the field's recent dead, calling a name and asking for help; anyone who answers walks to them. In the cleft and among rocks they can hold a person bodily and pull. They will not come through a burning line of grass while it burns, they gather round any pyre lit on the field, and at first light they thin and gho. The panic does not gho with them. Nobody can say why fire turns them or a pyre draws them, and the hunters are not the captain's: he uses them, as the companies use them against raiders, and he does not understand them.

**The field's own dead.** Marupala's beaten host still runs on the field after dark: men on foot in the dress of an old host, helmets gone, looking back over their shoulders, running for the road. They do not attack. They pass the false line without pausing, stop at the true one, and stand there looking at the road they cannot reach. The Khazryn leave their offerings at the true stones because their own landmarks for the edge are older than the company's work, and so the offerings mark the real line too.

**Pallivata's caravan.** Karasha guided it last dry season, and its lead wagon broke in the gully. Pallivata reached the shrine, read the ring from the roof, scratched what he had learned into the roof beams and left his satchel against the back wall, and went back down for his people. He died in the gully under his own wagon, both hands round a broken spoke he had scratched the same words into: _Stones lie. Not the near ones. Shelter south, past the dead tree. Roof. See the whole ring from roof. Do not answer. They use our voices._ Below them, over and over, _Karasha_. His drivers died at the false line and were counted among the field's dead. The collectors stripped his wagons of everything that could be carried and cut the harness; they did not look under the lead wagon. His body lies there still, dried by a year of sun and wind, with the wreck of his second wagon half buried a few paces on. The satchel in the shrine holds his caravan ledger: the escort fee paid to Krtiraya's company, Karasha's hire entered on the captain's word, and a consignment of cut gems carried for the house of **[Sundarka]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, at Pallivata's risk.

> [!TIP]
> The ledger and an escort receipt under the captain's seal together make a case at law. Either one alone is a merchant's grievance.

**This season.** Sundarka, a gem merchant of the river country, lost a fortune in Pallivata's caravan and took his loss to the court at Sandyapur. The court already had the accusation against Krtiraya and no living witness to it. It gave Sundarka its commission, under its seal, to ride the stage as bait and take whoever came to collect, with the goods in their hands and their owner alive. At Ashvapada he paid the escort, asked the captain for Karasha by name, and kept the receipt, on which the captain's note recommends Karasha "as requested". He hired blades on top of the escort he had paid for, loaded more lamp-oil than a stage of a day and a half needs, and chained a strongbox to the bed of his lead wagon holding his own gems and bags of the court's silver. He means to hold his wagons by fire until dawn and be found alive with the box. He told nobody: not his drivers, not his two guards, and not the blades he hired. The commission is folded under the receipt in his coat.

He does not know that the southern stones were moved. The court knew that caravans died at the southern stones, not why.

> [!WARNING]
> Sundarka is reckless with other people's lives, not a murderer. He walks his caravan onto the field on purpose and keeps the reason to himself, and he did not know where the true edge runs. Keep him ignorant of the false line, or he becomes the racket's partner instead of its hunter.

**[Vijesa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}** is a young soldier of the company that holds the eastern chain post. He has seen the dead of three caravans found along the southern stretch, and it was his letter that brought the accusation to Sandyapur. The court told him to watch for a merchant named Sundarka and to keep a fire on the road side of the stones on the night his caravan was stranded. He does not know where the true edge runs, only that people die at the southern stones. He has seen the Khazryn offerings at the cleft, and that is where he carries his fire, against his captain's orders.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The whitewashed stones mark the edge.** True everywhere except along the southern stretch, where the stones were moved.
- **Cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls.** True on both counts.
- **The herders' track saves half a day.** False. It runs onto the field.
- **Pallivata's guide came back alone.** True. The guide was Karasha.
- **The companies herd raiders onto the field.** True, and Krtiraya's racket is the same practice turned on the traffic.
- **The captain accused at Sandyapur.** True. He is Krtiraya.
- **The Khazryn never stop at the southern stretch.** True, because their offerings belong at the true edge.
