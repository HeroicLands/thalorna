**The Measuring Cord** is the recitation of the beginning that a Vedyari temple school teaches its children first, before the letters and before the count, and the rendering here is the one the school of the great [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyalendra]] temple at [[place-rajapur|Rajepur]] recites on the first morning of the year to the new children of every village of the janapoda, standing before [[place-sandstonealtr|the sandstone altar]] with a knotted cord in the master's hands. It is a catechism in couplets, a long line answered by a short one: a child's question, fixed by long use, and the master's answer, and the children say the questions and the master says the rest. The schools of the [[affiliation-trimurtisampradaya|Triyanga-sampradeya]], the tradition of the three forms, recite the same work with [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] and [[affiliation-rasikara|Rasikara]] named as the shaper's equals, and the schools of the [[affiliation-vyalendravada|Vyalendravada]] recite it with every god an aspect of the one shaper; the recitation carries both readings inside itself and says so; that is how a Vedyari work keeps a quarrel without settling it. The [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agmi-pantra]] holds that the schools recite the breaking too softly, and teaches its novices that section at the roadside in words of its own.

Where the work touches [[place-himashila|Himashila]], it keeps the silence the [[place-suryatempl|Surva temple]] keeps: the slab is a footstool, and the recitation does not say whose. Where it touches the gap between the conch and the pyre it says what every Vedyari household holds, and where it touches the fields where the gap never closed it says what [[lore-bloodfield|every people says of a blood-field]] in the school's own terms. The count of the ages is the school's, and the children are told so. At the end of the recitation the master gives the cord to the smallest child, and the children measure the temple court with it, knot by knot, before they are allowed to eat.

: Before the Cord {#before}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, what was there before the field?
  Light, and the light had no edge.
There was no here, because there was no there;
  no bank, because no river;
no day, because the light did not stop,
  and nothing was counted,
because a count begins at one, and one
  is a knot, and there was no cord.

Master, who tied the first knot?
  The shaper. Say his name.
Vyalendra, who had the cord in his hands
  before there were hands,
and tied one knot in it, and said: here.
  That was the first word.
Then there was a here, and so a there,
  and the light had an edge,
and the edge was the first wall, and behind it
  the first unmeasured dark,
and in front of it the first measured ground,
  one knot wide. That was the field.
```

: The Knots {#knots}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, how many knots are in the cord?
  As many as there are things.
He tied a knot for the sky and drew the vault
  with the compass, in one turn;
he tied a knot for the earth and laid the cord
  straight on it, bank to bank;
he tied a knot for the wall and took the chisel
  and cut the wall last and highest,
so that the measured country would have an edge
  it could lift its eyes and see.
He tied a knot for every beast, and named it,
  and the first he named was the serpent,
and that is the beast in his name;
  the schools dispute the rest.
He tied a knot for the grain and a knot for the ox,
  a knot for the cotton, a knot for the cane;
a knot for the cord itself, which is the knot
  you are holding, child. Hold it.

Master, how long did the measuring take?
  A thousand thousand years,
and a thousand thousand more, and the reckoners
  will give you a nearer figure
if you bring them a slate and a season,
  and the figure will be theirs.
The school says: long. The school says: it is done.
  The school says: he has not put the cord down.
```

: The Four Doors {#doors}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, where did the water come from?
  From under the ice, through four doors.
When the wall was cut he laid the ice on it
  as a roof is laid on a house,
and in the ice he tied four knots, and where
  each knot lay a river came out.
Chandraprabhava, the moon's source, the first,
  where the Chandramahi comes out warm;
Sarvaprabhava, the source of all, the second,
  where the priests read the Sarveda daily;
Mahaprabhava, the great source, the third,
  where the Mahanodi leaves the blue;
Bharavaprabhava, the fourth, the forest's,
  where the Bharava goes down to the trees.
Four knots. Four doors. Four rivers. And the plain
  between them is the field,
and every field you will ever plow or pay for
  is a knot of the cord between two doors.

Master, did he come down off the ice?
  He stepped down. There is a stone.
The temple at the moon's source washes it
  at the turn of every season,
and calls it a footstool, and does not say whose,
  and this recitation does not say.
The Osket say it is the mountain's own. The temple says nothing.
  You will say what you are told, which is nothing.
Under the stone the water does not freeze.
  That is all the school says of it.
```

: The Keeping {#keeping}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, when the measuring was done, who kept it?
  His sister. Say her name.
Mahajaya, who had the scale in her hands
  before there was anything to weigh,
and he gave her the cord's count, knot by knot,
  and she set it in the scale,
and whatever he had measured she held to the measure:
  the grain to its sheaf,
the market to its word, the year to its turning,
  the river to its bank.
Say what the scale weighs, child. Measure. Not judgment.
  She does not ask whether a thing is good.
She asks whether it is what it was measured to be,
  and if it is, she keeps it.
That is why the sabhra counts the water at the gate
  and why the Weighing is done in daylight,
and why a clerk who gives short measure has stolen
  from the sister, and not from you.

Master, the schools of the three say she is his equal.
  They do. Say it as they say it:
three faces of one work, the shaping, the keeping,
  the breaking, none the least.
And the shaper's schools say she is the shape sustained,
  his own hand holding still.
The recitation says both, and the altar you stand before
  was cut to the cord, and holds.
```

: The Breaking {#breaking}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, if everything was measured and kept,
  why is there a second age?
Because a kept thing seizes. Listen.
  The first age kept too well.
The boundary stones grew into one wall
  and the fields forgot the plain;
the granaries kept the grain to the last measure
  and did not open in the famine;
the scale held level and nothing moved on it,
  and the river kept its bank
and did not flood, and the field it did not flood
  went to dust under the measure.
Everything was what it had been measured to be,
  and nothing was alive.

Master, who broke it?
  His brother. Say his name.
Rasikara, who had the fire in his hands
  before there was anything to burn,
and he did not burn the field, child; mark that.
  He burned the wall the stones had grown into.
He broke the circle where it had seized, and only there,
  with the fire proper to the task,
no more, no less, and that is the whole of his law,
  and the ash-mark is the sign that you have learned it.
What yielded of itself he did not touch;
  the gentle one took that,
Vahrati, with her bowl, the way your mother takes
  the day's garland from the shrine.
Everything else burned down to the knot,
  and the cord was still there under the ash,
and the shaper took it up, and tied a knot, and said: here.
  That was the second measuring.

Master, how many ages have there been?
  The school says this is the third.
The schools of the three say the third; the shaper's schools
  say he never put the cord down,
and so there is one age and it is being measured,
  and the breaking is a knot in it like any other.
The flame-priests say the schools recite this part
  too softly, and they may be right;
they will tell you at the roadside, with the vessel open,
  and you may listen, and come back.
```

: The Breath Not Measured {#breath}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, if everything was measured, what is death?
  The one measure he did not take.
When he measured a body, knot by knot,
  the hand, the eye, the heart,
he left one length of the cord untied,
  the length of one breath,
and he gave that length to the one who had asked for nothing,
  the only one who had asked for nothing,
Kalavrata, who has no face, because a face
  would be a thing to measure,
and said: this is yours. Keep the breath nobody keeps.
  And the Gatekeeper took it.

Master, what is the breath for?
  For the soul to get out by.
If everything in you were measured and kept,
  the sister would hold you to your measure
and you would be what you had been forever,
  and that is the first age, child, and it is dust.
So there is a gap. The conch sounds at one end of it,
  and the pyre closes the other,
and between the two the way you have gone is open behind you
  for the length of a breath,
and the lamp burns, and nobody in the house quarrels,
  because the house is standing in the gap.
Then the fire takes what was measured, and the conch
  calls what was not, and the gap closes,
and the guides who have no names lead you to the gate
  and you are measured again,
with the same cord, in a different field, to a different knot,
  and the knot you are tied to next
is the one your duty tied. Not your wish. Your duty.
  That is why we do it unwillingly and do it well.

Master, what if the gap does not close?
  Then the breath is still in the doorway.
Where thousands died with no conch and no lamp and no fire,
  on a day that can be named,
the gap behind them never closed, and the field is torn,
  and the dead stand in the door.
The school does not send you there. The school says this:
  a measure the shaper left untaken
is not a measure for a king to leave untaken,
  and the one who forbade the fire at the ford
left open what the Gatekeeper was given to close,
  and the ford is open still.
The schools of the three say the dead there are the Gatekeeper's
  and no priest's to burn.
The flame-priests burned half of it anyway.
  The recitation says: the ford is open still.
```

: The Same Cord {#same}

```poetry {form=cosmogony lang=en}
Master, will this age end?
  Yes. Everything measured is kept, and everything kept seizes.
One day the stones will have grown into a wall again
  and the granaries will not open,
and the brother will break the wall and not the field,
  and the gentle one will take what yields,
and the shaper will stoop in the ash and find the cord
  and tie a knot in it and say: here.
And it will be the same cord. Mark that above everything.
  The knots are the same knots.
The here he says will be the here he said,
  one knot wide, with a wall behind it,
and the four doors will open in the ice
  and the rivers will come out at the same four places,
and a child will stand before a stone and ask
  what there was before the field.

Master, what do I do with the cord?
  Measure the court with it.
Begin at the altar, which was cut to it,
  and gho knot by knot to the gate,
and when you have the count, bring it to me,
  and I will tell you whether it is what the sister keeps.
If it is not, you will measure it again,
  and you will not eat until it is.
That is the school. That is the field. That is the whole of it.
  Gho and measure.
```
