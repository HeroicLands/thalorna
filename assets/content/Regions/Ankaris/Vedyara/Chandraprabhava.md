**Chandraprabhava** is where the [[place-chandrmahi|Chandramahī]] comes out of the ice under [[place-suryashkhr|Sūryashikhara]]. The [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka]] tradition does not treat this as the place the river happens to begin; it treats it as the place the river began, once, at the beginning, and has been beginning ever since.

The Sūrya temple stands on the shelf beside the outflow, and the pilgrimage to it is the most demanding of the four source-pilgrimages and the most made. Some thirty thousand people a year reach the shelf in the open months. Perhaps a tenth of them are carried the last stage.

## The Water

The spring does not freeze. Every other source on the wall shuts for the winter under twenty feet of ice, and this one runs. The pass beneath it therefore has drinkable water in months when the others have none. That is a large part of why [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]] is the greatest of the passes, and why a fortress was worth building at its throat.

The temple's explanation is [[place-himashila|Himashilā]], the slab at the head of the outflow, which it holds to be the god's footstool and washes at the turn of every season. The [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] hold the slab to be the mountain's own and step round it without touching it. Both have been saying so for as long as either has been here.

## The Nine Days

Two winters ago the spring iced over and stayed iced for nine days, which had never happened in the temple's record. It has not been entered in the record. The slab was exactly as it had always been before, during and after, gray and warm and giving under a thumb. The priests had nothing whatever to show anybody, and a temple with nothing to show is better off with nothing to say.
