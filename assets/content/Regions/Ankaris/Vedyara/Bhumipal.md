## Overview

**Bhumipal** ("earth-protector") is a kingdom that exists for one road. It holds the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]]'s northwestern march: the dry foothills where the [[place-graznmntns|Grazian]] wall comes apart into [[place-wstrndscnt|the Western Descent]], the road that goes round the range's end into [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]], and the arid shore of the [[place-sandvsmdr|Sandva-samedra]] south of them. The name is the ruling clan's before it is the kingdom's. Two million people live in it, nearly all of them within a day's walk of water. The Maharava keeps his court at [[place-sandyapur|Sandyapur]] on the coast, and the kingdom holds the land of [[place-bhumipalland|Bhumipal]]. The clan held the salt coast before the march was a kingdom of theirs; the kingdom before it was [Marupal]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, which died with its last king under the steppe host in 315 ([[lore-marupalfl|the Fall of Marupal]]), and the clan took the empty march about 325 ([[lore-bhmplraise|the Raising of Bhumipal]]).

[[place-marchroad|The march road]] is the one frontier of the subcontinent a loaded camel crosses without climbing, and the one by which a steppe host has come down into Vedyara. The court, the companies, the customs and the treaty all stand on that.

A captain of one of the march companies, taking on a caravan at Sandyapur, sets out the terms before the first wagon moves. "You can pay my company for the stage, or you can pay the same money at the next well to whoever is standing at it. Pay me and the well comes with it. And whatever your wrist says, out here I want to know what you can do with a horse."

## Character

The march is herding country with a road through it. A household is reckoned by its animals and by the wells it may use, and a man who holds a well holds more than a man who holds a field. The border families have married across the sand for centuries and most of them speak both tongues, which the river country regards as a moral failing and the march regards as trade.

The order of stations holds, and it holds loosely. The wrist is read at the temple gate and nowhere else on the road, and a caravan master asks what a man can do with a horse. A **Samonta** of the march is a man with a fort, a well and a hundred horse, and his descent is the third thing anyone asks about him.

Public religion changes every fifty miles on this frontier. The orthodox temples stand in the towns, the [[affiliation-svapnasarisameja|Svapnasari-sameja]] keeps its monasteries at the edge-towns above them, with its westernmost tower at the wells of [[place-marukupa|Marukupa]], and the herding clans keep their own observances at the wells and are not troubled about it.

## Government

The Maharava rules from Sandyapur. The crown's writ runs on the road, at the wells and along the coast; the herding country between belongs to the Samantas, who hold it by descent and by the forts their grandfathers built.

- **The Martial Council** is the Samantas of the road's forts and the captains of the hired companies. It meets before each caravan season and after it.
- **The Priestly Court** is small, drawn from the town temples, and its business is succession, the calendar and the law.

Succession is hereditary and is confirmed by the Martial Council, as it is in [[affiliation-shikhalaya|Shikhalaya]]. A Maharava of the march who cannot pay the companies is not deposed. He is simply not obeyed at the wells, which comes to the same thing within a season. The rest of the court holds the ordinary offices of a Vedyari crown, from the **Rojni** and the **Yuvarava** to the **Koshadyoksha** and the **Ganoka**.

## The Companies

Bhumipal keeps no standing host. It keeps five companies of horse and foot under captains who contract by the season, and it pays them out of the escort money. The companies are Vedyari, Dunhari and mixed, and they recruit from both sides of the sand. They buy their arms by the wagonload out of the Shikhalayan valleys, where steel is cheap and reliably supplied.

A company that is paid keeps the road. A company that is not paid keeps the road anyway and collects what it is owed from the traffic, and the difference between that and banditry is a season's arrears. The crown's chief domestic business is making sure the difference is never tested.

## The Treaty

The formal treaty with the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] is Bhumipal's, and it is renewed every generation. It governs the movement of pilgrim caravans and the treatment of merchants arrested for trade violations on either side of the frontier. The Vedyari copy is kept at Sandyapur, and each march-captain carries a copy of the clauses he is expected to enforce.

The treaty binds the Kingdom. It does not bind the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara tribes]], who signed nothing and who hold that the road crosses their grazing by their forbearance. The tribes raid in lean seasons and trade in fat ones. The crown's standing arrangement exists to make the first unprofitable, and every sheikh on the road understands it exactly as the crown does.

Four centuries ago a steppe host, [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]], came down the Western Descent and overran the whole of the march before it turned west across the desert. Every fort on the road was built after that, and with it in mind. Marupal's host lies unburned where the Descent opens onto the road, on [[place-lantustal|Lantustal]], a cursed field the road bends round and the companies chain off at dusk, and its burned capital, [[place-ludrapur|Ludrapur]], is where the companies winter.

## Economy

Salt is raked on the coast and goes inland and west by the wagonload, and dried fish goes the same way. Horses, hides and the wool of the sand come east; cotton, grain, worked metal and the manufactured goods of the river country gho west. The march grows little and carries much.

The crown does not tax the road by the bale. It sells escort, by the wagon and by the stage, collected at Sandyapur for the first stage and at [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] for the second, and a caravan that declines the escort pays the same money at the next well. The escort money is the largest item of royal revenue and the companies are the largest charge against it, and the two figures are read out together at the season's end.

## Relations

To the west and northwest lie the tribes and the Kingdom, and the whole of the kingdom's foreign policy is the road. Relations with the tribes are seasonal and conducted sheikh by sheikh; relations with the Kingdom are formal, cool and old.

To the north and east, across the broken end of the wall, lies Shikhalaya. The two kingdoms hold the north between them without sharing a border, and neither wants what the other has. Shikhalayan steel comes down to the march companies and march horses gho up to the pass-roads.

Across the Sandva-samedra to the west lies the eastern coast of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]]. The kingdom has no dealings with it. Its own coast trades north and east by the road and south by the coasting passage, and the far shore is a fact of the sea and not a market.

To the south and southeast lie the river janpodas and the coastal cities. Bhumipal holds a seat at the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|great pilgrimage assembly]] in its own right and sends a small delegation, which the river country receives politely and does not consult. Its dealings with [[affiliation-chendrapur|Chendrapur]] are the dealings of a road with a market at the end of it.

## Commerce and Currency

Bhumipal holds no seat in the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]], and it uses [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|the Vedyaran banking system]] all the same. [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|The Merchant Collective]] keeps its westernmost house at Sandyapur and a factor at Ashvapada, and between them they finance most of what moves on the road.

The Dunhari side of the trade runs on barter and clan credit, and a caravan settles in goods, in animals and in silver taken by weight. A Dunhari who holds a Collective letter of credit is rare enough that the men at the exchange know his name. See [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]] for the system and [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] for the institution.

## Outside the Ladder

A person placed outside the tarona, by transgression or by birth, is excluded from the wells, the temples and the courts.
