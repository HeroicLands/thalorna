On the ridges above the upper [[place-sarvadarivr|Sarvada]], the [[place-bharavarivr|Bhārava]] and the upper [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadī]] there are forts that belong to nobody. Each was the seat of a small kingdom of the [[lore-hundredcrwn|Hundred Crowns]], or of one of the mountain dynasties "whose names change as their dynasties rise and fall," and each was left when its kingdom stopped. The janapadas below call them the **Crownless Forts**, and they have never counted them.

## What Is There

A typical one is a ring of dry-stone wall on a spur, with a gate-tower, a cistern cut in the rock, and the footings of a hall inside. The roofs went long ago. Nearly every gate is walled up with rough stone, and the walling is the janapadas' work: when a kingdom's last garrison left, the villages below went up and blocked the gate so that nothing would lair in the fort, whether bandits, beasts or worse. The forts stand above the fields like shut boxes.

## Who Goes

- **Shepherds**, who use the outer walls as folds in the summer pasture and do not go inside.
- **Bandits**, who break a gate open when they want a hold. The fort above Rājapur's fields was cleared by Rājapur and two neighbors eighty-three years ago, the last armed conflict on Rājapuri ground, and walled up again afterward.
- **Heirs**, who come with old genealogies and a clan name written in a dead kingdom's charter, and stand outside the gate.

The forts are not _shorukshetras_ (blood-fields). Their kingdoms ended in successions, debts and lost wars fought elsewhere, and their dead were burned. The hill villages keep away from them at night all the same, because a fort with its gate walled up is a place someone wanted shut.

## Hooks

- **The charter.** A family with its clan name in a dead kingdom's charter wants its fort, and has asked the janapada below for leave to unwall the gate. The janapada's sabhā is divided: an occupied fort above the fields is a kingdom waiting to happen.
- **From the inside.** In one fort above the Bhārava the walling of the gate has been pulled down from inside, the stones lying outward on the path. Nobody went in. The villages want to know what came out.
- **The cistern.** A shepherd's boy dropped into a fort's cistern after a goat and came up with a bronze seal of a king nobody can name.
