The **Vyālendra source-temple** stands on the shelf at [[place-sarvaprbhv|Sarvaprabhava]], where the [[place-sarvadarivr|Sarvada]] comes out of the ice under the face of [[place-sthrnwall|the Southern Wall]]. It is the temple of [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyālendra]] the Shaper, and its priests are masons, carpenters and surveyors who have taken orders. There is no scholar on the establishment and no library in the building.

It is a small temple. A walled forecourt, a pillared hall of nine bays, a sanctum with the foundation-stone in it, a cistern, and a range of cells for twenty men. The whole work is dry-laid granite cut on the shelf, and the masons who cut it were the priests themselves.

## The Eleven Rebuildings

The temple's own record has it taken down and rebuilt on the same footing eleven times. Each rebuilding was begun and finished inside one open season, which is between nine and eleven weeks on that shelf, and the pace is the reason the establishment is what it is: a house of twenty men who can all cut, set and roof.

The interval between rebuildings has never been less than a century and never more than two. The masons read this as a fact about the mountain and not about their predecessors' work, and the record is written in those terms throughout. What the mountain does to the building is not stated anywhere in it. The oldest hands say the walls go out of true from the bottom, slowly, over about four generations, and that nothing anybody has tried in the footing has ever changed the rate.

The present building is in its ninth decade.

## The Measurement

Every day of the open season a priest reads the outflow against a graduated post set in the rock below the spring, and enters the reading in the record with the day and the weather. The series is nine hundred years long and unbroken except for the years of a rebuilding, when it was kept anyway, on the same post, by whichever man could be spared.

It is the best record of the wall's melt that exists. The [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] has twice offered to buy a copy and has twice been refused without a reason given, which the Collective reads as the price rising. The readings themselves go down with the last caravan of the year to the reckoners in the lowlands, who use them and do not own them.

## The Pilgrimage

The approach is the hardest of the four source-pilgrimages. The last two days are a stair cut in rock, there is no way to carry a man up it, and perhaps four hundred pilgrims a year finish it. They come for a child, mostly, the Shaper being the god of foundations and so of beginnings of every kind, and they come from the villages of the upper Sarvada rather than from any distance. The temple lodges them in the forecourt, feeds them from the cistern and the caravan stores, and asks nothing.
