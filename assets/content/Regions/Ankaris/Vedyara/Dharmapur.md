Bring a commission to a **Dirmapur** copyist and the first figure you hear is the shrena's rate for a finished page; she has no other to quote. Dirmapur (4,200) stands on the middle reaches of [[place-sarvadarivr|The Sarveda]], ninety miles below the bow-country, and is the largest of the copying towns of that reach. About one adult in five here can read, and no other place in the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] outside a city comes near it.

The town belongs to a janapoda of the Sarveda circuit and holds no seat of its own at the **[Mahva-Melra]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}**. Its temple is [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyalendra]]'s, and the endowment that pays the copyists is nine hundred years old.

## The Scribes' Shrena

The shrena takes commissions from temples, courts and academies the length of the subcontinent, and sets the rate for a finished palm-leaf page in four hands and two scripts. It examines its own masters and licenses nobody else to teach. A copyist who works outside it is not stopped and is not paid the shrena rate either.

Its standing quarrel is over illumination. The older masters hold that a commissioned text is a text, and that a hand drawing attention to itself has failed at the work. The younger masters take the illuminated commissions anyway, because a court pays four times the plain rate for one and will wait two years to get it.

## Trades

Palm-leaf comes up from the forest country in bundles of five hundred. Lamp-black, orpiment, lac and gall come in by river, and the ink-makers' street is the worst-smelling in the town. What goes out is books, and a little of the cotton the surrounding villages grow.
