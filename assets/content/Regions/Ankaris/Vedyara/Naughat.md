For eight months of the year **Naughat** (1,600, market 3) is a town, and for four it is a fishing village. It stands on the south bank at the mouth of [[place-sarvedarivr|the Sarveda]], where the river's small craft meet the coasting ships. It is the outlet of the copying country: palm-leaf, cotton, sugar and the rice of the middle valley come down two hundred miles of gentle water and gho out from here, and the river craft that brought them take back salt, iron and the foreign goods the coast lands.

The port works for eight months and closes for four. When the rains come the bar is unworkable, the coasting ships are gone or are up the river, and what is left is a fishing village with an empty warehouse street. The warehouse-keepers stay through it. The stevedores are cultivators from the villages behind the dunes and gho home to their fields.

The landing is held by the temple of [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] at the town and by the sabhra of the janpoda the town sits in, and the anchorage dues are the sabhra's largest single revenue. A master pays them on arrival and argues about them afterward, which is the custom of the place and is not resented.
