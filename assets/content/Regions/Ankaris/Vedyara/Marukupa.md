**Marukūpa** (900, market 3) is the last Vedyari water on [[place-marchroad|the march road]]. The name means the desert well, and there are four of them, sunk in a rock shelf where the foothills give out. West of here a caravan is on the sand for two days before it reaches a Dunhari well, and the arithmetic of that gap governs the whole of the traffic.

The village is a well-yard, a walled serai, a market of eight or ten permanent stalls, and the crown's post. The post holds a company detachment through the season and a skeleton of it through the rest, and its commander is the man who decides whether a caravan may go on. He is obeyed, because a party that has gone on against his word is not looked for either.

## The Water

The wells are the crown's and the water is sold, which is the practice of the march and not of the sand. A [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhari]] party arriving thirsty is watered first and charged afterward, by an arrangement nobody has written down and everybody keeps. The one season it was not kept is remembered on both sides with the details in dispute.

Salt and dried fish come up from the coast, grain and cotton from the plain, and all of it stops here to be watered and loaded for the crossing. Horses, hides and wool come the other way and go on to [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]].
