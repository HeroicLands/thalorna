**Chendrapur**, the "Moon City," cuts the gems of a whole subcontinent and keeps the only standing war-fleet in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]]. Its jewelers' work commands prices across all the known realms; royal courts from the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provenzia]] to the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] commission pieces from its master craftspeople, and its merchant families have gathered wealth over generations. The city-state holds [[place-chendrapurland|Chendrapur Land]], the lower reaches of [[place-chandrmahi|the Chandramahi]] and its delta, and [[place-chendrapur2|Chendrapur]] at the head of the delta is its seat.

Its government is a bargain between two parties who cannot do without each other. The crown cannot cut a stone, and the nine great houses that cut them cannot clear a cargo.

A newcomer with stone to sell learns this first from the crown's assayers at [[place-greatbazaar|the Great Bazaar]], and an assayer gives the same lesson to every foreign factor who comes to his table. "Do not sell a stone on a private assay in this city. A stone that has not crossed my table is a stone nobody here will take in payment afterward. Bring it to the floor, let it be weighed where everyone can hear, and then sell it to whichever House you like. The Houses buy here. They cut at home."

## The City of the Craft

The shrena halls keep a strict rule. An artisan apprentices for years before he earns the right to a workshop of his own, and Chendrapur's name for excellence has never been spoiled. The trade runs as a sequence of stages: a stone is assayed in one street, sawn in another, cut in a third, polished in a fourth and set in a fifth, and a house that holds a stage has held it for generations.

Wealth has made Chendrapur cosmopolitan. Traders and craftspeople from across the realms have settled here, and the markets speak a dozen languages. A foreign factor with no house and no charter may buy on the Bazaar floor exactly as a **Kulena** may, which is unusual in Vedyara and is the largest single reason the foreign trade comes here.

The temples of the [[affiliation-vrnkpnthn|Varnuka]] are the older seat of power. Their priests mediate disputes between the merchant families and bless the jewelcrafters' work before it begins, and a cutter who begins a stone without the blessing is not employed again. The city's prosperity, its people hold, flows from divine favor.

## The Moon House and the Nine

Chendrapur keeps a king, which among the great cities of the coast is the uncommon arrangement. The **Maharava of [[place-moonhouse|the Moon House]]** rules by descent, and the temples confirm the rule: the rites the Varnuka ushtakas perform at an accession are what make the man king, and no claimant they would not crown has ever reigned.

The Maharava rules with a court, and the court is the [Nine Houses]{.name data-type="affiliation"}. Their heads sit as Kulena. Each holds one of the nine seats, the charters of his own trade, and the quarter of the city his house has always kept, and a dispute between two men of one quarter goes to its Kulena. The seats descend within the houses; the crown confirms each succession and has twice refused one. The temples' first refusal of a king, about 312, is [[lore-moonsettle|the Moon House Settlement]] that set the Nine on their seats.

A Kulena is not a **Samonta** and holds no land of the king's grant. His standing is his house's charter, and a house that loses its trade loses its seat within a generation. That is the difference between this court and a kingdom's.

The court sits in [[place-moonhouse|the Moon House]] above the river stair, on nine seats of one height. The crown's own revenue comes from the tenth district, the waterfront and market ground that belongs to no house: the Great Bazaar, the assay, the customs and the mint are the crown's. A crown that could not tax the quarters taxes what passes between them, and everything passes between them there.

The rest of the court fills out the ordinary offices of a Vedyari crown. The **Rojni** holds her own revenues, household and voice; the **Yuvarava** is commonly given the customs of the river mouth to govern as his apprenticeship; the **Purohata** performs the rites the king's legitimacy rests on; the **Koshadyoksha** answers for the revenue, the gem-assay and the mint; and the **Ganoka** fixes the auspicious hour for sailings, marriages and coronations. The reckoners of the [[affiliation-ganokashalda|Ganoka-shalda]] compute the civil year here, and a court that follows them may find itself a day apart from a court that follows the year sighted at [[place-surtatempl|the Surta temple]].

## The Fleet

The war-galleys at the river mouth are the only standing fighting fleet in Vedyara. Every other power on the coast hires hulls in the season it needs them and lays them off after. Chendrapur keeps its own, builds them in its own yards at [[place-chandrmukha|Chandramukha]], and mans them off its own shore.

The **Nau-senapavi** commands them. He answers for the river mouth and the coast and lets the galleys out by the sailing season, and in a city that carries other cities' cargo as well as its own it is an office of consequence.

A galley is oared and carries no cargo. The coasting ships that carry the trade belong to the merchant houses and are hired hull by hull, and the flat-bottomed river-boats work above the delta and never cross the bar. [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyalendra]] has the cargo and keeps no fleet, so it hires its escort here. The rate is settled fresh every sailing season, and settled late, which suits Chendrapur and not Vyalendra.

The fleet works a season and not a year. When the rains come the roadstead outside the bar is untenable, the galleys come up into the yards, and the crews are paid off; when the rain stops they gho back out. A Nau-senapavi therefore spends two months fighting and ten months building, victualling and arguing about money, and the office is filled with that in mind.

A galley's crew works outside the order of stations, as every crew on this coast does, and nobody aboard asks to see a wrist. The men who owe the Maharava service in his host owe it aboard as well, and the deck is the one place in the city where they serve beside men the temples will not admit.

## Relations

Chendrapur is patron to the [[affiliation-rajeprjnpd|Rajepur Janpoda]] under a formal protection arrangement nearly two centuries old, and it has declined the kingdoms' requests to expel the Rajapuri scholar who argues for more dissolutions. It buys the gold of [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvargeri]] and the bows of [[affiliation-dnrktjnpd|Danurkot]], and stands on good terms with [[affiliation-shikhalaya|Shikhalaya]]. Its standing rival is Vyalendra, whose cloth its galleys carry under a hire neither city has ever made last more than a year. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] is licensed to stand at Chandramukha and nowhere else in Vedyara. It dates from the season a kingdom up the river held Rajepur's head-gate ([[lore-rjprprotct|Rajepur Under Protection]]).

## Commerce and Currency

Chendrapur strikes Vedyara's silver and gold. The Moon House strikes the silver [[miscgear-chendra|chendra]] of ordinary formal commerce, and the gold [[miscgear-suvorna|suvorna]] is struck here from Suvargeri gold; the copper [[miscgear-tomra|tomra]] is struck here as at every court. The city holds a seat in the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]], which sits here each spring, and [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]]'s letters of credit settle the larger trades. See [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]] for the system and [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] for the institution.

## Outside the Ladder

A person placed outside the tarona, by transgression or by birth, is excluded from the wells, the temples and the courts.
