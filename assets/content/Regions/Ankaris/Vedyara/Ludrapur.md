**Lūdrapur**, the walled town, was the capital of the march before [[affiliation-bhumipala|Bhūmipāla]] existed. It lies a few miles off [[place-marchroad|the march road]] between [[place-sandhyapur|Sandhyāpur]] and [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]], on a rise above a seasonal river that is dry for eight months of the year. In 315 AF the host of [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]] came down the road after destroying the kingdom's army at [[place-lanthusthli|Lanthusthalī]], and burned it ([[lore-marupalafl|the Fall of Marupāla]]). Nobody has lived in it since.

## What Is There

The wall-circuit stands almost whole, a ring of mud brick on a stone footing about a mile round, with four gates and the burn-marks still black on the inner faces where the timber galleries went up. Inside it the streets are mounds of fallen brick with the line of each house still readable in the grass. The palace was a mound before it was a ruin, a platform raised above the town, and its burned rooms are a hollow on top of the platform with the hearths still in place.

The temple is the best preserved building in the town and the emptiest. Its image was carried down to Sandhyāpur by the Bhūmipāla court a generation after the burning and installed there, and the temple at Lūdrapur has stood without a god since.

## Who Uses It

The march companies winter in it. The wall still turns the wind, the cisterns under the palace platform still hold the rains, and a company can water a hundred horses inside the gates. Each company has its own quarter, marked by the colors painted on the gateposts, and quarrels between companies are settled in the empty temple court where nothing holy is left to offend.

The dead of Lūdrapur were the townspeople, killed in their streets, and the town burned over them. The companies treat it as a ruin and not as a _shorukshetra_ (blood-field), and the ruin has given them no reason to think otherwise. They do not sleep in the palace hollow all the same.

## Hooks

- **The treasury.** Marupāla's treasury was never found. It was not in the palace when the host burned it, and the steppe did not carry it off, because the khanate's camps at Amradad had no march gold in them. A company sergeant has found a stair going down under the palace platform, below the cisterns.
- **The seal.** Bhūmipāla's title to the march rests on Marupāla having left no heir. A claimant arrives at Sandhyāpur with a seal cut with Ashvakīrti's name and a genealogy that runs through a daughter nobody recorded, and the companies, who are owed arrears, are listening.
- **The image.** The Sandhyāpur temple's image came from here. Its priests are quietly asking a party to find out whether the god went with the statue or stayed in the empty house.
