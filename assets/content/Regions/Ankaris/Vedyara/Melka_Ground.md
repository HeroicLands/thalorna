**The Melka Ground** is the plain where [[place-mahanodi|the Mahanodi]] takes its greatest tributary, and it is where the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|janpodas of Vedyara]] hold their twelve-yearly assembly. The ground is flat, broad and dry for eleven months of the year. Nobody lives on it. Between assemblies it carries one temple, [[place-sangema|Sangema]], a landing, and a great deal of grass.

## The Assembly

The [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} runs for forty days. The first thirty are the religious observances and the last ten are the business of the confederation, which is water-rights between janpodas on one river system, trade conventions, the reaffirmation of the great pilgrimage routes, and boundary disputes that have outgrown bilateral settlement. Seats are counted by river-circuit, and a janpoda speaks through the delegation of the water it drinks unless it holds a seat in its own right.

A quarter of a million people stand on the ground at the height of it. They come up the river by boat and down the pilgrim roads on foot, and the four circuits camp apart by long custom, each on the reach of the plain nearest its own water.

## The River Decides

The Mahanodi has changed its course four times within written record, and its behavior in the season before a Melka decides where on the plain the Melka can be held. The lanes, the camping grounds and the bathing places are laid out afresh each time by the temple, working from the previous assembly's survey and from whatever the last flood left.

The work takes the better part of two years and is the standing occupation of the temple between assemblies. "You do not inherit the Melka ground," a priest of Sangema tells a newcomer to the survey; "you measure it again."

## The Ground Between

Pilgrims come in the eleven intervening years as well, in ones and twenties rather than in delegations, and the bathing place at the confluence is held sacred whether the confederation is sitting or not. The grazing is let to the villages of the reach, on the understanding that the herds come off the plain in the year before a Melka and that nothing is built.
