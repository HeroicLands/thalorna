A priest of the Bhārava circuit who has served his turn at [[place-sanghafort|the Sangha-fort]] tells a newcomer the story in the order the janapadas care about it. First, there was an army. Second, it went home. Third, the fort stayed, and that is the part that matters.

## The Door

[[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvāra]], the conch-door, is the last crossing of the wall and the worst: a shelf cut above a gorge under the black face of [[place-kalashkhra|Kālashikhara]], two months from anywhere in [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]] worth reaching, and crossed by two or three parties in a good year. About 320 AF, in the generation [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]] came down the western end of the range, an adventurer brought a force over it from the Tānvüri side.

It was not a host. The conch-door carries no army, and the **Conch-Door Incursion** came over it as a few hundred picked men, which was enough. A janapada fields a few hundred militia on short notice and a few thousand on a long call-up, and it fields them alone. The adventurer went through the eastern janapadas one at a time, subjugated a series of them and threatened more, and no single sabhā could raise enough to stop him.

## The Third Army

The temple-republics have no army and no mechanism for raising one, and they raised one. The senior priests of the region called on every janapada within reach to send its militia, and the force they assembled put the adventurer back out through the door he had come in by. Then it dissolved, as soon as the threat ended. It is the third time in recorded Vedyari history that the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahā-Sangha]] has raised something like a confederation army, and the last; nobody can say what the first two were.

What the janapadas took from the affair was a precedent. They built the Sangha-fort on the Vedyari col, a squat stone work with quarters for sixty men, and have kept it garrisoned in rotation by the janapadas of the Bhārava circuit ever since. It is the only standing garrison the confederation keeps anywhere, and it has never been withdrawn. Its supply runs on [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] pack-trains under a contract renewed every third year at [[place-nurvhrn|Nürvhrn]], and the lowland factors have walked up for the renewal for four centuries without once being asked to stay the winter.

## Two Accounts of the Backing

Every Vedyari account says the adventurer came with Tānvüri backing, and the account the eastern janapadas give names a frontier faction and not the throne. [[affiliation-tanvurempr|The Empire of Tānvür]] says he was a private adventurer and nothing it did, and that neither side has ever seriously tried to rule the other. Both accounts are current.

What Tānvür did afterward is a matter of record. Its frontier officers have registered every Vedyari caravan through [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]] for four hundred years, and the way-station beyond the throat of [[place-jnanadvara|Jñānadvāra]] has kept its register of travelers without a gap for as long. Vedyari visitors are treated with courtesy there and are never shown the register.

## The Quarrel It Left

[[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharālaya]] holds that the whole northern frontier is the kingdom's to watch, and reads the fort as ground held inside it by a body with no business holding any. The kingdom raises the point at every Mela and has never done more than raise it. The one armed force ever to come over the wall came by the conch-door, which the kingdom does not hold.
