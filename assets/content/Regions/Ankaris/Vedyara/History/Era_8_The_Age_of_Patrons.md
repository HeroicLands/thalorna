A Vedyari polity of the present rarely fears a neighbor's army. It fears its patron's displeasure, its own succession and the Collective's factor at the gate, and the **Age of Patrons** is the two centuries in which that became true. It is the age a traveler walks into.

## What Happened

About 525 AF a kingdom up the river took Rājapur's head-gate village for a season and was put out by Chandrapur's money and a hired company, and the janapada, which had kept no soldiers since the dissolution, signed a formal protection with the city ([[lore-rjprprotct|Rājapur Under Protection]]). Small mountain kingdoms became patrons of Dhanurkota and Suvarnagiri on the same terms, and protection replaced conquest as the way a strong polity held a weak one.

About 560 the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]] scoured a temple in Vyālendra and was banned from the city for it, and a generation later the council that banned it put up a statue of the priest ([[lore-agnipartng|the Three Partings]]). About 590 the Martial Council at Sūryagarha acclaimed a dead king's widow ([[lore-widowaccl|the Widow's Acclamation]]). About 660 the Ādānashrenī cut off the craft-lines that would kill during a taking ([[lore-cutthread|the Cut Thread]]). Two winters ago the spring at Chandraprabhava froze for nine days, and the temple has not written it down ([[lore-nineice|the Nine Days of Ice]]).

## Rulers

- **Shikharālaya:** the widow **Jvalasundarī**, acclaimed about 590; three reigns since have kept the present policy on the Guides' Road; the present Mahārāja's successor is disputed between the two councils.
- **Chandrapur:** about 600 the temples refused a claimant for the second time, and about 640 the crown refused a House's succession for the second time. Neither refusal has been explained.
- **Bhūmipāla:** a line confirmed by the Martial Council of the forts since Kāvravīra.

## The Present

The age's quarrels are a traveler's quarrels: the Shikharālayan councils at odds over the next acclamation, [[being-anrjhrdvmbjkr|Anurāja Harshadevāmbujakar]]'s letters urging further dissolutions, the succession to Sangama's convening priest before the next Mela, and a spring that froze and was not recorded.
