Every Vedyari student of political philosophy learns the **Age of Kingdoms** by its last forty days, and that is a pity, because the two and a half centuries before them are the age the classical tongue was written in. The name is the scholars' own, from the [[skill-vedyarlng|Vedyari]] grammars: it runs from M 1 to M 240, 480 to 241 BF.

## What Happened

The philosopher-kings of [[place-madhavendra|Madhusthāna]] fixed the language and started the count ([[lore-stndrdmdhv|the Standardization]]), and in the same year the kings of [[lore-mhndkngdm|Mahānadī]] moved to [[place-rajapur|Rājapur]] and cut [[place-sandstonealtr|the sandstone altar]]. Kingdoms grew along the rivers and took janapadas into them, and gave some of them back.

The [[lore-rcitkngsmhnd|Recitation of the Kings]] remembers the age as two halves: the just kings, then the six who ended the line. The worst act of the six left the first _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in Vedyara. About 300 BF the second of them destroyed a rising of the upper-river villages at a ford and forbade the pyre to the dead ([[lore-unburnford|the Unburned Ford]]), and a decade later a wandering ascetic carried a living ember off that ground and founded [[affiliation-agnipantha|the Agnī-panthā]] ([[lore-kindlford|the Kindling at the Ford]]).

## Rulers

The Mahānadī kings of Rājapur, as the library's chronicle and the Memory-Keeper's prose commentary give them:

| King                                                | Reigned          | Remembered for                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Jayasūra**                                        | from 480 BF      | moving the court to Rājapur and cutting the altar                    |
| **Satyakīrti**                                      | about 380 BF     | recutting the upper channels; the just king the recitation dwells on |
| the dilettante                                      | about 320 BF     | the first of the six                                                 |
| **Dandavīra**                                       | about 305–290 BF | judging in anger and in haste, and the Unburned Ford                 |
| the incompetent, the paranoiac and the child-tyrant | to about 250 BF  | the middle of the six                                                |
| the drunkard                                        | to 241 BF        | the open granaries, and the death that ended the line                |

The recitation names the six by their vices and never by name; Dandavīra's name stands in the chronicle and in prose, not in the verse. The philosopher-kings of Madhusthāna stay unnamed in every account.

## Its End

In M 240 the drunkard died without an heir in a famine, after his guard had opened the royal granaries, and the villages asked a priest for a sabhā and no king. [[lore-fortydays|The Forty Days]] dissolved the kingdom and opened the [[lore-agecopyists|Age of Copyists]].
