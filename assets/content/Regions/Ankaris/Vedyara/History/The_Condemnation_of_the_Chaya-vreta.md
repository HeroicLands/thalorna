The [[affiliation-svapnasarisameja|Svapnasari-sameja]] reads dreams for anyone who asks, and the asking is the whole of its ethics. **The Condemnation of the Chaya-vreta** is the one time its [Council of the Dream]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} ruled on a line that did not wait to be asked.

## The Line

By the fifth century AF a line inside the Sameja had learned to work the waking dream: to reach into the mind of a person who was awake and unwilling and set a dream going there. The Sameja's other lines read the dreams of sleepers who had come to them. This one went to people who had not come.

## The Condemnation

About 470 AF the Council of the Dream, the senior council of oneiromancers that adjudicates disputed readings and keeps the tradition's registers, condemned the line, and it did something the Council had never done before: it held the condemnation open. A condemnation that is closed has been answered and is over. One held open stands until the condemned line ends, and it has not ended.

The monasteries closed to the line's students at once. No monastery will seat a [[affiliation-chayavreta|Chaya-vreta]] or name one. Nobody pursues them either. The Council's ruling was that the tradition would not keep the vow, and it left the rest of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] to decide what to do about the people who kept it anyway.

## What It Left

- **The vow outside the Sameja.** [[affiliation-chayavreta|The Chaya-vreta]] has been taught ever since one student at a time, master to student, with no house and no register.
- **The shadow-brotherhoods**, which grew from it and train Vedyara's assassins.
- **[[place-waitinghouse|The Waiting House]].** A line of the vow has been taught in the upper rooms of the Waiting House in [[place-chendrapur2|Chendrapur]] for most of that building's history.
