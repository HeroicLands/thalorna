A Vyālendri Loom-Master tells an apprentice that the pattern-book rule is older than the Council. It is older than the kings too, and the city's own account of the **Raising of the Vyālendri Crown** says so: the weavers were drawing patterns for one hall only before any lord thought of crowning himself among them.

## The First King

About 125 BF **Nīlapati**, a lord of the indigo valleys upstream, took the cloth city. The account the cloth-court keeps does not say how. It says that he was crowned in the city, not in his valleys, and that he chose the city because the cloth was there and the indigo was worth more woven than raw.

His line built what the Council still uses. They laid out the palace quarter at the top of the city, raised the audience hall with a dais for a throne, and set the street-plan that runs from the palace down to the water-gate. The Loom-Council governs a guild republic from a kingdom's street-plan, and visitors from kingdoms find it disorderly for that reason.

## Eleven Reigns

The crown lasted eleven reigns. Nīlapati is remembered by name; nine kings after him are remembered by nobody outside the cloth-court. The eleventh and last laid out the pleasure-house that is now [[place-sandhyagrha|Sandhyāgriha]], and about 150 AF his line ended and the Loom-Council sat down in his hall ([[lore-vylndrkngs|the End of the Vyālendri Kingship]]). How the line ended stays unresolved.

## What It Left

- **The palace quarter and the hall** of [[place-vyalendra3|Vyālendra]], where the Council sits on sixty-one seats round the step the dais stood on.
- **The loom villages** of [[place-vyalendraland|the Nilacharī valleys]], which answer to the houses of the Loom-Masters and have had no king above them since the eleventh.
- **The pleasure-house**, which outlived the court that built it.
