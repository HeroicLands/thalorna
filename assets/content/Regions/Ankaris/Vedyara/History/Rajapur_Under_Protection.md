Rājapur has had no army since [[lore-fortydays|the Forty Days]], and its sabhā says so with some pride. The **Protection** is what it has instead. [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]] has been its patron for nearly two centuries, and it was a season without water that made it so.

## The Season

About 525 AF a kingdom up the river came down and took [[place-khandapura|Khandāpura]], the janapada's upstream village at the head of the irrigation works. Whoever holds the head-gate holds the water of every village below it. The kingdom did not want Rājapur's fields. It wanted Rājapur's grain at a price it would name, and it held the gate shut through the planting to make the point.

[[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]] could muster perhaps a thousand cultivators and artisans, and they were not going to take a gate from a king's garrison. The sabhā sent to Chandrapur. The city's houses lent the money, the money hired a company, and the company put the garrison out of Khandāpura before the season's end.

## The Protection

The sabhā signed a formal protection with the city in the same year. Chandrapur answers for Rājapur's defense. Rājapur pays in grain from the common stores and in the work of its copyists, who keep the city's charters and correspondence in the library's hand. The arrangement has been renewed by every sabhā since.

## What It Left

- **The walled head-gate.** Khandāpura's gate-house was walled after the season, and the village has kept the gate and its wall since.
- **The patron.** Rājapur's first answer to any threat is an appeal to Chandrapur; its second is the Mahā-Sangha's disapproval; its third is the floodplain in the wet season.
- **The scholar.** Chandrapur's patronage is the reason [[being-anrjhrdvmbjkr|Anurāja Harshadevāmbujakar]], a Rājapuri who argues for further dissolutions, writes his letters from the city, and the reason the city declines to expel him.
