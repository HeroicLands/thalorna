In **Rajepur Under Protection**, [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rajepur Janapoda]] has had no army since [[lore-fortydays|The Forty Days]], and its sabhra says so with some pride. The protection is what it has instead. [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chendrapur]] has been its patron for nearly two centuries, and it was a season without water that made it so.

## The Season

About 525 AF a kingdom up the river came down and took [[place-khandapura|Khandapura]], the janapoda's upstream village at the head of the irrigation works. Whoever holds the head-gate holds the water of every village below it. The kingdom did not want Rajepur's fields. It wanted Rajepur's grain at a price it would name, and it held the gate shut through the planting to make the point.

Rajepur could muster perhaps a thousand cultivators and artisans, and they were not going to take a gate from a king's garrison. The sabhra sent to Chendrapur. The city's houses lent the money, the money hired a company, and the company put the garrison out of Khandapura before the season's end.

## The Protection

The sabhra signed a formal protection with the city in the same year. Chendrapur answers for Rajepur's defense. Rajepur pays in grain from the common stores and in the work of its copyists, who keep the city's charters and correspondence in the library's hand. The arrangement has been renewed by every sabhra since.

## What It Left

- **The walled head-gate.** Khandapura's gate-house was walled after the season, and the village has kept the gate and its wall since.
- **The patron.** Rajepur's first answer to any threat is an appeal to Chendrapur; its second is the disapproval of the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Janapadas of Vedyara]]; its third is the floodplain in the wet season.
- **The scholar.** Chendrapur's patronage is the reason [[being-anrjhrdvmbjkr|Anuraja Harshadevambujakar]], a Rajapuri who argues for further dissolutions, writes his letters from the city, and the reason the city declines to expel him.
