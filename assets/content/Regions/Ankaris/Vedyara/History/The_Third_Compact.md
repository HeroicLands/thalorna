A Standards clerk of the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] begins a new factor's history lesson with a date, which is rare in Vedyara: the Mahā-Mela of 320 AF. That is where the **Third Compact** was sworn, and every weight, road-tax and letter of credit the factor will handle in his working life runs on it.

## The Name

The formal name is the **Third Janapada Compact**, and it comes from the Mela. Twice before, the janapadas had sworn compacts among themselves at the twelve-yearly assembly, on the roads they shared and the water they drank, and nobody now can say when. The merchants put their law to the same assembly and took the same form for it, so it was sworn as the third. In running speech it is the Third Compact, or simply the Compact.

## The Generation Before It

The Compact was three generations in the writing. Its authors were merchant princes working across polities that were then at war with one another, and they could not unify the subcontinent and did not try. In the third, [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|The Storm of the Hundred Banners]] came round the western end of the wall and overran the northwestern march, and in the east [[lore-conchdoor|an adventurer came through the conch-door]] and took janapadas one by one until the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|Mahā-Sangha]] raised an army to put him out. The third of them counted from [[lore-fieldelephnt|the Field of Elephants]], about 270, a battle no kingdom won.

The Mela that next sat, in 320 AF on the twelve-yearly cycle that put a Mela in 716, heard the merchants' law, and the polities signed it. The Compact holds in common the roads, the caravanserai and the watch posts, along with the arbitration procedure, the weights and measures and the currency standards. The merchants' terms were simple: the rules of trade made the same on both sides of every border, and each polity free to sign or to watch its neighbors grow richer than it. Each signed.

## What It Made

The signatures constituted [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|the Assembly of the Compact]], which sits twice a year—in the spring at [[place-chandrapur2|Chandrapur]], in the autumn at [[place-vyalendra3|Vyālendra]]—to renew and amend the law, and the same instrument made the Merchant Collective answerable to it. Five polities hold seats: [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]], [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]], [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharālaya]], [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]] and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]]. They have signed every renewal since, and they share [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|the Vedyaran banking system]] that grew out of it. [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]] stands outside by its own constitution, because a seat would open its gold to the Collective's factors.

Chandrapur's settlement between its crown and its **Nine Houses** has held since the same generation, and the city has kept the gem trade its own for as long. The Collective has been the connective tissue of Vedyaran life for the four centuries since, and nothing moves without it.
