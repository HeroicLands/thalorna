"One of the hundred small kingdoms of Vedyara" is still how a Vedyari describes a court of forty people and a fort above each valley, and the phrase comes from this age. In the **Hundred Crowns** the kingdoms really were that many, and they were at war with each other for most of a century and a half: for water on the rivers, for ore in the hills, for fields wherever a field could be taken. Kingdoms absorbed janapadas and released them again, and mountain dynasties rose and fell fast enough that their names changed every few reigns.

## What Happened

Three generations of merchant princes worked across the borders while the wars went on, toward a law under which the polities could trade without needing to be one state. The [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]]'s own history begins among the polities "that were then at war with each other," and the third generation of those princes dated its work from the year after the age's great battle.

That battle was the [[lore-fieldelephnt|Field of Elephants]]. About 265 AF the king of [[affiliation-gomarga|Gomārga]] took three lineages' wells into the crown's hand to pay for a war on the river ([[lore-wellbrk1|the First Breaking of the Well Bargain]]). About 270 AF his coalition came down off the plateau against the river kingdom of **Purnatīra**, both lines broke among the war-elephants, and the dead were left to the rains on ground that became the second _shorukshetra_. About 280 the kings forbade the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]] to burn that field, and the sect went anyway ([[lore-agnipartng|the Three Partings]]). About 260 the Priestly Court of Shikharālaya won its own hall at Sūryagarha ([[lore-passcharter|the Charter of the Pass-Road]]).

## Rulers

- **Gomārga:** **Vrishasūra**, who broke the bargain and lost the war.
- **Purnatīra:** a river kingdom of the middle Mahānadī, broken at the Field of Elephants; its lands passed to janapadas within a generation.
- **Shikharālaya:** the Martial Council acclaimed a younger brother over the heir about 260, in the years the Priestly Court's hall was raised.
- **Chandrapur:** **Ratnasūra**, refused by the temples, and **Elvunātha**, whom the Nine Houses put on the throne.

## Its End

About 312 AF Chandrapur's temples refused to crown the dead king's son, and the nine great cutting houses brokered a cousin onto the throne on their own terms ([[lore-moonsettle|the Moon House Settlement]]). Three years later a steppe host came round the western end of the wall, and the [[lore-hardgenrtn|Hard Generation]] began.
