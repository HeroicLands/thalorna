**The Unnumbered Age** is everything [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] remembers from before it counted. A Vedyari scholar calls it the age of residue: stones nobody laid within memory, customs nobody can source, and names on the foothills older than the language the scholars write. None of it carries a year, and the [[affiliation-ganokashalda|Ganoka-shalda]]'s reckoners will not give it one.

## What It Left

- **[[lore-janpdform|The janpoda form]].** The temple-republics date their form to nearly three thousand years before the present, and hold that it has run unbroken since. They say so as a people says what it is, and nobody can test it.
- **The law of wells.** [[affiliation-gomarga|Gomarga]]'s accession oath, that the crown holds the wells and the droveways and does not adjudicate grass, is called a hundred generations old. It is older than any king's name the plateau keeps.
- **The older stones.** The megalithic courses under [[place-bowfort|the Bow-Fort]], fitted without mortar; [[place-lowercourses|the lower courses under Chendrapur]], which show along the river stair in the dry months; and the warm gray slab of [[place-himashila|Himashila]] at the head of [[place-chandrmahi|the Chandramahi]]. No account names who set any of them, and no account connects one to another.

## Its End

The age ends when the highland temples begin to keep an age of their own. The gold temples of [[place-suvargeri|Suvargeri]], the four academies of the bow and the first forest gift all date themselves to about 780 BF, and from them [[lore-foundingage|the Founding Age]] begins.
