The **Unnumbered Age** is everything [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] remembers from before it counted. A Vedyari scholar calls it the age of residue: stones nobody laid within memory, customs nobody can source, and names on the foothills older than the language the scholars write. None of it carries a year, and the [[affiliation-ganakashala|Ganaka-shala]]'s reckoners will not give it one.

## What It Left

- **The janapada form.** The temple-republics date [[lore-janpdform|their form]] to nearly three thousand years before the present, and hold that it has run unbroken since. They say so as a people says what it is, and nobody can test it.
- **The law of wells.** [[affiliation-gomarga|Gomārga]]'s accession oath, that the crown holds the wells and the droveways and does not adjudicate grass, is called a hundred generations old. It is older than any king's name the plateau keeps.
- **The older stones.** The megalithic courses under [[place-bowfort|the Bow-Fort]], fitted without mortar; [[place-lowercourses|the lower courses under Chandrapur]], which show along the river stair in the dry months; and the warm gray slab of [[place-himashila|Himashilā]] at the head of the Chandramahī. No account names who set any of them, and no account connects one to another.

## Its End

The age ends when the highland temples begin to keep an age of their own. The gold temples of Suvarnagiri, the four academies of the bow and the first forest gift all date themselves to about 780 BF, and from them the [[lore-foundingage|Founding Age]] begins.
