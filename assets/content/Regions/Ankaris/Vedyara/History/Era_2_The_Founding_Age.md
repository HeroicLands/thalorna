The oldest things in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] that carry a date are temples, and that is the whole character of the **Founding Age**. Its states are hardly remembered. Its institutions are still running.

## What Happened

About 780 BF the highland villages laid down what [[lore-hghlndfndn|the Highland Foundations]] name: the three gold temples round [[place-goldmountain|the Gold Mountain]], the four archery academies inside [[place-bowfort|the Bow-Fort]], and the first forest of [[place-bharavavana|Bhāravavana]] given to a temple as the god's portion. Each still runs on the terms it was founded on.

About 640 BF the [[lore-mhndkngdm|Kingdom of Mahānadī]] took the upper river and its fields. Its library does not record where its first kings sat. The upper-river villages call a terraced hill on the pilgrim road [[place-puranasthana|Purānasthāna]], the old seat, and graze goats on it; that is legend, and the library keeps it as legend.

## Rulers

The Mahānadī line traces itself to a heroic ancestor the recitation names only as legend. The kings of the old seat follow him, unnamed in any record the library holds, until the move to Rājapur.

## Its End

The age ends with the reign of the philosopher-kings of Madhusthāna, who fix the classical tongue and start [[lore-mdhvndrcnt|the count]] that every Vedyari court still keeps. Their first year, M 1, opens the [[lore-agekingdoms|Age of Kingdoms]].
