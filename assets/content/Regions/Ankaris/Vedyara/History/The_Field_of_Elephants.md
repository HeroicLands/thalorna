Every chronicle of the Hundred Crowns records the **Field of Elephants**, and none of them records a winner. That is the point a Vedyari teacher makes first: both armies came off the field broken, and the age's merchant princes took the lesson the kings could not.

## The War

About 265 AF the king of [[affiliation-gomarga|Gomārga]], **Vrishasūra**, wanted a war on the river. The plateau's wealth is herds and watering dues, and neither pays a host, so he took three great lineages' wells into the crown's own hand and spent their earnings ([[lore-wellbrk1|the First Breaking of the Well Bargain]]). His coalition was the plateau kingdom and the smaller crowns that followed it for a share.

Its enemy was **Purnatīra**, a river kingdom of the middle [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadī]] with good fields and a stable of [[being-vdyrnelphnt|war-elephants]]. Gomārga had elephants too, bought out of the river valleys for the purpose.

## The Field

About 270 the two hosts met on the long slope where the plateau comes down to the Mahānadī plain. The chroniclers agree on the elephants. Both lines put them in front, both lines' elephants were driven mad by the noise and the wounds, and both lines broke under their own beasts as much as under the enemy's. The fighting went on into the first storms of the monsoon, and when it ended there were more dead on the slope than the villages below could carry home or burn. The rains came, and the dead stayed where they were.

The slope became [[place-gajasthali|Gajasthalī]], the elephant ground, the second _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in Vedyara. It is the shallowest of the three.

## What It Left

- **The field.** Gajasthalī is a recurrence: in the first storms of the monsoon, drums sound along the slope at dusk and elephants trumpet for miles. No elephant will set foot on it at any season.
- **The river.** Purnatīra never recovered its strength. Within a generation its villages had passed to janapadas, and those janapadas still share one circuit-speaker at the Mela by habit, which is all that remains of the kingdom.
- **The plateau.** The war was lost and Gomārga returned the wells. The accession oath gained its clause about wells, and every Mahārāja has sworn it since.
- **The Compact.** The [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] counts its third generation of merchant princes from the year after the field, and the law they worked toward became [[lore-thirdcmpct|the Third Compact]].
- **The ban.** About 280 the kings of the age forbade the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]] to burn the field, and the sect went anyway ([[lore-agnipartng|the Three Partings]]).
