The **Festival of the Returning** is held in [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]] every autumn, and an academy master explains it to a new student in one sentence: the janapada welcomes home the graduates who come home, because once some did not. The **Highland War** is the once.

## The March

About 415 AF the mountain kingdom of **Dhaunapur**, one of the small kingdoms that stood patron to Dhanurkota and a neighbor of the gold country, marched on [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]]. It had the usual reasons a kingdom with a poor valley has for wanting a gold mountain. It also had something no raider had had before: Dhanurkoti graduates in its missile companies, serving under contract as graduates serve every kingdom that can pay.

## The Recall

The four academy masters met in the Bow-Fort and did what no master had done before. They recalled every graduate in Dhaunapur's service. A graduate's prerogatives come from the janapada, and the masters ruled that they did not stretch to shooting at a neighbor the janapada had no quarrel with.

Most came home. They rode with [[affiliation-bharyastan|Bharyastān]]'s four hundred horse, called under the gold treaty, to the gorge-mouth where the gold road comes down out of the hills, and they stopped Dhaunapur's host there. The field is [[place-sharamukha|Sharamukha]], the mouth of arrows. The dead were few by the standard of the river wars, and both sides carried theirs home to burn.

Dhaunapur sent a column against [[place-bowfort|the Bow-Fort]] in reprisal. It came in sight of the walls and turned back.

## What It Left

- **The Festival of the Returning.** Every autumn the graduates back from service abroad ritually re-enter the janapada, and it was first kept for those who answered the recall.
- **The struck roll.** The names of the graduates who stayed with Dhaunapur were struck from the academy rolls, and stay struck.
- **The palace at Bharyastān**, fortified that year in a way that would have been serious three centuries ago, and not since. A Bharyastāni heir who had not ridden to Sharamukha was passed over by the council at the next succession, one of the kingdom's two acclamations in four hundred years.
- **Three centuries of quiet.** Neither Suvarnagiri nor Dhanurkota has been seriously threatened since.
