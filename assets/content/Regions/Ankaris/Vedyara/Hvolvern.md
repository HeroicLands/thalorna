## Overview {#overview}

**Hvolvern** is a cliff at the head of a high valley on the far side of [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]], the side that faces [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]]. The valley opens off the northern approach to [[place-jnanadvar|Jnanadvar]] two days below the col, climbs west for a day between walls of gray rock, and ends against the cliff, well above the last trees. The people who farm the valley give the cliff its name: [[skill-hvarnlng|Hvarn]] builds it from _hvol_, "a place", and _vern_, "a house", and the common English epithet is "the cave houses". Neither shows from the Jnanadvar road.

From the upper fields the cliff is ledges, dark hollows and long stains where water runs down the rock. Lights show on it after dark, in places where no herder would sit, and on still mornings a haze lies along the face instead of rising from it. Lowland rumor puts [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]] halls above the tree line somewhere in the Reach. The rumor is old and nobody has confirmed it.

## The Short Roof

The valley belongs to a people of a little under two thousand who speak Hvarn as [[affiliation-hvarnguides|the Hvarn]] of the long house do. The long house calls them the [Lunvharn]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, "the short roof", because they winter in their own valley under roofs of their own and never come down to [[place-nurvhrn|Nurvharn]]. They sit on no winter council and draw no crossing, and the long house says so without interest, as it says most things about its neighbors.

Their valley is better farmed than its height allows. Dry-walled terraces climb both slopes, channels lead meltwater along the hillsides from the ice to every field, and barley ripens on ground where the Hvarn grow none. Yak herds graze the upper pastures, and the Lunvharn make more butter and wool than a valley of that size can use. Their sickles, pot-chains and plowshares are better iron than anything the Hvarn buy at [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-ber]], and in a lean autumn, when the long house goes down to buy grain, the Lunvharn do not.

Laden trains of yaks go up the valley head toward the cliff in every season the snow allows, and come down lighter. Asked about it, a Lunvharn herder says the cave houses are full, and says nothing more.

## Strangers

A stranger who climbs the valley is met in the lower fields, fed, and walked back down. The Lunvharn are courteous about it and do not argue, and they do not let anyone past the upper fields. A traveler who goes round them by the slopes finds the high ground above the terraces watched, and is met again.

## The Present {#present}

Last summer a silver prospector of the smithing clans of [[place-slvrgorges|the Silver Gorges]] crossed Jnanadvar behind a Hvarn party with two porters of his own, turned up the valley and was walked back down from the upper fields. He has spent the winter in the Gorges talking about a piece of silver ore he picked up on the valley track, dressed on three faces, and he is hiring porters and rope to try the cliff from the slopes above the terraces when the thaw opens the col. [Ourn]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the eldest of the Lunvharn hearth-heads, has sent word over to Nurvharn asking that no Hvarn hearth sell him a rope or walk him a stage. The winter council has not answered him, and the prospector has already offered the fee of a whole season to any Roadwalker who will take him up.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The prospector's porters.** The silver prospector wants hardy hands for the climb above the terraces this summer, and pays well for anyone who can rope a cliff.
- **Ourn's request.** Ourn wants someone the long house will hear to carry his request to the winter council before a Hvarn hearth takes the prospector's fee.

# The Truth of Hvolvern {#truth .secret}

**The hold.** The cliff is the face of [[affiliation-talgnugarhld|Talg-nugár Hold]], a lived-in Khazari hold of about six hundred, whose name means "the watching of the snow". Its homes are set in the face behind the ledges and hollows, the lights are their windows, and the haze along the face is the smoke of its flues, which follow the fissures of the rock and let it out along the cliff. Its passages run back into the mountain to the council-hall, the forges, the archive, the tombs and the refuge.

**The Lunvharn.** The Lunvharn are the hold's tied people, and the valley is the hold's larder. The hold's engineers laid out the terraces and lead the water, and the iron in the valley is the hold's work. The Lunvharn send up barley, butter, cheese, yak meat, hides and wool to the gate, and the hold sends down tools and iron, mends the channels and keeps the approaches. In a hungry year the hold opens its caches to the valley, and in a war it opens its refuge. The Lunvharn know the hold closely, its gate and its people by name, and have never been taken inside. "The cave houses are full" is the answer the valley gives every outsider, and it is true.

**The prospector's silver.** The piece of ore the prospector found is Khazari work, a scrap of dressed silver that fell from a load the hold sent down with the iron, and the dressing on its three faces is why he cannot let it go. The silver of the Gorges does run north under the Reach. The hold's lowest working follows it, and the slopes above the terraces where the prospector means to dig lie over that working. A party that digs there for two seasons breaks into it.

**The council's quarrel.** The council of the hold has not decided what to do about him. [[being-kavalannlpfrd|Kavalan Nalp-furád]], the Speaker at the Gate, wants the Lunvharn to turn him back as they turn back every stranger, with nothing cut. [[being-darakhtnvgrz|Darakh Tanv-guráz]], the Warden of the Watch, wants the slope above the terraces brought down before the thaw, so that the way he means to climb is gone. [[being-hamaktlghnth|Hamak Talg-hunáth]], the Master of Works, holds that a fall brought down on a sound slope is a forced cut, and that the record holds such a cut against the hold that made it for as long as the slope stands.

> [!TIP]
> Let the party see the hold before it understands it: a hollow that is a window, a ledge that is a sill, a stain that is a flue's smoke. The Lunvharn never lie about it. They answer a different question.
