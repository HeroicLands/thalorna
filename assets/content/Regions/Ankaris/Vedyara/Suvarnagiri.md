Suvarnagiri (2,400) is the capital of the [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri Janapada]] and stands in three parts on the lower slopes of the gold mountain. Upper Suvarnagiri is at the junction of the two highest streams, Middle Suvarnagiri in the central valley below the panning grounds, and Lower Suvarnagiri at the foot of the mountain where the Bhārava proper begins. Four miles of steep road join the highest part to the lowest. A Suvarnagiri who says the name means all three.

Each part is built round one of the three great temples of Mahájaya, and each has its own market, its own tank and its own quarter of priests' houses. The sabhā meets in whichever temple the year's rotation gives it, so the seat of government moves from part to part and back again in three years.

## The Weighing-Station

The weighing-station stands at Middle Suvarnagiri beside the common treasury, and it is the one thing in the janapada that does not rotate. Every ounce the panning families win comes here. At each new moon the previous moon's gold is weighed in the open, before whichever sabhā representatives have come down for it, and the weights are read out and entered.

Two or three hundred people attend at the height of the working season and a dozen in midwinter. Attendance is any Suvarnagiri's right, and nobody at the door asks a man his business.

## The Jewelers' Quarter

Lower Suvarnagiri holds the jewelers: some four hundred households of goldsmiths, gem-setters and enamelers, working the mountain's own gold against stones bought up from [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]]. The quarter's reputation on the continent is second to Chandrapur's and to nobody else's.

Its masters buy their stones by the year and sell their work through Collective factors who come no further up the valley than the bridge at [[place-kanakasetu|Kanakasetu]]. The iron-smelters of [[place-tamravana|Tāmravana]] work a mile upstream, and the two trades share a charcoal supply and a long quarrel over it.
