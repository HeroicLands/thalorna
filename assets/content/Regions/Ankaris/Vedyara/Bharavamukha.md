**Bhāravamukha** (1,100, market 3) stands at the mouth of the [[place-bharavarivr|Bhārava]] on the eastern coast, at the foot of the pilgrim road that runs the length of the river from the sea to the ice. It is the landfall for everyone who walks that road from a ship, and it is the outlet for the forest products that come down out of the temple-domains behind it.

The river is the least navigable of the four, and outside the flood season it carries almost nothing. The timber comes down on the flood; the rest comes on men's backs and on carts, and it is lac, resin, wax, honey, and the medicinal barks the lowland houses pay well for. The temple-domains sell them here and sell them nowhere else, and the prices are set at the quay by men who answer to no sabhā.

The port keeps the coast's season. It is busy from the end of the rains until they return, and the pilgrim traffic is heaviest in the first two months of that. The hostels at the river's foot are endowed out of the same forest products that pass the quay, which is an arrangement the priests explain at length and the shippers do not.
