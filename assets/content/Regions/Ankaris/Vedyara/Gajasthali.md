**Gajasthalī**, the elephant ground, is the long slope of coarse grass where [[place-vandhyabhumi|Vandhyabhūmi]]'s plateau comes down to the plain of the [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadī]]. About 270 AF the coalition of [[affiliation-gomarga|Gomārga]] and the river kingdom of **Purnatīra** met here with their war-elephants, both lines broke, and the dead were left to the rains ([[lore-fieldelephnt|the Field of Elephants]]). It is the second _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], and the shallowest: a recurrence, slight, and wide.

## What Is There

The slope runs for miles, and it is studded with bone. Elephant skulls and long bones work up out of the soil after every rain, and the villages along its foot have built them into walls, well-heads and the corners of cattle-pens for five centuries. A village wall in this district is as likely to be ivory-white at the base as stone.

In the first storms of the monsoon, the field repeats. At dusk drums sound along the slope where no drummer stands, and elephants trumpet in the dark loud enough to carry for miles. Nothing is seen close to.

## After Dark

By day Gajasthalī is grass and bone, and the drovers' children play at its edge. At night it is not a place to be. A traveler caught on the slope after dark hears the battle all round him: the drums, the trumpeting, the screaming of beasts and men, the ground shaking under a charge that never arrives. Shapes move at the edge of sight, and the panic of a line breaking under its own elephants comes up out of the ground. Nobody has been harmed by anything on it. A drover who slept out on the slope for three seasons running to save the droveway's toll was sent home to his lineage with his hands shaking, and never drove again.

Living elephants refuse the slope at any season, by day or night, and a mahout who forces one across has lost it. The Gomārgi droveway goes round the field's southern end, a day longer than the straight line, and the villages keep their doors shut on the field's side through the monsoon.

## Who Comes

- **Ivory-scavengers**, who dig in the dry season and leave before dark.
- **Gomārgi drovers**, on the droveway round it.
- **Temple buyers**, who purchase old ivory and do not ask where it was dug.

Nobody tends the field. The [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]] tried to burn it about 280 AF against the kings' ban, and it was a recurrence before the sect came and a recurrence after.

## Hooks

- **The tusks under the cairn.** A Kulina's house in Chandrapur wants the tusks of a famous war-elephant said to lie under a cairn at the center of the slope. Digging there takes more than one day, and the house wants it done by night, when nobody will see the hole.
- **The war-chest.** A court asks for a Threshold-keeper's warrant to question a dead general about the war-chest Purnatīra lost on the field. The warrant is refused on both its grounds at once, war and trade, and the claimant starts looking for someone less particular.
- **A night's wages.** A droveway keeper offers good money to anyone who will spend nine nights on the slope counting what passes, because a lineage claims the field has grown.
