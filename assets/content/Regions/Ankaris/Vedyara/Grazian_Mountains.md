Nothing crosses the Grazian wall without a guide's leave, and no state in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] or beyond it can grant that leave in the guide's place. The range closes the whole northern edge of the subcontinent, it is the highest on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]], and only six roads go over it.

A register-writer at the [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]] shrine, asked by a newcomer how busy the wall is, turns back a page of the crossing-book before he answers. "Three or four caravans a year on each of the six roads, all of them in the weeks when the ice lets go. The guides keep roughly half of what a load is worth and the merchant house that organized the caravan keeps the rest. The owner of the cargo keeps the privilege of having moved goods across the highest mountains in the world."

## Overview

The **Grazian Mountains** are the unaligned wall north of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyari subcontinent]] and south of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]]. [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]] lies north of the range's eastern reach. In the west the range ends in the foothills of the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]], and the road between Dunhara and Vedyara goes around it. The ridges answer to no state; the passes are watched by local guides, by the shrines at their summits, and by the occasional lowland garrison at their approaches.

The summits stay under snow and glacier ice throughout the year, even when the lower passes briefly open in late summer, and the peaks catch the dawn an hour before the lowlands beneath them. The glaciers have held their valleys to the same shape since before the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] arrived on the world, and their snowfields feed half the rivers of Vedyara and a meaningful share of the Khazryn's southwestern oases.

Vedyari geographers write **the Grazian Range** in formal scholarship and **the Grazian Wall** in poetry. The foothill janapadas keep a dozen older names in their own dialect, each of them older than the classical Vedyari tongue and none of them translating cleanly.

The range is impassable to organized travel for ten months of the year. Six crossings exist in its two thousand miles. Five are high passes, [[place-meghadvara|Meghadvāra]], [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]], [[place-tamradvara|Tāmradvāra]], [[place-jnanadvara|Jñānadvāra]] and [[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvāra]], each with a shrine at the summit that says its blessing over every caravan that attempts it. The sixth is [[place-gudesroad|the Guides' Road]], which no shrine blesses and no lowlander walks unled.

All six open briefly in late summer and are dangerous even then. A small population of mountain peoples, who live by guiding caravans across them, knows each road in detail. What crosses is silk and porcelain from Tānvür, gold and gems from Vedyara, and horses and silver from the Khazryn, in small, expensive shipments under the guides' protection. The volume is small, the value very high and the markup higher still.

## Geography

The range forms a great curving arc. It begins in the high northern country east of Tānvür's western frontier, sweeps south and west across the whole northern margin of Vedyara, and loses height steadily until it dies away in broken hill country above the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhari]] desert-margin. Its northern face is the southern boundary of the Khazryn Desert, the country of the [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Khazryn]] and the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Ātárzád]]. The arc runs some two thousand miles and is more than three hundred miles deep at its widest.

Scholars in Vedyara and in the Khazryn country name three sub-ranges:

- [[place-sthrnwall|The Southern Wall]]—the long, almost continuous escarpment that forms the northern border of Vedyara. Its peaks are the highest of the entire system, and the rivers draining south from them are the great river systems of the Vedyari subcontinent: the [[place-chandrmahi|Chandramahī]], the [[place-sarvadarivr|Sarvada]], the [[place-mahanadi|Mahānadi]], the [[place-bharavarivr|Bhārava]] and others. Vedyari pilgrimage tradition makes particular Southern Wall peaks the dwelling-places of [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka]] deities, and certain glacier-springs the literal cosmic sources from which their rivers flowed at the beginning of time.

- [[place-estrnreach|The Eastern Reach]]—the sub-range that arcs eastward toward Tānvür's western frontier. It is less massive than the Southern Wall and still formidable, and it is the principal overland barrier between the Vedyari and Tānvüri civilizations.

- [[place-wstrndscnt|The Western Descent]]—the last three hundred miles, where the wall comes apart. The ridges drop, the glaciers give out, and the range breaks into a belt of dry brown foothills between the oasis-belt to the north and the Vedyari marches to the south, before running out altogether in the Dunhari desert-margin. It is the one stretch of the range a caravan crosses without a guide, and the way a steppe host came down into Vedyara four centuries ago. The Khazryn tradition holds that the first Khazryn came down here into the great springs of the desert in the deep first age, and the older Khazryn genealogies still trace lineage-origins to particular peaks above the descent.

## The Trans-Grazian Peoples

A handful of mountain peoples live scattered across the range, in the few high valleys where sustained habitation is possible. They belong to none of the surrounding civilizations and recognize no outside authority. They are the trans-Grazian guides: the [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] of the western and central crossings and the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] of the Eastern Reach. Without them there is no trans-Grazian trade, and with them it costs a thousand times what the goods are worth in the cities of origin.

They speak their own languages, [[skill-osketlng|Ösket]] and [[skill-hvarnlng|Hvarn]], which are related to nothing in the lowland families and not to each other, and they keep their own austere religious traditions. Hereditary lines carry the detailed knowledge: the passes open in a given year, the loads a yak-route will take, and the weather signs that run ahead of the storms that close the high country in days.

The trans-Grazian peoples number thirty thousand across the entire range. They live in villages of stacked stone built into south-facing slopes: [[place-oskhelt|Öskhelt]] above the greatest pass, [[place-tsokhar|Tsökhar]] in the western valleys, [[place-nurvhrn|Nürvhrn]] under the Eastern Reach. They raise yaks and the small mountain horses bred to their altitudes, and spend about a third of every year hosting and guiding caravans through the passes whose keys they alone hold. Out of the trade season the lowlands forget them until the next caravan needs to cross.

## What the Wall Means to Its Neighbors

Each people that looks at the range reads something different in it.

- For the [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]], specific Southern Wall peaks are the dwelling-places of [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka]] deities, and the glacier-springs that feed the great rivers are sites of major pilgrimage. The annual ascent-pilgrimages to the lower slopes draw hundreds of thousands of devotees in the favorable months.
- For the Khazryn, the Western Descent is the ancestral homeland, the country their forebears came down from in the first age. Khazryn genealogies root themselves in particular peaks, and the surviving Khazryn princely houses keep ceremonial hereditary connections to particular valleys of the descent, most of which they have not visited in many generations.
- For the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Ātárzád]], the range is theologically irrelevant. The land was promised to them at **Mt. Shōfar** in the southeastern Khazryn highlands, and the wall marks the southern edge of the **Promise**'s geographic frame.
- For the trans-Grazian peoples themselves, the whole range is the dwelling-place of forces they call by names they do not share with outsiders. The travel-protocols a lowland caravan must keep to cross safely come from that reckoning, and outsiders are told what to do and not why.
- For the [[lore-tanvuriclt|Tānvüri]] of the eastern frontier, the Eastern Reach is the western edge of the celestial empire's mandate, beyond which, in the standard Tānvüri view, geography itself becomes increasingly suspect.

## The Trade

The trans-Grazian trade is what the range means economically to the lowland civilizations. Three to four caravans a year cross each of the six routes, in the favorable late-summer window. The goods are the high-value, low-weight specialties: silk and porcelain west, gold and gems east, and specialty medicines, manuscripts and certain ritual goods both ways.

The guides take roughly half the value as their fee. The caravan organizers take the other half, and are usually Vedyari merchant houses based in [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]] or one of the foothill janapadas.

On the Vedyari side the trade is governed as well as taxed. The kingdom of [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Vindhyālaya]] holds the greatest of the passes at its throat from the fortress-city of [[place-suryagarha|Sūryāgarha]], where every bale is weighed on entry and again on exit. The [[place-pssshrines|Pass-Shrines]] are a chain of Varnaka temples kept year-round at the summits by a hereditary line of **Ritūja** ushtakas, who live at altitudes that would kill an unaccustomed lowlander in a week. They bless the caravans and keep the only written record of who crossed and when. Between the customs-house and the shrines, a merchant knows the price of a crossing before he sets out.

## The Drenavar Confusion

Vedyari and Khazri folk-tradition sometimes confuses the Grazian Mountains with the [[place-drenavrspn|Drenavar Spine]] of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], a range half a continent away in the western Aurèldían world. The two share no geology, no culture and no mythology. The Khazryn name for the Western Descent, [[place-wstrndscnt|Dranavár]], is close enough to the Áelendan name for the Drenavar that travelers mistake one for the other in stories, and the two ranges are entirely separate.
