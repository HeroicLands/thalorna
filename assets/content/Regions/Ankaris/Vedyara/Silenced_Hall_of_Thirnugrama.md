The **Silenced Hall** stands in the hill country below the conch-door road, on the way up to [[place-sanghafort|the Sangha-fort]], and it is the whole of what is left of a janapada. **Thirnugrāma**, the village of the bell, refused the conch-door adventurer about 320 AF, and he shut its sabhā in this hall and fired the roof ([[lore-silenthall|the Silenced Hall]]). The Mela of 320 mourned the janapada on the roll, divided its land, and had the doors bricked up.

## What Is There

A temple hall of dressed stone, longer than most janapada halls, roofless since the night it burned. The doors are bricked to the lintel with the Mela's brick, plainer and redder than the hall's stone. The bronze bell that gave the janapada its name, a _thirnu_, still hangs in the gate on its iron bar, green with five centuries of rain. Nobody rings it. The hill villages that took Thirnugrāma's land have let the fields round the hall go back to scrub, and the path to the gate is kept open only by the priests who use it.

## Not a Blood-field

The hall was its own pyre. The sabhā burned with fire, the dead passed, and the ground is not a _shorukshetra_ (blood-field). What burned with them was the janapada's record: its survey, its boundary register and its genealogies, kept in the temple as every janapada keeps them.

That does not make the hall a pleasant place at night. The hill people keep away from it after dark, because a building where a whole assembly died in one fire is a sad and heavy place, and because of the bell.

## Who Comes

- **Priests of the Bhārava circuit** on their way up to a turn at the Sangha-fort, who stop to light a lamp at the bricked door and go on.
- **Descendants of Thirnugrāma's lineages**, scattered through the neighboring janapadas, who come on the anniversary and say the names of their families at the gate.

## Hooks

- **The petition.** A descendant of Thirnugrāma's lineages is preparing a petition to the next Mela, at Sangama, asking that Thirnugrāma be recognized as newly formed. Recognition would undo a four-century division of its land, and the janapadas that hold the land want the petitioner discredited before the assembly meets.
- **The bell.** A priest of the Sangha-fort, coming down late, heard the bell ring once in the night. Nobody else heard it, the bar has not moved, and the bell's clapper was taken out by the Mela's order five centuries ago.
- **The survey.** The petition fails without proof of the janapada's old boundaries, and every copy burned in the hall. A rumor puts a second copy in a temple on the Tānvüri side of the conch-door, where the adventurer came from.
