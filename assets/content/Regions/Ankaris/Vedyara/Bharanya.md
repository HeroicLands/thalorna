**Bharanya** (5,400) stands on the lower reaches of [[place-mahanodi|the Mahanodi]], and it is there for the temple. The great temple, [[place-bhrnytemple|the Mahajaya Temple at Bharanya]], is one of the three largest pilgrim-temples in central [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], and its senior priest is one of the three of [The Council of Three]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}.

The town belongs to a janpoda of the Mahanodi circuit and holds no seat of its own at the [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. What it has instead is the temple's standing, which is greater than the janpoda's and older.

## The Mounds

Nothing here is built on the flood-plain proper. Bharanya sits on four artificial mounds raised over generations, joined by causeways that stand about a man's height above the wet-season water, and the temple sits on the largest and oldest of them. In a bad year the causeways go under and the town becomes four islands for a month, which the townspeople treat as weather rather than as disaster. A pilgrim-guide of the town tells you to come after the fall of the water, when the causeways are dry and the flood-rites are done.

The river has come at the mounds twice within written record and taken neither. It has taken the landing four times, and the landing has been rebuilt four times, twice in a different place.

## What the Town Does

Pilgrims are the trade. There are lodging-houses along both causeways, garland-makers, sellers of lamp-oil and of the coarse red cloth the [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahajaya]] observance uses, and a shrena of boatmen who bring worshippers down from the upstream reaches and take them back. Most of the town's households live off some part of that traffic.

The rest is river work: grain out of the reach behind the town, the ferry across, and the yards that build the flat-bottomed craft the lower river uses. The temple owns the ferry and lets it by the year.
