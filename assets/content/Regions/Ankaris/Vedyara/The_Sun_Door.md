**Suryadvar** is the greatest of the northern passes and the only one whose season is long enough to be called a season. It leaves the terraces beneath the walls of [[place-suryagarha|Suryagarha]], climbs the western flank of [[place-suryashkhr|Suryashikhar]], and twenty days above the fortress reaches the fork where the road divides: north for the southern edge-towns of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]] and **[[lore-celestialroad|The Celestial Road]]** beyond, north-east by the longer branch for the western frontier of [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]]. From the fork it is thirty days to either end.

Three caravans in four that cross the wall in any year cross here.

## The Throat

The kingdom of [[affiliation-shikhalaya|Shikhalaya]] holds the throat, and the fortress sits across the road and not beside it. There is no way onto it that does not pass beneath the walls. Every bale is weighed on entry and again on exit, and the difference between the two weighings is the kingdom's single largest source of revenue and its single largest source of litigation.

A crossing needs a pass-token from the customs-house. The token is cheap, the inspection is not, and the delay between applying for one and receiving one is the instrument by which the crown decides who crosses early in the season and who crosses late—which, on a road that shuts for ten months, is the difference between a profit and a year's wait.

## Who Actually Takes You Over

[[affiliation-osketguides|The Osket]] do, as everywhere on the wall, but on this road they do it under Shikhalayan clan-wardens who hold the warden-right by inheritance and who are answerable, in theory, to the Maharava. A warden of Suryadvar, asked by a caravan master which of them decides, divides the answer in two: "I name the fee. The lineage names the season. You pay one of us and wait for the other." A warden who overrules a lineage on a question of weather does it exactly once.

## The Tantsou Branch

The north-eastern branch has carried a Tantsou community down into Suryagarha for centuries—merchants, a way-station's worth of clerks, and every few decades an imperial envoy who arrives, is received with great courtesy, and goes away again. Neither side has ever seriously tried to rule the other, and both quote that fact rather more often than a fact requires quoting.

## Hooks

- **The frame in the ice.** The thaw has brought a pack-frame with a [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] seal down out of the ice on the col, off the caravan that lost eleven people the season [[being-asvrshmbtn|Asvari Shambatan]] overruled a guide-mother. [[being-ubrn|Ubren]], who carried on that rope, asks the party to bring the frame down to [[place-suryagarha|Suryagarha]] before the Collective's factor reaches it, and to set what is on it beside the manifest the customs-house holds.
- **The date on the token.** A caravan master's pass-token is dated for the last week of the season, which on this road is a year's wait if the weather comes early. He asks the party to get the date moved at the customs-house without his name on the request. The clerk who can move it has not named a price.
- **Nine days without water.** The spring beside [[place-surtatempl|the Surta temple]] froze for [[lore-nineice|the Nine Days of Ice]] two winters ago, the caravans in the pass went without water, and the temple has entered nothing. A Suryagarha house that prices its winter crossings off that water asks the party to go up before the window and learn whether it has frozen again. The priests answer no Shikhalayan on the point.
