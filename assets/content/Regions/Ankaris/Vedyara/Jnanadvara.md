**Jñānadvāra**, the scholars' door, crosses [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]] north-east to the western frontier of [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]]. It is a month and a half of travel and the least dangerous of the eastern crossings, which is a comparison rather than a recommendation.

It carries the goods a caravan can afford to move slowly and the people nobody else will carry: manuscripts, medicines, instruments, the occasional astronomer, and the envoys of both sides. The name is not ironic. More scholarship has crossed the wall by this road than by all the others together, and the [[place-pssshrines|Pass-Shrine]] at its summit keeps the fullest crossing-register on the range because the people who use it are the people who write things down.

## The Way-Station

Beyond the throat, on the Tānvüri side, stands a frontier way-station of the celestial administration. It feeds travelers, houses them for as long as the weather requires, and records each of them in a register that has been kept without a gap for four hundred years. Vedyari visitors are treated with unfailing courtesy and are never, under any circumstances, permitted to see the register.

The [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Empire]]'s official position is that the Vedyari śreṇis are a tributary trade of no administrative consequence. Its frontier officers' practice – the register, the escorts, the standing order that a Vedyari party is met at the col and not before – is the practice of a frontier that is watched.

## The Guides

This is [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] ground. The Hvarn work the eastern crossings as the [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] work the western, are a smaller people, and are on noticeably better terms with the temple-republics than with the kingdom – the [[place-sanghafort|Saṅgha-fort]] two passes east is supplied through Hvarn hands, and everyone on both sides finds it convenient not to discuss what that costs.
