Nīlatīra (12,000) stands where the Nilacharī reaches the eastern sea, three days below the last of [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]]'s valleys. It exists to put cloth on ships. Nothing is woven here and nothing is grown here worth the naming. The bolts come down the river road in covered boats, lie in the yards for a season, and leave in a hull.

The town is one street a mile long with the bale-yards on the seaward side of it and the boat-quays on the landward. Behind the street are the lodging houses, the rope-walk, the chandlers and four temples. The [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Loom-Council]] holds the place through the guilds whose warehouses stand in it, and the master of the yards is a Loom-Master's factor and not a man of the town.

## The Season

The cloth year is the monsoon's. Boats come down through the rains, when the river is high enough to carry a laden hull over the two bars above the town, and the yards fill from the first heavy water to the last. When the rain stops the sea opens and the yards empty, and for eleven weeks the street works at night as well as by day. Out of season the town is quiet and something near a third of its people are away, at sea or up the river with the boats.

## The Seal

A bolt leaves a weaving hall in the city under the lead seal of the guild that wove it, and the seal is the guild's warranty of the dye. The Koshādhyaksha keeps a shed at the head of the street where every bale is opened, the seals counted against the boat's manifest, and the cloth-tax taken in coin before the bale goes to the yard. A broken seal is not a matter for the town. The bolt is held, the boat's master is held with it, and both go back up the river to the Council.

## The Galleys

Most of the cloth goes west and south in [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]]'s galleys. Vyālendra keeps no war-fleet and no deep-water hulls of its own, and the hire of the galleys is argued out between the two cities before every sailing season, on terms nobody has ever managed to fix for more than one year at a time. Tānvüri junks work the eastern run on their own account and take the finest goods direct, paying in silver and in the tin the dyers' mordants need.

## The Deck

A ship's master does not ask to see a wrist. The crews are made up in the lodging houses at the landward end of the street, and a third of the men in them are outcaste, and some number nobody counts are bonded servants who came down the river without a token. The temples take the position that what happens on a deck is outside their province. The Loom-Council takes the position that the cloth has to move. A runaway who reaches the yards is not sent back, and a runaway who is taken on the road above the town is.
