**Himaśilā** lies at the head of [[place-chandrprbh|Chandraprabhava]], on the shelf where the [[place-chandrmahi|Chandramahī]] comes out of the ice under [[place-suryashkhr|Sūryaśikhara]]. It is one piece, about the size of a cart, without a seam or a crack anywhere on it. Its upper face swells and hollows in long true runs, more like the whorl of a conch opened out than like anything built, and there is no ornament on it, no straight edge and no square corner.

## What It Is Like

To the eye it is polished stone: smooth, and a uniform gray with no vein, no figure and no variation anywhere, so that masons who have dressed marble all their lives find the absence of pattern harder to credit than the shape. To the hand it is faintly warm, which is the first surprise, and it gives a little under a thumb, the way soft-tanned hide gives, and comes back. The two do not reconcile, and that is what a pilgrim remembers, and why a fair number will not touch it twice.

Nothing has ever marked it. Not the glacier, which has ground the col flat all round it and parts to either side of it; not the temple's four centuries of washing; not any tool, fire or working anyone has brought up here. Nothing can be taken off it and nothing can be added to it, it cannot be shifted or tipped or dragged an inch from where it lies, and it neither weathers nor dulls nor discolors.

There is nothing written on it. Not an inscription, not a mark, not a sign of any kind – which is the thing a scholar cannot get past, in a country that carves a donor's name on every temple step. The rock shelf around it is cut over with four centuries of pilgrims' names and the temple's own dated dedications, and every one of them stops dead at the slab's edge.

## What It Does

The spring beneath it does not freeze. That is the whole of it: no rite touches it, no procedure affects it, and nobody has ever produced an account of how a slab of anything keeps water running under a glacier.

The [[affiliation-trimurtisampradaya|Trimūrti]] priests hold it the god's footstool and wash it at the turn of every season. The [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] hold it the mountain's own and step round it without touching. Neither has ever offered an explanation of the water, and neither has ever been asked for one by anybody who mattered.

## The Nine Days

Two winters ago the spring iced over, and stayed iced for nine days, which had never happened in the temple's record. Then it ran again.

The slab was unchanged throughout – gray, warm, giving – and is unchanged now. There is nothing to show and nothing to point at, and the temple has entered nothing in the record, on the ground that nine days of weather is not an entry.
