The Jenchai Region is the eastern archipelago of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]—a great volcanic island, a lesser island and their islets, carrying nine mountains and separated from the [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou Region]] mainland by a treacherous strait. The islanders call the land Teyan Jenjai, the Nine Mountains Above the Clouds of Heaven, and Jenjai in everyday speech; the mainland's dismissive name for it is Chaiwong. It is dominated politically by the sovereign [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai Empire]], which the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] has for a thousand years refused to recognize—considering the archipelago a rebellious province rather than an independent state. The islanders return the compliment, viewing themselves as heaven's favored inheritors of the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]] that Tantsou, in their telling, has long since forfeited.

Where the Tantsou Region is a continental civilization of imperial bureaucracy and ordered plains, Jenchai is its mirror-opposite: maritime, feudal, warrior-aristocratic, and shaped at every turn by the discipline of life on steep, narrow islands.

## Geography

The archipelago takes its name—"Nine Mountains"—from its nine volcanic peaks, eight on the great island and one on the lesser island at the strait's mouth. Each peak serves as both geographic center and cultural emblem of the province around it, and each province is named for a feature of its mountain. The great mountains rise straight out of the sea, their shoulders terraced for rice and their summits kept for shrines. Between them, coastal plains are narrow but intensively cultivated; interior valleys hold forests of cedar, pine, and sacred bamboo. The north is harsh and cold, the south mild, and the east faces the open ocean. The Emperor's own mountain, [[place-zojai|Zojai]], stands in the interior of the great island, with the capital [[place-lengwou|Léngwou]] on its lowest slope and the observatory mountain [[place-zijai|Zijai]] beside it.

The strait separating Jenchai from the mainland is the archipelago's single most important defensive feature. Notorious for sudden storms and shifting currents, it has repeatedly wrecked Tantsou invasion fleets and has shaped the islanders' attitude toward the sea as something closer to reverence than mastery. Shrines to the storm-aspect of the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Téngwou Rong Lei]] stand at every significant headland.

Beyond the two main islands, scores of smaller islands and islets stretch out in braided chains toward the open ocean. Many of these lesser islands are uninhabited or only seasonally occupied by fishermen, smugglers, and hermit-priests. The volcanic activity that built the chain is still active: eruptions, hot springs, and periodic earthquakes are part of ordinary Jenchai life and are interpreted theologically as heaven's continuing attention to its favored land.

The climate is temperate with a sharp maritime character: wet summers, mild winters in the south, and harsh winters in the north. Typhoons arrive every summer and are read as divine warnings, tests, or blessings depending on the year and the Great Lord's fortunes.

## Peoples and Culture

The Jenchai are descended from Tantsou stock—their language, script, calendar, and caste categories still echo the mainland—but a millennium of separation has produced a distinct people. They are, on average, harder-edged than mainland Tantsou: a society that lives on narrow terraces between steep mountains and dangerous seas has little patience for administrative abstraction. Physical discipline, personal loyalty, and refined aesthetic practice are the virtues the culture prizes above all.

Jenchai has produced some of [[place-worldthlrn|The World of Thalorna]]'s most exquisite poetry, calligraphy, ceramics, blade-work, tea-craft and garden design, and its retainers study these arts as spiritual practices. The same retainers carry out their lords' raids and reprisals. The Jenchai hold the two together as one discipline, and as the mark of a people that kept the Mandate while the mainland lost it.

The **caste system** inherited from Tantsou still organizes formal society, but it has been bent around feudal reality. The warrior lords dominate completely; the [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]] sorcerers, so central to mainland power, appear here only as subordinate retainers in individual lords' service. Personal names are granted more freely among the retainer classes than on the mainland—a mark of service rather than exceptional achievement.

The Jenchai call every ritual death, ordered or chosen, [Ruising]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the mainland's word for the ordered one; the mainland calls the chosen death [[lore-tseising|Tseising]]. On the islands a Ruising is a means of speech: it can express protest, preserve a secret, demonstrate loyalty to a fallen lord, or simply assert that honor outweighs life. It is taken seriously enough that a lord ordering a subordinate's Ruising stakes his own reputation on the judgment.

## Religion

The islands' faith is a divergent form of Téngwou Rong Lei. The broad pantheon—[[lore-tanzhodty|Tanzho]], the Celestial Dragon, and [[lore-weizhuodty|Wéizhuo]], the Jade Sovereign, Minister of Storms, Minister of the Moon, the [[lore-fvlmntlrds|Five Elemental Lords]]—is recognizable, but interpretation has fragmented along regional and domain lines. Coastal provinces elevate the storm and sea aspects; mountain provinces emphasize earth and metal; island shrines host cults specific to their locality. Each Great Lord claims some portion of heavenly favor for his own line, and the Mandate of Heaven—which in Tantsou is held singularly by the Emperor—has fractured into competing local claims; the Emperor's is the one claim to the whole.

The ceremonial Emperor of Jenchai serves this fragmented theology well: his role is to hold the symbolic center while the Great Lords pursue their regional pieties.

## Politics and Power

Real authority lies with a handful of **Daozhuo**—Great Lords—who rule their provinces as effectively independent fiefdoms, each with its own army, justice, taxes, and foreign policy. Below them sit the **Shuizhuo**, lesser lords who pledge personal allegiance in exchange for land. The **Rongléng Tengzhéi** ("Son of Heaven of Ten Thousand Dawns") sits at the ceremonial apex, distributing honor but commanding nothing.

The current balance rests on three or four roughly equal Daozhuo, none willing to risk open war against a coalition of the others. Every generation or so this balance collapses into open civil war before a new equilibrium emerges; the last collapse was the [War of the Empty Seat]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, 709–712 AF. The office of Teng Ja, the Emperor's sword-arm and the one lord the others obey while it is held, has stood vacant since 709 AF, and the race to fill it drives the politics of the eight domains. See [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]] for the detailed political structure.

## Trade

Jenchai exports the finest blade-work in the eastern world, along with sea-salt, dried fish, lacquer-ware, silk, ceramics, and the refined luxury goods of the Great Lords' workshops. It imports mainland jade, tea, bronze, and silver; and, through the long-distance eastern trade, it reaches the coast of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] and even (more rarely) the ports of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]].

Each Great Lord controls trade within his own domain; the Emperor does not. The result is a patchwork of harbor policies, tolls, and trading-house alliances. Merchant lineages who maintain good relations with multiple Daozhuo are among the wealthiest commoners in the archipelago, though their formal caste status keeps them socially subordinate to even the lowest retainer.

**Naval power** is Jenchai's greatest economic and military asset, described under [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]]. The distinction between merchant ship, warship, and pirate ship is less rigid here than in almost any other region of Thalorna.

## Relations with Neighboring Regions

The **west**, across the strait, is Tantsou—the rejected motherland. Raids along the Tantsou coast are a standing institution of Jenchai policy, and the Great Lords openly claim sovereignty over coastal territories that were once part of their ancestral province. The Empire treats this as piracy and insurrection; Jenchai treats it as the rightful reclamation of lands heaven intended for them.

To the **south**, Jenchai trading ships reach Vedyara and sometimes Zerathian ports, participating in the wider eastern and Vylarian commerce from a safe distance—the archipelago is far enough east that no western power has ever seriously projected military force against it.

To the **north**, the archipelago's outer islands trail off into frozen seas the Jenchai rarely cross. What lies beyond is mostly legend.
