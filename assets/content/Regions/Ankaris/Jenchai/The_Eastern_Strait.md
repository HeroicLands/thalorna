## Overview {#overview}

The Eastern Strait is the water between the mainland of the [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou Region]] and the archipelago of the [[place-jenchairgn|Jenchai Region]]. To the empire it is the eastern strait, the edge of its coast. The islanders treat it as the moat of the Nine Mountains. It is narrow enough that a ship leaves one coast and raises the other within a day or two, and it is the single most important defense Jenchai has. On its southern side it opens onto [[place-surtasamdra|the Eastern Sea]].

## The Water {#the-water}

Sudden storms come off the open sea without the warning the coasts give, and the currents shift between the headlands from one tide to the next. A master who crosses it takes the local pilots' word for the set of the water, and the pilots are Jenchai. Typhoons arrive every summer, and the islanders read each one as a warning, a test or a blessing. Shrines to the storm-aspect of the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Celestial Order]] stand at every headland on the Jenchai side, and a crew that makes the crossing leaves an offering at the first one it passes.

The lesser island and its red mountain, [[place-rinjai|Rinjai]], stand at the mouth of the strait. Every hull that passes between the mainland and the Nine Mountains passes within sight of its coast, and its coves shelter ships from the weather the strait makes. On the great island the western face of [[place-rojai|Rojai]] looks straight across the water.

## The Wrecked Fleets {#the-wrecked-fleets}

The [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] has sent invasion fleets across the strait for a thousand years, and the strait has wrecked every one. The fleets sailed in the best season with the best hulls the empire could build, and the storms and the currents broke them before they landed, or scattered them onto the islands' rocks. The islanders count the loss as heaven's own answer to the empire's claim. The empire counts it as a misfortune of the water and has never abandoned the claim.

## A Watched Crossing {#watched}

The strait is busy and it is watched. [[affiliation-shuiwojaidmn|Shuiwojai Domain]] keeps its fleet on the water between Rojai and the mainland coast, and the raiders of the [[affiliation-rinjaidmn|Rinjai Domain]] lie in the coves at the mouth. Every merchant hull is seen from the first headland to the last, and it crosses on the sufferance of whichever house holds that stretch of water. Those two houses are [The Strait]{.name data-type="affiliation"} bloc.

Trade crosses it all the same. Jenchai sends the finest blades in the eastern world, sea-salt, dried fish, lacquer-ware, silk and ceramics. It takes back mainland jade, tea, bronze and silver. Each side finds the other crude or decadent, and each needs the other's goods. The merchants who sail it pay tolls to the lords who watch it and pay the empire's bureaus on the mainland shore for the same passage back.

The raids cross it too. Jenchai flotillas, mercenary captains and pirates strike the mainland's eastern and southern coasts, seize its offshore islands and fishing grounds and sit off harbor mouths in the rice-shipping season. The empire answers with patrols and with the fleets that the strait keeps wrecking.

## Why Go {#why-go}

A trader crosses for the margin, since jade and silver are worth more to the islands than anything the mainland pays for blades and salt. A captain without a berth finds hire on either shore, from a lord who wants a crew or an official who wants a ship that knows the water. A pilgrim crosses to a headland shrine to make an offering to the storm. A Jenchai lord's agent crosses to buy silver, and the empire's agent crosses to buy a captain.

## Converging Interests {#present}

The Shuiwojai and Rinjai fleets make the strait pay: tolls and ransoms on its water, and plunder from the mainland coast. [[being-henzoshuiwo|Shuiwojai Henzo]] wants to be named Teng Ja. The coastal magistrates want the raids stopped and their captured officers returned. The court of Tantsou wants the strait cleared of the islands' ships and has no fleet that can do it. The pilots want the crossing left alone. Whoever carries cargo, letters or a hostage across the strait meets each of these interests at both ends of the crossing.
