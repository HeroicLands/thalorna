From the pilgrim camps in the valleys at its foot, Mount Shofar is a black cone standing over the flat-topped mesas, darker than the plateau it rises from and bare of anything green above its lowest springs. After sunset a single point of light shows high on its southern face. The camps go quiet when it appears, and the children are lifted up to see it. That light is the [[place-unconsflame|Unconsuming Flame]], and the mountain is the holiest ground the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] have.

## Overview {#overview}

**Mount Shofar** is a black-rock peak at the southern edge of [[place-dkrqrrgn|Diqraqor]], where the plateau comes down in its last long steps to the irrigated belt of the [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]]. It rises north of the eastern oases, three days' ride from [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], and stands some two thousand feet above the mesas round it, so that it can be seen from the belt road on a clear day and from the plateau's rim for days before a rider reaches it. Its slopes and the highland country round it belong to the [Burqalod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the keeper-tribe, and the priestly seat of [[place-beitshofar|Beit-Shofár]] lies at its foot.

Here, the Atarzád griots teach, [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]] revealed himself to the prophet [Yotekhor]{.name data-type="being/npc"} in a flame that burned in the open air on bare stone for three days and nights without fuel and without diminishing. He gave Yotekhor the [Promise of the Tellumel]{.name data-type="lore"}, the land below the mountain promised to the Flame-born, and the [Covenant]{.name data-type="lore/law"} that forbids them a king, for in their teaching Ataros alone is king of the Flame-born.

## The Mountain

The mountain is Diqraqor's own stone: black rock split into plates and tilted, ringing under a hoof, holding the day's heat all night in summer and keeping snow in its shadows for weeks in winter. On the north and west it falls into ravines that run with flash floods after a storm on the plateau, and the upland tribes keep their wells in them. On the south it comes down in broken steps to a ring of valleys where water that sank into the plateau comes out again as springs. Those valleys hold Beit-Shofár, the Burqalod villages, their cisterns and the pilgrim camps.

A stair climbs from Beit-Shofár to the Flame, a half-day's walk for a strong pilgrim and a full day for the old. Partway up it passes a shelf on the slope where the tomb of Yotekhor stands, the second-holiest site of the Atarzád; he died there without entering the land he had been promised. Above the tomb the stair grows steep and the rock grows bare, and it ends on the shelf where the Flame burns. The summit above that is broken rock, and nobody climbs to it.

## The Mountain and the Plateau

The mountain is where the Atarzád hold on Diqraqor begins. Burqalod herds graze the highland round it, and from there Atarzád flocks go up onto the plateau's southern slopes, where the upland tribes dispute the grazing and the line between them runs from well to well across the mountain's northern ravines. Those tribes are poor and hostile to both of the southern peoples, and they raid Atarzád herds more readily than anything else on the plateau. Trouble on the mountain is as likely to come from them as from a Tellumi rising. At festival time the Burqalod warrior society, the [Tulqenekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, rides the pilgrim tracks between the belt and the mountain.

## The Pilgrimage

Three times a year, at the spring equinox, at midsummer and at the autumn equinox, the Atarzád climb toward the mountain for [The Festival of the Promise]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. Every adult is expected to make the pilgrimage at least once before death, and many make it every year. They come up from the belt by the tracks from Kethramír and the eastern valleys, tribe by tribe, and camp in the valleys round Beit-Shofár.

The summer festival draws upward of fifty thousand pilgrims, more than the valleys can water, and the Burqalod ration the springs and cisterns by tribe for the length of it. The pilgrims climb the stair in an order the Flame's keepers set, file past the tomb, and gather at the Flame to hear the Covenant recited in full. Each family carries a lamp up and brings it down lit. At the spring and autumn festivals the camps are smaller, and the stair can be climbed in a morning without waiting.

## What the Tellumi Say

To the Tellumi whose cities the Promise took, the mountain is where their dispossession was declared holy. They do not climb it. In the quarters of the held cities the light on the mountain is not spoken of, and a Tellumi child who points at it is told to look at the ground.

## Converging Interests {#present}

When the summer festival crowds the mountain, the Burqalod ration the springs by tribe, and the upland tribes of the northern ravines dispute the grazing with Atarzád flocks from well to well and raid them when they can. The pilgrims bring the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]]'s own quarrel up the mountain with them. The Toqel on the shelf is about eighty and has said nothing of [Aqeshat]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Prophet, whose following grows, and with word of a union rising on the steppe the Council of the Twelve, which sits at Beit-Shofár, may soon need his answer on a Zhudek.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The festival water.** A dry spring has left the valleys short, and the midsummer pilgrims are coming. A Burqalod elder will pay a party to bring water up from the belt by a route the upland tribes do not watch.
- **The straggler.** A pilgrim family's son left the stair at the tomb and has not come down. The keepers on the stair say he went over the shoulder of the mountain toward the northern ravines, where the upland tribes keep their wells.
- **The surveyor.** A scholar from [[place-caldar|Kaldar]] wants the height of the mountain measured from the belt, and needs a party to carry the instruments onto the plateau's rim without offending the Burqalod.

:::secret {title="Who keeps the pilgrim tracks"}
The Burqalod pay an upland tribe to keep its riders off the pilgrim tracks at festival time, in horses and salt, every year. The tribe keeps no covenant with Ataros, and the Council of the Twelve has never been told; a Burqalod elder who let the compact become known would be repudiated by his own tribe. In a year when the payment is short, stragglers on the tracks disappear.
:::
