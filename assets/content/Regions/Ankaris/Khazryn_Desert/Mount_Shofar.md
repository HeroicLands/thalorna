## Overview

Mount Shōfar is a black-rock peak at the southern edge of the Stone Uplands, where the dark plateau runs down to meet the irrigated belt of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]]. It rises north of the eastern oases, a few days' ride from Kethramír, and its flanks are the ground the Ātárzád hold outside their four cities. To the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Ātárzád]] it is the holiest ground in the world. Here, their griots teach, Ātáröš revealed himself to the prophet **Mūshárā** in a flame that burned in the open air on bare stone for three days and nights without fuel and without diminishing, and gave him **the Promise of the Tellumel**: the land below, promised to the Flame-born.

## The Flame and the Tomb

The **Unconsuming Flame** still burns on the mountain, and it is the first holy site of the Ātárzád. Below it, on the slope, is the tomb of Mūshárā, the second; he died there without ever entering the land he had been promised. At the mountain's foot lies the priestly seat of [[place-beitshofar|Beit-Shōfár]], where the Council of the Twelve convenes each year, and the slopes and the highland country around them are held by the **Khorshad**, the keeper-tribe, from whom the Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame is always chosen. The covenant forbids the Ātárzád a king, and it was at Mount Shōfar that Mūshárā forbade it: Ātáröš alone is king of the Flame-born.

## The Pilgrimage

Three times a year—at the spring equinox, at midsummer and at the autumn equinox—the Ātárzád climb toward the mountain for **the Festival of the Promise**. Every adult is expected to make the pilgrimage at least once before death, and many make it every year. The summer festival draws upward of fifty thousand pilgrims, who camp in the valleys around the mountain and gather at the Flame to hear the Covenant recited in full. To the Tellumi whose cities the Promise took, the mountain is where their dispossession was declared holy.
