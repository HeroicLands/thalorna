A street of merchants' houses on the eastern edge of [[place-wilud|Vulud]] ends at a square tower of baked brick four stories high. A sand-front opened the street four years ago, a drift half filled its lower end the season after, and this season the wind has scoured the drift out again, so that the tower's doorway stands clear to its sill. The [[affiliation-rwlstrb|Uruvolur]] who found it call it **Bolidqiiso**, "the price-tower", because its lower floors are a counting-house of the [[lore-towercities|Tower League]], with its balances still standing, and because they have already sold the news of it twice.

## What It Is

The tower is not one of the city's light-towers. It is a counting-house built to the height of one, where a caravan yard's goods were weighed, sealed and held under the league's mark until the string that owned them left. It stands on the old gravel shelf, so that its upper floors are clear of the sand and its ground floor is half choked. The street side has a shaded doorway with a lintel of black stone. The lee side, away from the wind, opens through a low service door onto a walled court whose high walls have kept the dunes out of it, the one piece of open ground in the street where a party can wait out a storm.

Inside, the floors are:

- **The shaded entrance**, a long vaulted hall where the only cool air in the street gathers, and where [[being-bnscrpn|bone scorpions]] nest in the hollow walls.
- **The inner stair**, a broad stone stair to the upper floors.
- **The counting floor**, a long room of benches and bronze balances, where goods were weighed and sealed. Bronze weights still sit in the pans, and broken seals of dark wax lie in the dust where the league's clerks broke them when an account was closed. Storage niches line the end walls, each the height of a bale, with a stone block standing beside it to close it.
- **The weighing hall**, two stories high, under a great beam-balance hung from the roof, its two pans as wide as a cart. Its counterweights, stones the size of a cart, hang on chains in shafts that run down through the floor into the vaults below, and the floor over the vaults is cracked.
- **The strongroom**, behind the weighing hall, a small dry room of shelves whose door-frame still carries the league's seals.
- **The lamp-stair**, a narrow stair in the wall that climbs past the counting-house to the roof, where a small lamp-chamber once marked the tower for the yard. A [[being-numek|numaq]] colony roosts in it and pours out at dusk.

:::secret {title="The vaults and the counterweights"}
The vaults under the weighing hall are full of sand that has come up from below, and the cracked flags are the only thing holding the floor; a bar or a dropped weight breaks them, and whatever stands on them goes down into the sand to the chest. Each counterweight hangs from the drum by an iron pin. Knocking a pin out drops the weight down its shaft, and the falling weight hauls the opposite pan of the great balance to the roof.
:::

## The Watchman

A stone figure stands beside the shaded doorway, or at the foot of the inner stair, and the crews who have been inside have found it in both places. It is man-high and broad, with its hands held out before it, palms up. Its face, its shoulders and the signs cut over its body are worn almost smooth by two thousand years of sand. Its hands are polished bright. It is a [[lore-golemcrtr|construct]], set by the league to keep the counting-house, and the Uruvolur call it **Sovidiqraq**, "the stone escort".

:::secret {title="The polished palms"}
The palms are polished by seals. For as long as the house was worked, every porter who carried a load out laid its seal in the figure's open hands as he passed, and it lowered its hands and let him by. Its hands are held out for a seal, not for a throat.
:::

The league's writing has never been read, and the signs on the watchman can be read only as pictures. The walls of the counting floor and the weighing hall carry panels of the same pictures: a merchant holding up a seal to a stone figure that lowers its hands; a figure laid in the pan of a balance while the stone figure stands by; the service door with blocks set across it and a sky full of blown sand.

## What Folk Say

- **The Uruvolur** say the figure moves only while nobody is looking at it, until the house is closed; then it moves in plain sight. They say its head turns to follow fresh footprints in the dust, and it goes where the footprints go.
- **They say** it closes the house when the wind turns to storm, by setting blocks across the service door, and that it counts whoever is inside as an intruder. It presses an intruder flat between its palms, or lays him in the balance, or files him in a storage niche as goods are filed.
- **They say** it does not cross a threshold out of the counting-house: not the service door, not the lamp-stair, and not the shaded entrance once the sand has filled it.
- **The market at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]]** says a whole seal of the league is a pass, and that the figure does not trouble anyone who holds one up to it. A collector there pays more for a whole seal than for a broken one on the strength of it.
- **The tellers who have seen the palms close** say the signs that bind it are cut into its palms, which is why they are not worn away, and that a construct whose signs are broken stops.
- **Uruvolun** tells the street at the fire at Qiiso as the street she sold twice. Her tale has a counting-house door at the end of the street, which the Tantsou collector's men reached first, and has them carry out seals and weights of the counting-house while she waited at the street. The Uruvolur say Bolidqiiso is that door, and that the Tantsou's men were the first into the house in two thousand years. In the tale both buyers went home content.

## Treasure

The strongroom shelves hold engraved trade seals in neat rows, each whole and each worth a great deal to a collector, along with tower-lamp glass packed in straw: lenses and panes of the clear glass the league ground for its lights, uncracked. The bronze weights on the counting floor are the league's own, and a collector pays for those too.

:::secret {title="The silver under the flags"}
The strongroom floor rings hollow because the league's silver lies beneath its flags, in ingots stamped with the same mark as the seals. On the same shelf as the seals stands a crock of the league's dark sealing wax, which softens at a lamp flame and takes a seal's impression as cleanly as it did when the house was worked.
:::

## Reaching It

The street lies on the eastern side of the city, open for a few years at most before the next sand-front closes it. Uruvolun, a guide of the Uruvolur who has sold Vulud's streets before, has sold the news of the cleared doorway to two buyers at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] this season, a Bizarian factor and a Tantsou collector, and guides whichever of them arrives first.

# The Truth of Bolidqiiso {#truth .secret}

**What the watchman keeps.** Sovidiqraq keeps the house's goods, not its door. The league set it to hold every load weighed and entered here until the string that owned it came for it, and it holds them still: the bronze weights in the counting-floor balances, the lamp-glass in its straw, the engraved seals on the strongroom shelves and the silver under the strongroom flags. No owner has come in two thousand years. It takes no notice of anyone who carries nothing of the house's, and it hunts whatever of the house's is carried.

**The seal.** A load leaves a league house lawfully under a whole seal: a disk of the league's dark wax pressed with an engraved trade seal and fixed to the load. Sovidiqraq lets a sealed load go, and lets its carrier go with it, because a sealed load is a load released to its owner's man. A whole seal covers the one load it is fixed to or held against, and nothing else:

- a man who holds up a whole seal and carries nothing is a sealed load himself, and it passes him;
- a man who holds up a whole seal with a sack of weights on his back carries a second load, unsealed, and it comes for the sack;
- a broken seal is a closed account and covers nothing.

The engraved seals on the strongroom shelves still press an impression it honors, and the crock of wax beside them takes one. A party that finds both can write a bill for every load it means to carry.

**While the house is open.** In fair weather Sovidiqraq stands at the shaded doorway with its hands out for the seals of departing loads. It moves only while nobody is looking at it, and it moves only for the house's goods. It reads the dust: the deep prints of a man carrying weight are the ones it follows, and the shallow prints of the empty-handed it crosses without turning. Goods it finds set down out of their place it carries back and lays in the nearest balance pan. A man who fights it for a load it presses flat between its palms, and it leaves his body and his load together for the closing.

**When the house is closed.** When the wind turns to storm it walks to the foot of the inner stair and spends the afternoon closing the house, and its last act is to set blocks across the service door. From then until the wind drops it moves in plain sight, and it goes about the house in a clerk's order:

1. **The thieves.** It goes first to whoever carries the house's goods without a whole seal over them, alive or dead. It lays the thief in one pan of the great balance and what he took in the other, pins the beam level as a clerk pins a balance when a weight is entered, and stands by. A thief who lies still is held as surety for his theft until an owner claims both. A thief who climbs out of the pan it presses back down between its palms, and it goes on pressing until he stops climbing.
2. **The loose goods.** Whatever of the house's lies out of its place it gathers into the counting-floor pans.
3. **The stock.** Then it turns to anyone still inside who carries nothing and holds no whole seal. In a closed house an unsealed thing is stock, and it files him in a storage niche and sets the niche's block across him, as bales were filed. It does not unfile what it has filed.

When the wind drops it lifts the blocks from the service door and goes back to the doorway.

**Thresholds.** The tellings are right that it crosses no threshold out of the house. The lamp-stair belonged to the light and not to the counting-house, and it stops at the lamp-stair door. It stops at the service door, at the hatch of the strongroom's goods-chute to the court, and at the shaded entrance once the sand has filled it. A thief who gets out of the house with his theft is out of its charge.

**The binding.** The signs that bind it are cut into its palms, which are polished and not worn. Break them with a chisel or a bar and it stops where it stands with its hands still open. Buried in the vaults or hauled to the roof in the great pan, it is still bound, and it works its way back to its doorway within a season.

**Four years ago.** Bolidqiiso's street is the street of Uruvolun's tale. Her second sale of the news went to the Tantsou collector at Qiiso, whose men reached the street first and forced the counting-house door, the first to open it in two thousand years. On the counting floor they found the last loads the house still held for their owners, caskets of bronze weights each under a whole seal, and carried them out on a still day past the figure at the doorway. Sovidiqraq let them go, because every load they carried was sealed. They broke the seals at their camp and sold seals and weights at Qiiso, and they went home content without knowing why nothing touched them. The tale is true as told. Every sealed load in the house went out with them, and everything left in it is unsealed.

**Who is inside this season.** The Bizarian factor's crew of three came in two days before the storm.

- **The partner.** The wind rose at dusk on their first day and fell by midnight, and the house closed with the crew inside. One man had a sack of weights on his back, and he lies dead in a pan of the great balance with the sack in the other pan and the beam pinned level; he climbed out, and the watchman pressed him back down.
- **Kesukenod**, a Sovidur hired hand, had pried a whole seal from the strongroom door-frame and held it up, and the watchman passed him by. He dropped the seal to drag his partner off the pan, and an unsealed man with nothing in his hands in a closed house is stock. He is filed in a niche off the counting floor, alive, his chest pressed so that he cannot breathe deeply.
- **The hunter**, a Bizarian with the factor's brand on his wrist, sat out the closing on the lamp-stair. At dawn he came down, found his partners gone and filled his pack with weights from the counting-floor balances. The watchman came while his back was turned, and he struck at it with his crowbar. It pressed him flat beside the bench, and his pack lies with him for the next closing.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The footprints.** Its head does follow footprints, and it follows the deepest. The prints of the empty-handed do not draw it.
- **The seal as a pass.** A whole seal covers the man who holds it and nothing he carries besides. Qiiso's collectors pay for whole seals on the strength of a half-truth.
- **Intruders.** It counts no one as an intruder. It counts thieves, whom it weighs, and stock, which it files.
- **Uruvolun's tale.** Every word of it holds. Uruvolun took nothing out of the house four years ago, and she has never known why the Tantsou's men came out whole.

> [!TIP]
> Present the watchman as a clerk, never as a hunter. It does not hurry, it does not look at people, and it never goes out of its way for anyone who is carrying nothing. Describe where its head is turned every time it appears, and let the players notice that it is turned toward their packs.

> [!WARNING]
> The panels answer the question they seem to leave open. The figure in the pan is weighed against what he took, and a GM who describes the great balance with one pan empty spends the clue that the thief's theft lies in the other.
