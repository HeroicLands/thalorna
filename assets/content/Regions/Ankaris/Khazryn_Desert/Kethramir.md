## Overview

**Kethramír** is the fourth of the seven Tellumi cities and the door of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] to the north. The southern spur of the long caravan road comes down out of the Stone Uplands and ends here, and every string that wants the belt's dates, books and horses, or the guides who take light goods over the Grazian passes, arrives at its gates. About twenty-six thousand people live in the city, and in the weeks when the spring caravans are in, several thousand more sleep in its yards. The **Zarvān**, the scholar-tribe of the Ātárzád, took the city in the eighth year of the war and hold it with a light hand.

## Water

Three deep wells give the city its standing on the road: they have never failed, in a belt where springs do. Two stand in the central yard, a stone-lipped pair sunk through the rock to an aquifer that the first Tellumi tapped, and the third is in the Tellumi quarter, where the Mōbads bless it on the first day of each month. Around them the city has grown in courtyards of thick-walled adobe with narrow windows, built to hold cool air through the afternoon. A fourth source, a seep at the foot of the foothills outside the walls, feeds a small shrine to Zúráthrä and the garden of the shrine's keeper, [[being-zevaradthvrn|Zevârad Dathvarûn]].

## Trade and Life

Kethramír is a market because it sits where two kinds of traders must meet. A caravan from the interior has bought safe passage tribe by tribe along the spur, and it arrives with silk, spices, tea and steel, tired and poorer for the road; a belt merchant has dates, palm-sugar, salt, good horses and the books of the old quarter. The two sides meet in the central yard in rows of hired courtyards, with guides, interpreters, brokers and money-changers between them. Prices are argued for days, in at least three tongues.

The city's rule of hospitality is stricter than the law. A merchant who cheats or kills in Kethramír finds that the news travels along every string within days, and no guide will lead his caravan. The tribe enforces the rule because it brings the trade, and the guides' company enforces it because it brings their living. The well-keepers and the guide-masters have more say in the city's daily life than the Zarvān elders, who govern from a college of law at the edge of the yards.

## Under the Ātárzád

The Zarvān keep the scriptural archive and the law-codes of the Ātárzád, and they rule Kethramír like scholars: by precedent, patiently, and with a preference for rulings over soldiers. The garrison is small. The Tellumi quarter around the third well has its own Mōbad and its own courts, under the eye of a Zarvān magistrate who reads the Tellumi tablets and sometimes agrees with them.

The old **House of Kethramír**, the Tellumi priest-house, lives in exile in Amradad, led by Yázdín, the author of the commentary the Ātárzád have banned. A copy of her work passes through Kethramír in every caravan from the west, hidden in a bale of cloth, and a Zarvān clerk who finds one has to decide whether it is a crime or a book. Families from the old quarter, such as the merchant house of the Parnâzar, still trade in the minerals and stones that come down from the uplands, and the best of them have learned to deal with both peoples.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The book.** A Zarvān scholar wants a copy of the banned commentary, to answer it. A Mōbad in the Tellumi quarter wants the scholar's answer, to answer back. The party can carry both, and be paid by each for not telling the other.
- **The cheat.** A caravan master accused of fraud is held in the yards until the guides' company decides his case. Whoever he cheated is dead, and the other guides do not agree on who. A party hired to find the truth discovers that the company's ruling will bind the road for a season.
- **The spur.** The interior road beyond the city is the least safe stretch of the trade. A string leaving Kethramír with spices has to pay a toll to every tribe it passes, and the tribes know when the strings leave. The riders who sell guides and escorts here will tell a party which tribe is not honoring its own passage this year, for a price.
- **The wells.** If the third well turns brackish in a dry year, the Tellumi quarter has no other source, and the Zarvān magistrate has the power to share the other two or to keep them.
