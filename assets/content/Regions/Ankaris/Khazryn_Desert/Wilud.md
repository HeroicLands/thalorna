## Overview

From a day away, Wilud looks like a stand of broken teeth: nine tower tops, the highest of them forty feet of cracked brick, standing out of a sea of pale dunes in a country with no other thing taller than a camel. That is all of it that shows. The rest of a city of perhaps ten thousand lies under the sand, which moves a few yards in a year toward the north-east and has been moving for longer than anyone has counted.

Wilud is one of the [[lore-towercities|Tower Cities]], a lamp-lit way-town of the league that carried the first road across the waste. It sits in the sand country the tribes call the Hosikor, the sand range, a long march from any well that is not guarded. The league's engineers sited it on a gravel shelf above the sea of dunes, which was sound in their time. Its well failed first, the people left, and the dunes came over a shelf nobody was holding.

## The Dunes Give Back

The same wind that buried Wilud uncovers it. Every generation or so a sand-front passes over a quarter of the city and leaves a street of roofless houses open to the sky, with its walls whole to head height, its doorways clear and its cellars choked with fine pale sand. In a few years the next front covers it again. The tribes of the Hosikor say the sand lends the city to whoever it chooses and calls the loan in without notice, and a street that opens is a short lease.

## Danger

The danger is the sand itself. A newly opened street is a trench cut in a slope of loose dune, and a party digging at a doorway can bring the face down on its heads. The towers lean, and the highest has dropped a course of brick every few years. In the shade of the towers, where the only cool in the whole country gathers, Bone Scorpions and Giant Camel Spiders nest in the hollow walls and hunt the same gaps after dark.

Wilud is not a blood land. The city was emptied by a failing well, not by slaughter, and the dead it holds died in their beds.

## Treasure

What a street holds depends on the season: shop-stock of a dozen peoples, weights and seals of the league's counting-houses, lamp glass from a fallen tower, a household's silver in a jar under a hearth. Nothing that was worth carrying was left, but a party opening a house nobody has entered in two thousand years finds what the last family did not think of.

## A Hook

The Ruweles, the hidden-water bands who guide the Hosikor, are the first to learn when a street opens. They sell the news to two buyers at once, usually a Byzarian factor and a Tānvüri collector, and then guide whichever of them arrives first. A party that finds a fresh street unguided is a competitor, and the Ruweles, who know where everyone in the Hosikor is, sell its position to both buyers.
