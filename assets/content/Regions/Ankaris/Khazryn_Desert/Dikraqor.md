The spur leaves the main road at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] and heads south, and on the fourth day the sand gives way to a ground that rings under the hooves. Black rock, split into plates and tilted, climbs in long steps toward a skyline of flat-topped mesas. There are no wells on the plateau's surface. The water lies in the ravines, sometimes two hundred feet down a crack in the stone, and every crack belongs to someone who is watching you from the rim.

**Diqraqor**, "the stone range" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]], is the broad plateau that lies between [[place-hskrrgn|Khoshiqor]] and the [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], and runs down to the belt's northern edge. The main road does not touch it; the southern spur crosses it. It is the way the Tellumi cities reach the rest of the world, and the way the rest of the world reaches them.

## Land and Water

The plateau is high, dry and broken. Mesas and buttes stand a thousand feet above the plain, and deep ravines cut between them, some half a mile wide and some narrow enough to jump. In winter snow lies in the shadows for weeks. In summer the stone holds the heat all night. Water comes from rock cisterns cut into the ravine walls, from seeps in the ravine floors, and in the south from streams that run off [[place-graznmntns|the Grazian Mountains]] and sink into the plateau before they reach the belt.

[[place-mountshofar|Mount Shofar]] rises at the southern edge, a black-rock peak where the uplands meet the belt, and the holiest ground the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] have.

## The People

The tribes of Diqraqor are poor, because the land is poor, and hostile to both of the southern peoples and to strangers in general. They herd goats, sheep and a small hardy horse, and keep their wells behind walls of stacked stone. They hire out as escorts on the southern spur and to caravans bound for [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Amradad]], because a tribe that guards a string is a tribe that does not raid it. They also raid the outer gardens of the Tellumel and the herds of the Atarzád, and the feud between the two is the most active on the plateau.

In the south, the Atarzád graze their flocks on the uplands' southern slopes and consider the grazing theirs. The tribes do not agree, and the line between them runs along wells.

## Dangers

Falls, floods and collapse take more lives here than raiders. A ravine floor that is dry at noon can run with a flash flood from a storm forty miles away. [[being-gntscrpn|Giant scorpions]] hunt the warm rocks at dusk, and [[being-crglrkr|crag lurkers]] wait on the ledges above the narrow places.

In the ravines of the northern plateau, rock-cut tombs open into the cliffs, [[place-osikor|Oshiqor]], "the bone range", the burial ground of a people who vanished long before the tribes. Their galleries are painted and furnished with grave-goods, and in the deepest a [[being-damut|damut]] walks. The Atarzád hold them unclean and forbid approach.

## Hooks

- An Atarzád elder's son has been robbing the tombs for two years, and his father has learned of it from a trader who bought a painted jar.
- A tribe on the spur offers to guide a Tellumi house in exile to a hidden cistern, and the house cannot tell whether the price is the cistern or the house.
- A Tantsou scholar wants a rubbing from the painted tomb walls and will pay for a guide the Atarzád cannot forbid.
