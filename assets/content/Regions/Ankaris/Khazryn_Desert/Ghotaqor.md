The ground changes first. A hundred miles north of the last steppe well the grass thins to moss, spruce stands in the hollows, and the first frozen bog takes a horse to the hock. Beyond that the forest does not stop until it meets the ice, and the people who live in it have never heard of the road.

**Ghotaqor**, "the forest range" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]], is the northern third of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]]. It is the same cold-temperate forest that covers upper [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]], all of [[place-targud|Targud]] and much of [[place-malagna|Malagna]], and from the Velanthian side it looks like a continuation of the same woods with fewer people in them. About 60,000 live in it, over a land the size of a kingdom.

## Land and Water

Ghotaqor is spruce, larch and birch on sandy ridges, bog and standing water in every low place, and rivers that run slow, brown and cold from the first frost in autumn to the thaw in late spring. Snow lies for half the year. The northern edge is a low, broken coast of ice-scoured rock, where the sea freezes a mile out in winter and the seals come up through cracks in it. Water is never scarce here; safe ground is. The bogs stay soft until the new year even in a hard winter, and a party that trusts the white surface in autumn sinks with its pack animals.

## The People

The bands are small, fifty to two hundred people, and they are unorganized in the sense that matters to a settled lord: no band has a chief who can bind another, no tribute is paid, and no war is fought except over a particular lake or a particular insult. On the coast the bands take seals at their breathing holes and fish through the ice, and live in turf houses dug into the lee of the rocks. Inland they follow the deer herds on foot and on sleds, and they come to the coast in summer to trade meat and hides for salt.

Their furs go south. Sable, marten and ermine reach the market-camp at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] on the backs of Sovidur traders who go north each summer, and from there they travel on to [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión]]. The bands will take a stranger in who arrives hungry and half-frozen, because refusing him is bad luck, and will not take him in twice.

## The Summer Trade {#present}

The bands answer to nobody and take no part in the road, and their furs go south each summer with the Sovidur traders to Qiiso. The cut ridge and the hollow where the snow does not lie draw scholars from Velanthia and traders from Targud, who pay for any word of them.

The fur trade is the forest's one tie to the south, and this season it is fraying. A fur trader of [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] who went north two summers ago has not come back, and his backers do not know whether he is dead or has found something. A band on the coast has begun to bring ivory and worked amber to the summer trade, more than any seal-hunter could have found, and it will not say where they come from. Each winter the men of the [[affiliation-ghtkrtrb|Ghotakur]], from the steppe side of the forest's edge, go north through the forest to mend a wall there, and last winter three of them did not come home.

## Dangers

The cold is the first. The wolves and the great forest cats are the second, and the ground itself is the third. Travelers from the south underestimate how far a loaded sled can go in a day, and how many days a bog can hold a thaw.

The hunters speak of places they avoid: a ridge where the stone has been cut into walls and passages no band of theirs made, a hollow in the forest where the snow does not lie. Travelers say the walls are halls of the [[lore-khazariclt|Khazari]] or works of [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]]. The bands say only that the cut stone was there before them and that no one comes back from the passages the same.

## Hooks

- A scholar in Velanthia pays well for a map of the cut ridge, and the only person who has seen it is a deer hunter's widow who will guide no one.
- A fur trader from Qiiso has not returned from the north in two summers, and his backers want to know whether he is dead or has found something.
- A band on the coast has begun to bring ivory and worked amber to trade that no seal-hunter could have found, and will not say where it comes from.
