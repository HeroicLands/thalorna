## Overview

**Caldar** is a city of about twelve thousand in the foothills on the southern edge of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], between [[place-zargandur|Zargandûr]] and [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], where a copper-bearing spur of the Grazian foothills runs down into the belt. It is a later city than the seven. A scholars' college was founded there in the centuries after the seven cities, and the town grew around the college, which is why the Tellumi record of the seven does not count it. The Ātárzád took it in the war without a siege, because the college would not close its gates to either side, and they hold it now. It is known across the scholarly world for one institution, the **University of Caldar**, whose research faculty in alchemy has made the study of mineral transmutation, the stable conversion of base metals and common stones into substances of greater rarity and value, its specialty.

## Water and Ore

The city drinks from adits driven into the copper spur, which drain a cold, hard, faintly green water into stone cisterns at the foot of the hill. The Tellumi built a system of settling-basins to clean it for drinking, and the University depends on it twice, since the same water carries the minerals the faculty works. Below the settling basins the runoff goes to the gardens, and the date palms on the lower terraces have a bluish tinge to their fronds that the garden-keepers treat as normal. The mines themselves are old, deep and shared among a dozen workings, and the water in the oldest of them is not fit to drink.

## Trade and Life

Caldar sells ore, copper, verdigris and the faculty's products to the belt and to the Amradad market, and buys food from the neighboring oases. Wealthy mineral merchants from all over the belt fund research at the University in exchange for first option on any transmutation with a commercial use, and the merchant quarter, with its assay-houses and refiners, is the busiest part of town. The rest of Caldar is students: lodging-houses, stationers, glassblowers, instrument-makers and cheap eating-houses. Visiting scholars arrive from Amradad, Vedyara and the Haradian ports, by the Descent road and across the passes, and stay for years.

## The University

The renowned **Master Aldrin Kovoss** trained [[being-kayvondzrd|Kayvonad Zarid]] there from the age of fifteen, and Kayvonad's thesis on the heat underlying copper-to-silver transmutation was hailed as groundbreaking. The faculty is also a place of long quarrels. When **Dârvazad Kesvarûn** published a paper showing mathematical errors in Kayvonad's foundational work and Kayvonad answered with accusations of plagiarism, the scandal divided the department. Kesvarûn is now the University's favored elder statesman of alchemical research; Kayvonad keeps the title of Senior Researcher and little else, and a faction of younger scholars, **the Cabal of Progressive Theorists**, openly calls his methods outdated.

## Under the Ātárzád

The Ātárzád hold the city, and the University belongs to nobody. Its faculty includes Tellumi, Ātárzád and foreign scholars, and the Council of the Twelve has left it alone because it earns money and because Ātárzád sons study there. Tellumi professors may teach, and Tellumi students may attend, but they may not hold land in the city, and the water-tax falls on their households as it does elsewhere. The practical rule among the faculty is that a quarrel in the lecture-hall is an academic quarrel, and a quarrel in the street is a matter for the Zarvān magistrate.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The resurrection of Aldrin Kovoss.** Kayvonad claims his dead master achieved alchemical immortality, and he needs a party to find out where the old man was last seen. Kesvarûn would pay more to see the claim buried.
- **The Cabal.** The younger faction wants Kayvonad's notebooks, which hold the heat figures his thesis rests on. They will bribe, burgle or recruit to get them.
- **The runoff.** The water that runs off the refineries is not what it was when the Tellumi built the basins. A child's illness in the lower terraces is a symptom, and a physician from Zargandûr who traces it to the University finds the University's patrons unwilling to hear.
- **The old workings.** The deep mines under the spur break into passages that were not dug by any miner on the University's rolls. Students who go down looking for ore come back with stories.
