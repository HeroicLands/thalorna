## Overview

At the northern end of [[place-velkunar|Veshkun]], where the sand has been shovelled back from a gate-court of fitted stone, the settlers of [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]] keep a door. It is a double leaf of cedar, black with age and bound in bronze, hauled out of a buried passage in the first seasons of the resettlement and hung in the one arch of the old city that still has two standing piers. The Tellumi call the place **Veshkunvartun**, "the gate of the lost one". The settlers call it the Barred Gate, and they bar it before the last light has left the western wall.

Behind the door is the gate-court: a paved square sixty paces across, a guard-bench along each side, a well-head with no well under it, and a breach in the north wall where the sand lies banked to the height of a man. Beyond the door there is only the open rim, and the road north has been dead for as long as the town has had a memory of it. Nothing about the gate is hard to find. It is the first thing a traveler from [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]] meets, and the settlement's only road to the outside passes under it.

## The Knocking

Every night, between full dark and the first grey, something knocks at the outer face of the door. The knock is three blows spaced like a man striking with the butt of a spear, and then a pause long enough for a sentence, and then three more. Through the cedar a voice speaks, low and hoarse as if from a mouth full of dust. It says the words every cloth-teller of the Tellumi knows: _I am the gate of the house of the rim. I stand when the water flees. Open the gate._

The gate is not opened. This is the whole of the settlement's defense, and it has held since the first night the door was rehung. Three times the door has been opened in the dark: once by a warden who took the voice for a lost traveler, and twice by settlers who answered it. Each time those who went out were found in the grey hour walking north into the sand in single file, eyes open and saying nothing, and not one turned when called. Two were carried back. They sat in their houses without speaking until they died.

The knocking leaves marks. In the morning the outer face of the cedar is scored with fresh dents in two places only, at the height of a man's chest and at the height of a boy's.

## What the People Say

- **The warden's account.** The settlers who keep the gate say that the knocker is the champion of [[lore-velkunsong|The Song of the Lost Town]], [Berniray]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, come back to wake the house. They say it with the flat patience of people who have said it many times and are tired of being the only ones who believe it.
- **The Shekhul's account.** The priest of the settlement holds that it is not the champion. Berniray lived, served the [House of Kethramír]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and died in his bed with his name read. What stands at the gate has the oath in its mouth and nothing behind it. It is a thing that learned words from the dead and wears them, and the Shekhul calls it by the Tellumi word for what it is, the **Gate-dead**, _Vartunnilvur_.
- **The rival houses' account.** The exiled houses in the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] say the whole matter is a story the [House of Vahurdash]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} tells to keep strangers from the cellars, and one of them has paid a man to find out.
- **The caravan yards' account.** Cloth-tellers in Kethramír add a verse to the song when the audience can be trusted with it. The verse says that the keepers of the record-house are still keeping, and that they have not been told they may stop.

## The Dead Behind It

The Gate-dead is not the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow's whole work]], but it is the Shadow's work. The dead of the record-house, the keepers who would not leave their cellar when the city emptied, died without a name read over them and have lain for five centuries unreceived. They were few, and a few dead do not tear the world. What they did was leave a gap, an oath in a mouth with nobody to take it back, and the Shadow came in by the gap, as it does wherever a rite is owed and unpaid. The undead of every land are the same corruption wearing a local face. This one wears the face of a man who kept an oath past sense.

It cannot enter a barred door. It can be answered. That is the danger of the gate and the reason the town does not sleep deeply.

## Approaches

The gate is entered from the north, over the open rim, or from the town side by any of the settlement's lanes. Under the gate-court the cellars of the old city run in every direction, and the breach in the north wall opens into them: a stair of tilted slabs going down into the dark, which is how the champion came in on the night of the song. The settlers have walled the breach on the town side and set a bar across the stair, because the camel spiders of the vaults come up by it.

## Who Pays for the Truth

[Khorsád-Vahur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, head of the House of Vahurdash, wants the knocking ended, since a gate that cannot be opened at night costs the settlement every caravan that reaches the rim after dusk. He will not say so to the exiled houses and he will not say so to the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]]. A rival house in Amradad would pay a good deal more to learn that the gate cannot be ended, that the town is failing and that its tablets can be had.
