## Overview

The Vurinkarvem cliff over [[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]] is riddled with cut chambers and pilgrim stairs, and some of them are older than the city. At the bottom of one of the oldest, a gallery of seven bays below the level of the lower basin, a well-head of grey stone stands in a round chamber. The Tellumi of the quarter call it **Peshdenderat**, "the dry well". It has no water in it.

It is a deep shaft. A weighted line paid out from the lip runs for a hundred and forty fathoms without touching, which is more than three times the depth at which the great spring's water stands. The wall of the shaft is cut stone for the first forty fathoms, then rough rock, then something the plumb-lines come up wet and cold from, a damp that is not water. At the lip, in the dark of the moon, the stones whisper.

## The Voice

A drop of blood from the hand, let fall on the lip, is answered. The voice comes up the shaft, dry as a husk, and asks what is wanted. It has been known to call the party by name before it is told. It will give a secret for a secret, or a secret for blood, a thimbleful for a sentence. It knows the water-hours of every household, the names of the Tēqemōd informers in the quarter, where the clay of the burned archive lies and the hour the great fire-warden of the city sleeps. It has told a man the day he would die. It does not lie. It tells what it is asked and nothing it is not.

What it asks in return grows. A drop becomes a measure, a measure a cup, a cup a body drained. The voice is thin and polite and does not seem to want anything. The Tellumi who know of it say that it is not the dead and not a spirit. It is the **Tally**, and what it counts is what a city owes.

## The Sorcerer-Priest

The quarter's secret is that the Tally has a servant. A Shēkhulsīghot of the old rite comes to the well, the hidden one the Tēqemōd have hunted for ninety years. The hidden office has passed through three hands in that time, and he is the third. He is **Nurmelul**, a lean grey man of sixty whose hands are scarred to the wrist. He holds the old observances in a back room of the lower town and teaches in secret, and he believes he is keeping the Tellumi alive. Every secret that has kept him hidden has come from the well, and every secret has been paid for in blood: his own at first, then the blood of volunteers from the quarter, who give it for the cause.

The well takes more than it was given. The oasis is drying, slowly, from the edges in. The outer channels have lost a hand's breadth of flow, the date groves at the fringe of the town have thinned, and the great spring itself stands lower at the basin's edge than the old men remember. The water-masters have reset the channel-hours twice and do not know why.

## What the City Says

- **The water-masters.** The keepers of the channel ledgers see the numbers and say nothing. They suspect the well and they suspect a priest.
- **The Tēqemōd fire-wardens.** The garrison believes the quarter is poisoning its own water to blame the Flame. They search the lower town for the hidden Shēkhulsīghot and find his students.
- **The quarter's gate-keepers.** The water-master who holds the key to the courtyard gate keeps a ledger of the households that have given blood, each marked with a drop, and cuts their channel-hours when the oasis runs short. She does not say who the households are.
- **The pilgrims.** Visitors to the cliff shrines are told the lower galleries are flooded and closed. They are not, and the guides who say so keep the stair.
- **The exile house.** The House of Shirvan at Amradad pays a courier for the quarter's news and has begun to ask how the quarter knows so much.

## Reaching It

The gallery lies below the level of the lower basin, at the foot of an old pilgrim stair that begins in a courtyard of the Tellumi quarter. After rain, the lower bays flood in the time it takes to climb out of them. The approach is watched by the quarter's water-masters, who say the way is shut for repairs.
