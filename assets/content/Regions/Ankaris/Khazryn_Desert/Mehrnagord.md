## Overview

**Mehrnāgord** is the sixth of the seven Tellumi cities in the order of their founding, at the western end of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], where the last foothills of the Grazian Mountains come down to the belt and the road over the Western Descent ends. About thirty thousand people live in the city, and some one hundred and ten thousand more in the gardens, salt-works and villages of the oasis country round it. It is the largest of the three Tellumi cities still ruled by their own house, and the nearest Tellumi city to the lands of the west.

## Water

The foothills above the city are cut by dry watercourses that carry snowmelt for a few weeks in spring. The Tellumi built the oasis by catching that flood: stone terraces and check-dams hold it on the hill, and a web of buried channels leads what soaks through to springs at the city's foot. The channels need annual clearing before the melt, a task that falls to every household in the oasis by turns and has been recorded on tablets for as long as the tablets exist. The springs feed the gardens, the date groves and a pool beside the walls where the whole city washes on festival days. Water is steadier here than in the east of the belt, because the range stands directly at the oasis's back, and the Dunhari tribes who winter on the far side of the Descent envy it.

## Trade and Life

Mehrnāgord lives on the road and on salt. The salt pans lie on a dry lake-bed three hours west of the walls, and from them comes the white and the grey salt that is sold across the Descent into Vedyara's northwestern marches and west as far as the Dunhari oases. Caravans from the west reach Mehrnāgord without a guide, for the Descent is the one stretch of the range that needs none, and they leave with salt, dates, palm-sugar and the hard cheese of the oasis herds. The remainder of the economy is the traditional oasis life: gardens, groves, date wine for the Sultanate's merchants, and the spare cash of caravans resting between the sand and the sea.

The city is plain and old. Its walls stand on the foundations of the original city, rebuilt by every generation since, and the courtyard houses inside are of the same adobe and palm-beam as the first Tellumi built. A family counts its grandfathers' wells and its great-grandfathers' graves.

## The House and the Ātárzád

The ruling prince, **Pārván of Mehrnāgord**, is in his sixties, cautious, and inclined to accommodation with the Sultanate of Amradad and, warily, with the Ātárzád who hold the four lost cities. His household guard of about a hundred men is the largest of the three free houses', and it is no army. The Mihrān, the Ātárzád tribe of the western belt, sit at the gates of Shirvan a few days east and could take the city in a season. They do not, because they find a free Tellumi city more useful than a conquered one: its prince is a channel to the Sultanate, and its salt reaches markets the Ātárzád cannot.

Ātárzád merchants have sounded Pārván out about a trade compact. The exile houses regard the idea as treason, and the oasis is full of people who say so loudly, and as many who do not.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The compact.** Pārván wants an envoy he can trust to carry an offer to Amradad and a reply back, and the exile houses want to be sure he does not. A party could carry both messages and be paid by both.
- **The lean season.** When winter is hard in Dunhara the tribes come east over the Descent, in numbers and hungry. The oasis has seen it before, and Pārván's men are not enough to turn them. A party on the road in that season finds out how much of its safety depends on a purchase made last autumn.
- **The ancestral peaks.** The older genealogies root the houses in named peaks above the Descent, and a family with a dispute over precedence would pay to have a peak climbed and a claim proved. The upper slopes are cold, steep and unmapped.
- **The pans.** The salt-lake is a white glare in summer, and the crust over the brine will bear a camel in the morning and not at noon. Workers lost to it are not recovered.
