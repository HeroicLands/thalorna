## Overview

**Vahúrdash** is the fifth of the seven Tellumi cities and the poorest. It stands on the dry northern rim of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], where the snowmelt streams of the belt fail one after another and the Stone Uplands begin, and about four thousand people live in the new town, with perhaps sixteen thousand more on the irrigated strips along its channels. A drought in the deep past emptied the old city, and it stayed empty for generations. In the last generation Tellumi families from the free oases and the subject quarters of the conquered cities came back to settle beside its stones. The old city, **Old Vahúrdash**, lies round and under the new one, and the town's life is conducted with it in view.

## Water

Everything in Vahúrdash depends on the water that has been found again. The settlers' first seasons were spent with copies of the old city plans held in the archives of the other cities, the only record of where the first city drew its water. They traced the lines of the buried channels and reopened them one stretch at a time, and found two of the old wells still sweet. The town now drinks from those wells and irrigates from a single channel, cut and recut across the sand, that carries a thin flow from a spring higher up the rim. In a dry year the channel stops. The town measures its harvest by how many days the water ran, and families are known by which stretch of the channel they keep.

## Trade and Life

Vahúrdash sells what the rim grows, which is little: dates from the first replanted groves, olives and figs from the shaded terraces, and goats and their cheese. It buys everything else from Kethramír and the western belt. The town stands where the belt meets the uplands, so it is the place where the Tellumi and the herders of the Stone Uplands do business: herders bring wool, hides and horses to the edge of the settled ground, and the town's merchants trade them cloth, dates and iron for them. The herders belong to their own tribes. Each owns a handful of small oases in the uplands and treats the northern fields of the town as a neighbor's, to be respected or not depending on the season.

The town is built of the reclaimed stone of the old city, and the streets are narrow and straight, laid out to the old plan by people who copied it from a tablet. Everyone works. A child old enough to carry water carries it.

## The House

The **House of Vahúrdash** was revived with the settlement. Its head, **Khoršád-Vahūr**, holds the title by recent acclamation of the settlers, not by ancient right, and sits on the Council of the Princes as a kind of seventh among equals. The house keeps a token guard and no more. Its strength is its claim: a city given back to the desert and taken back from it, the one Tellumi city ever recovered. The Ātárzád of the central belt tolerate the settlement because it costs them nothing, pays a quiet tribute in dates, and holds the poorest ground on the rim.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The title.** Khoršád-Vahūr's rivals say an acclamation cannot make a prince. The tablets of the old city's own archive would prove the house's descent, and nobody has reached them. The house will pay a party to find them, and a rival will pay the same party to leave them lost.
- **The channel.** If the spring on the rim weakens, the town has weeks to find another source. A party with a water-diviner, a mapmaker or a map of the old channels is worth a great deal here.
- **The neighbors.** The herders' small oases lie close to the town's northern fields, and a settler who waters a goat at the wrong spring meets its owners. A Vahúrdash elder wants someone to carry gifts to the nearest tribe before the next dispute turns into a feud.
- **The edge of the sand.** North of the last field there is no road. The rim runs into broken ground and dry gullies, and the people of the town cross it only by day and in company.
