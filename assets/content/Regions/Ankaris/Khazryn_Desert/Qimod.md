## Overview

The walls are the first thing, because they are not broken. A wall of mud-brick twenty feet high runs round a town of perhaps three hundred houses, with a gate that stands open and a gatehouse with its roof on. The lanes inside are swept by the wind and nothing else. On the market square the stalls are up: poles, awnings, a weighing-beam hanging from its hook. It looks like a town that has stepped out for the afternoon, and it has looked like that for a very long time.

**Qìmod**, "the silent" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sowides]], stands on the southern spur four days out of [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qìso]], where the track to the [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] dips through a gap in the black rock. A well-head in the square still holds sweet water, and the tribe that keeps the well will draw it for a price, by rope, from outside the gate.

## How It Was Made

About 548 AF the third of the great unions was raised, and its _orqwen_ rode south down the spur in the week of the autumn market. Qìmod was full: Tellumi horse-dealers, Vedyari spice factors, a Byzarian wine-agent, Tānvüri silk-brokers, a hundred Sowides herdsmen come to sell. The gate stood open for the fair. By nightfall everyone in the walls was dead, and the host rode on at dawn with the stock and left the people where they lay in the square, because a town's dead are not the riders' dead and the riders had a road to take.

Each of the dead belonged to a people with a rite of its own, and none of those rites was performed. The Tellumi had no Mōbad, the Vedyari no conch, the Tānvüri no ancestor-shrine, and the Sowides among them were not laid out and not named. A great many people died at once without any one of the ways home.

## What It Is Like

Qìmod is a silence, and it is thin.

A traveler notices it at the gate. Wind goes through the lanes and makes no sound. A dropped coin falls without ringing. Voices go flat and small within ten paces, and a man shouting to a companion at the far side of the square sees his lips move and hears them as through felt. No bird nests in the walls. No insect crosses the sand within a bowshot of them, and the dung of passing camels lies unvisited by flies.

No fire stays lit. A lamp wick catches and the flame stands, then lies down and goes out. A tinder spark dies in the pan. The cold and the dark come at the same moment.

The hunters come into that, the [[being-yiqnotud|Yiqnotud]] of the steppe tribes. On any night of the year, shapes half made of the town's dead and half of the things that feed on them cross into the lanes and take hold of whoever sleeps within the walls. They cannot be heard coming. The first a man knows of them is a hand at his throat and the silence of his own cry. The Tellumi factors who have tried the town have been found in their blankets in the morning, mouths open, with nothing on them but a cold that did not belong to the season.

## How the Spur Lives with It

- **The track goes round.** The spur bends a half-day east of the gap, and the detour crosses poor ground that the tribes charge for.
- **The well is sold from outside.** The Shirzâri, a raiding tribe of the southern steppe, hold the well and draw it from beyond the gate. A caravan buys its water from them at the gap's mouth and is escorted past the town in daylight.
- **The Shirzâri profit from the fear.** They have never put out a warning that was not for sale, and a rider of theirs who meets a party headed for the gate asks first whether anyone is paying.
- **Nobody sleeps within sight of the walls.** Tellumi caravans make the stage beyond the gap even when the beasts are spent.

## What Is Still There

Everything. The counting-house on the square has a barred door and a stone vault below, and the vault was not opened. The shops stand stocked: brass lamps, bolts of cloth, sealed jars. The Shirzâri take nothing from the walls, because nothing they take has ever stayed theirs. The dead lie where they fell, in the square, in rows under a thin cover of drifted sand.

## Hooks

- **The vault.** The counting-house vault held the fair's deposits: silver in bags, with a clerk's tally sealed in wax. A Tellumi house in exile at Amradad believes it holds the dowry of a daughter and wants the tally, not the silver. The vault door has not been tried since the sack.
- **The well's owner.** A rival tribe has offered a party a share of the Shirzâri's tolls if it can draw the town's water from inside the walls, by day, and carry the proof out. The Shirzâri's right to charge rests on nobody having done it.
- **The market day.** The riders of the spur say that on the fair's anniversary the stalls in the square are heard, faintly, haggling, from outside the gate. A priest of one of the faiths that lost people in the fair wants to hold a market there with a priest from each of the others, and thinks that a day of every rite performed at once might hold the silence back. The Shirzâri want to know what it costs them.
