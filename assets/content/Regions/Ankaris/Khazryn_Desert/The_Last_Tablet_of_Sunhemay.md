## Overview {#overview}

The last tablet of Sunhemay is the tablet of clay on which the last prince of old [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]] pressed a line with his thumbnail, if the better of two tellings is true. It lies, by that telling, in the record-house of [[place-velkunar|Veshkun]], the town under the sand, with the keepers of the record who would not leave their cellar. Nobody has found it, and nobody knows what it says.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

Nobody living. The record-house lies under the sand of Veshkun with its clay and its keepers, and the only way down is through the old city's cellars. The cellar stair opens by the breach in the north wall of the gate-court of [[place-velkunvartun|Veshkunvartun]], where something knocks at the barred gate every night. The settlers of the [[affiliation-hsevahurdash|House of Vahurdash]] have walled the breach on the town side and set a bar across the stair.

## Its Story {#its-story}

[[lore-velkunsong|The Song of the Lost Town]] tells it. Sunhemay, prince of the town on the rim, refused a stranger the guest-right and counted his debts while the springs left the city one by one. When the town went out by the east door, the keepers of the record stayed in their cellar, and the sand came in over them. Of Sunhemay the tellers sing two endings. In one he came out with the rest and lived, a poor man in a conquered street. In the other he sat with the lamp in his hall until the oil was gone, reading what he was owed, and on the last tablet he pressed a line with his thumbnail. The cloth-tellers sing the second, and the song ends with the town a sand-filled plan and the prince's line under a walker's feet, unread.

## What It Does {#what-it-does}

It is a word, if it is anything. The Tellumi keep their names, debts and treaties on clay, and a prince's last line is the last entry in the account of the town he lost. Whoever reads it first decides what the town's last word was, and every party with a claim to the clay of Veshkun knows it.

## Who Wants It {#who-wants-it}

- **[[being-khorsadvahur|Khorsád-Vahur]]**, head of the House of Vahurdash, wants the clay of the old archive, and the prince's tablet with it, read by his own house first.
- **The exiled houses in [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|the Kingdom of Amradad]]** say the knocking at the gate is a story the House of Vahurdash tells to keep strangers from the cellars, and want the tablets before that house can choose what they say.
- **The cloth-tellers of [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]]** want nothing from it but the ending, and the one who brings back what the line says has a verse no teller has sung.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- The way down to the archive runs under the gate the town bars every night, and the House of Vahurdash pays to have the knocking ended ([[scenario-knockvelkun|The Voice at the Barred Gate]]).
- A rival house in Amradad pays a party to bring out the prince's tablet unread and sealed, and pays more if the House of Vahurdash never learns it was found.

:::secret {title="What the line says"}
The tablet is there, laid on a desk in a room without a door, the last in the archive. The line pressed into its edge by a thumbnail is one word in the old hand, _Paid_. It reads two ways. The Tellumi read it as the prince's debt to the woman at his gate, settled by the boy's half-skin of water. The [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] scholars read it as the city's debt to the prince, settled by the sand. The clay does not decide between them.
:::
