## Overview

The **Oasis of Shirvan** is the first of the seven Tellumi cities and the holiest, in the western half of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], where the Grazian foothills close in on the belt and the **Mōrávar cliff** stands over the town as a wall of pale rock. About sixty thousand people live inside its rebuilt walls, the largest city of the belt, and perhaps as many again work the gardens and canal villages that fan out below it. Tellumi tradition holds that [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] revealed the order of the world to the desert peoples here, in the first age, and the Tellumi Confederation still names Shirvan as its seat though its princes have not entered the city in ninety years.

## The Spring

You smell the city before you see its walls: wet stone, crushed mint, smoke. The great spring breaks from the foot of the Mōrávar cliff in a basin the size of a market square, and everything in Shirvan is arranged by how far it stands from that water. From the basin the flow divides among stone channels that run along the streets, under the houses and out through the walls into the date groves, each channel with a name, a keeper and a set hour. A household in the old quarter owns the right to a few hours of water in a given channel on a given day, and the right passes by deed. The ledgers of those deeds are the most valuable documents in the city, and the Tellumi water-masters who keep them know which of them the Ātárzád tax assessors have never seen.

When the Ātárzád took Shirvan they built a fire-temple on the basin's edge and kept an offering flame burning over the spring. The Tellumi do not drink at that side of the basin, and they have not said why to any Ātárzád official in ninety years.

## Trade and Life

Shirvan trades on pilgrimage and on dates. Pilgrims of both faiths come for the spring, the Ātárzád to the fire-temple and the Tellumi to a basin they may use only on the days their quarter is given. Their money feeds the inns of the lower town, the candle-makers, the guides who run groups to the cliff-face shrines, and the stalls that sell small clay flasks of spring water. Caravans from Kethramír come in to buy dates, palm-sugar and the white sandstone dressed at the cliff-quarries, and leave with iron, glass and cloth. The date groves below the walls are the largest in the belt, and the harvest brings every hand in the city out to the orchards for a month.

## Under the Ātárzád

The city belongs to the **Mihrān**, the Ātárzád tribe that holds the western end of the belt and the strictest of the twelve on matters of observance. Their fire-wardens walk the streets, and their rule is felt in small ways: a Tellumi shop may not open on an Ātárzád festival, a Tellumi household may not light a fire in its courtyard on certain nights, and the water-tax is assessed at the basin gate, where the assessor can see who comes to draw.

The Tellumi live in the older districts around the lower basin, in lanes too narrow for a settler's cart. They run the canals, the clinics, the counting-houses and the school that the Mihrān have never closed because the Mihrān sons attend it. Their Mōbads hold no temple. They hear cases in back rooms, and the quarter obeys them.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The Hidden Mōbad.** The Tellumi of the quarter swear that one Mōbad keeps the old observances somewhere near the spring, and the Mihrān fire-wardens have searched for him for ninety years. A party hired by the exiled House of Shirvan could be asked to carry something to him, or to find out whether he exists. A party hired by the Mihrān could be asked for the opposite, and both employers pay well.
- **The sunken archive.** The great temple's archive burned in the fall of the city, but the clay that survived fire was buried in the rubble under the lower basin. Tablets surface when a channel is dug out, and the water-masters take them away before the Ātárzád overseers notice. A tablet from the treaties between the seven cities would be worth a great deal at Amradad.
- **The cliff.** The Mōrávar cliff is riddled with cut chambers and pilgrim stairs, some of them older than the city. After rain the lower galleries flood, and a flood can fill a chamber in the time it takes to climb out of it.
- **Informers.** Every Tellumi household in the lower town is known to the Mihrān. Someone in the quarter reports, and nobody knows who.
