## Overview

**Zargandûr** is the third of the seven Tellumi cities, in the heart of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], and it was the war-city of the Tellumi: the seat of their strongest cavalry and the first city the Ātárzád took, in the second year of the war. About forty-five thousand people live inside its walls today. The **Bāhrām**, the largest and most martial of the twelve Ātárzád tribes, hold it as their garrison city, and their herds graze the pastures to the north. The walls are higher than any in the belt, because the Ātárzád rebuilt them after the war to keep the Tellumi in as well as other people out.

## Water

A snowmelt river comes down from the Grazian glaciers in the south and reaches the city through a weir-and-canal system that the Tellumi engineers built to feed the horse-pastures first and the town second. The river arrives in spring, brown and cold, and by the end of summer it is a trickle that the city shares by schedule. The weirs are the city's hardest-defended points, and a Bāhrām picket sits at every one. Whoever controls the weirs controls the horses, the grain and the date harvest, and the Tellumi water-engineers who keep them are watched more closely than any other group in the city.

## Trade and Life

Zargandûr lives on war and horses. The Bāhrām breed the long-legged desert horse on the northern pasture, and the horse-fair at the end of spring draws buyers from Dunhara and the Descent. Armorers, saddle-makers and bowyers fill the streets near the garrison, and the city exports blades and harness to the other oasis towns and the Amradad market. Everyone else sells to the soldiers: bakers, wine-sellers, scribes, fixers. Grain comes from the irrigated plain around the city, and a long season of dry weather is felt first at the garrison's bread.

Nights in the city are lit. The Tellumi quarter keeps lamps burning at every door until dawn, and a chandler's trade is one of the steadier ones in the quarter. The practice is old and nobody explains it to outsiders.

## Under the Ātárzád

The Tellumi quarter is a poor district in the shadow of the walls. The Breaking took the city's princes and officers, and the people left are craftsmen, smiths who work under Bāhrām overseers, and the families of men who died in the siege. The old war-house of Zargandûr survives in exile, and its heir, Khávar, trains in Amradad for a return he has set no date for. In the quarter, young men take a particular look when a Bāhrām patrol passes, and the patrols know the look.

The Bāhrām rule by garrison. They post watchers at the gates, the weirs and the three bridges, and a Tellumi caught carrying a blade after dark is beaten and fined. A prefect of the tribe sits in the old palace and hears complaints on feast days. The Ātárzád choose their **Dūkhen** from Bāhrām more often than from any other tribe, and the city is proud of that.

## The Field Outside the Walls

Outside the northern wall, past the last irrigated plot, lies the **Henhulpedin**, the blood field of the Breaking, which nobody crosses at night.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The reclamation plan.** Khávar needs to know how many men the Bāhrām keep at each gate. A party that can walk through Zargandûr and count would be paid in Amradad, and caught in Zargandûr would be treated as a spy.
- **The horse-fair.** Buyers from three lands meet at the fair, and so do the horse thieves of the uplands. A herd driven off in the dust of the sales is hard to recover, and a Bāhrām patrol on the road wants someone to blame.
- **The weirs.** In a dry year the Bāhrām cut the Tellumi plots off first. A Tellumi water-engineer would pay a stranger to open a sluice at the right hour, and the penalty for opening one is hanging.
- **Garrison brawls.** The streets near the garrison turn violent when the soldiers drink, and a stranger with a sword at his hip is a natural target.
