## Overview

**Ushtra-bēr** is the seventh of the seven Tellumi cities, the last founded, at the far eastern end of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], where the belt narrows against the foot of the Eastern Reach. About twenty thousand people live in the city, and perhaps seventy thousand more in the terraced villages of its valley. It is the farthest of the seven from the Western Descent where the Tellumi first came down, and the farthest from the Ātárzád heartland round Shirvan, which is why it is still free.

## Water

A glacier-fed stream, cold and gray with rock flour, drops out of the Eastern Reach into a high valley and runs past the city on its way to the belt. The Tellumi built it into a stair of terraces: a long ladder of walled fields, each watered from the one above, rising a thousand feet up the valley. The terraces grow barley, apricots and walnuts in addition to the date palms of the lower fields, because the cold winters kill dates above a certain height. In summer the stream runs high and loud enough to be heard from the walls, and in a warm year it floods. The city keeps watchmen on the upper terraces, and a horn from the heights sends the valley to its roofs.

## Trade and Life

The city lives by what the valley grows and by the trade of the east. The highland guides of the Eastern Reach, the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]], come down in autumn to buy grain and barley for the winter in their villages, and they sell wool, butter and worked horn. Caravans bound for the pass customs of the Tānvüri frontier, a long way east across the stone desert, pause here to rest the animals and take on a guide who knows which of the nomad tribes of the stone desert hold which wells. The caravans are few. Those that come stay a week, and pay in silver.

Ushtra-bēr is austere. The houses are of dressed stone, not adobe, with shuttered windows against the wind, and the streets are swept every dawn. The shrine to Zúráthrä at the head of the valley is the plainest in the belt, a spring in a stone ring with a single lamp. People rise early and eat sparingly, and a feast is a plain meal at an unusual hour.

## The Court

The ruler, **Vāhrām of Ushtra-bēr**, is fifty, hard-faced and exact, and his court preserves ceremonial forms that have been forgotten elsewhere in the belt. A petitioner kneels at a marked place on the floor and speaks only after the Keeper of the Tablets has read the petition aloud, and every decision is recorded on a clay tablet before the petitioner leaves. A stranger who presents himself as he would at an exile court in Amradad is made to repeat the whole ceremony. A visitor who learns the forms before going finds the court helpful, because the house has no other form of hospitality.

The house keeps a household guard of about fifty men, enough for ordinary security and ceremony. Like every Tellumi house, it maintains no army. The Ātárzád tribe that holds the eastern oases, the Zarvān, have never pressed it. The road to Ushtra-bēr is long, the valley is easy to hold, and the Zarvān scholars find the court's archive more interesting than its land.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The old forms.** The court's tablet archive holds ritual and legal practice that the rest of the belt has lost, and a scholar from Amradad or Caldar wants copies. Vāhrām allows access to those who learn the forms and earn his respect, and refuses everyone else.
- **The flood.** If the glacier melts fast in a hot summer, the stream rises in hours and the terraces below the city go first. A party in the valley when the horn sounds has a short time to reach the walls.
- **The pass-guide.** A caravan master heading east needs a guide who can read the wells of the stone desert, and the best one in the city has a feud with a family in the Hvarn villages. A party could settle it or be caught in it.
- **The winter.** Snow closes the valley road from late autumn to the thaw. A party that arrives late stays until spring, in a city that rations its fuel by household.
