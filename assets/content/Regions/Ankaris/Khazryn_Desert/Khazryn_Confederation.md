# terran_analog: "Canaanites of the post-Conquest period—indigenous urban-pastoral oasis polity under ancient hereditary princely houses (some still ruling on the margins, some in continuous exile)"
---

The **Tellumi Confederation** is what survives of an oasis civilization older than any city of the steppe: seven walled cities at the great springs along the north foot of the Grazian Mountains, ruled for most of three thousand years by hereditary princes and kept by a priesthood that wrote everything down. About ninety years ago the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Ātárzád]] took four of the seven. The Tellumi have lived since then in unresolved blood-feud with the people who hold their choicest ground, as a house in exile, a ruling prince on a lesser oasis, or a subject in a city their grandparents ruled.

The Tellumi call their country **Tellumel**, and by that name they mean only the belt: the narrow band of springs, canals and gardens between the mountains and the dry uplands. The waste north of it is the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]], and most of it was never theirs. The Confederation holds three oases of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] and claims all seven cities.

There are perhaps eight hundred thousand Tellumi. About two hundred and fifty thousand live in the three oases still under their own princes: some one hundred and forty thousand at Mehrnāgord with its gardens, ninety thousand at Ushtra-bēr, and twenty thousand around the revived settlement at Vahúrdash. About five hundred and fifty thousand live under Ātárzád rule in the four conquered cities and the irrigated districts around them, as a subject class of merchants, craftsmen, water-engineers, scribes, physicians and palm-grove tenders. The four princely houses of the lost cities are in continuous exile, mostly in [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Amradad]], where they keep their genealogies, their household priests, their court ceremonial and their explicit claim to the thrones from which their forebears were driven. The exile courts number a few hundred souls each and are scrupulous about continuity: a Tellumi prince in Amradad can recite the names of every ancestor back to the founding of his line.

The Ātárzád hold that the Tellumi are the people whose oases the Sacred Flame promised to the Flame-born, and that the Promise has been fulfilled. The Tellumi hold that the Ātárzád are trespassers whose theology cannot create a fact that history did not establish, and that no god may give one people the land of another.

## Origins: The Ancient Settlement

Tellumi tradition holds that the first Tellumi came down from the [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] by the **Western Descent**, the last three hundred miles of the range, where the wall breaks into brown foothills above the oasis-belt. They came in the deep first age, one lineage after another, and settled the great springs at the foot of the foothills as their families spread. The older genealogies still root each house in a named peak above the Descent, and most of those peaks no living member of the house has seen. The seven cities they built stand on one another: of the original walls only foundations remain, buried beneath the rebuilt walls of every generation since.

In order of founding, the seven are **Shirvan** (the holiest and the first, built at the great spring under the Mōrávar cliff), then [[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]], [[place-zargandur|Zargandûr]], [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], [[place-vahurdash|Vahúrdash]], [[place-mehrnagord|Mehrnāgord]] and [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-bēr]]. They lie in a single line along the belt, from Mehrnāgord at its western end by the salt pans of the Descent to Ushtra-bēr at its eastern end under the Eastern Reach. No city of the seven stands anywhere else. A later city, [[place-caldar|Caldar]], rose in the belt round a scholars' college and is not counted among them.

Four of the seven are Ātárzád-held: the [[place-oasishirvn|Oasis of Shirvan]], Ashkarad, Zargandûr and Kethramír. Mehrnāgord and Ushtra-bēr remain under their own princes. Vahúrdash, on the dry northern rim of the belt where the streams fail, was given back to the desert by a drought in the deep past and has been resettled within the last generation beside the stones of **Old Vahúrdash**.

The civilization that flourished before the Breaking was urban, literate and polytheistic, and old enough to have absorbed and outlasted several waves of nomadic incursion. Its scribes kept their records on baked-clay tablets in the Tellumi tongue, written in a syllabary brought down from the mountains, and the archive runs nearly two thousand years back from the Breaking without a gap. The Mōbads of the Great Temple of Āhúrdáén at Shirvan maintained the calendar, the records of dynastic succession, the treaties between the seven cities, and the commentary tradition that gave Āsháian theology much of its shape outside the Sultanate. Most of this archive burned when Shirvan fell. What survived was carried out by the fleeing princely households and is scattered across the exile courts in Amradad.

## The Breaking

The Ātárzád call the events the Conquest and the War of the Twelve Years. The Tellumi call them **Turem**, the Breaking, and the emphasis in their account is sharply different:

- The Ātárzád arrived as refugees. The Tellumi cities received them with the hospitality the desert extends to every wanderer in extremity, and settled them on marginal land at the edge of the belt under princely patronage.
- The newcomers waited until they were strong enough, then turned on their hosts in a religious war they had been planning from the start. It ended about ninety years ago, three generations back.
- The conquest was brutal: Tellumi temples demolished and their stones reused for Ātárzád fire-temples, public worship of Āhúrdáén proscribed, the Mōbads who refused to flee slaughtered, and sacrificial fires built directly over the holy water at Shirvan.
- The war was lost as much by decline as by Ātárzád skill. The seven-city confederation had weakened for generations, the princely houses were deep in faction, and the Sultanate of Amradad, which should have intervened, was absorbed in its own succession crisis at the decisive moment.
- The Promise the Ātárzád cite is a legal fiction. Even Āhúrdáén, supreme in the pantheon, would not violate ancestral right. The Sacred Flame is a real god and the keeper of fire, honored in his place, but he is not a sovereign who can grant kingdoms.

The Breaking is recited at every Tellumi festival, at every formal court ceremonial and at the funeral of every senior member of a princely house. It is the wound round which the surviving Tellumi identity is organized.

## Government

The Confederation is governed by the **Council of the Princes**, which meets annually at Amradad on neutral ground, hosted by the Sultanate as a courtesy to the exile community. Each of the seven princely houses sends its head (or, for the four in exile, the man or woman who would rule if the house were restored), the senior elders of the house, and the senior Mōbad attached to its court. The High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad presides as neutral convenor, a role the Sultanate's then-Mōbad took up about sixty years ago, when he agreed to underwrite the Council in perpetuity.

The seven princely houses are:

- **House of Shirvan** (in exile in Amradad). The senior house, first among the seven; by long custom the eldest Shirvan prince speaks first at every Council. The heir-in-exile is **Mōrá-Shirván**, a woman of perhaps forty, scholar and theologian, who has spent her life building a legal and historical case for the recovery of Shirvan. The case fills a small library and is updated continuously.
- **House of Ashkarad** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional merchant-house, with the most cosmopolitan court before the Breaking. Its head, **Sāmīm of Ashkarad**, grew wealthy on Vylarian Sea trade and quietly funds Tellumi agitation in the Ātárzád cities.
- **House of Zargandûr** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional war-house, which held the strongest military before the Breaking and lost the most leaders in it. Its heir, **Khávar of Zargandûr**, is twenty-seven, trains as a soldier in the Sultanate's forces and is widely understood to be preparing for a reclamation campaign.
- **House of Kethramír** (in exile in Amradad). The traditional priest-house, closest to the Mōbadate and the most theologically serious of the exile houses. Its head, **Yázdín of Kethramír**, is herself a Mōbad of considerable reputation and has written major commentaries on the Breaking.
- **House of Mehrnāgord** (still ruling). Holds Mehrnāgord at the western end of the belt, a city of thirty thousand in an oasis country of about one hundred and forty thousand. The ruling prince, **Pārván of Mehrnāgord**, is in his sixties, cautious, and inclined to accommodation with the Sultanate and, warily, with the Ātárzád. The exile houses consider him insufficiently militant.
- **House of Ushtra-bēr** (still ruling). Holds Ushtra-bēr at the eastern end of the belt, a city of twenty thousand under the Eastern Reach. Remote, austere and deeply traditional, it is ruled by **Vāhrām of Ushtra-bēr**, a hard man of fifty whose court ceremonial is said to preserve forms forgotten elsewhere.
- **House of Vahúrdash** (revived, partially ruling). The Vahúrdash settlement numbers about four thousand in the town and was reclaimed within the last generation. Its head, **Khoršád-Vahūr**, holds the title by recent acclamation rather than ancient right and sits at the Council as a kind of seventh among equals.

The Council does not govern; it coordinates. The three ruling oases govern themselves, and the four exile houses keep their formal courts and work diplomatically and commercially in Amradad and beyond. What the Council does maintain is a single diplomatic position toward the Ātárzád (no recognition, no treaty, a perpetual claim of sovereignty over the conquered cities) and the machinery of the diaspora: the legal codes by which Tellumi communities inside the Ātárzád cities govern their own affairs, the funding of Tellumi shrines and schools in Amradad and the Haradian ports, and a quiet network of agents who tell the exile houses how matters stand in the lost cities.

## The Diaspora and the Subject Population

The civilization survived the Breaking in two ways: through the oases the Ātárzád did not trouble to take, and through the subject population that stayed in the conquered cities. The second group is by far the larger, and it is the central fact of Tellumi life today.

Inside the four Ātárzád cities, Tellumi communities live in designated quarters, usually the older districts that the settler class has not rebuilt. They have not been slaves since two generations ago, when the Council of the Twelve abolished the formal bondage that had attached to the survivors in the war's aftermath. They remain subject in concrete ways: barred from holding land outside their quarters, barred from public worship of any Āsháian god but Ātáröš, barred from carrying arms outside the militias the Ātárzád permit, and bound to pay the **water-tax**, a levy on the use of city water that falls only on Tellumi heads of household and is widely understood to be punitive.

What the Tellumi have is expertise. They are the water-engineers, irrigation-masters and palm-grove cultivators on whom oasis life depends, and the principal physicians, scribes (in three languages), commercial bankers and astronomers of the cities. Most Ātárzád courts of any sophistication employ Tellumi specialists, most Ātárzád merchant houses use Tellumi bookkeepers, and the Tōqēl himself, Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame, is known to consult Tellumi astronomers when the Flame must be observed for omens.

That dependence is the structural fact of the relationship. It is the reason Ātárzád suppression of rebellion, however brutal, has always stopped short of depopulating the Tellumi quarters: the cities do not run without the people who keep their canals. Neither side finds the arrangement comfortable, and both have learned to live inside it.

## Religion

The Tellumi practice the **full [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian pantheon]]**, all twelve deities honored in their places: Āhúrdáén supreme; Ātáröš the Sacred Flame as one of the twelve and the means by which Āhúrdáén is made manifest; and Mítharä, Khóršádrä, Zúráthrä, Báhrámiš, Ázráván, Zárványä, Árávēštä and the rest honored at their seasons and in their domains. This is the original form of the faith as the Tellumi Mōbads systematized it, and the High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad and the Tellumi Mōbadate generally still maintain it.

The devotional emphases are three:

- **Āhúrdáén** receives the formal weekly observance, and the Tellumi insist that no other god, Ātáröš included, may rightly receive it. That is the central theological dispute with the Ātárzád, who raise Ātáröš to exclusive worship; the Tellumi answer that doing so violates the order Āhúrdáén established.
- **Zúráthrä**, the Mother of the Oasis, receives the deepest popular devotion. Every Tellumi oasis, and every Tellumi quarter inside an Ātárzád city, keeps a shrine to her at its principal water-source. Every wedding involves her blessing, and every child is presented at her shrine within forty days of birth. The destruction of her great shrine at Shirvan is among the bitterest wounds of the Breaking.
- **The Mōbadate** is the institutional authority. A Mōbad studies fifteen to twenty years before ordination, masters astronomy, mathematics, theology, law and at least three languages, and serves as something between a priest and a judge. The High Mōbadate at Amradad, held by **Aravdán the Stargazer**, is the senior religious authority for the whole Confederation, and in its own view for all Āsháian peoples, though the Ātárzád deny it.

The [[lore-khzrnclndr|Tellumi Calendar]] is twelve thirty-day months and a five-day intercalary festival at the spring equinox. It differs from the [[lore-commoncal|Vylarian Calendar]] and from the Ātárzád ritual count. Tellumi under Ātárzád rule keep their own and the city's side by side, while those in the free oases keep their own alone.

## Economy

A Tellumi oasis lives on what its water makes: dates and date-palm sugar, grain and garden produce from the canal-fed fields, wool, hides, and, at Mehrnāgord, salt from the pans of the western end. Horses of the long-legged desert breed are raised on the open pasture between the oases, mostly by the Ātárzád. The great cities add the crafts of the old quarters: metalwork, glazed pottery, weaving, instruments and books.

The wealth that matters to the Confederation is made elsewhere. Tellumi merchant-houses, mostly based at Amradad with branches in the Vylarian Sea ports, handle a meaningful share of the Sultanate's external trade and a near-monopoly of certain specialty goods: Tellumi scholarly books, scientific instruments and the rare date wine prized in the Sultanate. Wages paid to the Tellumi professional class in the Ātárzád cities flow in part to the Council through informal channels, as donations that maintain the genealogies, the exile courts and the schools.

The Tellumi pay no tax to any state. By ancient compact they pay a tithe to the Mōbadate, which funds the temples, the schools and the legal machinery of the Confederation as a whole.

## Defense

The Confederation keeps no army. The three ruling houses each retain a small household guard, about a hundred men at Mehrnāgord, fifty at Ushtra-bēr and a token force at Vahúrdash, enough for ordinary security and ceremony. The exile houses keep small protective retinues in Amradad, which Sultanate law forbids any military activity and tolerates while the retinues stay discreet.

Real security rests on two things: the protection of the Sultanate, which has consistently refused to extradite Tellumi exiles or cooperate with Ātárzád requests for action against the Confederation, and the Ātárzád cities' dependence on Tellumi technical labor, which deters campaigns of destruction. Khávar of Zargandûr's quiet preparations are the only serious military planning the Confederation does. The Council does not debate them openly, and privately its consensus is that no reclamation will come in this generation.

## The Blood-Feud

(See [[affiliation-tribestrzd#the-blood-feud|the Ātárzád article]] for the structural account.)

The Tellumi do not call it a feud. For them it is a case of stolen property in which the rightful owners are waiting for the moment restitution becomes possible, and what they seek is **return**, not revenge. The exile princes of Shirvan, Ashkarad, Zargandûr and Kethramír would, if they could, walk back into their cities, take possession of their forebears' palaces, rebuild the dismantled temples of Āhúrdáén and Zúráthrä and resume the government their families exercised for centuries. They say they would tolerate the settlers as a subject population, as the Tellumi have been tolerated, but with more dignity.

Whether that restraint would survive an actual reconquest is doubtful. The generation of exiles now living has carried the loss for ninety years.

## Famous Figures

**Aravdán the Stargazer**, High Mōbad of Āhúrdáén at the Great Fire-Temple in Amradad and presiding convenor of the Council of the Princes. About sixty years old, an astronomer of continental reputation, he has held the exile community together through three decades of small crises, and his weight in the secular government of the Sultanate is considerable (see [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate of Amradad]]).

**Mōrá-Shirván**, heir-in-exile of the senior house. Her legal-historical case for the restoration of Shirvan is the central document of Tellumi political claims. She is forty, unmarried, and some exile factions see her as a possible focal point for a more activist phase.

**Khávar of Zargandûr**, the young heir training in Sultanate military service. Ambitious, twenty-seven, and the figure round whom the militant exile faction increasingly organizes. The Council has neither endorsed nor forbidden his preparations.

**Yázdín of Kethramír**, Mōbad and head of the Kethramír exile house. The most rigorous of the exile theologians and author of the formal refutation of the Ātárzád Promise, _On the Ungrantable Land_, which is required reading in Tellumi religious education and contraband in Ātárzád territory.

**Pārván of Mehrnāgord**, ruling prince of the largest free oasis. He is the voice of accommodation on the Council. Ātárzád merchant interests have sounded him out about limited normalization, and he has answered neither way.

**The Hidden Mōbad of Shirvan**, an unnamed and possibly mythical figure said to keep the original ritual office at a secret place near the Shirvan spring, conducting the proper observances for Āhúrdáén in defiance of the Ātárzád prohibition. The Tellumi of the Shirvan quarter believe the office exists and that its succession has run unbroken since the Breaking. The Ātárzád launch periodic searches and have never found anyone. Whether the Hidden Mōbad is a person or a useful story, the belief shapes religious life in the quarter.

## Commerce and Currency

A Tellumi oasis trades the way an oasis people does. Within an oasis, and between neighbors of the same house, exchange runs on [[lore-bartercnmy|barter]] at customary rates for dates, grain, salt and wool, and on [[lore-kinhalcrdt|house credit]] between families that have dealt with each other for generations. The great cities add weighed silver and foreign coin, Vylarian Argo from the west and Khelâthi gezan pieces from the south, which circulate by their metal. The Tellumi strike none of their own. Caravans come down from the north by the southern spur and in from the west by the Descent, bringing metals, fine cloth, glass and spices, and leave with dates, palm-sugar, salt, books and horses. Moneylenders working on Vedyaran lines (see [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]]) have set up in the larger cities, to finance the caravans that cross the Grazian passes.
