## Overview

The [Vurinkarvem cliff]{.name data-type="place/feature"} over [[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]] is riddled with cut chambers and pilgrim stairs, and some of them are older than the city. At the bottom of one of the oldest, a gallery of seven bays below the level of the lower basin, a well-head of gray stone stands in a round chamber. The Tellumi of the quarter call it **Peshdenderat**, "the dry well". It has no water in it.

It is a deep shaft. A weighted line paid out from the lip runs for a hundred and forty fathoms without touching, which is more than three times the depth at which the great spring's water stands. The wall of the shaft is cut stone for the first forty fathoms, then rough rock, then something the plumb-lines come up wet and cold from, a damp that is not water. At the lip, in the dark of the moon, the stones whisper.

## Layers {#history}

The gallery is among the oldest cut in the cliff, older than [[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]], the first of the Tellumi cities, and no record says who sank the shaft. Shirvan fell to the Atarzád in the eleventh year of the Breaking, about 629 AF, its archive burned, and the [Ozhmarod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} have held the city since. Within the memory of the quarter's old men the great spring has begun to stand lower.

## The Voice

A drop of blood from the hand, let fall on the lip, is answered. The voice comes up the shaft, dry as a husk, and asks what is wanted. It has been known to call the party by name before it is told. It will give a secret for a secret, or a secret for blood, a thimbleful for a sentence. It knows the water-hours of every household, the names of the [Ozhmarod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} informers in the quarter, where the clay of the burned archive lies and the hour the great fire-warden of the city sleeps. It has told a man the day he would die. It does not lie. It tells what it is asked and nothing it is not.

What it asks in return grows. A drop becomes a measure, a measure a cup, a cup a body drained. The voice is thin and polite and does not seem to want anything. The Tellumi who know of it say that it is not the dead and not a spirit. It is the [Tally]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, and what it counts is what a city owes.

:::secret {title="The sorcerer-priest"}

The quarter's secret is that the Tally has a servant. A Shekhul of the old rite comes to the well, the hidden one the Ozhmarod have hunted for ninety years. The hidden office has passed through three hands in that time, and he is the third. He is [Nurmelul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a lean gray man of sixty whose hands are scarred to the wrist. He holds the old observances in a back room of the lower town and teaches in secret, and he believes he is keeping the Tellumi alive. Every secret that has kept him hidden has come from the well, and every secret has been paid for in blood: his own at first, then the blood of volunteers from the quarter, who give it for the cause.

The well takes more than it was given, and the oasis pays for it.
:::

The oasis is drying, slowly, from the edges in. The outer channels have lost a hand's breadth of flow, the date groves at the fringe of the town have thinned, and the great spring itself stands lower at the basin's edge than the old men remember. The water-masters have reset the channel-hours twice and do not know why.

## What the City Says

- **The water-masters.** The keepers of the channel ledgers see the numbers and say nothing. They suspect the well and they suspect a priest.
- **The Ozhmarod fire-wardens.** The garrison believes the quarter is poisoning its own water to blame the Flame. They search the lower town for the hidden Shekhul and find his students.
- **The quarter's gate-keepers.** The water-master who holds the key to the courtyard gate keeps a ledger of the households that have given blood, each marked with a drop, and cuts their channel-hours when the oasis runs short. She does not say who the households are.
- **The pilgrims.** Visitors to the cliff shrines are told the lower galleries are flooded and closed. They are not, and the guides who say so keep the stair.
- **The exile house.** The [House of Shirvan]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} in the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] pays a courier for the quarter's news and has begun to ask how the quarter knows so much.

## The Water Falling {#present}

Shirvan is short of water and does not know why. The water-masters have reset the channel-hours twice, the fringe groves are thinning, and the keeper of the courtyard gate cuts the hours of the households in her ledger when the oasis runs short. The Ozhmarod fire-wardens who hold the city believe the quarter is poisoning its own water to blame the Flame, and they search the lower town for the hidden Shekhul and find only his students. The Ozhmarod are also the tribe that listens most closely to [Aqeshat]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Prophet, who preaches the Tellumi out of the cities, and a quarter blamed for a failing spring is a quarter that can be expelled. In Amradad, the exile house asks its courier how the quarter knows so much.

## Reaching It

The gallery lies below the level of the lower basin, at the foot of an old pilgrim stair that begins in a courtyard of the Tellumi quarter. After rain, the lower bays flood in the time it takes to climb out of them. The approach is watched by the quarter's water-masters, who say the way is shut for repairs.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **How the quarter knows.** [Muresha of Shirvan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, heir-in-exile of the House of Shirvan, pays a courier for the quarter's news and wants someone who is not her courier to learn where it comes from.
- **The channel ledgers.** The water-masters see the numbers fall and suspect the well and a priest, and they want someone with no channel-hours to lose to go down the stair that they keep shut.
- **The hidden Shekhul.** The Ozhmarod fire-wardens want the hidden Shekhul, and they pay a stranger who can pass in the lower town to find the back room where he teaches.

:::secret {title="The sorcerer-priest"}

- **What the well costs.** Nurmelul believes the well keeps the Tellumi alive. A party that learns the oasis pays for every answer can tell the quarter, the water-masters or the fire-wardens, and whichever it tells decides what becomes of the quarter.

:::
