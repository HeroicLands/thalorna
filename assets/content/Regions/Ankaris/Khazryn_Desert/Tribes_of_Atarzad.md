# terran_analog: "Israelites of the Exodus and Conquest—twelve-tribe nomadic-pastoral confederation, monolatrous fire-cult, covenantal land-claim, post-bondage founding narrative under a singular prophet, blood-feud with the indigenous urban polytheists whose territory they seized"

The **Tribes of Atarzád** are a confederation of twelve allied tribes who hold four of the walled cities of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], the oasis belt at the north foot of [[place-graznmntns|the Grazian Mountains]], and the high ground around [[place-mountshofar|Mount Shofar]]. They are newcomers—by the reckoning of their own griots, three generations have passed since [The Breaking]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, the Conquest, and the forebears who came up out of bondage in the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] under the prophet [Yotekhor]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and received the [Promise of the Tellumel]{.name data-type="lore"} at the [Unconsuming Flame]{.name data-type="place/site"} are the stuff of recitation. By the reckoning of their adversaries, the [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi]], they are three generations of trespassers whose claim rests on theology rather than on right.

The Tribes are a power of one corner of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], not of the waste. The steppe tribes to the north answer to no one in Tellumel, the wells and grazing of the open waste belong to the tribes that own them, and an Atarzád caravan master buys safe passage there tribe by tribe like any other.

Both reckonings are correct. The Atarzád do not deny that the Tellumi were here first; they deny that _first_ matters. In their teaching, the land was promised to the Flame-born by [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]], the Sacred Flame himself. To the faithful Atarzád, that promise is more solid than any wall the Tellumi built before them and any deed the Tellumi kept after them.

## Origins: The Bondage and the Exodus

Every festival of the Atarzád opens with the recitation of the founding, and a child who falters in it is made to begin again. By that tradition the ancestors of the Twelve Tribes were a single labor-caste in the temple-cities of Au'Khelathu, brought from somewhere to the east in a generation now beyond memory and held there for centuries as builders, brick-makers, and irrigation-diggers under the Gar-Au's quarry-overseers. They worshipped Ataros in secret, in lamps kept burning behind hearth-stones, while the priests of the Khelathi temples sought to drive them into the worship of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]] and [[lore-thubaidty|Thuba'i]].

The [Exodus]{.name data-type="lore/history"} narrative is dense, much-elaborated, and central to Atarzád identity. Its core: a prophet named **Yotekhor**, born to a labor-caste woman but raised in a minor noble household after some accident of fortune, was called by Ataros at a desert flame that burned without consuming its tinder. In this narrative, Ataros commanded Yotekhor to lead the Flame-born out of bondage and across the desert into a land that had been kept for them. Yotekhor confronted the Gar-Au of the day, who refused; Ataros afflicted Au'Khelathu until the Gar-Au relented; the Flame-born departed.

The route of the Exodus is by tradition divided into three legs.

The **first leg** carried the Flame-born northeast out of Au'Khelathu, across the narrow desert that joins the [[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]] to the southern coast of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], and up the eastern shore of that sea, opposite the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]]. The Taruzelian merchant-states of the time, recognizing both an opportunity and a moral obligation, shipped supplies across the strait and provisioned the host with grain, fresh water, and what Taruzelian chroniclers euphemistically call "discounted iron." Atarzád law to this day forbids raiding into Taruzel, and the [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} merchant-houses maintain especially scrupulous trading relationships with Taruzelian counterparts in continued recognition of the obligation.

The **second leg** carried the Flame-born east-and-north through the **Dunhari uplands**—the long traverse of the high Dunhari plateau that runs between the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom]] (whose southern marches the Atarzád skirted with care) and the open [[place-dunharargn|Dunhari tribal interior]] to the south. The Dunhari sheikhs of the day were too occupied with their own internal feuds to oppose the migration; the Flame-born paid passage in temple-gold and proceeded.

The **third leg** was the [Wandering]{.name data-type="lore/history"} itself—the long traverse of the great central deserts and the high-cold plateaus that stretch between the eastern Dunhara and the southern Khazryn. The original generation died in the dry country; a new generation, born free, completed the crossing and prepared to enter the land. By the time the Flame-born came down off [[place-dkrqrrgn|Diqraqor]], the Stone Uplands, past Mount Shofar into Tellumel, those who had personally remembered Au'Khelathu were nearly all dead, and the people who would conquer the Tellumi were a wholly new generation—born free, raised in the Promise, hardened by the wilderness.

In their account, the Promise itself was given to Yotekhor at **Mt. Shofar**, a black-rock peak at the southern edge of the Stone Uplands, rising over the eastern half of Tellumel, where Ataros revealed himself in a flame that burned in the open air on bare stone for three days and nights without any fuel and without diminishing. The [Covenant]{.name data-type="lore/law"} Ataros is held to have given Yotekhor there is still recited in full at each celebration of [The Festival of the Promise]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}:

> _I am Ataros, the Sacred Flame, who lit the first hearth and shall light the last. The Tellumel I give to you and to your seed after you, in perpetuity, for as long as a flame burns upon a Flame-born hearth. Walk before me with truth in your mouth and the kindling in your hand. Honor no other before me. The Tellumel is yours._

Yotekhor died on the slope of Mt. Shofar, never having entered the choicest land himself. His tomb is the second-holiest site of the Atarzád after the Unconsuming Flame above it.

## The Conquest

The Conquest of Tellumel—what the Atarzád call the **War of the Twelve Years** and what the Tellumi call [[lore-khazrynclt|Turem]], the Breaking—followed under Yotekhor's successor, the Zhudek [Nozhikor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. The Tellumi at the time were a confederation of seven independent oasis-cities, each governed by a hereditary princely house and a temple of [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Ahurdaen]], militarily competent but unprepared for what came at them: a tribally organized people who had been hardened by the wilderness, who fought as twelve allied units, and who believed without reservation that the cities they were attacking had been promised to them and that to fall in the taking of them was to die in the bosom of the Sacred Flame.

The Tellumi had stone walls, water-engineering, professional cavalry, and several centuries of written law. The Atarzád had numbers, surprise, mobility, religious zeal, and the willingness to take losses no professional army would have accepted. Over twelve years the Atarzád took, in order: **[[place-zargandur|Zargandur]]** (year 2), **[[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]]** (year 5), **[[place-kethramir|Kethramír]]** (year 8), and finally the Tellumi's holiest seat, **[[place-oasishirvn|Oasis of Shirvan]]** (year 11), where the great spring gushes from the rock at the foot of the [Vurinkarvem cliff]{.name data-type="place/feature"} and where the Tellumi traditionally held that [[lore-ahurdaendty|Ahurdaen]] had revealed the cosmic order to the desert peoples in the first age. The fall of Shirvan ended the war. The surviving Tellumi princely houses retreated to peripheral oases or fled to the Kingdom of Amradad; the Tellumi population that remained was reduced to a subject merchant-and-craftsman class within the now-Atárzád cities.

That was about ninety years ago. The Atarzád have held those four cities since, and they have taken no more: the other Tellumi cities still stand.

## The Twelve Tribes

The twelve tribes are descended (so the genealogies say) from the twelve children of [Ebozor the Many-Sired]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a patriarch of the labor-caste in Au'Khelathu who lived several generations before Yotekhor. Each tribe maintains its own genealogy back to Ebozor, its own dialect-inflection of the [[skill-atarzadilng|Atárzadi tongue]], its own particular tribal customs, and its own apportioned territory among the conquered cities and the ground around them.

The most prominent five tribes are:

[Zhoqarod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: the largest tribe, holding most of Zargandur and the great pasturelands to its north. Traditionally the war-tribe; the Zhudek has been chosen from Zhoqarod in five of the last seven elevations.

Teghumod
: holders of Ashkarad and the gates where the caravan road from the steppe comes into the belt. The merchant-tribe; the wealthiest of the twelve and the most cosmopolitan, with substantial diaspora communities in Amradad and even in Taruzelian ports.

[Burqalod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: holders of the central highland country and the slopes of Mt. Shofar itself. The keeper-tribe; the Toqel (Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame) has by long custom always been a Burqalod.

[Tozhirod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: holders of Kethramír and the oases east of it. The scholar-tribe; their elders maintain the scriptural archive and the law-codes of Yotekhor.

[Teqemod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}
: holders of Shirvan and the western fringe. The militant tribe, considered most uncompromising on matters of theology and most likely to support the strict-observance party in inter-tribal politics.

The remaining seven ([Gholutod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Kelumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Zhimelod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Hozhirod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Noghezod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Bizhunod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, [Ghuzolod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}) are smaller, hold portions of the lesser oases or the herding-grounds between, and align variously with the larger five on inter-tribal questions.

## Government

The Atarzád have no king. Yotekhor explicitly forbade the office at Mt. Shofar—_"You shall have no king, for I, Ataros, am king of the Flame-born; if you raise a king over yourselves, he will eat your sons and your daughters and your fields"_—and the prohibition is taken with great seriousness. Several attempts have been made over the generations to elevate a particularly successful Zhudek to permanent kingship; all have ended in repudiation, and the most ambitious such attempt (by the Zhoqarod war-leader [Tulqenor the Bold]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, two generations ago) ended with Tulqenor's repudiation and exile by the [Council of the Twelve]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}.

What the Atarzád have instead is the **Council of the Twelve**, which convenes annually at **[[place-beitshofar|Beit-Shofár]]** (the priestly seat at the foot of Mt. Shofar) for a six-week session. Each tribe sends its Rezhul (hereditary chieftain), the senior elders of its principal lineages, and its senior priest. The Council sets common policy on defense, taxation, the disposition of disputed territories, the adjudication of inter-tribal disputes, and the renewal of the Covenant.

In times of major crisis—full-scale war, drought, religious schism, or a particularly dangerous Tellumi uprising—the Council may acclaim a **Zhudek** (Judge), a single figure granted broad temporary executive authority. The Zhudek is by tradition always a prophet or a warrior of demonstrated capability, always selected after consultation with the Toqel (who interprets the will of Ataros through the Unconsuming Flame), and always serves only for the duration of the crisis. Twenty-three Zhudek have been acclaimed in the three generations since the Conquest. The current generation has had none; the last died fourteen years ago after suppressing a Tellumi-led rebellion in the Shirvan district.

## Religion

The Atarzád practice the [[affiliation-ataros|Faith of Ataros]], **monolatrous devotion to Ataros the Sacred Flame**. They are not—strictly speaking—monotheists; they acknowledge the existence of the other eleven gods of the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian pantheon]] as real beings, even powerful ones. But they hold that Ataros alone is the patron of the Flame-born, that Ataros alone gave them the Promise, and that Ataros alone will receive their devotion. To worship the others—Ahurdaen, [[lore-khorsadradty|Khórsadra]], [[lore-mitharadty|Míthara]], [[lore-zurathradty|Zúrathra]], any of them—is, for an Atarzád, a covenant violation: a betrayal of the agreement at Mt. Shofar.

Several practical consequences follow:

- **No temples to other gods may stand on Atarzád land.** The Tellumi temples in the conquered cities were systematically dismantled in the generation after the Conquest. The stones were used to build the great fire-temples of Ataros that now dominate the city centers of Zargandur, Ashkarad, Kethramír, and Shirvan. Some Tellumi maintain that the foundations of the Shirvan fire-temple still incorporate the original altar-stone of Ahurdaen, hidden in inverted position deep below the floor—a charge the Atarzád priesthood denies and refuses to allow excavation to verify.
- **Imagery is strictly forbidden.** Ataros may not be depicted; only the Flame itself may stand for him, and even the Flame may not be reduced to a mere drawing. The Atarzád temples are aniconic. The Tellumi temples were full of deity-statues; this is one of the central charges the Atarzád lay against Tellumi religion.
- **The Toqel**, the Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame, is the senior religious authority of the Atarzád. By tradition always a Burqalod, always elderly, always elevated only on the death of the previous holder. The current Toqel is [Sighotor son of Nozhikor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a man of perhaps eighty winters who is widely held to have spoken with Ataros in dreams.
- **The Festival of the Promise** is celebrated three times yearly—at the spring equinox, at midsummer, and at the autumn equinox—by pilgrimage to Mt. Shofar. Every adult Atarzád is expected to make the pilgrimage at least once before death; many make it annually. The summer festival draws upward of fifty thousand pilgrims, who camp in the valleys around the mountain and gather at the Unconsuming Flame for the recitation of the Covenant.
- **Dietary law** distinguishes the Atarzád sharply from the Tellumi. Meat from any animal that did not die with its blood properly drained may not be eaten. Certain animals—most notably the desert hare, the wild boar, and several species of reptile—are wholly proscribed. The first fruits of every harvest and the first-born of every herd must be offered at a fire-temple before the remainder may be consumed.

## Economy

The Atarzád live off what they took: the irrigated gardens and palm groves of the four cities, the tolls of the caravan road where it comes into the belt, and the herds they graze on the uplands round Mount Shofar. They did not initially understand oasis-agriculture; the Tellumi water-engineers and palm-grove cultivators were essential to keeping the canals running after the Conquest, and a substantial Tellumi technical class persists in the cities today as a result. Over three generations the Atarzád have absorbed enough of the technique to manage the systems themselves, but the most senior water-masters in every Atarzád city are still, by overwhelming majority, Tellumi by descent.

Caravans coming down from the steppe pay the Atarzád at the gates of the belt: a toll on every loaded camel, a fee for a guide through the canal country, and a levy for lodging in the caravanserais that the fire-temples own. The Teghumod hold the gates of Ashkarad and take the largest share. Dates, grain, wool and the cattle and horses of the uplands go out in return, and Atarzád merchants carry belt goods to Amradad and to Taruzelian ports.

The cities are taxed; the herding tribes pay a tithe of cattle and horses; the Council of the Twelve maintains a modest standing treasury at Beit-Shofár. There is no central tax-collecting bureaucracy; each tribe is responsible for raising its own contribution and presenting it at the annual Council session.

## Defense

Each tribe maintains its own warrior society—the [Khemozekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Zhoqarod, the [Tulqenekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Burqalod, the [Ghishamekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Teqemod, and so on. The warrior societies are age-graded; young men enter them at sixteen and serve through their forties. There is no professional standing army; mobilization is by tribal call, with the twelve societies coordinating through the Council in time of inter-tribal action.

The Atarzád fighting style is built around mounted archery, fast-moving cavalry detachments, the use of the great horses of the uplands to outpace and outmaneuver heavier forces, and a distinctive willingness to take heavy losses for theologically-significant objectives. The Tellumi were defeated in the Conquest in part because they faced an enemy whose acceptable casualty rate exceeded any rate a normal professional force would have countenanced. The Atarzád have retained that institutional willingness.

## Relations with Other Peoples

To the **southwest**, the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] is the dominant Asháian-pantheon power and is, technically, the senior religious authority for all Asháian-faith peoples—the Shekhul of Ahurdaen's [Great Fire-Temple of Amradad]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the formal head of the Ashaian priesthood. Relations are complicated by the fact that the Kingdom's official religion is the full pantheon (with Ahurdaen supreme) and the Atarzád consider this idolatry. Diplomatic intercourse is maintained—the Teghumod merchants would not tolerate its breakdown—but it is cool. The Kingdom considers the Atarzád to be schismatics whose elevation of Ataros to exclusive worship is a covenant violation against Ahurdaen; the Atarzád consider the Kingdom to be polytheistic apostates from the original revelation of Yotekhor. The Kingdom hosts the largest Tellumi exile community in the world, which it has consistently refused to extradite.

To the **northwest**, the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] is a major trading partner. The Teghumod merchant-houses maintain offices in [[place-sanctara|Ulthora]] and [[place-alyssar|Alessar]], and Taruzelian bankers handle Atarzád commerce in the Vylarian Sea. Religious differences do not impede trade.

To the **east**, beyond the waste, lie [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] and (much further) [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]]. Contact is thin and through merchants; the Atarzád know of these distant civilizations but have little practical engagement with them.

To the **far west**, [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] is a special case. By Atarzád doctrine, no Flame-born may set foot in Au'Khelathu willingly—to do so is to return to the place of bondage. The Teghumod merchants who must reach the Northern Fertile Region for trade do so by sea via Taruzelian shipping rather than overland through Khelathi territory; the marginal cost of the longer route is considered a religious obligation. The current Gar-Au of Au'Khelathu has, in recent years, made overtures suggesting that any returning Flame-born would be welcomed and that the old bondage is many generations gone; the Council of the Twelve has not deigned to reply.

To the **north**, the open waste belongs to the steppe tribes, who are independent of everyone. The Atarzád pay them for passage and guest-right as any caravan does, and trouble on the uplands round Mount Shofar is as likely to come from a tribe's raiders as from a Tellumi rising.

Within Tellumel, the [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]] is the eternal adversary. The two peoples share the belt, share a handful of gods (whom the Tellumi honor and the Atarzád merely acknowledge), and are locked in a blood-feud that no living person remembers the beginning of. The Atarzád hold four cities by the Promise; the Tellumi claim them by descent from the high springs and centuries of continuous habitation. Neither will yield.

## The Blood-Feud {#the-blood-feud}

The Atárzád-Tellumi blood-feud is not a single ongoing war; it is a continuous low-level conflict punctuated by larger crises. Ordinary Atarzád and Tellumi live within the same cities, trade with each other, and (rarely but not never) intermarry, and most years pass without any large action. But:

- Tellumi rebellions periodically erupt in the conquered cities, usually centered on a particular grievance (a confiscated property restored to a Tellumi heir, a denied water-right, a Tellumi shrine secretly maintained that the Atarzád discover and demolish). The rebellions are suppressed, often brutally.
- Atarzád settler families occasionally extend cultivation into peripheral oases that the Tellumi consider ancestrally theirs; the Tellumi respond with raids; the Atarzád respond to the raids; the cycle escalates.
- Religious provocations—a Tellumi who openly worships Ahurdaen in an Atarzád city, an Atarzád who desecrates a peripheral Tellumi shrine—produce flashpoints that may take a generation to subside.
- The Tellumi exiles in Amradad maintain a continuous diplomatic and intelligence campaign aimed at recovery of the lost oases; the Atarzád maintain a continuous counter-campaign.

The current generation is in a relatively quiet phase. The last full-scale rebellion was suppressed fourteen years ago; the next is overdue.

## Famous Figures

**Sighotor son of Nozhikor**, current Toqel, Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame. Eighty years old, holds the office for life, by tradition the spiritual head of the Atarzád people. Said to dream of Yotekhor.

[Khemozor of Zhoqarod]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, current Rezhul of the Zhoqarod tribe, the senior chief of the largest tribe and the man most likely to be acclaimed Zhudek should the next crisis come. A career warrior, in his late fifties, considered politically moderate but militarily uncompromising.

[Aqeshat]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Prophet, a young woman (by some accounts only twenty-three) who has begun preaching that Ataros has shown her the imminent return of Yotekhor in flesh and that the Flame-born must prepare themselves through stricter observance, the demolition of the remaining Tellumi communities within the Atarzád cities, and the formal expulsion of the Tellumi population. The Council of the Twelve has not yet determined whether she is to be honored or repudiated. Her following grows.

[Zelumor the Renegade]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a former Teghumod merchant-prince who was excommunicated thirty years ago for marrying a Tellumi woman of high birth and refusing to repudiate her. He lives in Amradad with his wife and their children, who are formally Tellumi by his renouncement. He is wealthy, well-connected, and considered by some Tellumi exiles to be a useful ally and by others to be a useful idiot.

## Commerce and Currency

The Atarzád live on the tolls of the caravan road where it enters Tellumel and on the produce of the four cities' gardens and the uplands' herds. Inside the cities, trade runs through [[lore-bartercnmy|barter]] and [[lore-kinhalcrdt|tribal credit]] customs, with the Teghumod counting-houses at Ashkarad settling what tribes owe one another; the Tellumi craftsmen and merchants of the cities have begun to adopt modest moneylender practices along Vedyaran lines (see [[lore-vdyrnbnkng|Vedyaran Banking]]). Tolls are paid in kind or in coin as the caravan master prefers. Foreign coin (Vylarian Ardin from western trade, Khelathi gezan pieces from southern trade) circulates by intrinsic-metal value among traders.
