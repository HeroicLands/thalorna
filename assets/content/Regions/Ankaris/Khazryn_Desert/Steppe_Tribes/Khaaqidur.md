The **Khaaqidur**, "the gold people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the stone desert of [[place-idwkrrgn|Idvaqor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], who winter on its eastern edge and summer on the high steppe north of [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]]. No caravan crosses their ground and no Tantsou patrol rides into it. The garrison and the road know them from the tales the tribes of the road tell about them, and the tales are of gold and skulls.

## The Range

The Khaaqidur winter at the wells of the stone desert's eastern edge, in the beds of the dry watercourses below the last ridges, and drive their horses, camels and yaks up onto the high steppe north of the road at the thaw. Their chief well is [Dujar]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the smoke", a stone-lined shaft in a watercourse under a black ridge, where the smoke tent stands and the moot sits.

The tribe sells no passage, because the road does not need its ground. It trades horses and hides to the tribes of the road for grain and iron, and it pays for both in gold by weight, which the tribes of the road take and do not ask about.

## The Gold

The Khaaqidur wear gold where other tribes wear brass: on a bridle, at a belt, in a woman's hair, in thin plates sewn to a rider's coat. They lay out their dead with every piece of it on them, under open sky on the laying-out ground below Dujar, and no Khaaqidur takes a piece back. Nobody else goes near that ground.

The sky-gold is the tribe's own. It is a cup, a bridle-bit and a short knife of gold, which the tribe says fell from the sky into the first dovaq's camp. The dovaq keeps it and shows it at the investing of the next. It passes to the youngest of a dovaq's children, son or daughter, and the elder children lead the riders and never the moot.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The dovaq calls the moot into the smoke tent at Dujar, a closed tent of felt in which the shaman burns seed on hot stones, and the householders sit in the smoke and speak. What the shaman shouts in it is taken as the sky's word on the matter, and the dovaq speaks last.

A rider who kills an enemy of the tribe in a feud brings back the head. The skull is cleaned, rimmed in gold and made a cup, and the rider drinks from it at every moot for the rest of his life. A cup-holder speaks at the moot before a householder who holds none.

The dovaq is [Khaaqidlun Khaaqidur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "gold", a woman of twenty-four, the youngest of five, who keeps the sky-gold over two elder brothers who lead the riders.

## Feuds

The Khaaqidur and the [[affiliation-bdkrtrb|Budakur]] both winter on the eastern edge of the stone desert, and each claims the grazing the other drives its herds onto when the road shuts. Blood is owed both ways, and four cups at Dujar were Budakur heads. The Khaaqidur hold the Budakur in contempt as the garrison's dogs, paid in silk and sending their sons to the Empire.

On the high steppe the Khaaqidur are at feud with the [[affiliation-tjdrtrb|Tijodur]], whose summer grass lies beyond theirs. Two of the mounds at the Tijodur well hold men the Khaaqidur killed, and their skulls are cups at Dujar.

## The Price of the Budakur Grass {#present}

A dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage, and his riders have come as far east as Dujar. Khaaqidlun has sent him word that the Khaaqidur ride under his banner if the Budakur winter grazing is theirs when he has the steppe. Her elder brothers want to ride without the bargain, because a union is the one war in which the Budakur cannot call on the Tantsou garrison, and the shaman has burned seed twice on the question and given two answers.

The garrison has its own count. The Budakur have told it that the Khaaqidur are sworn already, and a Tantsou officer has asked the Budakur for the way to Dujar.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Khaaqidur count their dovaqs back to the one into whose camp the sky-gold fell, and the count runs youngest child to youngest child. They hold that they wintered at Dujar before the first banner. The feud with the Budakur is as old as the Empire's pay, and the Khaaqidur date it from the year the Budakur first took silk.

## Hooks

- A Tantsou officer wants a guide to Dujar and offers the price of a caravan's passage. The only guides who know the way are Budakur, and they want a cup back.
- One of Khaaqidlun's elder brothers wants the sky-gold looked at by someone who is not of the tribe, because he says the knife is not the knife his father showed.
