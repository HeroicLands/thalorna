The **Ghotakur**, "the forest people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the northern grass of the [[place-hskrrgn|Ghoshiqor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], east of the [[affiliation-svghrtrb|Savaghur]], where the steppe gives out against the spruce of [[place-gwtkrrgn|Ghotaqor]]. They herd horses and sheep like any tribe of the grass, and every winter their men leave the herds and go north into the forest to mend a wall. Nobody living knows how it was built, and the Ghotakur keep it because their fathers kept it.

## The Range

The Ghotakur summer on the high grass along the forest's southern edge, where the creeks run until midsummer and the first larch stand in the hollows. Their gathering place is [Jiboq]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the ice", a spring at the head of a valley that runs back into the trees, where the moot sits at the first frost and the wall-oath is sworn.

In winter the families go down into the sheltered valleys along the forest edge, a camp to a valley, each drawing from its own creek. Those valleys are the closest grass to the wall's road and the warmest ground on the northern steppe, and the Ghotakur hold them as the one thing the tribe cannot lose.

No branch of [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]] crosses their grass. Their riders take horses and felt to the autumn market at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] and bring back grain and iron, and they sell the forest bands salt and buy their furs on the way north.

## The Wall

Some days north of the valleys, deep in the forest, a wall runs through Ghotaqor. It is old work of laid stone and banked earth, built by people in the deep past, and the knowledge of its building is lost. Its stones have been falling for longer than any Ghotakur can count, and the tribe mends it where they fall with turf, timber and rubble, because nobody alive can set them as they were set.

What lies beyond it is the north, and the Ghotakur say no more of it than that the dead there do not lie still and the wall keeps them there. Every household owes a man to the wall each winter. The men go north once the bogs have frozen hard enough to carry a sled, mend what has fallen and watch what they cannot mend, and come home at the thaw. A man who has gone north and come back is wall-sworn, and he sits at the moot before any householder who has not. Some do not come back, and the tribe lays out no body for them.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The moot chooses the dovaq from the wall-sworn, and the dovaq leads the men north. From the first frost to the thaw each valley camp answers to its valley-mother, the eldest woman there, who holds its herds and its water and speaks for it, and a raid on a valley in winter falls on women, boys and old men.

The dovaq is [Ghotaqtoliz Ghotakur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "forest-spear", a broad man of about fifty with two fingers lost to the cold, who has gone north twenty-six winters.

## Feuds

The Ghotakur and the Savaghur claim the same winter valleys along the forest edge, and the two tribes have been at feud over them for three generations. In the ice winter a Savaghur camp starving on its own valley moved into a Ghotakur one while the men were north, and the valley-mother drove it out into the snow. The Savaghur lost children on that road, and the Ghotakur hold that the valley was theirs to keep.

## The Wall Takes Men {#present}

Last winter a long stretch of the wall fell in a night, and the men could not raise it again before the thaw. Three of them did not come home. Ghotaqtoliz needs more men north this winter than the households can spare, and fewer than ever are left to hold the valleys while they are gone.

A dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage, and his riders have come to Jiboq. Ghotaqtoliz has told them the Ghotakur swear to no one whose muster takes men from the wall, and that a man who sends riders to the wall in winter may ask the tribe anything in spring. The riders have not answered. The valley-mothers fear something nearer: the Savaghur, broken by the ice winter and offered herds by the same man, are counting the Ghotakur valleys again, and the men leave at the first frost.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Ghotakur hold that their fathers kept the wall before the first banner, and their tellings name the men who went north and did not come back, valley by valley and winter by winter. They do not say who built it. Their tellings of the feud begin with a winter the Savaghur took a valley and a summer the Ghotakur took it back, and no two camps agree which came first.
