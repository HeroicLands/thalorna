The **Soqritur**, "the fish people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the northern [[place-wlqtrrgn|Volketor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], whose grass runs down to a river on the frontier of [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]]. At a bend of the river stands one of the Velanthian oasis-and-river communities, a village of fishers and barley farmers that the Soqritur hold. The tribe governs it by a second law, its own and not the steppe's, and takes its fish and grain.

## The Range

The Soqritur graze the grass between the river and the springs of the inner margin, a day or two from the water at its farthest. Their gathering place is [Jozul]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the pool", a spring-fed pool on the grass where the moot sits and the dovaq is invested.

The held village lies at the river bend, under the tribe's riders and inside the frontier the Velanthian coronation rolls claim. Its fishers net the river and fish through the ice in winter, its farmers sow barley on the river flats, and the river-warden takes a share of both for the tribe. The tribe sells no stretch of the road and takes Velanthian grain and iron for horses at the village.

## The Two Laws

The riders' law of the steppe judges a Soqritur: feud, guest-right, the price of a horse and the ending of a blood debt. The river law judges the village: its fields, its nets, its marriages and its thefts, as its people kept them before the tribe held them. The village's own headman keeps the river law, and the river-warden sits beside him and hears with him any case between a villager and a rider. Neither law is heard in the other's court, and a rider who wrongs a villager is judged by the river-warden and the headman together.

The village is owed the tribe's protection and not its feud. A raid on the village is a raid on the Soqritur, and a villager killed by another tribe is avenged. A villager does not ride with the tribe and does not sit at the moot.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The moot chooses the dovaq, and the dovaq holds both laws and judges by the riders' one. Each spring, when the river ice breaks, the dovaq goes down to the village and takes the first fish of the year from the water with a hook, and the tribe's year opens with a feast on the river flats that the village and the tribe eat together.

The dovaq is [Soqritlun Soqritur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "fish", a woman of about forty who speaks the village's Velanthian as well as her own tongue.

## Feuds

The Soqritur are rivals of the [[affiliation-vrkrtrb|Vurakur]], their neighbors to the south, who take a yearly silver from the frontier communities and hold that every community on that stretch is theirs to ask. The village pays the Soqritur and nobody else, and the Soqritur have twice turned the Vurakur silver-rider back from the river.

## The River Between Two Hosts {#present}

The Velanthian Hosts of the frontier have told the communities to stop paying the tribes, and their atamans have sent riders to the village headman. He has answered them that the village pays the Soqritur and is protected for it. The Vurakur have answered for the communities that stopped paying with more riders, and the village fears that it is next, whoever it pays.

A dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage. The last union overran Velanthia's eastern fringe and took the frontier communities from the tribes that held them, and Soqritlun has not forgotten whose village it was then. She has sent the gathering man's riders home with nothing and has opened talk with an ataman of the nearest Host, who would take the village under his own protection and leave the tribe its fish.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Soqritur hold that they have grazed the river grass since before the first banner. When the last union broke, the tribe took the village at the bend, the river law came under the riders' law, and the first-fish feast is as old as that holding.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Soqritlun wants someone who is neither rider nor Velanthian to carry her terms to the ataman, because the Vurakur are watching the river road.
- A rider has killed a villager's son in a quarrel over nets, the river-warden and the headman cannot agree on the case, and the village wants an outsider to sit with them.
