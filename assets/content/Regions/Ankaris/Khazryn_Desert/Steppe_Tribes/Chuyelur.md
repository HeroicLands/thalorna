The **Chuyelur**, "the jackal people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the southern [[place-wlqtrrgn|Volketor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], whose grass runs down to the gray gravel of the [[place-hekardesert|Desert of Hek'ar]]. They live by the raid more openly than any tribe of the margin: herds from the March, flocks from their neighbors, and brides. Their old singer names every child, sings every raid and settles every quarrel, and a Chuyelur rider wants his name sung more than he wants the horses.

## The Range

The Chuyelur graze the grass and scrub on the southern edge of the margin, where the springs grow far apart and the grass gives out into Hek'ar. Their chief water is [Diqkhun]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the red", a spring at the foot of a red-rock ridge that runs all year, where the singer's tent stands and the moot sits.

They sell no stretch of [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]], which runs north of their grass, and they take a string that has bought no passage as lawful prey. They do sell water: the Black Flame knights who hunt the [[place-ruinsarkor|Ruins of Arkor]] in Hek'ar buy it from them, every faction alike, and the Chuyelur ride with none of them.

## The Singer

The Chuyelur have a dovaq and a moot like every tribe, and they have [Koyozlun Chuyelur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "song", an old woman who has sung the tribe's raids for forty years. A Chuyelur child has no name of its own until the singer gives it one, and a youth keeps a child's name until he has taken a herd in a raid and the singer names him for it. When two households quarrel past the moot's power to settle it, the singer hears both and gives judgment in a song, and the moot does not depart from it.

The dovaq is [Atazdovaq Chuyelur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "dusk-chief", a man of about forty whose name the singer gave him for a raid on the March's herds that every Chuyelur child can sing.

## Raiding and Brides

The Chuyelur raid the herds of the [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]] in spring and the flocks of the margin tribes in any season. They take brides by raid as well, a girl carried off from a neighbor's camp, and send the bride-price after her in horses so that the taking is a marriage and not a feud. Most of the brides come from the [[affiliation-nqkhkrtrb|Naqkhakur]] to the north, who pay ransom when they can, and two springs ago a Naqkhakur herder killed a Chuyelur raider at a clay pan. The two tribes have been at blood-feud since, and the singer has sung the dead raider's name at every fire.

## The Uniter's Song {#present}

Every bard of the steppe knows the song of the uniter, the one man who gathers the tribes and is remembered for it, and Koyozlun sings it better than any. Word that a dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage has set the young riders asking her for it every night. They want to ride north and join him, and their names sung into the song.

Koyozlun refuses to sing any living man into it. She says the song belongs to a man who has won and died, and that a singer who puts a living man in it has made an orghun with her own mouth. Atazdovaq wants the raids the union offers and does not want to cross the singer. The moot has asked her for a judgment on whether the tribe should ride, and she has not given one.

The Naqkhakur feud is the other half of it. A Chuyelur warband riding under a banner could take the Naqkhakur grain and call it a tithe, and the riders who want that most are the dead raider's brothers.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Chuyelur's own account is the singer's, and it is a run of raids, each with its rider's name, back to the first banner. Nothing in it is older, and the singer says nothing is.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Koyozlun wants a stranger who has seen the gathering man to describe him to her, word for word and with nothing added, before she gives the moot her judgment.
- A Black Flame faction in Hek'ar wants Chuyelur riders to lead it to a rival's camp, and Atazdovaq wants an outsider to carry his answer, because a Chuyelur seen riding with the knights is a story the singer has to sing.
