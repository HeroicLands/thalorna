The **Tijodur**, "the star people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the cold high steppe in the north of [[place-idwkrrgn|Idvaqor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], where the grass climbs into high valleys and the ground under it stays frozen through the summer. Every Tijodur is marked from childhood with the beasts of his line, in blue on the arms and shoulders. The tribe keeps a field of burial mounds, and every mound in it is an unpaid debt of blood.

## The Range

The Tijodur summer in the high valleys, where the grass is short and sweet and the nights freeze even at midsummer, and winter lower down, at wells in the lee of the ridges above the stone desert. Their chief well is [Votri]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the cold", a deep spring at the mouth of a high valley. Below it lies [Kuyad]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the buried", the field of the tribe's mounds, each a heap of stones over a timber chamber cut down into the frozen ground.

The road runs a long way south of the Tijodur, and the tribe sells it nothing. Its riders come down to the tribes of the road with horses and yak-hair felt in the autumn, and take grain and iron home.

## The Mounds

A Sovidur lays out the dead under open sky and digs a grave only for the one who was killed unavenged, so that he stays where his kin can find him. The Tijodur keep that custom harder than any tribe. A Tijodur killed by another tribe's hand goes into a chamber under a mound at Kuyad, in his clothes and his marks, with his horse killed beside him in its harness, and the cold keeps him as he went in.

The mound stands until his blood is paid, by a death, by horses or by a marriage, and the mound-keeper says when it is paid. Then the tribe opens the mound, carries the dead man up to the laying-out ground and leaves him to the four winds, his horse with him, as he would have gone if nobody had owed for him. Some mounds at Kuyad have stood for generations, and the mound-keeper can name each man in them and his marks and the tribe that owes for him.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The moot chooses the dovaq, who leads the riders sent after a debt. The mound-keeper holds the count, and no feud of the tribe is closed, by the dovaq or the moot, while its mound stands. A dovaq who makes peace with a tribe that owes for a mound has made a peace the tribe does not keep.

The dovaq is [Tijodkharu Tijodur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "star-patience", a lean man of about forty whose father lies under a mound the [[affiliation-khqdrtrb|Khaaqidur]] owe for.

## Feuds

Most of the mounds at Kuyad are owed by the tribes of the high steppe and the stone desert, and the Khaaqidur owe for more of them than any other. Two hold men the Khaaqidur killed and took the heads of, and the skulls are cups at the Khaaqidur well. The Tijodur hold that a mound whose dead man lacks his head cannot be opened until the head comes home.

## An End to Feuds {#present}

A dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage, and his riders have come to Votri. A tribe that swears to him puts its feuds aside while the union lasts, as every union has made the tribes do. For the Tijodur that means every mound at Kuyad stays shut, and every dead man in them stays in the ground.

Tijodkharu has told the riders the tribe swears to no one who closes its feuds unpaid. The riders have answered that the man makes the tribes that swear late pay the tribes that swore early, and the Khaaqidur have not sworn. The mound-keeper says that a debt paid by an orghun's order is not paid by the tribe that owes it, and half the moot wants the Khaaqidur heads home whoever makes them give them up.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Tijodur hold that they have buried their unavenged at Kuyad since before the first banner, and the oldest mounds there are older than any count of the tribe's dovaqs. When the last union broke, the tribe opened more mounds in one summer than in the generation before, as the feuds the union had closed came due.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- The mound-keeper wants the two Tijodur heads brought home from the Khaaqidur well, and will open both mounds for whoever brings them, whatever it costs the one who does.
- A young Tijodur rider has gone south alone to take a Khaaqidur head for his father's mound, and his mother wants him brought back before the Khaaqidur add his skull to their cups.
