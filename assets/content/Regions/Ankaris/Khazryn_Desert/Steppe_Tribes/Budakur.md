The **Budakur**, "the vulture people", are the [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the cold high steppe at the eastern end of [[place-idwkrrgn|Idvaqor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], where [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]] climbs to the western pass of [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]]. The Empire pays them to keep the pass clear, and they keep it clear. The other tribes of the eastern road call them the garrison's dogs, and the Budakur call that a description of being paid.

## The Range

The Budakur graze the high steppe below the pass in summer, with horses, camels and yaks, and drive the herds down to the wells of the stone desert in winter, when the road is shut and snow lies in the watercourses. Their chief well is [Dichul]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the silk", a stone-lined shaft in a sheltered bed below the last ridge, named for what the garrison pays in.

Their stretch of the road is the last before the Empire's. A caravan that has bought it gets water at the Budakur wells and a rider who takes it up onto the high steppe and hands it to the first Tantsou patrol, about a week out of the pass. The Budakur rider and the patrol officer know each other by name, and a caravan arriving with him is waved through with fewer questions.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The Budakur are four clans, each with its own head, its own wells and its own riders, and the dovaq speaks for the four together. When a dovaq dies, the four clan heads choose the next from among his cousins, never from his sons, so the office passes from line to line and no family keeps it.

The Empire's pay comes once a year, in silk, silver and grain, and the clan heads share it out by a count older than any of them. Each clan keeps one son at the Tantsou garrison as its surety. The boy is fed, taught the Empire's tongue and sent home when a brother or cousin comes up to take his place, and the sons who come home are the tribe's go-betweens with the pass.

The dovaq is [Budaqjilaq Budakur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "vulture-lantern", a man of sixty who spent four years at the garrison as a boy and speaks the Empire's tongue better than any rider on the road.

## Feuds

The Budakur's nearest enemies are the [[affiliation-vghlrtrb|Voghalur]] to the west, who take nothing from Tantsou and despise those who do. The two tribes mark the same well in a dry watercourse on their border, and blood has been owed both ways over it for longer than the clan heads remember. The Budakur are also at feud with the [[affiliation-khqdrtrb|Khaaqidur]] over the winter grazing on the stone desert's eastern edge, and four of the gold-rimmed skull cups at the Khaaqidur well were Budakur heads.

## The Garrison's Question {#present}

The gathering of the steppe frightens the Budakur more than any tribe on the eastern road. Their living is the Empire's pay, and a union of the tribes is the thing the pay exists to prevent. The garrison has asked Budaqjilaq which tribes have sworn to the dovaq of the northern steppe who is gathering them, and it has asked him to count the Voghalur riders who ride west to find the man's camp.

The clan heads do not agree. Two of them want to take the garrison's pay to count and to hold the pass; one of them has said in the moot that a union would pay better than the Empire and need no son at the garrison to do it. The four sureties at the garrison are the price of choosing wrong, and every clan head knows which of the four boys is his.

## Where They Come From {#history}

Tantsou has treated the tribes of its western frontier as a standing problem for centuries, giving gifts and titles to some chiefs and paying others to keep the pass clear. The Budakur are among the paid, and no Budakur elder remembers a year without the pay.

- A Budakur surety at the garrison has run, and his clan head needs him found before the officer notices that a son is missing and the clan's share of the pay with him.
- Budaqjilaq offers a party passage to the pass at half the price if it carries a count of the Voghalur riders to the patrol, and the Voghalur are watching the party at the hand-off.
