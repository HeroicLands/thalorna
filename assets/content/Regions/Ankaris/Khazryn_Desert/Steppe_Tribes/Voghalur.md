The **Voghalur**, "the wolf people", are a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] tribe of the stone desert of [[place-idwkrrgn|Idvaqor]], in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], and the hardest tribe on the eastern road. They keep the words of the three great unions by heart and say them at three standing stones at their central well. No other tribe holds that the unions were the steppe at its best, and the Voghalur hold it openly.

## The Range

The Voghalur range is the stone desert east of [[place-sowidwell|Sovid]], a flat country of black rock and pale gravel cut by dry watercourses, and their wells lie in the beds of those watercourses four and five days apart. Their central well is [Kholqon]{.name data-type="place/site"}, "the gathering", a shaft in a deep bed where three stones stand on the bank above the water, each as tall as a mounted man and each cut with the sign of one of the great banners. The tribe winters there, and the moot sits there in the autumn.

The tribe sells a stretch of [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]] east of Sovid. A caravan that has bought it gets water at the Voghalur wells, a rider, and the tribe's token for the next range. The Voghalur charge less than their neighbors and search more, and a string carrying silk for the east is searched by riders who make plain what they think of it.

## The Words at the Stones

The Voghalur keep what each of the three orghuns said to the tribes, as their elders received it, and they keep it as a witnessed word is kept, exactly or not at all. They keep no orghun's name; the steppe keeps none. The words are sayings of hardness: that a rider who sleeps under a roof forgets the sky, that a tribe paid in silk is a tribe bought, that the soft cities fell because they were soft. The stone-keeper says them at the investing of every dovaq, and a dovaq hears them with his back to the stones and the tribe in front of him.

[[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou]] gives gifts and titles to the chiefs of Idvaqor, and the Voghalur take neither. A Voghalur who wears Tantsou silk is not seated at the moot. The tribe trades with the road for iron and grain and buys nothing it cannot eat, ride or fight with.

## How the Tribe Is Led

The dovaq is [Voghalghakhiq Voghalur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "wolf-bow", a man of about forty who has held the office six years and who has never been inside a wall. The moot chooses the dovaq as every Sovidur moot does. The stone-keeper may refuse its choice, and the refusal stands: a man who has served a city, slept in a garrison or taken a gift from a customs-house is not invested at the stones.

## Feuds

The Voghalur are at blood-feud with the [[affiliation-kskrtrb|Kesukur]], who hold the names of the tribe the second orghun killed at [[place-wemaq|Vomek]] and say that the banner was a crime. The Voghalur say the same banner's words at Kholqon every autumn. Riders of the two tribes do not eat at one fire, and they keep apart at the hand-off wells under the truce.

Closer to home they quarrel with the [[affiliation-bdkrtrb|Budakur]], the tribe of the last stretch before the Tantsou pass, over a well in a dry watercourse that both tribes mark and neither holds for long. The Voghalur call the Budakur the garrison's dogs.

## The Banner {#present}

Of all the tribes on the eastern road, the Voghalur want the union most. Word that a dovaq of the northern steppe is gathering tribes by victory and by marriage reached Kholqon last autumn, and the stone-keeper said the words of the three banners at the moot that night without being asked. Voghalghakhiq has sent riders west to find the man's camp. He has not sworn, and the moot is divided on whether a tribe should swear to a man before he has a banner worth the name.

The young riders do not want to wait. They talk of riding east against the Tantsou patrols to show the man what the Voghalur are worth, and the elders know that a raid on the Empire's riders closes the pass to every caravan the tribe lives on. The Budakur, who take Tantsou's pay, are watching the Voghalur riders, and the garrison is paying them to watch.

## Where They Come From {#history}

The Voghalur hold that their dovaq swore under every one of the three banners and that the three stones mark those oaths. The tribes they rode beside tell it otherwise, and the stones carry signs and no words.

## Hooks

- Voghalghakhiq wants an outsider, who carries no tribe's quarrel, to ride west and bring back the gathering man's terms, word for word, as a witness would say them at the stones.
- A Tantsou officer at the pass wants to know which Voghalur riders are planning to strike his patrols, and he pays in silk, which no Voghalur can touch.
