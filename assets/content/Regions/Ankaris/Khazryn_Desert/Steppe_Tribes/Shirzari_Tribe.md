The **Shirzâri** hold the grass south of the [[affiliation-zrnshtrb|Zarnûsh]], and they take what the Zarnûsh cannot defend. They are the [[lore-sowidesclt|Sowides]] tribe that the others point to as raiding done as a trade: a riding party strikes a distant graze, drives off the herds and takes the people who tend them, then sends a rider with the price. A captive among the Shirzâri is fed and not harmed, and a captive whose kin cannot pay is married into the tribe.

## The Price

The Shirzâri's demand on the Zarnûsh is fifty horses, a share of the central well at Shir-Vân and permanent grazing on the southern steppe, in return for eight people taken from a southern graze. The Zarnûsh dowek has refused. The Shirzâri are in no hurry: each season the captives spend in a Shirzâri camp makes them more Shirzâri, and a price the Zarnûsh cannot pay today only rises as they have less left to lose.

## The Marriage Offer

The Shirzâri have offered a captive girl, the daughter of the Zarnûsh war captain's blood-brother, in marriage to the son of their dowek. They present it as generosity, and it is a way to end the quarrel without a ransom: the girl joins the dowek's line, and the Zarnûsh have no claim to take her back. The offer expires on a day the Shirzâri have named, and the Zarnûsh are divided on whether to accept.

## Caravans

The Shirzâri sell passage to anyone who can pay and do not sell it twice. A caravan that crosses their grass uninvited pays the price in goods, and its guards pay in the trouble of being searched. The tribe takes caravan tolls as its right and the Zarnûsh's herds as its sport.
