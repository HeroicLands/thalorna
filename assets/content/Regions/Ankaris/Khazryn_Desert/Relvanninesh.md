## Overview

Three days north-west of [[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]], where the irrigated strip of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] frays into broken ground and the last springs have failed, a low hill is crowned with something that catches the sun like a lake. It is the palace of [Nimeshun]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the "house of silver", a cadet line of the craft house of Ashkarad whose princes held a small oasis of their own and made their fortune in silvered glass: mirrors, lamp-chimneys and the clear lenses that the whole belt bought. The Tellumi call the palace **Relvanninesh**, "the princes' house". The tribes of the edge call it the Glass Princes' Palace and give it a day's ride of empty ground on every side.

The palace is built of the sand it stands on. The craft of the house was to fuse dune sand into glass, and its princes raised their walls, floors, stairs and roofs in it, green and smoke-black and, in the royal rooms, clear as water. Now the oasis is a dry hollow and the garden is a field of stumps, but the palace has not weathered a hair. The walls ring when struck. The windows hold the light.

## The Household

Twelve people live in the palace, and they have lived there for ninety years. They are the last prince, [Garmelul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, his wife, his daughter [Yelhule]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, his brother, his steward, a physician, a lamp-maker and five servants. They sit at a long table in the great hall in the clothes they wore when the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] came, and they have not aged a day. Their skin is clear and cold, with the faint green translucence of the glass the house worked. They breathe. They eat what they are given. They are polite, curious and very hungry for news.

A traveler who enters the gate is welcomed by the steward, given water to wash and a seat at the table, and asked nothing, as the law of the guest requires. The Tellumi rule of hospitality is absolute, and the house keeps it with an exactness no other house has ever managed.

## What the Tribes Say

- **The edge-tribes.** The Sovidur who graze the failed ground say the house was taken by a bargain, that the sand under it is awake and that a stranger who stays three nights does not leave with the years he came with. They bury animals that wander near.
- The [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}. The merchant tribe that took Ashkarad has a caravan road that bends three days around the hill. The tribe's old factors tell of a Teghumod column that rode up in the fifth year, rode away again at once, burned the wells and said nothing of what it found.
- **The craft quarter.** The families of Ashkarad whose grandfathers made glass for the house say the princes were good lords and bad at bargains. A woman in the quarter keeps a silvered mirror from the palace, and it shows nothing of the room it hangs in.
- **The exile house.** The [House of Ashkarad]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} in the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] is kin to the line, and does not discuss it.

:::secret {title="The east wing and the ledgers"}
The reflections in the east wing show every guest who has slept there, some nine hundred in ninety years, and the ledgers shelved in a vault under the cistern list them by name and the years each gave. The same ledgers record silver sent each midwinter to the House of Ashkarad at Amradad.
:::

:::secret {title="The bargain"}

The last prince, Garmelul, faced the loss of everything: the oasis, the palace, the line itself. Beneath the palace, in a cistern cut into the rock to hold the house's furnace-water, the sand had fused around a vein of black glass that the princes had never been able to cut. In the fifth year of [The Breaking]{.name data-type="lore/history"} he went down to it and spoke, and it answered.

Something lives under the sands, and the Tellumi who know of it call it the [Tenant]{.name data-type="being/creature"}. It is not a god, a spirit or an elemental. It is a piece of the outside, the same stuff that [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] is, brought up into the world in a seam of stone, and it has been waiting for a mouth. It gave the house what it asked. The line would survive, whole and unchanged, as long as the house was unbroken. In return the Tenant takes what the house can give, which is the one thing the house has never refused: guests.
:::

## The Table Is Laid {#present}

The household sits at table as it has for ninety years, and travelers still come up the line of glass rods: strings blown off the [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} road, the curious from Ashkarad's craft quarter, now and then a scholar. The steward welcomes each of them, and the prince asks each for news. This year there is a great deal of it: a dovaq gathering the northern tribes, a prophet the Atarzád council has not answered, an oasis at Shirvan drying from its edges. The Teghumod still bend their road three days round the hill, the edge-tribes still bury what strays near it, and the exile house still does not discuss it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The straight road.** A Teghumod caravan master loses three days to the bend round the hill on every crossing and wants to know whether it still needs keeping. The tribe's old factors will not tell him what the column of the fifth year found.
- **The kin who will not speak.** The House of Ashkarad at Amradad is kin to the line and will not discuss it. A rival house on the [Council of the Princes]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} wants to know why, and pays a party to sit at the table and come back with an answer.

:::secret {title="The east wing and the ledgers"}

- **The midwinter silver.** The ledgers under the cistern record the silver sent each midwinter to the House of Ashkarad at Amradad, whose head, [Nimeshay of Ashkarad]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, quietly funds Tellumi agitation in the held cities. A Teghumod factor who could show where the house's silver comes from would hold the exile house by its purse.

:::

## Reaching It

The palace stands on a hill in open ground. There is no shade and no water within a day. The track from Ashkarad is marked by a line of glass rods, stuck into the sand by the house's servants in the first years and never taken up. At noon the hill throws a glare that blinds.
