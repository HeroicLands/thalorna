## Overview

A day's ride north of [[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]], where the irrigated strip of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] gives out and the black rock of [[place-dkrqrrgn|Diqraqor]] begins, a bench of dark stone carries a round tower of pale limestone sixty feet high, with no door at the ground and no window in its whole height. The Tellumi call it **Tashbemsirvesh**, "the forbidden tower". The caravan guides call it the Empty Tower, though nobody has ever seen it empty.

It is a Tower of Silence. The Tellumi of Ashkarad and the farms around it have given their dead to the tower for as long as the city has had craft councils: the body carried up the stair by the Expositors, laid on the stone platform under the sky, and left to the vultures and the sun, while a Delkoshekh of [[affiliation-azravan|Azravan]] reads the Prayer of Passage and kindles the small fire on the ground below. The tower's rite is the same rite as the one at the great death-temples of Amradad, and its stair is older than the Atarzád conquest of the city.

## What Is Wrong

The platform is full. A hundred dead and more lie on it in rows, on their backs, hands crossed, in the clothes and shrouds they were laid in. They do not decay. Their skin is the color of a candle and does not flake in the wind, and the dust of the bench does not settle on them. By day the vultures, the big black _budaq_ of the road, perch along the parapet in rows from dawn to dusk. They watch the dead and they do not feed. A guide who has seen it describes it the same way every time: the birds look at the dead like men waiting to be asked.

On some nights the platform is empty. Nobody has watched it become so. A shepherd who slept at the foot of the stair swears the dead go down by the stair in order of age. A priest of Azravan who climbed it at the last dark of the moon found the stone swept clean and the rows' places marked in the dust, and he came down and has not spoken of it. The dead are back by dawn.

## What the Tellumi Say

- **The families.** Households of the Ashkarad craft quarter who gave their dead to the tower before the Breaking do not go near it, and do not say why. Their quarter keeps lamps lit on the nights of the dark moon. A household that lost a child in the siege of the city pays a Delkoshekh each year to ask the tower after the child, and the answer is always silence.
- **The Delkoshekh of Azravan.** The priests who tend the tower's rites in the city say the Tower is shut, that its Expositor is lost and that the rite cannot be performed there. They do not say the Expositor is dead. They do not give his name.
- **The Teghumod.** The merchant tribe that holds the city sends its caravans by the Empty Tower's road in daylight and turns them back at nightfall. The tribe pays no tithe to the tower and takes no tithe from it.
- **The guides.** Guides on the road north say that anything dead near the tower will not be where it was left in the morning, and they bury their own dead a day away.

## The Tower

The tower has one entrance, a stone stair on the outside, narrow and without rail, that climbs to a door of bronze at the height of three men. Below the platform are a ring of chambers where the Expositors lived and kept the rite's records, and below those a round pit, the **well of bones**, where what the birds leave is swept after a year. The tower stands on a stone shelf, with a small enclosure on the east side where the mourners' fire is kindled.

The Shadow works through the opening that was made, as it works wherever a spirit is bound back into the flesh it has left; the Tower's dead are held in their bodies, and the undead of every land stem from the same corruption. Nothing about the Empty Tower is natural, and no one who has seen the Expositor's work believes that it was done for any reason but grief.

## Reaching It

The tower is reached by a faint track from Ashkarad's north gate, a day's ride through bare stone, with no well on the way. A party mounted on camels does it between dawn and dusk. A party on foot sleeps on the road, and the road is not where to sleep. The Teghumod stations a patrol at the first marker-stone, a day's ride short of the tower, whose captain turns back anyone without a caravan's license to pass.
