## Overview

For most of the year **Beit-Shōfár** is a settlement of 3,000 at the foot of [[place-mountshofar|Mt. Shōfar]]. At the summer festival it is a camp of fifty thousand. The priestly seat of the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Ātárzád]] holds the Council of the Twelve, the common treasury and the Unconsuming Flame, and every adult of the Flame-born is expected to make the pilgrimage here at least once before death. Many come every year.

## The Flame

The Unconsuming Flame burns on the mountain, above the place where Mūshárā, who gave the Covenant on this mountain, died on its slope without entering the land he had promised his people. His tomb is the second-holiest site of the Ātárzád, after the Flame itself. The Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame, the Tōqēl, is the senior religious authority of the tribes. By custom he is always a Khorshad, the tribe that holds the central highlands and the slopes of Mt. Shōfar, always elderly, and raised only when the previous Keeper dies. The present Keeper is Aspánsár son of Yáhōshí, about eighty winters old, who is widely held to have spoken with Ātáröš in dreams.

The Festival of the Promise is celebrated three times a year, at the spring equinox, at midsummer and at the autumn equinox. The summer festival draws upward of fifty thousand pilgrims, who camp in the valleys around the mountain and gather at the Flame for the recitation of the Covenant. The temples of the Ātárzád are aniconic: only the Flame stands for Ātáröš, and no image may be made of him.

## Who Governs

The Ātárzád have no king; the prohibition was given at this mountain, and attempts to raise one have ended in repudiation. The Council of the Twelve meets at Beit-Shōfár once a year for a six-week session. Each tribe sends its hereditary chieftain, the Rēzhul, with the senior elders of its principal lineages and its senior priest. The Council sets common policy on defense, taxation, disputed territory and inter-tribal quarrels, and it renews the Covenant. Each tribe raises its own contribution and presents it at the session; the Council keeps a modest standing treasury here. In a crisis the Council may acclaim a Dūkhen, a judge with broad temporary authority, chosen after consultation with the Keeper. Twenty-three have served since the Conquest, and none holds the office now.
