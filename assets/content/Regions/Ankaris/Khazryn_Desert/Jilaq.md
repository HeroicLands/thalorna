## Overview

A caravan sees Jilaq two nights before it will be nearest to it, and it sees it lit. On the horizon to the north, low and steady, a row of yellow points stands over the dunes, as even as a necklace, and the camels lift their heads toward it. The guide does not turn the string. He has already turned it. The road leaves its line at the last cairn and swings south, and it stays south for three days, and nobody who has made the crossing before asks why.

**Jilaq**, "the lantern" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sowides]], was the chief city of [[lore-towercities|the Tower League]]: a ring wall of baked brick a mile across, a dry harbor of caravan yards, the league's counting-house, and a dozen light-towers, the tallest two hundred feet. The towers still stand. The lamp-chambers are black and empty, and on most nights nothing burns in them that a person could carry a flame to.

## How It Was Made

About 190 BF the first of the great unions forced the tribes together under a single _orqwen_, and the cities of the league were asked for tribute. Jilaq refused, as the league's charter said it must, and the host sat down before the ring wall for a season. When the wall was opened, the city was killed in a day and a night: the factors of a dozen peoples, the yard-hands, the lamp-keepers, the families of all of them. The union's riders took what they could carry and rode out. They did not lay the dead out, and no tribe's shaman said a name over any of them, because the city's dead were strangers to every rite the riders kept. The dead of the Tower League had no rite that anyone alive could perform for them, and the tribes the _orqwen_ led have been walking round the result ever since.

## What Comes Back

Jilaq is a recurrence, and it is thin.

On the anniversary of the sack, and on still nights through the dry months, the towers light themselves. The lamp-chambers fill with a steady light, bells ring in the yards though every bell was carried off or melted, and long ranks of figures can be seen at a distance in the harbor court, forming and breaking, never nearer than the length of a bowshot.

That is the part a traveler sees from the road. The rest is closer. The hunters the steppe tribes call [[being-yiqnotud|Yiqnotud]] gather where unreceived dead are, and Jilaq has held thousands of them for two thousand years. On any night of the year they cross onto the ring wall's ground in a form half made of the place: tall, wet-looking shapes that move between the towers and that can be touched, and that touch back. A man who camps within a mile of the wall is found in the morning with the sand around him scored in wide arcs and his face turned toward the lit towers. Tribes who graze the southern ranges lose a camel or two a decade to it, and a rider every generation or so.

## How the Road Lives with It

- **The road goes round.** The Celestial Road leaves its old line three days south of the city. The detour is worn deep and every tribe whose range it crosses charges for it.
- **Riders turn caravans back.** The tribes of the south edge of the Hosikor keep riders at the last cairn. A caravan master who asks about the short way is told once, by a dowek's man with a hand on his bridle, and a caravan that goes on is not followed.
- **A lamp at the foot.** On the night of the sack the herders who water at the nearest wells carry a lamp to the base of the fallen light-tower that stands along the road and leave it burning. They do not go nearer than that.
- **The Ruweles will not guide.** A hidden-water band sells a path across the empty ground south of the wall and will not sell one north of it, at any price.

## The Counting-House

The league kept its seal-rolls and ledgers in the counting-house at the foot of the tallest tower, and a ledger that shows a line of names also shows, in the same hand, every factor, clerk and yard-keeper who drew a wage in the city. The Sowides lay a dead person to the four winds by speaking a name. Nobody has tried to speak a city's worth of written names to the winds.

The same vaults hold the league's tower-glass: ground lenses from the lamp-chambers, clear as water and nearly flawless, stored in straw-lined chests. One lens is worth a caravan.

## Hooks

- **The factor's lens.** A Byzarian factor at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qìso]] will pay a fortune for one lens of tower-glass and will not say what the glass is for. He has hired two parties already, and neither came back to Qìso.
- **The counting-house names.** A ranger of the [[affiliation-sahritrb|Sahri]] with a bad conscience wants the ledgers brought out whole. He means to read the names to the four winds on the anniversary night, from the wall, and the nearest tribe has told him it will not stop him and will not bury him either.
- **The rider who stayed.** A caravan guide of the south ranges says he saw his brother, dead nine years, standing at the ring wall in the lit harbor court. He wants someone to go and tell him it was only the field. He wants someone to bring him a lens to prove it.
