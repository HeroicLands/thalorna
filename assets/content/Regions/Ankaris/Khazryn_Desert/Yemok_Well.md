A line of tamarisks marks the place, the first trees in a week and the last for a month. Under them a stone trough, long enough to water forty camels, stands beside a well-head carved with a dozen signs. A caravan arrives at Yemok in the afternoon, makes camp, and spends the evening in a ceremony that costs the master a month's profit and that every master considers money well spent.

**Yemok**, "the oath" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sowides]], is the first of two hand-off wells on the [[lore-celestialroad|Celestial Road]]. It lies about 550 miles from the March and about thirty days out, where [[place-wlqtrrgn|Welqator]] gives way to [[place-hskrrgn|Hosikor]]. East of it the road crosses the Long Dry, five days without a well.

## The Hand-off

A caravan buys passage a tribe at a time, and each tribe's rider speaks for it across his tribe's range. The riders of the Western Margin bring the caravan to Yemok. There the master shows his string of tokens, one for each range crossed, each cut with a tribe's sign, to the riders of the sands who wait at the trough. They read the tokens and tell him whether the caravan has kept the road-peace. If they find it so, they take it on. The master pays again, in goods or in the credit of his backing house, and the Margin's riders turn back with their pay and a witness's word that the caravan was handed over whole. Both sets of riders swear to it on the well-head stone, and the oath is why the well has its name.

The riders of the sands do not take a caravan on without the token. A caravan that has lost its tokens, or whose tokens were not earned, waits at Yemok until the riders' tribes agree what to do with it. That can take a day or a season.

The well is neutral ground. A truce binds every tribe whose range touches it, no blood is shed within sight of the tamarisks, and a feud between two tribes is suspended while their riders sit at the same trough. The tribes keep the truce because the well pays all of them: each takes a share of every toll the master pays there.

## The Water

The well is a stepped shaft a hundred and twenty feet deep, cut in an age before any tribe, with a stair cut in its wall for the bucket-men. It yields enough sweet water to fill a caravan's skins, and the price per skin is the second highest on the road. The camels drink their fill; the horses are watered last and carried through the Long Dry on skins.

## Dangers

The truce binds tribesmen and not strangers, and a caravan master who draws a blade at Yemok is dead before he sheathes it. Giant camel spiders live under the trough-stones, and the bucket-men have a rhyme about the hour to draw. In summer, a dust storm from the south will bury the tamarisks to the lowest branches in an afternoon.

## Hooks

- A caravan arrives with tokens from tribes that were not on its road, and its master swears he was robbed.
- The riders of the sands are demanding a larger share of the toll and are holding a string at the well until they are paid. The Margin's riders will not turn back until their own share is settled.
- A rider of the Margin has been found dead in the shade of the tamarisks, a token in his fist that belongs to a tribe that left the truce years ago.
