For eleven months the place is a ring of black stones round a deep well, a few hundred yards of trampled gravel, and a scatter of bones. Then the spring fleet comes in from the west and the autumn fleet from the east, and in ten days the empty ground becomes a city of felt tents and rope corrals that holds fifteen thousand people, four thousand camels and every kind of silver anyone has ever struck.

**Qiiso**, "the price" in [[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]], is the market-camp of the central [[place-hskrrgn|Ghoshiqor]], about halfway across the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]]. [[lore-celestialroad|The Celestial Road]] reaches it on about the sixty-fifth day out from the [[place-eastrnmrch|March]], and the southern spur to the [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] leaves it here. Most goods that cross the waste change hands at Qiiso instead of going on whole, and the price of water is the highest on the road.

## Layers {#history}

The camp meets at the deepest well on the road, and nobody on the road says who cut it. About 548 AF [[lore-thrdorqwnq|the third great union]] rode south down the spur that leaves Qiiso and killed everyone at the autumn fair of [[place-qimod|Qiimod]], and the spur has bent round that town ever since.

## The Camp

The camp has no wall and no houses. Its heart is the well, the deepest on the road, lidded in stone and watched day and night by riders of the tribe that holds its water; round it, in rings, lie the tents. Western factors pitch on the west side, Tantsou and Vedyari on the east, the Tellumi and Atarzád houses on the south, and the furs of the north come in on the north. Tribesmen camp in the gaps. A trader finds his own people by the color of the cloth over the tent-pole.

For ten days the camp is the busiest place for a thousand miles in any direction. Silk and tea are weighed against silver, porcelain is unpacked and counted shard by shard, horses are sold by the string, and spices and medicines pass through a dozen hands before they leave. Camels that crossed from the west are sold for camels that will cross to the west. A caravan from the March sells its cargo here, loads Tantsou silk bought from an eastern string and turns back, with its master having crossed only half the road.

## Who Keeps It

No polity does. The tribes whose ranges meet here hold a moot, the juchon, before each season. Their chiefs, the dovaq, swear a peace on the well-stone, and the peace holds for the ten days of the market. In the camp no blood is shed, no weapon is drawn, and a quarrel is carried to the moot and priced. A man who breaks the peace is killed outside the ring by the tribe he wronged, and the tribe that holds his water pays the blood-price.

The moot sells everything it can: stands, water, grazing for the beasts, the services of its riders, and its word. Its clerks keep tally with knotted cords and remember more than any ledger holds.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The moot meets before each season, and its dovaq swear the peace on the well-stone as they always have. Word has reached the camp of a dovaq gathering the northern tribes, and at the fires the factors of both ends of the road ask what a union would make of the market, since the first great union began by demanding tribute for the road.

## Dangers

Thieves work the camp, and the moot's law is severe: a man caught stealing a camel loses his hands. Giant camel spiders feed in the refuse pits at the rim, and a careless trader walking out to relieve himself in the dark has not always come back. The two blood-fields near the road keep every sensible caravan on the track as it approaches and as it leaves.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- A Bizarian factor and a Tantsou factor each believe the other has bought the entire spring supply of a single dye. Each approaches the party with a request for a quiet look at the other's baggage.
- A dovaq of the moot is dying, and two of his sons are bidding against each other for the right to speak for his tribe.
- A well-rider has been found dead in the ring, and the moot must find the killer before the peace breaks and the market empties.
