In the last week before the melt, every household in a Tellumi oasis sends someone down into the dry channels with a basket and a short hoe, and the water-master walks the bank behind them with a tablet, marking off each family's stretch as it is cleared. A week or two later the snowmelt comes out of the mountains brown and cold, the sluices open in the order the tablets give, and for the rest of the year a field is worth the hours of water it is owed. That is the Tellumel as its people know it: a country where every hour of water is written down, and where two peoples argue over who owns the water.

## Overview

**The Tellumel**, "the land of the Tellumi", is the irrigated strip along the north foot of [[place-graznmntns|the Grazian Mountains]], on the southern edge of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]]. It is forty to eighty miles deep where the snowmelt streams reach it and broken where they do not, and it runs about seven hundred miles from [[place-wstrndscnt|the Western Descent]] in the west to the foot of [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]]. Every city of the Tellumi stands in it. North of it the black rock of [[place-dkrqrrgn|Diqraqor]] begins within a day's ride, and beyond that the sand.

About 1,800,000 people live in the strip, most of the region's people on a small fraction of its ground, and two peoples share it while each claims the whole. The [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Atarzád]] hold the four great cities, the [[place-oasishirvn|Oasis of Shirvan]], [[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]], [[place-zargandur|Zargandur]] and [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], together with the university town of [[place-caldar|Kaldar]], the pastures between them and most of the lesser oases. The [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi Confederation]] rules three oases at the edges: [[place-mehrnagord|Mernagord]] at the western end, [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]] on the dry northern rim and [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-ber]] at the eastern end. Most Tellumi live in neither kind of place. They live in the quarters and canal villages of the four held cities, and they keep the water running there.

The tribes of the open waste regard the strip as a foreign country, and they raid its outer gardens when they dare.

## Land and Water

The Grazian wall catches the snow, and how much water a stretch of the belt gets depends on what the wall above it is like. From west to east that makes three kinds of country.

- **The western end.** Above Mernagord the range is coming apart into the brown foothills of the Descent, and its snow lies only a few weeks. The watercourses run in spring and are dry by early summer, so the Tellumi catch the flood on stone terraces and check-dams and lead what soaks in through buried channels to springs at the foot of the hills. The water is short but steady, because it comes out of the ground.
- **The middle belt.** From Shirvan to Kethramír the wall is high and the belt is at its deepest. Snowmelt rivers come out of the foothills in spring and fall to a trickle by the end of summer, and weirs turn them into canals. The great springs run all year, the spring under the [[place-vurinkarvem|Vurinkarvem]] cliff at Shirvan the greatest of them. Where neither reaches, as at Ashkarad, shafts and long sloping tunnels bring groundwater out from under the foothills, and Kethramír's three deep wells have never failed.
- **The eastern reach.** East of Kethramír the belt narrows against the foot of the Eastern Reach and breaks into separate valleys. Their streams come off glaciers, so they run highest at midsummer, when the western rivers are failing, and in a hot year they flood.

On the north side every channel gives out sooner or later, and the Tellumi call the line where the last one ends the rim. They do not farm beyond it. Past the rim lies the stone of Diqraqor, where the streams that never reach the belt sink into the plateau, and the only water belongs to the upland tribes who watch it from the ravine edges.

## Water Law

The Tellumi law of water is older than the conquest, and it runs in all seven cities, because the people who keep the channels are Tellumi in all of them. Its rules are few and every Tellumi child knows them.

- **Water is owned in hours.** A household owns a number of hours in a named channel on given days, written on its tablets as a deed, and the right passes with the deed. Hours can be sold, pledged or split into halves and quarters (_ninsut_ and _tunsut_). A field with no hours is a field with no water.
- **Every channel has a keeper.** A water-master opens and shuts the sluices in the order of the ledger, from the head of the channel to its tail, and keeps the ledger beside the households' tablets.
- **Whoever drinks clears.** Before the melt every household clears its stretch of channel, in turns recorded on the tablets, and a household that misses its turn loses its hours for the season.
- **Waste is paid for.** A household or a workshop that lets water run past its hours pays the fields below it, at a rate the keeper sets.
- **In a short year every hour is cut alike.** When the river falls the keeper shortens every hour on the channel by the same share, the prince's hours included.
- **The tablet decides.** A dispute is settled by reading the parties' tablets aloud and sending for an older one from the archive. An authority with no tablet in the matter has no standing in it.

The Atarzád lay their own claim over that law. The [Promise of the Tellumel]{.name data-type="lore"} gives them the land below [[place-mountshofar|Mount Shofar]] as far as the Grazian wall, and in their reckoning the water comes with the land. In each held city the tribe that holds it levies a water-tax at the basin gate or the weir, in coin or in kind. Its magistrate hears any water dispute that touches an Atarzád household, and in a dry year it sets its own order: the garrison and the herds first, the settlers' fields next and the Tellumi plots last. Quarrels over water between tribes go to the [Council of the Twelve]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, which settles the boundaries of the irrigated districts at its yearly session in [[place-beitshofar|Beit-Shofár]].

The two laws meet at every sluice. The Tellumi water-masters keep the ledgers of all four held cities because nobody else can read them, and the order they open the sluices in is the order the tribe has set.

:::secret {title="The second ledger"}
In every held city the water-masters keep two ledgers, the one the assessors see and the one the quarter drinks by. The second carries hours the tax has never measured: channels recut and never recorded, deeds the Breaking was supposed to have burned, and night hours on the weirs. In a dry year the Atarzád order is kept to the letter where the pickets can watch, and the masters make up the Tellumi plots' share at night from channels the assessors do not know exist. Shirvan's masters are the boldest at it and Zargandur's the most careful, because Zargandur hangs a man for opening a sluice out of turn.
:::

## Tellumi Ground and Atarzád Ground

No wall divides the two peoples' ground, and on a map it looks like a scatter. On the ground the rule is simple: **a field belongs to whoever holds the water it drinks.** A canal village whose channel runs from Shirvan's basin pays Shirvan's water-tax and answers to the tribe that holds Shirvan, whatever tongue its people speak. A garden on a terrace that drinks from Mernagord's buried channels is Tellumi ground, however close to Shirvan it lies. The last field on a channel is the border, and everyone in the belt knows which channel each village drinks from.

Atarzád ground is the larger. It takes in the four held cities and Kaldar with their irrigated plains, the lesser oases between them that the seven smaller tribes hold, and the open pastures between the oases where the Atarzád raise the long-legged desert horse. The [Zhoqarod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} graze the great pasture north of Zargandur, and in the east the [Tozhirod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} hold the oases beyond Kethramír. Above the belt Atarzád ground runs onto the plateau. The [Burqalod]{.name data-type="affiliation"} hold the highland round Mount Shofar, and Atarzád flocks graze the southern slopes of Diqraqor, where the line with the upland tribes runs from well to well.

Tellumi ground is the three free oases. Mernagord's runs from the foot of the Descent and the salt pans east to its last terraced watercourse, a day short of the first village that drinks from Shirvan. Vahurdash holds one channel on the rim and pays a quiet tribute in dates to keep it. Ushtra-ber holds its valley to the head of its glacier stream, a long way from any Atarzád field. Together they hold about a quarter of a million people. The rest of the Confederation's 800,000 live on Atarzád ground, in the quarters of the held cities, where a Delkoshekh's court sits under the tribe's magistrate, and in the canal villages that feed those cities.

The line moves when the water moves. An Atarzád settler family that cuts a new channel past the last Tellumi field has moved it, and the raids and reprisals that follow are the commonest form of the feud between the two peoples. A Tellumi oasis whose spring fails loses its ground the same way, and the reopened channel at Vahurdash is the one place the Tellumi have taken ground back.

## Current Situation {#present}

The feud between the two peoples is in a quiet phase, and the quiet is wearing thin. The last full rising, in the Shirvan district about 706, was put down by the last Zhudek the Council of the Twelve acclaimed, and the next is held overdue.

- **In the held cities,** [Aqeshat]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the Prophet preaches the Tellumi out of the quarters and her following grows. At Shirvan the great spring is falling, and the Ozhmarod fire-wardens blame the quarter.
- **In the west,** [[being-hemvanaymern|Hemvanay of Mernagord]] has left an Atarzád offer of a trade compact unanswered, and the exile houses call the idea treason.
- **In Amradad,** [[being-gureshayzarg|Gureshay of Zargandur]] trains for a reclamation that the Council of the Princes neither endorses nor forbids.
- **On the rim,** the revived house at Vahurdash cannot prove its descent, and its rivals say an acclamation cannot make a prince.
- **Above the belt,** word has come from the steppe of a union rising in the north, and the upland tribes take stragglers off the pilgrim tracks to Mount Shofar.

## Roads

Long-distance traffic comes into the belt at four places and moves along it on one road.

**The belt road.** The Tellumi call the road that joins their cities [Nivuntarlim]{.name data-type="place"}, "the road of the seven", and it runs the length of the strip from Mernagord to Ushtra-ber, along the canal banks and between the walls of gardens. It is a road for the one-humped camel, the horse and the donkey; carts use it only between a city and its own fields. Every held city tolls it at its gates, and the tribes of the lesser oases toll it between them.

| Stage                   | Days | The ground                                                                     |
| ----------------------- | ---: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mernagord to Shirvan    |    4 | terraced oasis country, then the first of Shirvan's canal villages             |
| Shirvan to Ashkarad     |    4 | the largest date groves in the belt, then open pasture                         |
| Ashkarad to Zargandur   |    3 | canal country, and the weirs on Zargandur's river                              |
| Zargandur to Kaldar     |    2 | horse-pasture and irrigated grain                                              |
| Kaldar to Kethramír     |    2 | the copper foothills on the south side of the road                             |
| Kethramír to Ushtra-ber |   15 | the eastern reach: one valley after another, with long dry stages between them |

The days are a loaded camel's; a rider on a good horse takes about half.

**The spur from the north.** The southern spur of [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Road]] leaves [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] and crosses Diqraqor in about twenty days. At the plateau's southern edge it divides. The main string goes down to Kethramír, where the spur ends and the trade of the interior meets the trade of the belt. A western track keeps to the edge of the uplands for several days and comes down to the gates of Ashkarad, which the [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the merchant tribe, hold and toll.

**The passes to the south.** The Grazian passes come down into the belt behind its cities, and they are open only a few weeks at the end of summer. [[place-meghadvar|Meghadvar]], the westernmost crossing of the high wall, comes down among the western cities thirty days from the Vedyari marches. [[place-gudesroad|The Guides' Road]] comes down somewhere east of it, forty-five days from the far side. [[place-suryadvar|Suryadvar]]'s northern branch reaches the middle belt thirty days from its fork, and [[place-tamradvar|Tamradvar]] comes over the shoulder of the Eastern Reach into the marches beyond the belt's eastern end. The guides who keep the crossings, [[affiliation-osketguides|the Osket]] in the west and [[affiliation-hvarnguides|the Hvarn]] in the east, take light goods only and charge for them by weight.

**The way west.** At the western end the road leaves Mernagord and goes round the end of the range by the Western Descent, the one stretch of the Grazian wall a caravan crosses without a guide. Beyond it lie the oases of the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]] and the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], the belt's chief market and the home of the Tellumi houses in exile, and [[place-marchroad|the March Road]] east into the Vedyari marches.

**The pilgrim roads.** Three times a year the belt road fills with Atarzád families on their way to Mount Shofar for [The Festival of the Promise]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and pilgrim tracks climb to Beit-Shofár from Kethramír, three days, and from the first of the eastern valleys. The Burqalod ride those tracks at festival time.

## The Western End

The western end is the oldest Tellumi ground and the quietest. Mernagord stands where the last foothills come down to the belt and the road over the Descent ends, and its oasis country holds the salt pans on a dry lake-bed three hours west of the walls. Caravans from the west reach it without a guide and leave with salt, dates and hard cheese. Above it the older Tellumi genealogies root each princely house in a named peak of the Descent, and most of those peaks have not been climbed by anyone of the house in living memory.

The tribe that holds Shirvan sits a few days east and could take Mernagord in a season. It does not, because a free Tellumi city is more useful to the Atarzád than a conquered one: its prince keeps a channel open to Amradad, and its salt reaches markets theirs cannot. The danger at this end comes from the other side. When the winter is hard in Dunhara, the Dunhari tribes come east over the Descent in numbers, and a hungry tribe does not ask whose water it is.

## The Eastern Reach

East of Kethramír the belt stops being a belt. It becomes a chain of valleys under the Eastern Reach, each with its stream, its terraces and its village, divided from the next by a day or two of dry stony ground. The Tozhirod hold the nearer valleys and leave them to their village elders. Mount Shofar stands on the northern skyline above the first of them, black over the mesas, and the pilgrim tracks climb toward it.

Ushtra-ber is the last valley and the last city, still under its own house because the road to it is long, its valley is easy to hold and the Tozhirod find its archive more interesting than its land. Beyond it the ground rises into [[place-idwkrrgn|Idvaqor]], the stone desert, and a caravan bound for the frontier of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] takes on a guide at Ushtra-ber who knows which tribe holds which well. The Hvarn come down from the Reach every autumn to buy grain, and snow shuts the valley road from late autumn to the thaw.

## The Year

The belt keeps two calendars, the Tellumi year that opens at the spring equinox and the Atarzád round of three festivals at Mount Shofar, and both follow the water.

A bad year in the belt is a short melt. The rivers come down late or thin, the keepers cut every hour on the Tellumi channels, the held cities cut the Tellumi plots, and by midsummer the quarrels at the sluices are the business of every magistrate from Shirvan to Kethramír.

## Trade

The held cities export dates, palm-sugar, leather, salt from the western pans, and the great Khazryn horses, the long-legged desert breed that the Atarzád raise on the pastures between the oases and that is prized across the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] for endurance. They import metals, fine cloth, glass, books and the rarer goods of the wider world. The Teghumod merchant-houses run the caravans: west by the Descent to Amradad and the Taruzelian ports, north up the spur, and south to the foot of the passes.

In the free oases the economy is the oasis economy it has always been: dates, palm-sugar, garden produce, Mernagord's salt, and trade with passing caravans. The free oases are small, their surpluses are modest, and their people reckon their wealth in continuity.

## Hooks

- **The new channel.** An Atarzád settler family has cut a channel past the last of Mernagord's terraces, and a Tellumi village now drinks from it. The prince wants the tablets that show which water the village drank before; the tribe that holds Shirvan wants the village. Both will pay a party that can find the tablets first.
- **The short melt.** The river at Zargandur has come down thin, and the dry-year order cuts the Tellumi plots. A water-master's apprentice offers a party good money to stand at a weir on the night the second ledger says it opens.
- **The pilgrim string.** An Atarzád family from Shirvan needs escort to Mount Shofar for midsummer, and the upland tribes have been taking stragglers off the pilgrim tracks.
- **The last valley.** A caravan for Tantsou has reached Ushtra-ber in late autumn, and its master wants to go on before the snow. The guide he has hired has a feud in the Hvarn villages above the valley.
