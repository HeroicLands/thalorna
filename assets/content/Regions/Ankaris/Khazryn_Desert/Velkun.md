## Overview

The people who live in [[place-vahurdash|Vahúrdash]] walk on Velkun every day. The settlement of four thousand is a small thing set down in the northern corner of a much larger plan, and what the sand has not taken of the old city surrounds it on three sides: a grid of streets forty feet wide, houses without roofs, courtyards with the stumps of palms in them, and at the dry rim, where the belt's last streams fail, a long wall of fitted stone with the sand banked to its parapet. This is the fifth city of the Tellumi, in the dry northern rim of the belt, the place where the streams ran out.

The Tellumi call the ruin Velkun, the lost town, to keep it apart from the living settlement that bears the old name. The clay archive keeps its wells, its debts and its dead in the records of the houses that left it.

## What the Tellumi Believe

A drought took it, and the Tellumi who tell the story do not say a drought only. They say the springs failed because the city was judged. The princes of the house that ruled it had broken their guest-right and the water left; the people who stayed learned the rest by the empty wells. The house that has reclaimed the ruins hears the story as an insult and counters it with another, that the drought was a trial and that returning is the answer.

## Danger

The danger is the ground. The old cellars run under the new settlement and under the open streets alike, and a floor that has held for five centuries gives way beneath a man's weight and drops him into a vault with a roof of cracked stone over him. Giant Camel Spiders nest in the vaults and come up at dusk to hunt the settlement's goats and sometimes its children; the people of Vahúrdash keep their cellar doors barred. Beyond the settlement, at the foot of the great wall, the sand moves, and a street open at noon may be closed by dusk.

## Treasure

The temple quarter is the prize: courses of carved stone, columns with their lettering still sharp, and the foundation of a great hall under the sand. The stones are the only pre-drought work in the belt that has not been quarried by a later house. Below the quarter, in the cellars of the record-keepers, is the thing the house of Vahúrdash wants most, the clay tablets of the old archive.

## A Hook

The revived house of Vahúrdash holds its title by acclamation, and the exiled houses at Amradad say that is not a title. The old archive holds the genealogies that would settle it. The house will pay a party to enter the cellars of the record-keepers' quarter and bring the tablets out unbroken, and a rival house in exile has made the same offer for the opposite purpose.
