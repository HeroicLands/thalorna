## Overview {#overview}

Kelod is a hole in the gravel the width of a market square, and from the rim you see only the first dozen flights of steps. They go down in square turns, wall-hugging, each flight a little narrower than the last, until the dark takes them. The stair is cut from the living rock and is wide enough for two loaded camels abreast, which is to say it was cut for work rather than ceremony; the steps are worn to a dish in the middle by feet that came every day for centuries.

[[lore-basinbldrs|The Basin Builders]] dug it, down through three hundred feet of gravel and clay to the water table that fed their lake. The lake fell, the water table fell after it, and today the lowest landing, which was once a pool, is dry stone with a drain at its center. The tribes of the [[place-hskrrgn|Ghoshiqor]], the sand range, camp at the rim in spring, when the stair gives off a cold air that smells of wet rock, and let nothing near the edge.

## What the Tribes Believe

The tribes say a woman lives at the bottom, the well-mother, who was the keeper of the water when the builders still had it and has kept it since. She lets one traveler in a year come down and drink; the rest are turned back. Parents tell children that those she turns back do not climb out. Offerings are thrown in from the rim on the first night of every spring: coins, bread, a lock of hair, a knife, a bracelet. The tribes have thrown them for as long as there have been tribes.

## Danger

The fall is the first danger. The steps below the hundredth are slick with a century of seep, the rail is the rock itself, and a loaded man who slips takes a stair-length of corners on his way down. The air thins past two hundred feet and a lamp burns low. At the bottom lives what the tribes mean by the well-mother: a long-bodied ambusher the length of a camel, pale and blind, that lies in the drain and takes whatever comes within reach, as it has taken a child lowered on a rope and an unwary bucket. It does not follow up the stair. Everything that has met it below the hundred and fiftieth flight has lost a limb or its life.

## Treasure

Three thousand years of offerings lie at the bottom, drifted in the drain and trodden into the dust: coin of every mint that ever traded in the waste, jewelry, blades, small bronzes. The deepest layer is older than the tribes and holds the builders' own votives, tiny figures of baked clay in a posture the tribes will not describe. A party that reaches the landing can fill a saddlebag.

## The Dry Year {#present}

A hard drought has dried every ordinary well within two days of Kelod. The tribe that holds the nearest ground wants the stair cleared on the chance it holds water again, and none of its own people will go down. Its elders are paying three times a caravan guard's wage for strangers who will, and they say the stair is empty. The offerings thrown in this morning lie on the upper landings, and the rope on the rim ends clean through.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **Three times a guard's wage.** The tribe's elders want the stair cleared to the bottom and pay strangers to do it, and they tell the party the stair is empty.
- **The cut rope.** Someone went down on the rope at the rim before the party came. The elders do not say who cut it, or who was on the end of it.
