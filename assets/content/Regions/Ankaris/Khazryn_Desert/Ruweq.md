## Overview

In the Idwakor, the east range, a line of low ridges stands out of the stone desert like the backs of kneeling animals. They are the Hollow Hills, and the wind gives them their name: at dusk, when the day's heat goes out of the rock, the air rushes through openings in the ridge flanks and the whole range moans on a single low note, audible a day's ride away. The tribes call the sound the breathing of the country, and the ridges are its lungs.

Behind the openings are caverns, a connected run of them under seven miles of ridge, most of them natural but all of them worked. Floors have been leveled, passages squared and the walls dressed smooth in places, and at intervals cisterns have been cut into the rock and still hold water, cold and sweet, at the end of the driest stretch of the eastern road. The caverns have never been mapped. A script is painted over their walls in red and white pigment, line upon line in a hand that never changes, with no one left to read it.

## What the Tribes Believe

The tribes of the Idwakor hold the caverns as their own country's lungs and bar them to everyone. A man who enters is said to take a breath the country needs, and a tribe that lets strangers in is said to have sold its own air. Watchers sit at the larger openings, and a stranger who comes down the ridge is turned off by two riders with arrows nocked, the way one turns off a lost animal. The tribes have said these things for as long as anyone remembers, and none of them says who made the caverns or why.

## Danger

The caverns are deep and have a way of turning a party round. Past the first few hundred yards the passages branch without pattern, and a lamp's light gives out in halls that are too large for it. A Sand Wurm lairs in the lower caverns, the largest of the tunnelers of the eastern waste, and it hunts by the tremor of feet; a party that runs wakes it. The Earthreaver, a tunneling dreadspawn, has opened passages of its own into the older ones, and its work can be told from the original by the ragged, spiraling cut.

## Treasure

Water is the treasure. The cisterns are the best in the Idwakor, and a tribe that held them could hold the road. Beyond water are the paintings, which are in better condition than any painted wall in the waste, and are worth to a scholar more than any quantity of silver. No one has taken so much as a handful of dust out of the Hollow Hills in living memory, and the tribes say that no one has been allowed to.

## A Hook

A Tānvüri scholar at the western passes has seen a copy of a single painted line, passed from hand to hand across five years and half the desert, and wants the rest. He will pay a fortune, and the tribes of the Idwakor have named their own price, which is not silver. They want a thing brought out of Tānvür, and they will not say what.
