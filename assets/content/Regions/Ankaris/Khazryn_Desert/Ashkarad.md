Ashkarad emerges from the Khazryn Desert as a jewel of cultivation and craft, sustained by deep aquifers that the city's inhabitants have tapped and managed for generations beyond memory. The town is renowned not for trade or military prowess, but for the generations of artisans who have established workshops and passed their skills to their children and grandchildren—metalworkers, leatherworkers, potters, and weavers whose names have become synonymous with quality across the desert and beyond. The workshops of Ashkarad are as much temples to their crafts as the shrines of the Āsháian pantheon that crown the city's central plaza.

The social structure of Ashkarad centers upon these craft families. Each major craft tradition is governed by a council of master artisans who control apprenticeships, materials, and pricing with meticulous care. A young person born into a metalworking family expects to spend decades learning the trade, and only after years of proven skill may they establish their own workshop and train apprentices. This system has preserved and deepened knowledge across centuries—Ashkarad's finest pieces represent the accumulated wisdom of countless generations of refinement.

The desert itself shapes Ashkarad's character—life here is precious and fragile, dependent upon water, shade, and the preservation of knowledge. The city's inhabitants view their craft traditions as acts of defiance against the desert's indifference. Markets are ordered and serene, water is conserved with religious fervor, and the temples stand as monuments to permanence in a landscape of endless dunes. Ashkarad's rulers are not warlords or merchant princes, but the councils of master craftspeople who ensure the oasis remains stable and knowledge is transmitted intact to the next generation.
