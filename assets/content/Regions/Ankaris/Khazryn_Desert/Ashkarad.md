## Overview

**Ashkarad** is the second of the seven Tellumi cities, in the heart of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]], and the one the rest of the belt buys its tools, harness and glazed jars from. About thirty-eight thousand people live inside the walls, and the workshops outnumber the houses in the old quarter, where every street takes its name from the craft that fills it. Since the Breaking the city has belonged to the **Ardashír**, the merchant tribe of the Ātárzád, who hold its gates, the point where the caravan road from the steppe comes into the belt, and its caravans. The craft families still hold the workshops, and the councils that govern each craft still meet.

## Water

Ashkarad has no great spring. Its water comes from deep aquifers that the Tellumi tapped in the first centuries of the city, through a network of shafts and long sloping tunnels that carry groundwater from the foothills to the canals under the streets. A shaft is a family possession. The same households have cleared the same tunnels for twenty generations, and the saying in the quarter is that a stranger who learns the way into one has learned more than the family would like. In the workshops the water is used three times: for the cooling vats and the dye tubs, then for the pottery clay, and last to the gardens. A craft council that wastes water pays the others for it.

## The Crafts

Each craft is governed by a council of masters that controls apprenticeships, materials and prices. A child born into a metalworking family expects to spend decades learning the trade and may open a workshop of their own only after years of proven work, at the council's word. The system has preserved knowledge for centuries, and the finest pieces made here carry the refinements of countless generations: damascened blades and tools, harness leather tooled with canal patterns, glazed jars that ring when tapped, woven cloth traded as far as the Haradian ports. The workshops are as much temples to their crafts as the shrines of the Āsháian gods that crown the central plaza.

The Ardashír buy what the workshops make and carry it where the markets are. They send strings of camels west by the Descent to Dunhara, and south and east to the foot of the Grazian passes, where the guides take the lightest goods across the range. A craft family sells to an Ardashír factor at a price the factor sets, and the family's wealth depends on which factor will bid.

## Under the Ātárzád

The Ardashír are the most cosmopolitan of the twelve tribes, and Ashkarad shows it. Settler merchants have taken the broad streets near the gates, with their own temple of the Flame and their counting-houses, and the old craft quarter lies behind them, entered through low arches. Tellumi craftsmen pay the water-tax, but the Ardashír, who need the goods, leave the councils alone. The result is a city that is quieter than Shirvan and richer than Zargandûr, and where the resentment runs through contracts rather than streets.

The exiled **House of Ashkarad** lives on this arrangement. Its head, Sāmīm, grew rich in Amradad on Vylarian Sea trade, and some of the money returns to the city as loans to craft families that would otherwise be ruined by an Ardashír price. Everybody in the quarter knows whose money it is, and the craft families take the loans anyway.

## Hooks and Dangers

- **The glaze that was lost.** One council claims that the formula for a deep blue glaze died with a master in the Breaking. A potter's apprentice has found a fired tile that matches it, and wants to know where it came from. The answer lies in a sealed cellar in the Ardashír quarter.
- **The price quarrel.** Two craft councils are at war over a shaft that both claim. An outsider with a reputation for fairness could mediate, and an outsider who takes a side makes an enemy of a family that controls half the city's water.
- **The loan.** Sāmīm's money has bought loyalty in the quarter, and the Ardashír have noticed the pattern. A party sent from Amradad to carry coin into Ashkarad is walking into a watched trade.
- **The tunnels.** The deep shafts run for miles under the foothills. Some break into older workings no one has mapped, and air in a long tunnel can turn foul without warning.
