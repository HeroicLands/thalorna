The League council meets in the Merchant Hall of [[affiliation-altinkale|Altinkale]], a vast, ornate building that is parliament and exchange at once, and the business on its table is narrow: tariffs, the joint fleet and army, the three marches, and quarrels between member cities. Everything else belongs to the cities. The Byzarian League is a confederation of five trading cities at the crossroads of eastern and western [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], and it is a commercial pact. Its members grant one another trade privileges, charge outsiders common tariffs and defend one another, and each governs itself inside its own walls. About eight million people live under it in [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]].

The League's power is its position. It holds the overland routes that link the markets of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] to the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]], to [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] and to the distant east, and an outsider remembers it for the bargain: a League contract is slow to make, exact to the word, and kept.

## The Five Cities

- [[affiliation-altinkale|Altinkale]], _the Golden Fortress_, is the wealthiest and most politically influential of the five and the League's capital in all but name. The council convenes in its Merchant Hall, its banking houses finance trade across half of Ankaris, and its merchant-princes set the League's foreign policy, or try to.
- [[affiliation-denizara|Denizara]], _the Sea-Between_, is the League's principal seaport on the Vylarian coast, where maritime and overland trade meet. Its harbor is one of the busiest in the eastern [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], and its shipyards build the fast galleys that protect League commerce from pirates and from Haradian competitors.
- [[affiliation-yesilhan|Yeşilhan]], _the Green Caravanserai_, is an inland city at the junction of the caravan routes east toward the Khazryn and south toward Dunhara. Eastern goods—spices, silk, incense—first enter League hands in its vast bazaars and caravanserais, and its caravansary masters are famously shrewd.
- [[affiliation-gumushisar|Gümüşhisar]], _the Silver Citadel_, is a fortified mountain city guarding the eastern passes into Byzaría. Its garrison keeps the mountain routes open and discourages eastern warlords and desert raiders, and its silver mines pay for the League's collective defense.
- [[affiliation-karatas|Karataş]], _Blackstone_, is a mining and manufacturing city in the mountainous interior, built on rich deposits of iron and semi-precious stones. It makes the arms, armor and metalwork the other cities trade, and its craftsmen are renowned throughout the region. It is the least glamorous of the five and the one the others could least do without.

## Who Decides

Each city keeps its own council, its own coin standard, its own courts and its own guard. Inside the walls, hereditary rulers and merchant houses govern together, with an elaborate bureaucracy that manages trade, taxation and relations with the other cities. The council of each city sits by the weight of the houses behind its members, and its First of the Council, elected by the council for a fixed term, holds the city's seal while he presides.

The League council is where the cities meet, and weight there is counted three ways. Altinkale's bankers dominate it by money, Denizara's admiralty by ships, and Gümüşhisar's military governor by the plain fact that the League's prosperity rests on his garrison. The council governs only three districts directly, the [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern]], [[place-southrnmrch|Southern]] and [[place-northrnmrch|Northern]] Marches: frontier country outside every city's charter, held by lords the council commissions and can dismiss, garrisoned by conscripts all five cities raise, and paid for from the common treasury and the silver of Gümüşhisar.

A city's officers are the people a trader meets. The Chancellor keeps its charters, treaties and correspondence; the Treasurer keeps its customs, its tolls and the loans it raises against them; the Harbormaster keeps the port, its pilots, its wharves and the dues on every hull that enters; and the Master of the Mint keeps the coin struck in the city's name and the standard it is struck to. A Consul Abroad holds the city's seal for trade disputes in a foreign port. The Captain of the Watch commands the city guard and the militia the guilds are bound to raise. The Warden of the Weights inspects measures, coin and quality in the markets, and his is the office that makes the city's word good. A Guild Master speaks for a chartered trade before the council and answers for its members' conduct, and an Arbitrator settles disputes between houses under the commercial custom.

A house factor of Denizara explains the order of things to a Haradian buyer at his first contract: "Do not take a quarrel to the council. The council has never heard of you. Take it to an arbitrator you and your counterpart both name, and bring your copy of the terms, because he will ask for it before he asks your name."

Standing in a League city runs from the register outward. A freeman of the city is enrolled in its register, trades in its markets and pleads in its courts. Below him are the resident, who lives and works under the city's protection without its freedom or a vote, and the unfree, bound in service or debt with no property and no right to contract. Below them all is the man struck from the roll: no charter, no protection, no standing in the courts, and every contract he holds void. Above the freeman stand the guild master, the house factor who manages a great house's warehouses, ships and correspondents abroad, the house head who masters its capital and its name and elects the council, the councillor, and the First of the Council.

## People and Faith

Byzarian culture joins Helionite intellectual traditions, Vylarian administrative practice, and eastern influences from Harad and the desert peoples. The cities are multilingual and multi-faith, full of merchants, scholars and travelers from every corner of Ankaris. Byzarians are master traders and diplomats, practiced at crossing the cultural divide between east and west, and their cities are famous for their bazaars, silk-weavers, jewelers and spice markets. In those bazaars western Vylarian goods meet eastern spices, Dunhari incense and the occasional luxury from [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] or [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]]. [[skill-byzarnlng|Byzarian]], the League's trade tongue, has more words for kinds of business agreement than most languages have for colors.

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]] pantheon is the dominant faith, in a distinct eastern rite that puts mysticism, contemplation, personal revelation and elaborate ritual ahead of institutional authority. Its clergy are monks more than priests, and their monasteries are centers of learning, of manuscript copying, and of quiet political influence. The [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian Pantheon]] has a significant following as well, above all among the merchant communities with eastern connections.

## Neutrality and War

The League steers between the great powers with careful neutrality. The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] would like to absorb it; the [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Haradian]] guilds would like to dominate its trade, and Harad is the League's great rival at sea; the Āsháian-worshipping peoples to the east regard it as a useful but unreliable partner. The League survives by being indispensable to all of them and subservient to none.

Its wars are border wars, against desert raiders, steppe confederations and the occasional eastern warlord. Conscripts fight them under the march lords, and the council contracts mercenary companies when the frontier turns dangerous.

## Commerce and Currency

Each city strikes its own coin, and the League as a whole uses the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian currency]] (Aurion / Argentus / Octus) as its standard, with full access to the paper-script system through [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]] moneylenders chartered under the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]]. The Byzarian League is a post-imperial commonwealth member: it takes full part in the trade and banking infrastructure of the Vylarian system and is not subject to imperial governance or imperial taxation. See [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian Currency]] for the full system.
