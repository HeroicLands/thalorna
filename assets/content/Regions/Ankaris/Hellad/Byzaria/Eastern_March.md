The Eastern March is the frontier district of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]] on the desert side of [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] — the high passes east of [[affiliation-gumushisar|Gümüşhisar]] and the dry country at their feet, where the League's territory ends and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]] begins. It is a march in the old sense: land held not because it is worth anything in itself but because whoever holds it holds the road, and the road is the whole reason the League exists.

Like the League's other marches it is common territory, outside the charter of any city. Its lord is commissioned by the League council in [[affiliation-altinkale|Altinkale]] and answers to it; its garrison is drawn from the joint army all five cities raise by conscription; its treasury is the common treasury, fed by the silver of the Gümüşhisar mines. The present holder of the commission is **Lord Commander Vasilis of the Eastern March**, who has held it for two decades and is by any practical measure the League's senior soldier.

## Geography

The March begins at the eastern gate of Gümüşhisar and runs east and downhill. Its western part is the pass country: bare limestone ranges, snowbound from late autumn to spring, cut by the principal pass the Silver Citadel guards and by a handful of lesser mule-tracks that the garrison watches and the smugglers use. The pass road is paved for most of its length, walled where it climbs, and punctuated by way-forts a day's march apart.

East of the passes the ranges fall away in a series of dry foothills into the steppe margin — grass and scrub in spring, dust by midsummer, the first of the Khazryn's endless country. Here the road forks: north-east into the steppe and the Celestial Road, south-east toward the oasis-belt and the hinterland of [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]]. The March's outer limit is the last fortified caravanserai on each fork; beyond it the League claims nothing and patrols only as far as its horses can ride and return in a day.

Water is the March's governing fact. The passes have snowmelt and the foothills have a few reliable springs, every one of which has a fort, a shrine or a caravanserai built on it. There is no other water between the foothills and the first Khazryn oases, which is why armies from the east die in the March before they reach the walls of Gümüşhisar.

## What It Guards Against

The March faces three kinds of trouble. **Raiders** from the steppe — small mounted bands of the Khazryn confederations, testing the foothills for unguarded herds and unescorted caravans — are the constant one, and the garrison's patrols exist to make raiding unprofitable rather than impossible. **Bandits** in the passes themselves, often deserters or ruined muleteers, are a police problem that the way-forts handle. The **great khanates** that form in the central Khazryn once in a few generations are the reason the walls are as thick as they are; when one rises, the March is where the League learns of it first, and the Lord Commander's dispatches are what decide whether the council hires mercenary companies or calls up the reserves.

The Haradian frontier to the south-east is a different matter — a border with a rival that trades, not one with an enemy that raids. The March's southern forks are watched for Haradian customs officers as carefully as for Dunhari raiders, and the Lord Commander's correspondence with the Confederation's border governors is as much diplomacy as soldiering.

## Garrisons and Command

The March's strength is a chain of posts rather than a single army: the way-forts along the pass road, the watchtowers above the mule-tracks, the outer caravanserai-forts on the steppe margin, and a mounted reserve at Gümüşhisar that can reach any of them within two days. The soldiers are League conscripts — farm boys from the plateau and the coast, serving terms that turn into careers when the aptitude is there — under commissioned captains, many of whom rose from the ranks. [[being-athngrsktkls|Athênagoras Katakálos]], a farmer's son from the Denizaran countryside who has held a captaincy in the March garrison for a decade, is the type: competent, respected, and never expected to go home.

When the frontier turns critical the Lord Commander contracts mercenaries. [[being-arkdsphlmds|Arkádios Philomédis]] and his company have taken the League's silver for twenty years whenever Vasilis has needed more than conscripts, and the relationship is one of mutual respect on both sides.

The Lord Commander's command is distinct from the city's: Gümüşhisar governs itself and keeps its own walls and its own garrison under its military governor, who sits on the League council. The March is everything beyond the city's charter, and the two commands are meant to check each other. In practice they cooperate closely, because they face the same country, and the monks of Gümüşhisar's eastern-rite monasteries serve both as chroniclers, healers and go-betweens with the peoples beyond the passes.

## Peoples

The March's own people are few and hard. In the passes live the mining villages that feed Gümüşhisar's smelters, the muleteers and inn-keepers of the pass road, and the garrison families that have settled around every fort. On the steppe margin live upland herders of Byzarian stock, and beside them — some seasons — the winter camps of steppe clans who come in under the League's truce to graze the foothills and sell horses at the outer caravanserais. Āsháian shrines stand at those caravanserais alongside the Aurèldían ones; the March is the one district of Byzaría where the two faiths are practiced in roughly equal numbers.

## Economy

The March produces silver, tolls and contracts. The mines in the ranges around Gümüşhisar are the League's single greatest source of revenue, and their output is minted in the city and spent, in large part, on the March itself. Every caravan crossing the passes pays the League's tariff at the outer caravanserai and the pass-fort, and the collectors are League officers rather than city ones. Supplying the garrison — grain, fodder, remounts, leather, iron — is a business in its own right, and the merchant houses that hold the contracts are among the wealthiest in Gümüşhisar. Beyond that there are horses bought from the steppe clans, wool from the upland herds, and the small, steady trade in what the smugglers bring over the mule-tracks and nobody asks about.
