The Northern March is the frontier district of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]] on the landward, northern rim of [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]], where the plateau climbs into rough uplands and descends on their far side into the black-earth grain country of [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]]. It is the quietest of the League's three marches and the one the council in [[affiliation-altinkale|Altinkale]] thinks about least – which is the highest compliment a border can be paid. Nothing comes over it that the League does not want, and what the League wants most is grain.

Like the Eastern and Southern Marches it is common territory outside any city's charter, governed by a lord commissioned by the League council and answerable to it, with a small detachment of the joint army at his disposal. The office is styled Lord of the Northern March. It is by custom given to a member of an Altinkalan house – the capital lies nearest, and its bankers finance the grain trade the March exists to protect – and it is regarded in Altinkale as an honorable posting for a younger son rather than a command for a soldier.

## Geography

The March is upland: high pasture and stony ridges above the plateau proper, colder and wetter than the country to the south, with oak and pine in the sheltered valleys and snow on the passes for a third of the year. Streams run north off the ridges toward Velanthia's rivers and south into the plateau's valley bottoms; the watershed is, in practice, the frontier. On the Byzarian side the villages farm barley and keep cattle; on the far side the land opens into the horse-country of the southern Velanthian Hosts.

Two roads cross the March. The **grain road** runs from Altinkale north over the lowest pass to the Velanthian grain-belt towns, and along it, from late summer until the snow closes the pass, the wagon trains come south. A lesser drove road further east carries Velanthian horses down toward [[affiliation-karatas|Karataş]] and the eastern plateau. Both are guarded by League customs posts at the frontier and by way-forts at the passes.

## What It Guards

The March's business is not war. Velanthia and the Hellád have rarely quarreled, and the Hosts on the far side of the frontier are farmer-warriors with their own steppe border to worry about. What the March guards is the **grain road** – against bandits in the passes, against smugglers who would rather not pay the League's tariff, and against the possibility that the wagons stop coming. The coastal cities cannot feed themselves from their own terraces, and a bad year in Velanthia is felt in Denizara's bread prices within the season. The Lord of the Northern March is expected to know the state of the Velanthian harvest before the council does, and his dispatches are read in the counting houses of Altinkale as closely as any merchant's letter.

The March's other duty is the one the League never says aloud: it watches the north. When a great khanate rises in the central [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], Velanthia's eastern fringe is among its first targets, and a Velanthia overrun would bring the steppe to the League's northern passes rather than its eastern ones. The way-forts are built, and the pass roads walled, with that possibility in mind.

## Garrison and Governance

The garrison is small: a League detachment at the customs posts and the pass-forts, and the lord's own household at his seat on the grain road. The lord's court settles the March's justice under League law – grazing, water, tolls, the endless disputes between Byzarian villages and the Velanthian drovers who winter their herds on the Byzarian side of the passes by long custom and no treaty. Relations with the atamans of the nearest Hosts are conducted by the lord in person, usually at the autumn horse-fair on the frontier, and are on the whole cordial: both sides make money from the road and neither wants the other's trouble.

The March's relationship to the capital is that of a country estate to a great house. Altinkale's bankers hold the grain contracts, Altinkale's merchants own the wagons, and the lord is very often an Altinkalan by birth. The other cities tolerate the arrangement because the alternative – grain in Altinkalan hands _and_ a March in Altinkalan hands, with no council commission to revoke – is worse.

## Peoples

The uplanders of the Northern March are Byzarian hill-farmers of Helonic stock, [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]] in the eastern rite, plain-spoken and clannish, with a reputation in the coastal cities for stubbornness and honesty in roughly equal measure. Along the frontier they have intermarried with Velanthian drovers for generations, and the border villages speak a dialect of [[skill-byzarnlng|Byzarian]] thick with Velanthian loanwords. A few small monasteries of the eastern rite stand in the high valleys, chroniclers and hospitallers for the road; the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] shrines of the other marches are absent here.

## Economy

The March is a passage and a pasture. Its own produce is barley, cattle, timber from the high valleys and the cheese and wool of the ridges, most of it sold down to Altinkale. Its revenue is the grain road: the League's tariff on every wagon that crosses the frontier, the tolls of the pass-forts, and the trade of the inns and wheelwrights along the way. Velanthian grain, horses and furs come south; Byzarian silks, Karataş iron, coastal wine and olive oil, and the philosophical and medical books of the Hellád go north. It is not a rich district, and its lords are not men who expect to become rich by holding it.
