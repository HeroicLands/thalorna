## Overview {#overview}

**Siderash** is an iron mining town of about 3,200 people on the first slopes of the northern range, a few days' wagon ride above the plateau's edge. Its name comes from the Bizarian _siderin_, iron, closed with the word ending _-ash_. The ore is red and close to the surface, so the town is as much a smelting place as a mining one: bloomeries line the stream below the wall, and the smoke of the charcoal stacks hangs in the valley on still days.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The road leaves the Northern March's grain road at a toll post and turns up a side valley. The first sign of the town is the stream, which runs rust-red below the bloomeries. Travelers pay a gate toll in coin or in a measure of grain, and the gatekeeper weighs the grain.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Siderash buys nearly all it eats. The barley and sheep villages of the upper plateau lie a day below it, and each autumn they sell the town grain, cheese and wool against bar iron and nails. The grain wagons that come down from [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] pass a short ride away and sell the surplus of a good harvest. The forests of the slopes feed the bloomeries with charcoal, and a bad winter for the charcoal burners is a bad year for the town. What Siderash sends out is bar iron, ingots and rough forgings, which go down to [[place-khryshteikhos2|Krusteikhos]] in the empty grain wagons and on to the smiths of [[place-orashion2|Órashion]] and [[place-byzaris|Bizaris]].

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

[[affiliation-khryshteikhos|Krusteikhos]] holds the town, which lies nearer the capital than any other mining place, and its Merchant Hall farms the iron to three houses that buy it at a fixed price each season. A resident bailiff chosen by those houses sits in the gatehouse, and a squad of the city's guards keeps the road. The Lord of the Northern March, who is by custom a Krustéikhian, has no authority inside the walls, and the bailiff says so politely and often.

## The Present {#present}

The fixed price has not moved while the cost of grain has. The smelters grumble that the houses profit from bread and from iron both, and the bailiff has asked the Merchant Hall for a better price. Bands from the mountain country watch the ore wagons on the side road, and the squad is too small to guard every wagon in a season of heavy loading.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The smelters' hall serves a long table in the evening and lets its loft to travelers. The bailiff's house takes buyers from the capital.
