## Overview {#overview}

**Kladerash** stands on the brow of a hill above one of the sheltered valleys of the [[place-northrnmrch|Northern March]], where the drove road comes down from the frontier toward [[place-orashion2|Órashion]]. Its name is [[skill-byzarnlng|Bizarian]], "the boughs", from _kladerin_, a branch, closed with the word ending _-ash_, and the barley village in the valley gave it. Some thirty oaks, very old, stand apart from one another on the brow, and every trunk is pierced from the roots to the crown with hollows, each the size of a man's head and rimmed with smooth bark. Wild bees hive in most of them, and on a warm day the whole brow hums.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The brow is common grazing of the village below by the March's custom, and no lord has claimed the oaks. The village leaves the trees standing and takes the honey of the bees.

## Layers {#history}

The village says its first families found the oaks as they stand now, hollows and bees and all, and that nobody has cut one since, because a hollow oak makes poor timber and a good hive. Its grandmothers say the spirits of the hill bored the hollows for the bees; the monks of the valley say only that the oaks are older than their chronicle.

## What Is There Now {#present}

This spring the village's honey-hunter climbed one of the oaks after the bees and fell when a bough broke under him. He lives, but he cannot walk, and nobody else in the village climbs. The hives of the brow are the best in the valley, and their honey sells down the drove road. The village wants a climber for the summer's taking, and the honey-hunter's family wants his ropes and his smoke-pot brought down from the oak where they hang.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbors}

The bees are many and quick to anger, and high on a trunk there is no running from them. The oaks on the north side of the brow are gray and dead in their crowns, and their boughs break. Bands from the high country work the drove road in winter.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

A stand of oaks on a grassy brow, set well apart, each trunk thick enough that two men can barely join hands round it. The hollows climb every trunk in a loose spiral, a pace or two apart, from knee height to the first great boughs and on up the boughs themselves. Each hollow is round, its lip grown over smooth with bark, and narrows inside to a slot at the back. Bees go in and out of most of them, and comb shows pale in the slots. On the north side four oaks stand dead in their crowns, the bark falling away from the upper hollows, which are empty and black with rot. The honey-hunter's rope hangs from a broken bough on the nearest of them.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The summer's honey.** The village pays a share of the honey to a party that climbs the oaks and takes it.
- **The rope on the dead oak.** The honey-hunter's family wants his gear brought down from the dead oak where it hangs.
- **The monastery's wax.** The little monastery up the valley burns candles in its chapel through the winter and buys its wax from the village; it pays the party directly for comb brought down whole.

# The Truth of Kladerash {#truth .secret}

**The camp.** The brow is **Helluristo**, "the place of the green light under leaves", a summer camp of the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]], abandoned. A company of Senali came to the hill each year in the high summer, when the bees' comb is full, and slept on the open brow under the oaks. Over the years they opened hollows in the living trunks as the oaks grew and led the bark to heal round them, so that each tree became a stack of hives, and they took a share of the honey before they went on and left the bees the rest.

**Its leaving.** The company stopped coming when the Senali drew back from the hill country into the remote forests, long before the village's first families came up into the valley. The bees have kept the hollows ever since.

**The dead crowns.** The four oaks on the north side are dying from the crown down, because rot has got into their upper hollows, which nobody tends. Their boughs carry nothing. The living oaks are sound, and their hollows are the safe holds for a climber's hands.

> [!TIP]
> The living oaks are safe to climb and the dead ones are not, and the honey-hunter's rope marks which is which. The camp's season, the hollows opened in the living wood, the rot in the north side's crowns and the monastery's need for wax are this note's own account; a game master may change them.
