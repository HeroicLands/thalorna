A graduate of the Academy of the Silver Veil asked why Kalydria teaches its daughters to listen answers by quoting the first rule of her school, and she quotes it in the founder's words: the city could not keep its freedom by arms, and would keep it by conversation.

## The Founding

By about 260 BF the war was fifty years over. Kalydria had come under the Republic with the rest of Heliónis, and it kept its council, its theaters and its patron families. What it did not keep was any influence over the men who now governed Heliónis from the Republic's seat. The governors and the senators who came to the province were not educated men, and their households were not educated households. The patron families saw that whoever taught those households would be heard in the Senate.

**Lékhisîra Kálîdês**, of a patron family, founded the Academy to do the teaching. She took girls from the city's best houses and trained them in music, philosophy, rhetoric and the social arts, and she placed them as companions in the households of governors and senators. Each was educated to be better company than anyone else in the house.

## The First Rule

The Academy's first rule is the sentence its graduates carry. The city could not keep its freedom by arms and would keep it by conversation. The rule says nothing of the Republic's soldiers or of the Senate, and the omission is the point: the Academy never names an opponent, and a graduate never admits to being an instrument.

## What the Academy Left

- **The Academy of the Silver Veil**, the most distinctive institution of Kalydria, whose graduates are hetairai and are never called courtesans in the city.
- **The graduates' web** across Mídhalión, an unofficial intelligence service with no office and no master.
- **The root of the Velvet Hand.** The syndicate recruits from the Academy's graduates and dropouts alike, and its trade is the Academy's skill turned to another purpose.
- **A reputation.** Kalydrian taste, in manners and entertainment, travels farther than Kalydria's arms ever could.
