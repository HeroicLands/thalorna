A foreign envoy who asks to be taken to the government of Helionis has asked for something that does not exist. [[place-helionis|Helionis]] has never been united under one ruler, and its people are too proud, too argumentative and too sure of their own city-state's constitution to submit to another's. Seven sovereign city-states share the region and its 1.2 million people. What binds them is a common culture, the [[skill-helonclng|Helonik Language]], the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], and a network of festivals, athletic games and religious observances that holds them together socially while leaving each of them free.

## Who Decides

Each city-state decides for itself. Three of them lead the region, and each is a power in its own right:

- [[affiliation-thyrenae|Thirenae]], the oldest and most prestigious, is the intellectual capital of Helionis and, by its own reckoning, of all the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. The great academies are centered there, including the original seat of the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]]. A philosophical council drawn from the senior scholars of the academies and the heads of the oldest families governs it. Its patron is [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]], goddess of knowledge and wisdom.
- [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]] is the naval power. It commands the most important harbors and controls the sea lanes of the eastern [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]]; its citizen-sailors are formidable fighters, and its fleet is the largest in the region. It is a democracy whose assembly of free citizens votes on war, trade and law, and its politics are loud, passionate and occasionally violent. Its patron is [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]], god of order and justice.
- [[affiliation-kalydria|Kalidria]] is known across [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión]] for its artists, playwrights and musicians. The [[affiliation-slvrvlacdmy|Academy of the Silver Veil]] is based there, and its theater festivals draw audiences from across the Vilérian Sea. An oligarchy of wealthy patron families governs it, competing to fund the most lavish productions. Its patron is [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]], goddess of dreams and visions.

The other four are [[affiliation-theradon|Theradon]], [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]], [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] and [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]], the last a small coastal city-state that governs the district around it. Their constitutions run to oligarchy, government by the wealthy patron families or by the wealthiest and most educated citizens. Kostaros joined the Games late. The older six let it compete but not sit on the bench of arbiters, and it resents the exclusion.

Whatever a city-state calls its constitution, it works through broadly the same forms, and each city-state names and apportions its own offices. The citizen body, citizens by descent, meets in the assembly, which stays sovereign. A council chosen by lot prepares the assembly's business for a single year, and no one sits on it twice. Magistrates are elected or allotted to the city-state's markets, walls, treasury and courts, and an auditor examines every magistrate's accounts at the end of his year; until he clears them, the magistrate may not leave the city or town where he served. The _strátimor_ commands the city-state's forces by land and sea and is the one officer the citizens elect, without term limit. The _arsir_ presides for a year, gives the year his name and hands the office back.

Around them stand the offices a stranger is likeliest to meet. The _agóranir_ is warden of the market: its weights, its coin, its quality and its disputes. The _nomasfilir_ guards the laws and may halt a decree of the assembly that contradicts them. The herald speaks for the assembly and for the city-state abroad, and his person is inviolable even among enemies. An ambassador is elected to treat with another city-state and answers to the assembly for what he agrees. The _ghimarir_ keeps the gymnasium and pays for it from his own purse, which makes the office an honor.

Standing runs in a short ladder. At the bottom is the _átimor_ (the dishonored), stripped of civic honor by the courts and barred from the assembly, the agorin and the temples while still living among those who barred him. Above him come the slave, owned outright; the freedman, who works and trades but is barred from the assembly for life; and the metic, a resident foreigner who is taxed and liable to the levy, with no vote and no right to own land. The citizen votes and serves in the militia. Councilor, magistrate, strátimor and arsir are the rungs a citizen climbs by lot and election.

## War Between Neighbors

The city-states compete constantly in trade, athletic games and artistic achievement, and sometimes in war. Helionite warfare is ritualized and restrained by the standards of western Ankaris under [[lore-heladtruce|the Truce of the Games]], which is cold comfort to the people caught in it, and it comes often enough to keep the citizen militias in fighting trim.

## Character

What unites the [[lore-helioniteclt|Helionite]] people is a shared conviction that the examined life is the only life worth living. Philosophy, rhetoric and public debate are civic duties in Helionis, and the agorin of every Helionite city-state is the center of its political, commercial and intellectual life. Citizens argue there about everything from grain prices to the nature of the gods.

The Academy of the Silver Veil, which trains the hetairin (educated companions), stands for the Helionite belief that education, social grace and intellectual accomplishment are inseparable. Its graduates are among the most sought-after companions, conversationalists and gatherers of intelligence in Midhalión.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] is more deeply rooted in Helionis than anywhere outside [[place-alyssa|Alessa]], and for a plain reason: the Arcane Order's intellectual foundations were laid here by the scholars of the Epistémion (the Academy of Knowledge), the great Helionite federation of learning that the [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} vivisected when it conquered the cities between 335 and 312 BF. [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], the Senate, took out the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|College of Arcane Philosophy]] in 312 BF and chartered it as the Arcane Order in 73 BF, and the other colleges—ethics, logic, metaphysics, natural philosophy and the rest—were left diminished but intact. Those colleges are strongest in Helionis, and the Helionite academies keep the last real echo of the Epistémion's first vision: a federation of _all_ knowledge, in which arcane study was one domain among equals. Helionites regard the Arcane Order as their intellectual offspring, so their relationship with it is closer and more contentious than anywhere else.

## Relations

The city-states stand together with the Aureldian Pantheon, the Epistémion and the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], and they keep the Arcane Order at arm's length. [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]] is a cultural rival and partner, and the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] is their trading partner across the water to the south.

## Commerce and Currency

The City-States of Helionis use the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian currency]] (Solden / Ardin / Ogdel) as their standard currency, with full access to the paper-script system through [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] moneylenders chartered under [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]]. [[affiliation-tolvan|The Tolvan]] still claims the city-states' taxes on paper, and the city-states do not pay them. See [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian Currency]] for the full system.

## Current Situation {#present}

The seven keep their own constitutions and act together only for common defense and the shared festivals. The Truce of the Games is what keeps their wars short, and the sanctuary where it is kept is the one place all seven meet.

## History {#history}

The Vilérian Republic conquered the seven cities between 335 and 312 BF ([[lore-helionscnq|the Conquest of Helionis]]). The Truce of the Games has kept the wars between them restrained since.
