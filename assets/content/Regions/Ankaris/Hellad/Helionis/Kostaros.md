## Overview

**Kostaros** is a coastal city of about 30,000 people, one of the seven city-states of [[place-helionis|Helionis]]. An oligarchy of its wealthy patron families holds the government, and the city governs the [[place-kostarosdst|Kostaros District]] around it, which takes in the city of [[place-myrtillos|Mirtelos]]. The land around the two cities is too thin to feed them, so the city buys most of its grain from overseas and pays for it with the catch and the sea trade of its quays.

> Not to be confused with the Bizarian port town of the same name, [[place-kostaros|Kostaros]], which is the city's daughter-port from the colonizing centuries ([[lore-daughtercts|the Daughter-Cities]]).

## Politics {#present}

The assembly of Kostaros sits among the other six in the festival calendar and the Truce of the Games, and it is the only one of them with no seat on the bench of arbiters at the Sanctuary. The older city-states let its runners and chariot teams compete and have never let its envoys judge, which the patron families take as an insult to a city that pays for its own fleet and its own walls. They answer with money: the families fund a larger share of the Games' prizes than their city's size warrants, and they remember which envoys voted against them.

The patrons' real power is the grain contract. Kostaros eats what its merchants buy overseas, so the families who charter the grain ships also decide what the bread costs in the city and at the quays of [[place-myrtillos|Mirtelos]], and an assembly that wants a cheaper loaf has to persuade them. Beyond the two cities the district holds fishing hamlets and terrace villages that sell oil, wine and salt fish to the quays, and the oil and salt fish pay for a small part of the grain.

## History {#history}

Kostaros joined the Games later than the other six city-states, and its place in the Truce rests on that late entry: it keeps the Truce, competes under it and is still not seated among those who enforce it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The empty hold.** A grain ship due from overseas is three weeks late. The families blame the weather, the Mirtelos dockworkers blame the families, and the price of bread is rising in both cities.
- **The silent bench.** A Kostaran envoy wants a party to carry a petition to the arbiters at the Sanctuary that the bench has refused to hear, and says the Games themselves depend on whether it is read.
- **The quay at night.** Smugglers are unloading Taruzelian cargo at Mirtelos under the grain ships' cover, and a patron family quietly pays for the harbor watch to look the other way.
