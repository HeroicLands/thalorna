## Overview {#overview}

**Rakiria** sits on a high shelf of rock above the tree line, about 2,400 people behind a wall with more towers than the size of the town calls for. The gold is thin: threads of metal in white quartz, and flecks in the gravels of the cold streams. What it yields in a year would not fill a strongbox, and the town guards it as though it were a treasury, since in effect it is the treasury's most valuable outpost.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

The quartz is hauled out of narrow adits, crushed to powder under heavy stone rollers and washed over fleeces and sluices, and the gold is melted into small bars at a furnace inside the keep. Almost all goes down to [[place-athenikos2|Athenikos]], where the assembly spends it on the fleet and the mint, and what the town keeps is the wages the metal pays.

Nothing grows at Rakiria. The shelf is bare rock, and the villages within a half-day's reach are summer huts of shepherds who drive their flocks up the pastures and fall back to the valleys by the first snow. Hard winters push the shepherds' families into the town's lower quarter, behind the second wall. The bread comes up from the southern lowlands on pack-mules in the three months the passes are open, and a winter's supply is stored in the granary before the snow shuts the road. A town that runs short in Filin, the last month of winter, has nowhere to turn.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

[[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]] keeps Rakiria under a wall-warden the assembly trusts, with a garrison of paid spearmen rather than levy, and the keep holds a strongroom that only he and the treasurer's clerk can open together. Miners are bound by an oath sworn at the gate and searched at it.

## The Present {#present}

Rakiria's marauders are thieves from within as often as bands from outside. The searches are strict, and the miners resent them. A year of poor gold is blamed on the diggers, and a year of poor grain is blamed on the warden.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The second search.** A miner is turned back from the gate with nothing on him, yet the keep's count of quartz sacks is short. The warden wants the shift searched again in front of the whole town, and the foreman asks the party to stand witness.
- **The granary count.** The granary holds a month less than the warden's ledger says, and the passes have only just closed.
