## Overview {#overview}

**Ghalirea** climbs a hillside stained green by the copper in it, about 2,600 people in a town whose houses are roofed with slabs of the same rock. Its wall runs along the top of the slope, and the lower streets stand open on the valley side behind a second, lower wall that the people finish a little more each year. Rain turns the streams below the town the color of verdigris.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

The ore is cut from galleries along the green seam, crushed at stamping floors by the stream and smelted in clay furnaces. The copper is cast into ingots and stacked in the warehouse by the upper gate until a train is made up. Bronzefounders at [[place-thyrenae2|Thirenae]] and in the ports take most of it, and the tin they alloy it with comes from the mountains too, from [[place-tumaron|Tumaron]].

The slopes around Ghalirea are too steep and too poisoned by the ore to grow much. A ring of small villages in the valleys below, a few hours from the gate, raises goats, sheep and some barley and sends cheese, wool and meat up to the town. The bread comes from far off: grain ships unload on the coastal belt, and mule trains carry the sacks over the long passes to Ghalirea, a journey of many days that the town pays for in copper.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

[[affiliation-thyrenae|Thirenae]] holds Ghalirea. Its wall-warden commands a garrison of levy spearmen. The smelter-masters, who own the furnaces and hire the crews, hold the real weight in the town's affairs, and the warden has learned to ask them first.

## The Present {#present}

The grain trains are the town's weak point. A narrow stretch of the pass road runs through a gorge where bands of marauders wait for a train too lightly escorted, and Ghalirea has begun to hire its own guards instead of trusting the city-state's.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The poisoned stream.** The villages below the town report sick goats and a stream that runs greener each season. The smelter-masters say it is the rain.
- **The escort.** A train carrying the town's whole winter grain needs guards who will not sell the route, and the warden cannot say whom he trusts.
