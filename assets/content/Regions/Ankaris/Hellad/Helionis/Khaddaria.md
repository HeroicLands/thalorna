## Overview {#overview}

Khaddaria is the part of [[place-helionis|Helionis]] that the philosophers do not write about. It is the high country behind the coasts and valleys of the city-states: ranges of iron-red and copper-green rock, passes that close in winter, its northern ones on the line of [[place-krvgr|Karvogora]], and, beyond the last foothills, the dry north-east where the land runs out toward [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] and [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]]. About 300,000 people live there, which is a quarter of Helionis. They live in twelve fortified towns and in the villages that cluster around them for safety, and they dig the ore and metal that the academies, the smiths and the mints of the seven city-states are built on.

## The Land {#the-land}

Mountains cover about half of Helionis by area, and nearly all of that half is Khaddaria. The country begins in the foothills behind the coastal belt and runs on, ridge after ridge, to ground some 330 miles from the sea. The rock is metal-bearing nearly everywhere: iron in red seams, copper in green-stained hills, tin in the gravels of the gorges, lead and silver in the deep galleries, pigments and alum in the bleached slopes, and white marble in the quarries. Very little of it will grow a crop. Barley and pulses on narrow terraces, goats on the scree and charcoal from the beech and oak slopes are what the land gives, and a mining town buys the rest.

To the north-east the ranges end in a last ridge and fall away into stony steppe. Below the ridge lie salt pans and seasonal marshes, and beyond them, a few days out on the steppe, the inland seas, broad salt waters with reed margins, bitter shores and almost nothing living on them. The seas are not a destination. They lie on the caravan roads between Helionis, Bizaria and the steppe, and the caravan towns stand at the foot of the ridge where those roads leave the mountains. The towns exist because the roads do.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

No single power holds Khaddaria. Each of its twelve towns belongs to whichever of the seven city-states stands nearest to it, and that city-state garrisons it, appoints its wall-warden and takes its metal. [[affiliation-thyrenae|Thirenae]] holds three, [[affiliation-theradon|Theradon]] three, [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]] two, [[affiliation-kalydria|Kalidria]] two and [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] two. [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]] and [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]] hold none: they are the sea's polities, and the grain ships they send up the coast feed the mountains by way of the coastal belt and the passes. The hinterland's 300,000 people are counted inside the population of the city-states that hold them.

## Current Situation {#present}

The hinterland is rich in metal and short of everything else, so its politics are the politics of supply. A mining town that loses its grain road for one season starves, and a town that cannot send out its ore stops paying its garrison. The holding city-states know this, and they spend more on the roads than on the walls. The towns, for their part, know exactly how much the city-states depend on them, and a wall-warden who has served a few years speaks for his town to the assembly that appointed him as often as he speaks for the assembly to the town.

## Roads and Trade {#roads-and-trade}

Everything moves by mule and ox-cart. Grain comes up from three directions: over the long passes from the coastal belt, up the lower roads from the foothill lowlands, and over the northern passes from the grain country of Velanthia. Ore, bar iron, copper ingots, silver, tin, pigments, alum and marble go down the same roads in the other direction, to the smiths and mints of the city-states and to the ports, where Pelagoran and Taruzelian ships carry them across the sea. The caravan towns stand where the road from the north-east comes in. Wool, horses, hides and salt arrive from the steppe margin and from Bizaria, and olive oil, wine, cloth and finished metal go out in exchange.

## Dangers {#dangers}

**Marauders.** Bands of two kinds work the hinterland. Hill bands, drawn from the mountains' own outcasts, runaway miners and men whose clans lost a feud, raid the mule trains in the narrow gorges. Mounted bands from the steppe to the east ride in over the north-eastern passes to take herds, caravans and whatever a thin garrison leaves unguarded. A town's wall protects its streets and furnaces. The villages outside it protect themselves, with palisades, watch-fires, bells and a rule that every household lives within sight of the next, and for that reason the people of Khaddaria live close together even where there is room not to.

**The Black Flame.** [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] has a strong presence in the hinterlands. The faith of Ústaron's destructive fire finds its followers among smelter crews, caravan guards, hill bands and men who have no city-state to answer to, and its shrines and cells stand in the places where the city-states' priests do not reach. The cohort settled on the high passes of Bizaria ([[lore-heladvezhnur|the Vezhnur Settlement]]) lies across the border range. Its presence is plainest at Khasidon, Sufanos and Zarakia.

**The mountains.** Rockfalls, flooded galleries and passes shut by snow take as many people as the marauders do, and every road has a place where a train waits out the weather.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The silent road.** Three mule trains have left Khaddaria for the coast and none has reached a city-state's gate. Each holding city-state blames a different band, and each has a reason to want the others to believe it.
- **The shut pass.** A rockfall closes the northern pass in the week the grain from Velanthia is due. Two towns send diggers to clear it and a third sends none, and its warden says the road never mattered to him.
