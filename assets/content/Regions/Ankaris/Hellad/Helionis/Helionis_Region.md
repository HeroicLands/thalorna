## Overview

When the cities of Heliónis meet at a sacred site for the games, they send runners, wrestlers and chariot teams against one another, and they treat taking part as a duty owed to the gods. A season later the same cities may be at war. Heliónis is seven independent city-states that share a tongue, a pantheon, a calendar of festivals and an unshakable confidence in their own civilization, and agree about almost nothing else. Three million people live there, in the hills and valleys east of [[place-vylariargn|Vylaría]] and along one short stretch of coast. An outsider remembers Heliónis for its philosophers and its theaters, for the hetairai of Kalydria, and for the fact that the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] was born out of its academies.

Heliónis was once the cultural heart of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], and it is still the place the rest of the west goes to learn. Philosophy, art and magic are its traditions, old gods and heroes are its history, and it carries all of them forward while the empire that conquered it fades.

## The Country

Heliónis lies east of Vylaría, south of [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] and west of [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]], and it reaches the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] only along a short coast at its south-western corner, where its port cities face the water and, across it to the south, the islands of [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]]. The land is a patchwork of olive-clad hills, narrow fertile valleys and abrupt mountain ranges, with rocky shores along its one stretch of coast. The roads through the valleys and over the passes connect the cities and, as often, divide them.

The climate is warm and dry. The sun is bright, the sea is blue to the horizon, and the light has a clarity that artists and philosophers have celebrated for millennia. The soil is thin but productive: olive groves, vineyards and hardy grain grow on terraces cut into the hillsides.

## Seven Cities, and the Three That Lead

Heliónis is organized around its cities, and every city of note has its academies, its gymnasia and its theaters. Helionite culture prizes intellectual achievement, artistic excellence and personal virtue above everything else, and public debate on philosophy and politics is a cherished part of civic life. The region has produced many of Ankaris's greatest philosophers, mathematicians, playwrights and sculptors, and the tradition of inquiry that made them is unbroken.

Three cities lead the seven:

- [[affiliation-thyrenae|Thyrenae]] is the intellectual capital and holds the great academies.
- [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]] is the democratic naval power that commands the sea lanes.
- [[affiliation-kalydria|Kalydria]] is the city of artists and theaters, and home of the **Academy of the Silver Veil**.

The other four are [[affiliation-theradon|Therádon]], [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]], [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] and [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]]. Each of the seven has its own government, laws and customs. They combine for common defense and for the shared religious festivals, and they compete fiercely in everything else: trade, athletic contests, intellectual prestige and, now and then, open war. The [[affiliation-ctysttshlns|City-States of Heliónis]] note sets out how they are governed.

Women's standing varies from city to city. In some cities women are confined to domestic life; in others they take part in intellectual and even political life. The hetairai (educated companions), trained in music, philosophy, rhetoric and the social arts, are the most socially mobile women in Helionite society. The Academy of the Silver Veil trains them, and its graduates serve as companions, advisors and subtle political agents across [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]].

## Gods, Festivals and Mysteries

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]] runs through every part of Helionite life, and Heliónis regards itself as the spiritual heartland of the Aurèldían faith. Its great temples are among the most magnificent in the world. Its festivals combine solemn ritual with athletic contests, theatrical performances and public feasting, and they draw pilgrims from across the Vylarian Sea.

Three of the gods stand highest here. [[lore-menervadty|Ménérva]], goddess of knowledge and wisdom, holds special prominence in a region that lives by its learning. Artists and mystics revere [[lore-theriadty|Aethería]], goddess of dreams and visions. [[lore-janusdty|Árdavon]], god of order and justice, presides over legal and civic affairs.

The mystery cults are a distinctly Helionite institution: secretive religious societies that promise their initiates deeper spiritual knowledge through revelation in stages. Some are purely spiritual. Several have real political influence.

## Magic and the Birth of the Ordo

Heliónis has the oldest continuous tradition of organized arcane scholarship in western Ankaris. Long before the Ordo Arcanis existed, Helionite philosophers studied magic alongside mathematics, natural philosophy and metaphysics, as a branch of knowledge to be investigated, debated and set in order, and the great academies kept their own schools of arcane study. By the height of the classical period Heliónis had a body of arcane theory far in advance of anything the Vylarian Republic possessed.

### The Panepistemium

The greatest of these Helionite institutions was the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Panepistēmion]], _the place of all knowledge_. Vylarian tongues wore the name down to **Panepistemium**, often clipped to **Epistemium**; rendered, the name is the Academy of Knowledge. Founded around 400 BF, it was a federation of scholars across the city-states that took in every domain of inquiry: metaphysics, ethics, logic, natural philosophy, political thought, aesthetics and more. It was neither a guild nor a government body. Its scholars shared research and debated theory, and they kept their standards by peer reputation and scholarly consensus, with no legal authority behind them.

In its original form the Epistemium gave equal standing to every college. The study of ethics, logic, law, the mind and the natural world stood level with the arcane, and the College of Arcane Philosophy was one college among many. That college developed the foundational theories of elemental classification, of opposed forces, and of structured magical practice that became the Ordo's [[affiliation-hexhodai|Héx Hodäi]].

An ethics lecturer of the Epistemium at Thyrenae puts the matter to new students on their first morning: "Everything the Ordo teaches about the six schools was argued out in rooms like this one, by people who also argued about justice. They kept the first argument and dropped the second. You are here for both."

### The War and the Dragons

In 335 BF the Vylarian Republic turned its legions against the Helionite city-states, and the city-states fought back with the most devastating weapon they had: their mages. The war between Vylaria and Heliónis became the worst magical conflict in the history of western Ankaris. Facing the Republic's stronger conventional armies, the city-states loosed sorceries that shattered formations, poisoned the land, and killed on a scale no sword or spear could match. Some of the most powerful mages became mage-warlords in their own right, commanding forces their own cities could not fully control. The Epistemium had theorized about the ethical limits of arcane power for generations, and it proved helpless to stop its own members from turning that knowledge into weapons.

The Republic's answer was the **Dragon Riders**, warriors bonded to dragons whose presence destabilized magic. Near the dragons, spells misfired, wards collapsed and arcane power turned back on whoever wielded it, often with fatal results. The mage-generals who had shattered legions with a word found their own sorceries detonating in their hands, and the Riders broke the great mages of Heliónis where legions could not. How the Republic first secured the dragons' aid is in no account of the war (see [[lore-thebargain|The Bargain]] and [[lore-dragondead|The Dragon Dead at Therádon]]), and the records of the period are conspicuously incomplete. The city-states fell, the last of them in 312 BF.

The aftermath shaped everything that followed. In 312 BF the Vylarian Senate reached into the Epistemium, took out its College of Arcane Philosophy, and placed it under a magistracy, the **Praefectura Arcana**. In 73 BF the college was chartered as an instrument of state power, with military enforcement and a legal monopoly on arcane practice, and became the Ordo Arcanis. The other colleges—ethics, logic, metaphysics, natural philosophy and the rest—were left in place, diminished and shaken. The Ordo's Vylari terms and its institutional structure sit on a foundation that is Helionite through and through. Helionites know it and have never let the Vylarians forget it, and they are quieter about the fact that Helionite mages made the Ordo necessary in the first place.

On paper the Ordo is still the College of Arcane Philosophy within the Epistemium, and in practice it controls the whole institution. In Heliónis, the Epistemium's birthplace, the other colleges still carry real weight, and the Helionite academies are the last place in western Ankaris where its first vision still has force: a federation of all philosophical inquiry, in which a scholar of ethics stands equal to a scholar of magic.

### The Ordo in Heliónis Today

The Ordo is more deeply rooted in Heliónis than anywhere except [[affiliation-provinclys|Alyssa]]. Its Helionite chapters are among the most prestigious in the order, their libraries among the most extensive and their scholars among the most accomplished, and a disproportionate number of the Ordo's great theorists, researchers and teachers have been Helionite.

The relationship is still uneasy, because the Helionite habit of independent inquiry does not sit well with a monopoly. Several city-states have negotiated arrangements that keep a measure of academic freedom: a Helionite philosopher may study arcane theory and debate magical philosophy without joining the Ordo, so long as he does not _practice_. The line between theory and practice is blurred, and both sides exploit the blur. Unlicensed practitioners are more common in Heliónis than the Ordo would like, and its Quaesitorium treads more carefully here than elsewhere: cracking down hard in the land that gave the Ordo its foundations would be both politically unwise and plainly ironic.

## Relations

The Republic conquered Heliónis between 335 and 312 BF, during its expansion across the Vylarian Sea ([[lore-helionscnq|The Conquest of Heliónis]]). The conquest was military, and what followed ran the other way. Vylaria ruled Heliónis, and Helionite culture, education, philosophy and arcane scholarship reshaped Vylaria. The Republic's legal tradition drew on Helionite political theory, its theology took on Helionite subtlety, and the Ordo Arcanis was built on Helionite intellectual foundations. Vylarian aristocrats have sent their children to Helionite academies for centuries, and the most educated Vylarians speak Helonic as a second language. As the empire declines, Heliónis has regained practical independence while keeping its formal ties, and the relationship is that of a grown child to an aging parent: respectful, complicated and occasionally resentful.

Across the southern sea, the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] is an ancient civilization with which Heliónis has exchanged ideas for centuries. Helionite philosophy has shaped Khelâthi theological debate, and Khelâthi mathematics, astronomy and architecture have enriched Helionite scholarship. That relationship is warmer and more equal than the entanglement with Vylaria.

Harad, across the water to the south, is a natural trading partner, and Helionite olive oil, wine and luxury crafts flow through Haradian ports to markets across the world. [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] is a cultural rival and admirer whose people have adopted much of Helionite philosophy and art, sometimes to the Helionites' annoyance. Relations with Byzaría to the east are complicated: the two share cultural roots and have grown apart over centuries.

## What Heliónis Is Known For

- **The academies:** schools of philosophy, science and magic that have taught for centuries; the most prestigious draw students from across Ankaris.
- **The Academy of the Silver Veil:** the Kalydrian institution that trains hetairai in companionship, diplomacy and subtle influence, whose graduates serve in courts across Mídhalión.
- **The mystery cults:** secret societies of initiation in stages, some purely spiritual and some with political ends.
- **The games:** regular contests between the cities in running, wrestling, chariot racing and other sports, held at sacred sites, in which taking part is a sacred duty.
- **The theaters:** Helionite tragedy, comedy and satire, the most celebrated literary tradition in western Ankaris.
