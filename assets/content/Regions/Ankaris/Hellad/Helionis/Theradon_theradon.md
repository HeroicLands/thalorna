## Overview {#overview}

Theradon is governed by the citizens who are most certain they ought to be. Its assembly is made up of the wealthiest and most educated of them, who hold that this makes them uniquely qualified to guide the city-state toward justice and virtue, and the same conviction shapes the city-state's manners toward everyone else. It is a Helionite city-state of terraced hillsides in the Helionite interior, known for its philosophers, physicians and natural historians and for an [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]] chapterhouse. About 190,000 people live under its assembly, and 6,000 of them in the town of [[place-theradon2|Theradon]]. Theradon fell to the [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} in 330 BF, the first city of the war to fall, and lost its mage-warlord and his workshop in the same season. The chapterhouse does not teach that year.

## Who Decides

The constitution is oligarchic, and it works inside the forms the seven city-states share: a sovereign assembly, a council chosen by lot, audited magistrates, an elected strátimor and a year-naming arsir. What sets Theradon apart is who it admits to the work. The wealthiest and most educated citizens run the city-state, and they treat government as a branch of philosophy.

The result is real intellectual achievement beside real arrogance. A student from abroad who has been called a barbarian in Theradon describes it this way: "They call everyone beyond the last olive terrace a barbarian, and they say it so kindly that you cannot even be offended properly." The citizens' disdain for the "barbarous" lands beyond [[place-helionis|Helionis]] is barely concealed, yet their philosophers have taken the hard questions of justice, knowledge and the good life seriously, and their work is argued over throughout the civilized world.

## Learning and Faith

Scholars from across the realms come to study with Theradon's philosophers, physicians and natural historians. Theradon's libraries hold some of the oldest written texts in the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]], and its colonnades are full of students in dialectic with their teachers. The Epistémion (the Academy of Knowledge) chapterhouse, part of the federation [[lore-panepistfnd|founded around 400 BF]], gives Theradon its standing as a place of study.

The temples of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian pantheon]] keep high standards of ritual propriety, but the academy is the center of spiritual life, because the pursuit of wisdom is treated here as a sacred duty. Markets are orderly and regulated, and art and architecture are built to elevate the soul and sharpen the intellect. Theradon is sublime and stifling in the same afternoon: beauty and insight beside a narrow certainty about what counts as truth and a proper life.

## Current Situation {#present}

Theradon holds three of the twelve hinterland towns: [[place-nafrithos|Nafrithos]] with its silver, [[place-tumaron|Tumaron]] with its tin and [[place-mubaria|Mubaria]], a caravan town. Hill bands try the gorge road to Tumaron, the silver loads of Nafrithos are the mark every marauder in the mountains wants, and steppe bands raid the road at Mubaria.

## Commerce and Currency {#commerce}

Theradon uses the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian currency]] (Solden / Ardin / Ogdel) as its standard currency, with full access to the paper-script system through [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] moneylenders chartered under [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]]. [[affiliation-tolvan|The Tolvan]] still claims the city-states' taxes on paper, and the city-states do not pay them. See [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian Currency]] for the full system.

## History {#history}

Theradon was the first city of the war to fall. In 330 BF the [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} took the town and its mage-warlord's workshop and carried away what the workshop held ([[lore-dragondead|the Dragon Dead at Theradon]]); the warlord died when the town fell, and his stripped seat still stands on the terraces above it ([[place-warlordseats|the Seats of the Mage-Warlords]]). About 625 AF the councils of Theradon and Thirenae, the city-states with the oldest colleges, agreed with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s Helionis chapter that a philosopher may study, teach and dispute arcane theory without belonging to the Order so long as he does not practice ([[lore-heladconcordat|the Concordat of Theory]]).
