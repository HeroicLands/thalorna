Hellád is the stretch of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]]'s northern shore that lies between [[place-vylariargn|Vylaría]] and [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]]: a band of mountainous peninsulas, island chains and narrow coastal plains where the Aurèldían west meets the roads to the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] and, far beyond them, [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]]. About eleven million people live there, and few of them would call themselves Helladic first. A Helionite gives the name of his city; a Byzarian gives his trade and his master. Hellád is the name for the whole, and the whole is a country of city-states: fractious, argumentative and proud. Where [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldía]] is the civilization of kings and cathedrals and Tānvür the civilization of empire and classification, Hellád is the civilization of the city. An outsider remembers it for its philosophers in the west and, in the east, for the caravan cities that hand the goods of the far east to the western sea.

Two cultures divide the region, and they are cousins who disagree about nearly everything. [[place-helionis|Heliónis]] in the west, three million people in seven city-states, is the intellectual heartland of the Aurèldían world. [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]] in the east, eight million people, belongs to the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]], a confederation of five merchant cities that controls the overland approaches to the desert and the eastern sea. Around and between them lie smaller polities that answer to neither: contested islands, harbor towns, and a mountainous interior where local lords and bandit-kings rule their own villages.

## The Lay of the Land

Hellád is small beside the regions that flank it, and its position makes up for its size: it sits where the sea lanes, the caravan routes and two cultural spheres meet. The terrain is mountainous almost everywhere. Rugged peninsulas run out into the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], narrow fertile valleys cut between them, and archipelagos of small islands ring the coast. The interior plateaus carry terraced olive groves, vineyards and hardy grain, and the coastal margins are densely built over.

The coast is warm, with hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters, and its light has a clarity that Helionite painters and philosophers have celebrated for as long as either has worked. Salt water is close to nearly every settlement of any size. A Pelagoran pilot who has worked the coast for twenty years teaches the country to his passengers by its harbors: "Count from the sea. Every town worth the name can see salt water or the road down to it, and the inland cities face their nearest port the way a field faces the sun. If you want to know who matters in a valley, ask whose ships carry its oil."

Eastward the country changes. In Byzaría the warm coast climbs into dry, steppe-like uplands and then into the mountains that wall off the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]]. The Byzarian cities sit astride the passes, at the chokepoints where the sea trade and the caravan trade change hands.

## Two Peoples of One Stock

The Helladic peoples are overwhelmingly human, descended from an old stock whose tongue gave rise to [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]] and its sister languages, [[skill-byzarnlng|Byzarian]] among them. They divide into two related cultures.

The [[lore-helioniteclt|Helionites]] of the western peninsulas are pastoral, philosophical and, in their older cities, democratic. Their academies, philosophical schools and civic assemblies have produced more of the continent's theological, metaphysical and political writing than any other region. Their cities govern themselves in every form a Helionite has found an argument for—oligarchy, democracy, hereditary tyranny, elected kingship—and quarrel over which form is best, often settling the question by war. Their _hetairai_ (educated companions), schooled in music, philosophy, rhetoric and the social arts, are known across Mídhalión as companions to the powerful and as subtle political agents.

The [[lore-byzarianclt|Byzarians]] of the eastern confederation are commercial and polyglot. Their five cities—[[affiliation-altinkale|Altinkale]], [[affiliation-denizara|Denizara]], [[affiliation-yesilhan|Yeşilhan]], [[affiliation-gumushisar|Gümüşhisar]] and [[affiliation-karatas|Karataş]]—are merchant republics whose people descend from generations of marriage among Helonic, Haradi, Khazryn and Vylarian families. Guilds and banking houses run them. The League settles its business in trade councils far more often than on battlefields, and the First of the Council who holds each city's seal serves a fixed term, under heavy constraint from the council that elected him.

## The Gods in Two Rites

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]] is the formal religion of the whole region. Its theology has been worked out further in Heliónis than anywhere else: the great Helionite temples host philosophical debates as readily as liturgies, and the canonical interpretations that the western kingdoms treat as authoritative were written by Helionite religious scholars.

Byzaría keeps the same gods in its own eastern rite, and its cities are more mixed in faith. Aurèldían worship dominates civic life, but substantial minorities follow the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] faiths and even the [[affiliation-varakpnthn|Varnaka]] gods, served by small temples and itinerant holy men. Byzarian tolerance is famous by Ankarian standards, and its reason is practical: forbidding a faith would cost a city more in trade than it could ever recover in orthodoxy.

## Who Holds Power

Hellád has no common government. Heliónis is a patchwork of sovereign [[affiliation-ctysttshlns|city-states]] bound by language, gods and philosophy, and always at war with one another or close to it, over trade, territory and honor. The Byzarian League is the most organized power in the region: a true federation with common tariffs, a shared currency, and a joint army and fleet. Its five cities guard their own autonomy jealously, and the League council's authority is limited by design.

Beneath and between the two sit dozens of smaller polities. Some are independent islands; some are pirate havens dressed up as harbor oligarchies; some are highland chieftaincies that acknowledge no outside sovereign. Here and there a fortress from the Vylarian era is still held by a hereditary commander whose commission the empire stopped paying a century ago.

## Trade

Hellád is one of the great commercial regions of the world. Helionite merchant fleets work the whole of the Vylarian Sea. Byzarian caravans and cogs carry the transfer from road to ship, moving silk, spice, porcelain and tea from the far east to the western markets. The region's own exports are olive oil, wine, marble, philosophical and scientific manuscripts, fine ceramics, and the finished luxury goods of its artisan guilds.

## The Ordo in Hellád

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] is well established across the region, with major chapter-houses in Heliónis and at least one chapter-hall in each Byzarian city. Helionite scholars have always been of two minds about it, respecting its scholarship and distrusting its monopoly. Heliónis has also produced more independent arcane practitioners than any other region of the Aurèldían world, more than the Ordo would like.

## Neighbors

Westward along the shore lies Vylaría, heartland of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], which [[lore-helionscnq|conquered Heliónis]] between 335 and 312 BF and still keeps close and complicated ties with it. Farther west, across the sea, lies Aurèldía proper. Its relations with Hellád are old, dense and friendly: Heliónis is culturally and religiously part of the western sphere, and its thought runs through every Aurèldían court.

North of the Byzarian uplands lies [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]], whose grain feeds the coastal cities. Beyond it are the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], whose merchants and mercenaries are a familiar sight in Helladic ports. That relationship is commercial and transactional, without the religious tension that marks the frontier between the Nordmen and the Aurèldían kingdoms farther west.

Southward, across the Vylarian Sea, lie the northern coasts of [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]]. Helladic and Xerathian merchants have traded across those waters for a thousand years, and Helionite philosophers have long studied the theology of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] at a respectful distance.

To the southeast, where the coast bends toward the eastern shore of the sea, lies Harad, the League's great rival at sea and Heliónis's trading partner. Eastward lies the Khazryn Desert and, beyond it, the world of the Āsháian faiths. The Byzarian League is the gateway through which eastern goods and eastern ideas have always entered the western world.
