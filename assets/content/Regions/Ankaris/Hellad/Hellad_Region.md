Hellád is the country of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] that lies east of the [[place-vylariargn|Vylaria Region]], between the [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia Region]] to the north and the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] to the south, and it touches the sea only along a short coast at its south-western corner, opposite the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]]: a mountainous country of uplands, narrow valleys and passes where the Aureldian west meets the roads to the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] and, far beyond them, [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]]. About eleven million people live there, and few of them would call themselves Helladic first. A Helionite gives the name of his city; a Bizarian gives his trade and his master. Hellád is the name for the whole, and the whole is a country of city-states: fractious, argumentative and proud. Where [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia]] is the civilization of kings and cathedrals and Tantsou the civilization of empire and classification, Hellád is the civilization of the city. An outsider remembers it for its philosophers in the west and, in the east, for the caravan cities that hand the goods of the far east to the western sea.

Two cultures divide the region, and they are cousins who disagree about nearly everything. [[place-helionis|Helionis]] in the west, three million people in seven city-states, is the intellectual heartland of the Aureldian world. [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]] in the east, eight million people, belongs to the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]], a confederation of five merchant cities that controls the overland approaches to the desert and the eastern sea. Around and between them lie smaller polities that answer to neither: harbor towns and a mountainous interior where local lords and bandit-kings rule their own villages.

## The Lay of the Land

Hellád is small beside the regions that flank it, and its position makes up for its size: it sits where the sea lanes, the caravan routes and two cultural spheres meet. The terrain is mountainous almost everywhere, and narrow fertile valleys cut between the ranges. The interior plateaus carry terraced olive groves, vineyards and hardy grain, and the short coast on the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] is densely built over.

The coast is warm, with hot, dry summers and mild, rainy winters, and its light has a clarity that Helionite painters and philosophers have celebrated for as long as either has worked. Most of its towns lie inland, and the short coast is the whole country's door to the sea. A Pelagoran pilot who has worked the coast for twenty years teaches the country to his passengers by its harbors: "Count from the sea. Every town worth the name can see salt water or the road down to it, and the inland cities face their nearest port the way a field faces the sun. If you want to know who matters in a valley, ask whose ships carry its oil."

Eastward the country changes. In Bizaria the warm coast climbs into dry, steppe-like uplands and then into the mountains that wall off the Khazryn Desert. The Bizarian cities sit astride the passes, at the chokepoints where the sea trade and the caravan trade change hands.

## Two Peoples of One Stock

The Helladic peoples are overwhelmingly human, descended from an old stock whose tongue gave rise to [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]] and its sister languages, [[skill-byzarnlng|Bizarian]] among them. They divide into two related cultures.

The [[lore-helioniteclt|Helionite]] people of the west are pastoral, philosophical and, in their older cities, democratic. Their academies, philosophical schools and civic assemblies have produced more of the continent's theological, metaphysical and political writing than any other region. Their cities govern themselves in every form a Helionite has found an argument for—oligarchy, democracy, hereditary tyranny, elected kingship—and quarrel over which form is best, often settling the question by war. Their _hetairin_ (educated companions), schooled in music, philosophy, rhetoric and the social arts, are known across Mídhalión as companions to the powerful and as subtle political agents.

The [[lore-byzarianclt|Bizarian]] people of the eastern confederation are commercial and polyglot. Their five cities—[[affiliation-khryshteikhos|Krusteikhos]], [[affiliation-nesthiros|Nesthiros]], [[affiliation-karvashion|Karvashion]], [[affiliation-argyrteikhos|Argirteikhos]] and [[affiliation-orashion|Órashion]]—are merchant republics whose people descend from generations of marriage among Helonic, Taruzeli, Khazryn and Vylarian families. Guilds and banking houses run them. The League settles its business in trade councils far more often than on battlefields, and the First of the Council who holds each city's seal serves a fixed term, under heavy constraint from the council that elected him.

## The Gods in Two Rites

The faiths of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] are the formal religions of the whole region. Its theology has been worked out further in Helionis than anywhere else: the great Helionite temples host philosophical debates as readily as liturgies, and the canonical interpretations that the western kingdoms treat as authoritative were written by Helionite religious scholars.

Bizaria keeps the same gods in its own eastern rite, and its cities are more mixed in faith. Aureldian worship dominates civic life, but substantial minorities follow the traditions of the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]] and even the [[affiliation-vrnkpnthn|Varnuka gods]], served by small temples and itinerant holy men. Bizarian tolerance is famous by Ankarian standards, and its reason is practical: forbidding a faith would cost a city more in trade than it could ever recover in orthodoxy.

## Who Holds Power

Hellád has no common government. The [[affiliation-ctysttshlns|City-States of Helionis]] are a patchwork of sovereign city-states bound by language, gods and philosophy, and always at war with one another or close to it, over trade, territory and honor. The Bizarian League is the most organized power in the region: a true federation with common tariffs, a shared currency, and a joint army and fleet. Its five cities guard their own autonomy jealously, and the League council's authority is limited by design.

Beneath and between the two sit dozens of smaller polities. Some are pirate havens dressed up as harbor oligarchies; some are highland chieftaincies that acknowledge no outside sovereign. Here and there a fortress from the Vylarian era, such as [[place-unpaidfort|the Unpaid Fort]], is still held by a hereditary commander whose commission the empire stopped paying a century ago, when [[lore-crownssettle|the Settlement of the Crowns]] left the far forts' commissions unpaid.

## Trade

Hellád is one of the great commercial regions of the world. Helionite merchant fleets work the whole of the Vylarian Sea. Bizarian caravans and cogs carry the transfer from road to ship, moving silk, spice, porcelain and tea from the far east to the western markets. The region's own exports are olive oil, wine, marble, philosophical and scientific manuscripts, fine ceramics, and the finished luxury goods of its artisan guilds.

## The Arcane Order in Hellád

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] is well established across the region, with major chapter-houses in Helionis and at least one chapter-hall in each Bizarian city. Helionite scholars have always been of two minds about it, respecting its scholarship and distrusting its monopoly. Helionis has also produced more independent arcane practitioners than any other region of the Aureldian world, more than the Arcane Order would like.

## Neighbors

Westward lies Vylaria, heartland of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], which [[lore-helionscnq|conquered Helionis]] between 335 and 312 BF and still keeps close and complicated ties with it. Farther west, beyond Vylaria, lies Aureldia proper. Its relations with Hellád are old, dense and friendly: Helionis is culturally and religiously part of the western sphere, and its thought runs through every Aureldian court.

North of Helionis and the Bizarian uplands lies Velanthia, whose grain feeds the coastal cities. Beyond it is the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands Region]], whose merchants and mercenaries are a familiar sight in Helladic ports. That relationship is commercial and transactional, without the religious tension that marks the frontier between the Nordmen and the Aureldian kingdoms farther west.

Southward, across the Vylarian Sea, lie the northern coasts of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]]. Helladic and Zerathian merchants have traded across those waters for a thousand years, and Helionite philosophers have long studied the theology of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] at a respectful distance.

Off the short coast to the south lie the islands of Taruzel; the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] is the League's great rival at sea and Helionis's trading partner. Along the whole of the League's southern border lies the Kingdom of Amradad, on the eastern shore of the sea. Eastward lies the Khazryn Desert and, beyond it, the world of the Ashaian faiths. The Bizarian League is the gateway through which eastern goods and eastern ideas have always entered the western world.
