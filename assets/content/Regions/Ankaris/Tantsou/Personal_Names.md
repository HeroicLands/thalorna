Most people in the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] never have a name of their own. A personal name is an honor the empire grants for outstanding service, and until it is granted, a person is identified by clan, by position within the clan, and by place. The [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]], [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]] and [[lore-shuonong|Shuonong]] live and die that way, and so do most of the castes above them.

## Identification Without a Name

An unnamed person is known by a description that a registry clerk could file: "Fourth Son of the Battle Warden of the Clan of Moon Rises Over Still Water, near Weichai." It says who his father is and what his father does, which clan holds them, and where they live, and it is exactly what the classification tablet he carries records. In daily life his neighbors shorten it to "Fourth Son" or "the Warden's boy," and a magistrate reads it out in full.

## The Grant of a Name

Only the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] and the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchéi]] may be granted personal names, and only for outstanding service or achievement. The grant is a ceremony. A person's liege lord or a senior member of the clan confers it before witnesses, and the registry enters it beside the old description, which is never struck out. The name itself is a phrase describing what the person did, what they are, or what heaven seems to intend for them.

## Warriors' Names

A warrior's name speaks of power, speed and battle, and it is usually drawn from weather, beasts and weapons:

Zho Shou Teng Ja
: "Dragon's Rain of Heavenly Arrows"

Bui Ja Wuo Rao
: "Ice Blade of Thunderous Battle"

Weng Hieng Rao Chai
: "Wind Tiger Roars Across Thunder Mountain"

## Scholars' Names

A scholar's name speaks of the stars and of insight, and it is usually drawn from the heavens and the auspicious signs:

Zhi Zhai Leng Cheng
: "Rising Bright Star Bird"

Teng Zho Leng Wuen
: "Fifth Auspicious Star of the Celestial Dragon"

Kuo Gwo Wou Teng
: "Ancient Tortoise with the Celestial Platform"

## Two Names Earned

A Wohou officer known for years as "Second Son of the Shield Bearer of the Zhowou Clan, near Téngmou" led his troops to a victory that saved a province. His liege lord granted him the name Weng Hieng Rao Chai, "Wind Tiger Roars Across Thunder Mountain," for the fury of his charge, and he has signed nothing else since.

A Tanchéi scholar known as "Third Daughter of the Sage of the Zhi Zhai Clan, near Weichai" observed and recorded a new star that her seniors had missed. She was granted the name Zhi Zhai Leng Cheng, "Rising Bright Star Bird," for the discovery, and the star carries her name in the court registers.
