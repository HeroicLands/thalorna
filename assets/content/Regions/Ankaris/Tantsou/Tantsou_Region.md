## Overview

The Tāntsou Region is the far east of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]: a vast expanse of river basins, temperate plains and coastal lowlands walled in by mountains, which has been the cradle of [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tāntsou civilization]] for more than three millennia. An outsider remembers it for its porcelain and its silk, and for the registers. Where the [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían]] west is a mosaic of sovereign kingdoms bound by a shared faith, Tāntsou is one continuous civilization whose political form is nearly always imperial, whose eight castes have outlasted every dynasty, and whose celestial bureaucracy understands this land as the one place where the cosmos has been properly classified. The west of Ankaris has no emperor and does not want one. The east has had little else.

## Geography

Mountain ranges enclose the region and divide it into a series of great river basins. The northern lands reach a cold coast. To the west lie the passes and high deserts of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] frontier, where the [[lore-celestialroad|Celestial Roads]] end. To the southwest the unaligned [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] stand between Tāntsou and [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], crossed by the long passes of [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]], Jñānadvāra and [[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvāra]]. To the east lies the open ocean and the strait that separates the mainland from the Jénchài archipelago.

Three great rivers make the basins. Fed by mountain glaciers, they run through the heartland to the eastern ocean and carry commerce, tribute and imperial edicts on the same water. The valleys between them are the most intensively cultivated land in the world: rice, millet, mulberry for the silkworm, tea and orchard fruit feed the crowded river cities. Above the valleys, terraced hillsides climb the foothills in staircases, kept by villages whose terraces have passed down through named lineages for thirty generations or more. A terrace is the first thing a Tāntsou points to when asked what the registers are for.

The climate runs from one extreme of the region to the other. The north is cold-temperate, with hard winters and short summers; the central basins are continental, with sharp seasons and monsoon rains; the southern coast is subtropical, warm and wet, and gives way to mangrove shoreline at its margins. Earthquakes are common everywhere, and the imperial bureaucracy keeps ministries for predicting, tracking and answering them, an administrative tradition older than any other on the continent.

## Peoples and Culture

The Tāntsou are overwhelmingly human. The rare [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] who walk among them do so under a formal arrangement, possibly a coerced one, that ties certain mountain enclaves to the imperial court; the rarer [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] keep halls in the western mountains and seldom come out of them. Everything else is shaped by the eight castes, from the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazhò]], the Sons of the Dragon at the top, through the [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]] sorcerers bound in service beneath them, the warrior nobles, the celestial scholars, the artisans, the peasant farmers and the merchants, to the outcast [[lore-noutsou|Nòutsou]] at the bottom. A person's caste fixes their trade, their dress, their name, whom they may marry and which protections the law extends to them.

Cultural life is refined and deeply formal. The imperial tradition prizes calligraphy, poetry, painting, tea-craft, music and a complex cuisine whose techniques pass down through lineages. The civilization that makes some of Thalorna's finest porcelain and its most subtle lyric poetry is the one whose caste laws sanction collective punishment, and whose prisons hold the grandchildren of disfavored families as a matter of routine. Tāntsou intellectuals treat the two as a single thing: classification is refinement, and refinement is classification.

Family honor is kept on the ledger of [[lore-ancstrwrshp|ancestor worship]]. Every household keeps an altar, every generation's conduct is weighed, and when the alternative is dishonor, [[lore-tseising|Tséising]], ritual suicide, is the established means of restoring a lineage's standing. The poorest [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]] villages keep these rites as carefully as the court does.

## Religion

The religion of Tāntsou is the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Téngwou Rong Léi]], the Celestial Order: a pantheon conceived as a vast spiritual bureaucracy, of which the earthly government is a working mirror. At its apex is **Tānzhò**, the Celestial Dragon, who is the principle of classification itself, a sorter and not a creator. Beneath the Dragon, the **Jade Sovereign** (Wéizhuo), the **Minister of Storms** (Raogui), the **Minister of the Moon** (Yòuwéi) and the [[lore-fvlmntlrds|Five Elemental Lords]] each administer a domain. Common piety runs through ancestor worship, which treats virtue as spiritual capital and vice as debt.

Against this ordered cosmos stand the [[lore-infrnlrlms|Infernal Realms]], the zones where the Great Sorting was left unfinished. Demons, foreign gods and the Unclassified at large are understood as pressure on the borders of the classified world, and imperial ritual and the work of the Wánduo sorcerers are, among other things, the continuous keeping of that border. Other faiths are neither tolerated nor persecuted; they are ignored, since a foreign pantheon is by definition an attempt to classify the same things less well.

## Politics and Power

The political order is the Empire itself. A hereditary [[lore-mandatehvn|Son of Heaven]] rules by cosmic appointment, which is a different thing from divine blessing. Beneath him, the Warlords (_Wòzhuo_) of the Zhazhò hold the provinces and the armies and balance the throne; the Wánduo sorcerers are the imperial family's owned property and indispensable to its governance; and the Tānchéi scholars run the administration. The [[lore-weizhairui|Wéi Zhài Ruì]], the court's game of political manipulation, is understood as part of imperial statecraft and not as a corruption of it.

No serious threat has ever reached the Empire from the west. Its worst enemies have come from within, in succession crises, caste revolts and Wánduo conspiracies, or from the east, from [[place-jenchairgn|Jénchài]], the breakaway archipelago whose "rebellious" status has been a thousand-year stalemate of raid and counter-raid.

## Trade

The region stands at the eastern end of the Celestial Roads, the overland routes that run west through the Khazryn and reach [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] at last. Silk, porcelain, tea, lacquer, jade, spices and finished metalwork go west; silver, gold, amber, coral, glass and horses come east. The imperial bureaucracy taxes, documents and classifies every significant movement of goods, so smuggling is a highly developed profession.

Coastal trade carries less prestige and far more volume. Tāntsou trading ships work the southern ports and reach Vedyara's outport at [[place-nilatira|Nīlatīra]] and the northern coasts of [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]]. The port cities hold large foreign merchant quarters, carefully walled, carefully watched and carefully taxed.

## Neighbors

To the east, across the narrow strait, is Jénchài. The Empire calls the archipelago a rebellious province; the archipelago calls itself the true inheritor of the Celestial Mandate and the Tāntsou ruling house usurpers. The result is a perpetual low war at sea, punctuated by the occasional disaster in either direction.

To the southwest, beyond the eastern arc of the Grazian Mountains, contact with Vedyara is old and substantial and runs on mutual respect with almost no political integration. Vedyaran scholars travel the Tāntsou courts and Tāntsou monks study Vedyaran philosophical texts, and neither side has ever seriously tried to rule the other.

To the west, beyond the mountain passes, the Khazryn Desert is a buffer crossed by caravans and, now and then, by an imperial expedition. The Aurèldían kingdoms beyond it are real and remote, a faint rumor of cathedrals and princes.

To the north, the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukari]] frontier is a fortified marchland of watchtowers and garrison colonies. The Empire has treated Grukar raids as a nuisance and has generally been right to. The steppe beyond, the Black Wilds, is another matter this generation: the warlord [[being-wogenhenzhuo|Wògèn Hènzhuo]] has united its tribes, and his riders are already in the northern provinces.
