The Noutsou are the caste that is not a caste: the slaves, prisoners of war, condemned criminals and the disgraced who have been cast out of the caste they were born to, and they are the floor of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]]. A Noutsou has no name the law recognizes, no property, no marriage and no road out, and the law values his life below that of any other person in the empire. The [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]] are also slaves, and the comparison stops there: a Wánduo is owned because he is precious, and a Noutsou because he is nothing.

## How a Person Becomes Noutsou

Three roads lead down into the caste, and none leads back.

**Capture and debt.** Prisoners taken in war, captives from the border districts, and debtors who cannot pay are reduced to Noutsou by a magistrate's judgment. The reduction is entered in the registry as permanent.

**Sentence.** A magistrate may sentence a person convicted of murder, great theft or sedition to slavery for life in place of death or mutilation. The sentence falls on the criminal's descendants too: every child he fathers after it is born Noutsou.

**Outcasting.** A member of any higher caste who breaks the [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] gravely enough, or commits a moral crime the court will not name aloud, may be stripped of his caste and his clan and entered as Noutsou. It happens rarely, and the higher castes count it worse than death—a Wohou offered the choice between outcasting and [Ruising]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the ordered suicide, takes the knife.

## The Law of the Slave

The law gives a Noutsou two things. It forbids killing or maiming him without cause, and the penalty for doing so is the penalty for damaging property, paid to his owner; and it allows him to petition for elevation after an extraordinary service, a petition almost never granted.

Against those it sets everything else:

Obedience
: a Noutsou obeys any command from a member of any higher caste, and refusal is answered with death or the lash.

No property
: everything he earns or is given belongs to his owner.

No marriage
: no union of Noutsou is recognized, and their children are Noutsou from birth.

No movement
: he goes nowhere without his owner's leave.

No name
: he is recorded by his owner's name, a number and at most an epithet, and his classification tablet names the owner, not the bearer.

No end
: the condition is permanent, and its stain is held to pass to every descendant.

## Living as Property

A Noutsou is used for whatever the owner needs: the fields, the mines, the household, the galley, the entertainment of guests. The law's protection against gratuitous killing is enforced, when it is enforced at all, as a claim between owners. The other castes hold the Noutsou in contempt and avoid them. A free man seen to keep company with a slave loses face for it, and a Tsaihuo craftsman who eats at a slave's table may be shamed before his guild.

## The Narrow Ways Out

Three ways out exist in law, and few people walk any of them.

Manumission
: an owner may free a slave, who is entered as [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]] and given land to work.

Service
: a slave who shows extraordinary courage in the empire's service—a stand on the field, a lord's life saved—may petition for elevation.

Purchase
: a slave with a rare skill who is allowed to earn may in time negotiate the price of his own release.

Freedom does not end the mark. A freed slave is entered in his new caste with the notation of what he was, and his neighbors treat him as what the notation says. His grandchildren may be accepted; his children usually are not.

The law offers no general road out. No court frees slaves in numbers, no reform has ever been attempted, and a child born to Noutsou parents is Noutsou before he draws breath. What breaks the chain in any one family is an owner's decision or a stroke of fortune, and the system is built so that neither comes often.
