The porcelain that reaches [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] by the Celestial Roads, the lacquered scale on a garrison soldier's back and the hands that set a broken bone in a river town all belong to one caste. The Tsàihuo are the productive middle of [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tāntsou]], the fifth caste: the artisans and craftsmen who make the empire's goods, tools, weapons and works of art, the common soldiers who carry its spears, and the Yàiwō, the healers, who stand within the caste and are deferred to by everyone above it.

## The Makers

A Tsàihuo workshop is a household: a master, his sons and apprentices, a kiln or a forge or a loom, and a seal registered with the district guild that every piece it sells must carry. The caste makes nearly everything the empire uses—cloth, pottery, lacquerware, ironwork, carpentry, jewelry, armor and arms—and its best work is what foreigners know Tāntsou by. A potter's family may fire the same glaze for twenty generations, and a guild that loses the secret of one has lost something the registry records as a loss.

## Rights and Restrictions

The [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] give the Tsàihuo three rights:

- **Trade**: an artisan may sell his own work for profit, at his own stall or through a merchant.
- **Craft**: he may follow his trade and train others in it under his guild's rules.
- **Arms**: the Wònong among them may carry weapons, as described below.

They bind the caste with three restrictions:

- **Service**: a Wònong answers a [[lore-wohou|Wòhou]] commander's call to war.
- **Marriage**: Tsàihuo may marry in law, and marriage outside the caste is shunned; a craftsman who weds a merchant's daughter loses his guild's countenance.
- **No office**: no Tsàihuo may hold an administrative post or take part in governance.

## The Wònong

The _Wònong_ (common soldiers) are the Tsàihuo who bear arms. They are the empire's spearmen and archers in war and its watch in peace: they guard the gates and granaries, walk the market streets, take a thief to the magistrate and stand behind the officer who reads a sentence. They are the only people below the Wòhou who may carry a weapon openly, and they are treated with more deference than an ordinary craftsman because their work can kill them.

When a shopkeeper in a Tāntsou town is robbed, it is a Wònong he goes to, and the Wònong who walks that street usually knows already who did it. The watch enforces the Caste Laws at street level, investigates small crimes and keeps order in its own quarter, and it takes larger matters to the Wòhou magistrate above it.

## The Yàiwō

The Yàiwō (healers) are the empire's apothecaries and physicians. They are Tsàihuo by caste, and the respect they command runs far past it: a Warlord rises when a Grand Healer enters his hall, and a Tānchéi scholar takes her advice without argument. The Tāntsou say that illness does not ask a patient's caste, and neither does the healer.

**What a healer believes.** The Yàiwō teach that the body is a small copy of the Great Sorting. Health is a body whose elements, organs and flows of Léi are each in their proper place, and illness is a breach of that classification: something displaced from where it belongs. Body, mind and spirit are three sortings of one person, and treating one alone leaves the work half done. A healer's Mandate of Health mirrors the Emperor's [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]], each the keeping of order in its own domain, and the healer is taught that living in good order prevents more sickness than any remedy cures. [[lore-medicine|Medicine]] sets out the practice in full.

**The ranks.** A healer rises through four:

1. _Chéizhuì_ (apprentice), learning the foundations at a master's side.
2. _Yàigui_ (practitioner), licensed to treat and to dispense remedies.
3. _Daoyài_ (renowned healer), a master of proven skill.
4. _Wōyài_ (grand healer), whose knowledge of the body is held to be nearly complete, and whose counsel is sought by the Zhazhò.

**The two branches.** The _Yàitsài_ (herb-masters) are the apothecaries, who compound remedies from roots, leaves, minerals and animal parts. The _Zhayài_ (life-givers) are the physicians, who dress wounds, set bones, cut and stitch, and sit with the patient.

**Where medicine ends.** Some illness comes from the spirit side—an ancestor who has withdrawn his advocacy, a debit on the moral ledger falling due as sickness, or the touch of the Hénléi, the unsorted chaos. A Yàiwō who suspects one of these calls in a [[lore-tanchei|Tānchéi]] scholar, or in a village a Léigèn, and the two treat the patient together.

**Public health.** The Yàiwō are responsible for the empire's sanitation, its quarantines and its response to epidemics, and they teach the common people how to keep a clean well and a clean kitchen. A province swept by plague is held to be a province whose classification is failing, and the healers who contain it are doing the empire's spiritual work as much as its medical work.

## The Elder Races

Most [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] and [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] who live within the empire are classified Tsàihuo, because their work fits no other caste so well. Sinalëan woodcarvers, weavers and jewelers and Khazárian smiths, masons and engineers make work that sells for many times the price of a human master's, and the richest of them live better and wield more influence than many Wòhou, whatever their tablets say.
