Ask who feeds [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou]] and the answer is the sixth caste. The Nouwén are the peasant farmers, laborers and common folk who work the terraces, the paddies, the forests and the rivers, and they are most of the empire's people. They are bound to the land they work and owe tribute of produce and labor to the lords above them, and they have a spiritual life the court does not share: the ancestor altar in every hut, and the Leigen, the spirit masters who rise from their own villages and are consulted, quietly, by Warlords.

## Life and Labor

A Nouwén household works land it will never own. Every field, wood and fishing reach in the empire belongs to a [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]] lord or to the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] administrator who holds it under him, and the family that works it holds only the right to keep working it. In return it renders a share of the harvest at the threshing floor and sends its men to the corvée when the district's dikes, roads or granaries need hands. A village is a cluster of households that have worked the same terraces for as long as the registry remembers, under a headman who reports to the local magistrate and who is himself Nouwén.

The bond runs both ways in law, if not in force. A family may not leave its land without permission, and a lord may not drive it off without cause: a household that pays its tribute keeps its fields, and the magistrate who lets a lord evict a paying tenant answers for it.

## Rights and Restrictions

The [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] give the Nouwén three rights and hold them to four restrictions.

The right to the land
: protection from unlawful eviction, and the right to work assigned land in exchange for tribute.

The right to the ancestors
: shrines, rites and the veneration of ancestral spirits are protected by law, and no lord may forbid them.

The right to petition
: a household may bring a grievance or a plea for relief before the local magistrate.

Tribute
: produce and labor are owed to the Zhazho and Wohou above; a household that fails to render them is flogged, and a headman who hides a village's shortfall may be put to death.

No ownership
: the land belongs to the ruling castes, and a Nouwén is bound to his district and may not leave it without leave.

No marriage
: a Nouwén couple may live together and raise children, and the law recognizes no bond between them.

No personal name
: a Nouwén is known by clan, position and place, as "Second Son of the Ox-Herd of the Clan of Still Water, near Weichai."

## The Ancestors

In a Nouwén house the altar comes before the hearth. [[lore-ancstrwrshp|Ancestor worship]] is the center of village faith, and its teaching is plain to any child: the family's dead are posted officials in the celestial bureaucracy. They carry the household's petitions, argue its case before the bureaus, and spend the credit on their own moral ledgers to sway what comes. A dead grandmother who is fed, honored and remembered is an advocate in heaven; one who is neglected stops answering.

So the obligations are practical ones. The shrine is swept, the seasonal rites are kept, and the dead are provisioned with offerings of rice, wine and paper goods burned for their use. A family that fails in them weakens its advocates, and the consequences are expected in the fields: a failed crop, a sick child, a lawsuit lost.

## The Leigen

The Leigen (spirit masters) are Nouwén born with the gift of speaking with the dead. They arise in the villages, not in the temples, and they do for the peasantry what the temples of the higher castes do for theirs: they carry the living family's business to its ancestors and bring back the answer.

A Leigen is called for five kinds of work:

Communion
: speaking with a household's dead to relay a petition, receive an instruction or learn what an ancestor lacks.

Healing
: treating the sickness that comes from the spirit side, when an ancestor has withdrawn his advocacy, a ledger debit has fallen due as illness, or the [Henléi]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}, the unsorted chaos, has touched a house.

Registration
: performing the rites that enter a birth, a union or a death with the celestial administration, the filings the higher castes make through the temples.

Leading the rites
: the seasonal ceremonies, the rites of passage and the village festivals.

Divination
: reading signs and omens to learn which bureau is ascendant, which petition will be heard and what the dead require.

A Leigen is Nouwén by caste and treated in the village almost as the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchéi]] are treated in the cities. Warlords consult them, out of sight of their own scholars, on matters the Tanchéi cannot settle—a haunting, a curse on a line, an ancestor who will not rest. Their craft is not celestial scholarship and makes no claim to be: it is passed from teacher to apprentice in the villages, with a knowledge of plants, animals and the ways between the living and the dead that no temple library holds.

## Children Given to the Fields

Every child in the empire is tested at the age of ten. A child of the [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]] who shows too little of the gift is not returned to the sorcerers' compounds and cannot be sent to a family it never had, so it is given to a Nouwén village. These adoptees are taken in and raised as the village's own; their origin is known to everyone and held against no one, and the families who receive them count it a blessing.

## Philosophy of Lei

In the [[lore-phlsphyleiwo|Leiwo]] framework the Nouwén are the tenders of the earth's Lei: their labor draws its generative force into grain and keeps the empire's most basic balance from tipping.
