Every child in [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou]] is tested at ten, and the ones who show the gift do not go home. They become Wánduo, the second caste of the empire and its property: sorcerers born with the power to work the cosmic energy, bound to the service of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]] from the day they are claimed, ranked above generals and scholars, and owning nothing, not even themselves. A Wánduo counselor sits at a Warlord's elbow and a Wánduo artificer forges the weapons the empire's enemies cannot copy, and both belong, in law, to the house they serve. The Tantsou see no contradiction in it. Their power is exactly why they are ranked high and exactly why they are owned.

## The Testing

The test comes in a child's tenth year, in every village and every quarter, whatever the caste of the family. A child who shows the inclination is claimed the same season, entered in the registry as Wánduo and taken to the sorcerers' compound of the house that will own it. It does not see its birth family again, and the family is forbidden to seek it.

The test cuts the other way too. A child born in the compounds to Wánduo parents who shows too little of the gift has no family outside to return to, so it is given to a [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]] village and raised there as the village's own.

## What the Sorcerers Do

**Counsel.** A Wánduo counselor advises the Emperor or a Warlord on magic, on war and on the spiritual health of the province. In practice the great sorcerers of the empire stand at the center of its decisions, and the Zhazho govern through a triad of their own authority, their Warlords and their Wánduo.

**Artifice.** Wánduo artificers make the empire's enchanted arms and instruments, imbued with the elemental energies: blades that keep their edge through bone, banners that turn arrows, lamps that do not gutter in a storm. No other craftsman can copy them, and each one is entered in the registry as a possession of the house that commissioned it.

**Ritual.** The great rites need Wánduo and nobody else: the rites that turn a storm from the rice terraces, that strengthen or loosen the classification of a stretch of land, and, it is said, that hasten or slow the passing of time within a ring of stones. They demand precision and great reserves of power, and a province that loses its ritualists in war is a province that cannot answer a flood.

## The Monopoly

Magic in Tantsou belongs to the Wánduo and to no one else. A person of any other caste found working it is put to death at once, without the long inquiry the [[lore-tseising|courts]] give lesser crimes. The law exists so that every source of arcane power in the empire answers to the Zhazho and no rival school of sorcery can grow in the provinces. The Leigen of the villages, who speak with the dead, are tolerated because the Tanchéi class their gift as communion, not sorcery, and the distinction is watched closely.

## Rights and Restrictions

The [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] protect the sorcerer as a treasure is protected:

Protection
: a Wánduo's life is worth more in law than that of any caste below the Zhazho, and harming one is an offense against its owner and the throne.

Magic
: the sole right to perform the rites, craft artifacts and give arcane counsel.

Latitude
: within the task he is set, a Wánduo chooses his own methods, and his owner does not instruct him in his art.

And they bind him as property is bound:

Bondage
: he belongs to his Zhazho owner and may not leave the estate without leave.

Obedience
: disobedience is punished by death or by the permanent severing of his power, and the Wánduo count the second worse.

No marriage
: Wánduo may lie together only with their owner's consent, and their children belong to the owner from birth.

No property
: everything he makes or is given is his owner's.

## Sorcerers in Rebellion

A Warlord who rises against the throne brings his Wánduo with him, because they are his. The registry remembers rebellions in which a provincial lord's fire and earth sorcerers, his wielders of air and his binders of spirits held an imperial army at a river for a whole season. Every Emperor since has watched how many sorcerers each Warlord keeps, and the right to claim a promising child is jealously contested between the throne and the provinces.

## The Elder Races

No member of the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale Folk]] or the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]] is ever classified Wánduo. Their magic is part of their nature and does not work the way human sorcery does, and the chattel system has no hold on it. The Wánduo know this better than anyone. Elder magic is the one power in the empire that does not answer to their monopoly, and the sorcerers' compounds watch the Elder enclaves with a suspicion the Tanchéi prefer not to put in writing.
