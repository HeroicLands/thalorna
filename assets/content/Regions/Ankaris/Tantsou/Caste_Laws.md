A district magistrate breaking in a new clerk gives him the law of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] in one sentence before he lets him touch a brush: everyone who comes through that door is one of eight things, and the first question you answer about any of them is which. The **Zhaolou**, the Caste Laws, are the foundation of Tantsou society. They fix the rights, duties and restrictions of each caste, so that the social order and the cosmic order stay in step, and they are enforced with punishments that are severe and often fatal, above all where magic is concerned. [[lore-tseising|Law and Honor]] describes the courts that enforce them and the penalties they hand down.

## The Eight Castes

The society of Tantsou is divided into eight castes, from the highest:

1. [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]]—Sons of the Dragon: the Emperor, his family and the Warlords, the administrative lineage the celestial bureaucracy designated, holding absolute authority.
2. [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]]—chattel sorcerers, born with innate magical ability and bound to serve the Zhazho, the one caste permitted to practice magic.
3. [[lore-wohou|Wohou]]—warrior nobility, the commanders and administrators who alone may command armed force.
4. [[lore-tanchei|Tanchéi]]—celestial scholars: interpreters of omens, keepers of the sacred texts and advisors on celestial law.
5. [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]]—artisans, craftsmen and common warriors, the productive backbone of the economy, including the _Wonong_ (common soldiers) and the Yaiwo (healers).
6. [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]]—peasant farmers, who work the land and feed the empire, and among whom the Leigen (Spirit Masters) arise.
7. [[lore-shuonong|Shuonong]]—merchants and usurers, who trade and lend and make nothing themselves.
8. [[lore-noutsou|Noutsou]]—slaves and outcasts, with almost no rights and no path upward.

## Universal Principles

Four rules apply across every caste, and the clerk learns them first.

**Magic belongs to the Wánduo.** Any practice of magic by anyone else is punished by immediate death. Every child in the empire, of every caste, is tested for magical inclination at the age of ten, and a child who shows it is taken and reclassified as Wánduo.

**Marriage follows caste.** Legal marriage is open to the Zhazho, the Wohou, the Tanchéi and the Tsaihuo. The lower castes may live together, and their unions carry no standing in law. [[lore-marriage|Marriage]] gives the customs in full.

**A personal name is an honor, never a right.** Most people are identified by clan, position and district; a named person has earned the name by service. [[lore-personlnms|Personal Names]] explains how.

**The castes are nearly closed.** Movement between them is extraordinarily rare. The one regular exception is the testing of children, which raises a child of any caste to Wánduo or places a Wánduo child who shows no magic among the Nouwén.

## The Imperial Registry

In a society built on classification, accountability is existential. If the cosmic order depends on everything being sorted, the administration must know what has been sorted, so every person in the empire, from the Son of Heaven to the lowest Noutsou, is recorded in the **Dao Nong Chou**, the Great Person Record: the imperial census, which is the mortal twin of the celestial Master Registry kept by the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Archive of All Records]] (Chou Sei). The census is complete. Nobody falls through it. To be unrecorded is to be unclassified, and what is unclassified dissolves into the Henléi.

The Dao Nong Chou is not one document but a distributed registry kept at every level of the administration. Village headmen report to the local magistrates, the magistrates to the Warlords, and the Warlords to the imperial chancellery. Every birth is registered, every death recorded, and every marriage, change of household and reclassification documented, copied and filed. For each person the registry holds a name, or the identifying designation of the unnamed castes, together with caste, clan, household, district of residence and every classification event of note: the result of the magical test, criminal convictions, military service, and any promotion or demotion between castes.

The census is kept with such thoroughness because the cosmology demands it, and the empire's suspicion of its own subjects is only the second reason. The Tanchéi teach that the Dao Nong Chou is the mortal shadow of the celestial registry, and that a person missing from it is a gap in the classification that weakens the cosmic order, just as a lost record in the Archive would send the thing it describes sliding toward the Henléi. A census official who lets gaps stand in his rolls is complicit in that erosion, not merely negligent. The census also serves taxation, conscription, the allocation of labor and the enforcement of caste boundaries, since a person who cannot be found in the registry cannot be taxed, drafted or held to account under the Teng Lou. The administration's spiritual obligation and its practical need to control the population point the same way.

## The Classification Tablet

Every person who travels beyond the district where they are known by face carries a _Zhao Mou_, a classification tablet: a flat plaque of hardwood, usually palm-sized, inscribed with the bearer's caste, clan or household, district of registration and the authority that issued it, with the issuing magistrate's or Warlord's seal stamped in lacquer on the reverse as its authentication. The tablet is the physical proof that the bearer exists inside the sorted order. A person met outside their home district without one cannot be told apart from the unclassified, and may be treated accordingly: a magistrate, a garrison commander or a commercial official may demand to see the tablet, and a traveler who cannot produce one is held until the registry confirms who they are.

The tablets differ by caste. A Zhazho's is lacquered, gilt-edged and sealed by several authorities. A Wohou officer's records his rank and the jurisdiction he commands. A Tsaihuo artisan's records his guild and his trade. A Nouwén farmer's is a plain board with one seal, minimal and sufficient. The Noutsou carry tablets too, and theirs name the owner rather than the bearer. A foreigner who has been classified carries one as well: a merchant holding a _Shuonong Dou Chou_, the temporary trading classification, receives a tablet good for the length of his business, and a foreigner classified by imperial decree receives one under the Emperor's own seal, an object of real prestige and, in practice, a guarantee of safe passage anywhere in the empire.

Forging a tablet is among the gravest crimes in the Teng Lou, because it is an act of unauthorized reclassification, an attempt to put oneself in a category the cosmic order did not assign. The penalty is execution, carried out in public to show that the classification cannot be got around. The [[affiliation-haluozhao|Black Lotus Society]] trades in forged tablets, which is one of the reasons the empire counts it an existential threat and not a criminal nuisance.

## Foreigners and the Unclassified

The caste system classifies the inhabitants of the empire. Foreigners are not inhabitants. A trader from the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]], a diplomat from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], a scholar from [[place-helionis|Helionis]], a wanderer from anywhere beyond the border: each has no caste, no moral ledger, no ancestor spirits posted in the celestial bureaucracy, and no bureau with jurisdiction over their affairs. In the cosmology they are unclassified material, not hostile like the Henléi, simply unsorted: a document that arrived at the office without a filing number.

The Tantsou mean this literally. A foreigner has no spiritual infrastructure in the empire. The temples cannot process his petitions, since there is no ledger on file; the magistrates have no framework for his disputes, since there is no caste to fix jurisdiction; and the celestial administration has no bureau for him at all. He is present in the physical world and absent from the filing system.

### Administrative Classification

The system does not leave foreigners unsorted forever, because their presence inside the borders would otherwise be a standing breach. It has three answers.

**Imperial decree** (_Tengzhéi Zhaolou_). The Emperor, as the celestial bureaucracy's appointed administrator, may classify a foreigner by his own decree. This is the highest form of classification, rare and prestigious, reserved for dignitaries, ambassadors and people who have done the empire an extraordinary service. The foreigner is assigned a caste, usually Wohou for a military ally, Tanchéi for a scholar or Tsaihuo for an artisan, a provisional ledger is opened for him, and the machinery can then process him. His standing lasts exactly as long as the Emperor wills it.

**Bureau classification** (_Sei Zhaolou_). Far more often a foreigner is classified through the ordinary channels, by the bureau whose jurisdiction his presence touches. A foreign merchant entering to trade is classified by the commercial administration, which issues the Shuonong Dou Chou, roughly "merchant transit document," granting him Shuonong-equivalent standing for the length of his business. A foreign physician offering his services might receive a temporary Tsaihuo classification from the local magistrate. These classifications are functional and narrow: the merchant can buy and sell, and cannot petition a temple, bring a claim outside a commercial dispute or take any part in civic life.

**No classification.** A foreigner who enters without obtaining any classification, a wanderer or an uninvited traveler who slipped past the paperwork, has no standing whatever. He cannot own property, make a contract, seek the law's protection or conduct any business the classification governs. In practice he is treated much as a stray animal is treated: not persecuted unless he causes trouble, and without rights, protections or recourse. A crime committed against him is no crime under the Teng Lou, since the victim does not exist in the legal system, and a crime he commits is a disruption of the order, dealt with swiftly and harshly.

### The Foreigner's Experience

For a visitor from western Ankaris the experience of being unclassified is profoundly disorienting. The empire is not hostile; the Tantsou understand that foreign trade and diplomacy serve its interests. But the systems that make daily life work do not see you. You cannot enter a temple and file a petition for anything. You cannot appeal to a magistrate when you are cheated. Your money is taken, because commerce is pragmatic, and your personhood is not recognized. You are a walking administrative gap, tolerated, managed, confined to a trading quarter or a diplomatic compound, and spiritually quarantined from the classified population.

A foreigner with a bureau classification stays marked as other. The classification processes him without absorbing him. His ledger, if one is opened, starts at zero with no ancestral credit; his petitions, if accepted, are handled last; his children, if born in the empire, may be classified, always with the notation that the lineage began outside the sorted order. A classified foreigner is a sorted document in a foreign language: filed, and never fully read.

## The Elder Races and the Classification

The [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]] and [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]] are a different case entirely, because they are not unclassified. They are older than the empire and older than humanity, and the Great Sorting, the Tanchéi teach, classified all of existence, including beings that existed long before people were sorted into castes. The Elder Races were sorted. They have their place.

That place is not quite the human one. The Elder Races are classified functionally, by the role each performs within the empire, and not by the cosmological reasoning behind the human castes. A human Tsaihuo artisan is Tsaihuo because the Great Sorting designated that category for craft-workers; a Sinalo master jeweler is Tsaihuo because that is the nearest administrative category the system has for what he does. The classification fits, and it was not made for him.

### Caste Placement

**Tsaihuo**, the majority. Most Sinale and Khazari within the empire are classified as [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]], since their craftsmanship, Sinalean woodwork, textiles and jewelry, Khazárian metalwork, masonry and engineering, makes it the obvious functional fit. Their work commands extraordinary prices and respect, and lifts individual artisans to wealth and influence far above their nominal standing.

**Tanchéi**, the scholars and priests. Sinalean sages and Khazárian masters of engineering and earth-lore are classified as [[lore-tanchei|Tanchéi]] when their knowledge serves the empire's scholarly and spiritual institutions. The priests who keep their own peoples' traditions are placed here too, because "keeper of sacred knowledge" is the nearest equivalent the caste system offers.

**Wohou**, the warriors and the nobles. Khazárian warriors of proven valor and Sinalean officers who serve in the imperial military are classified as [[lore-wohou|Wohou]], and so are the leaders of enclaves and strongholds who must deal with the imperial administration, because that standing carries the authority such dealings need. In rare cases an Elder noble is classified [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]].

**Never Wánduo.** Magic is intrinsic to the Elder Races, woven into their nature in a way no human shares, and no Sinalo or Khazár is ever classified as [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]]. The Wánduo classification exists for humans born with innate magic who must be controlled through the chattel system, and Elder magic does not work through the petition model at all; it is not channeled through mental forms built from aura as human magic is, whether Arcane Order or Wánduo. The system can categorize the Elder Races and cannot explain their magic, a loose end the Tanchéi prefer not to examine.

### The Dual Existence

The Elder Races live in two worlds at once. Inside the empire, in human cities, at court, in trade and in military service, they accept and work within their assigned classifications: they attend the Son of Heaven when summoned, serve in the armies when called, and trade under the same rules as any other Tsaihuo or Wohou. They understand that dealing with the human world means fitting the human hierarchy, and that refusal would mean reclassification downward, or treatment as unclassified, with all the loss of standing that entails.

Inside their own enclaves, the Sinalean forest sanctuaries and the Khazárian mountain holds, the human classification does not apply. Each people keeps its own understanding of its place in the world, its own spiritual traditions and social structures, all of them older than the Great Sorting, or at least older than the human account of it. A Sinalo master craftsman classified as Tsaihuo finds the designation administratively useful and cosmologically meaningless. He is Sinale. The paperwork is a human concern.

The empire manages this tension and never resolves it. The classification insists that it sorted everything, the Elder Races included; the Elder Races' quiet refusal to take their classifications to heart is, from the cosmological side, a low-grade breach that has been running for millennia. The Tanchéi know it and have collectively decided not to think about it too hard.
