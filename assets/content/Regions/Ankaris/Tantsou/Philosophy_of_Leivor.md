A Tānchéi lecturer opens the first lesson of the year by setting a cup of water, a candle and a stone on the table, and asking her students why each of them is still what it is. The answer she wants is Léi. **Léiwō** ("spirit-sacred," usually rendered "the Way of Léi") is the body of thought through which the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tāntsou]] explains how the cosmos works, why it holds together and what happens when it fails. It is the language beneath the empire's religion, magic, medicine and government, and a scholar, a sorcerer and a healer all reason in it.

Léiwō teaches that the world is sustained by **Léi**, the cosmic energy that runs through everything classified. Léi keeps the Great Sorting in force. It keeps fire burning as fire, water running as water and stone standing as stone, and without it the sorted world would dissolve back into the Hénléi, the primordial chaos of the Unclassified.

## One Energy, Many Models

The Tāntsou Léi is the same cosmic force every magical tradition draws on. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] pictures it in elemental geometries; the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] feel it as a living current through the natural world; the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] hear it as a resonance in stone and metal. Each people builds its own mental model of one reality.

The Tāntsou model is administrative. Léi is the energy that keeps sorted things in their categories. The model sees some things the others miss, and it is blind where they are not.

## Sorted and Unsorted

Other peoples divide existence into body and spirit, or the material and the divine. Léiwō divides it into sorted and unsorted.

**The sorted world** is everything the Great Sorting classified: the elements, the creatures, the spirits, the laws of nature and the celestial bureaus themselves. It takes in the mortal plane and the heavens alike, because both were classified. Gods and mortals stand in one system, and the gods are simply filed at a higher level.

**The Hénléi** is not a place but a condition: what exists wherever classification breaks down. It waits at the edges of every category, in the gaps between the elements, in paradox and contradiction. When a thing loses its classification—a fire that does not behave as fire, a person whose caste is in doubt, a rite performed wrongly—the Hénléi seeps in through the gap.

## Léng and Ha

Léi runs in two flows.

**Léng** (bright flow) is Léi tending toward order. It sorts, distinguishes and strengthens the boundaries between things, and it belongs to clarity, authority and the day.

**Ha** (dark flow) is Léi tending toward dissolution. It blurs boundaries, merges distinctions and loosens classifications, and it belongs to dreams, ambiguity and the night.

Neither is good or evil, and both are needed. A cosmos of pure Léng would be frozen: perfectly classified and unable to change or grow. A cosmos of pure Ha would be the Hénléi itself, formless potential without shape. The cosmos works only with both in balance, and so the Bureau of Entropy (_Hén Séi_) is kept, dangerous as it is.

## The Seven Domains

Magic in Tāntsou is ordered by the **domains of classification**, the basic categories set at the Great Sorting. Five are material, and each is administered by one of the [[lore-fvlmntlrds|Five Elemental Lords]], the bureau chiefs of the elements.

1. **Air** (_Léi_): breath, speech and the medium through which every other classification moves. Air carries; it connects sorted things to one another and keeps the world from going stagnant. Its lord is Wéngdao Zhòuwō.
2. **Fire** (_Hò_): transformation, purification and passage. Fire turns things from one classification into another—the physical into the spiritual, the living into the ancestor—and it carries spirit documents up to the celestial archive. Its lord is Rīnhò Daowéng of the Bureau of Transformation ([[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Hò Séi]]).
3. **Earth** (_Tōu_): stability, foundation and persistence. Earth holds classifications in place; it is the sorted world's resistance to change. Its lord is Daotōu Wōchài.
4. **Metal** (_Jing_): distinction, boundary and enforcement. Metal draws the sharp line between one category and the next, and it is the domain of law, judgment and precision. Its lord is Wōjing Ja.
5. **Water** (_Shòu_): flow, adaptation and passage between states. Water lets a thing change from one sorted condition to another without passing through the unsorted. Its lord is Zhòushòu Zhò.

Two domains lie beyond any single lord, because they are too near the root of the classification for one bureau chief to hold.

6. **Spirit** (_Wōléi_): the soul, consciousness and the link between the mortal and celestial planes, through which petitions go up and directives come down. The celestial bureaus govern it together, the Ministry of Final Account ([[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Singwou]]) and the Bureau of Night Counsel ([[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Méi Séi]]) above all.
7. **Neutral** (_Hou_): pure Léi, the energy older than the Sorting, embodied in Tānzhò the Celestial Dragon. It is the unsorted energy that sustains everything sorted. A practitioner who works it reaches beneath the classification to touch the raw cosmic force, with great power and great danger. No bureau administers it and no official holds it. It is Tānzhò's alone.

## Magic as a Petition

Every magical tradition channels the cosmic energy through mental forms, structured images built from the practitioner's own aura, and the form decides how the energy flows. The Tāntsou build their forms as documents.

A [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]] sorcerer preparing a working builds a formal petition in his mind's eye: addressed to the right celestial bureau, stamped with the right seals of authority, and filed through the proper channels. The petition is the form. The seals hold it together, the address decides which domain the energy runs through, and the wording fixes the effect. The image genuinely shapes the energy, as the Ordo's geometries shape theirs, and it gives Tāntsou magic a character of its own.

**What the model does well.**

- Large workings shared by many sorcerers, who sign one petition together.
- Reliable effects that rarely fail once begun, thanks to the procedure's own redundancies.
- Wards, blessings and long-lasting effects, which the bureaucracy processes slowly and keeps processing.
- A working and a temple petition share one mental architecture, so magic and worship join without a seam.

**What it does badly.**

- It is slow to begin, because the form is elaborate.
- It cannot improvise: there is no form for unauthorized action.
- It is blind across jurisdictions. An effect that falls between bureaus, or under none, is truly harder to work.
- It depends on belief. A Wánduo who loses his faith in the celestial bureaucracy may find his forms coming apart in his hands.

## Léi in the Body

The [[lore-medicine|medicine]] of Tāntsou is built on Léiwō. The body is a small copy of the sorted world: each organ belongs to a domain, each system is a channel of the administration, and health is every classification in the body kept in order. Léi runs through channels in the body, one for each domain, and the Yàiwō healers learn to find which channel is disturbed and to set it right. A needle redraws a boundary inside the body; an herb adds an element where one is lacking.

The healers assign the domains to the body this way:

- **Air**: the lungs and the breath, the channels of speech and breathing.
- **Fire**: the heart and the blood, the engine of transformation.
- **Earth**: the spleen and the gut, the foundation.
- **Metal**: the liver and the body's defenses, the boundary between self and other.
- **Water**: the kidneys and the bladder, the channels of flow.
- **Spirit**: the mind and the soul, the seat of administration.

## Léi in Government

Each caste is held to carry its own kind of Léi:

- the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazhò]], administering Léi, the authority to classify and govern;
- the [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]], channeled Léi, the power to work cosmic energy through the petition's form;
- the [[lore-wohou|Wòhou]], enforcing Léi, which keeps classifications in place by force;
- the [[lore-tanchei|Tānchéi]], recording Léi, which keeps the classification's records and precedents;
- the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]], sustaining Léi, which keeps the physical world in order through labor.

Marriage, ritual and daily custom are all arranged to keep Léi flowing properly within and between the castes. Mixing across caste lines is a classification error as well as a social offense, because it weakens the sorting and opens gaps the Hénléi can seep through.

## Practice

**Daily cultivation.** Every Tāntsou, of whatever caste, is expected to keep his own Léi in order each day. The commonest practice is **Léichéi** (spirit-wisdom), a slow meditative exercise performed at dawn in courtyards and village squares, which is held to bring the body's classifications back into line with the cosmos. Breathing exercises strengthen the Air domain, and the mindful doing of one's caste work strengthens one's place in the Sorting.

**The rites of passage.** Births, marriages and deaths must each be registered with the celestial administration. A birth is classified: the child's caste confirmed, its channels read and its existence filed with the bureaus. A [[lore-marriage|marriage]] merges the spiritual accounts of two households. A death sends a soul to the Ministry of Final Account, and the rite eases its journey.

**The Festival of Harmonious Léi.** The greatest festival of the year, the **Hou Léi Juì**, celebrates the continued success of the Great Sorting. The Emperor performs its central rite, an audit of the empire's Léi in which he and his Tānchéi review the classification and declare it in good order. It is a religious observance, a state ceremony and an administrative procedure at once, and any failure or interruption in it is taken as a dire omen for the coming year.
