The Empire of Tantsou is the oldest continuously governed state in the world. It has held the eastern mainland of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] for more than three millennia, and it has watched every other great power on the continent rise, flourish and decline from the cold vantage of an institution that counts its own history in dynasties. The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] was a republic, became an empire and began its long decline inside fourteen centuries; Tantsou's archive is older than the Vilérian republic by more than eighteen hundred years, and Tantsou takes seven hundred years over one serious succession crisis. It governs the [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou Region]], some 120 million people, and it holds that it is not a state at all but the mortal face of a cosmic order.

That claim is the key to everything else. The celestial bureaucracy of the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Celestial Order]] administers the heavens by the same principles, and through the same offices, that the imperial bureaucracy uses to administer the earth, and the Tantsou hold that these are two faces of one reality and not two systems that happen to resemble each other. Outsiders, missionaries and foreign theologians have argued with the claim for centuries. The Tantsou consider the argument beneath attention.

## At a Glance

The throne
: the Emperor, the **Son of Heaven** (_Tengzhei_), who holds the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]] by cosmic appointment and rules by being correctly classified.

The capital
: [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], two cities on a wide river with the walled enclave of the Zhazho on the island between them.

The Princes
: the _Zho Zhei_ of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], each holding a province with its armies and answering to the Emperor alone; the kingmakers of every succession dispute.

The provinces
: [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]], the richest, on the delta and the coasts; [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]] in the west; [[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]] in the north; [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]] in the southwest.

The sorcerers
: the [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]], property of the imperial family, ranked second of the eight castes and owning nothing.

The administration
: the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] scholars, who read the omens, keep the sacred texts and staff the ministries.

The law
: the [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] and the [[lore-tseising|Teng Lou]], enforced by the Princes' magistrates; every subject is recorded in the census and every traveler carries a classification tablet.

The frontiers
: [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]] across the eastern strait, the fortified marches to the north toward the far-northern [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] nests, the quiet, expensive passes into the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] to the west.

The game
: the [[lore-weizhairui|Wei Zhai Rwi]], the court's contest of manipulation, in which every house of consequence is a player.

## The Celestial Administration and the Emperor

Religion, politics, magic and social rank are one system in Tantsou, viewed from different angles. The Emperor is the celestial bureaucracy's appointed administrator of the mortal plane. His line holds the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]] (_Tengwo_), which is a cosmic appointment and not a divine blessing: it grants administrative authority over the empire's classification system, and nothing else. The Son of Heaven does not rule by divine right in the Aureldian sense. He rules by being correctly classified, and a line that loses that correct classification, through dynastic decay, ritual failure or the corruption of its household, loses the Mandate to a line more correctly classified.

Power sits in three places. The Emperor and his household hold the ritual center and the final authority. The Princes, who are [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]] like the Emperor, hold the provinces and the armies, and they have repeatedly made and unmade emperors in succession disputes. The [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]], the chattel sorcerers who are the legally owned magical slaves of the imperial family, channel the cosmic energy through bureaucratic forms and are indispensable to every function of government that mortal administration alone cannot perform.

## Current Situation {#present}

The Emperor on the throne, [[being-tengrauemp|Tengrau]], is young and newly crowned, and he is still finding the reach of his own power. The Retired Emperor, [[being-yanzhortrd|Yanzho]], lives in the background of the court. The Caste-Registrar, [[being-cstrgstrtnts|Third Son of the Archivist of the Clan of Lanterns Along the Bank]], is the young Emperor's chief counselor, and every Prince courts him for that reason. The [[lore-weizhairui|Wei Zhai Rwi]] is played hard around the young Emperor, because each of the four Princes means to be the one who shapes him.

The Princes stand in no fixed order. The Prince of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]], [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], is the strongest and the most feared: his water-gifted [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]] bring rain to the delta while the rain fails elsewhere, and his granaries sell rice to the Princes whose provinces have gone dry. Some at court say his sorcerers are behind the drought, and nobody has proven it. The Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], is younger, rising fast, a reformer with the young Emperor's ear. The Prince of [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]], [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], and the Prince of [[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]], [[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]], are bitter rivals, and each takes the other's loss as his own gain.

The south is hungry. Its rice harvests have failed several years running, smaller rivers are dry in their beds, and in some districts the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]] have risen against their lords and the magistrates who collect the tribute. The [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] are divided over the cause, between a breakdown of classification in the domains of Earth and Water and a weakening of the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]], a reading no scholar puts in writing. In some corners of the court the quiet question is whether the Emperor's filing is still in order.

Every frontier is pressed at once. The steppe warlord [[being-wogenhenzhuo|Wogen Henzhwo]] holds the tribes of [[place-blackwilds|the Black Wilds]] together under one banner, the [[affiliation-hwenhatoucnf|Hwenhatou Confederation]], and his riders are in the northern districts. The general [[being-tuijawengyan|Tuija Wengyan]] is in open revolt with disaffected soldiers and minor Zhazho nobles behind him, and his forces hold real gains in the border provinces while the alliance that carried them there begins to crack. The house of [[affiliation-yanchaizhao|Yanchai Zhau]], brought low in the court's game, has a new head who has vowed to restore it by any means that work. In the remote northwest the [[lore-chaizho|Chaizho]] range through the country the great earthquake broke, and the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] and [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]] hold them there at heavy cost. Across the eastern strait [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]], in practice a sovereign kingdom, raids the eastern and southern coasts and chokes the coastal trade.

:::secret {title="Yanchai Zhau and the Black Lotus"}
The new head of Yanchai Zhau has staked what is left of the house's standing on a secret alliance with the [[affiliation-haluozhao|Ha Lwo Zhau]], the Black Lotus Society, and most of his own council does not know of it.

The embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] asks for direct relations with the Empire. The court reads the request as a breach of the Speaker's protocol, since a foreigner reaches the Emperor only through the Speaker; the Prince of Doulwen takes it up, and the game, not the Emperor, decides it ([[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dunavarre to Tantsou]]).

## The Capital and the Provinces {#holdings}

The Emperor reigns from [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], the capital, and holds the central river basins around it through his own officers. Its Governor, [[being-gvrnrzhowen|Eldest Son of the Gate Captain of the Clan of the Heron on the Wall]], keeps the city for the throne, and the Speaker carries every question put to the Emperor by anyone but his attendants and the Princes, hearing it in a gate-court outside the wall of the Emperor's enclave, whispering it to him and speaking his answer aloud. The rest of the empire is four provinces, each held by a Prince from a seat of his own:

[[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]]
: the delta of the great rivers and the eastern and southern coasts, about 34 million people, ruled from the port city of [[place-wongsen|Wongsen]]

[[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]]
: the terraced uplands of the southwest and the passes toward Vedyara, about 26 million, ruled from [[place-mwonou|Mwonou]]

[[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]]
: the western basins and the passes where the Celestial Road comes in, about 22 million, ruled from [[place-doulwen|Doulwen]]

[[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]]
: the cold north and its marches, about 20 million, ruled from [[place-kwozheng|Kwozheng]]

The throne's own basins hold the remaining 18 million or so. About one person in twenty lives in a town or a city, some six million people in all. Zhowen holds two million of them and the four seats about half a million more; the rest live in the river cities and market towns of every province, each entered in its district's register. Every subject in the empire is on the rolls of a district, and a district registrar, rotated from district to district, keeps them under the Caste-Registrar; the district's seal stays with the office when its holder moves on.

## The Hierarchy of Castes

Tantsou society runs in eight castes, which fix a person's profession, legal standing, dress, marriage and the name, if any, by which they may be known. From the highest:

[[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]]
: Sons of the Dragon: the Emperor, his family and the Princes

[[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]]
: chattel sorcerers, bound to serve the Zhazho

[[lore-wohou|Wohou]]
: warrior nobility: commanders and administrators

[[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]]
: celestial scholars: omen-readers and keepers of the sacred texts

[[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihwo]]
: artisans, craftsmen and common warriors

[[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]]
: peasant farmers and laborers

[[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]]
: merchants and usurers

[[lore-noutsou|Nwitsou]]
: slaves and outcasts

The [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] set out the rights, duties and restrictions of each. The classification of persons is the subject of the legal code and the foundation of the administration, to a degree no Aureldian kingdom's class system approaches: Tantsou civilization treats the sorting of people as one part of the sorting of reality, and the ministries that track the movements of comets also track the ritual purity of provincial officials.

## The Celestial Order

The religion of Tantsou is a celestial bureaucracy, a vast spiritual administration on which the earthly government is modeled. The Celestial Dragon ([[lore-tanzhodty|Tanzho]]) is the First Classifier, the principle of order itself; the Jade Sovereign ([[lore-weizhuodty|Weizhwo]]), the Minister of Storms ([[lore-raoguidty|Raugwi]]), the Keeper of the Jade Seal ([[lore-youweidty|Youbwi]]) and the [[lore-fvlmntlrds|Five Elemental Lords]] administer their own domains of classified existence. The Emperor's authority flows down from the Mandate of Heaven, and the common people sustain their spiritual lives through [[lore-ancstrwrshp|Ancestor Worship]], a system of moral ledgers in which merit and default are recorded and inherited.

Against the order stand the [[lore-infrnlrlms|Infernal Realms]], the zones where the [Great Sorting]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} was never finished and the Unclassified presses on the borders of the sorted world. Imperial ritual, the work of the Wandwo and the moral discipline of the whole population are understood as the keeping of that boundary, without which, in the Tantsou view, the cosmos would in time dissolve. The [[lore-celestlrdr|Celestial Order]] note sets out the whole framework.

## Trade and the Frontiers

The Empire's external trade is enormous. It flows west along [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Roads]] through the Khazrin toward [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión]], and by sea south toward [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]]. The bureaucracy taxes, documents and classifies every significant movement of goods; smuggling is a highly developed profession; the port cities keep large foreign merchant quarters, carefully walled and watched.

The northern frontier toward the far-northern [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] nests is a marchland of watchtowers and garrison colonies that needs constant attention. The western passes into the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] are quiet and expensively garrisoned. Within the empire, caste revolts, Wandwo conspiracies and the manipulations of the [[lore-weizhairui|Wei Zhai Rwi]] occupy the court without pause.

## History {#history}

The dynastic record the imperial archive can document begins around 2500 BF ([[lore-tantsoudyn|The Opening of the Tantsou Record]]) and runs through more than a dozen dynasties, each a reassignment of the Mandate from a decaying house to a more correctly classified successor. The Celestial Order traces the same line very much further, to [[lore-greatcnvrg|the Great Convergence]] at the zero of the [Celestial Calendar]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}; the empire holds both accounts at once and has a doctrine for doing so, set out in [[lore-clndrstrlgy|Calendar and Astrology]]. The Princes have made and unmade emperors in succession disputes more than once, and the worst dangers the empire has met have come from within: succession crises, caste revolts and Wandwo conspiracies.

The most recent reassignment of the Mandate is several centuries old. The present dynasty is in the period its own official historians call mature: past the vigor of its founding centuries, not yet in the terminal corruption that would warrant a transfer of the Mandate, and showing the strains of a late dynasty on every frontier. Its years are counted reign by reign. Each Emperor's reign is its own era, the count begins again when a new Emperor receives the Mandate, and the documents of the old era are cross-referenced to the new one.

More than a thousand years ago the island province of [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]] broke away in a bloody war of secession ([[lore-jenchaiscs|The Secession of Jenchai]]). The strait wrecked every invasion fleet the empire sent after it, no imperial expedition has reasserted sovereignty over the archipelago, and the court has never recognized the break: in every register Jenchai is still a province in rebellion, and the war across the strait has stayed a stalemate of raid and counter-raid. No army from the west has ever reached the empire across the Khazrin. About 548 AF the tribes of the desert were forced together for the third time, and the union never came near enough to Tantsou to threaten it ([[lore-thrdorqwnq|The Third Orghunoq]]).

The records give no year for how the present strains gathered. For generations the empire's border garrisons kept the tribes of the Black Wilds divided, with gifts to one chief and raids against another. Wogen Henzhwo bound the tribes together while infighting among the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] lords of the north thinned the frontier garrisons and the court looked to its own intrigues, and a run of hard winters and failing grazing drove the united tribes south ([[lore-wogenunion|Wogen's Union of the Black Wilds]]). Tuija Wengyan, granted the name Iron Spear Piercing the Clouds for his service, received a direct imperial order to hold his position on a critical campaign, judged it a mistake, attacked and won. The court stripped him of his rank and exiled him, and the registry recorded the victory under another commander's name; in exile he gathered soldiers disaffected by the purge that followed him and minor Zhazho nobles with grievances of their own, who brought their Wandwo with them ([[lore-tuijarevolt|The Revolt of Tuija Wengyan]]). Yanchai Zhau, formidable in the court's game for generations, lost its standing under a former head who played it badly. In the northwest the great earthquake the country there calls the [Kwojen Touhwen]{.name data-type="lore/history"} brought down Khazari holds and tore up Senalian forest, and the Chaizho came up out of its fissures.

The newest entries are the Retired Emperor's withdrawal from the throne and the young Emperor's accession, which opened a new era ([[lore-tantsouaccsn|The Young Emperor's Accession]]). The first years of the new reign are the years of the drought in the south ([[lore-southfamine|The Famine in the South]]), of Wongsen's rice, and of the Prince of Doulwen's rise at the young Emperor's side.
