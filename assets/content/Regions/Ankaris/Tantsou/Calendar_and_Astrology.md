A Tanchei astrologer hired to choose a wedding day asks three questions before she opens her tables: which year of the reign, which year of the Generation, and which month. Time in the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] is administered as well as measured. The calendar is the classification applied to time, and keeping it correctly matters to the cosmic order as much as keeping the elements or the castes. The empire uses two systems side by side: **dynastic year-naming** for law, trade and government, and the [Celestial Calendar]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, which follows the great cycles of [Lei]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} and sets the empire's ritual duties.

## Months of the Year

The twelve months of the Tantsou year follow the cycle of classification: each is governed by a domain or a power and stands for a phase of the cosmic sorting. Each month is an administrative period with its own filing days, its own rites and its own bureaus in charge.

Ten of the twelve run thirty days. [Jingyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, the month of the courts' heaviest case loads, runs thirty-two, and [Shouyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, the month of the autumn trading season, runs thirty-three: the busiest months of the administrative year are also its longest.

[Juiyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Festivals"
: Early Spring. The year's opening administrative period. All ledgers are settled from the previous year, new petitions are filed, and the cosmic accounts are reopened. The Emperor performs the annual audit of the empire's Lei. Failure to properly observe Juiyou's rituals is believed to leave the entire year's filings unprocessed.

[Mouyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Wood"
: Mid-Spring. The month takes its name from the growing timber of the spring woods, and the [Bureau of Creation]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (Tsai Sei) is ascendant. This is the month of planting, growth and the renewal of living things. Agricultural petitions are filed through the [Bureau of Allocation]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, and the Nouwen conduct planting ceremonies to register their crops with the elemental administration.

[Hoyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Fire"
: Late Spring. The [Bureau of Transformation]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (Ho Sei) is ascendant. A month of change and purification, when old classifications are burned away to make room for new ones. Households burn the year's spent spirit paper: denied petitions, lapsed contracts and documents that no longer apply. The forges are blessed, and metalworkers file their annual production reports.

[Houyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Balance"
: Early Summer. The midpoint of the year, when the [Celestial Administration]{.name data-type="affiliation"} conducts its mid-year review. [Leng]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} and [Ha]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} flows are at equilibrium. This is the most important month for resolving inter-bureau disputes, settling jurisdictional conflicts, and ensuring that no domain has accumulated more authority than its classification warrants. The mid-year festival ([Hou Nie Jui]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}) is held here.

[Touyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Earth"
: Mid-Summer. Stability and foundation. The earth element is ascendant, and this month belongs to repair: roads are mended, walls and dikes shored up, and granaries made sound. The Nouwen begin preparing for harvest, and the Bureau of Allocation processes crop-yield projections.

Jingyou - "Month of Metal"
: Late Summer. Distinction and enforcement. The metal element draws sharp lines between categories, and this month is associated with law, judgment, and the reinforcement of boundaries. Courts process their heaviest case loads, caste inspections are conducted, and the enforcement arms of the bureaus are most active. The Wohou caste considers this their patron month.

Shouyou - "Month of Water"
: Early Autumn. Flow and transition. Water carries Lei between classifications, and this month governs the movement of goods, people, and spiritual resources. Merchant caravans launch their autumn trading seasons, the [Bureau of Passage]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (Dou Sei) processes travel petitions, and purification rituals are conducted to cleanse accumulated spiritual residue.

[Wengyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Winds"
: Mid-Autumn. Communication and transmission. The wind carries spirit documents between the mortal and celestial planes, and this month is when the celestial administration's annual directives are believed to arrive. The Tanchei spend this month in intense divination, reading omens to interpret the year's incoming celestial dispatches. The season of introspection begins.

[Youjui]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of the Moon"
: Late Autumn. The Minister of the Moon ([[lore-youweidty|Youbui]]) is ascendant. The boundary between Sorted and Unsorted loosens as Ha flow strengthens with the lengthening nights. This is the month of prophecy, dreams, and night-counsel—when the [Bureau of Night Counsel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (Mei Sei) is most active and the veil between the mortal and spirit worlds is thinnest. Divination is most reliable during Youjui, but so is the risk of [Huenlei]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} seepage.

[Chiaoyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Winter"
: Early Winter. Contraction and conservation. The classification system draws inward, strengthening its core boundaries as the external world grows cold and still. Resources are conserved, spiritual reserves are hoarded, and families begin preparing their offerings for the great mid-winter settlement with the dead.

[Zhenyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Snow"
: Mid-Winter. The month of the dead. The [Festival of the Dead]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} (Singlei Jui) is held here—the most elaborate and expensive ritual in the Tantsou calendar, when families settle their annual accounts with their ancestor spirits. Massive quantities of spirit money and goods are burned, ancestral ledgers are reviewed, and the Leigen commune with the dead to receive their instructions for the coming year.

[Chenyou]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} - "Month of Frost"
: Late Winter. Closure and preparation. The final administrative period of the year. All outstanding filings must be completed, all spiritual debts settled, all unresolved petitions either processed or formally withdrawn. The [New Year Purification]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} (Shui Nie Wo) ritual cleanses homes, temples, and administrative offices to prepare for the fresh cycle. Hearth Monitors (Tsaiken) file their final annual reports on each household's moral conduct.

## Dynasty-Based Year Naming (Secular Calendar)

In law, trade and government, a year is named for the dynasty and the reigning Emperor. Court records, contracts, filings and histories all use it, and each reign is its own era.

**Naming Convention:**

Format
: "Year X of the [Emperor's Name] Emperor of the [Dynasty Name] Dynasty"

Example
: **"Year 5 of the Zhuozho Emperor of the Tanzho Dynasty"** (Tanzho Zhuozho Wuen Nie)

**Era-Based:** Each emperor's reign constitutes a separate administrative era. When a new emperor receives the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]], a new era begins and the year count resets. Documents of the old era stay valid and must be cross-referenced to the new one, and every succession sets whole bureaus of scholars to that work for years.

## The Celestial Calendar (Spiritual Calendar)

The Celestial Calendar is the deeper and older system, kept by the Tanchei astrologers and, the Tantsou hold, by the celestial administration itself. The dynastic calendar counts the reigns of men; the Celestial Calendar counts the great cycles by which the classification renews and keeps itself.

## Zero Date and the Great Convergence

The Celestial Calendar begins at [[lore-greatcnvrg|the Great Convergence]] (Dao Rong Hou), which the [[lore-celestlrdr|Celestial Order]] reckons at exactly **6,958 years ago**. It was the first perfect alignment of every celestial body, elemental force and spiritual energy after the [Great Sorting]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}: the moment, the Tantsou hold, when [[lore-tanzhodty|Tanzho]]'s classification first ran in full, every element sorted, every bureau staffed and every jurisdiction drawn, and the cosmic administration began its first cycle of business.

The figure is exact because the zero of a calendar is exact, and inside the empire it is not offered as tradition or as a way of speaking. It is the date every bureau files against, the date a Tanchei computes a Generation from, and the date a Nouwen household names when it burns the year's spirit money—stated as plain fact from the imperial court down to a village hearth-shrine, and no more open to argument than the number of elements.

The Celestial Order teaches that the Great Convergence is the founding of cosmic time and the founding of the first imperial dynasty with it. The first Emperor was the first mortal designated to administer the mortal plane—the initial appointment under the Mandate of Heaven.

## The Epoch and the Archive

The dynastic record is a separate instrument, and it does not reach anything like so far. The oldest imperial dynasty the [Bureau of Records]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} can date from documents in its own custody begins around **2500 BF**—some thirty-seven centuries after the zero the Celestial Calendar counts from. Both figures are current in Tantsou and they are not rivals. One is the epoch of cosmic time, held as revelation and stated as fact. The other is the earliest point at which the empire's own filing cabinets can produce an emperor, a dynasty and a year together.

The Tanchei explain the gap as a matter of jurisdiction, by the doctrine of [celestial custody]{.name data-type="lore/theology"}. The mortal Bureau of Records was not staffed at the Convergence. The Mandate was issued first, and the administration beneath it was assembled over the cycles that followed; until it existed there was no office on the mortal plane to hold a copy, and the filings of the earliest dynasties went where every spirit document goes at the New Year Purification—upward, into the celestial archive, and onto the [Dao Choulei]{.name data-type="lore/theology"}, the Master Registry of all classified things. An entry in celestial custody is an entry. The doctrine of the Mandate already holds that a filing is valid whether or not the reviewing bureau has processed it, and the celestial administration is not known for speed. A record the mortal plane cannot produce is therefore a record the mortal plane was never issued: an administrative condition, the Tanchei say, and no hole in the past.

This is taught, it is believed, and it also does precisely the work the empire needs done. The legitimacy of the Son of Heaven rests on an unbroken appointment reaching back to the founding of time, and the archive can vouch for the last thirty-two centuries of it. The Tanchei who hold the forbidden texts are the only people in the empire positioned to notice the difference, and the caste's settled practice—as with the Elder Races and the classification system—is to leave it where it lies. A scholar who raised it would not be contradicting doctrine. He would be filing a question with a bureau that has no form for it.

## Celestial Generations

The Celestial Calendar is structured around [Celestial Generations]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} (Teng Dao), each lasting **27 years**. A Generation represents one full administrative cycle—the time it takes for the classification system to process a complete round of cosmic business, from initial filing to final resolution.

Each Generation moves through three **Phases of Lei**, each lasting nine years:

[Cheng Lei]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} (Ascending Phase)—Years 1–9. Leng flow is ascendant. New classifications are established, new projects initiated, new filings opened. This is a phase of expansion, dynamism, and institutional growth. The empire tends toward ambition, conquest, and innovation during Ascending phases—all of which the classification system tolerates because the cosmic administration has surplus capacity.

[Hou Lei]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} (Harmonized Phase)—Years 10–18. Leng and Ha are in balance. The classification system operates at peak efficiency. This is the phase of maintenance, stability, and administrative consolidation. The empire's institutions run smoothly, the bureaus process their workloads without backlog, and the boundaries between sorted categories are at their strongest. The Tantsou consider this the ideal state—the way things are supposed to be.

[Jong Lei]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} (Descending Phase)—Years 19–27. Ha flow is ascendant. Classifications begin to loosen, boundaries blur, and the cosmic administration approaches the limits of its current cycle. Old filings expire. Institutional fatigue sets in. The risk of Huenlei seepage increases. This is a phase of consolidation, retrenchment, and preparation for the next cycle's renewal. Wise emperors focus on preservation during Descending phases; ambitious ones who attempt expansion during this phase risk overextending the classification system.

## The 15 Spirit Animals

Each year within a Celestial Generation is associated with a **spirit animal**—a classified spiritual entity whose characteristics influence the cosmic administration's processing during that year. The 15 animals cycle through the 27-year Generation in overlapping patterns, with each animal appearing in at least two of the three phases.

The spirit animals are patterns that shape how the classification handles its business in a given year, and the Tantsou neither worship them nor treat them as charms. A child born in the [Year of the Wind Dragon]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} is classified under the Wind Dragon's pattern for life, and it governs how her moral ledger is kept, which jurisdiction her petitions fall to and which bureau is likely to handle her affairs.

## Holidays and Festivals

The Tantsou festivals are celebrations, and they are also filing days the empire must meet to keep the classification running. A district that misses one has failed to send heaven its paperwork, and the Tanchei expect the consequences to show in the harvest, the weather and the courts.

## Festival of Harmonious Lei (Hou Lei Jui)

The most significant festival in Tantsou, held on the first day of Juiyou at the new year. The Emperor performs the **annual audit**—a formal review of the empire's Lei, in which he (assisted by the Tanchei) symbolically opens the celestial filing system for the new year, confirms that the previous year's accounts are settled, and authorizes the processing of new business. Feasts, lantern-lightings and public performances surround the rite, and the people's part in them is counted as their contribution to the audit: every household that takes part is entered in the empire's reckoning of its Lei.

Time
: First day of Juiyou (new year, transition from winter to spring)

Castes Involved
: Zhazho (Emperor and Warlords lead the audit), Tanchei (process divinations and verify the cosmic accounts), Wanduo (perform the magical workings that formally open the new filing cycle), Nouwen (community participation registers the population's collective Lei)

## Festival of the Five Elements (Wuen Tsai Jui)

A five-day festival in which each day is dedicated to one of the primary elemental classifications. Its purpose is to confirm that the elements are holding: that air still behaves as air, fire as fire and earth as earth. Each day's rituals are specifically designed to test and reinforce one element: kites and bells on the day of Air, forge-work on the day of Metal, purification burning on the day of Fire, water rituals on the day of Water, construction on the day of Earth.

Time
: Mid-Mouyou, when the elements are believed to be in their most stable alignment

## The Mid-Year Festival of Balance (Hou Nie Jui)

Held at the height of summer in Houyou, this is the mid-year reconciliation, when heaven balances its half-year accounts. Disputes are settled, conflicts resolved, and inter-bureau jurisdictional issues addressed. For ordinary people, this is the time to settle debts, resolve family quarrels, and ensure that their moral ledgers are not accumulating hidden debits.

## Moon Festival (You Jui)

Celebrated during Youjui in late autumn, when the Minister of the Moon's jurisdiction is strongest and the boundary between Sorted and Unsorted is thinnest. The festival has two purposes: to receive heaven's yearly dispatches, read through prophecy and divination, and to strengthen the boundary the rising Ha flow is wearing thin. The lanterns are lit as points of Leng, the bright flow, to shore up that boundary, and they are lovely as well. Offerings to Youbui request that her bureau maintain classification integrity through the dangerous winter months.

## Festival of the Dead (Singlei Jui)

The most elaborate and expensive festival in the calendar, held during Zhenyou in mid-winter. This is the yearly settlement with the ancestor spirits, when families burn massive quantities of spirit money and goods to provision their ancestors for the coming year, review the ancestral ledgers, and receive commands from the dead through the Leigen. See [[lore-ancstrwrshp|Ancestor Worship]] for the full implications of this festival, including its role in reinforcing the spiritual aristocracy.

## New Year Purification (Shui Nie Wo)

The final administrative event of the year, held during Chenyou. Every household, temple and office is ritually cleansed and its accounts closed. Old spirit documents are burned (transmitting them to the celestial archive), expired filings are cleared, and the Hearth Monitors (Tsaiken) submit their final annual moral reports on each household. The purification closes the books, and the new year's audit opens them again.
