## Overview {#overview}

Zhowen, the Dragon's Center, is the capital of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] and the seat of the Emperor, a city of about two million people on one of the great rivers of the [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou Region]]. It is two cities, one on each bank of the [Tengshou River]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the River of Heaven, which is large and wide. Between them, on an island in the middle of the stream, stands the walled enclave of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], which nobody but the Zhazho and their personal attendants may enter.

The city's hallmark is order. Each bank is the mirror of the other, street for street and quarter for quarter, and both are laid out from the island outward by one plan. Zhowen does not follow the lie of the land: where a hill stands in the way of a street, the hill is cut down; where a marsh lies across a quarter, it is filled; and the river itself runs between banks faced in cut stone and straightened to the plan. The throne holds the river basins around the city through its own officers, and their people, some 18 million, send their grain to it.

## Arriving {#arriving}

Most travelers come to Zhowen by water. Grain barges, tribute boats and passenger boats come down the river and in along the canals, which run straight across the basin to the city and enter it at right angles to the river. From the water a newcomer sees the enclave first, a long wall on the island with the roofs of the palace rising behind it, and then the two cities on either side, so alike that the two skylines cannot be told apart from the water.

At the quays the city's officers board each boat before anything comes ashore. They read every passenger's classification tablet, the _Zhau Mou_, and the cargo master's manifest, and a traveler outside the district where he is known by face goes no farther without a tablet that the registry can confirm ([[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]]). A Tantsou merchant pays his dues at the quay and walks into the city. A foreign merchant is classified by the commercial officers of the quay, who issue him the _Shwonong Dou Chou_, the merchant transit document, for the length of his business. A foreign envoy waits on the boat, or in the customs hall, until the chancellery says where he is to be lodged.

Those who come by land travel the embankment roads that run beside the canals. The roads end at gates in the outer quarters, where the same officers read the same tablets. There is no city wall to pass through. The gates are posts in the open street, and the registry, not stone, keeps the city closed.

## The Court and the Game {#present}

The Emperor on the throne, [[being-tengrauemp|Tengrau]], is young and newly crowned, and he is still finding the reach of his own power. The Retired Emperor, [[being-yanzhortrd|Yanzho]], lives in the background of the court. [[lore-weizhairui|The Jade Fan Dance]] (_Wei Zhai Rwi_), the court's game of intrigue, has been played hard around the young Emperor since his accession, because the Princes (_Zho Zhei_) each mean to be the one who shapes him. The Caste-Registrar, [[being-cstrgstrtnts|Third Son of the Archivist of the Clan of Lanterns Along the Bank]], keeper of the rolls on which every subject's caste is entered, is his chief counselor, and every Prince courts the Caste-Registrar for that reason.

The four Princes stand in no fixed order. The Prince of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]], [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], is the strongest and the most feared. His water-gifted [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]] have brought rain to the delta while the rain fails elsewhere, and his granaries sell rice to the Princes whose provinces have gone dry. Some at court say his sorcerers are behind the drought. Nobody has proven it, and the saying is itself a move in the game. The Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], is younger, rising fast, a reformer with the young Emperor's ear. The Prince of [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]], [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], and the Prince of [[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]], [[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]], are bitter rivals over an old marriage between their houses and an heir who died, and each takes the other's loss as his own gain.

None of these positions holds for long. In Zhowen a rumor, a shaming or a misfiled petition does as much harm as an army, or more, and a reading of the stars by the right [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] can raise a family or destroy it. The rise of a gifted Wandwo, or of a group of them, can tip the balance between the Princes in a season.

When the embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] reaches Zhowen asking for direct relations with the Empire, the Prince of Doulwen takes it up first and openly, for the foreign weight it adds to his hold on the Emperor's ear. The court reads the request as a breach of the Speaker's protocol, since a foreigner reaches the Emperor only through the Speaker, and direct relations ask for a door that has never existed. Granting it would shift power among the Princes, so the game decides the question, not the Emperor. The other three Princes each read the embassy as a move against them, and each works to turn it against its sponsor ([[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dúnavarre to Tantsou]]).

In the city the game shows as price and paper. The price of rice in the markets of both shores follows the granaries of Wongsen. The offices on the shores are still at the work of the new reign: each Emperor's reign is its own era of the calendar, and the scholars of the chancellery cross-reference every filing of the old era to the new one ([[lore-clndrstrlgy|Calendar and Astrology]]). A filing that waits in that work can wait a year, and a player in the game can lose by the delay. Rice from Wongsen reaches the city in barges that the Governor's officers hold at the canal locks until the seals are checked, and a barge held there moves the price of rice on both shores.

## Order and Law {#order-and-law}

Zhowen has two authorities, and the river divides them. The enclave belongs to the Zhazho, and its own walls keep it. Everything else, the two shores, their administrative centers and their caste quarters, is held for the throne by the **Governor of Zhowen**, [[being-gvrnrzhowen|Eldest Son of the Gate Captain of the Clan of the Heron on the Wall]], an officer of the Emperor who answers to the throne alone.

The bridges are the law made visible. A long bridge runs from each shore to the island: the [Jingdou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the Gold Road, from one shore, and the [Bwidou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the Silver Road, from the other. A person who sets foot on one without permission is shot dead by the archers on the enclave walls. No magistrate hears the case, and no plea is entered. Permission to cross is written, and the officers at each bridge foot keep the day's list of those who hold it. A forged permission is the surest way to send a person to the archers, and servants who carry a trader's messages across the river are the ones sent.

The only people who speak to the Emperor are his attendants and the Princes. Everyone else, a minister, a general, an ambassador, speaks to the **Speaker**, who hears the question, whispers it to the Emperor and speaks the Emperor's answer aloud. The Speaker comes out to the gate-court of the enclave to hear them, and the audience is held there, at the outer court gate: a petitioner crosses the bridge on his written permission, waits in the gate-court and goes back across the bridge, and he never sets foot inside the wall. The answer the Speaker gives is the answer on the record.

On the shores the law is the _Teng Lou_, the Heavenly Law, read by the _Gwichai_, the magistrates, in the courts of the administrative centers. The _Wonong_ walk the streets, take a thief or a brawler to the magistrate and stand at the doors of the courts. A case climbs as it does anywhere in the empire: the street, then the magistrate, and above the magistrate the Governor's court, which in the capital takes the place a Prince's court holds in a province. Every household of the city is on the rolls of a district, every district has its registrar, and the registrars are rotated from district to district under the Caste-Registrar, leaving each district's seal with the office when they move on. A person who is not on a district's rolls has no standing in Zhowen's courts at all.

## What the City Lives By {#what-the-city-lives-by}

Zhowen lives by governing. The ministries of the imperial administration, the chancellery that receives the reports of every Prince, and the offices of the Caste-Registrar and the census stand in the administrative centers on the two shores, and the Tanchei who staff them, with their clerks, copyists, seal-cutters and messengers, are the largest single trade in the city. The [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] officers who command the city's garrison and hold the throne's basins keep their households in the city as well.

Two million people eat more than any basin grows. The river and the canals carry the tribute grain of the throne's own basins and of the provinces to the city's granaries, which stand in long rows along the quays of both shores. Where the tribute falls short, as it does in the dry provinces, the granaries make up the difference with rice bought from Wongsen, at the price Wongsen's Prince names. The Keeper of his granaries sells to the throne as he sells to the other Princes.

The court's demand feeds a city of craftsmen. The [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihwo]] workshops of Zhowen make silk, lacquer, porcelain and fine metalwork to the standards the court sets, and the best of it goes west along [[lore-celestialroad|The Celestial Road]] with the caravans that buy it. The [[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]] merchants who buy, store and move all of it are rich, indispensable and ranked beneath the farmers who grow the city's food. Every bale and crate that moves through the quays is taxed, entered and sealed, and the smugglers of Zhowen are as skilled at their trade as the scribes are at theirs.

## Quarters {#quarters}

**The enclave.** The island holds the palace of the Emperor and the households of the imperial family, behind a wall that rises straight from the water on every side. Its gates face the two bridges and nothing else. The Princes cross into it when they attend the Emperor; their attendants cross with them; and the city sees no more of it than its wall, its roofs and the archers on its walks. Before each gate the bridge widens into a gate-court, a paved court on piers outside the wall, where the Speaker comes out to hear petitioners.

**The bridges.** The Jingdou and the Bwidou each run on piers of cut stone across the full width of the stream between a shore and the island, and each is a marvel of engineering. They are also the emptiest streets in the city: on most days the only people on them are the petitioners and the others the list permits.

**The administrative centers.** On each shore, facing the island across the water, stand the halls of the administration: the ministries, the chancellery, the courts, the offices of the registry and the Governor's own hall. The two centers mirror each other across the island, hall for hall, so that a petitioner on either bank finds the office he needs in the same place.

**The caste quarters.** Outward from the centers lie the quarters of the castes, each in its own district and each district on the rolls of its own registrar. The order follows the ladder of the castes: nearest the centers live the Wohou and the Tanchei who staff the offices, beyond them the Tsaihwo workshops, then the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]], then the Shwonong markets and warehouses along the canals, and outermost the [[lore-noutsou|Nwitsou]]. The Wandwo have no quarter of their own, since they live in the households of their owners. Each quarter on one bank has its twin on the other, laid out to the same plan, with streets of the same width meeting at the same angles.

**The quays and the granaries.** Along both shores, above and below the island, run the quays where the barges unload, and behind them the granaries. The canals meet the river at the quays, and their locks are kept by the Governor's officers.

## Faith and Festivals {#faith-and-festivals}

Every household of Zhowen keeps an ancestor shrine, and the city's temples of the [[affiliation-tengwouronglei|Celestial Order]] file the petitions of the living with the bureaus of heaven. The festivals are filing days as well as feasts ([[lore-clndrstrlgy|Calendar and Astrology]]), and the capital keeps them on the largest scale in the empire.

The year opens with the [Festival of Harmonious Lei]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, when the Emperor performs the annual audit, the review of the empire's Lei in which the Tanchei verify heaven's accounts and the Wandwo open the new filing cycle. The feasts, lantern-lightings and public performances that surround it fill both shores, and every household that takes part is entered in the empire's reckoning.

In late autumn the [Moon Festival]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} lights lanterns along the whole length of both banks, as points of the bright flow that shore up the boundary of the sorted world, with offerings to [[lore-youweidty|Youbwi]], Keeper of the Jade Seal. In midwinter the [Festival of the Dead]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} fills the city with the smoke of burned spirit money, and the year ends with the [New Year Purification]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, when every household, temple and office is cleansed, its accounts are closed and the Hearth Monitors submit their reports.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

A traveler lodges where his classification puts him. A Tantsou traveler finds an inn of his own caste in his caste's quarter, and an innkeeper reads every guest's tablet before the guest sleeps. A traveler who cannot show a tablet cannot be entered and is not given a bed.

A foreign party has fewer choices. A merchant with a transit document lodges where the commercial officers send him, in a walled and watched compound for foreign traders. An embassy lodges in the compound the chancellery assigns it, and keeps to it until it is summoned. A foreigner with no classification at all has no lodging the law recognizes; he sleeps on a boat or in the street, and he is held the first time a Wonong asks for his tablet.

## History {#history}

Zhowen is the capital of the present dynasty. The court reckons the city's years as the calendar does, by the dynasty and the reigning Emperor. Each Emperor's reign is its own era, its filings are kept in the chancellery on the shores, and when a new Emperor receives the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]] the era's count begins again. The newest entries are the Retired Emperor's withdrawal from the throne and the young Emperor's accession, which opened a new era and set the chancellery to cross-referencing the old one. The first years of the new reign are the years of the drought in the dry provinces, of Wongsen's rice, and of the Prince of Doulwen's rise at the young Emperor's side.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The petition.** When the Dúnavarre embassy arrives, the Prince of Doulwen's officers need its papers filed in the right office ahead of the cross-referencing that would hold them for a year. Three other households want the papers misfiled, and the clerks of the chancellery are for sale ([[scenario-flngnwrgn|A Filing in the New Reign]]).
- **The list.** A servant of a foreign merchant has vanished from the traders' compound, and the merchant believes he was sent to the bridge with a forged permission to be shot. The officers at the bridge foot keep the list.
- **The rice.** A string of rice barges from Wongsen lies at a canal lock above the city, held by the Governor's officers on a question of seals while the price of rice rises on both shores. Wongsen's factor wants the barges released; a grain merchant who stands to gain from every day they wait wants them held.
