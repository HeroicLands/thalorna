## Overview {#overview}

Jenshou, Nine Waters, is a district town of [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]] in [[place-mwonouplnd|the Mwonou Uplands]], downriver of the provincial seat at [[place-mwonou|Mwonou]]. Here a great river leaves the uplands for the central basins and breaks into nine channels across its own silt before it joins again below the town. About 7,000 people live in Jenshou.

The town is two towns. The lower town stands on a mound above the channels, with the magistrate's hall, the market and [[place-jenshouhall|the District Hall of Jenshou]], where the district's register is kept. The upper town lies on the flat behind the [Tablet Bank]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the dike that carries the boundary-stones of the three upper villages, and the rice terraces climb the slopes above it.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Jenshou lives by rice and by the river. The terraces and the channel lands grow rice in an ordinary year, and the [[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]] of the river trade buy and sell along the channels, where ferries cross at each of the nine. The potters and dyers of the lower town sell their wares at its market.

The dikes are the town's life. Every Month of Earth the district corvée mends the banks of the nine channels and the Tablet Bank, a thousand men or more for thirty days, and a year the corvée is called elsewhere is a year the banks go unmended.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Prince of Mwonou, [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], holds the district through an administrator of the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]], who keeps its revenue, its courts and its corvée, and through the magistrate who hears its cases. The district registrar, [[being-jnshoregstr|Second Daughter of the Seal-Cutter of the Clan of Cold Spring Under Pines]], keeps the rolls of every person in the district at the District Hall, and the district's [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] keep its annal there.

## The Present {#present}

The rain has failed over the uplands, and the nine channels run low between banks raised against floods. The upper terraces, farthest from the water, are dry. The district granaries are open, and the famine petitions of the upper villages come to the magistrate's court.

The District Hall feeds the dead who have nobody else to feed them: the tablets of the lines the old flood left without an heir, and the tablets of [[place-rinnou|Rinnou]]. The rice comes from the district stores, at the district's cost, in a year when the stores are short for the living.

## History {#history}

The year of Jenshou is written in its annal, which a house of the [Kwo Clan]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} kept for nine reigns without a missing year. In a reign of a dynasty long since passed, a Prince of the province called the district's corvée upriver to raise his ancestral hall at the provincial seat, and in the second and third years of the call the dikes went unmended. In the seventh year of the reign it rained for nineteen days, the Tablet Bank opened, and the three upper villages and the upper town went under the water. Two thousand six hundred and ten people were not found at the count, and the magistrate who had petitioned three times for the corvée's return answered for the dike with his life. The Tanchei schools teach the record of those years as [[lore-annaljenshou|The Annal of Jenshou]].

Later, an administrator of the district took his own life in protest when his Prince ordered the village of Rinnou entered whole as [[lore-noutsou|Nwitsou]], and the one thing he asked was granted: the tablets of Rinnou were carried unbroken to the District Hall. His testament is [[lore-lastrecordiron|The Last Record of Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain]].
