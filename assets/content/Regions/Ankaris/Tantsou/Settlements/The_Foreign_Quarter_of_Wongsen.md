## Overview {#overview}

The Foreign Quarter of Wongsen is the walled part of [[place-wongsen|Wongsen]], the Sea Gate, where the foreign merchants who trade at the port live. Wongsen is the seat of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]] and the port where the delta of the great rivers meets the sea, and the quarter is where the outside world is let into it: inside walls, through gates, watched and taxed. The [[lore-tantsoutribclt|Tantsou Tributary Peoples]] of the southern coast deal with the foreigners more readily than the people of the core, and the quarter is tributary country.

A party comes to the quarter because a foreigner in Tantsou ends up there. It is where a foreign merchant lives for the length of his business, where his goods wait in the warehouses, where his classification is checked at the gate, and where, the sailors say, a traveler who needs a tablet he does not have can be offered one.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The quarter is the Prince of Wongsen's. He rules the port through his [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] officers, and one of them, the Warden of the Foreign Quarter, [[being-wrdnfrgnqrtr|Second Son of the Tower Captain of the Clan of Banners Above the Harbor, near Wongsen]], keeps its walls and its gates and taxes and watches the merchants inside them.

The merchants hold their place by classification. A foreigner is no inhabitant of the empire, and a foreign merchant who comes to trade is classified by the commercial administration, which issues him the _Shwonong Dou Chou_, the merchant transit document, with a tablet to carry. It gives him the standing of a [[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]] for the length of his business. The province enters him as a Foreign Resident, kept in the walled quarter of a port. The great Shwonong houses of the empire hold its trade with the foreign merchants of the quarter, and their factors meet them there.

The quarter stands on the [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]]. A foreigner inside the borders is unclassified until a bureau says otherwise, and an unclassified person in the sorted world is a standing breach, so the empire classifies the foreigners it trades with and keeps them where it can see them. Every port of the empire that trades by sea keeps such a quarter, carefully walled, carefully watched and carefully taxed. A classified foreigner stays marked as other: his petitions, when a bureau accepts them, are handled last, and the quarter's walls put the same rule where it can be seen.

The quarter's population is not counted. The province enters each merchant as a Foreign Resident only for the length of his business, and a trading classification runs out in days, so the merchants who fill the quarter change as their business ends.

## Places {#places}

- **The gates.** The Warden's officers keep them, and a foreigner going in or out shows his tablet with the seal of the bureau that issued it.
- **The warehouses.** Foreign goods wait here, documented and taxed, for the Shwonong factors who buy them, and a merchant's goods stay here when his classification does not.
- **The streets.** Tributary people of the southern coast speak Tantsou in the accents and borrowed words of their own kingdoms, and the foreign merchants do their daily business with them.
- **Behind the warehouses.** Sailors of the quarter say that a tablet can be bought behind the warehouses that no bureau issued.

## Current Affairs {#present}

The delta has rain while the other provinces go dry, and their Princes buy rice from the granaries of Wongsen at the price the Prince of Wongsen names. The rice goes up the rivers from the port in barges, and every significant movement of goods through Wongsen is documented, classified and taxed.

On the eastern coast the fleets of the Jenchai houses of the [[affiliation-shuiwojaidmn|Shuiwojai Domain]] and the [[affiliation-rinjaidmn|Rinjai Domain]] take tolls and ransoms on [[place-easternstrt|the Eastern Strait]] and plunder the mainland shore. The coastal magistrates want the raids stopped and their captured officers returned, and the court has no fleet that can clear the strait. Tantsou ships still work the southern ports and reach [[place-nilatir|Nilatir]], the outport of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]], and the northern coasts of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]].

## Dangers {#dangers}

Smuggling is a highly developed profession in every port of the empire, and the quarter is where it meets the outside. The [[affiliation-haluozhao|Black Lotus Society]] trades in forged tablets, and forging a tablet is punished by execution in public, because it is an attempt to put oneself in a category the cosmic order did not assign.

A foreigner without classification has no standing at all: he cannot own property, sign a contract or seek the law's protection, a crime against him is no crime under the law, and a crime by him is a breach of the order, punished fast. A merchant whose classification runs out is that foreigner the morning after.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The expiring classification.** A foreign merchant's trading classification runs out in days, and his goods are still in a warehouse of the quarter. He needs a buyer, an extension or a way to move his goods before the Warden's officers come for the tablet, and he needs it from people the law can see.
- **The wrong tablet.** A tablet bought from the wrong person in the quarter is either a forgery or a trap, and each answer has someone who wants it found: the Warden, whose officers watch the quarter, and the Black Lotus, which trades in forged tablets.
- **The officer across the strait.** A coastal magistrate wants a captured officer back from the Jenchai houses across the strait, which the Empire calls a rebellious province, and he wants the offer carried by someone who is no Tantsou official: a foreigner of the quarter, who is no subject of either.
