## Overview {#overview}

The Tablet Bank is the dike of [[place-jenshou|Jenshou]], the river town of [[place-mwonouplnd|the Mwonou Uplands]] where a great river breaks into nine channels across its own silt. It stands above the town and holds the river off the flat where the upper town lies, with the rice terraces of the three upper villages climbing the slopes behind. Along its crest run the boundary-stones of those three villages, each cut with a seal and each set from the district register, which fixes every terrace above the bank.

A party comes to the bank for its stones or for its river: to stand witness when a stone is set, to carry a page of the register from the District Hall to the crest, to work on it with the corvée, or to see the place where [[lore-annaljenshou|The Annal of Jenshou]] records the river breaking through.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The bank is the district's, and the district is [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]]'s. The Prince of Mwonou, of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], holds Jenshou through an administrator of the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]], who keeps its revenue, its courts and its corvée, and every Month of Earth the district corvée mends the banks of the nine channels and the Tablet Bank, a thousand men or more for thirty days. The magistrate of Jenshou answers for the dike of his district.

The stones belong to the register. The district's [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] set a boundary-stone from the district register kept at [[place-jenshouhall|the District Hall of Jenshou]], on the mound of the lower town, and a stone that leans is set upright before witnesses and its seal renewed. A dispute over a stone between two terraces goes to the magistrate's court.

## The Bank's Layers {#history}

The annal of Jenshou records the bank through the reign of the Houweng Emperor, of a dynasty long since passed. In its first year the corvée raised the bank by half a span. In the third the ground shook, and one boundary-stone was found leaning toward the river; it was set upright before witnesses and its seal renewed. In the year after the third, the corvée was called upriver by the Prince's seal to raise his ancestral hall at the provincial seat, upriver, and in that year the magistrate's court heard more disputes over boundary-stones between terraces than over anything else. In the fifth and sixth years the dikes went unmended, and in the sixth the three hundred and ten men left in the district raised the Tablet Bank at the sixth stone, where it was lowest.

In the seventh year it rained for nineteen days, and on the twentieth the Tablet Bank opened at the stone after the third. The three upper villages and the upper town went under the water, and the lower town on its mound was not reached. Two thousand six hundred and ten people were not found at the count, and the magistrate answered for the dike with his life. The annal writes that the river _came_, the verb for heaven's allotment, at the request of an officer of the Prince's court, and that the bank _opened_, the verb for a bank that was not kept, and the Tanchei schools teach the two verbs in two readings that disagree.

In the eighth year the corvée of Jenshou and of two neighboring districts, two thousand men for sixty days, closed the breach. The Tanchei set the boundary-stones of the drained terraces again from the district register, which had come through the flood in the lower town, and not one terrace was lost to the register. In the ninth year the bank was raised by one span along its whole length, and a new stone was set at the stone after the third, its seal cut in the presence of the district.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The rain has failed over the uplands. The nine channels run low between banks raised against floods, and the Tablet Bank stands high above a river that comes nowhere near its crest. The upper terraces, farthest from the water, are dry, the district granaries are open, and the famine petitions of the upper villages come down past the bank to the magistrate's court in the lower town.

The corvée is still due on the bank every Month of Earth. Among the men who raise it are the men of the upper villages, whose terraces behind it have nothing to drink.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbors}

The river is the bank's neighbor. A flood that tops a sound bank is heaven's allotment to a district, and a bank that fails is a bank that was not kept, so a bank that fails puts its magistrate before the Prince's court.

Hunger is the other. The upper villages are short of rice, and in some districts of the uplands the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]] have risen against the magistrates who collect the tribute.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

- **[1] The crest.** The top of the bank above the flat of the upper town, with the channels below it on one side and the terraces climbing away on the other. The boundary-stones stand along it, each with its seal.
- **[2] The stone after the third.** The stone between the third and the fifth carries no number, because an official record writes no four ([[lore-numerology|Numerology]]); the register calls it the stone after the third. The annal names it as the place where the bank opened.
- **[3] The sixth stone.** The stone the annal names where the bank stood lowest before the flood.
- **[4] The view down.** From the crest the lower town stands on its mound across the channels, with the magistrate's hall, the market and the District Hall where the register of every stone is kept.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The stone that walked downhill.** A household of an upper village says that a stone on the bank has been shifted toward its neighbor's terraces, in a year when a terrace's share of the water matters more than its rice. The Tanchei set the stone again from the register, and the clerk who carries the copied page from the District Hall to the crest wants an escort that neither household has paid.
- **The seal at the stone after the third.** A scholar from the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] who has read the annal wants to stand at the stone after the third and see its seal for himself. He needs guards, and someone to stand beside him when the district's officers ask for his tablet, and the district's Tanchei teach the first reading.
- **The corvée in a dry year.** The Month of Earth comes, and the corvée is called to raise a bank against a river that is barely running. The men of the upper villages ask why, the magistrate who answers for the dike wants the work done, and a party on the road finds itself among a thousand men deciding whether to pick up their baskets.
