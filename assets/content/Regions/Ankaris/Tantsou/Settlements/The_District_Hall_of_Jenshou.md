## Overview {#overview}

The District Hall of Jenshou is the registrar's hall of the district of [[place-jenshou|Jenshou]] in [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]]. It stands in the lower town, on the mound above the nine channels, and the flood that took the upper town never reached it. Every person of the district is entered in its register, by caste, clan, position and place, and every terrace by its boundary-stones.

A party comes here to be entered, to have a classification read or corrected, or to find a person by the description the register gives. Others come to see a page they are not allowed to see.

## Inside {#inside}

The register fills the hall: bound rolls of the district's persons and terraces in their cases, the annal of Jenshou beside them, and the district's seal. The hall's bell hangs above the cases; the annal records it ringing without a hand when the ground shook.

At the back of the hall stand the shelves of the dead. Four hundred and six tablets are of the lines the old flood left without an heir, recovered from the shrines under the water. Beside them stand the tablets of [[place-rinnou|Rinnou]], whose people are [[lore-noutsou|Nwitsou]] and keep no line. The district sets rice before them at every death-day and at the [Festival of the Dead]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and the cost is entered to the district.

## Who Is There {#who-is-there}

The district registrar keeps the rolls under the Caste-Registrar of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]]. A registrar is rotated from district to district, and the district's seal stays with the office, never with the person, so the seal of Jenshou has passed through many hands and never left the hall. The district's [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] keep the annal and enter the year's signs in it with their readings.

## The Present {#present}

The rain has failed over the uplands, and the district stores that feed the tablets are the stores the living draw on. The hall still sets the rice before the shelves at every death-day. Each petition for a ration, each famine petition and each death passes through the register, and the hall's clerks write more in a hungry year than in a good one.

## History {#history}

When the Tablet Bank opened in the flood of the seventh year, the upper town went under the water and the lower town did not. The district register, kept on the mound, came through, and the Tanchei set the boundary-stones of every drained terrace again from it; not one terrace was lost to the register. The recorder's entry for that flood, written in the verb a Prince's officer asked for and sealed with the court's seal and her own, is the heart of [[lore-annaljenshou|The Annal of Jenshou]].

The tablets of Rinnou came to the hall by the one request of a dying administrator, set out in [[lore-lastrecordiron|The Last Record of Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain]].

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The description.** A traveler newly classified at the frontier needs his entry copied into the district's rolls before he may go farther, and the clerk finds a page that already describes someone very like him.
- **The rice for the shelves.** The district stores are short, and a party is asked, by a household in arrears, to learn whether the rice set before the tablets of the orphaned lines is all reaching them.
- **The second reading.** A Tanchei master who teaches the second reading wants the sealed entry of the seventh year compared with the copies the schools learn from, and the district registrar must be persuaded to open its case.
