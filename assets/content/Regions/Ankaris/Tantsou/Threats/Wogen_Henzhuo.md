# Appearance {#appearance}

A broad, unhurried man in a felt coat sewn with iron plates sits his horse a little apart from his captains and lets them do the talking. He is past his youth, his face cut by wind and old scars, and he watches whoever is speaking rather than the one he is speaking to. When he does talk, it is quietly, in the clipped trade-speech of the frontier markets, and the riders around him go still to hear it.

# Dossier {#dossier}

Wogen Henzhuo, called the Master Tiger of the Steppes, is the warlord of the [Hénhatou]{.name data-type="place/region"}, the Black Wilds: the steppe and broken hill-country that lies north of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] beyond the marches toward the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm Region]]. He has done what no one in living memory managed and united its feuding tribes under one banner, and his riders are raiding the empire's northern provinces. He is a strategist before he is a warrior, and the frontier commanders have learned that the campaigns he loses are the ones he meant to lose.

## Background

**The divided steppe.** For generations the tribes of the Hénhatou fought one another over grazing, wells and blood debts, and the empire's border garrisons kept them divided with gifts to one chief and raids against another. Wogen began as the war-leader of a minor tribe. He bound the others to him one at a time: by marriage where a chief had a daughter, by treaty where a chief had sense, and by slaughter where he had neither. To the tribes he presented himself as their strength gathered in one hand, and he promised them the riches of the south.

**The open door.** He chose his moment. Infighting among the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] lords of the north thinned the frontier garrisons, and the court was too busy with its own intrigues to notice the steppe uniting. By the time a Warlord's report reached the capital, the border was porous and the tribes answered to one man.

**The hungry years.** A run of hard winters and failing grazing drove the tribes south in any case. Wogen turned that hunger into a war aim: the steppe could starve, or it could take the empire's rice basins.

## Psyche

### Personality

Patient, watchful and cold. He listens far more than he speaks, never threatens what he does not intend to do, and keeps promises to allies and enemies alike, which makes both trust him more than they should.

### Motivation

Land and grain for the tribes first; after that, a throne. He does not want to burn the empire, he wants to rule a piece of it, and he studies its registers and its castes as carefully as its forts.

### Strengths

He turns raiders into soldiers. His horsemen ride in disciplined companies, strike supply lines and outposts instead of towns, and withdraw before an imperial army can force a battle. He reads his enemies' politics as well as their ground.

### Weaknesses

The confederation is held together by his person and by plunder. A season without victories, or a blow to Wogen himself, would set the tribes at each other's throats again, and every chief he subdued remembers how.

## Plot Hooks

The Burned Granaries
: A chain of supply depots in the northern provinces burns in one night. The garrison commander blames the riders; the party finds a forged order that emptied the guard the night before, and the forger was someone inside the province.

An Offer to the Exiles
: Wogen's envoys are seeking out exiled Wohou and rebel factions, offering them places in the realm he means to carve from the north. One of them has approached [[being-tuijawengyan|Tuija Wengyan]], and the empire would pay well to know the answer.

The Lost Caravan
: A Shuonong merchant's caravan vanished on the northern road with a cargo the merchant will not name. The steppe riders have it, and Wogen is willing to bargain for something other than silver.

The Example
: Peasants in a northern district, crushed by war taxes, have begun to say the Master Tiger would treat them better than their lord. The magistrate wants the talk stopped, and the people saying it are not wrong about the taxes.
