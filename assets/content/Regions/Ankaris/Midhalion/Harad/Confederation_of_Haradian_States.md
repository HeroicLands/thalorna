The Confederation of Haradian States is a collection of city-states on the eastern edge of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]]. Harad is a powerful maritime region known for its naval supremacy, merchant guilds, and complex political intrigue. The coastal states are filled with wealthy trading cities, while the inland areas are home to tribal communities and ancient ruins. Roughly twelve years ago, Harad fought and won a war of independence from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], but the freedom the veterans bled for has been seized by merchant oligarchs, and the confederation's politics are as cutthroat as they ever were under imperial rule.

## Geography

Harad occupies the eastern shores and hinterland of the Vylarian Sea. The coastline is deeply indented, creating natural harbors that have made Harad the premier maritime region of western Ankaris. Inland, the terrain rises from fertile coastal plains through rolling hills to arid scrubland that transitions into the wastes of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]] and [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]] to the east. The climate is warm and dry, with hot summers tempered by sea breezes.

An archipelago off the eastern coast plays a critical role in controlling trade routes, further strengthening Harad's dominance on the seas. These islands serve as naval bases, trading entrepôts, and—for those who know the channels—smuggling havens.

## History

### Under the Empire

Harad was among the first regions absorbed by the expanding Vylarian Republic, and when the Republic became the Empire in Year 1, Harad was already an old possession. For centuries it served as the empire's eastern anchor—the gateway to trade with [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]], [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]], the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]], and lands beyond. Haradian harbors funneled eastern wealth into imperial coffers, and Haradian ships carried Vylarian goods to markets the legions could never reach. It was, by any measure, one of the empire's most valuable provinces.

It was also one of its most restive. The Haradian city-states had been trading powers long before Vylaria conquered them, and they never fully accepted the role of provincial subjects. Haradians paid their taxes, obeyed their governors, and sent their sons to serve in the legions—but they did so with a resentment that grew deeper with each generation.

### The Rise of the Merchant Princes

The seeds of rebellion were planted not by radicals but by success. As the Empire's reach extended eastward and southward, Haradian merchants became indispensable intermediaries—they knew the languages, the sea routes, the customs, and the contacts that Vylarian traders lacked. The great trading families of [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]], [[affiliation-kethara|Kethara]], and [[affiliation-ashkabel|Ashkabel]] grew spectacularly wealthy, building commercial empires within the political empire that ruled them.

These **merchant princes**—the heads of the great trading houses—accumulated power that sat uncomfortably alongside Vylarian authority. They controlled the shipping lanes, the warehouses, the counting houses, and the networks of agents and factors that made eastern trade possible. A Vylarian governor might issue edicts, but it was Haradian merchants who decided whether goods actually moved. The empire needed Harad's commerce far more than Harad needed the empire's governance.

The Vylarian court was not blind to this. As the merchant princes grew wealthier and more influential, the imperial response was predictable: heavier taxation and tighter regulation. New tariffs were imposed on Haradian exports. Preferential trade agreements were rewritten to favor Vylarian merchants. Harbor fees were raised. Customs inspections became more frequent, more intrusive, and more expensive. The Vylarian governor's office, once a sleepy posting for minor aristocrats, was given to harder men with explicit instructions to bring the merchant princes to heel.

The taxes were punishing enough, but what truly galled the Haradians was their powerlessness to contest them. Haradian merchants had no representation in the imperial Senate. They could petition, but petitions were ignored. They could bribe, but the bribes bought only temporary relief before the next round of levies. They paid the taxes that financed the empire's legions and its roads and its bureaucracy, but they had no voice in how those taxes were set or spent. The phrase that became the rallying cry of the independence movement—_we bleed gold for an empire that gives us nothing but chains_—was not poetry. It was accounting.

### The Independence Movement

The merchant princes who would eventually lead the revolution did not begin as revolutionaries. They began as lobbyists, petitioning the imperial court for trade reforms, tax relief, and representation in the Senate. When lobbying failed, they tried economic pressure—coordinated slowdowns in the harbors, delayed shipments, sudden shortages of critical goods. The empire responded with punitive measures: arrests, confiscations, and the deployment of additional legions to "maintain order."

It was at this point that the merchant princes made a calculated decision. If they could not reform the empire from within, they would leave it. But a revolution requires more than money and resentment—it requires soldiers, and the merchant princes had warehouses full of goods but no armies. What they had was a cause that resonated far beyond the counting houses.

The recruitment was brilliant. The merchant princes wrapped their commercial grievances in the language of liberty. Broadsheets appeared in every harbor district, every tavern, every market square—printed at guild expense but attributed to "the people of Harad." They spoke of freedom, of self-determination, of a Harad that governed itself rather than serving as a cash-box for foreign aristocrats. The imagery was powerful: broken chains, rising suns, ships under Haradian flags rather than imperial pennants. The merchant princes positioned themselves not as wealthy men protecting their profits but as patriotic leaders defending their people's dignity.

And the people responded. The harbors were full of men who had spent their lives loading and unloading cargo for the enrichment of Vylarian factors. The fishing villages were full of families whose sons had been conscripted into imperial legions to fight wars that had nothing to do with Harad. The inland tribes had endured decades of imperial administrators who taxed their livestock and conscripted their young men while offering nothing in return. The grievances were real, even if the men who channeled them had their own reasons for doing so.

The militias that formed in the port cities drew from every class. Dock workers drilled alongside ship captains. Fishermen sharpened boarding pikes next to merchants' sons. The tribes sent warriors from the hinterland—fierce fighters who cared nothing for merchant politics but hated the empire with a purity of conviction the coastal Haradians could only admire. For a brief, extraordinary moment, Harad was united across class, geography, and culture by a shared conviction that freedom was worth bleeding for.

### The War

The Haradian War of Independence began roughly twelve years before the present day, and it began badly. The empire's response to the initial uprising was swift and brutal. Vylarian legions garrisoned in Harad moved to crush the rebellion before it could spread. The first major engagements—skirmishes outside [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]] and an attempted blockade of [[affiliation-kethara|Kethara]]—went decisively in the empire's favor. The Haradian militias, brave but disorganized, were no match for professional legions in open battle.

The early months were desperate. Tamavar was briefly besieged. Haradian militias were routed in three successive engagements on the coastal plains. The inland tribes, fighting on terrain they knew, had more success harassing imperial supply lines, but they lacked the numbers to force a decision. Morale among the rebels wavered. The merchant princes, who had expected a swift and relatively bloodless separation—a negotiated departure backed by economic leverage—found themselves financing a real war with real casualties and no guarantee of victory.

What saved the rebellion—before Haradian arms could save themselves—was [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]].

The merchant princes had sent envoys south at the very start of the uprising, and the Kheperi court received them with the elaborate hospitality that meant serious interest. Ta'Kheperu had its own reasons for wanting a weakened Vylaria. The empire's eastern provinces—Harad chief among them—were the interface between Vylarian and Kheperi spheres of influence, and a Harad still under imperial control was a Harad whose harbors served Vylarian strategic interests. An independent Harad, by contrast, would be a grateful trading partner with no reason to favor Vylarian commerce over Kheperi.

The calculation was straightforward, and the Kheperi were not sentimental about it. Ta'Kheperu provided gold, weapons, naval supplies, and—critically—experienced military advisors who understood how to fight a professional imperial army. Kheperi gold kept the rebellion solvent during the desperate early months when the merchant princes' own resources were hemorrhaging. Kheperi weapons armed the militias that imperial confiscations had left short of steel. And Kheperi naval advisors helped the Haradian captains organize their ragtag flotilla into something approaching a coherent fighting force.

The aid was never publicly acknowledged. The Kheperi court maintained the polite fiction that it was merely engaged in "humanitarian commerce" with a troubled neighbor, and the merchant princes understood that advertising foreign backing would undermine the revolution's narrative of Haradian self-determination. But everyone involved—including the Vylarian court—knew exactly what was happening. The empire's protests to Ta'Kheperu were met with bland diplomatic assurances that fooled no one and were not intended to.

What saved the rebellion on the water was the sea itself. The Vylarian legions were formidable on land, but they depended on naval supply lines to sustain operations in Harad—the overland routes through the mountainous interior were too slow and too vulnerable to tribal raiders. The Haradian captains knew these waters better than any Vylarian admiral. They knew the tides, the shoals, the hidden channels between the islands, the harbors where a shallow-draft vessel could shelter but a deep-keeled warship could not. They began a campaign of naval harassment—cutting supply convoys, intercepting troop transports, and making the eastern Vylarian Sea too dangerous for imperial shipping to operate without heavy escort.

The war at sea was fought not by a navy but by a flotilla—everything the Haradians could press into service. Warships from the merchant fleets, armed trading vessels, fishing boats fitted with rams and boarding planks, even harbor craft loaded with archers. Against the Vylarian navy's heavy warships, these lighter vessels were individually outmatched. But they were fast, they were numerous, and their captains knew every rock and current in the eastern sea. They fought a war of attrition—raiding, retreating, drawing the Vylarian squadrons into unfamiliar waters, refusing the decisive fleet engagement that the empire wanted.

The turning point came at the **Battle of Tamzîr Shoals**.

A Vylarian naval squadron—the largest force the empire had assembled in the eastern sea—moved to break the Haradian blockade of the strait between the mainland and the outer islands. If the squadron succeeded, the empire could resupply its garrisons, land reinforcements, and strangle the rebellion. The Haradian captains understood that this was the moment: if the squadron passed, the war was lost.

The Haradian fleet—a chaotic assemblage of warships, armed merchantmen, fishing boats, and harbor craft under the command of multiple captains who had never fought together—met the Vylarian squadron in the shallow waters of the Tamzîr Shoals. The Haradians had chosen the ground. The shoals were a maze of sandbars, submerged rocks, and treacherous currents that the deep-drafted Vylarian warships could not navigate. The imperial admiral, committed to the channel, found his ships unable to maneuver, unable to retreat, and unable to bring their superior firepower to bear. The Haradian vessels, lighter and shallower, worked the channels around them like wolves around elk.

The battle lasted two days. By the end, the Vylarian squadron was destroyed—ships burned, captured, or driven onto the shoals to break apart. The imperial admiral was killed. The Haradian captains, several of whom had expected to die in the engagement, found themselves in command of the eastern Vylarian Sea.

The Battle of Tamzîr Shoals broke the empire's ability to project power into Haradian waters. Without naval supremacy, the Vylarian garrisons on land were isolated, cut off from supply and reinforcement. Over the following months, they were reduced one by one—some by siege, some by negotiation, some by desertion as imperial soldiers realized that no relief was coming. The empire, already stretched thin by pressures on its other borders, chose pragmatism over pride. Vylaria's reluctant recognition of Haradian sovereignty followed within the year.

### The Betrayal of the Revolution

Victory tasted of ashes.

The merchant princes had financed the war. They had organized the supply lines, bribed the officials, printed the broadsheets, and paid the soldiers. They had also—quietly, during the chaos of the war itself—been positioning themselves to control whatever government emerged from the fighting.

The Confederation that took shape in the months after the victory was, on paper, a democratic federation of equal city-states. In practice, the merchant guilds wrote the charter. The Grand Council's voting rules gave disproportionate weight to property owners. The judiciary was staffed with guild-friendly magistrates. The tax code was rewritten to favor commercial enterprises. The harbors, the customs houses, the warehouses, the banking networks—all the infrastructure that made Haradian commerce possible—remained firmly in the hands of the same families that had controlled them before the war.

The veterans—the dock workers, the fishermen, the tribal warriors, the ship captains who had sailed into the teeth of the Vylarian fleet at Tamzîr Shoals—discovered that the freedom they had bled for was not for them. They had been promised land, guild membership, pensions, and a voice in the new government. What they received was a discharge, a small purse of silver, and the realization that the men who had paid for the revolution now owned the result.

The merchant princes had never lied, exactly. They had spoken of freedom, and Harad was free—free from imperial taxation, free from Vylarian governors, free from conscription into foreign legions. But the princes had meant _their_ freedom: freedom to trade without imperial interference, freedom to set their own tariffs, freedom to accumulate wealth without sharing it with a distant emperor. The common people of Harad had heard something different in those same words, and they had fought and died for a promise that was never actually made.

The guilds moved quickly to consolidate. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]]—the banking cartel that had underwritten the war—called in its debts. The new Confederation owed the Compact staggering sums, and the Compact used this leverage to ensure that government policy served commercial interests above all else. The [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]], originally a brotherhood of war veterans, was co-opted by the merchant marine interests and transformed from a fraternal organization into a shipping cartel. The [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]] continued as it always had, operating in the shadows, profiting from the chaos of transition.

Within two years of independence, the Confederation was an oligarchy in all but name. The guild families lived in palatial estates. The veterans lived in harbor tenements. The inland tribes, who had sent their warriors to fight and die for a coastal revolution, received nothing at all and were, if anything, worse off than before—at least the Vylarian administrators had maintained the roads.

The bitterest irony was not lost on the people who had fought: they had traded one set of masters for another. The Vylarian governor's palace in Tamavar was now the headquarters of the Auric Compact, and the men who sat behind its desks were no kinder than the men they had replaced. The taxes were different—guild fees instead of imperial levies—but they fell just as heavily on the same backs. The conscription was different—private enforcers instead of imperial legions—but the fist was just as hard.

### The Suppression

The guilds did not simply ignore the veterans and the common people—they actively suppressed them. The men and women who had led the militias, captained the war-flotilla, and organized the resistance were precisely the people the new oligarchy could least afford to leave at liberty. They were experienced fighters with popular followings, they had networks forged in combat, and they had a legitimate grievance that resonated with every dock worker and fisherman in Harad. They were, in a word, dangerous.

The suppression was not a single dramatic crackdown—that would have been too visible, too reminiscent of the imperial oppression the revolution had supposedly ended. Instead, it was a slow, methodical campaign of marginalization. Prominent veterans found themselves accused of debts they did not owe, charged with crimes they did not commit, or implicated in conspiracies that the guild intelligence services had fabricated. Some were bought off with minor posts and small pensions—enough to live on, not enough to organize. Others were arrested, tried in guild-friendly courts, and imprisoned. A few simply disappeared.

The most dangerous—the captains and militia leaders who refused to be silenced—were declared outlaws. Some were accused of piracy (an easy charge to fabricate against any ship captain who had served in the war-flotilla). Others were charged with sedition, conspiracy against the Confederation, or the catch-all crime of "disrupting public order." Warrants were issued. Bounties were posted. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]]'s enforcers and the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s patrol ships were given broad authority to hunt them down.

Many of the outlawed veterans took to the sea. If the guilds wanted to call them pirates, they would be pirates. The waters around the Haradian archipelago—the same island channels and hidden coves that had sheltered the war-flotilla—became the hunting ground of men and women who had once been heroes and were now fugitives. They raided guild shipping, sheltered in ports that the Corsair League could not easily reach, and maintained loose networks of mutual aid among the dispossessed. They called themselves by various names—the Free Captains, the Unchained, the Shoals Brotherhood—but the guilds called them all the same thing: pirates.

The undercurrent of rebellion in Harad has never gone away. It rises periodically—a harbor strike in [[affiliation-kethara|Kethara]], a tax riot in the dock districts of [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]], a tribal raid on a guild caravan—and each time it is ruthlessly suppressed. The guilds have learned from the empire's mistakes: they do not send legions to crush dissent, because legions create martyrs. Instead, they use economic pressure (revoked licenses, called-in debts, blacklisted families), targeted enforcement (guild enforcers are quieter than soldiers), and the simple, grinding weight of an economic system designed to keep the powerless in their place.

But the anger does not dissipate. It accumulates. The veterans who fought at Tamzîr Shoals are growing old, but their children have inherited the betrayal. The dock workers and fishermen know what was promised and what was delivered. The inland tribes remember everything. And on the waterfront, in the taverns and the veterans' halls and the hidden anchorages of the archipelago, people still pass around the old broadsheets—the ones the merchant princes printed during the revolution, with their broken chains and rising suns and brave words about freedom—and they read them with a bitterness that the men who wrote them never intended.

This is the Harad of the present day: free, prosperous, and seething. The merchant guilds rule through money and the institutions they control. The veterans and their children nurse the betrayal. The outlawed captains raid from the island channels. The inland tribes trust no one. And everywhere—in the dock districts, in the fishing villages, in the tribal camps—people remember the words that were used to recruit them, and they wonder when someone will mean them.

It is into this world that the [[affiliation-thetamzir|The Tamzîr]] sails. Her captain fought at the Shoals. He believed the promises. He watched the merchant princes steal the victory. And now he runs cargo on the margins of a Confederation that would rather he did not exist—too famous to ignore, too dangerous to tolerate, too stubborn to kill quietly. The guilds want him brought to heel; the veterans want him to raise the flag again; and all he wants is to keep his crew alive and his ship moving. It is not a sustainable position, and everyone involved knows it.

## Culture and Society

Haradian culture is mercantile at its core. Wealth, reputation, and the ability to strike a profitable deal are the highest virtues. The great trading houses—sprawling family enterprises that combine banking, shipping, warehousing, and political influence—are the true power in Harad. Their warehouses line every harbor, their agents operate in every port of the Vylarian Sea, and their influence reaches into the courts of [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]], [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]], and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]].

Society is sharply divided between the guild elite and everyone else. The guild families live in palatial urban estates, patronize the arts, and educate their children in rhetoric, mathematics, and mercantile law. Below them, a large middle class of independent merchants, skilled craftspeople, and ship captains forms the economic backbone. At the bottom, dock workers, sailors, and the urban poor live in cramped harbor districts and are increasingly restless.

The inland tribal communities are culturally distinct from the coastal cities. They maintain older traditions, speak archaic dialects, and resent the coastal cities' dominance. The tribes provided many of the foot soldiers for the war of independence but have seen little benefit from it.

Haradian cuisine is famous across Mídhalión—spiced meats, flatbreads, honeyed pastries, and strong coffee are staples. The bazaars of the great port cities are legendary, offering goods from across the known world.

## Government

The Confederation is governed by a Grand Council composed of representatives from each member city-state. In theory, each city has an equal voice; in practice, the wealthiest cities and their merchant guilds dominate proceedings. The office of Arch-Consul rotates among the cities, but the position is more ceremonial than powerful—real decisions are made in back rooms by guild leaders.

Each city-state maintains its own internal government, militia, and laws, creating a patchwork of regulations that can be bewildering for outsiders. Smuggling and legal arbitrage—exploiting differences between city-state laws—are profitable industries.

## Religion

Religious practice in Harad is syncretic. The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]] pantheon was imposed during the Vylarian period and remains dominant in the coastal cities, where [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Vénusia]] (prosperity) and [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Ménérva]] (knowledge) are especially popular among the merchant class. [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Táranon]] (storms) is honored by sailors and naval officers.

However, the eastern states and the inland tribes maintain strong connections to the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian Pantheon]], an Āsháian tradition that predates the Vylarian conquest. [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]] (order and harmony), [[affiliation-korsadra|Khóršádrä]] (knowledge and eternal light), and [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]] (prosperity and hedonism) have significant followings. In many Haradian cities, Aurèldían and Āsháian temples stand side by side, and it is not uncommon for a single family to patronize both traditions.

## Magic

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] maintained a strong presence in Harad during the Vylarian period, and it remains active in the major cities. However, the post-independence political landscape has complicated the Ordo's position. The merchant guilds view the Ordo as a potential tool of Vylarian influence and have placed restrictions on its activities. Some guild leaders would like to expel the Ordo entirely; others recognize its utility and seek to co-opt it.

Independent magical practitioners—hedge mages, alchemists, and fortune-tellers—are more common in Harad than in Vylaria proper, partly because the Ordo's grip is looser here. The Ordo is not pleased about this and continues to press for enforcement of its monopoly, sometimes using Vylarian imperial agents as proxies.

## The War of Independence—Summary

The full history of the war and its aftermath is detailed **above**. In brief: the Haradian War of Independence (roughly twelve years ago) was a defining event for the confederation and for the broader politics of the Vylarian Sea. Driven by imperial overtaxation and suppression of Haradian commerce, the merchant princes recruited the common people with promises of freedom and self-governance. Covert aid from [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]]—gold, weapons, and military advisors—proved critical in sustaining the rebellion through its desperate early months. The war began with early defeats on land but was won at sea, culminating in the decisive **Battle of Tamzîr Shoals**, where a makeshift Haradian fleet destroyed a Vylarian naval squadron in treacherous shallow waters. The merchant guilds that financed the war then seized political power, outlawed or imprisoned many of the freedom fighters who had won it, and the freedom the veterans bled for became an oligarchy controlled by the same families that had paid for the revolution.

## Relations

Relations with [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] are officially correct but privately hostile. The empire has not forgiven the loss of Harad, and Vylarian agents—often working in concert with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]—continue to operate in Haradian cities, gathering intelligence and cultivating sympathizers. Haradian merchants, for their part, are aggressively expanding into markets the empire once dominated.

[[place-helionis|Heliónis]] to the west is a natural trading partner, and the two regions share cultural and intellectual ties. [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|The Empire of Ta'Kheperu]] to the south is Harad's most important long-distance trading partner, and the relationship runs deeper than commerce. Kheperi gold, weapons, and military advisors were instrumental in Harad's war of independence—a debt the merchant princes publicly deny and privately honor through preferential trade agreements, reduced harbor fees for Kheperi vessels, and quiet diplomatic coordination that consistently favors Kheperi interests over Vylarian ones. The post-independence surge in Haradian-Kheperi commerce has been one of the most significant economic shifts in the Vylarian Sea: trade routes that once funneled eastern wealth through Vylarian intermediaries now flow directly between Haradian and Kheperi ports, enriching both and further diminishing the empire's commercial position. For Ta'Kheperu, the investment in Haradian independence has paid for itself many times over.

Relations with [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]]'s nomadic tribes are complex—the desert people control overland trade routes that the coastal cities depend on, creating a mutually profitable but often tense relationship.

## Notable Features

- **The Grand Bazaars:** The markets of Harad's port cities are legendary, offering goods from across the known world.
- **The Merchant Guilds:** The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] (banking and trade finance), the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]] (shipping and naval power), and the [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]] (luxury goods and information brokerage) are the three great guilds that effectively rule the confederation.
- **The Haradian Navy:** The most powerful naval force in the eastern Vylarian Sea, built from the ragtag fleet that won independence, now operated largely through the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]].
- **The Battle of Tamzîr Shoals:** The founding myth of the confederation, celebrated annually with naval reviews and public festivals.
- **The Archipelago:** The island chain off the coast, controlling eastern sea routes and serving as naval bases and trading posts.

## Major City-States

- **[[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]]**—The largest and wealthiest city; de facto capital. Seat of the Grand Council and the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]].
- **[[affiliation-kethara|Kethara]]**—The naval stronghold controlling the strait to the archipelago. Home of the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]] and the war veterans.
- **[[affiliation-varoshan|Varoshan]]**—The eastern gateway where sea trade meets the overland caravan routes. Most cosmopolitan city; dominant [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] faith.
- **[[affiliation-ashkabel|Ashkabel]]**—The cultural heart. Famous for ship design, academies, and a more balanced political culture.

## Commerce and Currency

Confederation of Haradian States uses the [[lore-hardncrncy|Haradian currency system]]—the post-secession monetary system administered through the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]]'s Bayt al-Khazînah. The Haradian system uses the same denomination structure as the Vylarian (Aurion / Argo / Bit) but is backed by Haradian reserves rather than the Vylarian [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|The Aerarium Imperii]]; the Haradian Aurion is slightly underweight by the Vylarian standard. The Bayt-affiliated moneylender network handles the banking infrastructure; Vylarian script is _not_ honored here, and Haradian script is not honored in Vylarian territory. See [[lore-hardncrncy|Haradian Currency]] for the full system.
