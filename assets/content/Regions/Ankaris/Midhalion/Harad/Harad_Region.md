> The pilot comes aboard where the strait narrows under the walls of Kethara, a gray-bearded man in a salt-stiff coat who smells of tar and strong coffee. He takes the steering oar without asking, reads the water ahead—pale green over sand, dark blue where the channel runs—and points his chin at the islands off the bow. "My name is **Nadûmar**, and twelve years ago I took a fishing boat with a ram spiked to her bow into the shoals out there and watched an emperor's squadron break up on the sand. Everything you buy in Harad was paid for that week. Remember who paid for it, and remember who collects."

## Overview

Harad Region is the eastern shore of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] and the archipelago that lies off it, within [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]]. It is the country of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Confederation of Haradian States]]: five merchant city-states that won their independence from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] at sea twelve years ago and are governed, in everything but name, by the guilds that paid for the war. Some twelve million people live here, in great port cities, inland caravan towns, fishing villages and the tribal country of the hills.

An outsider remembers Harad for its harbors. It is the premier maritime region of western Ankaris, and its ports carry the trade between the Vylarian Sea, the caravan roads of the east and the southern shore of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]]; its bazaars, its cooking and its fleets are known in every port of Mídhalión. A newcomer has to be told the other half: the dock workers, fishermen, captains and tribal warriors who won the war were promised land, guild membership, pensions and a voice in the new government, and they received a discharge and a small purse of silver. The coast is free, prosperous and seething.

## The Water and the Land

"Every bay on this coast is a harbor," Nadûmar says, "and every harbor has a guild sitting on it." The Haradian coast is deeply indented, and its bays make the finest natural harbors on the Vylarian Sea. Behind them the land climbs from fertile coastal plains through rolling hills to arid scrub, and in the east the scrub gives way to the wastes of [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]]. The climate is warm and dry, and the summers are hot, tempered along the coast by the sea breeze.

Off the coast lies the **Haradian archipelago**, a chain of islands divided from the mainland by a strait. Its channels, shoals and sheltered coves hold the Confederation's naval bases and trading posts, and they hold smuggling havens for those who know the passages. The pilot's first warning concerns them: do not take a deep-keeled ship into the island channels without someone aboard who knows the water. The [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzîr Shoals]] in those waters are a maze of sandbars, submerged rocks and hard currents. During the war a Vylarian squadron, the largest the empire had gathered in the eastern sea, came to break the Haradian blockade of the strait; the Haradian captains met it in the Shoals, where its heavy warships could neither maneuver nor retreat, and in two days of fighting they burned its ships, captured them or drove them onto the sand. That battle won the war, and Harad marks it every year with naval reviews and public festivals.

## Five Cities, Five Sets of Laws

"Learn the harbor before you learn the city," Nadûmar tells his passengers. "What is lawful at one quay is contraband at the next." Each city-state keeps its own laws, militia and council, and the patchwork of regulations between them makes smuggling and legal arbitrage—profiting from the gap between one city's rules and another's—two of the most profitable trades on the coast. Each of the five holds a city and the country around it:

- [[place-tamavar2|Tamavar]], the seat of the city-state of [[affiliation-tamavar|Tamavar]] (some three million people), is the largest and wealthiest city of Harad and its de facto capital. The **Grand Council** of the Confederation meets in its **Grand Council Hall**, and the great guilds keep their headquarters in the marble-faced **Guild Quarter** along the **Grand Harbor**, a deep bay behind breakwaters and fortified islands that can shelter hundreds of ships. Behind the harbor the old city is a maze of narrow streets, covered bazaars and tenements, and in a dry-dock a captured Vylarian warship stands as the **Tamzîr Monument**.
- [[place-kethara2|Kethara]], the seat of [[affiliation-kethara|Kethara]] (some 2.2 million), is the naval stronghold on the strait to the archipelago. Its **Strait Fortress** commands the passage, its **Naval Yards** build the Confederation's warships, and its marines are the nearest thing Harad has to a standing army. Where Tamavar runs on money, Kethara runs on pride: its great families are admirals' families, its **Admiral's Council** governs the city, its annual **Fleet Review** commemorates the Shoals, and its **Veterans' Quarter** houses the sailors and marines who fought the war and never received what they were promised.
- [[place-varoshan2|Varoshan]], the seat of [[affiliation-varoshan|Varoshan]] (some 2.3 million), is the easternmost city, a dusty, polyglot crossroads of mudbrick and tile where the coastal trade meets the caravans from Dunhara and the Khazryn at the **Caravan Gate**. Its **Caravansary Quarter** of hostels, stables and warehouses serves the overland trade, and its council seats representatives of the inland tribes beside the city's merchants. The great guilds hold less sway here, because the inland peoples Varoshan lives by will not deal with guild enforcers.
- [[place-ashkabel2|Ashkabel]], the seat of [[affiliation-ashkabel|Ashkabel]] (some two million) on the southern coast, is the cultural heart of the Confederation. Its **Design Yards** conceive the finest Haradian ships, its **Academy of the Tides** teaches navigation, astronomy and natural philosophy, and the colorful buildings and open-air theaters of the **Painted Harbor** line its waterfront. It is the least guild-dominated of the cities, and artists, free-thinkers and the occasional fugitive who have fallen afoul of guild politics elsewhere find refuge there.
- [[place-azhun2|Azhûn]], the seat of [[affiliation-azhun|Azhûn]] (some 2.5 million), spreads its white-walled warehouses along a natural harbor at the mouth of the [[place-alzriver|Alz River]], the conduit between the Dunhari desert and the western sea routes. Its merchant guilds hold absolute power and meet in the **House of Factors** above the harbor, and a captain who breaks guild protocol there finds no harbor master willing to accept her ship and no crew willing to sign on.

Smaller places fill the country between them. [[place-qadhirun|Qadhirun]] is a port town and one of the most populous in the region, with quarries above the port, guilds of its own and an arena whose fighters belong to a gladiators' guild. [[place-kashmuret|Kashmuret]] is an inland trading town where the caravan routes from the Dunhari desert and southern [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] converge; caravans stop there to restock, establish credit and settle prices with local factors, and a merchant who swindles or reneges in Kashmuret is blacklisted from the caravan trade for years. [[place-miravel|Míravel]] is a merchant town, and [[place-sulun|Sulûn]] a coastal village.

## Peoples and Culture

Harad is a human country, and a sharply divided one. The guild families live in palatial urban estates and educate their children in rhetoric, mathematics and mercantile law. Below them stands a large middle class of independent merchants, skilled craftspeople and ship captains, and below that the dock workers, sailors and urban poor crowded into the harbor districts. The coast honors wealth, reputation and the ability to strike a profitable deal above every other virtue. Spiced meats, flatbreads, honeyed pastries and strong coffee are its staples, and the bazaars of its port cities sell goods from across the known world.

The **inland tribes** are culturally distinct from the coast. They keep older traditions, speak archaic dialects and resent the dominance of the cities. They sent many of the foot soldiers of the war of independence, harried the imperial supply lines in country they knew, and received nothing from the peace.

The language of the region is [[skill-haradilng|Haradi]], a rhythmic, long-voweled tongue with a deep vocabulary for navigation, commerce and the sea, and the working language of the guilds and the shipping routes.

## Faith and Magic

Religion in Harad is syncretic. The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]], brought by the Vylarian conquest, dominates the coastal cities, where the merchant class favors **Ólvenía** for prosperity and **Ménérva** for knowledge, and sailors honor **Táranon** of the storms. The eastern states and the inland tribes keep to the older [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian Pantheon]]—[[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]], [[affiliation-korsadra|Khóršádrä]] and [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]] have large followings—and in Varoshan it is the dominant faith. In many cities the temples of the two stand side by side, and a single family often patronizes both. In Varoshan's **Temple of Two Fires** the rites of both are practiced under one roof.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] keeps chapters in the major cities, but the guilds regard it as a possible instrument of Vylarian influence and restrict its work, and the Ordo presses in turn for its monopoly to be enforced. Hedge mages, alchemists and fortune-tellers are more common here than in Vylaria, and in Varoshan the eastern traditions that come in with the caravans—Āsháian fire-worship, Dunhari sand-divination and Vedyaran meditation—are practiced openly.

## Who Rules

On paper the Confederation's Grand Council gives each city-state an equal voice, and the office of **Arch-Consul** rotates among the cities. In practice the Council's voting rules weight property, the wealthiest cities and their guilds decide its business, and the Arch-Consulship is more ceremonial than powerful. Three great guilds rule Harad in fact, and a traveler meets each of them differently:

- The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]], the banking cartel headquartered in Tamavar, financed the war and holds the Confederation's debt. You meet it as a letter of credit accepted across Mídhalión, as a seal on a contract that binds like a court order, and—if you default—as an enforcer at the door.
- The [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]], the shipping cartel rooted in Kethara, grew out of the veterans of the Tamzîr Shoals and serves as the Confederation's de facto navy. Its captains enjoy customs exemptions and the right to carry weapons in Haradian ports, and a ship that sails outside its umbrella meets port restrictions and the occasional "mistaken" confrontation at sea.
- The [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]] trades openly in luxury goods and secretly in information, and it moves the cargoes the other guilds will not touch. Its agents identify themselves by sigil rings and coded phrases, and its membership rolls are not public.

Beneath the three, the [[affiliation-sodnaqirin|Sôd-Naqîrîn]], the Council of the Guild-Wardens seated at Tamavar, charters the guilds, registers masters, examines apprentices and judges disputes between trades, and the Confederation backs its rulings.

## Coin and Script

Say you land with Vylarian traveler's notes in your purse: the first moneylender you visit pushes them back across the table unhonored. The [[lore-hardncrncy|Haradian currency]] uses the Vylarian denominations—1 **Aurion** is 160 **Argo**, and 1 Argo is 8 **Bits**—but the coins are struck in Harad, and the banking behind them is the **Bayt al-Khazînah**, the House of the Treasury that the Sôd-Naqîrîn's treasurer runs. The Haradian Aurion is about 7 percent underweight by the Vylarian standard; it passes at face value anywhere in Harad, and a Vylarian or Khelâthi changer who assays it pays less. The Argo is struck to full weight. Bayt script is honored at any Bayt-affiliated moneylender in the Confederation and at no Vylarian house, and Vylarian script is honored at no Haradian one, so a merchant crossing between the two converts through coin, most often at the Heliónite houses that keep accounts on both sides.

## The Anger Under the Coast

Nadûmar's account of the peace is a veteran's, and he does not hide it: the merchant princes who printed the broadsheets about freedom meant their own freedom to trade, and the men who sailed into the Shoals heard something else. The merchant princes answer that they never lied: they spoke of freedom, and Harad is free of imperial taxes, imperial governors and conscription into foreign legions. What followed the peace is not in dispute. Prominent veterans were accused of debts they did not owe and crimes they did not commit, bought off with small posts or imprisoned, and the captains who refused to be silenced were declared outlaws. Many of those took to the island channels and now raid guild shipping under names of their own—the **Free Captains**, the **Unchained**, the **Shoals Brotherhood**—while the guilds call all of them pirates.

The unrest rises and is put down: a harbor strike in Kethara, a tax riot in Tamavar's dock districts, a tribal raid on a guild caravan. The guilds answer with revoked licenses, called-in debts, blacklisted families and quiet enforcers, and they send no legions, because legions make martyrs. On the waterfront and in the veterans' halls people still pass around the old broadsheets with their broken chains and rising suns. The [[affiliation-thetamzir|Tamzîr]] and her captain, a veteran of the Shoals, run cargo on the margins of that order: the Compact counts him an irritant, the League would have him under its flag, and the Merchantry is the guild most likely to offer him work.

## Neighbors

Across the sea to the west lies [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], Harad's natural trading partner, with which it shares cultural and intellectual ties. Along the northern shore lies [[place-vylariargn|Vylaría Region]]; relations with the empire are officially correct and privately hostile, Vylaria has not forgiven the loss of its eastern province, and its agents, often working with the Ordo Arcanis, gather intelligence in the Haradian cities.

To the north, the [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]] of the [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría Region]] runs down from the passes of [[affiliation-gumushisar|Gümüşhisar]], and one fork of its road turns south-east toward Harad's hinterland. The [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]] treats that border as one with a rival that trades: its garrison watches the southern forks for Haradian customs officers, and its Lord Commander's letters to the Confederation's border governors are as much diplomacy as soldiering. At sea the League is Harad's great commercial rival, and the Haradian guilds would like to dominate its trade.

To the east the hills fall to the deserts of [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], whose nomads hold the overland routes the coastal cities depend on, in a relationship that profits both and is often tense. Across the sea to the south lies the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]], Harad's most important long-distance partner. Khelâthi gold, weapons and advisors kept the rebellion alive in its desperate first months; the merchant princes deny the debt in public and repay it with preferential trade and reduced harbor fees for Khelâthi ships, and the trade between their ports now runs directly, without Vylarian intermediaries.
