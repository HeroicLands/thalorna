## Overview

Kashmuret lies inland from the coast, positioned at the convergence of trade routes that funnel goods from the Dunhari desert and southern Vedyara northward toward the interior city-states. It is smaller and less cosmopolitan than Azhûn, but no less crucial to the confederation's economy – the town serves as a waypoint where caravans restock, establish credit, and negotiate prices with local factors before undertaking the dangerous journey to distant markets. Kashmuret's bazaars are famous for their cloth merchants, spice brokers, and money changers who have cultivated relationships with trading houses across three continents.

The town's character is defined by transience. Merchants arrive, conduct their business, and depart within weeks or days. The permanent population – innkeepers, warehouse owners, scribes, and guild officials – have adapted to this rhythm, maintaining a certain cosmopolitan efficiency that prioritizes trust and swift transaction above all else. A merchant's reputation follows them to Kashmuret, and word travels fast through the caravan network; those who swindle or renege on deals find themselves blacklisted from trade for years.

Kashmuret's architecture reflects its role as a transit hub: the largest structures are caravanserais and warehouses, their courtyards designed to shelter merchants, pack animals, and goods alike. The town's temples are numerous but modest, maintained more for the comfort of travelers than for grandeur. Nevertheless, the town has produced its share of legendary merchants whose names live on in trading houses throughout Harad – men and women who built fortunes on shrewd judgment and the ability to read the subtle languages of negotiation.
