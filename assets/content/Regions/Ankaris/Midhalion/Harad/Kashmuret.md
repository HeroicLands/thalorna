## Overview

Break a bargain in Kashmuret and the caravan trade forgets your name for years. A merchant's reputation follows him here and word travels fast along the caravan network, so a trader who swindles or reneges finds himself blacklisted from the trade long after he has left town. Kashmuret, a trading town of about 12,000 on the largest island of [[place-haradregin|Harad]], faces the mainland crossing: the trade routes from the Dunhari desert and southern [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] converge on the Amradi shore opposite, and their goods come over the strait to Kashmuret on their way to the city-states of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Confederation of Haradian States]]. It is smaller and less cosmopolitan than [[place-azhun2|Azhûn]] and no less important to the Confederation's economy: caravan masters stop here to restock, establish credit and settle prices with the local factors before the dangerous journey on to distant markets.

## A Town of Transients

Kashmuret lives by people who are leaving. Merchants arrive, do their business and are gone within weeks or days, and the permanent population—innkeepers, warehouse owners, scribes and guild officials—has fitted itself to that rhythm with a cosmopolitan efficiency that puts trust and quick dealing above everything else. A factor's house here lends, stores and brokers for merchants it may never see again, which is why it cares so much what the caravan network says about them.

The bazaars are famous for their cloth merchants, spice brokers and money changers, who keep relationships with trading houses across three continents. The town has produced its share of merchants whose names live on in trading houses throughout Harad, men and women who built fortunes on shrewd judgment and a reading of the small signals of a negotiation.

## The Town

Kashmuret is built for transit. Its largest buildings are caravanserais and warehouses, their courtyards made to shelter merchants, pack animals and goods together. Its temples are numerous and modest, kept more for the comfort of travelers than for grandeur.
