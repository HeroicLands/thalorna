A moneylender of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]]'s dock district who comes down in the morning and finds a broken chain link chalked on his door does not open his shop. He sends for the guild's enforcers and goes to his strongroom, and he finds the lock whole, the box open and the bonds of every bondsman he holds gone, with the same link scratched on the lid. Somewhere in the harbor a man who owed seven years' work is already on a boat. The link is the mark of the Unchained, the veterans of the war sworn in their own halls to break the guilds' hold on the people the guilds hold by debt.

## Who They Are

The Unchained are the sworn core of the veterans' halls of the port cities: the dock workers, fishermen, marines and militiamen who fought the war of independence ([[lore-taruzelwar|The Taruzelian War of Independence]]), were promised land, guild places, pensions and a voice, and were given a discharge and a small purse of silver ([[lore-taruzelbetrayal|The Betrayal of the Revolution]]). Most veterans' halls are what they appear to be, places to drink and argue under the old broadsheets. In some of them the Old Hands keep an oath besides, and those halls are the Unchained.

They are strongest in the Veterans' Quarter of [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] and the dock districts of Besikar, and they have halls in every port city of the Confederation. The war's veterans are growing old, and most of the Links sworn in the last few years are their sons and daughters, who were children at the Shoals and have inherited the grievance with the broadsheets.

## The Name and the Mark

The name and the mark come from the broadsheet that raised the war: a broken chain, a rising sun, a ship under a Taruzelian flag, and the words _we bleed gold for an empire that gives us nothing but chains_. The guilds printed it. The Unchained read the chain as a debt, and they take it literally. Under the law of every member state a debtor can be bound to work off what he owes, and the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] counts him Unfree, reached by its laws only through his master. The bond that holds him is a sealed paper in his creditor's strongroom, and the Unchained burn bonds.

## What They Do

- **They take bonds.** A night's work is usually a strongroom: a moneylender's, a factor's, a labor broker's, the strongroom of a house that buys debts. The Breaker's crew go in by a door the hall has learned, take every bond for a bound man or woman, and burn them where the debtor can see the ashes.
- **They move the freed.** A bondsman whose bond is ash is still known on his own quay, so the hall takes him down to the boats at night, to the hull of one of the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] or a boat of the [[affiliation-shoalsbrthd|Shoals Brotherhood]], and he goes to sea, to the hills or across the strait.
- **They shelter the outlaws.** A Free Captain who needs a crew, a surgeon or a week ashore comes to a hall that keeps the oath, and the Unchained carry the Brotherhood's messages and buy its bread.
- **They strike.** A harbor strike in Yamuzel or a tax riot in Besikar's dock districts is often called in a hall that keeps the oath, and the guilds know it.

They do not kill. The oath forbids a Link to draw blood except to save his own life or another Link's, and a Breaker who brings back a dead watchman is cut from the hall with the man who killed him. The guilds call them arsonists all the same, because a strongroom that burns rarely burns only bonds.

## Joining the Unchained

A Link is sworn in his hall after dark, with the doors barred and the Old Hands standing under the broadsheet. An Old Hand stands for him and answers for him. He puts his hand on the broken chain on the paper and reads the words under it aloud, and the hall answers with the four things it was promised: land, the guild, the pension, the voice. He is told the name of his Keeper and of no one else. A Link who betrays a hall has his name cut out of the broadsheet with a knife, and every hall that keeps the oath is told it.

## Meeting the Unchained

A party meets the Unchained by drinking in the wrong hall and asking the right question, or by being in a strongroom on the wrong night. A bondsman who wants his bond burned needs someone to learn where it is kept, and a hall with a strongroom in mind needs someone a moneylender's clerk does not know by sight. A party that crosses the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] may find a hall willing to hide it for a week, and a party hired by the Compact to find a Breaker will be watched from every doorway of the quarter.

[[affiliation-crimsnldgr|The Crimson Ledger]], whose business is lending to the desperate and seizing their labor when they default, has lost more bonds to the Unchained than any honest moneylender, and its enforcers hunt Breakers with a zeal the guilds' own lack.

## The Present {#present}

The halls are fuller than they have been since the outlawing. The Compact's calls on the harbor streets come sooner and dearer this season, every man bound for a called debt is a strongroom for the Breakers, and the talk in the halls that keep the oath has turned from strikes to a rising. The Old Hands are divided on it, because the oath forbids a Link to draw blood except to save a life. [[being-rzznpddn|Razzina Padduni]], keeper of a hall in Yamuzel's Veterans' Quarter, argues every night that a rising needs a captain the whole coast would follow, and the man the halls want is [[being-zbzmjkdn|Zabuzum Jakuduni]], who has not answered them. In Besikar [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]], the elder of a waterfront hall, preaches patience and carries the veterans' petition to the Arch-Consul before every session. Three Breakers of the Besikar halls have been taken in the last two years.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The bondsman.** A dock worker of the Veterans' Quarter has been bound for a debt he says he has paid. [[being-rzznpddn|Razzina Padduni]] needs someone a moneylender's clerk does not know by sight to learn which strongroom in Besikar holds his bond.
- **The petition.** [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]] asks a party with standing in Besikar to stand behind him at the residence while he reads the petition, so that the Arch-Consul sees the veterans have friends who are not veterans.
- **The song.** A Compact factor has paid the harbor taverns not to let the halls sing [[lore-paidattheshoals|What Was Paid at the Shoals]] this year, and Razzina asks a party with a singer among it to sing it first.

:::secret {title="What the halls are planning"}
The Old Hands of the Yamuzel halls, the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] and the harbor veterans mean to rise at the [[lore-fleetreview|Fleet Review]]. Crossbows, boarding pikes and a chest of Vylarian silver, brought from a Sazumun warehouse under a Merchantry seal, lie under the floor of a cooper's shop below Razzina's hall, and she has guessed whose coin paid for them. She has sent the day of the rising to the Besikar halls by Zilmar Ezzinu, whose hall is the one that informs for the Compact. He has not yet passed it on, and his next meeting with the Compact's enforcer falls before the Review.
:::

# The Truth of the Unchained {#truth .secret}

**One hall is sold.** The Keeper of a waterfront hall in Besikar has informed for the Auric Compact since the first year of the outlawing, when its enforcers took his son for a debt and gave him back for a name. Three Breakers of the Besikar halls have been taken in the last two years, each on the night of a strongroom, and every one of those nights was decided in his hall. The Old Hands of Yamuzel have begun to suspect a Besikar hall and do not know which. A party that carries a message between the cities, or takes part in a night's work in Besikar, may be the one that learns it, and the Keeper will know that before the party does.
