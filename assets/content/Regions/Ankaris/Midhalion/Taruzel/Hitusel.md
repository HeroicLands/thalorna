## Overview

Below Hitusel are the tanning pits, and the smell of them is the first thing a visitor learns about the place: it reaches a traveler on the coast road a mile before the village does. Hitusel is a fishing village of about 2,000 on a low headland of the western coast of [[place-visizel|Visizel]], half a day's ride north of [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]], in the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]].

The name is a warning to strangers: _hitus-_, to lie hidden under water, with _-el_, the ground of it. A reef runs out from the headland a mile into the sea, and at high water nothing of it shows but a line of pale water where the swell breaks. The village's boats go round it by a channel every fisherman's child learns before it can swim, and a stranger's boat goes round it with a Hitusel man aboard or not at all.

## Arriving

The coast road from Sazumun runs north through sandy country of coves and smokehouses and comes to Hitusel over the neck of the headland, between the drying racks for the nets and the village cistern. The village lies on both sides of the headland, above two coves, and its houses are low, of rough stone and lime, with their backs to the west wind. On the southern cove the boats are drawn up on the sand above the tide line, a few dozen of them, broad open boats with one mast. On the northern cove, downwind of everything, are the pits.

There is no harbor and no quay. A ship that wants Hitusel's leather or fish anchors off the southern cove in settled weather and sends her boat in.

## The Boats and the Pits

The village lives by two trades. The fishermen take sardine and mackerel in nets close inshore, and bream and grouper on lines over the reef's edge, and their wives and daughters salt and smoke the catch for the Sazumun markets. They go out in heavy leather jerkins against the wind, and the jerkins come from the pits.

The pits are a row of stone-lined tanks cut into the slope above the northern cove, fed with sea water and fresh water by channels, and their master is the hideworker [[being-dpmnjkdn|Dapuman Jakuduni]]. He tans and works leather the old way, in the pits his father worked before him: the soaking, the liming, the scraping and the long tan in bark and water. He makes boots, harness and the fishermen's jerkins, and buyers ride up from Sazumun for his hides because the city tanneries' leather cracks in two winters. He works the pits alone with two hired men, and his hands and his back are failing him.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The village is governed by the [[affiliation-cnclhtsl|Council of Hitusel]], the heads of the households that own a boat or a pit, who meet on the beach of the southern cove in fair weather and in the cistern house in foul. Its First of the Council is [[being-tasrnzttni|Tasiran Zattuni]], whose family owns more boats than any other. The council allots the cove's beach and the drying racks, settles quarrels over nets and catch, and deals with the Sazumun buyers and the city-state's tax collectors for the whole village.

Hitusel has no watch. A theft from a boat or a house is taken to the council, and in a village of 2,000 the council usually knows the thief before the theft is reported. The usual sentence is restitution and a fine to the council, and a man who steals twice is put out of the village and goes to look for work in Sazumun.

## The Failing or the Quarrel {#present}

Hitusel's pits are failing with their master. [[being-dpmnjkdn|Dapuman Jakuduni]]'s hands and back are going, he has no son and no apprentice, and the Sazumun buyers who ride up for his hides fear the old way of tanning will end with him. The council's quarrel is the reef. A boat from Sazumun went out round it at night with no Hitusel man aboard and came back whole, so somebody in the village has shown the channel to strangers. The fishermen want the seller found, and [[being-tasrnzttni|Tasiran Zattuni]], First of the Council for the fourth spring, will have to judge a neighbor.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The apprentice.** Dapuman Jakuduni has no son and no apprentice, and the Sazumun buyers fear his pits will close with him. One of them offers a party the price of a good horse to find a young hideworker in the city willing to go to Hitusel and learn the old way.
- **The stranger's boat.** A boat from Sazumun went out round the reef at night without a Hitusel man aboard and came back without striking it. The fishermen want to know who in the village sold the channel.
