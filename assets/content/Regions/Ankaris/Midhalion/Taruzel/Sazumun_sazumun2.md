## Overview

The harbor at Sazumun never sleeps, because the tide does not. Twice a day the sea comes up the mouth of the [[place-alzriver|Alz River]] and lifts every hull at the quays, and twice a day it goes out and takes the outbound ships with it, so the porters, tally-men and pilots of the city keep the tide's hours and not the sun's. About 200,000 people live behind the white-walled warehouses of the quays, in the seat of the city-state of [[affiliation-sazumun|Sazumun]] on the western coast of [[place-visizel|Visizel]]. Above them, on the headland over the river mouth, stands the tall white tower of the [[place-houseoffactors|House of Factors]], where the merchant guilds govern the city.

The name is the toll's: _sazum-_, to levy at the gate, with _-un_, where the act is done. Sazumun is the conduit through which goods flow between the desert trade that crosses the strait from the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]], the market towns of the islands and the sea routes west to [[place-helionis|Helionis]] and the western realms, and it earns its living by what passes through it and what it charges on the way.

## Arriving

A ship comes in on the flood. A bar of sand and gravel lies across the river mouth, and a ship crosses it by the marked channel on the rising tide with a harbor pilot aboard, sent out from the pilots' station under the headland. Before the pilot comes, the ship waits in the roads for a flag. The Harbor Master watches the roads from the top of the House of Factors' tower, and when she has read a ship's colors and her papers have come ashore in the pilot boat, she hoists the color of the guild whose trade the ship carries, and the pilot goes out. A ship refused sees a plain black flag, and no pilot comes.

At the quay a tally-man of the guild comes aboard with a clerk of the warehouse. Every bale is counted, sealed with a lead seal and carried into a bonded warehouse, and it stays there under seal until it leaves the city. The tariff falls due when the goods leave the quay, not when they land. Between the two, the goods may be sold in the warehouse, borrowed against, sold again and finally shipped out, and the House's schedule says what each of those costs. A captain must name, before his cargo is landed, which guild's monopoly it falls under. A captain who names the wrong one pays twice, and one who argues takes the question to the Registrar of Monopolies, who names the guild it belongs to.

By land a traveler comes down the valley road from the Alz woods, past mills and orchards, with a toll post of the city's guilds at every bridge, or up the coast road from [[place-hitusel|Hitusel]] through the fishing coves. Neither road has a gate at the city. The toll posts have already asked the traveler's name three times, and the city takes that as enough.

What strikes a stranger first is the white: the warehouse walls along the quays, limewashed every year, and the House's tower above them. The second is the noise, since the tally-men call their counts aloud and a bell on the pilots' station rings each turn of the tide.

## Politics {#present}

The House of Factors keeps Sazumun's order, and the order is under strain from below the headland. [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]] waits on a moneylender's charter from the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]] and gives dinners for its clerks, while the record [[being-pzsnkhdzm|Pazasan Khidizum]] kept of what leaves the Hitusuni warehouses untaxed has gone missing. The [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] still hold the house's receipts for the war's prize cargoes. The timberwright [[being-zttyblnn|Zattuya Balinuni]] has been asked to mark next winter's whole cut of keel timber for one buyer, to the lengths of the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s galleys, and the League has ordered nothing. Three foreign merchantmen have stuck on the bar since spring with harbor pilots aboard, and the harbor guild has fined the pilots. Sazumun is [[being-zbzmjkdn|Zabuzum Jakuduni]]'s birthplace, and Ashuraz has offered him a cargo of Merchantry silk for the Amradi shore, which the captain has not come to Sazumun to hear.

## The Quarters

- **The quays** line the north bank of the river mouth for a mile, and behind them stand the bonded warehouses, three and four floors high, with iron-barred doors and a seal on every lock. Most of them belong to the [[affiliation-hitusuni|Hitusuni]], the great house of the city. The south bank holds the shipyards and the timber basin.
- **The lane behind the warehouses** is the city's back room: ship-chandlers, money-changers, sail-lofts, notaries and the narrow shop of the apothecary [[being-mglshhtsn|Mugilush Hitusuni]], where the rare goods of the warehouses pass across the counter on the way to their buyers.
- **[Jiramun]{.name data-type="place/site"}** is the city's great street market, on the level ground between the quays and the slope: _jiram-_, to speak, with _-un_, where the act is done, the place where the speaking is done. It is a mile of stalls and awnings where every tongue that comes ashore is spoken, and the interpreters who stand at its corners are hired by the hour. Spices, silks, rare woods, dyes, desert leather and [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] cotton are sold there, and almost anything else that left a warehouse with its tariff paid.
- **The temple streets** climb the slope above Jiramun. Temples of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] stand near shrines of [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|The Twelve of the Flame]], the Ashaian gods, sometimes on the same street, and the merchant councils recognize both as legitimate paths. The Aureldian temples are endowed by houses, and their endowments are carved over their doors.
- **The factors' houses** stand on the slope under the House of Factors, behind white walls with their gardens inside, each with a window toward the harbor.
- **The timber basin** on the south bank fills with the winter flood. Alz timber comes down the river in chained rafts and lies in the basin until the shipwrights of the south bank buy it, and the timberwright [[being-zttyblnn|Zattuya Balinuni]] chooses the standing trees in the woods that most of it comes from.
- **The salt pans** lie on the flats south of the river mouth, square shallow ponds of seawater drying in the sun, and Sazumun's white salt goes out in jars packed in straw.

## Markets and Trades

Sazumun sells passage. Its great trade is goods in transit, landed, stored, sold and shipped again: desert spices and leather from across the strait, silks and dyes from the east, rare woods, and the cargoes of every ship of the western routes. What it sells of its own is timber, salt and the leather of Hitusel, and its shipyards build sound merchant hulls of Alz oak. The houses of the city also sell storage, credit against stored goods and the knowledge of what is stored, which the Hitusuni sell best of all.

The money is the [[lore-trzlncrncy|Taruzelian Currency]], and Vylarian script goes unhonored on the quays. The moneylenders affiliated with the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]] keep their tables in the lane behind the warehouses. The tariff schedule of the House of Factors is posted in the House's arcade, and every captain reads it before he decides what to sell in Sazumun and what to carry on.

The street markets overflow with languages, accents and goods, and the city's old joke is that the sea itself lays its treasures at Sazumun's feet. After a winter storm the beaches north and south of the mouth are walked at dawn by the city's poor, looking for what the sea has thrown up. By the guilds' protocol, flotsam picked up on a beach has crossed no quay and pays no tariff, and it is sold in Jiramun the same morning.

## Who Lives Here

The population is a polyglot mixture of Taruzelian merchants, Dunhari traders, Ashaian priests and adventurers from every corner of the known world. The houses of the quays, Hitusuni and [Visizuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} first among them, employ half the city as porters, tally-men, clerks and guards. The pilots are a guild of their own, and they live in a cluster of houses round the pilots' station under the headland.

The city keeps more people of note than its size suggests. [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]], head of the Hitusuni, sits in the House of Factors, and [[being-pzsnkhdzm|Pazasan Khidizum]], a guard on his warehouses, has spent years writing down what leaves them. [[being-tshdfntm|Teshuda Funatum]] keeps the best table in the city for a factor of the slope. [[being-ltryqzkn|Lataruya Qizikuni]] pleads family and inheritance cases for the houses, and [[being-vszzpm|Visiza Zupumi]] guards whoever pays her. [[being-zbzmjkdn|Zabuzum Jakuduni]], captain of _[[affiliation-thetamzir|The Tamzir]]_, was born in Sazumun to cattle herders, worked its docks as a boy and shipped from them as a deckhand.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The merchant guilds hold absolute power, and their councils meet in the House of Factors. Its Speaker, a merchant prince chosen by the House, calls the councils to sit and reads their agreements into the House's register. The Keeper of the Tariff keeps the schedule the House sets and the record of who has paid it. The Registrar of Monopolies keeps the roll of each guild's monopolies and of the agreements between them, and names the guild a dispute belongs to. The Harbor Master, a factor named by the House, accepts or refuses every vessel by guild protocol, and the present one is [Nibura Visizuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a factor of the Visizuni who has held the tower for nine years. Competition between the guilds is fierce and disciplined, and the guilds have learned that stability, however rigidly kept, yields more wealth than chaos.

Every quay, warehouse and market row in Sazumun belongs to a guild, and the guild keeps it. A shopkeeper robbed in the night goes to his own guild's watchmen, who keep his row, and a quarrel between two guilds' men goes to the Registrar. The city's courts hear freemen's cases under the guilds' rules. Order is cheap to keep here, because its sanction is total: a captain, a merchant or a house that breaks guild protocol is outside protocol, and from that day no harbor master accepts the vessel, no crew signs on with her and no guild trades with the offender. The names of those outside protocol are painted on a black board at the foot of the House's stair, and they stay there until the House strikes them off.

## Where a Party Lodges

The captains' inns along the quays keep the tide's hours, serve a meal whenever a ship comes in and post the tide-table by the door. The lodging houses round Jiramun are cheaper and louder, and their hosts find an interpreter for any tongue by the morning. A party with a letter to a factor of the slope lodges behind his white wall and eats at his table, and in one house on the slope that table is Teshuda's.

## History {#history}

Sazumun's name is the toll's, and the city has always lived by what passes its quays. In the war the captains of the flotilla stored their prize cargoes in the Hitusuni warehouses under the house's seal, to be sold when the war was won, and when the most stubborn of those captains were outlawed the house kept the cargoes and the seals ([[lore-taruzelbetrayal|The Betrayal of the Revolution]]). The House of Factors has ruled the city absolutely since independence, and the Hitusuni have sat in its hall longer than any other house.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The black board.** A Khelathi captain whose name went up on the board this spring swears he broke no protocol, and nobody in the House will tell him which one he is supposed to have broken. He hires a party to find out before his crew drifts away to other ships.
- **The interpreter.** A Dunhari trader in Jiramun hires a party to stand beside his interpreter during a sale, because he believes the interpreter is telling the buyer more than he is told to.
- **The flotsam.** A girl who walks the beaches brings a sealed bale she found on the sand to the apothecary's counter, and its lead seal is a Hitusuni seal, unbroken.
- **The keel timber.** The timberwright Zattuya Balinuni has been asked to mark next winter's whole cut of keel timber in the Alz woods for one buyer, to lengths she has only ever marked for the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s galleys, and the League has ordered nothing.

# The Truth of the Keel Timber {#truth .secret}

**The cut is sold.** The guild that holds the Alz timber monopoly has sold the keel timber of the next two winters' cut, every trunk long and straight enough for a galley's keel, to a broker of [[place-helionis|Helionis]]. The Registrar of Monopolies has entered the sale in the House's roll as timber for Helionite merchant yards, and the price is paid in advance, in good coin. The Corsair League pays for its timber late, as it pays its designers late, and the guild's masters have had enough of carrying its debt.

**The buyer.** The Helionite broker is buying for a yard of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]], Taruzel's great rival at sea, and the lengths he asked for are the lengths of the League's newest galley. The drawings for that galley are already on their way to the same yard from [[place-bazunel2|Bazunel]], as the [[affiliation-rusakuni|Rusakuni]]'s own note tells. With both, a Bizarian yard can build the League's galleys before the League has built its own. The guild's masters do not know about the drawings, and believe they have sold timber to a rival for merchant hulls.

**Who can stop it.** Zattuya Balinuni can refuse to mark the trees, and lose her place in the woods. The League can claim the timber as a matter of the Confederation's defense in the Grand Council, if it learns of the sale in time, and it will have to pay its debts to the guild first. The Harbor Master can refuse the broker's ships at the bar in the spring, which only the House can order and which will cost the guild its advance.

**The other hooks.** The black board belongs to the [[place-houseoffactors|House of Factors]], and its note tells how the Khelathi captain's name came to be on it. The bale on the beach went over the side of one of the night boats that carry the [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]]'s cargoes out of the Hitusuni warehouses with their seals unbroken and their tariffs unpaid. Its seal is the proof Pazasan Khidizum has never had, and Mugilush Hitusuni knows it the moment he sees it. The interpreter in Jiramun is honest, and the buyer he speaks to has paid the trader's own clerk for the price the trader means to accept.
