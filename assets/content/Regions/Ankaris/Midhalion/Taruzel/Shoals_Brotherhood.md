"The Shoals are no man's," the Reader tells a new Hand on the sand at low water, with the crew standing round and the flood already coming in over his feet. "We did not take them. We were put here." The Shoals Brotherhood is the body of outlawed sailors that holds the [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]] west of [[place-khamisar|Khamisar]]: some four hundred men and women in two dozen boats, who live in [Sulimar]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a hidden lagoon at the heart of the Shoals, among the wrecks of the Vylarian squadron that was destroyed there in the war. The guilds of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] call them pirates. They pilot ships through the Shoals for a share of the cargo, raid the shipping of the guilds, and dive on the wrecks for their metal.

## Who They Are

The Brotherhood was founded by crews of the war's flotilla whose captains were outlawed in the two years after the peace ([[lore-taruzelbetrayal|The Betrayal of the Revolution]]). They had fought in the Shoals, they knew the water better than anyone left alive, and the one place in the islands where the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s patrol galleys would not follow them was the water where the empire's squadron had died. Their children and the sons and daughters of the veterans' halls fill most of the boats now, and the men who fought in the battle are its Elders.

The Brotherhood's boats are what the flotilla's boats were: fishing boats, pinnaces and long harbor boats with a mast that unships, shallow enough to cross a bar at half tide and light enough to be dragged over sand. A Brotherhood crew is twelve to twenty, and each boat keeps its own council at anchor.

## The Qatilush

The Brotherhood lives under a code its founders swore on the sand of the Shoals after the outlawing: the **Qatilush**, the Keeping, from _qatil-_, to keep faith, with _-ush_, the thing that the act leaves behind. It is not written down. The Reader of the Keeping holds it by heart and reads it at the spring muster and at every swearing, and the first article answers the guilds' charge in the guilds' words: since the guilds called them pirates, pirates they would be, and only to the guilds.

1. **The guilds made us pirates, and we take from the guilds.** No fisherman's boat, no veteran's hull and no poor man's cargo is touched.
2. **Every taking is shared by lot.** A share to every Hand, a full share to every Brother, a share and a half to the Caller, and a share to the dead, paid to their families ashore.
3. **The Caller commands in a fight and in a chase.** At anchor every crew decides by its own voices.
4. **No Brother hides a taking from the muster.** A thing kept back is a thing stolen from the dead's share.
5. **The Shoals keep their dead.** Nothing taken from the squadron's dead is sold, and no ram of the squadron goes to any buyer while the Brotherhood holds the Shoals.
6. **A prisoner who asks is put ashore alive.**
7. **An informer is given to the sand.**

The fifth article governs the Brotherhood's one treasure. The divers bring up the squadron's nails, chain, anchors and bronze fittings and sell them by weight across the strait, but the rams, the great bronze beaks at the warships' bows, stay where the Brotherhood can count them.

## Joining the Brotherhood

A Hand is brought to the Brotherhood by a Brother who stands for him and answers for him through his first year. He swears at low water on a bar of the Shoals, standing where the flood will reach, and the Reader reads him the Qatilush article by article while the water rises. He answers each article aloud. If he is still answering when the water is at his knees, the crew votes on him, and a Hand is taken by a show of hands with the sea at his waist. Nobody has ever been refused at that depth, and the Brotherhood counts that as the point of the rite.

A Hand who keeps the code a year becomes a Brother at the next spring muster. An Elder is a Brother who fought in the Shoals in the war, and no one becomes an Elder now.

## Meeting the Brotherhood

Most people who meet the Brotherhood are aboard a ship in the channels west of Khamisar at dusk, when a boat with no lantern comes out of the Shoals and offers a pilot. The price is a share of the cargo, set by the pilot and not bargained, and a master who pays it is taken through water no chart shows and set out on the far side by morning. A master who refuses goes round the Shoals or through them alone. A ship carrying the cargo of a house of the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] is not offered a pilot, and is lucky to be left alone.

The Brotherhood sells what it takes and what it raises across the strait and in Amradi harbors, and it buys its bread, cloth, tar and arrows through the veterans' halls of [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]]. The [[affiliation-unchained|Unchained]] carry its messages ashore, and the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] take their hulls through the Shoals with its pilots and lie in Sulimar when the League's galleys are out.

## Relations

- **The Corsair League** hunts the Brotherhood in the channels and has never once followed it into the Shoals. Some League captains hire Brotherhood pilots on the quiet, and some sailed with the Elders in the war.
- **The Auric Compact** is the enemy the code names: its cargoes are the first the Brotherhood takes, and its bounty on the Caller is the largest the Compact posts.
- **Yamuzel's watch-boats** patrol the channels from the marine posts on Khamisar's western shore. Their crews are veterans' sons, and the Brotherhood's boats know which nights they are out.
- **The Vylarian Empire** wants the rams. Its factors in the eastern harbors have let it be known that the navy pays well for any ram returned to [[place-castamar|Kastamar]].

## The Present {#present}

The League's galleys hunt the channels round the Shoals, and no League galley has yet followed a Brotherhood boat inside them. Ashore, the halls of Yamuzel that buy its bread and tar talk of a rising, and the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] who lie in Sulimar would be on the water for it. The rams stay where the fifth article puts them, with the empire's factors offering silver for any one of them and the admirals of Yamuzel wanting a column of them at the Strait Fortress.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The pilot.** A Brotherhood boat comes out of the Shoals at dusk and offers a party's ship a pilot, and the master would rather risk the sand than pay the share the pilot names, which is not bargained.
- **The bread.** The Yamuzel hall that buys the Brotherhood's bread and tar has had its credit stopped by its chandler, and [[being-rzznpddn|Razzina Padduni]] wants a party to carry coin to the chandler before the boats run short.

# The Truth of the Brotherhood {#truth .secret}

**The dead's share is unpaid.** The League doubled its patrols in the channels two seasons ago, takings have been thin since, and the Keeper of the Lots has paid the families of the dead nothing for two seasons. The Caller, [Burakan Zupumi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Brother of thirty who was a boy in the boats at the Shoals and is the first Caller who is not an Elder, means to pay them. He has raised two of the squadron's rams with his own crew, by night, and sunk them on the seaward side of the ring, away from the lagoon where the Keeper of the Lots counts the rest. A Vylarian factor in [[place-mercavia|Delalara]] has offered him their weight in silver for both, delivered to a ship off the Shoals before the autumn storms.

The Elders do not know. If they learn of it before the rams are sold, Burakan will be judged under the fifth article, and the Brotherhood will split between the crews who would give him to the sand and the crews who have not eaten well since spring. If they learn of it after, the Brotherhood will have broken its own code for Vylarian silver, and the Free Captains will not lie in its lagoon again. A party can be hired by the factor to see the rams delivered, by an Elder who suspects to find them, or by Burakan himself, who needs a hull that can carry a ram and a crew that is not his.
