## Overview

Every spring [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] pays the sea a debt the sea never agreed to. On the first fair morning after the [[lore-thunderheadvigil|Thunderhead Vigil]], when the storm has spoken on the hill and the swell has lain down, the First of the Council walks out along the old breakwater of the Grand Harbor in a plain coat, without his seal, and the houses of the city follow him, each carrying a stone. The rite is called the Harbor Due, _besiku yutalush_, the harbor's payment, and until it is paid no Besikar hull clears the harbor mouth for the season's first voyage.

The Due is the rite with which [[lore-seaowednothing|When the Sea Owed Nothing]] closes. [[affiliation-vezakuni|The Council of the Guild-Wardens]] (Vezakuni) restored it as part of the [Recovered Compacts]{.name data-type="lore"}, from the old roll kept in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]]'s [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]], and Besikar has kept it every spring since the second year of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]. The houses keep it as though they had never stopped.

## The Morning

The day is not fixed. In a year when the first lightning comes on the night of the Vigil, the Harbormaster's watchers read the sea at dawn each morning after, and when they call it fair the Harbormaster's bell rings on the quay and the city comes down to the water. In a year when the Vigil passes without lightning, the Due waits for the first storm, and the ships wait with it.

The First goes out first, alone, along the stones to the head of the breakwater, where the winter's gaps show as raw places in the line. Behind him come the houses, one by one, in no order of rank, each with its own stone: the great houses with a dressed block on a litter that four men carry, the small houses with a stone a child can hold in two hands. No house comes without a stone, and no house carries another's. The Guild Quarter and the old city turn out to watch from the quays, and the boats of the harbor lie off the breakwater with their oars shipped.

At the head of the stones the First reads the roll aloud. The houses on the stones answer the two lines everybody knows, _It gave, and it took back_, and at the close, _We set, and we set again._ Then the city's harbor masons call the count: how many stones the sea took back over the winter, and how many the city has carried out to set in their place.

## The Tally

The First cuts the count into a tally-stick of olive wood, notch for notch, and splits it down its length so that each half carries every notch. He gives one half to the Treasurer of [[affiliation-besikar|Besikar]] for the city's strongroom. He throws the other into the harbor mouth, saying what the roll has the founder say: _Bring it back when you want to settle._ Nobody waits for an answer. The First walks back along the stones to the quay, where his seal and his coat of office are waiting, and the houses stay behind to set their stones where the winter took them, which goes on until dusk.

The strongroom keeps every half-tally the city has cut, one for each spring, on a shelf of their own. The sea has not brought one back.

## Who Comes Here

- **The houses of Besikar**, from the greatest to the smallest, because a house that carries no stone has said in front of the whole harbor that it does not owe.
- **The captains of the city**, who will not sail before the Due, and the crews who would rather not sail with a captain who did.
- **The harbor masons**, who count the stones and set them, and whose guild is paid for the year's work out of the city's harbor dues that day.
- **The hill tribes' traders** who are in the city, who tell the story differently at their own fires: in their telling the founder, [Tanaduya]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is a hill woman who came down to the shore with a goat-hair rope.
- **Foreign captains** whose ships lie in the harbor, who cannot leave until the Due is paid either, because the Harbormaster opens the harbor mouth to nobody before it.

## Hooks

- **The house without a stone.** A small house of the old city, ruined over the winter, cannot pay for a stone and will not borrow one, and its head has asked a party to find him one that is his own before the bell rings.
- **The first stone.** The harbor masons have asked the First, quietly, to let them set the year's stones before the houses come out this spring.

# The Truth of the First Stone {#truth .secret}

**The first stone moved.** The roll says the sea has never taken back the first stone, under which the founder lies, and it does not promise that the sea never will. This winter a storm lifted the head of the breakwater, and the first stone has shifted a hand's breadth seaward and cracked across its face. The harbor masons have wedged it from below and filled the crack with mortar the color of the stone. They want to set the year's stones before the houses come out so that nobody stands on the first one, and their master has told only the First. The First has not decided whether the count he cuts into the tally this year will include it.
