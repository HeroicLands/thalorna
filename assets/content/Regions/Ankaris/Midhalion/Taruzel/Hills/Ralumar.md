## Overview

The goats come out of the doorways in the morning in a long gray stream, under lintel stones as long as a man is tall. Each lintel is cut on its face with three or four lines of small square letters, worn shallow and filled with lichen, and the goats rub their backs on the door-jambs beneath them as they go out.

Ralumar is a ruin on the spine of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]], in the hill country of [[place-tulurel|Tulurel]], a half-day's climb south of the old military road from the head of the valley above [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]]. It is a flat-topped hill ringed by a wall of fitted stone, with a spring at its foot, and inside the wall stand the roofless stone houses of a town that nobody has lived in since before the conquest. Its name is the coast's, from _ralum-_, old, with _-ar_, where the thing is kept, the place where the old is kept. The [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]], on whose ground it stands, call it "the Doors".

The Qazishuni winter their flocks in its houses. The [[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]] sits each autumn in its court. On the coast [[affiliation-vezakuni|The Council of the Guild-Wardens]] (the Vezakuni) and the great houses both want something from its stones.

## What Is There

The wall runs round the whole hilltop, some four hundred paces, built of big stones fitted without mortar and standing to twice a man's height in most places. It has one gate, on the north side, facing the pass road.

Inside, the houses stand in terraces down the slopes of the hill, small ones of a room or two along narrow stepped lanes, their roofs long gone. The hilltop is a paved court with a cistern cut into the rock at its middle, and round the court a ring of stone benches. Five great houses face the court, each of several rooms round a yard of its own, and each with one doorway onto the court under a single long lintel. The five lintels are the cut stones. Their letters are the [[skill-merazanscrpt|Merazan Script]], in an old square shape that few coast scribes read, and four of the lintels are whole. The fifth has cracked across the middle, and its left half lies face down in the doorway.

The Qazishuni roof parts of the great houses each winter with brush and goat-hair cloth and fold their goats in them, and the floors are deep in old dung. Hearth-smoke has blackened the walls. The cistern holds water through the summer, and the Qazishuni sell it to anyone they let in.

## What the Hills Say

The Qazishuni's tellers say that the Doors were the houses of the coast's cities, one house for each, and that the coast's grandfathers came up into the hills to swear their bargains with one another here, because a bargain sworn at sea level does not hold. They say the letters on the lintels are the bargains, and that the hills were there to see them sworn. Their winter tellings call the place the house where the coast told the truth once.

The caravan masters of Pashinar call Ralumar a bandits' town and keep clear of it. The scribes of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] call it a compact-house, the hall of a city's merchant princes in the centuries before the conquest, and the Vezakuni's copyists of the [Recovered Compacts]{.name data-type="lore"} say that the lintels are compact-treaties and want them copied.

## Who Comes Here

- **The Qazishuni**, who winter several camps in the houses and keep the place through the Keeper of the Doors, an elder of the tribe. A stranger who comes up without leave is turned back at the gate.
- **The Council of the Hills**, which sits in the court for five or six days each autumn, with the elders of three tribes camped round the hill.
- **The Vezakuni's copyists**, who have asked the council's leave to copy the lintels and have been named a price the Vezakuni have not paid.
- **Factors of the great houses**, who come up in pairs with mules and offer to buy the stones for their beauty, to set in a Besikar garden.
- **Travelers on the pass road**, caught by weather, who are let in for the water and the shelter of a roofed corner at the Qazishuni's price.

## Layers {#history}

The wall and the houses are older than the conquest, and nobody has lived in them since. The hill tellings say the coast's cities swore their bargains to one another here, one great house for each, and the coast's scribes call it a compact-house of the merchant princes' centuries before the empire. In living memory it has been the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]]'s winter fold, and the Council of the Hills' place of sitting.

## What Is There Now {#present}

Ralumar is wanted for its stones. The [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]]'s copyists want the lintels copied for the Recovered Compacts and have not paid the price the Council of the Hills named. Factors of the great houses come up the hill in pairs with mules and offer to buy the stones for a Besikar garden, and the cracked half of the fifth lintel lies face down in its doorway where any of them could see it. The Keeper of the Doors turns away whoever comes without leave, and [[being-qtshqzshni|Qatush Qazishuni]], the Lawspeaker, speaks the law of the hills at every sitting in the court below the lintels.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- A Vezakuni copyist hires a party to get him through Ralumar's gate with paper and charcoal for one day, and offers a bonus for every lintel he can copy.
- A Besikar factor offers a party a good price to bring one cut stone down to Pashinar by night, and says that the cracked half in the doorway will do.
- The Keeper of the Doors asks a party that has been let in to carry a message from the council to the coast and speak it once, to the right person.

# The Truth of the Lintels {#truth .secret}

**What the stones say.** Each of the five lintels is a compact-treaty, cut in stone over the door of the house of the city that swore it. Each names one of the island cities, the cities it trades with under the compact, the goods, the dues, the measures and the forfeit for a broken bargain. The cracked fifth lintel's two halves together make a whole treaty. The Recovered Compacts are right that the compact-treaties are real, and that they are older than the empire.

**Where they differ from the Recovered Compacts.** Every lintel closes with the same clause. The compact is sworn "before the hills", at Ralumar, with the keepers of the place as its witnesses, and the witnesses take a witness's share of every load that crosses the hills under the compact: one part in fifty, paid in silver at the gate of the Doors. The Vezakuni's restored compacts reproduce the treaties' forms and leave the witnesses out entirely. No lintel names a guild-warden either. The compacts are sworn between the merchant princes of the cities in person, and the Vezakuni's claim that its wardens stand in the compact-houses' line has no stone behind it.

**Why both sides want the stones.** The Vezakuni want them because they are the oldest record of Taruzelian compact law there is, on no parchment the empire ever touched, and they would answer the charge of the [[affiliation-tevran|Great College of Guilds]], the Vilérian Tevran Maldor, that the Recovered Compacts are invented. To use them the Vezakuni would have to publish the witness clause. The great houses want them gone for the same clause. A witness's share written in stone is the debt the hills can point to, and every house that crossed the hills in the war, the [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] first, would be answering for it in front of the [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]].

**Who knows.** [[being-qtshqzshni|Qatush Qazishuni]], the Lawspeaker, holds the witness clause by heart. It is part of the law of the hills he speaks at the opening of every sitting, as "the coast pays at the Doors", and the elders hear it every year without knowing that it is written over their heads. He cannot read the lintels. One Vezakuni copyist was let in three years ago and made rubbings of two lintels, and the copyist's superior has kept them locked in the [Vezakar]{.name data-type="place/site"} at Besikar ever since. The factors who offer to buy the stones have been sent by a house that has read the copies.

**What follows.** A party that brings a clean copy of all five lintels down from the hills holds something three bodies want. The Vezakuni will pay for it and bury the clause, or publish it, as its council decides. A great house will pay more to see the copy burned. The tribes' councilors at [[affiliation-qudumel|Kudumel]] would carry it to the Grand Council, and with it the hills' first written claim on the coast.
