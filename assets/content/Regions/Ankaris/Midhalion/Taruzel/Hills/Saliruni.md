At a Saliruni well the keeper comes out of the shade of a goat-hair awning before the travelers have reached the stones, with a brass cup in one hand and a skin in the other. The cup comes first, and it is free. The skin is sold, at a price the keeper names once and does not discuss.

The Saliruni (from _salir-_, wet, the company of the water) are the hill tribe of [[place-nishurel|Nishurel]], the driest of the islands of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]], some 80,000 people whose camps move between the wells of its bare limestone interior. Nishurel has no river and almost no springs, and the wells of the hills are the tribe's. A Saliruni well is a shaft cut down through the rock, lined with stone at its head and covered with a stone lid, and every one of them has a keeper and a household that has kept it for longer than anyone counts.

## The Well and the Cup

Every well on the hill roads keeps the same rule. The keeper offers a stranger the cup, and the stranger drinks it. A traveler who refuses the cup has refused the keeper's hospitality, and the next well on the road is found dry, because the keeper of the first has sent a boy ahead. A traveler who drinks the cup is the keeper's guest until the next well, and a guest who is robbed on that stretch is the keeper's to avenge. The water itself is sold by the skin at the price the [[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]] sets each autumn, and members of the tribe drink free.

## Camels and Guides

The Saliruni keep goats like every hill tribe, and camels besides, bred from animals the caravan trade brought over the strait. The camels carry the hill trade, and they carry the tribe's other living: guiding. The caravan houses of [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] hire Saliruni guides at the Caravan Gate to take their goods over the hill roads to the western shore, two days with a guide and impossible without one, and the guide buys the water, greets the keepers and drinks the cups for the whole train.

## The Fires

The small domed shrines of whitewashed stone on Nishurel's hilltops are Saliruni shrines of [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|The Twelve of the Flame]] (the Ashaian Pantheon), each with a fire kept burning inside it. Every camp's hearth is lit from the nearest shrine's fire, and a camp that moves to a new ground carries a coal from the old shrine in a clay pot and lights its hearth from that until it reaches the next. The Saliruni hold that the hills' faith is older than Kudumel's, and the priests of the [Temple of Two Fires]{.name data-type="place/structure"} in Kudumel come up to the shrines at midsummer and do not dispute it.

## The Unpaid Share

During the war of independence the house of [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] carried the rebellion's supplies over the Nishurel hill roads with Saliruni guides and Saliruni escorts, when the legions held the coast roads. The house's head promised the tribe a share of the hill-road tolls once the war was won, by clasped hands and before witnesses, and never in writing. The house has not paid. The Saliruni elders raise the debt at every sitting of the Council of the Hills, and the tribe's councilors on Kudumel's council have stopped asking the house for it.

## Quarrels and Debts {#present}

The unpaid share is the tribe's quarrel, and this season the tribe has acted on it. The Saliruni guides at the Caravan Gate will not take a [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] train over the hill roads, though they guide every other house's, and the tribe's councilors on Kudumel's council have stopped asking [[being-llmnblnn|Ellamuna Balinuni]] for the share. A Tribal Councilor of the hills has not come down to the council's session, and his household camps by the west gate without him.
