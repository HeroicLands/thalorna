## Overview

Above the last terrace wall on [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]] the road narrows to a mule track, and the first thing a traveler meets there is a cairn of white stones with a goat skull set on top of it. Past it the land belongs to the hill tribes. On a still evening their camps show as threads of smoke along the ridges, and the herders' dogs come down to the track before the herders do.

Tulurel is the hill country of the islands of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]]: the interiors of the three larger islands, Qalirel, [[place-nishurel|Nishurel]] and [[place-visizel|Visizel]], from the last terraces of the coastal plains up to the ridges. Its name is the coast's word for it, from _tulur-_, high, with _-el_, the ground of it, the high ground, and in the harbor towns it is simply "the hills". About 240,000 people live there, in three tribes: the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]] of Qalirel, the [[affiliation-saliruni|Saliruni]] of Nishurel and the [[affiliation-haduruni|Haduruni]] of Visizel. On the Confederation's maps the hill country belongs to the city-states whose islands it stands on. In the hills it answers to the tribes' own [[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]], which sits once a year in the ruin of [[place-ralumar|Ralumar]] on Qalirel's spine.

The hill country is what the coast remembers when a levy is needed. The tribes garrisoned the legions' forts for imperial pay for more than a century, sent the foot soldiers of the war of independence down to the coast, and have received nothing from the peace. The forts stand ruined along the ridges, and the road between them is going back to scree.

## The Land

The hill country has the same bones on every island, limestone under a thin soil, and a different face on each.

- **Qalirel's spine.** The longest of the three runs sixty miles down the middle of Qalirel, rolling oak and scrub country rising to a bare ridge of white rock and thyme. The ruined forts of [[lore-easternline|The Eastern Line]] stand on the ridge, and the old military road crosses it at the pass above [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]]. Springs are few and every one of them has an owner. The Qazishuni winter their flocks in the oak country and take them up to the ridge in summer.
- **Nishurel's bare hills.** Nishurel's interior is low, pale and nearly treeless, cut by dry gullies that run with water a few days in the winter. Wells are the country's wealth. The Saliruni keep them and sell their water by the skin, and their hill roads are worn paths from one well to the next. The small domed fire-shrines of the Ashaian faith stand on the hilltops, a fire kept in each.
- **Visizel's woods and scrub.** Visizel's hills catch the western rain, so their western slopes carry oak, pine and chestnut down into the upper valley of the [[place-alzriver|Alz River]], while the eastern slopes in the rain shadow are scrub and stone. The Haduruni hold the high ground above the timberwrights' camps, behind a boundary of stone heaps that the timberwrights do not pass without leave.

Summers are hot and dry, and in late summer a spring that ran in May can be a damp patch of moss. The rain comes in winter, in short hard storms that wash out the tracks and fill the cisterns. Snow lies on Qalirel's ridge for a few days most winters and almost never on Nishurel.

## The Tribes

[[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]] (~110,000)
: the company of remembering, the largest tribe, herders of goats, sheep and horses along Qalirel's spine. They hold the water of the old forts' cisterns and keep Ralumar.

[[affiliation-saliruni|Saliruni]] (~80,000)
: the company of the water, keepers of the wells and the fire-shrines of Nishurel, with camels as well as goats, and the guides Kudumel's caravan houses hire for the hill roads.

[[affiliation-haduruni|Haduruni]] (~50,000)
: the company of the closed way, of Visizel's high woods and scrub, who sell the timberwrights the right to cut on their side of the valley and watch every tree that falls.

A tribe is a body of kin, many thousands strong, spread across its island in camps of a few dozen households each. A camp moves with its flocks between a winter ground and a summer ground, and lives in tents of black goat-hair cloth stretched over stone folds that stay where they are from one year to the next. The head of a camp is an elder (_kalurum_), and the elders of a tribe are the people who speak for it.

## Living in the Hills

The hill tribes live by their animals and by what the coast has to pay to cross them. Goats are the wealth of every camp, for their milk, their cheese, their meat and the black hair that is woven into tents and cloaks. The Qazishuni breed small, hard horses that the legions once bought for their auxiliaries. The Saliruni keep camels for the caravan trade. Every tribe sells cheese, wool and hides down to the coast's markets, and buys grain, iron, oil and salt there.

The other half of the living is passage. Water on the hill roads is sold, a guide is hired, and a caravan that crosses a tribe's ground pays that tribe for it, in silver weighed on the tribe's own scales. Hill men also take service, as their grandfathers did in the forts: as caravan guards, as the escorts of guild timber trains, and as hired swords in the harbor towns. A hill man's price for a season's service is known in every caravanserai, and it is not low.

The hills keep their own law. A quarrel inside a camp goes to its elder, a quarrel between camps to the elders of the tribe, and a quarrel between tribes to the Council of the Hills, and most of them are settled in goats. A killing is paid for by the killer's camp to the dead man's camp at a price the elders set, and a camp that will not pay starts a feud the other camps of both tribes then have to end. The coast's courts and the Confederation's magistrates have no standing above the last terrace. A coast man who wrongs a hill man is answered by the hill man's camp, and a hill man who breaks the coast's law on the coast is hanged by the coast.

## Speech and Faith

The tribes speak [[skill-taruzelilng#hill-taruzeli|Hill Taruzeli]], the oldest-sounding speech in the islands. A harbor clerk follows it with effort. It keeps the old words for herding, water, kin and oaths, and it has almost none of the ledger words the coast lives by. Its greeting is an offer of milk or water, and a hill man counts what he is owed in goats.

The hill faith is [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|The Twelve of the Flame]] (the Ashaian Pantheon), as it is in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]], and it is older in the hills than anywhere on the coast. Each tribe keeps a fire burning on a hilltop of its own ground, the Saliruni's in the domed shrines of Nishurel, the Qazishuni's and the Haduruni's in rings of stone open to the sky. A fire that goes out is relit from another tribe's, and a camp that lets its fire go out pays the camp that relights it. The Aureldian gods of the coast have no shrine in the hills.

## Crossing the Hills

A traveler crosses the hill country with a hill guide or not at all. The guide is hired at the last village of the plain or at a well, from the tribe whose ground the road crosses. On each tribe's ground the same three rules hold. Water is bought from whoever keeps the well or the cistern, at that year's price. A cup is offered at every well, and it is drunk. A stranger who goes off the track without a guide is a thief until he proves otherwise, and the camps take his animals first and ask his business afterward.

The roads are worse every year. The legions' military road over Qalirel's pass is the one paved road in the hills, and the winter rains take more of its paving each year, because nobody keeps it up. Nishurel has no paved road at all. On Visizel the timber track up the Alz gives out at the Haduruni's boundary, and the hills beyond are crossed by goat paths.

## The Ruins

Two kinds of ruin stand in the hills. The forts of the Eastern Line are the newer: square walls of dressed stone on the ridges, roofless, their gates gone, their cisterns still holding water that the tribes sell. The older is Ralumar, a walled hilltop of stone houses on Qalirel's spine above the Pashinar valley, older than the conquest, whose long door-lintels carry lines of cut letters. The Qazishuni winter their flocks in its houses, and the Council of the Hills sits in its court.

## The Debt the Hills Count

The tribes carried the war's supplies over the hill roads, and on Nishurel the house of [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] promised them a share of the hill-road tolls for it once the war was won, by clasped hands and never in writing. The house counts an unwritten bargain as no debt, and the tribes count it as one. Every camp in the hill country knows the sum, and the Saliruni's elders say it at every sitting of the council. This season a Kudumel caravan master is offering a party double wages to take a Balinuni train over the Nishurel hill roads, and he will not say why the hill guides he usually hires have all refused.

## Current Situation {#present}

The hills are counting what the coast owes them. The Saliruni's guides will not take a [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] train over Nishurel's hill roads, and their councilors on Kudumel's council have stopped asking [[being-llmnblnn|Ellamuna Balinuni]] for the share. On Qalirel's pass road a new gate hangs below the third fort, kept by men who say they hold the road for the hills, and the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]] neither built it nor have broken it, while the caravan masters of Pashinar blame the hills for the toll. On Visizel the timberwright [[being-zttyblnn|Zattuya Balinuni]] wants a stand of oak on the [[affiliation-haduruni|Haduruni]] side of the boundary. At [[place-ralumar|Ralumar]], the Lawspeaker [[being-qtshqzshni|Qatush Qazishuni]] still waits on the price the council named the Vezakuni for copying the lintels.
