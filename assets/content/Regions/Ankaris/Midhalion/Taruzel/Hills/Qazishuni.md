A Qazishuni camp on the move fills a mile of track: goats first, a hundred to a herder, then the sheep, then the horses on long lines, then the women with the folded tents on mules and the old men walking with the children. The dogs run the length of it all day, and a caravan that meets it on the pass road waits for it to go by.

The Qazishuni (from _qazish-_, to remember, the company of remembering) are the largest of the three hill tribes of [[place-tulurel|Tulurel]], some 110,000 people in about five hundred camps along the spine of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]]. They winter in the oak country above the terraces of the north-western plain and in the gullies above [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]], and in summer they take their flocks up to the bare ridge, where the ruined forts of [[lore-easternline|The Eastern Line]] stand.

## Horses and Forts

The Qazishuni are the horse people of the hills. Their horses are small, rough-coated and sure on rock, and they were the mounts of the hill horsemen the legions hired to garrison the forts. For more than a century nearly every Qazishuni camp had a son in a fort and the empire's silver in a jar under the hearthstone. Since the revolution nobody pays for the forts, and the Qazishuni hold their cisterns anyway, because the water in them is the only water on the ridge. The Keeper of the Cisterns sells it to every caravan on the pass road at the price the [[affiliation-hillcouncil|Council of the Hills]] sets.

A Qazishuni herder still knows silver better than any people in the islands. Children learn to weigh a coin on the tongue, and a Qazishuni elder can tell the empire's coin from the Confederation's with his eyes shut.

## The Rememberers

The tribe's name is its boast. Every camp has a Teller of the Winter Fire, who keeps the tribe's tellings and gives them on winter nights with a goat-hair cloth hung over the door against the wind. The tellings are given word for word as they were heard, with a fixed opening and a fixed close, and a teller who changes a word is corrected by the old people at the fire. [[lore-roadpaidthrice|The Road Paid Three Times]] is a Qazishuni telling. So are the genealogies of the camps, the boundaries of every summer ground, and the list of what the coast has promised the hills and not paid, which grows longer.

## Keeping the Doors

[[place-ralumar|Ralumar]], the walled ruin on the spine above the Pashinar valley, stands on Qazishuni ground, and the tribe keeps it. Its camps winter their flocks in the roofless houses, the tribe's elders meet in its court, and an elder of the tribe holds the Council of the Hills' office of Keeper of the Doors. The Qazishuni call the place "the Doors", for its cut lintels, and they turn away anyone who comes to it without leave.

## The Coast

The Qazishuni sell cheese, wool, hides and horses in Pashinar and in the markets of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]]'s outskirts, and hire out as caravan guards on the pass road. Besikar, whose island Qalirel is, has never sent an official above the terraces. This season a gate of new timber hangs across the pass road below the third fort, kept by men who say they hold the road for the hills, and the Qazishuni have neither built it nor broken it.

## Quarrels and Debts {#present}

The Qazishuni are blamed for a gate they did not build. The new toll-gate below the third fort on the pass road is kept by men in goat-hair cloaks who say they hold the road for the hills, and every caravan master who comes down into Pashinar tells the tale of the man who kept the pass for three paymasters. The tribe has neither built the gate nor broken it, because a hill man who breaks the coast's law on the coast is hanged by the coast. The list of what the coast has promised the hills and not paid grows longer at every winter fire. The factors of the great houses come up to [[place-ralumar|Ralumar]] to offer for its cut stones, and the Keeper of the Doors turns them away. [Qatush Qazishuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Lawspeaker, is of this tribe.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The coast's tale.** A Qazishuni elder wants a party that has crossed the pass to tell the caravan masters of Pashinar that the hills did not hang the gate, and to be believed.
- **The stones.** A Besikar factor offers a party a good price to bring a cut stone down from Ralumar by night, and the Keeper of the Doors is the person it will have to pass.
