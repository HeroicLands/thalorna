## Overview

Break a bargain in Pashinar and the caravan trade forgets your name for years. A merchant's reputation follows him here and word travels fast along the caravan network, so a trader who swindles or reneges finds himself blacklisted from the trade long after he has left town. Pashinar, a trading town of about 12,000 on the eastern shore of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]], the largest island of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]], faces the mainland crossing: the trade routes from the Dunhari desert and the southern part of the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] converge on the Amradi shore opposite, and their goods come over the strait to Pashinar on their way to the city-states of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]. It is smaller and less cosmopolitan than [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]] and no less important to the Confederation's economy: caravan masters stop here to restock, establish credit and settle prices with the local factors before the dangerous journey on to distant markets.

The name is the town's trade: _pashin-_, to name a fair price, with _-ar_, where the thing is kept. A _pashinu_ is a fair price, one both sides leave content with, and Pashinar is where the strait comes to find one.

## Arriving

Pashinar's bay is the one good harbor on the strait side of Qalirel, and the ferries from the Amradi coastal ports land there, a day's sail with a fair wind. They are broad, slow sailing craft built to carry mules and bales together, and they come in on the morning breeze and tie up three abreast at the ferry quay. The first building a caravan master reaches is the weigh-house at the head of the quay, where the Warden of the Weights' measurers, the _ashurum_, weigh his bales, test his coin and seal what they have weighed with a lead tag. The second is the board's toll-house beside it, where he pays the landing dues on the tags.

Beyond the quay the town climbs a shallow valley between two dry hills. Its main street is the beginning of the old military road over the pass to [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], four days for a mule train, and caravans going west leave by the gate at the top of the street, where the farriers and rope-sellers have their stalls.

## A Town of Transients

Pashinar lives by people who are leaving. Merchants arrive, do their business and are gone within weeks or days, and the permanent population—innkeepers, warehouse owners, scribes and guild officials—has fitted itself to that rhythm with a cosmopolitan efficiency that puts trust and quick dealing above everything else. A factor's house here lends, stores and brokers for merchants it may never see again, which is why it cares so much what the caravan network says about them.

The bazaars are famous for their cloth merchants, spice brokers and money changers, who keep relationships with trading houses across three continents. The town has produced its share of merchants whose names live on in trading houses throughout Taruzel, men and women who built fortunes on shrewd judgment and a reading of the small signals of a negotiation.

The factors' houses stand on the slope above the bazaars, behind blank walls, with a courtyard for guests and a counting room off it. [[being-pzrshilmmn|Pazirush Ilammuni]] keeps the door of one of them and waits at its table, where Dunhari caravan masters, Vedyaran brokers and Taruzelian factors eat together and bargain over the coffee.

## The Town

Pashinar is built for transit. Its largest buildings are caravanserais and warehouses, their courtyards made to shelter merchants, pack animals and goods together. Its temples are numerous and modest, kept more for the comfort of travelers than for grandeur.

The trades of the town serve the road. The farriers, saddlers and rope-makers work by the western gate, and the armorers' street below it sells to caravan guards. [[being-hshdyqsdn|Hushaduya Kusaduni]] works there, an armorer whose fine daggers and throwing knives are made to be beautiful, and she wants the guards who buy them to prove them useful. On winter evenings, when the ferries sail only on settled days and the caravanserais are full of men waiting, fighters box and fence for purses in the caravanserai yards, and [[being-gnrbbd|Genira Bibudi]], a gladiator who fights fast and close, is the one the caravan men bet on.

## The Forgetting

The blacklist of the caravan trade is a book. The [[affiliation-gldbrdpshnr|Guild Board of Pashinar]] keeps it through its Warden of the Weights, and the caravan masters call it [the Forgetting]{.name data-type="lore"}. A name goes onto it when two witnesses swear before the Warden that its owner broke a bargain struck in Pashinar or on the road from it: a debt refused, a short weight, a cargo sold twice. The Warden reads the new names aloud on the ferry quay at the first sailing of spring, and the ferry masters carry copies to [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] and the Amradi ports. A name stays on the Forgetting for seven years. No factor in Pashinar lends to a man on it, and no caravanserai takes his animals.

A caravan master in good standing carries the other half of the system on his belt: a _rasunush_ (tally), a stick of olive wood notched at the weigh-house for every bargain he settles in Pashinar, sealed at one end with the Warden's lead. A factor reads the tally before he reads the man. A long tally with no gaps lends a stranger more credit than any letter, and a caravan master who loses his tally starts again from nothing.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The town is governed by its [[affiliation-gldbrdpshnr|Guild Board]], the guild officials among its permanent townsfolk, who sit in the toll-house above the ferry quay under a First of the Board who keeps the seal. This term she is [Qazima Zazuruni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, whose house owns the largest warehouses on the quay. The Warden of the Weights is [Ashuran Dapumi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who keeps the weigh-house and the Forgetting, and the clerk of the Forgetting is [[being-rsnmgttn|Rasunum Gatituni]], who has kept the board's books for thirty years and enters every name in his own hand.

Order in Pashinar is mostly credit. A merchant robbed in a caravanserai goes first to its keeper, who is answerable to the board for what happens under his roof and pays for a theft he cannot recover. A quarrel over a bargain goes to the weigh-house, where the Warden's measurers weigh the goods again and say who was short. The board keeps a small watch of hired men at the quay and the gate, and leaves the streets between to the caravanserai keepers, who keep their own porters with staves.

## The Present {#present}

Pashinar is losing its trade one debt at a time. The [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] of Kudumel, who want every caravan on the strait landing at the Caravan Gate, have bought the debts of the town's factors through the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] and call them in one at a time, each with a month to pay, and one factor whose note was called has fled across the strait with his ledger. Caravan masters coming down from the pass complain of a new toll-gate on the road to Besikar, kept by men who say they hold it for the hills. On the ferry quay this spring the Warden of the Weights read a Dunhari caravan master's name on the Forgetting, and the master swears he broke no bargain. Above the ferry quays [Yazila Ilammuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"} keeps her counting-room and lends to the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] against their next takings.

## Where a Party Lodges

A party that comes with a caravan lodges in its caravanserai, sleeps on the gallery above its animals and eats at the common fire. The caravanserais by the quay are noisy and cheap, and those by the western gate are quieter and kept for trains about to go over the pass. A party with a letter to a factor's house lodges in the guest rooms off its courtyard, eats at its table among the caravan masters, and is expected to be a guest worth having.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A name on the roll.** A Dunhari caravan master has landed to find his name read on the Forgetting, and he swears he broke no bargain in Pashinar or anywhere. He cannot trade, cannot lodge his mules and cannot go home without a cargo, and he offers a party half his freight to find out who swore against him.
- **The called debt.** The debts of Pashinar's factors are being bought up through the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] and called in one at a time by the [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] of Kudumel. A factor whose note has been called has a month to pay it, and wants a party to collect what three caravan masters owe him before the month is out.
- **The gate on the pass.** The caravan masters coming down into Pashinar complain of a new toll-gate on the pass road ([[place-qalirel|Qalirel]]), and one of them wants to know who keeps it.

# The Truth of the Forgetting {#truth .secret}

**Names are entered at a price.** The Warden of the Weights, Ashuran Dapumi, has entered four names on the Forgetting in the last two seasons on the oaths of witnesses he was paid to believe. All four belong to caravan masters who land at Pashinar every year and never at Kudumel. The man who brought the witnesses, and paid the Warden, pays in Kudumel script and calls himself a cloth broker; nobody in Pashinar's cloth market has bought or sold from him.

**Rasunum knows.** Rasunum Gatituni wrote the four names in and recognized two of the witnesses, who swore to other bargains broken in other years by other men. He has said nothing, because the Warden names the clerk of the Forgetting and can unname him. He has copied the four entries and the witnesses' names onto a separate sheet, which he keeps in the lining of his writing-case. The Dunhari caravan master in the first hook is the fourth name.
