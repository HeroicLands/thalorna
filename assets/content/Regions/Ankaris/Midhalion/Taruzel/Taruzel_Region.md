> The pilot comes aboard where the strait narrows under the walls of Yamûzel, a gray-bearded man in a salt-stiff coat who smells of tar and strong coffee. He takes the steering oar without asking, reads the water ahead—pale green over sand, dark blue where the channel runs—and points his chin at the islands off the bow. "My name is **Nadûmar**, and twelve years ago I took a fishing boat with a ram spiked to her bow into the shoals out there and watched an emperor's squadron break up on the sand. Everything you buy in Tarûzel was paid for that week. Remember who paid for it, and remember who collects."

## Overview

Tarûzel Region is the archipelago in the eastern [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] that lies off the coast of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], within [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]]. It is the country of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Tarûzelian States]]: five merchant city-states that won their independence from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] at sea twelve years ago and are governed, in everything but name, by the guilds that paid for the war. Some twelve million people live here, in great port cities, market towns, fishing villages and the tribal country of the island hills.

An outsider remembers Tarûzel for its harbors. It is the premier maritime region of western Ankaris, and its ports carry the trade between the Vylarian Sea, the caravan roads of the east and the southern shore of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]]; its bazaars, its cooking and its fleets are known in every port of Mídhalión. A newcomer has to be told the other half: the dock workers, fishermen, captains and tribal warriors who won the war were promised land, guild membership, pensions and a voice in the new government, and they received a discharge and a small purse of silver. The islands are free, prosperous and seething.

## The Water and the Land

"Every bay on this coast is a harbor," Nadûmar says, "and every harbor has a guild sitting on it." The islands' coasts are deeply indented, and their bays make the finest natural harbors on the Vylarian Sea. Behind them the land climbs from narrow coastal plains through rolling hills to arid scrub on the heights of the larger islands. The climate is warm and dry, and the summers are hot, tempered along the coast by the sea breeze. To the east, across a strait some forty miles wide at its narrowest, lies the Amradi shore, where the caravan roads from [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]] reach the sea, and their goods cross to the islands by ship.

The **Tarûzelian archipelago** is half a dozen islands strung over some 140 miles of sea, the largest of them about sixty miles long. The channels between them, their shoals and their sheltered coves hold the Confederation's naval bases and trading posts, and they hold smuggling havens for those who know the passages. The pilot's first warning concerns them: do not take a deep-keeled ship into the island channels without someone aboard who knows the water. The [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzîr Shoals]] in those waters are a maze of sandbars, submerged rocks and hard currents. During the war a Vylarian squadron, the largest the empire had gathered in the eastern sea, came to break the Tarûzelian blockade of the island channels; the Tarûzelian captains met it in the Shoals, where its heavy warships could neither maneuver nor retreat, and in two days of fighting they burned its ships, captured them or drove them onto the sand. That battle won the war, and Tarûzel marks it every year with naval reviews and public festivals.

## Five Cities, Five Sets of Laws

"Learn the harbor before you learn the city," Nadûmar tells his passengers. "What is lawful at one quay is contraband at the next." Each city-state keeps its own laws, militia and council, and the patchwork of regulations between them makes smuggling and legal arbitrage—profiting from the gap between one city's rules and another's—two of the most profitable trades in the islands. Each of the five holds a city and the country around it:

- [[place-besikar2|Besîkar]], the seat of the city-state of [[affiliation-besikar|Besîkar]] (some three million people), is the largest and wealthiest city of Tarûzel and its de facto capital. The **Grand Council** of the Confederation meets in its **Grand Council Hall**, and the great guilds keep their headquarters in the marble-faced **Guild Quarter** along the **Grand Harbor**, a deep bay behind breakwaters and fortified islands that can shelter hundreds of ships. Behind the harbor the old city is a maze of narrow streets, covered bazaars and tenements, and in a dry-dock a captured Vylarian warship stands as the **Tamzîr Monument**.
- [[place-yamuzel2|Yamûzel]], the seat of [[affiliation-yamuzel|Yamûzel]] (some 2.2 million), is the naval stronghold on the narrows at the heart of the archipelago. Its **Strait Fortress** commands the passage, its **Naval Yards** build the Confederation's warships, and its marines are the nearest thing Tarûzel has to a standing army. Where Besîkar runs on money, Yamûzel runs on pride: its great families are admirals' families, its **Admiral's Council** governs the city, its annual **Fleet Review** commemorates the Shoals, and its **Veterans' Quarter** houses the sailors and marines who fought the war and never received what they were promised.
- [[place-qudumel2|Qudûmel]], the seat of [[affiliation-qudumel|Qudûmel]] (some 2.3 million), is the easternmost city, on the island nearest the mainland, a dusty, polyglot crossroads of mudbrick and tile where the sea trade meets the caravan goods of Dunhara and the Khazryn, ferried over the strait and landed at the **Caravan Gate**. Its **Caravansary Quarter** of hostels, stables and warehouses serves the caravan masters who cross with their goods, and its council seats representatives of the hill tribes beside the city's merchants. The great guilds hold less sway here, because the tribes and caravan peoples Qudûmel lives by will not deal with guild enforcers.
- [[place-bazunel2|Bazûnel]], the seat of [[affiliation-bazunel|Bazûnel]] (some two million) on the southern shore of the islands, is the cultural heart of the Confederation. Its **Design Yards** conceive the finest Tarûzelian ships, its **Academy of the Tides** teaches navigation, astronomy and natural philosophy, and the colorful buildings and open-air theaters of the **Painted Harbor** line its waterfront. It is the least guild-dominated of the cities, and artists, free-thinkers and the occasional fugitive who have fallen afoul of guild politics elsewhere find refuge there.
- [[place-sazumun2|Sazûmun]], the seat of [[affiliation-sazumun|Sazûmun]] (some 2.5 million), spreads its white-walled warehouses along a natural harbor at the mouth of the [[place-alzriver|Alz River]], and its quays are the conduit between the Dunhari desert trade and the western sea routes. Its merchant guilds hold absolute power and meet in the **House of Factors** above the harbor, and a captain who breaks guild protocol there finds no harbor master willing to accept her ship and no crew willing to sign on.

Smaller places fill the country between them. [[place-sharimun|Sharîmun]] is a port town and one of the most populous in the region, with quarries above the port, guilds of its own and an arena whose fighters belong to a gladiators' guild. [[place-pashinar|Pashînar]] is a trading town on the largest island, facing the mainland crossing, where the goods of the caravan routes from the Dunhari desert and southern [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] come over the strait; caravan masters stop there to restock, establish credit and settle prices with local factors, and a merchant who swindles or reneges in Pashînar is blacklisted from the caravan trade for years. [[place-shamular|Shamûlar]] is a merchant town, and [[place-hitusel|Hitûsel]] a coastal village.

## Peoples and Culture

Tarûzel is a human country, and a sharply divided one. The guild families live in palatial urban estates and educate their children in rhetoric, mathematics and mercantile law. Below them stands a large middle class of independent merchants, skilled craftspeople and ship captains, and below that the dock workers, sailors and urban poor crowded into the harbor districts. The islands honor wealth, reputation and the ability to strike a profitable deal above every other virtue. Spiced meats, flatbreads, honeyed pastries and strong coffee are its staples, and the bazaars of its port cities sell goods from across the known world.

The **hill tribes** of the larger islands' interiors are culturally distinct from the port cities. They keep older traditions, speak archaic dialects and resent the dominance of the cities. They sent many of the foot soldiers of the war of independence, harried the imperial supply lines in country they knew, and received nothing from the peace. The forts and military road they once garrisoned for imperial pay still cross the hills ([[lore-easternline|The Eastern Line]]).

The language of the region is [[skill-taruzelilng|Tarûzeli]], a rhythmic, long-voweled tongue with a deep vocabulary for navigation, commerce and the sea, and the working language of the guilds and the shipping routes.

## Faith and Magic

Religion in Tarûzel is syncretic. The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]], brought by the Vylarian conquest, dominates the coastal cities, where the merchant class favors [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenía]] for prosperity and [[lore-menervadty|Ménérva]] for knowledge, and sailors honor [[lore-taranondty|Táranon]] of the storms. The eastern states and the hill tribes keep to the older [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian Pantheon]]—[[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]], [[affiliation-korsadra|Khóršádrä]] and [[affiliation-aravesta|Árávēštä]] have large followings—and in Qudûmel it is the dominant faith. In many cities the temples of the two stand side by side, and a single family often patronizes both. In Qudûmel's **Temple of Two Fires** the rites of both are practiced under one roof.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] keeps chapters in the major cities, but the guilds regard it as a possible instrument of Vylarian influence and restrict its work, and the Ordo presses in turn for its monopoly to be enforced. Hedge mages, alchemists and fortune-tellers are more common here than in Vylaria, and in Qudûmel the eastern traditions that come in with the caravans—Āsháian fire-worship, Dunhari sand-divination and Vedyaran meditation—are practiced openly.

## Who Rules

On paper the Confederation's Grand Council gives each city-state an equal voice, and the office of **Arch-Consul** rotates among the cities. In practice the Council's voting rules weight property, the wealthiest cities and their guilds decide its business, and the Arch-Consulship is more ceremonial than powerful. Three great guilds rule Tarûzel in fact, and a traveler meets each of them differently:

- The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]], the banking cartel headquartered in Besîkar, financed the war and holds the Confederation's debt. You meet it as a letter of credit accepted across Mídhalión, as a seal on a contract that binds like a court order, and—if you default—as an enforcer at the door.
- The [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]], the shipping cartel rooted in Yamûzel, grew out of the veterans of the Tamzîr Shoals and is the Confederation's de facto navy. Its captains enjoy customs exemptions and the right to carry weapons in Tarûzelian ports, and a ship that sails outside its umbrella meets port restrictions and the occasional "mistaken" confrontation at sea.
- The [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]] trades openly in luxury goods and secretly in information, and it moves the cargoes the other guilds will not touch. Its agents identify themselves by sigil rings and coded phrases, and its membership rolls are not public.

Beneath the three, the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezâkûnî]], the Council of the Guild-Wardens seated at Besîkar, charters the guilds, registers masters, examines apprentices and judges disputes between trades, and the Confederation backs its rulings.

## Coin and Script

Say you land with Vylarian traveler's notes in your purse: the first moneylender you visit pushes them back across the table unhonored. The [[lore-trzlncrncy|Tarûzelian currency]] uses the Vylarian denominations—1 **Aurion** is 160 **Argo**, and 1 Argo is 8 **Bits**—but the coins are struck in Tarûzel, and the banking behind them is the **Merâzûnî**, the House of the Treasury that the Vezâkûnî's treasurer runs. The Tarûzelian Aurion is about 7 percent underweight by the Vylarian standard; it passes at face value anywhere in Tarûzel, and a Vylarian or Khelâthi changer who assays it pays less. The Argo is struck to full weight. Merâzûnî script is honored at any Merâzûnî-affiliated moneylender in the Confederation and at no Vylarian house, and Vylarian script is honored at no Tarûzelian one, so a merchant crossing between the two converts through coin, most often at the Heliónite houses that keep accounts on both sides.

## The Anger Under the Coast

Nadûmar's account of the peace is a veteran's, and he does not hide it: the merchant princes who printed the broadsheets about freedom meant their own freedom to trade, and the men who sailed into the Shoals heard something else. The merchant princes answer that they never lied: they spoke of freedom, and Tarûzel is free of imperial taxes, imperial governors and conscription into foreign legions. What followed the peace is not in dispute. Prominent veterans were accused of debts they did not owe and crimes they did not commit, bought off with small posts or imprisoned, and the captains who refused to be silenced were declared outlaws. Many of those took to the island channels and now raid guild shipping under names of their own—the **Free Captains**, the **Unchained**, the **Shoals Brotherhood**—while the guilds call all of them pirates.

The unrest rises and is put down: a harbor strike in Yamûzel, a tax riot in Besîkar's dock districts, a tribal raid on a guild caravan. The guilds answer with revoked licenses, called-in debts, blacklisted families and quiet enforcers, and they send no legions, because legions make martyrs. On the waterfront and in the veterans' halls people still pass around the old broadsheets with their broken chains and rising suns. The [[affiliation-thetamzir|Tamzîr]] and her captain, a veteran of the Shoals, run cargo on the margins of that order: the Compact counts him an irritant, the League would have him under its flag, and the Merchantry is the guild most likely to offer him work.

## Neighbors

Across the water to the north-west lies the short coast of [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], Tarûzel's natural trading partner, with which it shares cultural and intellectual ties. Beyond it, along the northern shore, lies [[place-vylariargn|Vylaría Region]]; relations with the empire are officially correct and privately hostile, Vylaria has not forgiven the loss of its eastern province, and its agents, often working with the Ordo Arcanis, gather intelligence in the Tarûzelian cities.

To the north, the [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría Region]] of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]] reaches the sea along a short stretch of coast beside Heliónis. At sea the League is Tarûzel's great commercial rival, and the Tarûzelian guilds would like to dominate its trade.

To the east, across the strait, lies the Kingdom of Amradad. Tarûzel keeps cordial relations with it: Amradad, long antagonistic to Vylaria, was among those who helped the Tarûzelians win their independence. Beyond the Amradi shore lie the deserts of Dunhara and the Khazryn, whose nomads hold the overland routes the island cities depend on, in a relationship that profits both and is often tense. Across the sea to the south lies the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]], Tarûzel's most important long-distance partner. Khelâthi gold, weapons and advisors kept the rebellion alive in its desperate first months; the merchant princes deny the debt in public and repay it with preferential trade and reduced harbor fees for Khelâthi ships, and the trade between their ports now runs directly, without Vylarian intermediaries.
