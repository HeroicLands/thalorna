## Overview

Two plumes of smoke rise over the north side of [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]]'s eastern market, and a newcomer can tell the temple's two halves apart by them before he reaches the door. The thin gray plume climbs from the smoke-hole of a low mudbrick dome, where the eternal flame of [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]], the Sacred Flame, has burned since before the empire came to the islands. The darker one, smelling of charcoal and hot iron, comes from the chimney of a pale stone hall beside it, where a forge-fire burns for [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], the Forge-Lord. The Temple of Two Fires is the one house in the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]] where the faiths of [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|the Twelve of the Flame]] and the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] are served under one roof, and Kudumel, a city whose households keep the Ashaian fire-rite at sunrise and sunset while its harbor trades with the west, is the city that needs it.

## What Is There

The **fire-house** is the old temple: a square of mudbrick under a low dome, its walls a yard thick, with no window and a single door facing the sunrise. On a stepped stone altar in the middle of the floor burns the flame. Its keepers feed it sandalwood and dried tamarisk with long bronze tongs, and they wear a cloth over the mouth so that no breath touches it. Twelve fire-bowls stand round the walls, one for each god of the Ashaian Twelve, and they are lit from the altar every dawn. The floor is swept twice a day, and the ash goes into lidded jars along the north wall.

The **hall of the Twelve** stands west of it, on the harbor side, in the dressed pale stone of the western cities: a long hall behind a portico of columns, built when the governors of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] rebuilt the shrine. At its far end, under a hood of black iron, a forge-hearth burns charcoal for Ústaron, kept by a smith of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], with an anvil before it on which nothing is worked except on the god's days. The altars of the other eleven gods of the Aureldian Twelve line the walls, and each carries the name of the house that endows it, cut into the stone beneath the offering ledge.

Between the two lies the **court**, open to the sky and paved in red tile, with a cistern at its center. Twelve columns stand round it, one for each domain the two pantheons share, and each is carved on two faces: on the side toward the hall with the symbol of the Aureldian god, and on the side toward the fire-house with the symbol of the Ashaian. The thunderbolt of [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]] faces west on the column whose east face carries the spear of [[lore-bazhramisdty|Bazhrámiš]] in its spiral of cloud. The golden tree of [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] backs onto the overflowing chalice of [[lore-aravestadty|Aravesta]], and the black gate of [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] onto the black sun of [[lore-azravandty|Azravan]]. The faithful of each rite pray at the same column with their backs to one another. The doorkeeper tells a newcomer one thing at the gate: pray at the face that is your god's, and do not walk round to the other side while someone is praying there.

In the gate passage the fire-keepers sell ash tokens, small discs of clay pressed with ash from the altar and stamped on one face with the anvil under its sheltering flame and on the other with the altar-flame in its ring of sparks. A caravan master hangs one round his neck for the desert road, and a western captain nails one over the door of his cabin. Beside the tokens hangs the endowment board, a long painted plank in two columns, the fire-house's benefactors on the left and the hall's on the right. Many houses of Kudumel appear in both.

## Who Keeps It

The fire-house is kept by its **Shekhul**, the master of its fire, [Sharimush Qatuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Kudumel man of sixty who has held the flame for twenty-two years. Four Delkoshekhs serve under him, two of them the temple's Flame-Readers, and a dozen Roqeshekhs feed the fire in watches through the day and night. The Keeper of the Fire-Lineage holds the record of the flame's descent, which traces it kindling by kindling to the [Great Fire-Temple of Amradad]{.name data-type="place/structure"} across the strait. The fire-house keeps the Great Fire-Temple's rite in its full form, with the long liturgies and the night watches that the hill shrines and the household fires shorten, and it is the only fire in Taruzel that does.

The hall of the Twelve is kept by a **college** of priests of the Aureldian faiths, each serving the altar of his own god, under a presiding priest the college chooses for five years. The present one is [Pashinuya Lartuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a priestess of the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]], because the hall is endowed by merchant houses and most of them endow Ólvenia first. The smith at the forge-hearth answers to the Sacred Forge and not to the college.

Both halves live on endowments and on the offerings of the day. The houses that keep the city's caravan trade endow the fire-house, the houses that keep its shipping endow the hall, and the greatest houses endow both and make sure the board shows it.

## Who Comes Here

- **The households of the city**, at sunrise and sunset, to light a household lamp from one of the twelve bowls when their own fire has gone out.
- **Caravan masters** before a crossing, to have a Flame-Reader read the road in the altar fire.
- **Western captains and their crews** before a sailing, at the west face of Brazemas's column.
- **The Kaliharan factors and sailors** of the harbor, who keep the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]], the World-Weaver, and light a lamp at her altar in the hall before every long road.
- **The chapter of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|the Order]] (Thordimalon)**, by letter. It holds that a Flame-Reader who reads true is working an art the Order licenses, and it has twice asked the council of Kudumel to register the temple's readers. The council has twice put the petition off.

## The Temple's Year

The temple keeps the festivals of both rites, the Aureldian ones in the hall and the Ashaian ones in the fire-house, and the court belongs to both. At the [[lore-silentpassage|Festival of the Silent Passage]] the hall's altar of Sélmoros is closed to the living for three nights, as every temple of his is, but the fire-house never closes. A Kudumel family whose house patronizes both rites crosses the court on those nights and speaks its dead aloud at the fire. On the night of the Ashaian [Festival of the Departed]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, when the fire-house kindles its [Death-Flame]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}, the priests of Sélmoros stand in the court at the west face of the black gate's column until the flame is lit.

On the first morning after the [[lore-thunderheadvigil|Thunderhead Vigil]] the Shekhul reads the temple's oldest roll aloud in the court. It is the roll from which [[lore-seaowednothing|When the Sea Owed Nothing]] was copied for [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], kept in a cedar case in a niche of the fire-house wall, and in it the storm on the hill is Bazhrámiš. At the [[lore-goldenharvest|Festival of the Golden Harvest]] the procession of Ólvenia's priestesses sets out from the hall's portico and goes round the eastern market, and the merchants of Kudumel lay their offerings of gain at both faces of the tree-and-chalice column.

## What Is Said of It

Kudumel says that the fire-house is older than the city and that the city was built round it. The Vylarian governors' reasons for rebuilding are told two ways. The Aureldian college says the old temple was falling and the empire saved it. The fire-keepers say the empire wanted its own gods inside the city's oldest house, and that the hall was built on the side facing the harbor so that the empire's ships would see it first.

## Current Situation {#present}

The temple is being asked about its fire from both sides of the strait. An envoy of the Great Fire-Temple of Amradad has crossed to read the record of the fire-lineage, the first such visit anyone at the temple remembers, and the Shekhul, Sharimush Qatuni, wants to know who sent for him. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s chapter, which has twice asked Kudumel's council to register the temple's Flame-Readers, now asks for one Roqeshekh by name, and she has stopped reading. The guilds of the coast are divided over the Order: the Compact would expel it as Vylaria's instrument, the Merchantry hires its mages, and the League wants its battle-magic, and Kudumel's council has kept the petition off its order.

- **The cracked token.** A ship's master swears that the ash token over his cabin door cracked across the anvil face on the night his ship was lost under him, and he wants the temple to tell him which god refused him.

# The Truth of the Two Fires {#truth .secret}

**The flame went out once.** In the winter of the Vylarian rebuilding, the governor's masons opened the old dome to set the new roof against the hall, rain came in through the gap, and the altar fire went out in the night while its keeper slept. The Shekhul of the day relit it before dawn from the only fire still burning in the precinct: the new forge-hearth in the hall, which the Sacred Forge's smith had kindled a month earlier from the fire-house altar, as a courtesy between the two fires. The eternal flame of Ataros has been, since that night, the child of Ústaron's hearth, which was its own child. The fire-lineage record says nothing of it. Each Shekhul is told on the day he takes the fire, and no one else alive knows. If the Great Fire-Temple of Amradad learned it, it could rule the lineage broken, and the full rite of the fire-house would be a rite performed over a borrowed fire. If the college of the hall learned it, the Aureldian houses of Kudumel would claim the older half of the temple as theirs.

**The envoy** has come because a Delkoshekh who left the temple after losing the Shekhul's succession to Sharimush told the Great Fire-Temple's priests that something happened to the flame in the rebuilding winter. He knew that much from his own master and did not learn the rest. The envoy has been told to read the lineage record for the years of the rebuilding, and to ask the Shekhul to swear to it at the altar.

**Temada Bissi reads true.** She sees in the flame, a talent the Thordimalon registers and takes into its own service, and she stopped reading because Sharimush told her to, to keep her name off the Order's rolls. The chapter had her name from a pupil of the fire-priests of the caravansaries' back courts, who have their own quarrel with the temple, which calls them hedge-readers.
