## Overview

The House of Factors stands on the headland above the harbor of [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]], on the north side of the [[place-alzriver|Alz River]] mouth, and it is the tallest building in the city. The merchant guilds' councils meet in it, and through them it governs the city-state of [[affiliation-sazumun|Sazumun]]. Every ship that comes to Sazumun is accepted or refused from its tower, and every tariff the city charges is set in its hall.

## What Is There

**The tower.** The House is a square tower of five floors on a broad base, built of stone and limewashed white like the warehouses below it, with a flat roof and a tall mast for the Harbor Master's flags. The top floor is the Harbor Master's room, which has a window on every side and a long table under the seaward window where the papers of ships in the roads are laid out. From it the whole bar is in sight, and the roads beyond it as far as the haze allows.

**The arcade.** The ground floor of the base is an open arcade on three sides, where factors, captains and brokers meet to trade on their feet. The tariff schedule hangs on its inner wall, painted in black on long white boards, one board to a guild, and the Keeper of the Tariff's clerks repaint a line whenever the House changes a rate. The arcade is open to anyone in the city from sunrise to sunset, and it is the best place in Sazumun to hear what a cargo is worth.

**The hall.** Above the arcade lies the hall where the guilds' councils sit, a long room with benches in ranks on either side and the Speaker's chair at the end. The House's register, in which the Speaker reads every agreement between the guilds, lies on a stand before the chair. The Registrar of Monopolies keeps his roll in a room behind it. The hall is closed to anyone who is not a merchant prince, a guild master or a clerk of the House.

**The stair and the board.** A broad stair climbs from the street to the arcade. At its foot stands a tall board painted black, and on it, in white letters, are the names of every captain, merchant and house outside protocol. A name goes up when the House orders it and comes down only when the House strikes it off. Porters spit on the step below the board as they pass, and nobody stands on it.

## How It Works

The guilds' councils sit when the Speaker calls them, which in the sailing season is every few days. A council's agreement binds when the Speaker has read it into the register before the assembled masters, and it is posted in the arcade by the next morning. A merchant prince speaks for his house in the hall, and a guild master for his guild, and the [[affiliation-hitusuni|Hitusuni]], whose warehouses line the quays below, have sat there longer than any other house.

The Harbor Master answers to the House alone. The present one is [Nibura Visizuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a factor of the [Visizuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} shipping house, a tall, gray-haired woman who has kept the tower for nine years and is seldom seen below it. She reads a ship's papers when the pilot boat brings them in, checks her master's name against the board and her cargo against the roll of monopolies, and hoists a guild's color for a ship she accepts. A plain black flag refuses one. There is no appeal from the flag except to the House itself, and the House sits when the Speaker calls it.

## What Is Said of It

The quay porters say the House is the only building in Sazumun that pays no tariff, and that it charges the others for the privilege. The pilots say the Harbor Master can tell a ship's cargo by how she sits in the water before the papers come ashore. The tally-men say the black board has never held fewer than forty names.

## The Present {#present}

The House sits often this season and argues more than it posts. A Khelathi captain whose name went up on the black board this spring swears he broke no protocol, and nobody in the House will tell him which one. [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]], whose house has sat in the hall longest, courts the treasury's clerks for a moneylender's charter, and the [Visizuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, whose factor [Nibura Visizuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"} keeps the tower, watch the Hitusuni as carefully as ever. Three foreign merchantmen have stuck on the bar since spring with harbor pilots aboard, and the harbor guild has fined the pilots.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The Khelathi captain.** A captain on the black board hires a party to learn which protocol he is supposed to have broken before his crew drifts away.
- **The charter dinner.** [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]] wants guests at his dinner for the Merazuni's clerks who can tell him afterward what the clerks said when he left the room.
- **The pilot's fine.** A harbor pilot fined a year's wages for a grounding on the bar asks a party to stand beside him when he appeals to the House, if the Speaker will call it.

# The Truth of the Board {#truth .secret}

**A third of the names broke no protocol.** Every name on the black board went up by order of the House, and the House orders it when the Registrar of Monopolies reports a breach. The Registrar reports a breach when one is found, and he also reports one when a merchant prince asks him to, for a captain who carries a rival's cargoes, a merchant who undersold a guild or a house that would not sell. A name put up that way is as good as any other: the captain's crew leaves him, the guilds stop trading with him, and he has nobody to ask why.

**The Harbor Master keeps count.** Nibura's house, the Visizuni, carries many of the cargoes the Hitusuni store, and each house keeps a careful account of the other. She refuses what the board tells her to refuse, and in a book of her own she writes down every name she has refused and, beside each, the merchant prince who asked the Registrar for it, when she can learn it. Most of the requests come from the Hitusuni and from the guilds that store with them. She keeps the book as insurance, against the day the Hitusuni ask for a Visizuni captain's name.

**The Khelathi captain.** The captain in the city's first hook carried a cargo that a factor of the Hitusuni had meant to buy cheaply on the quay and sold it in [Jiramun]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the street market, instead. His name is in Nibura's book with [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]]'s beside it. A party that gets the book, by persuasion or theft, holds the name of every merchant prince who has used the board as a weapon, and that is enough to bring half the House against the other half.
