## Overview {#overview}

The houses of the [[place-paintedharbor|Painted Harbor]] are ochre, blue and red, one color to a house from the waterline to the roof, and a ship coming up from the south sees them before she can make out a single window. Behind them Bazunel climbs a low slope of flat roofs, cisterns and stepped streets to a ridge of vineyards. It is a city of 150,000 on the southern shore of [[place-tashilar|Tashilar]], the seat of the city-state of [[affiliation-bazunel|Bazunel]] and the cultural heart of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]: smaller and less wealthy than [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], less martial than [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] and less cosmopolitan than [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]], and ahead of all three in its theaters, its astronomers and the lines of its ships.

The name is the sail's: _bazun-_, to spread cloth to the wind, with _-el_, the ground of it. Bazunel is also the city where people go when the guilds of another city have closed their doors on them. Artists, free-thinkers, debtors with a grievance and the occasional fugitive come here because the council will not send them back, and a good share of the city's talent arrived that way.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The harbor is a round bay open to the south. [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] ships make it their first Taruzelian port, and in the sailing season their striped sails lie along the eastern quays beside the coasters from the channels. A ship from the north rounds the island's western end and comes in past the slipways of the [[place-designyards|Design Yards]], which line the bay's western arm, with the half-built hulls on them standing higher than the houses.

A harbor clerk of the council meets every ship in a skiff at the mouth of the bay. He writes down the ship, her master and her cargo, and then asks one question the clerks of other Taruzelian harbors do not ask: which house of Bazunel stands surety for her? A ship with a surety ties up on the Painted Harbor, and the house that named her answers for her dues. A ship with none pays a deposit against them and lies at the outer quays under the sea wall, a long walk from anywhere.

A party walking from the northern coves comes by the ridge road through terraced vines and almond orchards, and from the last hilltop sees the whole bay below. Bazunel keeps a sea wall along the harbor and no wall on the landward side. The road becomes a street at the first cistern-house, and the stranger is in the city before anyone has asked his name.

The paint is the first thing a stranger sees. The second is the water-sellers, because the city has no river and few springs, and every street has a cistern with a stone lid and a seller beside it with a yoke of jars. The third comes after dark, when lamps move about on the roofs of the upper town and the city's astronomers go to work.

## Politics {#present}

Bazunel's council keeps its balance, and the coast's quarrels lean on it. Agents of other cities' guilds arrive with warrants the magistrates will not hear, and the newcomers' lanes of Zatturel fill. [[being-brshrskn|Abarash Rusakuni]], who sits for the shipwrights and patronizes the theaters, wants the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] to charter the [[place-designyards|Design Yards]] as a trade of their own, free of the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]], which owes his house its fees on years of galleys. The talk from Besikar of council seats that pass from head to heir would end the vote of the crafts and the academies on which the city's balance stands. On the bench [[being-tarrukeshkar|Tarruk Eshkarum]] says that [[being-ytlshpshtm|Yutalush Pushutum]]'s even hand is a refusal to take a side, and the city argues over whether it can afford to take none. A cartographer's chart of the channels was stolen from her rooms this winter, and a factor of the League, a broker of the Merchantry and a Helionite merchant who pays in Vilérian coin have all asked after it in the harbor taverns.

## The Quarters

Bazunel is small enough to cross on foot in an hour, and each of its quarters looks like what it does.

- **The Painted Harbor** curves round the head of the bay. The merchant houses keep their warehouses on the quay and live above them, each behind its own color, and between the houses the theaters climb the slope in half-rings of stone steps. In the evenings the harbor taverns put out their card tables, and the city's money changes hands over them.
- **The Design Yards** fill the western arm: stone slipways, tiled sheds, timber stacked to the eaves, and above the longest slip the whitewashed loft of the [[affiliation-rusakuni|Rusakuni]], where the lines of the Confederation's ships are drawn full size on the floor.
- **The upper town** lies along the ridge. Its houses are older and plainer, built round inner courts and whitewashed without a trace of color, and they belong to the old noble families, whose seats on the council are older than the guilds' presence in the city. The halls of the [Academy of the Tides]{.name data-type="affiliation"} stand among them. The Academy's astronomers rent the flat roofs of the upper town by the season, and nearly every roof on the ridge carries a stone sighting-pillar.
- **[Zatturel]{.name data-type="place/site"}** is the name the city gives the lanes behind the eastern quays, where the newcomers live: _zattur-_, to release a fastening, with _-el_, the ground of it, the ground where a knot comes undone. Its houses are let by the room and by the week. Copyists, engravers, scene-painters, tutors and physicians who left another city in a hurry live there, and its taverns are full of other cities' accents.

## Markets and Trades

Bazunel sells skill. Its most valuable goods leave on paper: the drawings of the Design Yards, sold to yards across the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]], and the Academy's charts, which captains buy at the stalls on the Academy stair and copy at their peril. Behind them come the trades of a city that paints, plays and reckons for a living: pigments and painted cloth, playbooks and broadsheets, sand-glasses, sounding-leads and brass sighting-instruments, and the services of physicians and navigators. The terraces of Tashilar send in wine, figs and almonds, and the Khelathi ships bring southern hardwoods, dyes and fine cotton.

The fish market fills the eastern quay at dawn and is gone by the second hour. The water-sellers work all day, and in a dry summer the price of a jar of water is the first thing the city argues about. The money is the [[lore-trzlncrncy|Taruzelian Currency]], and Vilérian script is not honored on the quays. Moneylenders affiliated with the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]] keep their tables at the foot of the Academy stair, and they change Khelathi coin at a better rate than the card tables do.

The theaters play from late spring to the first autumn gales. A company is paid by a patron, a merchant house or one of the old families, whose name is painted over the stage for the season; the steps are free to stand on, and a cushion to sit on costs a copper. In summer the companies take their plays up the ridge road to the hill villages of Tashilar and play on the village stages.

## Who Lives Here

Shipwrights, draughtsmen and timber merchants work the western arm. Astronomers, navigators, cartographers and physicians live in the upper town, and so do the old families. Actors, painters, musicians and scene-builders live wherever the theaters are, which is mostly the harbor. Merchant houses hold the quays, and the Rusakuni, whose head [[being-brshrskn|Abarash Rusakuni]] sits for the shipwrights on the council and is the theaters' most generous patron, stand above the rest. In season the Khelathi captains and their crews are the largest foreign company in the city, and they lodge aboard.

Two residents show the city's temper. [[being-ytlshpshtm|Yutalush Pushutum]], a healer-priest of the [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]], keeps a clinic below the temple and treats whoever comes through its door, the poor of Zatturel and the council's own men alike. [[being-tarrukeshkar|Tarruk Eshkarum]], one of the council's magistrates, regards that even hand as a refusal to take a side, and says so from the bench.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

A council of merchant-princes governs Bazunel, and it is the most balanced council in Taruzel. The merchant houses sit on it through the Councilor of the Houses, who speaks for the trade of the Painted Harbor. Beside him sit the Councilor of the Crafts, usually a master shipwright of the Design Yards; the Councilor of the Academies, usually the head of the Academy of the Tides; and the Councilor of the Old Families. The First of the Council, [[being-slmyshlmni|Salmuya Shilmuni]], is elected by the council for a fixed term and holds the city's seal while she does. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] and the other great guilds keep agents in the city and no seats.

The refuge is a matter of law. A stranger who asks to stay is enrolled on the council's roll as a Resident, living under the city's protection without its freedom. The council's courts do not hear another city's guild warrant: a guild that wants a man back from Bazunel has no court to ask. A debt under seal is another matter, because a debt is a debt in any Taruzelian court, and a creditor may sue a Resident for what he owes like anyone else. He may not take the man off the street. The worst the council does to anyone is strike him from the roll and put him aboard the next ship out, and a person struck from the roll has no protection, no bench in the studios and no seat in the theaters.

Order in the streets rests on the houses and the guilds. A shopkeeper robbed in the night goes first to the house or guild he belongs to, which keeps its own street and wants the thief found before its neighbors hear of it. If that fails, he goes to the magistrates, who sit each morning in the arcade below the council hall and hear complaints in the order they are made. The council's watch is small. It keeps the quays, the yards' gates and the cistern-houses, and it is out in strength only on a theater night, when half the city is on the harbor steps.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The harbor inns stand above the taverns, and their rooms look out on the bay and smell of paint and tar. Their hosts ask which house stands surety for a guest's ship and charge accordingly. In winter, when the theaters are dark, the companies let their players' rooms to travelers, cheaply and with no questions. Zatturel lets rooms by the week to anyone who pays in coin, and its landlords do not write down names. A party with a letter to a house of the harbor lodges in that house's rooms above its warehouse, and its business is known on the quay by morning.

## History {#history}

Bazunel's trading families grew rich under the empire as intermediaries of the eastern trade, and its old families held their seats on the council before the guilds came to the city, in the plain white houses of the upper town. The city became the Confederation's refuge by law: its council enrolls any stranger who asks to stay as a Resident, and its courts do not hear another city's guild warrant. Artists, free-thinkers, debtors with a grievance and fugitives from guild politics elsewhere have come under that law ever since, and much of the city's talent arrived that way.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The warrant.** An agent of a Besikar guild arrives with a warrant for a copyist living in Zatturel, and the magistrates refuse to hear it. He hires a party to bring the copyist aboard a ship lying outside the harbor, where the council's law does not reach.
- **The dark stage.** A theater's patron has withdrawn in the middle of the season, and his name has been painted out over the stage. The company's manager wants to know why, before the other patrons hear of it, and wants a new patron found by the next play.
- **The surety.** A party's ship arrives with no house to stand for her. A factor of the harbor offers to stand surety in return for a small errand, a parcel carried up to the upper town after dark.
- **The roof.** An astronomer of the Academy finds that someone has been using her rented roof on the nights she does not, and has left chalk marks round the sighting-pillar that are not hers.

# The Truth of the Refuge {#truth .secret}

**The roll is for sale.** Every newcomer enrolled as a Resident gives the council's clerks a name, a trade and the city he came from, and the roll of Residents is kept in the upper town by the clerks of the Councilor of the Old Families. The old families have no ships and few warehouses, their seats are older than their money, and the roll is the one thing in Bazunel they hold that others want. They sell from it. They never sell to the guilds of another city, because that would end the refuge and the refuge is what fills the roll. They sell to employers.

**The buyer.** The best customer is a factor of [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|The Crimson Ledger]], who buys each quarter the names of the newcomers who list a trade with a steady hand: copyists, engravers, seal-cutters and painters. He finds them in Zatturel and offers them work. The work is copying, at first honest. When it stops being honest, the price of refusing is a letter to the city the copyist fled, giving the guild that wants him his lodging and his new name. Most of them end in one scene-painters' loft on the harbor, and the [[place-paintedharbor|Painted Harbor]]'s own note says what is made there.

**The hooks.** The copyist in the first hook is one of the Ledger's, and the Besikar agent's warrant is real: the Ledger wants him gone before he talks, and has made sure his old guild heard where he was. The patron in the second hook withdrew when he learned that his company had begun buying its scenery from the Ledger's loft at the loft's prices, and he is frightened, not offended. The parcel in the third hook is the Ledger's payment for the quarter's names, on its way to the clerk who keeps the roll. The chalk marks on the roof in the fourth belong to the Ledger's lookout, who signals from the ridge to the ship in the roads that carries the loft's work out of Bazunel.
