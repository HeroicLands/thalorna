## Overview

On a clear morning the hills of the Amradi shore stand along the eastern horizon from the walls of [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]], forty miles off across the strait, and the sun comes up behind them onto an island the color of dust. This is Nishurel, the north-easternmost of the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] and the nearest to the mainland: _nishur-_, to be bright, with _-el_, the ground of it, the bright ground, for the glare off its pale limestone and its whitewashed walls. It is about thirty-five miles long, and its two million and more people make up the city-state of [[affiliation-qudumel|Kudumel]] of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]].

Nishurel is the island the east comes to first. Its ferries carry the caravan goods of [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] over the strait, its roads carry camels as often as mules, and its faith is the faith of the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]].

## The Land

Nishurel is the driest of the islands. In late summer the east wind comes off the mainland hot and full of grit, and for a week or two at a time the sky goes yellow and the harbor masters close the quays at noon. The rain falls in winter, little and hard, and every roof and terrace on the island drains into a cistern. The coastal plain is narrow and stony, farmed in barley, beans and figs behind walls of piled stone, with date palms round the wells.

Inland the ground rises in low, bare limestone hills cut by dry gullies that run with water a few days a year. The hill country of the interior, part of [[place-tulurel|Tulurel]], is the country of the [[affiliation-saliruni|Saliruni]], the island's hill tribe, and the hill tribes hold seats on Kudumel's council beside the city's merchants. Their hill roads, worn paths between wells, cross the island from the eastern shore to the western, and the caravan houses of Kudumel hire tribal guides to take goods over them.

On the hilltops stand small fire-shrines of the Ashaian faith, each a domed room of whitewashed stone with a fire kept in it, and a traveler sees one or two on the skyline from almost anywhere on the island.

## Harbors and Roads

- **Kudumel and the Caravan Gate.** The city stands on the eastern shore, facing the strait. The ferries from the Amradi coast land at its Caravan Gate, a day's sail across in summer and longer when the winter wind is against them.
- **The western shore.** Across a channel some ten miles wide lies the north-eastern cape of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]]. The fishing harbors on Nishurel's western side trade with Qalirel by small boat, and the island's goods for the western cities go out that way or round by sea from Kudumel.
- **The north beaches.** The northern end of the island is long, open beaches with no harbor, empty except for fishermen's huts and the tracks of the herds that come down to the shore in winter.
- **The hill roads.** No road on Nishurel is paved. The legions' forts on the ridges stand ruined, and the paths between the wells are the tribes' to keep and to close.

## Crossing Nishurel

A party landing at the Caravan Gate with business on the western shore crosses Nishurel in two days by the hill roads, and it does not go alone. The caravan houses hire guides at the Gate, and a guide's first act is to buy water for the road at the city's wells, because the wells on the hill roads are the tribes' and are sold by the skin. The road climbs out of the plain through stony fields to the hills, where the herds of goats and camels move between the wells and the shrines stand on the skyline. At each well the guide pays, greets, and drinks a cup with the keeper of the well. A traveler who refuses that cup has insulted the keeper, and the next well on the road is found dry.

The western descent comes down to the fishing harbors on the channel, where boats wait to take passengers over to Qalirel.

## Tracks from the North Beaches

Herders coming down from the hills report something on the north beaches that has no business there: the tracks of camels, laden ones by the depth of them, coming up off the sand from the sea and going inland by night toward the hill roads. No harbor stands on that shore and no ferry lands there. The officers of the Caravan Gate say nothing has come in, and the dues they collect are down this season. A Kudumel merchant who deals in caravan goods, and who is losing sales to someone selling the same goods cheaper, offers a party good silver to learn where the camels come from and whose goods they carry.

## Current Situation {#present}

Nishurel's trade is leaking round its Gate. Herders find the tracks of laden camels coming up off the north beaches by night, the Caravan Gate's dues are down, and goods sell in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]]'s bazaars under the price of goods that paid them. In the hills the [[affiliation-saliruni|Saliruni]] still wait for the share of the hill-road tolls that the [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] promised them in the war, and this season the guides will not take a Balinuni train over the hill roads. A Tribal Councilor has not come down to Kudumel's council, and his household camps by the west gate without him.

# The Truth of the North Beaches {#truth .secret}

**Who lands there.** Caravan masters from the Amradi shore are sending part of their goods over on small open boats, sailed by night across the strait in calm weather and run up onto the north beaches, where camels brought down from the hills meet them. The goods go inland by the hill roads and are sold on the western shore and in Kudumel's own bazaars without ever passing the Caravan Gate, so they pay no Gate dues and sell under the price of goods that did.

**Who knows.** The hill guides who bring the camels down are paid by the bale, and the tribes whose wells the camels drink at are paid for the water at the usual rate and ask no questions about where the loads came from. Two members of Kudumel's council know as well, because they take a share of every landing, and they make sure the Gate's officers are never sent north.

**What follows.** The merchant who hires the party wants a name to take to the council. The names a party can find are two councilors of the council he would take them to. A party that brings proof to the wrong councilor is offered a share; a party that brings it to the right one starts a quarrel inside the city's government that every caravan house in Kudumel takes a side in.
