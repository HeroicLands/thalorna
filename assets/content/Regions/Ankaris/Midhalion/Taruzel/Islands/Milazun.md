## Overview

In the weeks of the rose harvest a ship coming down from the narrows smells Milazun before her lookout sees it. The island is the smallest of the inhabited islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]], about fifteen miles long, and it lies south of the narrows between [[place-khamisar|Khamisar]] and [[place-tashilar|Tashilar]]. Its name is its trade: _milaz-_, sweet, with _-un_, where the act is done, the place where the sweet is made. Some two hundred thousand people live on it, and it belongs, with Khamisar, to the city-state of [[affiliation-yamuzel|Yamuzel]] of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]].

Milazun lives by scent and sweetness: rose-water and attar of rose, honey, candied fruit, and the perfumes of [[place-shamular|Shamular]], its one town.

## The Land

The island is a cluster of low hills around a handful of sheltered valleys that open to the south, out of the north wind. The valleys hold the rose gardens, walled terraces of pink and red roses planted in rows, with orchards of quince, apricot and pomegranate between them. The hillsides above are thyme and sage scrub, and the island's beehives stand there in rows of clay pipes, so that Milazun's honey tastes of thyme.

The roses are picked in late spring, before sunrise, while the dew is on them, by whole villages working together. The petals go the same morning to the **still-houses**, stone buildings with copper stills and long rows of fires, where they are distilled into rose-water and, at far greater cost, into a few drops of attar. A still-house works day and night through the harvest, and the smoke from its fires mixes with the smell of the roses over every valley.

The guild families of the Confederation, and of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] above all, keep summer houses on Milazun's hills, walled villas with gardens and cisterns, and their households cross from the larger islands when the summer heat comes on.

## Harbors and Roads

- **Shamular.** The town stands on the island's one good harbor, a bay on the southern shore where the merchant ships load rose-water, attar and honey.
- **The coves.** Small coves round the coast hold fishing boats and the private landings of the summer houses. Boats cross from the northern coves to Khamisar's southern shore and from the southern ones to Tashilar's northern coves, a few hours either way.
- **The valley lanes.** No road on Milazun is longer than a morning's walk. Walled lanes link the valleys to Shamular, wide enough for a donkey with two baskets of petals.

## Crossing Milazun

A party can walk Milazun from end to end in a day, and outside the harvest it is the quietest island in Taruzel. The lanes run between garden walls overhung with roses and fruit trees, and the villages sit at the heads of the valleys, each round its still-house and its cistern. In the harvest weeks the lanes are crowded from before dawn with pickers and donkeys, and every still-house hires extra hands to keep its fires; a traveler who wants work for a fortnight finds it here. The summer houses on the hilltops keep their gates shut and their own watchmen, and a stranger on their lanes is turned back politely.

## The Attar in Besikar

Milazun attar is sold by the drop, at a price in silver that only the guild families pay, and each still-house seals its flasks with its own mark in wax. This year a Besikar perfumer who has bought from the same still-house for twenty years opened a sealed flask and found the attar cut with a cheaper oil, and the still-house swears its flasks left Milazun pure. Two more perfumers in Besikar have found the same since. The still-house's master, whose name and credit are ruined if the story spreads, hires a party to find where between his still-house and the Besikar quays the attar is being cut.

## Current Situation {#present}

Milazun's quarrel is its seals. Attar sold in Besikar under the seal of a still-house that has sold to the same perfumer for twenty years has been found cut with cheaper oil, and two more Besikar perfumers have found the same. The still-house's master stands to lose his name and his credit, and the Warden of the Seals in [[place-shamular|Shamular]], [Meraza Latari]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has been brought a flask whose seal she cannot match. In Shamular someone has quietly bought up the debts of the perfumer [[being-kdmyqbsn|Kadumuya Kubasuni]], whose license carries a seat on the town's board. The guild families of Besikar keep their summer houses on the hills behind closed gates.

# The Truth of the Attar {#truth .secret}

**Where it is cut.** The attar is cut in Besikar, not on Milazun and not at sea. The still-house sells its flasks through a Besikar house that buys the whole of its attar each year and resells it to the city's perfumers. A clerk of that house has learned to lift a wax seal with a warm blade and set it back, and he takes a third of each flask, tops it up with a cheaper oil, and sells the stolen attar to perfumers in Helionis, where nobody knows the still-house's seal.

**Who knows.** The house's head does not know, and would hang the clerk if he did, because the house's credit rests on the still-house's seal as much as the master's does. The clerk keeps the stolen attar in a rented loft above a Besikar coffee-house until a ship for Helionis sails, and he keeps the warm blade and a lump of the still-house's wax in the same box.

**What follows.** The flasks themselves give the clerk away: a seal that has been lifted and set back shows a thin line of darker wax around its edge when the flask is held to a candle. The still-house's master can see it when it is shown to him. Besikar's guilds care more about a broken seal than about the attar, and a seal-breaker brought before them is ruined however much he has sold.
