## Overview

"Deep water is free," a pilot of [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] tells a captain who asks his fee. "You are paying me for the shallow." The waters of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] are the strait between the islands and the Amradi shore, the narrows at the heart of the archipelago, and the channels that wind between the six inhabited islands and the rocks and islets around them. Taruzelians call them all **Zupumun**, from _zupum-_, to run, with _-un_, where the act is done: where the water runs. A current is a _zupumush_ in Taruzeli, and every pilot in the islands can name the currents of his own waters and the hours they turn.

Two waters are both called "the strait", and a newcomer has to be told which is meant. **The strait** in a merchant's mouth is the crossing to the mainland. **The strait** in a sailor's mouth, and in the name of the [[place-straitfortress|Strait Fortress]], is the narrows under Yamuzel.

## The Strait

The strait to the mainland is forty miles wide at its narrowest, between [[place-nishurel|Nishurel]] and the Amradi coast, and wider to the south, where [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]]'s eastern shore faces the mainland across fifty miles and more. It is open water, deep and generally kind, and its traffic is the ferries: broad-beamed sailing craft that carry caravan goods, animals and caravan masters from the Amradi coastal ports to the Caravan Gate of [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] and to [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]]. A ferry crosses to Kudumel in a day with a fair wind. In summer the north wind blows steadily down the strait and both crossings are easy; in winter the wind drops and shifts, sudden storms come off the mainland, and the ferries sail only on settled days.

## The Narrows

The narrows lie between the south-western cape of Qalirel and the eastern cliffs of [[place-khamisar|Khamisar]], three miles across at their tightest. The beacon of [[place-sharimun|Sharimun]] burns on the Qalirel side every night of the sailing season, and the Strait Fortress of Yamuzel stands on the other. A ship going through the archipelago from north to south, or from the strait to the western islands, passes through here or goes the long way round outside the islands. The current in the narrows runs hard with the tide, and merchant ships wait in the roads below the fortress for slack water and a convoy.

## The Channels

West and south of the narrows the water breaks up into channels, between Khamisar, [[place-visizel|Visizel]], [[place-milazun|Milazun]] and [[place-tashilar|Tashilar]] and the rocks and islets scattered among them. Some channels are a mile wide and deep enough for any ship; others narrow to a cable's length between reefs, or run over sand that moves with every winter storm. The worst ground of all is the [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]], west of Khamisar, a maze of sandbars, submerged rocks and hard currents. The pilots' first warning to any captain is the rule of the whole archipelago: do not take a deep-keeled ship into the channels without someone aboard who knows the water.

The channels are also full of places to hide. Coves under cliffs, inlets behind islets and anchorages screened by reefs are known only to the people who use them: fishermen, smugglers, the watch-boats of Yamuzel, and the outlaw captains who raid guild shipping and whom the guilds call pirates.

## Who Sails Them

- **Pilots.** Every harbor keeps its pilots, and Yamuzel's are the best in the islands. A pilot boards a ship at the edge of his own waters and leaves her at the edge of the next pilot's, and he charges by the draft of the ship, so a deep-keeled hull pays most.
- **Convoys.** Merchant ships passing through the archipelago gather below the Strait Fortress and go through the channels in convoy, under the escort of the navy's galleys or the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s.
- **Ferries and island boats.** Small sailing boats cross between the islands every day: from Nishurel's western shore to Qalirel, from Khamisar's southern coves to Milazun and on to Tashilar.
- **Smugglers and outlaws.** The differences between the five city-states' laws and tariffs make smuggling one of the islands' great trades, and the channels are its roads.

## The Lantern off the North Cape

The channel between Nishurel and the north-eastern cape of Qalirel is ten miles wide and deep, and every pilot calls it safe, but this year two ships have been lost on the rocks off Qalirel's north cape on clear, calm nights. The survivors of the second said they steered for a lantern they took for the light of a fishing harbor, and struck before they reached it. By the time the Besikar house that holds the salvage rights on that shore sent its men down, the fishermen of the cape had stripped both wrecks. A shipowner who lost a hull and its cargo offers a party a share of anything it recovers to find out who shows the lantern.

## Converging Interests {#present}

Every party on the coast wants a hand on the water this year. [[being-dlzrtndn|Delzuru Tanaduni]] wants the Grand Council to grant the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]] a toll on every hull through the narrows, collected by League galleys, and the foreign masters who wait in the roads for a convoy mean to resist it. The League's patrols sweep the channels for the outlaw captains, and the coves hide them as they always have. Two ships have been lost on the rocks off Qalirel's north cape on clear nights this year, steering for a lantern, and the Besikar house that holds the salvage rights on that shore found both wrecks stripped. On Nishurel's north beaches, where no ferry lands, herders find the tracks of laden camels coming up off the sand, and the Caravan Gate's takings are down.

# The Truth of the Lantern {#truth .secret}

**Who shows it.** A handful of men of the fishing harbor on Qalirel's north cape show the lantern from a rock above the reef on nights when a ship's lights are seen in the channel. Their harbor is in debt to the Besikar house that holds the salvage rights on that shore, and the house takes the best of every wreck by law. The fishermen strip a wreck in the hours before the house's men can ride down from the nearest guild estate, and they sell what they take across the channel on Nishurel, where the Besikar house's rights do not run.

**Who else knows.** The whole harbor knows, and keeps it to itself. The harbor's own boats went out to the second wreck and pulled six of its crew from the water, because the fishermen want cargo, not drowned sailors, and the survivors were fed and sent on to Besikar the next morning without being told what they had seen.

**What follows.** The house that holds the salvage rights wants the wreckers hanged and the harbor's debts called in, which would empty the harbor. The shipowner wants his cargo back. The fishermen want their debts gone. A party that learns the truth has to decide which of them to tell.
