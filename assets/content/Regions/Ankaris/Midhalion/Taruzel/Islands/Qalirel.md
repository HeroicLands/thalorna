## Overview

From the deck of a ferry crossing from the Amradi shore, Qalirel shows first as a long gray ridge that does not end, and a passenger who watches it for an hour sees it still running south along the horizon. It is the largest of the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]], about sixty miles from its north-eastern cape to its south-western one and twenty across at its widest, and its name says so: _qalir-_, long, with _-el_, the ground of it, the long ground. Some three million people live on it, and it is the country of the city-state of [[affiliation-besikar|Besikar]], the largest and wealthiest of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]].

The island has two faces. Its north-western coast, toward [[place-helionis|Helionis]] and the open sea, is cut into deep bays, and the greatest of them holds [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] and its Grand Harbor. Its eastern coast faces the strait and the mainland, straighter and more exposed, and the caravan goods of the east come ashore there at [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]]. Between the two runs a spine of hills, and over the spine runs the road that joins them.

## The Land

A narrow coastal plain rings the island, wider on the north-western side, where the rain comes in winter off the sea. It is farmed hard: barley and beans on the flat, vines and fruit trees on terraces cut into the first slopes, and walled gardens round the country houses of the Besikar guild families, who keep estates within a day's ride of the city. Every village has its cistern and its well, and in late summer, when the wells run low, water is sold by the jar.

Above the terraces the land climbs through rolling hills of oak and scrub to a long ridge of bare limestone and thyme, where the goats outnumber the people. The interior above the last terraces is part of the hill country of [[place-tulurel|Tulurel]] and belongs to the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]], a hill tribe that keeps its own councils and pastures and resents the harbor towns below it. Along the ridges stand the ruined hill forts that the legions built and the tribes garrisoned for imperial pay ([[lore-easternline|The Eastern Line]]), and the military road the legions cut still crosses the spine between them.

## Harbors and Roads

- **The north-western bays.** The Grand Harbor of Besikar, behind its breakwaters and fortified islets, is the finest anchorage on the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]]. The smaller bays along that coast hold fishing harbors and the private quays of guild estates.
- **The eastern shore.** Pashinar's bay is the one good harbor on the strait side, and the ferries from the Amradi coastal ports land there, a day's sail with a fair wind. The rest of that coast is beaches and low cliffs, with fishing boats drawn up on the sand.
- **The south-western cape.** [[place-sharimun|Sharimun]] stands on the cape over the eastern mouth of the narrows, and its name is its work: _sharim-_, to burn through the night, with _-un_, where the act is done. Its beacon burns on the headland every night of the sailing season, and its quarries cut building stone from the hill behind the port.
- **The pass road.** The old military road climbs from Pashinar over the pass to the western plain and Besikar: four days for a mule train. The Confederation does not keep it, and every winter's rain takes another stretch of its paving. The alternative is the sea, round the south-western cape and through the narrows, in a shipping house's hull at that house's freight rate.
- **The coast road.** A cart road runs the length of the north-western plain from Besikar to Sharimun, busy with farm wagons, quarry carts and the riders of the guild houses.

## Crossing Qalirel

A party bound from Pashinar for Besikar has the choice the caravan masters have: the pass road or the sea. On the road, the first day goes through the dry eastern plain, past caravanserais, mule lines and the factors' riders going up and down with letters of credit. On the second the road climbs into the oak hills, and the inns give out. Herders' dogs come down to the road to look at the mules, and the herders behind them do not come down. The pass is a day of switchbacks under the ridge forts, their walls open to the sky, and from the top on a clear morning both coasts are in sight, the strait behind and the western sea ahead. The descent goes down through oak woods to the terraces, and on the fourth day the road is lined with walled gardens, cisterns and the tenements of Besikar's outskirts.

The pass road has its own rules. Water is bought at the forts' old cisterns from whoever holds them that year. A caravan that pays a herder for passage is left alone; one that does not finds its strays gone in the morning. In bad years the road has been closed by washouts for a month at a time, and the shipping houses of Besikar raise their freight rates the same week.

## The Gate on the Pass

This season the caravan masters coming down into Pashinar have a new complaint. A gate of new timber, iron-banded, hangs across the pass road at its narrowest place, below the fort the hill tellings call the third fort, and the men who keep it ask a toll in coin of every mule train and say they hold the road for the hills. The toll is set a little above what the sea would cost, and some caravan masters have paid it while others have turned back to Pashinar and hired a hull. Every caravan master who comes down repeats the hill tale of the man who kept that pass for three paymasters at once ([[lore-roadpaidthrice|The Road Paid Three Times]]). One of them, short of patience and of silver, offers a party a share of his freight to find out who the gatekeepers are and who pays them.

## Current Situation {#present}

The long island's two coasts are pulling against each other. On the pass road a new gate hangs below the third fort, its keepers saying they hold the road for the hills and asking a toll a little above the sea freight, and some caravans have turned back to Pashinar to hire a hull. In [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]] the [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]] of Kudumel call in the factors' debts one at a time. In [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] [[being-trmnqzkn|Tirmana Qizikuni]] presses for an audit of the treasury, and the Grand Council's summer session will open in the customs-house on the quays. Off the north-eastern cape two ships have been lost this year on clear nights, steering for a lantern, and the [[affiliation-qazishuni|Qazishuni]] on the spine have neither built the pass gate nor broken it.

# The Truth of the Gate {#truth .secret}

**The gatekeepers are not hill men.** They are seven hired men from the Besikar waterfront, dressed in goat-hair cloaks bought in Pashinar, and they speak the coast's Taruzeli with the hill words put on like a coat. Their paymaster is a factor of a Besikar shipping house, who comes up the road from the western side once a month with their wages in a mule's pannier, and he takes the gate's takings back down with him. The toll is set above the sea freight on purpose, so that the caravans turn back and go round in the house's hulls, and what the gate takes from the caravans that do pay is profit on top.

**The herders of the pass know.** They have watched the gate go up, and they know the men at it are strangers, and they know the caravans blame the hills for the toll. They have not touched the gate, because a hill man who breaks a gate on the coast's road is a hill man the coast hangs. A party that asks them with courtesy, and pays for the water, is told when the paymaster comes and which path he takes down.

**What follows.** A factor caught with his house's seal on the gatekeepers' wages is a factor his house disowns at once, and the caravan masters of Pashinar, who keep long memories and talk at every well, are the people a party can sell the proof to. The house would rather buy it first, at a better price.
