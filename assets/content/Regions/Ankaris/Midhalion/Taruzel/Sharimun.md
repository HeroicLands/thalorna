## Overview

Every night of the sailing season a fire burns on the headland above Sharimun, and a ship working through the narrows in the dark steers by it. The town takes its name from that fire: _sharim-_, to burn through the night, with _-un_, where the act is done. Sharimun is a port town of about 30,000 on the south-western cape of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]], the largest island of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]], and it stands over the eastern mouth of the narrows, three miles across the water from the cliffs of [[place-khamisar|Khamisar]] and the [[place-straitfortress|Strait Fortress]] of [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]].

It is among the most populous towns of the region, and it lives by four things: the stone of its hill, the glass of its furnace-houses, the fights in its arena, and the lawsuits of the whole [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]], whose Arbitrators hold their court here. Its own [[affiliation-gldbrdshrmn|Guild Board of Sharimun]] governs the town and leaves the Confederation's court alone.

## Arriving

A ship coming down the western coast of Qalirel sees the beacon tower first, a squat round tower of pale stone on the bare headland, and then the hill behind the town, cut back in white terraces where the quarries have taken it. The harbor lies in the lee of the cape, a basin closed by a stone mole, and the quarry quay along its inner side is stacked with cut blocks waiting for hulls. The Harbormaster's men meet a ship at the mole, take her dues and send her master up to the customs-house on the harbor square, where he pays before anything comes ashore.

A traveler who comes overland arrives by the coast road from [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], two days for a carter, along the farmed plain of the north-western coast. The road is busy with farm wagons going one way and quarry carts going the other, and the carts are what a newcomer hears first: iron tires on stone and drivers shouting at oxen. Above the town the road passes between the quarry terraces and comes down into the harbor square by the only street wide enough for a loaded cart.

## The Beacon

The beacon tower stands at the end of the headland, an hour's climb above the harbor by a path cut in steps. On its roof is an iron basket the height of a man, and the _sharimum_ (beacon-keepers) light it at sunset and feed it with pine and pitch until dawn, every night from the first sailings of spring to the last of autumn. The fuel goes up the path on mules every afternoon. From the tower the keepers can see the Strait Fortress across the narrows, and they watch for its signal fire answering theirs.

The beacon is the town's office and the town's pride. The Keeper of the Beacon is a member of the Guild Board, and the board pays for the fuel out of the harbor dues. The present Keeper is [Belgina Sinnuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a pilot's daughter of fifty who has kept the fire for eleven seasons and climbs the path every evening herself, to look at the basket before it is lit.

## The Court of the Arbitrators

On the harbor square, opposite the customs-house, stands the [Khurutun]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a long hall of quarry stone with a deep porch where litigants wait out of the sun. The name means the place where disputes are weighed: _khurut-_, to weigh a dispute, with _-un_. Here the Arbitrators of the Council, officers of the Confederation's [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]], hear disputes between parties of different member cities, where the laws of one city and another disagree: a Besikar underwriter against a Kudumel caravan partner, a Yamuzel shipwright against a Bazunel buyer. Each Arbitrator sits alone, with a clerk and a scale on the table before him, and gives his judgment, his _khurutush_, at the end of the day's hearing.

The court sits in two long sessions a year, and in those months Sharimun fills with merchants, factors and their witnesses. They come with their papers and their bodyguards, because a suit worth a voyage to Sharimun is often worth a knife. [[being-rmkshtmdm|Rumukush Temadum]] is one of the men they hire to walk them from their lodgings to the porch. The chandler [[being-bshmytlmn|Bashimuya Telmuni]] supplies the candles for the evening hearings, which run late when a case is long.

Few merchants plead their own case before the Arbitrators. They hire a member of the [Litigants' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, whose hall stands at the top of the square, and the guild's litigants know the laws of all five cities and the habits of every Arbitrator. [[being-pshtnqzkn|Pushutan Qizikuni]], who carries the name of the great Besikar house, is one of the most senior of them, a pleader in commercial suits who takes young litigants into his chambers and is hard on them.

## Quarries, Glass and the Arena

The quarries cut a fine pale limestone from the hill behind the town, and the guild houses of Besikar build their fronts in it. The quarrymen work the terraces from first light, the blocks go down to the quay on sledges and to the coast road on carts, and [[being-zzrnqsdn|Zazuran Kusaduni]] is one of the carters who drive them north.

Below the quarries runs a street of furnace-houses, where the glassmakers work with sand brought by sea and the ash of the beacon's pine. Their furnaces glow all night, and their street is warm in winter. Every furnace-house works under a license from the [Glassworkers' Guild of Sharimun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, and the guild families of the Confederation buy their best work: [[being-qsdnyshdm|Kusadan Yushudum]] makes the glass birds, ships and figured lamps that stand in guild halls from Sharimun to Besikar.

The arena lies in the oldest quarry pit, under the terraces. Its walls are the cut rock, its seats are benches hewn into the rock face, and on a fight day the whole town that can afford a bench is in it. The fighters belong to the [Gladiators' Guild of Sharimun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, which owns the arena, trains free fighters and convicts together, registers every fight and takes its share of every purse. Convicts sentenced to the arena fight for their freedom. Free fighters fight for the purse and the crowd. The champion of the arena is [[being-nbrnfsnn|Niburan Fusanuni]], a former quarry laborer who came into it as a convict, fought five years for his freedom and now fights as a free man. The swordmaster [[being-khrtshklrm|Khurutush Kalurum]] keeps a fencing yard near the arena gate, where he teaches the sons of guild families and is careful never to teach them like gladiators.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The town is governed by its [[affiliation-gldbrdshrmn|Guild Board]], seven masters who sit for the town's guilds in the guild hall on the harbor square. The First of the Board holds the town's seal; this term he is [[being-qdrmbshrni|Qadurum Basharuni]], a quarry master whose house cuts the largest of the terraces. The Arbitrators answer to the Grand Council alone, and the board's magistrates hear none of their cases. The magistrates hear the town's own: debts, thefts, brawls on the quay and quarrels between guilds.

A shopkeeper robbed in the night goes first to the warden of his own guild, who knows its apprentices and its debtors. If the warden cannot help, the shopkeeper lays the theft before a magistrate of the board, and a magistrate's sentence is a fine, a flogging at the harbor post or, for a theft above a set sum, the arena. The town watch is quarrymen, paid by the board and armed with staves, who walk the square and the quay at night and do not go into the furnace street, where the glassmakers keep their own peace.

## The Present {#present}

Sharimun is full of litigants and short of patience. The Arbitrators' session fills the lodging-houses on the square, and merchants come with bodyguards as well as papers. A Kudumel merchant's suit against a Besikar house turns on a ship's master who has gone to sea, and the session closes in twenty days. A young furnace-hand has been sentenced to the arena by a magistrate of the [[affiliation-gldbrdshrmn|Guild Board]] for a theft his mother swears he did not commit, and the arena's champion, [[being-nbrnfsnn|Niburan Fusanuni]], wants to know who paid the man who accused him. Across the narrows the Strait Fortress prepares for the Fleet Review, and the Keeper of the Beacon, [Belgina Sinnuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is the member most often outvoted on the board that pays for her fire.

## Where a Party Lodges

The harbor inns take sailors and quarry buyers and are cheap outside the court's sessions. In a session the lodging-houses on the square fill with litigants' clients, charge by the week and expect payment in advance. A party that comes by the coast road can eat better than anywhere in the town at the roadside tavern an hour short of the gate, where the cook [[being-bssshtndn|Bissush Tanaduni]] feeds carters and travelers on the plain food of the country, and the carters stop whether they are hungry or not.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The witness.** A Kudumel merchant has a suit before the Arbitrators against a Besikar house, and the case turns on a ship's master who signed the charter and has since gone to sea. The session closes in twenty days. The merchant's litigant wants the master found and brought to the porch of the Khurutun to swear.
- **The accuser.** Niburan Fusanuni still knows the name of the man who accused him of the theft that sent him to the arena, and he wants a party to learn what that man was paid, and by whom.
- **The sentence.** A young furnace-hand has been sentenced to the arena for a theft from his master, and his mother swears he took nothing. The Gladiators' Guild collects him at the end of the month.

# The Truth of Sharimun {#truth .secret}

**What the arena pays for a convict.** The Gladiators' Guild pays a fee to the magistrate's clerk for every convict sentenced to the arena, entered in the clerk's book as a cost of the court. A convict costs the guild no wage and no share of a purse, and a convict who wins brings in more at the gate than any free fighter. So the magistrates sentence to the arena more often than the law asks, and a theft that would earn a flogging in Besikar earns the arena in Sharimun. The quarry masters on the board know, because most of the convicts come from their own terraces, and a laborer who argues about his wage is the one most often accused. The furnace-hand in the third hook was accused by a master who owed him three months' wages.

**What a place in the Arbitrators' book costs.** Cases before the Arbitrators are heard in the order they are entered in the court's book, and the clerks of the Khurutun sell places in it. A Besikar house's suit is heard in the session it is entered. A merchant without a litigant who knows the price waits two years or more. Every litigant of the guild knows the clerks' price, and they put it in their own fees and say nothing.
