## Overview

A ship bound for Besikar passes between fortified islets and two long breakwaters before the city opens in front of her, and inside them lies the [Grand Harbor]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, a deep natural bay that can shelter hundreds of hulls at once. It is the finest anchorage on the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]]. Besikar, a city of about 400,000 on the north-western coast of [[place-qalirel|Qalirel]], is the seat of the city-state of [[affiliation-besikar|Besikar]], the largest and wealthiest member of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]], and the city where the Confederation's [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]] meets.

The name is the harbor's: _besik-_, to shelter from the swell, with _-ar_, where the thing is kept. Everything else in Besikar follows from the water. The banks that lend against cargoes stand on the quay where the cargoes land, the Council that votes the Confederation's tariffs sits a street from the customs, and the dock workers who carry it all live in the old city behind the warehouses and count none of it.

## Arriving

The Harbormaster's launch meets every hull at the inner breakwater. Its clerk reads the ship's papers on deck, measures her draft against a painted pole, and gives her master a berth and a chit for the dues, which fall on tonnage and are paid at the customs before any cargo leaves the quay. The harbor has two arms. The northern arm holds the naval yards, where the Confederation's warships are built and refitted behind a wall, and no merchant berths there. The southern arm holds the fishing fleet, the coasters and the smaller traders. Between the two, along the deep water, run the merchant quays of the Guild Quarter, where the great hulls of the eastern trade lie three deep in the sailing season.

From the water a newcomer sees three things before any other. The first is the marble front of the [Guild Quarter]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a mile of warehouses and counting houses faced in white stone along the quays. The second is the old city climbing the hill behind it, brown and crowded, its roofs broken by the domes of the covered bazaars. The third is a black warship in a stone dock on the waterfront below the naval yards, her oar-ports empty and a Taruzelian flag at her stern: the [[place-tamzirmonument|Tamzir Monument]], a Vilérian ship taken at the [Battle of Tamzir Shoals]{.name data-type="lore/history"} ([[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]]).

A traveler who comes overland arrives by the pass road from [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]], four days for a mule train, and enters the city through its outskirts: walled gardens, cisterns, rope-walks and the newest tenements, built on the slopes where the old city has run out of room. The caravan inns at the head of the road are the first place in Besikar where a stranger is asked his business. The second is the customs.

## Politics {#present}

Besikar is waiting for the Grand Council's summer session, and its quarrels are lining up for it. [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]] keeps the Arch-Consul's residence beside the Hall and the most generous table it has seen, and every house that wants a measure read early dines at it or sends a clerk. [[being-trmnqzkn|Tirmana Qizikuni]] wants the audit of the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]]'s strongroom, under [[being-yshdyjkdn|Yushuduya Jakuduni]]'s keeping in the Vezakar, and talks among the houses of council seats that pass from head to heir. The Compact's calls on the old city's shopkeepers and fishermen come sooner and dearer this season, and the veterans' halls fill. [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]], elder of a waterfront hall, preaches patience there and carries the veterans' petition to the residence before every session. In the dock below the naval yards the veterans sit up with the [[place-tamzirmonument|Tamzir Monument]] at night, saying someone means to burn her, and the Compact's petition to break her up has been placed early on the Council of Elders' order for the first time.

## The Guild Quarter

The Guild Quarter is a canyon of marble, five streets deep, where fortunes are made and lost over a single shipment of eastern spice. Its warehouses stand on the quays with their counting houses above them, and behind them the palaces of the great houses climb in terraces to the street of the guild halls. The [[affiliation-qizikuni|Qizikuni]], the heaviest banking house of the city, keep their bronze doors open there from first light to the evening gun, and a captain who walks in is handed a cup of coffee before he is asked what he wants. The heir of the house, [Zarutan Qizikuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, runs the underwriting desk on the quay below.

Three buildings on the Quarter belong to the whole Confederation.

- **The Auric Compact's house** is the old palace of the Vilérian _therkos_ (governor), a long gray building with a colonnade on the harbor side and a garden behind high walls. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] finances the ships in the harbor from it, insures their cargoes and lends to the Confederation that meets down the street. Its doorkeepers carry staves, and a debtor called to the house goes in by the side door.
- **The [Vezakar]{.name data-type="place/site"}**, the seat of [[affiliation-vezakuni|the Council of the Guild-Wardens]], the Vezakuni, stands in the rebuilt hall of the old compact-house of Besikar's merchant princes. The Vezakuni charter the guilds there, examine apprentices and judge disputes between trades. Under its floor lies the strongroom of the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]], the Confederation's treasury and mint, and a detachment of Confederation marines stands at its stair day and night. Every evening the runners of the moneylender houses go in at the same door with the day's script chained to their wrists, and the treasury's clerks clear it before the lamps are lit.
- **The [[place-grandcnclhall|Grand Council Hall]]** is the old imperial customs-house at the head of the quays, where the twenty-five councilors of the Confederation sit twice a year. The Arch-Consul's residence stands beside it, and this year it is kept by [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]] of [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]], who sets the most generous table it has seen.

## The Khelathi Factorage

At the southern end of the Guild Quarter a walled compound runs down to a quay of its own, and the hulls tied up at it fly the colors of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]. This is the Khelathi factorage. Its warehouses, its counting house and the lodgings of its factors and clerks all stand inside one wall, with one gate onto the city, and the gate is shut at sunset and opened at first light by the factorage's own keepers. The head factor lives inside it. So do some forty Khelathi merchants and clerks in the sailing season, and fewer in winter.

A Khelathi hull that ties up at the factorage quay pays a lower harbor due than any other foreign ship in Besikar, and her cargo is landed before the Vilérian cargoes on the next quay. The Confederation does not explain the arrangement, and the Harbormaster's clerks do not discuss it. The dock workers who unload at the factorage are paid by the day at the gate and are not allowed past the warehouses.

## The Old City

Behind the Guild Quarter the old city climbs the hill in a maze of narrow streets, stepped lanes, covered bazaars and tenement blocks five floors high. The dock workers, sailors, porters and craftspeople of Besikar live here, most of them Residents of the city, who work under its protection without its freedom or a vote. The bazaars are roofed in brick vaults with light falling through holes in the domes, and each holds one trade: spice, cloth, copper, coffee, rope, old clothes, and in the lowest of them the fish that came in that morning. The smell of roasting coffee lies over the whole hill.

Every few streets there is a veterans' hall, a room on a ground floor with benches, a cistern and a broadsheet on the wall gone brown at the edges, showing a broken chain and a ship under a Taruzelian flag. The men who fought the war meet in them, and in the hard years since the peace the halls have become the places where the old city settles its own quarrels, collects for its funerals and hears what is said on the quays. A tax riot in the dock districts begins in the halls, and the city militia knows which ones.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The city-state is governed by its [Council of Elders]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, which sits in the council-house on the street of the guild halls under a First of the Council who holds the city's seal for his term. Its Chancellor keeps the charters, its Treasurer the customs and tolls, and its Harbormaster the port. The council serves largely at the pleasure of the three great guilds, whose houses stand around it.

The vote that seats the council belongs to the Roll, the book in the Chancellor's keeping that lists every property in the city carrying an elector's vote. The Council of Elders closed it by an act in the Confederation's second year, the [Closing of the Roll]{.name data-type="lore/law"}, and that act is the city's one written law of standing. A property entered in the Roll at the Closing carries its vote with it to whoever buys or inherits it. A property not entered at the Closing, whether built, enclosed or first registered since, carries no vote whoever owns it. So a house on the Roll sells for far more than its walls are worth, and no veteran of the war owns one.

Order on the quays belongs to the Harbormaster, whose men carry staves, keep the berths, search what they are told to search and turn smugglers over to the customs. The Guild Quarter keeps itself: each house has its doorkeepers, and the Compact's enforcers collect its debts in their own way. In the old city a shopkeeper robbed in the night goes first to the warden of his bazaar, a man paid by the stallholders to keep the vault, who knows every thief who works under it. If the warden cannot help, the shopkeeper needs the city's court, and a Resident cannot plead there in his own name: he finds a freeman to stand for him as his _rumukum_ (surety), and the freeman takes a share of anything recovered. The city militia, drilled by property-holders' sons, is called out only for a riot or a fire.

## Where a Party Lodges

The sailors' inns of the southern arm are cheap, loud and honest about it, and their hosts change foreign coin at a poor rate. The caravan inns at the head of the pass road are cleaner and quieter, and their stables are good. A party with a letter to a house of the Guild Quarter lodges in that house's guest rooms above the counting house, eats at its table and finds its own business known on the quay by morning.

## History {#history}

Under the empire Besikar was the seat of the province's rule and the counting-house of its eastern trade. The _therkos_ kept his palace on the quay, the imperial collectors read the tariffs at the lectern of the customs-house at the head of the merchant quays, and the city's merchant princes met in their compact-house behind. When the merchant princes paid for the war of 707, Besikar was besieged by the legions, and the city was freed only when the island garrisons fell after the victory at the Tamzir Shoals in 708 ([[lore-taruzelwar|The Taruzelian War of Independence]]). One of the warships taken there was towed in and docked below the naval yards as the Monument.

Independence put new masters in the old buildings. The Auric Compact took the _therkos_'s palace, the Grand Council took the customs-house and cut the empire's device from its pediment, and the Vezakuni rebuilt the old compact-house as the Vezakar, with the Merazuni's strongroom under its floor. In the Confederation's second year the Council of Elders closed the Roll, so that only the properties entered then carry a vote. In the hard years since the peace the veterans' halls of the old city have become the places where it settles its own quarrels, and the tax riots of the dock districts have started in them.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A vote for sale.** A tenement block in the old city, crowded to the roof with dock workers' families, is entered in the Roll, and its owner has died without an heir. Three buyers want it, none of them for the rents, and the Chancellor's clerk who will record the sale wants a party to learn who stands behind each one.
- **The night watch.** Veterans have begun sitting up in the [[place-tamzirmonument|Tamzir Monument]]'s dock at night with lanterns and boat-hooks, saying someone means to burn her. The Harbormaster wants them gone before the sailing season brings the Vilérian hulls in.
- **The clerk at the gate.** A young Khelathi clerk of the factorage slips out of the gate before it closes and asks a party, in careful Taruzeli, to carry a sealed letter to a ship lying in the roads, and to give it to nobody else.
- **The runner.** One of the moneylender houses' evening runners has not reached the Vezakar, and his satchel was chained to his wrist.

# The Truth of Besikar {#truth .secret}

**Who holds the old city's votes.** Fourteen tenement blocks of the old city stand on the Roll, entered at the Closing because their owners were enrolled houses then, and the Roll gives each block one vote. The Chancellor's book shows fourteen different owners. Eleven of them are nominees of the Qizikuni: a ship's chandler, two widows, a notary and the rest, each paid a small yearly sum to sign what he is given. The eleven votes go where the house says, and the families who live in those blocks have never been told that the buildings they sleep in vote. The tenement in the first hook is the twelfth block to come up for sale, and one of its three buyers is a Qizikuni nominee.

**What the factorage holds.** The head factor's strongbox holds the other end of the Confederation's debt to Au'Khelathu: the receipts for the gold, the weapons and the naval stores of the war, each signed by an envoy of the merchant princes, and beside them the notes on which several houses of Besikar borrowed from Khelathi lenders in the first months of the rising, the [[affiliation-qizikuni|Qizikuni]]'s among them. The head factor has never presented one. Every lower harbor due and every Khelathi cargo landed first is interest on them, and every house that owes knows it. The clerk in the third hook has copied a page of the factor's ledger listing the notes by house, and his letter is the copy, going to a Khelathi captain who has promised to carry him home.

**Who wants the Monument burned.** The Auric Compact's factors have said in the council-house that the Monument is bad for trade, and the veterans believe the Compact means to burn her. The truth of it is in the [[place-tamzirmonument|Tamzir Monument]]'s own note.
