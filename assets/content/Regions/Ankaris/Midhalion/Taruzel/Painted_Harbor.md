## Overview

The Painted Harbor is the waterfront of [[place-bazunel2|Bazunel]], a curve of quays and houses round the head of the city's bay on the southern shore of [[place-tashilar|Tashilar]]. It runs from the fish market on the eastern quay to the foot of the [[place-designyards|Design Yards]] on the western arm, about a mile of stone quay, and most of what Bazunel is known for stands on it or within sight of it: its merchant houses, its theaters and its taverns.

## What Is There

**The houses.** Each merchant house of the harbor keeps its warehouse on the ground floor, its counting rooms above and its family above that, under a flat roof with a cistern. Each paints its whole front one color, from the waterline to the parapet: ochre, rust, deep blue, sea-green, red. The doors and shutters are painted a second color, and the house's mark is painted large over the warehouse door. A house repaints every spring before the sailing season, and the smell of fresh paint is the harbor's first sign that the year's trade has begun.

**The theaters.** Between the houses, at four places along the curve, the slope above the quay has been cut into half-rings of stone steps facing the water, and a wooden stage stands at the bottom of each on the quay itself, so that the bay and the ships in it are the backdrop to every play. The largest holds some three thousand people. In the season a company's patron has his name painted on a board over its stage, and the board is repainted when the patron changes. Behind each theater a tall house holds the company's lofts, where scenery is painted and stored and players lodge.

**The taverns.** The harbor taverns open onto the quay, and in the evenings they set out card tables under awnings. A good part of Bazunel's money changes hands across them, and so do a good many of its drawings, charts and debts.

## The Harbor by Day and Night

By day the harbor works. Porters carry bales between the ships and the warehouse doors, the council's harbor clerks walk the quays with their tablets, and the water-sellers go from house to house with their jars. In the early afternoon the quay goes quiet for an hour while the heat lies on the stone.

On a theater night the whole city comes down to the water. The steps fill from the top before sunset, the play begins as the light goes, and lamps are hung along the stage front as it darkens. The council's watch stands at the foot of every theater, because a crowd of three thousand on stone steps above a quay is a danger to itself. After the play the crowd goes to the taverns, and the card tables run until the small hours.

## What Is Said of It

The harbor's porters say each house paints its own color so that its masters can find its quay from the harbor mouth at dusk. The old families of the upper town, whose houses are white, say the color came with the merchants and the white was there before it. The players say the city painted itself to be a stage. A house that changes its color has changed its owner, and the harbor's word for a bankrupt house is one that has gone white.

# The Truth of the Lofts {#truth .secret}

**One company's patron does not exist.** The board over the smallest of the four stages names a merchant house of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] that has a counting room on the harbor and no ships, no warehouse in Besikar and no family. It pays its company's season in full every spring, in good coin, and the company spends most of it on scenery from one scene-painter, at prices no honest company would pay. The scene-painter's receipts are clean, the company's books balance, and the money that comes out of the scene-painter's loft at the end of the season is money with a theater's season behind it. The house belongs to [[affiliation-crimsnldgr|The Crimson Ledger]], and so does the loft.

**What the loft makes.** The loft's upper floor holds a dozen copyists, most of them newcomers from the quarter behind the eastern quays, at long tables under the skylights. They copy paintings for collectors who will never see the originals, cut the seals of houses that have never heard of them, and, on the best paper the Ledger can buy, copy the master script of the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]]. The paintings and the seals leave by sea. The script stays in the Confederation, because it passes best where the Merazuni's clerks are busiest, at the evening clearing.

**Who knows.** The scene-painter knows. Two of the copyists know what the script is and would talk for passage out of Bazunel. The company's manager suspects nothing worse than a patron who overpays, and the company's players are the best in the city.
