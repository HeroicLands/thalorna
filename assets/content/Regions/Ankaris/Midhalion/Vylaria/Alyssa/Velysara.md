## Overview

A day's ride from Velisara there is a tract of good farmland that nobody farms. It is fenced, it is kept clear at [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s charge, and it is where [[lore-recensio|the Therona of Alessa]] ended in 75 BF ([[place-recensiogrnd|The Fenced Ground]]). A scholar of the city's minor chapterhouse of the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]], taking a new student out on the road to see the fence, says little about it and a great deal about the city they ride home to: "Velisara is the place that remembers. Look at the forums when we get back. White columns, paved squares, the whole golden age laid out for you. Now look at the joins in the marble."

The city is one of the secondary cities of the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]], with about twenty thousand people. Its white columned buildings and paved forums echo the architecture of the empire's golden age, and the marble facades, though still impressive, show weathering and repair rather than renewal.

## Life in the City

Velisara's economy rests on agricultural trade. The surrounding country produces wheat, wine and olive oil, which flow through the city's warehouses toward the imperial capital and the sea routes beyond.

The forums are still the centers of civic life, but the crowds that once thronged them have thinned, and the voices of merchants and civic orators carry a note of nostalgia mixed with anxiety about an uncertain future. The local aristocracy keeps the old patrician traditions with religious fervor, as though ritual propriety alone could arrest the empire's slow fade.

The city still draws scholars and seekers of knowledge to the chapterhouse, where the teachings of classical philosophy are preserved and handed on to the next generation.

## Dignified Endurance

The citizens of Velisara acknowledge, with the resignation of generations, that Vylaria is no longer the mighty realm it was, and they cling to the institutions, laws and traditions their ancestors built. Here the echoes of imperial greatness can still be met. The cost of keeping up the echo, instead of building new glory, weighs ever more heavily on the people who pay for it.

## The Old Quarter and the Chapter

Two institutions give Velisara a reach its forums no longer do. The first is the chapter of the Thordimalon, which stands in its own walled precinct beside the scholars' chapterhouse and is famous across western [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] for its [[affiliation-pirethos|Pirethos]] tradition: some of the most accomplished fire-mages of the Order trained here, and the chapter keeps large training grounds, a respected library and close ties with the regional office of the [Sekran]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the Order's hunters, which shares its precinct. Many of the children the Order enrolls in the province are sent to the chapter at ten or eleven, and the city is used to the sight of them walking to the training grounds in pairs behind a master. Among the chapter's mages is [[being-klstrtsxrchds|Kalestratos Zarkedis]], the heir of a fallen house of [[place-skoraz|Soraks]], whose mentor [[being-melvaristhar|Melvaris Tharkis]] sits on its council.

The second is in the old quarter, east of the forums, where the [Temple of the Black Flame]{.name data-type="affiliation"} occupies a compound of dark stone: the largest temple of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] in the Province of Alessa. It is not hidden and not ostentatious. Velisarans speak of it with the careful neutrality they keep for things the empire tolerates without approving, and they do not walk past its gate after dark without a reason. Its acolytes come from all over the province, and the city's council receives its priests at the state ceremonies, where they make the offering before the Orzas's image like everyone else.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Velisara is a chartered town under the Province of Alessa, with two _tavemoi_ elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ answerable in their own persons for the city's taxes. The council's seats belong by long habit to the city's old families, who keep the patrician forms at every meeting and pay for the upkeep of the forums out of their own purses, as their ancestors did when the city could afford them. The city watch keeps the streets. The Order's precinct is under the Order's own law, and a quarrel that touches one of its members goes to the chapter's council, not to the _tavemoi_; the Sekran's office answers to nobody in the city at all.

## Where a Party Lodges

The inns of Velisara are on the forum's south side, in buildings that were the guildhalls of trades the city no longer has, and their rooms are large, cold and cheap. Scholars visiting the chapterhouse lodge in the lanes behind it, where widows of the old families let rooms to them, and a party that comes to see the fence is directed to the same lanes. The innkeepers of the forum add a warning for strangers who ask the way to the Fenced Ground: the Order's patrol turns travelers back once and does not ask twice.

- **The column.** A builder's crew from Orzara arrives at night with a wagon and a crane at the north end of the [Old Forum]{.name data-type="place/site"}, and the watch has been told to let them work.

# The Truth of Velisara {#truth .secret}

The joins in the marble are the city's secret, and the old families keep it at a cost. Velisara cannot pay both its taxes and the upkeep of its forums, and the council will not let the forums fall. For a generation it has instead been selling them, a piece at a time. Builders from the capital buy columns, capitals and facing slabs from the less-walked ends of the forums to set in new houses in [[place-orzara|Orzara]], and the city's masons replace each one, the same week, with a copy of brick and stucco painted to match. From the middle of the Old Forum nothing has changed. At the north end, under the porticoes where few people walk, a quarter of the columns are brick, and the council's accounts record the sales as repairs.

The scholar who tells his student to look at the joins knows, and the remark is as far as he will go. The builder's crew arriving at night is the largest sale yet: the six columns of the old council hall's porch, the building the council itself meets in.
