## Overview

A day's ride from Velysâra there is a tract of good farmland that nobody farms. It is fenced, it is kept clear at the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo]]'s charge, and it is where the [[lore-recensio|Recensio of Alyssa]] ended in 75 BF. A scholar of the city's minor chapterhouse of the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistemium]], taking a new student out on the road to see the fence, says little about it and a great deal about the city they ride home to: "Velysâra is the place that remembers. Look at the forums when we get back. White columns, paved squares, the whole golden age laid out for you. Now look at the joins in the marble."

The city is one of the secondary cities of the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alyssa]], with about twenty thousand people. Its white columned buildings and paved forums echo the architecture of the empire's golden age, and the marble facades, though still impressive, show weathering and repair rather than renewal.

## Life in the City

Velysâra's economy rests on agricultural trade. The surrounding country produces wheat, wine and olive oil, which flow through the city's warehouses toward the imperial capital and the sea routes beyond.

The forums are still the centers of civic life, but the crowds that once thronged them have thinned, and the voices of merchants and civic orators carry a note of nostalgia mixed with anxiety about an uncertain future. The local aristocracy keeps the old patrician traditions with religious fervor, as though ritual propriety alone could arrest the empire's slow fade.

The city still draws scholars and seekers of knowledge to the chapterhouse, where the teachings of classical philosophy are preserved and handed on to the next generation.

## Dignified Endurance

The citizens of Velysâra acknowledge, with the resignation of generations, that Vylaria is no longer the mighty realm it was, and they cling to the institutions, laws and traditions their ancestors built. Here the echoes of imperial greatness can still be met. The cost of keeping up the echo, instead of building new glory, weighs ever more heavily on the people who pay for it.
