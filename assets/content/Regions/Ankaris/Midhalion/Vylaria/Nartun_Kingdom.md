## Overview

Nartun Kingdom is a country of forest owned by temples. It is the land of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]], about two and a half million people in the forested uplands of the north-east of the [[place-vylariargn|Vylaria Region]]: an independent Vylari-speaking realm that [[place-orzara|Orzara]] does not govern, ruled from the holy city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]]. An outsider knows it for its charcoal and carved wood, which go down into the empire by the wagonload, and for its priests, who come down the same road to preach.

A _kurthos_'s freedman secretary in Orzara, asked what [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) makes of the kingdom, gives the official answer and the practical one in the same breath: "The Senate calls it a wayward province that will come back. Nartun calls the Senate a corrupted institution that has strayed. Both say it politely, in the same language. Meanwhile every smith in Hilen burns Nartun's charcoal." Nartun's people speak an archaic dialect of [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]], its aristocracy holds its authority through religious office, and its king is anointed by the high priesthood. The kingdom keeps no Vylarian magistrate and takes no direction from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], which it left about 224 AF ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]).

## The Land

The kingdom lies north-east of the empire's provinces. To the south the wheat country of [[place-hylen|Hilen]] rises into wooded hills, and the border runs along a stream below the Hilen march-town of [[place-ashenmere|Ashenmere]], where the imperial road crosses into the kingdom by a bridge of three arches. To the west the mountains of [[place-moktur|Moktur]] stand above the treetops. To the north the forest thins, over many days' travel, into the forest-steppe of [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]].

Most of the kingdom is upland forest: oak and beech on the lower slopes, pine and fir on the ridges, and clearings in the valleys for rye, oats, flax, orchards and summer pasture. Winters are colder than in the provinces, and snow lies on the ridges from midwinter into spring. Summers are wet, and the forest roads are mud for a week after every storm. They are earth tracks and, across the wet bottoms, log causeways. The one paved road is the imperial road from Ashenmere to the holy city, which the kingdom keeps in repair because the pilgrims use it.

## Temple Land and the People on It

Most of the forest belongs to the temples. Each great temple of the holy city holds temple-estates in the uplands, tracts of forest with their villages, mills and charcoal grounds, and a priest called a prior governs each one for its temple. He keeps the estate court, collects the dues in coin, charcoal and days of labor, and decides which stands may be cut and by whom. A forester in the prior's service walks the stands and marks with an axe-blaze every tree that may be felled. Some temple forest is held by lay barons, families of no priestly office who hold it for rent and service and live in timber halls among their tenants. The crown holds forests of its own around the holy city and toward the Moktur mountains.

The people of the kingdom live in villages of a few hundred, and their villages are of two kinds. **The lumber settlements** stand at the forest's edge where the cutting is, such as [[place-kalderenna|Kalderenna]]: sawpits, timber yards, the turf-covered mounds of charcoal clamps smoking day and night, and the huts of fellers, sawyers, charcoal-burners and carters. They move when the cutting moves, and an old settlement leaves a clearing full of stumps and black earth behind it. **The woodcrafters' villages**, such as [[place-dremenna|Dremenna]], stand in the valleys and stay put. Their street is a row of workshops with the doors open, where carvers, turners, coopers and joiners work the timber the lumber settlements send down, and their best work goes to the temples as altar-panels and screens.

Every temple-estate keeps a school, where a priest teaches the village children to read the prayers posted at the shrine and to reckon a weight of silver. A Nartunese charcoal-burner can usually read, which a tenant in Hilen seldom can.

## The Withering

In stands all over the kingdom, trees are dying from the crown down. The leaves go grey and drop in midsummer, the bark loosens in sheets and shows a black stain beneath, and within two or three years the stand is dead wood on its feet. The woodsmen call it [the Withering]{.name data-type="lore"}.

The temples call it a judgment. The high priesthood teaches that the forest dies where its people neglect the rites, and when the Withering appears on an estate, the prior orders penance: a procession around the dying stand with lamps, a fast, and the village's dues to its temple raised for the year. The woodsmen keep their own tellings. Some blame the cutting crews of the big contracts, who take a whole valley at once; some blame the smoke of the charcoal clamps; some say a stand dies where a tree was felled without the prayer for its spirit.

The dead stands are cut for charcoal, because dead wood burns as well as any, and the price of charcoal at Ashenmere has fallen for it. The old charcoal-burners who cut only live, chosen timber are losing their customers to young crews who cut the dead stands cheaply.

## What the Kingdom Lives By

The forest feeds the kingdom by way of the empire. Charcoal is the greatest trade, and every smithy and furnace in Hilen buys it. Timber, pitch, potash, honey and wax, and the woodcrafters' carved and turned work go down the imperial road to Ashenmere, and grain, wine, oil, salt and cloth come up, because the uplands grow little wheat. Every price in the kingdom is a weight of silver, and every load is weighed at a temple scale. The coin is the empire's, taken by weight; paper notes are not taken at all.

## Current Situation {#present}

The forest is dying, and the kingdom quarrels over why. The temples order penance where the Withering appears and raise the village's dues for the year. The lay barons, [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]] loudest, blame the priors who sell whole valleys to the contract crews, and the priors, [[being-mlknssrvths|Melkanas Sarvothis]] first among them, go on selling. The [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] calls the Withering a sign against the Order, and the woodsmen fear something older. Dead stands burn as well as live ones, so the price of charcoal at Ashenmere has fallen, and the old burners who cut only chosen timber are losing their buyers to young crews. In the holy city the high priesthood moves to judge the king ([[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]]).

## Settlements

The holy city of Nartun is the kingdom's one city. Its other people live in temple villages, lumber settlements and woodcrafters' villages of a few hundred each, in the halls and hamlets of the barons, and in market villages where the forest roads cross.

# The Truth of the Withering {#truth .secret}

The Withering is a fungus. It travels on felled timber with the bark left on, on the saws and axes of cutting crews and on the wheels of timber wagons, and it enters a living tree through a fresh wound. It spreads fastest along the valleys let to the big cutting contracts, which the priors sell to raise the coin their temples ask of them, and it follows the contract crews from valley to valley. Old charcoal-burners who cut only chosen trees and peel them where they fall carry it least.

Several priors have seen that the Withering follows the contracts, and none has said so. The contracts pay the dues the high priesthood demands, and a judgment on the village is no fault of the prior's. The remedy is plain work: peel and burn the bark where a tree falls, burn the brash, and rest a valley the blight has reached. A party that proves it threatens the temples' income and their teaching at once. [[being-brnksksrns|Branakis Kaseranis]], a charcoaler who blames the forest's decline on overcutting by the big timber concerns, is closer to the truth than the temples, and the Withering is the proof he lacks.
