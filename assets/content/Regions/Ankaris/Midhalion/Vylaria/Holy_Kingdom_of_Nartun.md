## Overview

A king who reigns by a priestess's leave rules the Holy Kingdom of Nartun, an independent Vilari-speaking realm in the forested north-east of the [[place-vylariargn|Viléria Region]]. It shares its culture and language with the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] and keeps its own sovereignty under a theocratic monarchy. Viléria's power rests on legions and _kurthoi_ (senators). Nartun's rests on faith: the kingdom defines itself through its devotion to the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian pantheon]], holds the land of [[place-nartumkngdm|Nartun Kingdom]], about two and a half million people, and is ruled from the holy city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]].

The secretary of a Nartunese priest-noble, briefing a visiting Vilérian envoy before an audience, begins with the point the envoy is least likely to expect: "You come from a state that has [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) and faith. We have faith, and a kingdom that takes its character from it. The king will receive you. The Holy Mother made him king, and she can unmake him, so when you speak to him, speak as though she were listening."

## Character

Its people practice the Aureldian faiths with an intensity that makes even Vilérian priests uncomfortable, and they regard the kingdom as the true spiritual heir of Vilérian civilization, purified of the empire's corruption. They speak an archaic dialect of [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari]], and every rite in the kingdom is said in the kingdom's older ritual dialect, which keeps sounds the empire's speech has lost. Every dedication on a temple wall is cut in the runes of [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon]], and a priest who cannot read them cannot serve.

### The Holy Mother and the King

The kingdom has two heads, and the higher one wears no crown. The Holy Mother is the high priestess of Nartun, chosen for life by the high priests of the holy city from among themselves and always a woman, because the kingdom holds that a sovereign is born to the throne from a mother's hand. She sits in the [Mother's Hall]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at the top of the holy city. The king's hall stands on the terrace below it, and the whole kingdom reads that arrangement as a statement of rank.

A king is of the royal house by blood, but he is no king until the Holy Mother anoints him. She pours the oil on his forehead in the Mother's Hall before the assembled high priests, and the crown is set on his head only after it. She may refuse, and a refused heir waits, sometimes for years, while the high priesthood decides whether a younger kinsman would serve better. The anointed king is expected to embody [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] (justice) and [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]] (wisdom), and the high priesthood can sit in judgment on one who does not. If a majority of the high priests finds against him, the Holy Mother wipes the oil from his forehead with ash (_elza_) in the same hall, and the next of the royal house is anointed in his place. The kingdom has deposed kings this way, and every king knows the names of those it deposed.

### Priests, Priors and Barons

A priestly aristocracy governs everything below the throne. The priest-noble families raise their children for the temples, and only they may hold the high offices: a high priesthood, the Holy Mother's chair, the priorates of the temple-estates, the king's seal and the [House of Scales]{.name data-type="place/structure"}. The same families fill the offices of temple and crown, so a priest-noble may keep the king's seal in the morning and sit in his temple's chapter in the afternoon. Advancement in Nartunese society asks for both lineage and demonstrated piety.

Most of the land belongs to the temples. Each great temple of the holy city holds temple-estates in the uplands, governed for it by a priest called a prior, who keeps the estate court, collects its dues in coin, charcoal and labor, and decides which stands of forest may be cut. Some temple forest is held by lay barons, families who hold land of a temple or of the crown for rent and service, keep a hall and a manor court, and send their sons into the host and the Order. A baron holds no priestly office and never will, and he takes his orders on the forest from a prior.

### The Host and the Order

Nartun is not expansionist, and it is not weak. Its army is small and professional, and its knights, the [[affiliation-sacredflame|Order of the Sacred Flame]], are among the finest heavy cavalry in the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. The knights hold the walls and gates of the holy city and keep chapels at the kingdom's borders. [The Holy Militia of Nartun]{.name data-type="affiliation"} stands behind them: the freemen and yeoman archers of the temple-estates, whom the priors muster when the kingdom calls.

The kingdom's chief strength is moral authority. Its pronouncements on matters of faith carry weight across the Aureldian world, and rulers and churchmen throughout the [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión Region]] seek out its scholars and theologians.

## The Flame and the Forge

Nartun's knights keep a word the empire's temples have dropped. The rites of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], the Forge-Lord, said in Nartun's ritual dialect, call his fire _karvila_, the Flame, and the Order takes its name from that word. In the empire the god's orthodox faith calls itself [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], and the word Flame belongs to its enemy, [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]], whose open temples in [[place-orzara|Orzara]] and the commercial cities of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]] say it in every rite.

So the quarrel runs three ways over one word. The Black Flame's priests call Nartun's knights the other Flame and say the Order kept the true name of the god's fire without the courage to use it. The Sacred Forge's priests in the empire share the Order's faith and its hatred of the Black Flame, and wince at its name. The Order answers both that the word is older than either temple, and refuses to change it.

## Relations

Nartun keeps correct but cool relations with the Vilérian Empire. The break dates from about 224 AF, when the high priesthood refused obedience to an Orzas who would not proscribe the Black Flame and anointed a king of its own ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]). The empire regards Nartun as a wayward province that should be reabsorbed, and Nartun regards the empire as a corrupted institution that has strayed from the true path. The border with the empire's [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]] is a stream below the march-town of [[place-ashenmere|Ashenmere]], where a chapel of the Order faces a forge-shrine of the Sacred Forge across the water.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] has no presence in Nartun. The kingdom holds the Arcane Order's secular power and political manipulation to be antithetical to proper spiritual life, and mages who work in Nartun answer to the priesthood, not to the Arcane Order. A mage found working in the kingdom without a temple's license goes before an examiner.

## Current Situation {#present}

The high priesthood is moving against the king. [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]], eleven years crowned, owes his throne to the Holy Mother's refusal of his cousin, and the lower city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]] says the high priests mean to sit in judgment on him before winter. [[being-vrthlszrkms|Varthelas Zerkemis]], High Priest of Sélmoros, teaches that the Withering falls where the rites are neglected, lays it at the king's door, and is gathering the high priests' votes. [[being-ilvnssrkls|Ilvenas Sarkelis]], the Holy Mother, anointed the king and is the one hand that can wipe the oil away. The king knows enough to be afraid.

The forest is the kingdom's other quarrel. The Withering kills stands across the uplands, and nobody agrees why. The temples call it judgment, the lay barons blame the priors who sell the cutting of whole valleys to contract crews, the [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] calls it a sign against the Order, and the woodsmen fear something older. [[being-mlknssrvths|Melkanas Sarvothis]], prior of the largest temple-estate of Sélmoros, sells the cutting, and the baron [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]] says in the king's hall that it is killing the forest.

## Commerce and Currency

Nartun has no mint of its own and uses the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian currency]] (Solden / Ardin / Ogdel), but it takes every coin by weight. A price in Nartun is stated in weight of silver, by the pound of the holy city (_solkimon_), and a merchant pays it in whatever Ardin and Ogdel make up the weight on a temple scale. Each temple-estate keeps a scale and a sworn weigher (_thradinos_), and the House of Scales in the holy city keeps the standard weights that every other scale in the kingdom is tried against.

The kingdom takes no paper. The notes of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] moneylenders are worthless north of the border stream, and a merchant who comes up from Hilen with a purse of notes changes them into coin at Ashenmere before he crosses. The kingdom's money is kept by its temples instead. Each temple's treasury holds the coin of its estates and the deposits of its faithful, lends to barons and merchants at interest the temples call an offering, and pays the priors' drafts on one another in coin carried under the Order's escort.

The kingdom lives on its forest. Charcoal, timber, pitch, potash and carved and turned woodwork go down to Hilen, and grain, wine, oil and cloth come up. [The Withering]{.name data-type="lore"}, a blight that kills stands of forest from the crown down, is changing that trade, and the temples call it a judgment.

## History {#history}

The kingdom was made by one refusal. Before it, [[place-nartum|Nartun]] was the oldest sacred city of the Empire's heartland and its priesthood was the Empire's. About 220 the faith of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]] split into the Sacred Forge and the Black Flame ([[lore-ustaranschism|The Ústaran Schism]]), and the orthodox clergy asked the Orzas Karomfos to proscribe the Black Flame. He refused, because it had temples in his capital and officers in his legions.

About 224 the high priesthood of Nartun withdrew from imperial obedience ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]). It anointed a king of its own, [Daronos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the first to take the throne from the priesthood's hand, and raised the Order of the Sacred Flame to hold the city's walls and the land around it. The legions sent to bring the city back reached it in the autumn, found its gates shut and its knights on the walls, and turned back at the first winter. No Orzas has sent a second army.

The kingdom keeps the shape the break gave it. Its people speak an older Vilari as a mark of the break, the Arcane Order has had no place in it, and its knights keep the old word for the god's fire that the Black Flame also uses. Its kings are of the house descended from Daronos, and the high priesthood has deposed some of them, so every king knows the names of the deposed. When the last king died without a son, the high priests divided between his two cousins, and the Holy Mother refused the elder for a winter and then anointed the younger, Kelvaras Daronis, who has reigned eleven years since.
