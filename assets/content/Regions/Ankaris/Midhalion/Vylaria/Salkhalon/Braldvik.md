## Overview

Braldvík is the largest of [[affiliation-provincvld|Sâlkhâlon]]'s four principal fishing towns, about nine thousand people on a deep bay whose boats work the rich offshore grounds. It is a town of net-lofts, smokehouses and the boatwrights who keep the fishing fleet afloat. Their craft are humbler than the warships of [[place-castamar|Castamar]], but more numerous. It is also the only place on the Vylarian coast that carries a Nordmal name, and every child in it knows why, because once a year the town relights its smokehouses from one fire and tells the story while it does.

The catch is smoked rather than salted, and a smokehouse hand tells a new boy why that matters: "Salt-fish is food. Smoke-fish is something you can sell to a man who has a choice." Braldvík smoke-fish is a recognized article of trade in the markets of [[place-mercavia|Mercavia]] and beyond, and a buyer knows a barrel of it by the brand burned into its head: a line of northern runes that nobody in the town can read.

## The Winter of the Banked Fire

Nine days before the autumn equinox of 523 AF, 237 HF by the [[lore-domseycount|Dómsey count]], a Provenzian merchant galley came into the bay with a crew of forty Malagna men at her oars. Their captain, **Hvirnmýl**, had sailed south with the [[lore-southrnfleets|Southern Fleets]] and taken the galley and her pilot in the Gulf of Batáren, and then he did not go home: the pilot had told him of a warm sea beyond the western strait with the empire's heartland on its shore. The crew took the galley down the ocean coast, through the strait under her own imperial colors, and east along the northern shore for the rest of the summer. The fishing village on this bay was the first place they came ashore. They took its stores and its smokehouse, killed four men who stood against them on the shingle, and held it for nine days.

On the ninth night, the eve of 1 Karnavar and by the crew's own count the eve of [[lore-vetrnaetr|Vetrnaetr]], when the north honors its dead, a squadron came along the coast from Castamar. Its commander, **Hôlvêthôs Mârendâtês**, sent fire-pots into the galley at her moorings, and the wind off the sea carried the fire up the beach into the village's own boats, forty-one of them drawn up on the shingle, and into the net-lofts behind them. By morning the bay had burned from one end to the other. The raiders, with no ship and the whole western ocean between them and home, went up onto the north headland and dug in. The town calls it **Hvirnmýl's Head**.

The legate who came with the soldiers chose to starve them out rather than climb to them, and the village starved alongside. It had no boats, no stores, and no salt to keep the little the men caught from rafts and borrowed skiffs. On the headland the crew kept a fire banked in a pit-house every night, the only light on that stretch of coast all winter, and in the north a banked fire under a smoke-rack is how a winter's fish is kept. **Ilthêrâ Sâlkhênôs**, who had lost her husband on the shingle and his boat in the fire, climbed the headland in the first month of winter with a basket of fish and came down with it smoked. For the rest of the winter the village carried fish up and the crew sent it down cured, keeping a share, and the legate's pickets let the baskets through, because stopping them meant feeding the village from the legion's own stores.

In the spring of 524 the governor, **Zênôthâs Vârêlândis**, came from Mercavia with terms. The crew would row in the Castamar fleet for seven years and then be given passage home, and their captain would row with them. Nine had died in the winter. Of the thirty-one who walked down from the headland, twenty-eight went to the oars and three stayed in the village by the governor's leave. One of the three was **Dvilgvir**, who had kept the fire banked through the winter. He married Ilthêrâ the next year, and their children grew up in her house and carried its name. On the beach, before he went to the oars, Hvirnmýl gave Dvilgvir his iron helm.

## How the Name Stayed

The crew called the bay Braldvík, the inlet of the banked fire, for the light on the headland. The village took the name the same summer, and for a reason that had more to do with money than with the raiders. Its first cargo of smoke-fish went to Mercavia in barrels Dvilgvir had branded with the name in runes, and the Mercavia factors bought it as fish from the Northmen's fire and wrote the Northmen's word on their tallies. A village that had lost every boat it owned needed buyers more than it needed its old name. The census rolls at Mercavia carried that older name for two more generations, and nobody in the town uses it.

Braldvík is Vylarian in its speech, its faith, its law and its families. Three men out of forty stayed, and their grandchildren's grandchildren are as Vylarian as anyone in Mercavia. The town keeps one Nordmal word, which is its name, and one line of runes on its barrels.

## Coal Night

On the night before 1 Karnavar, the night the bay burned, the keeper of the **Old Coal** carries it through the town in Hvirnmýl's helm, the iron gone black with two centuries of embers, and every smokehouse in Braldvík lights its autumn fire from it. The coal lives banked in the Sâlkhênôs smokehouse, and the town holds that it has not gone out since Dvilgvir first banked it on the headland. A smokehouse lit from any other fire may smoke what it likes, but it may not burn the rune brand on its barrels, and the brand is what the Mercavia markets pay for.

The present keeper is **Sôrimâ Sâlkhênôs**, a smokehouse mistress of sixty, the last of her line to carry the name. She keeps a second coal banked in a cellar against the night someone tries to put the first one out, and she does not say which cellar.

## The Boat-Bill

The morning after Coal Night a Braldvík boat sails to Castamar, and its master hands the admiralty a bill: forty-one fishing boats with their nets and lofts, burned by the squadron of Hôlvêthôs Mârendâtês on the night before 1 Karnavar 523, with a year's interest added. The wording has not changed since 524. The sum has grown past anything the fleet could pay, and the admiralty files every copy and pays none. Braldvík crews sail for the merchant houses and for [[place-tholkezora|Thôlkezôra]]'s smugglers but seldom for the fleet, and a Castamar marine in a Braldvík tavern on Coal Night drinks alone. The town keeps its grudge for the navy that burned its boats to kill forty men. It keeps none for the men.

## Trouble in Braldvík

- **The coal goes out.** A Mercavia shipping house has bought two smokehouses in Thôlkezôra and wants the rune brand for its own barrels. The cleanest way to break the Sâlkhênôs hold on it is a coal that dies on Coal Night, and Sôrimâ's cellar coal is the second thing the house's men will look for.
- **The brand is read.** A sergeant of a signed [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Compact]] company in Castamar's pay reads a barrel-head in a Mercavia tavern and laughs, then stops. Under the town's name the runes say _Dvilgvir lives; tell his mother at Gnarthborg_. A hall in [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]] may still count the Sâlkhênôs as kin, and kin in the north arrive with claims.
- **The helm.** A man from Hvirnmýl's own hall in Malagna arrives with the hall's tally of the crew and asks for the captain's helm. The town carries its fire in it, and nobody in Braldvík has ever asked whether it was a gift or a pledge.
- **The bill is answered.** A new admiral at Castamar, short of oarsmen for the hunt for the Bethûan privateers, offers at last to settle the Boat-Bill: one Braldvík crew for the fleet for every boat the fleet owes. The town must choose between the money and the grudge, and either side may hire the party to carry its answer.
