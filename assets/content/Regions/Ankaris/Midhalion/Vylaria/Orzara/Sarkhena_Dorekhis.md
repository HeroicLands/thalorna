## Sarkena Dorekis—The House of the Twelve

At the heart of the [Vanor]{.name data-type="place/site"} of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] (see [[place-arvon#pelthra-sarkhena-the-temple-district|the Temple District]]), where the crowded forest of domes and pylons and timber halls draws back to leave a single sweep of open pavement, stands **Sarkena Dorekis**, the Temple of the Twelve—the greatest temple in the western world, and the supreme house of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], _the Twelve_, whose worship is the state religion of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Empire]]. It is the one sanctuary that honors no single god but the whole pantheon at once: twelve thrones beneath one roof, the divine order of the cosmos rendered in marble and made the official creed of Vylaria. To an Orzaran it is simply **the Temple**—the word needs no qualifier in the City, for there is only one that matters.

A sacristan of the House of the Twelve gives each new novice the same first instruction: "Walk to the center of the floor and look up before you do anything else. The disc of light on the stone moves from god to god through the day, and on a feast day it comes to rest on the god whose day it is. Start from there."

### The Dome

What every visitor remembers is the dome. It rises in a single unbroken span so wide that the eye refuses to grant it—a vault of coffered stone that, by every rule the masons of the world know, should have collapsed under its own weight the day the centering was struck. Visiting architects from the [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia Region]], from the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]], from the far eastern empires have stood beneath it and declared flatly that it cannot stand. They are not entirely wrong. The dome is the quiet work of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]], whose wards run unseen through the ribs of the stone, and whose mastery the Empire is pleased to display in a building where every subject of the western world will crane their neck and wonder. At the dome's crown a single round oculus is left open to the sky, so that across the day a disc of sunlight wheels slowly down the interior, touching each of the Twelve in turn—and on certain feast-days, by reckonings the priesthood guards closely, falling at the appointed hour upon the god whose festival it is.

### The Keepers

The Temple of the Twelve is not the property of any one of the Twelve. It is kept, by ancient arrangement, by the priests of the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]], who serve [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]]—the Keeper of the Gates, god of order, thresholds, and the frameworks within which all else is held. That the god of doors should hold the keys to the house of the whole pantheon is a logic no Vylarian needs explained: where the Twelve are gathered under one roof, in the keepers' understanding, it is Árdavon who governs the roof itself. The Árdavan custodians tend the common rites that honor the pantheon entire, marshal the great calendar of festivals so that twelve priesthoods may each process to the center in their season without treading on one another, and adjudicate the endless small precedences and jealousies of a building shared by a dozen proud clergies. It is a quiet, formidable power—and a partisan one, for in their teaching Árdavon is no neutral arbiter but a god with his own loves and hatreds among the Twelve.

### The Twelve Within

Within, twelve great niches ring the floor beneath the dome, one to each deity, each with its altar, its image, and its perpetual offerings: [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]] of the open book, bountiful [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]] the Thunderer, [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] the Silent Judge, and all the rest in their order. The individual priesthoods keep their own great temples elsewhere in the district; here, at the center, their gods stand together as a single pantheon, and the rite that matters most is the one that honors all Twelve at once.

The one delicate seam is the niche of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], the Forge-Lord—for Ústaron's worship is sundered, and the City must decide which of his warring faiths attends his flame in the house of unity. The Temple's answer is the orthodox one: the central fire belongs to [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], who hold the Forge-Lord's creative aspect, whose creed the other eleven priesthoods can abide and whose smiths and knight-orders are woven into the civic fabric of the capital. [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] has no altar here. It is eschewed by the rest of the pantheon and keeps its own magnificent temple apart in the district. The Black Flame's priests do not dispute the altar; they deny that what is tended on it is Ústaron (see [[lore-ustaranschism|the Ústaran Schism]]). That exclusion is the standing scandal of the Vanor, for the Black Flame's harsh creed sits closer to the Vylarian imperial temper than the Temple's gentler priests care to admit, and its patrons in [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) have never forgiven the house of the Twelve for shutting their god's truest face outside its doors. The Árdavan keepers, who count the Black Flame a cosmic enemy, intend to keep it that way. So the most sacred building in the Empire holds, at its very heart, a small cold war: a religious settlement the priests prefer and a political grievance the throne's own sympathies keep alive.
