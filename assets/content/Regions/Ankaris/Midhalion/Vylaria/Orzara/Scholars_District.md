## Overview

The Scholars' District is the one quarter of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] where nothing is for sale. It covers [Mernel]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Scholars' Hill, around the [Circle of Marendas]{.name data-type="place/site"} and the statue of [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]], goddess of wisdom, and it is the main campus of [[affiliation-panepistmm|The Academy]] and the greatest seat of that institution anywhere in the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]. Its colleges, lecture halls, great reading-library, gardens, gymnasia and walled residence halls belong to a student body drawn from the patrician and well-propertied houses. It is a scholarly quarter and not a working one: ordered, expensive and quietly proud, chartered to keep no trade or workshop within it. Among the colleges stands the chapter house of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]], whose domes are the ones a traveler sees glittering from the road.

A porter of one of the residence halls, who has locked its gate at dusk for twenty-two years, tells each autumn's new students what the district expects of them: "You may argue with anyone on this hill about anything, and you will. You may not buy, sell or brawl past the Circle, and you may not go through the bronze doors under the domes unless someone inside has asked for you by name. Be back inside my gate by the second hour of night. I lock it whether you are inside or not."

## Arriving

Mernel rises north of the central saddle, and the road up it from the [[place-platandstr|Platan District]] climbs in long ramps between retaining walls hung with ivy. At the top the ground opens into the Circle of Marendas, three hundred yards of paving with the goddess at its heart, seated, an open book on her knee and an owl on the back of her chair. The lecture halls stand round the Circle in a ring of colonnades, each college with its own portico and its own arms carved over the door, and between the porticoes the students sit on the steps and argue.

The great reading-library closes the Circle's north side, a hall of pale stone longer than a racecourse's straight, lit by high windows along both walls and roofed with gilded tile. Beyond the Circle the hill is walled gardens and residence halls, each hall a block of rooms round a courtyard with a single gate and a porter's lodge, and the gymnasia lie on the hill's eastern shoulder among plane trees. Nobody cooks on Mernel. The halls' kitchens are licensed, the district's bread comes up from the [[place-grandmarketorz|Grand Market]] every morning, and the lanes smell of ink, wet stone and the gardens.

The chapter house of the Thordimalon stands on the hill's highest ground, behind a wall twice a man's height, and its three gilded domes rise above the trees, lit at night from within by a cold blue glow. The sign above its bronze doors bears the seal of the Epistémion, because on paper the house is the College of Arcane Philosophy, one college among the others on the hill.

## The Day on Mernel

The district keeps the hours of its lectures. A bell rings from the reading-library at dawn, and the readers, _lorezoi_, begin their lectures in the porticoes at the first hour, each to a crowd of students on stools and steps who write on wax tablets and dispute every second sentence. The great disputations are held on the Circle itself in the afternoons, one master of the College of Logic against one of the College of Law, say, before a crowd of several hundred, and the students of each college cheer their own master and jeer the other.

Evenings belong to the residence halls. The porters lock the gates at the second hour of night, and a student locked out spends the night on the Circle or in the Watch-house. The chapter house keeps its own hours, and its gate opens at night more often than by day.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About twelve thousand people live on Mernel. The students are the sons, and a smaller number of daughters, of the senatorial and propertied houses, and they hold the right of residence as their fathers' dependents or as the noble-born. The readers and masters of the colleges live in houses on the hill's southern slope, and the keepers of the colleges' endowments, the _frenkoi_, see to the halls, the rents of the colleges' properties across the city and the payment of the porters, cooks, copyists and gardeners, who are citizens or registered servants of the colleges.

The district neither admits nor lodges the poor, and it keeps no trade school of its own: the practical trades learn their crafts in the guild-halls of [[affiliation-tevran|Great College of Guilds]]. The Thordimalon's _marendoi_ (masters) and their students live inside the chapter house wall, and the other colleges see them at the disputations and almost nowhere else.

## Who Keeps Order

The Epistémion's charter makes the district its own. The colleges' proctors keep order among the students, fine them, gate them and send them home to their fathers, and [[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] enters a college's gate only when the college asks it in. The Watch-house on the Circle of Marendas is a small one, and its watchmen spend their nights collecting students from the paving and returning them to their halls. The ward's _peltros_ (ward magistrate) has the easiest bench in the city, since nothing on Mernel may be sold.

The chapter house answers to nobody on the hill. Its gate is kept by its own people, the colleges' proctors do not go through it, and the Watch does not go near it.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Trade.** No stall, shop or workshop is licensed on Mernel. A student who sells his lecture notes on the Circle is fined by the proctors, and a peddler who climbs the hill is sent down it by the Watch, his goods seized and a fine levied by the ward's _peltros_.
- **A brawl between colleges.** The colleges' quarrels are fought in disputation. A brawl on the Circle costs every student caught in it a term's fees and a letter to his father, and a second brawl sends him home.
- **The bronze doors.** Nobody passes the chapter house gate unless a member of the Order has asked for him by name. A student found inside the wall without leave is handed back to his college and is not admitted to the College of Arcane Philosophy afterward. Anyone else found there is handed to the Watch and brought before the Criminal Tribunal as a housebreaker.
- **Teaching what the Order keeps.** The Thordimalon's code forbids teaching magic to anyone outside the Order, and its members enforce it on Mernel as everywhere. A reader of another college whose lectures stray into what the Order counts its own is warned once. A second time, the Sekran opens a hearing on him, and his college suspends him from teaching while it sits.

## What the District Fears

Mernel fears the Seldan's displeasure and the Order's. The [College of Political Philosophy]{.name data-type="affiliation"} examines the foundations of the laws that fund it, and the Seldan has watched it warily for as long as there has been a Seldan. A reader whose lectures are reported in the wrong house on the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] loses his chair and sometimes more. The district also fears fire in the reading-library, which holds the working copies of every book the Epistémion teaches from, and the porters keep buckets of sand at every door.

## Hooks

- **The locked-out student.** A student of a great house spent last night outside his hall's gate, and the Watch found him on the Circle at dawn with no memory of the evening. His father wants a party to find out where he was before the proctors do.
- **The disputation.** A master of the College of Law is to dispute tomorrow against a reader of the College of Political Philosophy, and someone has paid the College of Law's students to break up the disputation if it goes against their master. The reader's college hires a party to keep the Circle quiet.
- **The name at the gate.** A member of the Order sends a party a note asking for them by name at the chapter house gate, at night.

# The Truth of Mernel {#truth .secret}

A reader of the College of Political Philosophy is writing the case against the throne's succession. The Orzas names his successor and adopts him, and that has been the law of the throne for as long as there has been a throne. The treatise argues from the record of [[lore-restorehouse|The Restoration]] that House Helarkales came to the throne by the Seldan's acclamation of Makandas and by no Orzas's naming, that an Orzas who dies having adopted nobody leaves the choice to the Seldan by right, and that the Seldan's acclamation is what makes an Orzas lawful at all. It is the legal case for the claimant the Seldan would choose if the old Orzas dies without naming one.

The reader is paid by a lobby of _kurthoi_ (senators) on the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]], through the endowment of his college, and the _frenkos_ who keeps that endowment has seen the payments. The treatise is three books long, and its fair copy is kept in the reader's house on the southern slope, not in the reading-library. The Thordimalon's chapter house knows it exists, because one of the reader's copyists is the Order's. A claimant backed by the guard or the legions would burn it and its author, and a claimant backed by the Seldan would have it read from the platforms of the [[place-platandstr|Platan District]] the day the old Orzas dies.
