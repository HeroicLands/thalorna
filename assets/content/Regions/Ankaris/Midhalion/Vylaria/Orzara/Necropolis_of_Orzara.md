## Overview

The dead of [[place-orzara|Orzara]] lie beyond the thrima, never within it, and the Necropolis is where they go. It spreads across the open plain a short way beyond the city, a vast city of the dead that has grown for as long as Orzara has: mile upon mile of tombs, mausolea, grave-terraces and memorial avenues, ranked by wealth from the marble house-tombs of the senatorial families down to the pauper-trenches of the destitute. The tombs of the orzoi rise in monuments visible from the city's edge.

A sacristan of [[place-pnthnrldnm|Sarkena Dorekis]] (the House of the Twelve), who walks out with the funeral processions as part of his duty, tells the novices how to behave: "Speak low, and speak first to the clergy of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]]. The clergy of [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]], the Silent Judge, keep this ground jointly with the priests of [[affiliation-nahild|Nahild]], and you will be polite to both. Do not ask what Nahild's people keep and tend in the deeper vaults. Everyone in the capital has a rumor about it, and nobody repeats theirs above a whisper."

## The Two Priesthoods

Two priesthoods that would have little to say to one another anywhere else tend the Necropolis jointly: the Aureldian clergy of Sélmoros, the Silent Judge, and the Asguardian clergy of Nahild. Their shared custody of the dead is the root of the surprising power of Nahild's faith in the capital.

The Necropolis is a city in its own right, with its own watch, its own quiet economy of funerary trades and its own silent avenues. It lies well apart from the [Vilarpolthamis]{.name data-type="place/site"} on the city's opposite flank.

## The Roads of the Dead

Orzarans call the Necropolis the _zemrolara_, the city of the dead, and they reach it by the roads that leave the city's eastern gates. The tombs begin at the edge of the Clearance and line both sides of each road for the better part of a mile before the Necropolis proper: small house-shaped tombs of the propertied families, each with a bench outside its door where the family sits on the anniversaries, and an inscription facing the road so that every traveler reads the name. A family in mourning walks out in the gray _elztholbema_ (ash-gown), the eldest son first, and the hired mourners of the funeral trades walk behind with the pipes.

The roads open onto the great avenues, and the avenues are ranked. Nearest the city stand the marble house-tombs of the senatorial families, some as large as a townhouse and as carefully kept, with gardens, fountains and a freedman of the family living in a lodge by the gate. Beyond them run the long walls of stacked niches where the guilds' burial clubs lay their members' ashes, a niche for every member who kept up his dues. The monuments of the Orzoi stand on a rise of their own. At the far edge, where the plain begins to roll, are the pauper-trenches, dug wide and filled in turn, and marked by nothing.

## The Keepers of the Vaults

The clergy of Sélmoros receive the dead, judge the rites and speak the commendations over every grave. The clergy of Nahild keep the ground. They are the [Keepers of the Vaults]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (_zemrolthauroi_, tomb-guards), a priesthood of perhaps two hundred under a First Keeper, and they dig the trenches, wall the niches, keep the watch and hold the oldest part of the Necropolis, which is underground.

The city gives the Keepers its unclaimed dead: the paupers no burial club pays for, the drowned that nobody names, the bodies of the condemned that the courts will not release to their families. The Keepers take them without a fee, and the city has never asked them to account for one. It is the cheapest arrangement in the capital, and it is the root of the surprising power of Nahild's faith in a city that worships the Twelve.

## The Deeper Vaults

The oldest ground of the Necropolis lies under the first terraces, nearest the city. There the rock is cut into galleries that are older than any tomb above them, and the Keepers hold them. Their door is a low arch of bronze set in the face of the first terrace, its leaves worked with Nahild's sign, and a Keeper stands at it day and night. The unclaimed dead go in through it on biers at dusk, and nobody but a Keeper has passed it in living memory.

What lies beyond it is the city's favorite whisper. Orzarans say:

- that the vaults run under the Clearance and the walls and come up in the cellars of the [Warrens]{.name data-type="place/site"};
- that the Keepers feed their dead, and buy meat in the outskirts by the cartload to do it;
- that great houses send their shamed dead there by night, and that a family's name can be buried with them for a price;
- that on still nights the watchmen on the paupers' ground can hear digging under their feet.

## Trouble in the Necropolis

- **The released body.** A family whose son was hanged for a robbery he did not commit wants his body back from the Keepers to bury in the family tomb. The court released it to the Keepers a month ago, and the Keepers say they cannot return it.
- **The digging.** A watchman of the paupers' ground hires a party to sit out a night with him, because he has heard the digging three nights running and his captain has told him to stop hearing it.
- **The palace's question.** A clerk of the Orzas's household is seen twice in a month at the Keepers' door, after dark and without his badge of office. A senator's agent wants to know what he is carrying in and what he is carrying out.

# The Truth of the Vaults {#truth .secret}

The unclaimed dead do not all stay buried. The Keepers raise the strongest of them in the lowest vaults as [[lore-haugverdir|Hrúmverdir]], the mindless dead of their goddess, and set them to work in the dark. Several hundred stand down there now. They dig the new galleries, cut and carry the stone the Keepers wall the niches with, and bring each night's dead down from the door, and none of them has ever been above ground. The digging under the paupers' ground is real.

The senior priest of Sélmoros at the Necropolis knows, as each of his predecessors has known, and he says nothing, because the city's dead are buried cheaply and on time and the Sélmoros clergy would have to do the Keepers' work without them. [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]] has not been told.

The throne has. A clerk of the Orzas's household carries a question to the First Keeper every few weeks and carries her answer back: whether what stands in the vaults could be useful, as labor in a lean year or as a garrison in a bad one, if the legions are ever not enough. The First Keeper's answer has been the same each time, that it could not, and it is not true. She wants it never to be, because on the day one of the vaults' dead is seen in daylight the city will burn the Keepers' door and every priest of Nahild behind it, and because she does not know what her goddess would make of a city that had asked.
