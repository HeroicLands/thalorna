## Sarkena Dorekis—The House of the Twelve {#overview}

At the heart of the [Vanor]{.name data-type="place/site"} of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] (see [[place-arvon#pelthra-sarkhena-the-temple-district|the Temple District]]), where the crowded forest of domes and pylons and timber halls draws back to leave a single sweep of open pavement, stands **Sarkena Dorekis**, the Temple of the Twelve—the greatest temple in the western world, and the supreme house of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], _the Twelve_, whose worship is the state religion of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Empire]]. It is the one sanctuary that honors no single god but the whole pantheon at once: twelve thrones beneath one roof, the divine order of the cosmos rendered in marble and made the official creed of Viléria. To an Orzaran it is simply **the Temple**—the word needs no qualifier in the City, for there is only one that matters.

A sacristan of the House of the Twelve gives each new novice the same first instruction: "Walk to the center of the floor and look up before you do anything else. The disc of light on the stone moves from god to god through the day, and on a feast day it comes to rest on the god whose day it is. Start from there."

### The Dome

What every visitor remembers is the dome. It rises in a single unbroken span so wide that the eye refuses to grant it—a vault of coffered stone that, by every rule the masons of the world know, should have collapsed under its own weight the day the centering was struck. Visiting architects from the [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia Region]], from the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]], from the far eastern empires have stood beneath it and declared flatly that it cannot stand. They are not entirely wrong. The dome is the quiet work of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]], whose wards run unseen through the ribs of the stone, and whose mastery the Empire is pleased to display in the temple’s vast dome. At the dome's crown a single round oculus is left open to the sky, so that across the day a disc of sunlight wheels slowly down the interior, touching each of the Twelve in turn—and on certain feast-days, by reckonings the priesthood guards closely, falling at the appointed hour upon the god whose festival it is.

### The Twelve Within

Within, twelve great niches ring the floor beneath the dome, one to each deity, each with its altar, its image, and its perpetual offerings: [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]] of the open book, bountiful [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]] the Thunderer, [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] the Silent Judge, and all the rest in their order. The individual priesthoods keep their own great temples elsewhere in the district; here, at the center, their gods stand together as a single pantheon, and the rite that matters most is the one that honors all Twelve at once. The one seam among them is the Forge-Lord's niche.

## The Refused Niche {#present}

The Forge-Lord's niche is the one seam in the house of unity. [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]]'s worship is sundered ([[lore-ustaranschism|the Ústaran Schism]]), and the City must decide which of his warring faiths attends his flame. The Temple's answer is the orthodox one: the fire in his niche belongs to [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], who hold the Forge-Lord's creative aspect, whose creed the other eleven priesthoods can abide and whose smiths and knight-orders are woven into the civic fabric of the capital. [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] has no altar here. It is eschewed by the rest of the pantheon and keeps its own magnificent temple apart in the district.

The Black Flame's priests do not ask for the Forge's altar. They hold that the fire tended there is the Forge-Lord's lesser face, and that his truest face is shut outside the doors. At every accession they petition the new Orzas, who is chief priest of the state cult in his own person, for a place in the niche where their flame may burn beside the Forge's, and at every accession the Árdavan keepers answer for the Temple that the Twelve have twelve niches and one fire in each. No Orzas has overruled them. The refusal is the standing scandal of the [Vanor]{.name data-type="place/site"}, because the Black Flame's harsh creed sits closer to the Vilérian imperial temper than the Temple's gentler priests care to admit, and its patrons in [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) have never forgiven the house of the Twelve for shutting their god's truest face outside.

The petition is alive now because the next accession is near. The reigning Orzas is old and has adopted nobody.

- **The Black Flame.** The High Flame-Warden of Viléria, who oversees the faith's registered temples across the Empire, has the next petition drafted, and its patrons in the Seldan are counting which claimant will receive it. A claimant who promises the niche gains their votes and the goodwill of a faith that preaches openly in every commercial city of the Empire.
- **The keepers and the Forge.** The Árdavan keepers count the Black Flame a cosmic enemy and mean to keep it outside. The senior clergy of the Sacred Forge in Orzara already press the city's magistrates against the Black Flame's preaching, and both priesthoods will set themselves against any claimant who promises the niche.
- **Nartun.** The high priesthood of [[place-nartum|Nartun]] refused obedience to an Orzas who would not proscribe the Black Flame ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]). A niche granted under the dome would end any hope of bringing the holy kingdom back to the Empire, and the claimants who hope for that know it.
- **The Order.** [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Thordimalon]], whose wards hold up the dome, takes no side in the Black Flame's quarrel with the Temple, and says so whenever it is asked.

Open conflict between faiths is forbidden absolutely in the Vanor, so the quarrel is fought with votes, sermons and slanders whispered into senatorial ears. The most sacred building in the Empire holds, at its very heart, a small cold war: a religious settlement the priests prefer and a political grievance the throne's own sympathies keep alive.

## The Keepers {#who-rules-it}

The Temple of the Twelve is not the property of any one of the Twelve. It is kept, by ancient arrangement, by the priests of the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]], who serve [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]]—the Keeper of the Gates, god of order, thresholds, and the frameworks within which all else is held. In the keepers’ understanding, where the Twelve are gathered under one roof, it is Árdavon who governs the roof itself. The Árdavan custodians tend the common rites that honor the pantheon entire, marshal the great calendar of festivals so that twelve priesthoods may each process to the center in their season without treading on one another, and adjudicate the endless small precedences and jealousies of a building shared by a dozen proud clergies. It is a quiet, formidable power—and a partisan one, for in their teaching Árdavon is no neutral arbiter but a god with his own loves and hatreds among the Twelve.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The draft.** A clerk of the High Flame-Warden of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] wants a party to carry the petition's draft to a claimant's house on the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] without the Árdavan keepers' friends learning which house received it.
- **Ash in the brazier.** A priest of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] who tends the Forge-Lord's niche finds black ash in its brazier at the morning rite, and wants to know who passed the sacristans' night watch before he reports it to the keepers.
- **The keepers' candidate.** A patron of the Black Flame in [[affiliation-seldan|the Seldan]] hires a party to learn which claimant the Árdavan keepers have promised to stand behind.
