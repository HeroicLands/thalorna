## Overview

The Wharves feed [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] in the dark. The barges of the [[place-vylarisrvr|River Vylaris]] come in through the night with the grain, the oil and the wine, so that the bakers have flour at dawn, and by first light the quays below [Nerval]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the River Hill, are stacked with sacks and loud with porters. The district runs along the river from the river gate to the foot of the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]]: the quays and their water-stairs, the long state granaries under the imperial seal, the warehouses of the chandlers and the great houses, the fish-market, the halls of the watermen and the porters, and the walled [Strangers' Row]{.name data-type="place/site"}, where the city's registered foreign traders live. At its heart lies the [River Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"}, with a great aqueduct-fountain throwing water higher than the barges' masts.

A barge-master who has brought grain down the river for twenty-five years tells every passenger who comes ashore at the water-stairs the three things the Wharves will ask him: "The Watch will ask you who you are. The customs clerk will ask what you are carrying. And at dusk the Watch will ask you where you sleep, and if you are not a citizen and have no paper, the answer had better be outside the line."

## Arriving

Most people who come to Orzara by water come ashore here, and the Wharves are the first of the city a traveler sees from the deck. The quays are stone, wet and worn hollow, and along the river the city's boundary runs as the towpath and the open quayside, swept and kept clear of stalls the way the Clearance is kept clear inland. Barges tie up along its inner edge at the water-stairs. Each water-stair has its customs clerk at a table under an awning and a watchman leaning on his cudgel at the foot of it, and nothing comes up the stairs that has not been counted, sealed and entered on a wax tablet.

Above the quays the warehouses stand shoulder to shoulder, five stories of brick with timber hoists swinging from their gables. The state granaries are longer and lower, whitewashed halls on stone piers with the imperial eagle painted over their doors and a seal of wax and cord across every bolt. The fish-market fills a long open shed at the downstream end, and its smell reaches the River Circle on a still morning. The river gate stands at the upstream end, where the river road crosses the Clearance into the city, and the River Circle opens just inside it.

## The Day on the Wharves

The Wharves work through the night and rest at noon. The grain barges come in after dark, and through the small hours the porters carry the sacks from the barges to the granaries and the warehouses by lamplight, calling the count in a chant that carries up the hill. At dawn the fish boats land, the fish-market opens, and the handcarts start up the stairs and the river road toward the Grand Market. By noon the quays are quiet and the porters sleep in the shade of the warehouses, and in the afternoon the Wharves belong to the chandlers' clerks, the factors and the customs-men going over the morning's tablets.

At dusk the Watch clears the Wharves of strangers. A foreign sailor who came ashore at noon must be back aboard his ship, inside the Strangers' Row, or across the Clearance by the time the River Circle's watch-house sounds its horn, and the Watch walks the quays with lanterns to see that he is.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About forty thousand people live in the district. The watermen and the porters have their guild-halls on the River Circle and their homes in the lanes above the warehouses, and many of them are veteran-citizens, since a man with twenty years of carrying a legion's kit on his back carries a sack well. The granary-keepers live beside their granaries. The customs-men, the chandlers' clerks, the warehouse-keepers and the boat-builders who mend the barges live here too, and the fishermen's families crowd the downstream lanes beside the fish-market.

The Strangers' Row is the one quarter of the city where non-citizens live by right of a license. It is a walled lane of warehouses, lodgings and counting-rooms behind the fish-market, with one gate onto the quays, and the traders registered there are Taruzelian, Nordman and Khelathi. The Taruzelian factors are the most numerous and the least welcome. They traded here when [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] was a province, they stayed when it was not, and their houses' silks and spices go up to the [[place-grandmarketorz|Grand Market]] every morning. The Nordmen are mostly ship-masters and their crews. The Khelathi are few, careful and watched, because Khelathi gold paid for the war the Empire lost.

## Who Keeps Order

[[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] keeps a watch-house on the River Circle with a full watch-cohort, and its men stand at every water-stair. The customs-men of [[affiliation-tolvan|The Fiscal Court]] collect the duties on what comes ashore and seal what they have counted. The state granaries answer to the Veramus of the Grain, who sits for [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]] and whose granary-keepers break and remake the seals in front of a witness each time a granary is opened. The watermen's and porters' guilds keep order among their own men, and a porter who steals from a sack is tried by his guild before the Watch hears of it. The ward's _peltros_ (ward magistrate) licenses the fish-market and the cookshops and condemns the warehouses that lean too far.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Cargo without the seal.** Goods carried off the quays without the customs seal are seized by the customs-men of the Fiscal Court, and the carrier and the owner are fined their value. A broken granary seal is a charge of theft from the state before the Criminal Tribunal, and its sentence runs from forced labor to the arena.
- **A stranger ashore after the horn.** A foreign sailor or trader found outside the Strangers' Row after dusk spends the night in the watch-house and is put back aboard or across the Clearance in the morning, with a fine paid to the _peltros_ before he goes. A second offense loses a registered trader his license.
- **A registered trader's gate.** The Strangers' Row's gate is shut and barred from the outside by the Watch at dusk and opened at dawn, and a trader found climbing its wall is fined by the _peltros_ and struck off the register.
- **Fire on the quays.** No fire is lit on the quays or aboard a barge tied up at them. The porters carry closed lanterns, and an open flame near the granaries costs its owner a fine and his boat, forfeited on the _peltros_'s order.

## History {#history}

The quays below Nerval have landed the capital's bread for as long as the city has eaten from the river, and the state granaries under the imperial seal were raised along the River Circle to hold it. Their reserve has dwindled through the long decline, sold off to meet one charge after another, until the granaries hold about a week. Foreign traders are registered and lodged in the walled Strangers' Row. Since Taruzel won its independence twelve years ago, its factors on the Row have been the merchants of a power the empire fought and lost to.

## What the Wharves Fear {#present}

The Wharves fear a low river and a burned granary, and the quarrel on the quays is over where the next grain comes from. In a dry late summer the barges ride low and the night's grain comes in short, and behind the week in the granaries nothing stands. The Grain House of [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] sells the city Hilen's bread and wants a duty on foreign grain. [[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]], whose shipping house on the Wharves brings the only grain that can reach the capital faster than Hilen's, wants trade free and the Khelathi contract. On the Strangers' Row the Khelathi factor, [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]], offers the throne grain on credit and silver for its paper, and the hawks of the Seldan call it a chain. The Veramus of the Grain answers for the granaries, and [[affiliation-cleanshlds|The Clean Shields]] hold the grain road and the barge moorings above the river gate, paid on the day by a paymaster nobody names. The Taruzelian factors fear the day the Seldan decides that a Taruzelian in Orzara is an enemy and not a merchant, and every riot elsewhere brings the Watch to the Row first.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The sack count.** A porters' guild-master suspects that one of the night gangs is carrying one sack in forty to a warehouse that is not on any tablet. He wants a party to work a night on the quays and follow the sack.
- **The last ship.** A Nordman ship-master missed the horn and spent the night in the watch-house, and his ship sailed without him with his cargo aboard. He wants it back, and he wants to know who paid his crew to leave.
- **The spire.** A Taruzelian factor of the Strangers' Row wants to buy a measured drawing of the spire on the [Spire Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} in the Warrens, and will pay a party to make it at night.

# The Truth of the Wharves {#truth .secret}

Two of the state granaries on the River Circle are half empty. The granary-keeper of the two halls nearest the river gate sold their reserve grain three lean winters ago, a little at a time, through a broker in the [[place-grandmarketorz|Grand Market]], and replaced the bottom of each bin with sand under a crust of grain a man's arm deep. The seals on the doors are good seals. A seal-cutter in the [[place-gildedqrtr|Gilded Quarter]] cut him a copy of the granary's seal-stamp, and every time a granary is opened before a witness the keeper breaks the true seal and remakes it with the true stamp, so the witness sees nothing wrong.

The Veramus of the Grain counts the city's reserve by the granaries' tallies, and the tallies say the two halls are full. The keeper means to buy the grain back after a good harvest and has never had the coin to do it. The whole reserve is about a week of bread for the city, so two halls of sand are not a shortfall the city can absorb. When the city opens its reserve against a blockade or a failed harvest, the porters reach the sand in the first hour, the week the council counts on is a day or more shorter than it believes, and the throne has to buy the missing grain on borrowed money while the queues are already forming.
