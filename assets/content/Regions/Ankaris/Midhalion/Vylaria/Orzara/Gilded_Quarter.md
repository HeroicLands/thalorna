## Overview

The Gilded Quarter is the one ward of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] where craft and luxury still keep house together inside the city. It climbs the lower slopes of [[place-selvin|the Laurel]], toward the walled villas of the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] above it, and its streets hold the clean and costly trades the district law allows within the thrima: goldsmiths, jewelers, perfumers, fine tailors, apothecaries and instrument-makers, who make and sell in the same shop to the rich who live up the hill. The physicians, the scriveners, the mask-makers and the seal-cutters keep their rooms among them, and the city's better baths and inns stand along its lower streets. At its foot lies the [Circle of Waters]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the grandest of the city's fountain-displays.

A goldsmith's journeyman, sent down to the Circle to meet a customer's steward, explains the quarter to a new apprentice on the way: "Everything up this street is made for somebody on the hill, and everybody who makes it belongs to somebody on the hill. My master holds his bench by a waiver from a house on Selvin. If the house falls, so does the waiver, and so does my master, out through the gate with his tools in a sack. So we make what the house asks, and we make it first."

## Arriving

The Circle of Waters is the quarter's front door. Its basins step down the slope in three tiers of white marble, and on feast days the fountains are run for spectacle: jets that rise and fall in time, arches of water a cart could pass under, and a column at the center that throws its water higher than the roofs of the inns. On ordinary days the jets play low, the basins are full and still, and the quarter's water-carriers fill their jars at the lowest tier.

From the Circle the streets climb Selvin in long flights of steps between shopfronts. The shops are narrow and deep, with a counter open to the street by day, a workroom behind it and the household above, and each carries its trade's sign over the door: a gilded ring, a pestle, a pair of shears, a bronze lyre. The [Street of Masks]{.name data-type="place/feature"} climbs from the Circle's upper side, where the mask-makers model the wax faces that the noble houses keep in the cupboards by their doors, and the seal-cutters work at small windows beside them, cutting stamps for every house, guild and magistrate in the city. The streets smell of scent, hot metal, resin and lamp-oil, and they are quiet: the quarter's customers are carried up in chairs and do not shout.

The better inns of the city stand on the streets just above the Circle. The [Silver Lamp]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the best known of them, a house of three courtyards with its own bath, where provincial _kurthoi_ (senators) lodge when they come to the capital without a house of their own, and where a party with a patron's letter and the coin to match is lodged well and watched closely.

## The Day in the Quarter

The quarter opens late and closes late. The shops open at the second hour, when the stewards of the great houses come down from the Heights with their masters' orders, and the morning is the quarter's busiest time: a steward with a list, a goldsmith with a tray of rings, an argument about weight and price conducted in low voices across a counter. In the afternoon the masters of the houses come down themselves, in chairs, to the tailors, the perfumers and the physicians, and the Circle of Waters fills with bearers waiting for them.

The goldsmiths, the jewelers and the instrument-makers work through the evening by lamplight at their licensed hearths, each a small brick furnace the size of a bread oven with a brass tag from the _peltros_ (ward magistrate) nailed beside it and a water-butt at the door.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About thirty-five thousand people live in the Gilded Quarter, and nearly all of them live above or behind the place they work. Few of the masters are citizens. A craftsman who is not a citizen holds his right to sleep inside the city by a waiver tied to his employment, and the only employers who can sign for a goldsmith are the great houses he works for. So on paper every master in the quarter is a servant of a house on the Heights, his waiver signed by its steward and renewed each year. The house takes first choice of his work and the right to name his price, and the master takes the house's protection, its custom and its friends.

The physicians are the exception: most of them are citizens, and the best of them are rich. The scriveners write letters, contracts and petitions for the houses that do not keep a scribe of their own, and they know more of the Heights' business than the houses would like.

## Who Keeps Order

The ward's _peltros_ licenses the hearths and the shops, and his inspectors walk the streets weekly with the fire-law in one hand and a tablet in the other. The goldsmiths' guild keeps the assay and the hallmark, and its assay-master tries any piece of gold or silver sold in the quarter on demand. [[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] keeps a watch-house on the Circle of Waters, and at night its patrols walk the shop streets closely, since nowhere in the city is there more worth stealing behind a wooden shutter. The great houses look after their own craftsmen, and a thief who robs a goldsmith working for a house on Selvin answers to that house's men before he answers to the Watch.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **An unlicensed hearth.** A furnace without the _peltros_'s tag is put out by the Watch and its owner fined. A second offense shuts the shop.
- **Gold below the mark.** A piece sold with the goldsmiths' hallmark that fails the assay is broken, its maker expelled from the guild and his waiver reported to the court that granted it. The buyer may sue him before the Civil Courts for twice the price.
- **Poison across the counter.** An apothecary may sell the drugs on the physicians' list only against a physician's written order, and he keeps the orders. A sale without one costs him his license, and a sale that ends in a death is a charge before the Criminal Tribunal.
- **A seal cut without a warrant.** A seal-cutter cuts a stamp only for its registered owner, and only on a written warrant from that owner's steward, guild or court. A stamp cut without one is a capital crime before the Criminal Tribunal, and the stamp of a noble house lifts the case to the imperial tribunals.

## What the Quarter Fears

The Gilded Quarter fears the fall of a house. A proscription on the Heights ends the waivers of every craftsman the house signed for, and within the month the Watch walks those masters out through the gate and the court sells their benches to the next house's craftsmen. With the old Orzas's succession in doubt, every master in the quarter knows which house his waiver hangs on and how that house stands. The quarter also fears fire from its own hearths, and the Great Burning is remembered in its fire-law clause by clause.

## Hooks

- **The waiver.** A goldsmith's sponsoring house has been named in a proscription, and his waiver dies at the month's end. He hires a party to find him a new house to sign for him before then, and he can pay in gold.
- **The second stamp.** A seal-cutter has been asked, with a warrant that looks right, to cut a copy of a granary's stamp. He suspects the warrant and wants a party to find out who wrote it.
- **The mask.** A new house on Selvin has ordered a cupboard of ancestor masks from the Street of Masks, and the mask-maker has been given a face to copy for one of them that he recognizes from another house's cupboard.

# The Truth of the Gilded Quarter {#truth .secret}

The apothecary who compounds the old Orzas's medicines keeps a ledger. The Orzas's own physician lives in the palace, but he sends his orders down to an apothecary's shop on the street above the Circle of Waters, the one shop in the city he trusts to make up what he asks without asking why. The apothecary records every order, as the law requires, and the ledger shows what the Orzas takes, how much, and how the doses have grown through the last year. To a physician who reads it, it shows that the Orzas has months, not years.

The apothecary has understood this for half a year. Three weeks ago he sold a copy of the last year's orders to the steward of a house on [Torvel]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, for more gold than his shop has made in a decade, and he has been afraid ever since, because the steward knows he could sell it again. The ledger itself is in a strongbox under his counter. Every claimant to the throne would pay for it, and the physician in the palace would have the apothecary killed for keeping it.
