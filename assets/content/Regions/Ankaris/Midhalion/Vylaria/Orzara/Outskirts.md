## Overview

[[place-orzara|Orzara City]] is made outside itself. Beyond the Clearance, the swept strip of open ground that rings the city, sprawl the outskirt communities, the Anthrima in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], "beyond the line": the unplanned, half-legal overflow of a city that outgrew its bounds long ago, and the home of most of the people who keep it running. The right to dwell inside the thrima belongs to citizens and their sponsored servants alone, so nearly half a million resident laborers, journeymen and migrants live out here, and so do all the trades the city will not house. The smithies and foundries, the potteries, the timber- and stone-yards and the stinking trades crowd the ground downwind and downriver, the gate-yards ring every gate, and the walled garden-villas of the very rich stand apart on the pleasanter hills and riverbanks.

A teamster who has driven wagons to the capital's gates for thirty years tells every new driver what to expect when the city comes in sight: "You will see the marble from a day out and the smoke from two. The smoke is where you are going. Nothing with a wheel or a hoof goes in through the gate, so you unhitch in the yards and sleep in the yards, and your load goes in on somebody's back. Keep your blade in the wagon. Out here nobody cares what you carry, and at the gate everybody does."

## Arriving

A traveler on any of the great roads reaches the outskirts long before he reaches the city. The road runs between tombs for its last miles, since the dead lie outside the thrima, and then between yards, inns, workshops and tenements, thickening into a town that does not stop until the Clearance. There it stops dead. The last tenement stands at the outer edge of the hundred-foot strip, the first building of the city stands at the inner edge, and between them the gate-road crosses the swept ground under the eyes of [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|The Vylarian Military]].

Each great gate has its gate-yard: a town of stables, ostlers' yards, caravanserais and teamsters' lodgings, where the road-caravans halt, the draft animals are housed and fed, and the wagon-cargo is broken down onto porters and handcarts for the journey through the gate. The largest stand at the [Paldor Gate]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the great gate where the [Paldor]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Triumphal Way, begins, at the [Hilen Gate]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on the grain road, and at the [Salkalon Gate]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on the road to the coast. A yard sounds of hooves and shouting all day and smells of dung, hay and wet leather, and its inns are the cheapest beds within a day of the capital.

Between the gates the outskirts are tenements, workshops, gardens and lanes, built wherever someone could build. Nearest the Clearance they are poorest: in a lean year the lean-tos creep out onto the strip itself, and the Watch comes through to pull them down. Farther out the ground grows more varied, a weaving town here, a potters' town there, a village of market gardens that sells its cabbages at the gate every morning.

## The Smoke

South of the city, downwind of the summer northerlies and downriver of everything, lies the [Smoke]{.name data-type="place/site"}, Karvuma to its own people, where the city's fire-trades are kept. It is a belt of furnaces several miles long under a haze that never clears: the smithies and foundries, the brick- and lime-kilns, the potteries, the charcoal-burners' yards, and beyond them, farthest out and farthest downriver, the tanneries, the dye- and fulling-works and the shambles. A wagon of iron goes in at one end of the Smoke and comes out the other as nails, hinges, pots, lamps, blades and tools, and the goods go up the road to the gate to be sold inside by the chandlers and the markets.

The smiths of the Smoke keep the feast of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], the Forge-Lord, in [Thumbandi]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, the smiths' month, and the shrine of [[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]] at the Smoke's center is the busiest temple outside the city. Every smith in the Smoke brings his first piece of the year to its altar.

### The Steel Town

At the heart of the Smoke, behind a wall of brick three times a man's height, stands [Thalbezara]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the Steel Town, where [Vylarian Steel]{.name data-type="miscgear"} is made. It is the walled foundry-town of the [Steel Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, the Thalbezalon, and nobody goes through its single gate unless he belongs to the guild or has been sent for. From outside a visitor sees the wall, the gate with the guild's mark carved over it, and the tops of a dozen chimneys that smoke day and night.

The guild is chartered under the Smiths' Federation of [[affiliation-tevran|Great College of Guilds]], and it holds the most famous _kelthon_ in the Empire: a kept secret, a technique held as property of the same order as land, inherited by an heir and sued over in court. Steel, _thalbezon_, is the guild's word, and it is never used of the common iron a village smith beats out. The guild's masters, the _gartoi_, each hold a bench, which is to say a share in the secret, and the masters' families are known in the trade abroad as the Steel Houses. An apprentice is bound to a bench for seven years and swears the guild's oath at the Sacred Forge's altar before he is let into the workshops. Every finished piece is struck with the guild's mark, its _thumisa_, and with the mark of the bench that made it.

The guild sells its steel in bars to the capital's armorers and in finished blades to whoever can pay, and its best blades go to [[affiliation-sorvan|The Imperial Guard]], to the great houses and abroad, at prices that buy a farm. Its _kelros_ (guild master) sits on the Smiths' Federation's seat on the Tevran's Council when the seat comes to the guild's turn.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About four hundred and eighty thousand people live in the outskirts, more than in the city inside the line. They are the resident laborers and journeymen of every trade the city bans, the porters, carters, stall-hands and sweepers who walk in through the gates at dawn and out again at the closing bell, the teamsters and ostlers of the gate-yards, the migrants who come from the provinces looking for work, and the families of all of them. Most are residents of the Empire with no citizenship, and they count themselves Orzarans, since the city could not survive a week without them.

On the pleasanter hills and riverbanks upstream, well clear of the Smoke, lie the _vildori_, the walled suburban garden-villas of the very rich, with their gardens, their quiet and their own guards: the genteel opposite face of the same ground outside the line.

## Who Keeps Order

The city's law ends at the thrima. The [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]] governs Orzara inside the line, and the outskirts belong in law to the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]], whose _therkos_ (governor) sits at [[place-alyssar|Alessar]] and sends nobody. In practice the City Watch holds the gate-roads and the gate-yards, where the customs-men and the residence-check work, and the [[affiliation-aediles|Magistrates of the Wards]] license the yards nearest the gates. Beyond the yards the outskirts keep their own order. The yard-masters keep the gate-yards, the guild wardens keep their trades, the gang-masters who hire out labor keep their gangs, and the priests of the Sacred Forge settle more quarrels in the Smoke than any magistrate. Inside Thalbezara the Steel Guild keeps its own men on its own wall.

It is dirtier, wilder and more lawless out here than within, and it swells and shrinks with the fortunes of the Empire. In bad years it breeds plague and unrest, and in good ones it gathers new blood and restless energy.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Building on the Clearance.** No building, stall or shelter may stand on the hundred-foot strip. The Watch pulls down what it finds and burns what it cannot pull down, and the builder is fined if he can be found.
- **A weapon under arms across the line.** No man crosses the Clearance into the city armed and harnessed for war. A traveler's blade goes through the gate wrapped and declared, and a man in harness at the gate is turned back. An armed company that tries the gate is a matter for the Sorvan.
- **A forge brought inside.** A smith who carries his trade inside the line is fined by the Pelthralon and his tools seized.
- **The Steel Town's gate.** A stranger found inside Thalbezara's wall without leave is held by the guild and handed to the Watch at the gate, and the guild sues him before the civic courts for whatever he might have seen.
- **The steel mark.** A blade sold with a forged guild mark is broken and its seller sued by the guild for the price of a true blade. A man who cuts a forged mark is a forger of a registered mark, and that is a capital crime.

## What the Outskirts Fear

The outskirts fear a bad year. When the grain comes short the price of bread rises on both sides of the line, and the outskirts go hungry first, because nobody outside it draws a pension to meet the price. When plague comes it comes here first and stays longest. The outskirts also fear the sweeps inside the city, because every non-citizen the Watch puts out across the Clearance needs a bed out here, and the lean-tos on the strip are the measure of how the Empire fares. The Smoke fears fire like everywhere else, and it fears the day the guild's chimneys stop smoking, because half the Smoke lives off the Steel Town's orders.

## Hooks

- **The broken-down load.** A teamster's wagon was unloaded in the Hilen Gate's yard, and one of the crates never reached the gate. The teamster pays a party to find which porter carried it and where it went.
- **The lean-tos.** The Watch means to clear the Clearance below the Salkalon Gate tomorrow at dawn, and the people living on it have nowhere to go. A Sacred Forge priest asks a party to find them room before the Watch arrives.
- **The forged mark.** A blade bought in the Gilded Quarter as Vylarian Steel broke on its first blow, and it carries a perfect guild mark. The guild's _kelros_ hires a party to find who cut the mark, quietly, before the price of true blades falls.
- **The sent-for.** A party is summoned through Thalbezara's gate by a master of the guild who will not say why in writing.

# The Truth of the Steel Town {#truth .secret}

The guild has lost a third of its secret. The _kelthon_ of Vylarian Steel is held in three parts, and no master holds more than one: the Bench of the Clay knows how the crucibles are made and of what earth, the Bench of the Charge knows what goes into each crucible with the iron, and the Bench of the Fire knows how long, how hot and how slowly each firing runs. Each part passes from a master to his heir by word, and the guild's oath forbids writing any of it down. Forty years ago the last master of the Bench of the Fire died before he had adopted an heir, and the whole of his part died with him.

The guild has rebuilt the firing by trial, melt after melt, and its steel is still the best on the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. It is not the old steel. The watered blades, whose surface shows a pattern like moving water and which bend double and spring back, have not come out of Thalbezara since the old master died. The guild has said nothing. For the highest commissions, a great house's sword or a gift from the throne to a foreign crown, it sells blades from the old master's time, kept in the guild's strongroom since his death, re-hilted and struck with a new year's mark. There are thirty-one left.

The _kelros_ and the two surviving bench-holders know. So does the old master's last apprentice, who left the guild the year his master died and is still alive somewhere in the Smoke. The guild has never found him, and it believes he knows the firing.
