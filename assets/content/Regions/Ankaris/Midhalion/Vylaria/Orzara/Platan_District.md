## Overview

Most people come to the Platan District because somebody has sent for them. It is the civic heart of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]], laid out in the central saddle between [[place-arvel|Eagle Hill]] and [Torvel]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, and everything in it is built to hear a case, record a decision or announce one. The basilicas of the civic courts, the hall of the [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]], the central hall of [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Imperial Scribal Guild]], the grandest public baths and libraries in the city and the speakers' platforms all stand round the [Circle of Árdavon]{.name data-type="place/site"}, under the statue of [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] of the Gates, lord of order and justice.

An usher of the civil basilica, who has stood at its doors for nineteen years, gives every new litigant the same advice on the steps: "Find out which court you are in before you open your mouth, and find out who your judge dines with before you pay your advocate. Leave your knife with the doorkeeper. And if a clerk in a gray stole comes down the steps with your name on a tablet, go with him quietly, because he is from the Offices and your case is not ours any more."

## Arriving

The Platan District is the end of the city's grandest road. The [Paldor]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Triumphal Way, runs dead straight from the great gate to the [Orzas's Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} at the district's upper edge, where the colossus of the first Orzas stands before the gate of the [Inner Wall]{.name data-type="place/structure"}. The crowned rune on its plinth encloses a smoothed patch of stone where the script's rule wants a name ([[lore-eaglecityrse|The Raising of the Eagle City]]). Every accession and every triumph passes it, and the guides of the avenues stop their parties there to look up.

Below the Orzas's Circle the ground levels into the saddle, and the Circle of Árdavon opens across it: three hundred yards of pale paving with the god's statue at its heart, his right hand raised and his left on the bar of a gate. The basilica of the Civil Courts closes the north side of the Circle, a long hall of columns with bronze doors and a roof of gilded tile, and the basilica of the Criminal Tribunal faces it across the paving, plainer, older and windowless along its lower story. The [Hall of the Five]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, where the Verdan Orzara sits, stands between them on the east, under a portico whose five columns are carved with the five portfolios. The Scribal Guild's central hall fills the west side, its doors open from first light and its steps crowded with scribes for hire.

The speakers' platforms stand at the Circle's edge, three stone stages the height of a man, their fronts set with the bronze rams of warships. From them the criers read the proclamations of the throne and the council at noon, and the boards behind them carry the posted notices: the dole-days, the sales of condemned property, the names called to the courts and, in a bad year, a proscription. The [Legions' Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} lies above the district on the road that climbs to [[place-thaurzurgalis|The Guard Camp]], and the [Velmiros]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the city's green, spreads below it toward the Grand Market, so that a litigant who has lost his case has a walk under trees on his way home.

## The Day in the Platan

The district keeps court hours. The basilicas open at the second hour after dawn, and before then the steps fill with litigants, their advocates, the advocates' clients and the witnesses each side has brought. A man of standing arrives with his own crowd of clients, because the size of a man's following on the steps is evidence of a sort, and the judges of the Civil Courts read it. The scribes on the Guild's steps write petitions, summonses and sworn statements at a fixed rate, an Ogdel for ten lines and an Ardin for a sealed copy, and every one of them carries the Guild's registered seal on a cord at the neck.

At noon the criers mount the platforms and the whole Circle stops to listen. In the afternoon the courts rise, the litigants go to the baths, and the district's great baths fill with the same men who were shouting at each other in the morning. Business continues there in the warm rooms, and more cases are settled in the baths than are ever judged in the basilicas. The libraries beside the baths lend nothing and charge nothing, and their reading-rooms are full of advocates looking up the law their opponents have just cited.

By dusk the district empties. The basilicas are locked and sealed, the Guild's hall closes its doors on its archive, and the Circle of Árdavon is left to the Watch, the fire-watch on the Guild's roof and the few hundred people who live here.

## Who Lives and Works Here

The Platan District houses about fifteen thousand people, most of them in the upper floors of the colonnades and the streets behind the basilicas. They are the clerks of the courts and the council, the court ushers and doorkeepers, the advocates who can afford to live near their work, the bath-keepers and their slaves, and the copyists of the Guild, who are citizens or the Guild's own registered servants. Every one of them holds a citizen's right of residence or a waiver tied to an office of the district, and the ward's registers are the most carefully kept in the city.

The district's real population arrives every morning and leaves every evening. Litigants come from every ward, and witnesses come with them. The [[affiliation-aediles|Magistrates of the Wards]] send up their appeals, [[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] brings its prisoners to the Criminal Tribunal, and the stewards of the great houses on the [[place-heightsorz|Heights]] come down to see that their masters' business is called in the right order.

## Who Keeps Order

The Veramus of Law presides over the civic courts from the Hall of the Five, and the district is his ward in all but name. The ward has its own _peltros_ (ward magistrate), whose bench is the quietest in the city because so little is sold here. The Watch-house on the Circle of Árdavon is one of the strongest in Orzara, with a full watch-cohort and an armory deep enough to arm it in legionary kit, because a verdict that goes the wrong way in front of a crowd can become a riot before the judge has left the bench. The court ushers keep the doors, and the Criminal Tribunal keeps its own jailers for the prisoners waiting below its floor.

Above all of it, and apart from it, are the imperial tribunals in the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]], on the other side of the Inner Wall. A case touching the nobility, [[affiliation-seldan|The Vylarian Senate]] or the throne can be lifted out of the Platan at the throne's word, and the Offices' clerks in their gray stoles come down the hill to collect it.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Trade on the Circle of Árdavon.** Only the Guild's registered scribes, the sellers of wax tablets and the water-sellers may work on the Circle or in the basilica porches. A stall set up there is pulled down by the Watch and its keeper fined by the _peltros_.
- **Smoke.** No cookshop, bakery or hot-food stall is licensed in the ward, and the district's meals are carried in cold from the Grand Market. A brazier lit on the Circle is a fine on the spot.
- **A blade in the basilicas.** A knife is left with the doorkeeper and taken back on leaving. A blade found on a man inside a basilica costs a fine and a night in the Watch-house, and a blade found on a man in the Criminal Tribunal is a charge in that court.
- **An unregistered pen.** A petition or sworn statement drafted by a scribe who is not registered with the Guild is void, and the scribe is fined and his materials seized. A counterfeit Guild seal is a capital crime before the Criminal Tribunal.

## History {#history}

The first Orzas walled the crown of Arvel above the saddle and moved the Senate up to it ([[lore-eaglecityrse|The Raising of the Eagle City]]). Below the wall the Platan's courts, baths and platforms have spoken for the city since, and the Scribal Guild's central hall keeps the working copies of the city's rolls across the Circle from the Hall of the Five. The platforms have read every proscription and every Orzas's death the city has heard.

## What the District Fears {#present}

The Platan fears the noon proclamations, and all three it dreads are close. A short dole is never far off, because the throne pays the dole with borrowed money. A proscription can be read from the platforms on any day the throne needs a house's property. The reigning Orzas, [[being-zrmlshlrkls|Zarmelas Helarkales]], is seventy-eight and failing, and every clerk in the Hall of the Five knows that the day his death is read the Circle of Árdavon fills with citizens waiting to hear who reigns, and the Watch-house shutters its windows. Lists are worth money before they are posted, and the Offices' clerks in their gray stoles lift cases out of the civic basilicas at the throne's word. The district keeps a fire-watch all night on the roof of the Guild's hall, where the copies of the rolls are kept.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The witness.** An advocate's case is called tomorrow, and his chief witness has not been seen since he went into the baths. The advocate pays a party to find him before the second hour.
- **The early copy.** A scribe on the Guild's steps is offered ten Soldeni to make a copy of a list before it is posted on the platform boards. He wants a party to learn what the list is before he decides.
- **The gray stole.** A client's case was lifted out of the civil basilica into the Offices in the middle of a hearing, and the client has not come back down the hill. His family wants to know which tribunal holds him.

# The Truth of the Platan {#truth .secret}

The citizen roll of Orzara carries the dead. The roll that sets the dole is kept in the Hall of the Five under the Veramus of the Rolls, and its working copies are made in the Scribal Guild's hall across the Circle. A veteran-citizen who dies is struck from the roll when his death is reported to his ward, and in the poorer wards nobody reports it. For a generation the ward clerks of the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]] and the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]] have drawn the dole of their dead every month in the dead men's names, and pocketed the coin. The roll now carries about four thousand names that belong to nobody.

The Veramus of the Rolls knows, and takes a share through a freedman who collects from the clerks. Two senior copyists of the Guild know, because they have copied the same dead names for twenty years, and they have kept a private list of them against the day it is worth something. The other four _veramusoi_ do not know, and the Tarmeks pays out against the roll as the Veramus of the Rolls certifies it. The reform faction in the Seldan argues for trimming the rolls and testing every citizen's claim. The day the Seldan orders that audit, the copyists' list is worth a fortune to the Veramus of the Rolls, and a seat on the council to whoever brings it to his colleagues first.
