## Overview

The Warrens are where [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]'s citizens live. They are the dense tenement wards, Katsur in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], that fill every valley and lower slope the grander quarters leave them, and they are packed thickest on [Nerval]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the River Hill, above the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]]. Their buildings are _tengali_ (tenements), five, six and seven stories of brick below and timber above, crowded wall to wall along stepped lanes, and they are home to nearly two hundred thousand people: the veteran-citizens and their families, and the sponsored working folk whose livelihoods keep them inside the line. At their heart lies the [Spire Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"}, built about a soaring spire hauled from a conquered province.

A Watch sergeant walking a new recruit through the lanes of Nerval on his first night patrol stops at the foot of a stair and points up it: "Ninety stairs to the top of that one, and a family on every landing. The ground floor is a cookshop, the first floor is a man who has done well, and the top floor is a man who has not. Every one of them did twenty years in a legion or married someone who did, and every one of them knows exactly what the Empire owes him. Remember that before you raise your cudgel."

## Arriving

A visitor comes into the Warrens down a lane off an avenue and is in another city within a dozen steps. The lanes are narrow, stepped and dark, their paving worn into troughs down the middle, and the _tengali_ lean together above them until the upper galleries nearly touch and the sky is a strip the width of a door. The ground floors are shops: cookshops with a hot counter on the street, taverns, bakers' shops, cobblers, barbers and the ward's licensed sellers of oil and wine. Above them the stories rise in brick to the second or third floor and in timber above that, with galleries hung with washing, flowerpots and drying fish, and narrow stairs climbing inside from the lane to the roof.

At every crossing of two lanes stands a street shrine, a niche in the wall with a lamp and the painted figures of the household gods of the street, and the people of the lanes keep its lamp lit and its offerings fresh. The blank stone fire-walls that run between the wards are the tallest things in the Warrens after the _tengali_ themselves, and each was built after a fire. The Spire Circle opens in the valley below Nerval, three hundred yards of paving with the spire at its heart: a column of dark stone carved with a script nobody in the Warrens can read, so tall that its gilded tip catches the sun while the lanes below are still in shadow.

## The Day in the Warrens

The Warrens wake before the rest of the city and sleep last. The cookshops light their ovens before dawn, the water-carriers fill their jars at the street fountains, and by first light the lanes are full of men going to work: porters down to the Wharves, watchmen to their watch-houses, guards, overseers and stewards up to the great houses. The women of the stairs keep the lanes by day, and the children run them. At noon the cookshops sell hot food over the counter to anyone with an Ogdel, and in the evening the taverns fill with old soldiers drinking by legion.

The month turns on the dole. On dole-day the veterans of each ward collect their pensions in coin at the ward's pay-hall with a token from the roll, each at the rate of the rank he left the legion with, and the Spire Circle is the largest pay-hall in the city. The queue starts at dawn and is orderly, because the Watch is there and because every man in it knows the man in front of him. The day the pay comes short is the day the Warrens remember for a generation.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About a hundred and ninety thousand people live in the Warrens. Most of them are citizens or a citizen's household: veterans of the legions discharged at thirty-five, their wives, who hold the citizenship for life, and their children, who hold it while they are dependents. Beside them live the sponsored working folk, servants and employees of someone who matters, holding a waiver that lasts as long as their work. The rest are the city's poor who have a right to be here and little else.

The veterans organize the Warrens by legion. Men of the same legion take rooms on the same stair, drink in the same tavern and stand together at the dole, and a stair often calls itself by its legion's name. A new veteran coming home to the city looks for his legion's stair before he looks for work. The landlords are mostly great houses and rich citizens who never see their buildings, and their rent-men collect at the month's end, the day after the dole.

## Who Keeps Order

The Warrens keep their own order first. Each stair has a man whose word counts on it, usually the senior veteran, and a quarrel on the stair is settled by him before anyone thinks of the Watch. A thief caught on a legion's stair is beaten by the legion and handed over afterward.

[[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] keeps a watch-house on every Circle in the Warrens, the Spire Circle's the largest, each walled and provisioned to stand alone for days. The watchmen are veteran-citizens themselves, many of them living in the wards they patrol, and they double as the fire-brigade, with hand-engines, hooks and a cistern in every watch-house. The ward magistrates of [[affiliation-aediles|Magistrates of the Wards]] license the shops, enforce the building law and condemn the _tengali_ that lean too far, and their inspectors are the best-bribed men in the Warrens.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Sleeping inside without the right.** A non-citizen with no waiver found asleep inside the thrima spends the night in the watch-house and is put out across the Clearance at dawn. A second time, the Watch brings him before the Criminal Tribunal, which sentences him to a year's labor on the city's works.
- **Fire above the ground floor.** No cooking fire, brazier or forge is allowed above the ground floor of a tenement. A fire found burning upstairs costs the tenant a fine and the landlord a larger one, both levied by the ward's _peltros_. A fire that spreads from it is a charge against the tenant before the Criminal Tribunal.
- **Building too high.** The district law limits a tenement to seven stories, with masonry to the third floor and party-walls of brick. A landlord who adds an eighth story of timber has it pulled down by the Watch on the _peltros_'s order, at his own cost, and pays a fine on every room in it.
- **A riot on dole-day.** The Watch musters in full legionary kit on every dole-day, and a man who starts a fight in the dole queue is struck from the roll for a month by the ward's pay-hall. A man who draws a blade in the queue answers to the Criminal Tribunal, and its sentence is forced labor.

## History {#history}

The Warrens filled every valley and lower slope the grander quarters left as the city outgrew its hills. Since [[lore-veteranslaw|the Veterans' Law]] made the citizenship a soldier's reward, the men who served their twenty years have come home to the capital by the thousand and taken the stairs by legion, so that a stair on Nerval is still known by the legion whose men first filled it. Fire has run through the lanes again and again, and each blank fire-wall between the wards was built after one of them; [[lore-greatburnng|the Great Burning]] of about 403 is the one every ward remembers. As the empire receded, wards burned in lean decades were rebuilt cheaper and higher, in more timber, and the dole that keeps the stairs paying their rent came to be paid with borrowed money.

## What the Warrens Fear {#present}

The Warrens fear fire, the short dole and dear bread, and every power in the city courts them through those fears. Fire comes here first, and the landlords build higher than the law allows: [[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]], whose house owns most of the tenements on Nerval, wants the height limit lifted, his rents fall due the day after pension day, and a landlord has added an eighth story of timber to a tenement that the _peltros_ has not ordered down. No man on the stairs makes his rent without the dole, and the state granaries hold a week of grain. The veterans' tribune, [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]], promises the River Circle's and the Spire Circle's stairs that the pension is untouchable, and every claimant courts him for the Circles. [[being-rzmsplthn|Razemos Pelthen]], Master of the Irons, sells the Warrens' crowd to whichever claimant promises the accession games. The night sweeps go up every stair with lanterns for sleepers without papers, and the preachers of [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] on the Spire Circle tell the Warrens that each of these fears is the empire's weakness, and find more listeners every year.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The legion's stair.** A veteran has come home to the city to find his legion's stair let to strangers by a new landlord, and the legion's old men want to know who bought the building and why.
- **The eighth story.** A landlord has added an eighth story of timber to a tenement on Nerval, and the _peltros_ has not ordered it down. A tenant on the seventh floor pays a party to learn why before the winter rains.
- **The widow's token.** A veteran's widow reported her husband's death to the ward two winters ago, and she hears that his pension is still drawn every month at the pay-hall of another ward on a token in his name. She wants a party to find the man who is drawing it.

# The Truth of the Warrens {#truth .secret}

About three thousand people sleep in the Warrens every night with no right to be inside the line, and the Watch sells them the right by the night. The watch-house on the Spire Circle issues small wooden tallies, notched for the month, to anyone who pays a Bit a night to its sergeants, and a watchman on a night sweep who finds a sleeper holding the right tally walks past. The tallies are sold through the cookshops, and the coin goes to the sergeants, the centurions of the watch-cohort and its captain, in shares fixed by rank. The sleepers are the city's working poor who cannot afford the walk out across the Clearance and back every day: porters, stall-hands and the wives of men in the outskirts who keep a stall in the Warrens.

The sweeps still happen. When the Veramus of Order wants to see numbers, the captain of the Spire Circle house sends his men up the stairs of the people who have not paid, and the Watch puts a hundred of them across the Clearance in a night. The Veramus counts the numbers and is satisfied. The legions' stairs know all of it, and the old soldiers who keep them take a share of the tally trade on their own stairs and keep the Watch's secret for it.
