## Overview {#overview}

The doors of the Grand Orrery open at the great hours with a sound of deep bells. The Orrery, also called the Mechanical Court, is an enormous arcane-mechanical automaton housed in a hall of [[place-palvar|Palvar]] (the Grand Palace) in [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. It is part clock, part theater and part diplomatic weapon, built of brass, crystal, enchanted gears and animate figures the size of living men. Travelers cross a continent to see it, and ambassadors are meant to carry its story home.

A junior of [[affiliation-sorvan|the Sorvan]] who has stood the hall's door at the changes of the watch can describe the next three minutes with his eyes shut, and he shows a new recruit what to expect: "Stand to the side and keep your face still. The bells come first. Then armored legionaries roll out on hidden tracks and salute together, and gilded provinces come after them as crowned women who bow toward the throne. A clockwork eagle spreads its wings. Miniature Orzoi of the past pace a circuit and return. The joints whisper and the eyes glow with a soft light, and the first time you see it you will want to look at the visitors instead. Do that. Watching a stranger's face while the eagle opens its wings is the best part of the post."

## The Hall and the Machine {#inside}

The hall is long and high, its floor of dark porphyry, its walls lined with the bronze doors of the figures' stables. The visitors stand behind a rail of gilded bronze at the hall's foot, and the throne's chair of state stands empty at its head on the hours when the Orzas does not attend. The figures roll out from the side doors on tracks sunk flush in the floor and pass between the rail and the chair, so that every bow is made toward the throne and every visitor sees it made.

A celestial dial fills the vault above the pageant and tracks the wandering of sun, moon and stars in real time. The Orrery tells the hour, and it tells a story as well: the inevitability of Vilérian order, the obedience of the world and the eternity of the throne.

The provinces that bow are the provinces the Orrery was built to show, and the machine has not been changed for the ones the Empire has lost. The figure for Taruzel still comes out at every great hour, crowned, and bows to the throne whose rule her merchant princes threw off twelve years ago. Envoys from Taruzel are not brought to the hall, and the chamberlains who arrange the embassies know which hour each envoy sees.

## Who Keeps It {#who-is-there}

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] maintains the Orrery jealously. Only a handful of its Magi-Artificers know the full secret of its workings, and they come to the hall at night, when it is shut, with their own lamps and their own guards. The Sorvan keeps the door, the palace chamberlains keep the schedule of who sees which hour, and the cost of keeping the machine running is a closely guarded line in the imperial accounts. The guard on the door knows only what the pageant does, and he knows it better than anyone who has ever watched it from the floor.

## The Machine and the Money {#present}

The Orrery shows an eternal throne to envoys whose masters lend to it. The throne pays the veterans' pension with borrowed money, chiefly from the great houses of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], and it borrows abroad as well: the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] lends against the customs of [[place-aelissium|Elissara]], which its own clerks collect at the river mouth outside the walls, and [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]] offers loans of its own. The League's agents and the Khelathi envoys are shown the eagle at the noon hour with every courtesy the palace has.

The Orrery's keeping is a line nobody outside the treasury and the Order is shown. This spring the strongroom audit of [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]] seats a reformer's voice for the first time beside the throne's and the Scribal Guild's, and that seat asks to read the line. The Imperial _Tarkelos_ (treasury officer) answers that it is the Order's to disclose. The Order answers that it is the throne's.

Inside the Order the machine is an argument. Its Traditionalists hold that the Orrery is the imperial tie made visible, and that the Order lives by that tie. Its Reformers hold that the Order spends its masters' best secrets on a show for a throne that cannot pay its soldiers without borrowing, and that the Order's future lies with every crown and not with one. The debate is unresolved, and the Magi-Artificers who keep the machine are on both sides of it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The wrong hour.** A palace chamberlain has learned that a Taruzelian envoy is to be shown the noon hour by a rival's arrangement. He hires a party to find out who changed the schedule before the figure bows.
- **The reformer's line.** The reformer's seat at the strongroom audit of [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]] wants the Orrery's cost before spring, and asks a party to learn it from someone in the palace who has carried the Magi-Artificers' lamps.
- **The night visit.** A Magi-Artificer of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|the Thordimalon]] who keeps the machine wants a party to stand guard in the shut hall while he works, and does not want his own Order to know he is there.
