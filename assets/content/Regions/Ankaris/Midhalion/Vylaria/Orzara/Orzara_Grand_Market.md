## Overview

Everything [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] eats, wears and burns is sold in the Grand Market, and almost none of it is made there. The Grand Market, Delalon Melvar in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], spreads across the central flats below the [Velmiros]{.name data-type="place/site"} around the [Grand Market Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"}, the largest licensed market in the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]. Its stalls are licensed to the inch, its weights are tried against the city's bronze standard, and its rows are sorted by ware under a district law that says what may be sold where. Around the Circle stand the chandlers who retail what the city buys from beyond its bounds, the counting-houses of [[affiliation-danvar|The Bankers' Guild]], and the Council Hall and guild-halls of [[affiliation-tevran|Great College of Guilds]], whose trades keep their workshops outside.

A money-changer who has kept a table on the Circle for thirty years teaches his new boy the market's first rule while they set out the felt: "Nothing is sold in the wrong row. Oil is sold in the Oil Row, cloth in the Cloth Row, and a man who sells oil in the Cloth Row is selling it to the _peltros_. Learn the rows, learn the bells, and learn whose name is on a stall's license, because it is never the man behind the stall."

## Arriving

The Grand Market starts before dawn. The porters come in first, up from the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]] and in from the gate-yards beyond the Clearance with handcarts, sacks and baskets, and the Circle's lanes fill with the chant of men calling the count of their loads. By the first bell the stalls are open. The Circle itself is three hundred yards of paving under ranks of awnings in the colors of the rows, red for meat, yellow for oil, white for cloth, and blue for the money-changers' tables under the colonnade. At its heart stands a bronze figure of a merchant weighing grain on a beam scale, taller than a house, its pans worn bright where children climb into them.

The streets that run off the Circle are the rows. Each takes one ware and keeps it: the [Oil Row]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the [Cloth Row]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the wine-vaults under their arches, the potters' street, the spice sellers in a covered lane that smells of pepper and resin from end to end, the fish sellers on the side nearest the river. Behind the rows the chandlers keep their warehouses and shops, and a chandler will supply a great house with anything it needs, from lamp-oil to a dozen chairs, through a single account settled at the year's end.

On the Circle's east side stand two buildings that do not sell anything. The counting-house of the Danvar's Orzara chapter is a square block of gray stone with no windows on its ground floor and a door guarded day and night. The quarterly Council of the Danvori meets in its upper hall. Beside it the Tevran Maldor's Council Hall rises in pale marble under a portico of thirty-six columns, one for each seat on its Council, and the guild-halls of its trades stand along the streets behind it, each carved with its trade's arms over the door.

## The Day in the Market

The market runs by bells. The first bell opens the stalls, the noon bell opens the money-changers' tables to the paper notes of the Danvar, and the closing bell shuts every stall within the hour. The fixed days are kept on the Circle itself: grain on the first day of the market week, horses and draft beasts never, since they are sold in the gate-yards, and slaves on the third day under the colonnade, where the licensed dealers show them on a platform and the _peltros_'s clerk records each sale.

At the closing bell the Grand Market changes population. Most of the hands who work its stalls are not citizens and hold no waiver, and they cannot sleep inside the city. Within the hour after the bell thousands of stall-hands, porters and carters leave the Circle together and walk the avenues out to the gate-roads, and the Watch at the gates counts them across the Clearance. The citizens who hold the licenses stay behind, and the rows are left to them, their households and the Watch.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About forty-five thousand people live in the district, above the shops and in the streets behind the rows. The licensed stall-holders are citizens, most of them veterans who bought into a trade with their savings and their discharge bounty, and their households live above the stall. The chandlers are rich and live well. The Danvar's clerks and its moneylenders, the Tevran's officers and the guild-masters who serve on its Council keep houses near their halls, and the money-changers of the colonnade are the best-informed men in the city about what is happening to the price of everything.

The district's working population is several times its resident one. The stall-hands, porters, criers, sweepers and water-sellers come in every morning from the outskirts and go out every evening, and the market could not open a day without them.

## Who Keeps Order

The _peltros_ (ward magistrate) of the market ward holds the busiest bench of [[affiliation-aediles|Magistrates of the Wards]], and his inspectors walk the rows all day. The Keeper of the Weights tries the stall-holders' scales against the city's bronze standard pound, which is kept in the ward office on the Circle and brought out to the Circle's edge when a weight is disputed. The guild wardens of each trade watch their own rows and bring their complaints to the _peltros_. [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|The Vylarian Military]] keeps a watch-house on the Grand Market Circle, guards the counting-house door, and counts the stall-hands out at the closing bell.

The Veramus of the Grain, who sits for [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]], watches the market more closely than any magistrate. The price of bread in the Grand Market is the price the city lives by, and the Veramus's agents report it to him at every noon bell.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **A ware in the wrong row.** A stall-holder selling outside his licensed row is fined by the _peltros_, and a third offense loses him the license.
- **A false weight.** A light weight is broken with a hammer on the Circle in front of the stall it came from, and the stall-holder pays a fine of twenty times the short measure. A weight found light a second time loses the license.
- **A maker selling his own work.** The Tevran's charters reserve the market to licensed sellers, and a craftsman from beyond the Clearance who sets up a cloth on the paving and sells his own pots is fined and his goods seized.
- **Grain held back.** In a lean month the Veramus of the Grain may order the grain dealers' stores opened and sold at his price. A dealer found holding grain back after the order loses his stock to the state granaries.

## What the Market Fears

The Grand Market fears two prices. One is the price of bread: when it rises, the citizens come to the Circle to see why, and the Watch-house doubles its guard. The other is the price of paper. The Danvar's notes pass on the Circle as readily as coin, and the money-changers know how quickly a rumor that one great house's notes will not be honored can empty the colonnade and close every stall that took them. The market also fears the closing bell on a day of trouble, when thousands of hands are walking out to the gates at once and a single shout can turn the crowd.

## Hooks

- **The broken weight.** A stall-holder in the Oil Row swears that the weight broken on the Circle this morning was not the weight the inspector took from his stall. He wants a party to find the man who switched it.
- **The paper.** A chandler has taken a note of a great house in payment for a year's account, and the money-changers of the colonnade will not touch it. He hires a party to carry it to the house on the Heights and come back with coin.
- **The licensed name.** A party is asked to find the citizen whose name stands on a spice stall's license, because the license is due for renewal and the man behind the stall has never met him.

# The Truth of the Grand Market {#truth .secret}

A sixth of the spice and silk sold in the Grand Market belongs to Taruzelian houses. The licenses on the covered lane and half the Cloth Row stand in the names of veteran-citizens who have never stood behind the stalls. Each is paid a few Ardini a month to keep his name on the paper, and the stalls are run by the factors of the merchant houses that paid for the war in which [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] won its independence from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Empire]]. The goods come up from the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]] through the registered foreigners' quarter as Vylarian cargo with Vylarian seals.

The _peltros_ of the market ward knows the pattern, because it is plain in his own registers, and he is paid to keep inspecting other rows. The money-changers know it, because the Taruzelian houses settle their accounts in Soldeni and in nothing else. Nobody has told the Seldan. A _kurthos_ (senator) who brought the licenses before the Seldan in the present mood would shut half the covered lane in a day, put the city's spice in the hands of whichever Vylarian house bought the licenses next, and send the Taruzelian factors looking for whoever had talked.
