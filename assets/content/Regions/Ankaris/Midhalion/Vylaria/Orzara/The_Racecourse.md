## Overview {#overview}

On a race day a veteran of the City Watch can tell where the Racecourse is without looking, because the roar carries across half of [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. The Rathauna Melvar is a colossal oblong arena, the largest in the world, with tiers of seating rising in a vast U around a central spine. It lies along the valley between [[place-antel|Antel]] and [Nerval]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, and its crowd can reach a quarter-million.

A Watch veteran who has worked the [Racecourse Circle]{.name data-type="place/site"} on race days since his discharge teaches the new men what the day asks of them: "Know the colors. The racing factions wear them in the stands and in the streets, and the rivalry that begins on the track ends in a brawl by the third race. We do not take sides. We stand between the colors until the last chariot is home and the crowd turns toward the gates, and then we stand between them again."

## The Arena {#inside}

The track runs the length of the valley floor around the spine, a long low wall set with turning-posts at either end, statues, altars and the tall counters that tip a bronze eagle down as each lap is run. The starting gates close the open end of the U, and the curved end holds the triumphal gate through which the winners ride out. The tiers climb the hillsides on both flanks, stone below and timber above, and the cheapest seats at the top are so far from the track that their occupants watch the crowd as much as the chariots.

Halfway along the Antel side stands the throne's box, roofed and hung with purple, where the Orzas or his deputy gives the signal for the start. Across the track from it the factions' blocks of seats face the box, each block in its colors, and a man who sits in the wrong block learns his mistake before the first race.

Beneath the tiers run the vaults: stables, workshops, kennels and cages for the beast-hunts, the cells for those condemned to die in the arena, cookshops and wine-sellers, and the betting-tables where the factions' clerks take wagers in coin and in the Danvar's paper.

## What the Racecourse Stages {#who-is-there}

The Racecourse holds the great events of the people:

- **Chariot races**, the obsession of the city, run in the colors of the racing factions whose rivalries spill into bloody street brawls.
- **Gladiatorial combats**, beast-hunts and public executions.
- **Mock sea-battles**, staged on the grandest occasions by flooding the arena floor from the aqueducts, with real ships and real, dying men.

The racing factions, chief among them the [[affiliation-zargoi|Zargoi]] (the Irons) and the [[affiliation-ethloi|Ethloi]] (the Golds), are the Racecourse's standing population. Each keeps its stables beyond the Clearance, where the city's law puts its animals, and its race-day stalls, charioteers, grooms and trainers in the vaults and the streets around the Racecourse Circle, its own betting-tables and its own followers, who wear its colors, sit in its block, fight in its name and draw on its fund when they are hurt or out of work. Each is run by a master who answers to nobody but his faction.

The Racecourse is one half of "bread and games", and the throne pays for the games. An Orzas ignores the mood of those crowds at his peril. The [[affiliation-citywatchorz|City Watch]] keeps a watch-house at the Racecourse Circle, where the race crowds and the factions gather and, often enough, brawl.

## Who Sells the Crowd {#present}

The Racecourse is where the city shouts, and every claimant to the next reign wants to hear what it shouts for him.

- **The games of the next reign.** The reigning Orzas is old and has adopted nobody, and the man who follows him will open his reign with games. The masters of the racing factions want the contract for them, and the master of the leading faction wants it most. He holds his faction's followers, its stables and its betting-books, and through the books he holds the debts of more senators' sons than any moneylender in the city. He is offering the crowd's roar to whichever claimant will promise him the contract.
- **The claimants' seats.** A man who hopes to be named comes to the races and is seen in the stands, and the crowd's welcome is reported to the palace by nightfall. The factions decide that welcome, and the claimants know it.
- **The Watch between the colors.** The watchmen at the Racecourse Circle are veteran-citizens, and the brawls grow with every race day. The captain of the watch-house has asked the Veramus of Order for more men and has been told the city cannot pay for them.

:::secret
**For the GM:** The master of the leading faction fixed a race last season for a _kurthos_ (senator) who owed more than he could pay, and the senator sent his instructions in his own hand. The master keeps the letter. It is his strongest hold over the Senate and his worst danger: the faction's followers would tear him apart for a fixed race, and the senator would pay a great deal to have the letter burned. A party hired by either side, or by a rival faction's master, can find the letter in the master's strongbox in the vaults under the tiers.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The roar for hire.** A claimant's steward wants a party to learn what the master of the leading faction has been promised by the others before his master goes to the races on the next feast day.
- **The senator's son.** A _kurthos_ of [[affiliation-seldan|the Seldan]] learns that his son owes the leading faction's betting-books more than the house can pay, and hires a party to buy the debt back without the faction learning who is buying.
- **More men.** The captain of the Racecourse Circle watch-house of [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|the City Watch]] hires a party out of his own purse to stand between the colors on the next race day.
