## Overview

Every morning before dawn several thousand people climb the Heights to stand at someone's door. The Heights are the twin patrician hills of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]], [Torvel]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Coronal, and [[place-selvin|the Laurel]], and they hold the walled villas, terraced gardens and city-houses of the _kurthoi_ (senators) and great families who do not lodge in [[place-arvon|Inner City]]. They are prized for their air, their views and their distance from the crush, and they are reached only on foot, up lanes and stairs too steep and narrow for anything wider than a chair. Torvel is the older and prouder of the two, ringed with the ancestral villas of the senatorial houses and facing the palace across the central saddle. Selvin is greener and newer, the address of the rising and the rich, with the [[place-gildedqrtr|Gilded Quarter]] climbing its lower slopes.

The doorkeeper of an old house on Torvel, who has opened the same door at dawn for thirty years, tells a new client where to stand: "Freedmen and clients of the house by the door, strangers by the wall, and petitioners in the lane. The master comes out to the step and greets the first rank by name, nods to the second and has his steward take the third's letters. Come every morning for a year and you will be moved up a rank. Miss a morning and he will notice."

## Arriving

The way up is the lesson. From the central saddle the [Torvel Stairs]{.name data-type="place/feature"} climb the Coronal in four hundred steps between high garden walls, broken by landings where the bearers set their loads down and the clients catch their breath. Selvin is reached by gentler lanes that wind up through the Gilded Quarter, and the newest houses there have paved them at their own expense. Nothing on the Heights comes by cart. Firewood, water, wine, bread and furniture come up on the backs of bearers, one load at a time, and the bearers' guild keeps its hall at the foot of the Torvel Stairs.

At the top the lanes run between blank walls. The houses turn their backs to the street and their faces to their gardens, and what a visitor sees from the lane is a wall of dressed stone, a door and a doorkeeper. Inside the door is the hall of the masks, where the wax faces of a house's ancestors stand in their cupboards, and beyond it the courtyards and the terraces, stepping down the hill in gardens of cypress, box and roses, each with its view of the palace or the river. The [Circle of Heroes]{.name data-type="place/site"} crowns Torvel's broad summit, ranked with the statues of the Empire's great figures, and the old houses of Torvel count their own ancestors among them.

## The Day on the Heights

The Heights run on the morning call, _pramolza_. Before dawn the clients climb from every ward to their patrons' doors, in their best mantles, and stand in the lane in the order of their standing until the door opens. The master greets them, takes their petitions, hears their news and sends them on with a small coin, a dinner invitation or a promise, and by the second hour the lanes are full of clients going down again and stewards going down to the [[place-platandstr|Platan District]] and the [[place-grandmarketorz|Grand Market]] on the house's business. In the afternoon the houses visit one another, and in the evening they dine, and a dinner on the Heights is where the business of [[affiliation-seldan|The Vylarian Senate]] is done before the Seldan meets.

## Who Lives and Works Here

About twenty-five thousand people live on the two hills, and most of them are servants. A great house keeps a household of two or three hundred: its stewards, doorkeepers, cooks, gardeners, bearers, scribes, nurses, musicians and slaves, the freedmen who manage its affairs, and the clients who live in its outbuildings. The free non-citizens among them hold their right to sleep inside the city by the house's waiver, and a house's household is as large as its steward can get waivers signed for.

The master families themselves are a few hundred people in all. On Torvel they are the old senatorial houses, whose villas have stood on the same ground for centuries and whose masks fill their halls. On Selvin they are the rising houses, families raised to the senatorial order within living memory, rich from grain, banking and the Imperial Offices, among them [[affiliation-mardovanes|House Mardovanes]] near the crown of the hill.

## Who Keeps Order

The houses keep their own order. Each has its doorkeepers and its men, and a quarrel between servants of two houses is settled by their stewards, usually before dinner. [[affiliation-citywatchorz|The City Watch]] keeps a watch-house on the Circle of Heroes and walks the lanes lightly, and its watchmen do not go through a house's door without the master's leave or a magistrate's writ. The ward's _peltros_ (ward magistrate) inspects the bearers' weights and the houses' water-pipes, and is very polite about both.

The Veramus of Works and Waters, who sits for [[affiliation-verdanorzara|Orzara Courts]], matters more on the Heights than any magistrate. The hills are fed by a branch of the aqueduct that climbs them through pressure-towers, and each house's pipe takes its allotted share. The Veramus sets the allotments.

## What Is Forbidden Here

- **Water above one's allotment.** A house that widens its pipe or taps the branch above its share is fined by the Veramus of Works and Waters. In a dry summer the Veramus cuts the pipe, and the house buys its water by the jar from the bearers for the rest of the year.
- **Armed men.** No house may keep more than twelve armed men within the thrima, the hallowed boundary inside which no legion marches. A house found keeping more is reported to the imperial tribunals in the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]], and the charge there is treason, whose sentence for a house is proscription: its master's death and its property forfeit to the throne.
- **A cart or a team.** Nothing on wheels larger than a handcart is allowed on the lanes. A cart found on them is taken to the watch-house and its owner fined by the _peltros_ before he may have it back. No house keeps a stable on the Heights without a license that costs as much as the stable, and an unlicensed beast is seized and sold outside the line.
- **A client in the wrong rank.** No law forbids it. A client who pushes into a rank above his own is not greeted, and the house's steward does not see him again.

## What the Heights Fear

The Heights fear a proscription. A name posted on the boards of the Platan District puts a house's property and its master's life at the disposal of whoever collects them, and the doors on Torvel have opened before now to men with a writ and a cart for the masks. With the old Orzas failing and the succession in doubt, every house on both hills is counting which claimant it stands behind and which claimant would post its name. The Heights also fear a dry summer, when Torvel's old cisterns run low before Selvin's new ones, and the oldest houses in the city buy water by the jar.

## Hooks

- **The morning call.** A client of a house on Torvel has been standing in the second rank for nine years and has never been moved up. He pays a party to learn what he would have to do to be greeted by name.
- **The pipe.** A house on Selvin has been drawing twice its water allotment all summer, and the house below it has been dry since midsummer. The dry house wants proof before it goes to the Veramus.
- **The dinner.** A party is invited to dine at a house on the Heights, and the steward who seats them puts them at the master's left, which is where the house puts the people it wants seen.

# The Truth of the Heights {#truth .secret}

The houses on Torvel are arming through the bearers' guild. No house may keep more than twelve armed men inside the thrima, and every old house on Torvel keeps eleven. Over the last two years four of them have also hired bearers: veterans of the legions, discharged and citizens, signed on through the bearers' guild to carry water and wood up the Torvel Stairs, and paid three times a bearer's wage. The guild's hall at the foot of the stairs keeps their carrying-poles on racks along one wall, iron-shod and the length of a legionary's spear, and the guild's master keeps a list of which veteran carries for which house.

The bearers number about four hundred, and between them they hold the only way up the Coronal. The houses that hired them have not told one another, and each believes it is the only one. [[affiliation-sorvan|The Imperial Guard]] has heard that the bearers' guild has grown, and has not yet asked why. The day the old Orzas dies, four hundred trained men with spear-length poles stand between the Torvel Stairs and the Platan, and nobody in the city has counted them as soldiers.
