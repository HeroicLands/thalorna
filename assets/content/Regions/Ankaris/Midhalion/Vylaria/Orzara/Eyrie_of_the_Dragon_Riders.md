## Overview

The Eyrie of the Dragon Riders is a set of galleries cut into the rock under the crown of [[place-arvel|Eagle Hill]], the highest of the [[place-sixhillsvyl|Six Hills]] of [[place-orzara|Orzara City]]. The [Dragon Riders]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} were quartered in it for nearly three centuries, and it has been sealed since the Republic refused to renew its lease with the dragons, about 45 BF ([[lore-ridersdisb|The Lease Refused]]). [[place-palvar|The Grand Palace]] stands on the rock above it, and [[affiliation-sorvan|The Imperial Guard]], the Orzas's guard, holds its door.

Nobody who is allowed to talk about the Eyrie has been inside it. A palace steward who takes new servants on their first walk round the terraces stops them at a railing and points down: "That door is the Eyrie. You will never be sent to it, you will never carry anything to it, and if anybody asks you about it, you have never seen it. The guardsmen at the door do not answer questions, and they do not like being asked them."

## Layers {#history}

The [Dragon Riders]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Republic were quartered in the galleries under Arvel for nearly three centuries. About 45 BF the Republic refused to renew its lease with the dragons, disbanded the Riders and sealed the Eyrie under its chancery's seal ([[lore-ridersdisb|The Lease Refused]]). The first Orzas walled the crown of the hill above it, the [[place-palvar|Palvar]] rose on the rock, and the Sorvan has stood at the door since.

## The Door

The Eyrie has one door that anyone can point to. It is set in the north face of the hill, inside the ring of the [Inner Wall]{.name data-type="place/structure"} of [[place-arvon|Inner City]] and below the palace terraces, at the end of a short paved court that nothing else opens onto. It is a plain round-headed arch cut in the living rock, a little higher than a tall man and wide enough for two to pass, closed by two leaves of bronze gone dark green with age. A band of lead runs across the join of the leaves, stamped with the seal of the Republic's chancery, and the seal is whole.

Two guardsmen of the Sorvan stand at the court's mouth day and night. The post is the dullest in the palace, and the Sorvan gives it to men it trusts. No order book in the guard's keeping says why the post exists, and the officer of the watch reads it into the duty roster every morning in the same words: "the door under the hill."

From the [Platan District]{.name data-type="place/site"} below, a sharp eye can pick out a second mark on the hill's face, a darker patch of stone halfway down the north slope where an opening has been filled with masonry of a different color. The palace engineers call it the old vent, and it is too small for a man to crawl through.

## What Orzara Says of It

The city has had nearly eight centuries to make up stories about the hill, and it has made up many.

- **The egg.** The commonest telling in the city holds that the Republic sealed a dragon's egg under Arvel when the Riders were disbanded, and that it will hatch on the day the empire falls. Nurses use it to quiet children and orators to frighten the Senate. The Republic's own chancery entered the thing it kept under a word that means _egg_, and the city knows the word if not the line.
- **The sleeping mount.** Palace servants tell each other that the last dragon of the corps lies asleep in the hill, and that the floors of the lowest kitchens are warm in winter because of it.
- **The wrong size.** The Helionite academies cite the Eyrie in their argument that there were never any dragons: the door is a man's door, and the vent is a man's vent, and nothing with wings goes through either. Their scholars put the point in print, and a few have come to Orzara to measure the door from the court's mouth.
- **The drakes.** The countryside of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan People]] holds that the mounts were great mountain drakes that men called dragons, and that the Eyrie was a stable for drakes. Its tellers have never seen the door.

## Who Wants In {#present}

The Sorvan keeps the door, and does not say on whose order. [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]] has never asked to open it, and its masters change the subject when it is raised. Antiquaries of [[affiliation-panepistmm|The Academy]] petition the palace every few years for leave to enter, and the petitions are received, acknowledged and filed. A few families in the provinces claim descent from the Riders and keep a name, a ring or a piece of harness that they say came out of the hill before it was shut.

This year a Helionite scholar has leave to measure the door for a book that will say there were never any dragons, and a chamberlain of the palace who wants no foreigner writing about the hill has asked the Sorvan to see that he never finishes. A guardsman of the door-post is deep in debt to a moneylender of the Danvar.

## Trouble Under the Hill {#hooks}

- **The night's lease.** A guardsman of the door-post, deep in debt to a moneylender of [[affiliation-danvar|The Bankers' Guild]], offers to sell a party one night inside the court and the loan of his key to the outer bar. He does not have a key to the seal, and he does not mention it.
- **The broken seal.** The officer of the watch finds a hairline crack in the lead band across the door one morning, beside a stamp that is still unbroken. He must decide whether to report it, to whom, and what it means if somebody on the other side made it.
- **The measurer.** A Helionite scholar has leave to measure the door and wants a party to escort him to it and keep him alive while he does. His book will say there were never any dragons, and a chamberlain of the palace, who wants no foreigner writing about the hill at all, has asked the Sorvan to see that he never finishes.
- **The rider's ring.** A provincial family brings to the capital a signet it says belonged to a Rider, cut with a device nobody in the heralds' office can place, and asks the Sorvan to compare it with whatever is behind the door.

# The Truth of the Eyrie {#truth .secret}

> [!TIP]
> Describe the rooms and let the players do the arithmetic. The Eyrie's finding is its size, and it lands hardest when a player says it first.

Behind the bronze leaves a stair of sixty steps goes down through the rock to a single long gallery, and every room of the Eyrie opens off it. All of them are cut for men.

- **The quarters.** Thirty cells along the gallery's eastern side, each with a stone bed-shelf, a niche for a lamp and a peg for a cloak. A refectory with one long table and a cistern.
- **The tack-room.** Racks of saddles of a make no horse has ever worn: high-backed, built on frames of bent ash and bronze, with girth-straps that lie coiled on the floor in loops of thirty and forty feet. Harness for something very large hangs on pegs in a room whose door a man must stoop to pass.
- **The chapel.** A plain room with an altar and no image on it. On the wall behind it the corps' roll is cut in the stone, the name of every Rider in the order of his oath, column after column. The last column stops halfway down the wall.
- **The strongroom.** At the gallery's far end, behind a second door of bronze with its own seal, a small square room is cut to fit one thing: a stone bed with four bronze rings at its corners for carrying-poles, sized for a chest that two men could lift. The floor from the door to the bed is worn smooth in a narrow path. The room is empty.

The "old vent" opens off the tack-room and was a light-shaft. There is no other way in, and no room or passage anywhere in the Eyrie that anything larger than a man could have entered.

No dragon was ever housed under Arvel. The dragons were parties to a bargain, not beasts in a stable, and where they lodged between campaigns is in no record. The Eyrie housed the men who rode them, the harness the men carried out to them, and the chest. The chest held what the Republic carried out of Theradon ([[lore-dragondead|The Dragon Dead at Theradon]]): the dragons' dead, bound and kept in bronze jars, which is the thing the dragons served for and the thing the Riders were quartered beside. When the chest left the strongroom, and where it is now, nothing in the Eyrie shows, and the hold it stood for is still in force.
