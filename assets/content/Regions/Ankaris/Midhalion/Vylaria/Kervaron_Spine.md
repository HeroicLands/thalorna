## Overview

The Kervaron Spine is the great arc of high mountains that closes the western frontier of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], between the Vilérian peninsula and the eastern reaches of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre Region]] and the northeastern marches of [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia Region]]. It is snow on the peaks, forest in the deep valleys, glacier-fed rivers, and high pastures that bloom for two months of the year. Its dozen narrow passes are the only practicable crossings from east to west, and each of them is held by a clan of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan People]].

A mule-train master who has taken Vilérian goods over the Kervaron every summer of his working life explains the arrangement to the factors who hire him: "[[affiliation-seldan|The Vilérian Senate]], the Senate, lists the range as imperial territory, and you will see the boundary-stones at the passes to prove it. Do not mention them to the clans. You pay the passage-gift at the first camp, they see you through, and nobody calls it a toll or a treaty. That is the whole law up there."

Two claims stand over the same mountains. The imperial chancery has listed the Kervaron as Vilérian since the early [Vilérian Republic]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, and has never governed a valley of it. The Áelendan, the eastern clans of the [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar People]], hold the high peaks to be the dwellings of [[affiliation-firstgods|The First Gods]] and the great glacier-springs to be the sources from which the world's first waters poured. The clans recognize no Vilérian claim, and they have turned back every column sent to enforce one.

## The Passes and the Passage-Gift

A train bound west climbs out of the foothills of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]] and [[place-moktur|Moktur]] on a paved imperial road that stops at the last boundary-post, where the paving ends in a single worn step. Above it a mule-track winds up through beech and then fir to the first camp, a cluster of stone huts and summer tents in a meadow below the pass, where the clan that holds the pass keeps its herds in season. The passage-gift is paid there, in coin, cloth or steel, and the train is not searched. A clan escort walks it over the pass and down the far side as far as the clan's own pastures reach, and from there it is the next clan's road, or the road down to [[place-kavrenath|Kávrenath]] and the toll at its gate.

The gift buys three things: the right to pass, a guide who knows which snow-bridges will bear a mule, and news. The clans watch the [[being-lthgnt|Lithogiant]] that walks the length of the range, and when it comes toward a pass, the clan that holds it closes the pass and moves its herds off the high pastures. A train that has paid learns where the walker is. A train that has not learns the hard way, if it learns at all.

The passes open when the snow lets them, usually late in spring, and close with the first heavy fall of autumn. A caravan caught on the wrong side of a pass by an early snow winters in the clan's valley at the clan's pleasure, and the factors in [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] count the passage-gift as cheaper than that.

## The Boundary-Stones

At each of the principal passes stands a Vilérian boundary-stone, a squared pillar a little taller than a man, cut with the chancery's lettering and the eagle of a legion. The legionary expeditions of [[lore-westernwars|The Western Wars]] planted them, and the chancery's list of the empire's territories cites them still. The clans leave them standing and treat them as ritual objects of obscure foreign meaning. Some carry ribbons or a smear of butter at the base, and none has been cut again since it was set. The lithogiant has walked through two of them and left nothing standing, and the clans regard this as a comment.

## The Spring-Stones

Higher up, at the heads of the glacier-springs, stand the spring-stones, which are Áelendan work from top to bottom. They are cut, dressed and set by Áelendan hands, carved in the [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thurarth Script]] runes with the name of the spring and of the river-mother who is seated in it, and every clan child is taught to read them aloud. They stand at the springs, never at the passes, and no clan confuses them with the legion's pillars.

The clan that holds a spring renews its stone every year at the thaw. The elders climb to the spring head before dawn on the first clear morning after the snow-line lifts, re-cut whatever runes the winter has worn, and leave at the foot of the stone a thing the giver made and would miss: a carved spoon, a belt, a child's first weaving. The rite is done in silence, and a stranger who comes on it is asked to wait below until it is finished. Most Vilérian merchants never see a spring-stone. The few chancery surveyors who have reached one have entered it in their reports as "an Áelendan marker of the boundary", which it is not.

## The Twelfth's Ground

Below the passes on the Vilérian side lies [[place-twelfthcamp|The Twelfth's Last Camp]], the fort where the rear cohorts of the [Doran Hilvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"} wintered when the regent's council sent the legion up into the range ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]). The Áelendan let the legion climb, closed the passes behind it, and waited for the snow. The legion's eagle never came down. Since then no Vilérian merchant has haggled over the passage-gift, and the chancery has not raised the range with the clans since [[lore-crownssettle|The Settlement of the Crowns]].

## The Names

The Áelendan name the range only in their own tongue and rarely share the name with outsiders; it is said to mean _"the bones of the world's first dawn."_ **Kervaron** is an old chancery word whose original meaning is disputed and most often rendered _"the stag-walked place."_ The Pelwar of Élavendre, who descend from the same root population as the Áelendan and hold the range in comparable reverence, use a Pelwar form of the Áelendan name and quietly back their tribal cousins in anything that touches the Kervaron. The chancery knows it and has not raised the matter diplomatically in nearly a century.

## From the Tarvénian Side

From [[place-asturath|Asturath]] the Kervaron is the eastern wall of the kingdom, and the Empire lies beyond it. The road west from the passes comes down out of the clans' pastures to [[place-kavrenath|Kávrenath]], three days below them, where three valleys meet under a fortress facing east. A train that has paid its passage-gift to the clans pays again at Kávrenath's gate, and the Astúrathi count the range as the clans' in all but the Empire's paperwork.

The fortress is the seat of the king of Asturath's Warden of the March, [Estaril Dómivar]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who holds powers of war and justice along the road that no lord holds in the interior. His garrison keeps a chain of watch-posts up the valley toward the passes. Not everything that comes over them is lawful. Smugglers go round the gate by the valley paths, imperial deserters come over to sell their swords to the crossbow companies of the valleys, and in a bad year on the Vilérian side refugees come down the road in hundreds.

The Áelendan count the Kervaron and the [[place-toravarrange|Toravar Range]], which closes Tarvénia's north and west, as two arms of the same sacred uplift.

## Converging Interests {#present}

The clans hold the passes, and the empire lists them. The [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] counts the range as imperial territory on the strength of its boundary-stones, the chancery has not pressed the claim since the Twelfth was lost, and Vilérian trains cross by paying the passage-gift at the first camp below each pass. The steel factors need their mules over the range before the autumn fairs. A spring-stone has gone from its spring while a dealer in Orzara sells a carved stone with Áelendan runes as a relic of the First Gods, and the clan that lost it has closed its pass to every Vilérian train. A young chancery surveyor has orders to re-measure the boundary-stones, all of them, and the clans have not been told he is coming.

## Trouble in the Kervaron {#hooks}

- **The walker in the pass.** The lithogiant is coming toward a pass in the middle of the season, and the clan has closed it. A factor with a cargo of steel that must be over the range before the autumn fairs offers a party double wages to take the mules over anyway.
- **The taken stone.** A spring-stone has gone from its spring, and a dealer in Orzara is selling a carved stone with Áelendan runes on it as a relic of the First Gods. The clan that lost it has closed its pass to every Vilérian train until the stone comes back.
- **The surveyor.** A young chancery surveyor arrives with orders to re-measure the boundary-stones and enter their condition. He means to do it properly, which means visiting all of them, and the clans have not been told he is coming.

# The Truth of the Kervaron {#truth .secret}

The chancery counts twelve passes, and so do the caravan masters. The clans use a thirteenth. It is a high summer route over the ice between two of the listed passes, too steep for a mule and open for perhaps six weeks a year, and no clan escort has ever taken a stranger over it. It is how the clans close a pass behind a column: while a legion climbs the listed road, a war-band goes over the ice and comes down behind it, and the pass is shut from above before the column's rear knows anyone is there. It is also how Áelendan war-bands have come down into the Vilérian frontier districts in retaliation and gone home again without passing a single boundary-stone.
