## Overview

Volkemara, the harbor town, is one of [[place-salkhalon|Salkalon]]'s four great fishing towns, with about eight thousand people, and its harbor is crowded with the boats and the curing-sheds that supply fresh and salted fish to the capital and the interior provinces. It lives by the catch and the fickle sea, and its people keep to the old coastal customs and a wary devotion to the sea's moods.

In a hard winter the price of Volkemara's salt fish is felt as far inland as the capital's markets, and the town's net-masters and curing-houses wield quiet weight in the provincial fish trade. A net-master explains the weather to a newcomer in terms of money: "A good month at sea is a cheap month in [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. A bad one, and the capital pays us for the sea's temper."

## Arriving

Volkemara lies in a shallow bay behind a spit of shingle, and its harbor is the spit's inner curve: no mole, no quay, only a long beach where the boats are drawn up bow to the land in rows of a hundred, each boat painted with an eye on either side of its stem. The curing-sheds stand behind the boats in long open rows, roofed and walled on the windward side, and the fish are split, salted and stacked in them under weighted boards. The town rises behind the sheds in whitewashed houses with blue doors, and over every door hangs a dried fish, head down, which is the town's old charm against drowning.

At the end of the spit stands the [Weather Stone]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, a pillar of black rock taller than a man, cut with the marks of the winds. Every morning before dawn the harbor's Priest of Storms of [[affiliation-brazemas|the Faith of Brazemas]] climbs the shrine-steps beside it, reads the sky and the water, and hangs a cloth on the stone: white for an open sea and black for a closed one. On a white morning the beach empties in an hour. On a black one no boat goes out, and the town mends nets.

## What Volkemara Lives By

The boats fish the grounds within a day's row, with drift-nets by night and lines by day. Each boat belongs to a net-master, who owns its nets and its share of the grounds, and its crew of six take a share of the catch by custom: two parts to the boat, one to each man. The catch comes up the beach at dawn in baskets, and the women of the curing-houses split and salt it before noon, with salt from [[place-thremuzara|Thremuzara]]. The best of it goes fresh to the inland towns by fast cart, packed in wet weed, and the rest is salted, barreled and sold through the factors of [[place-mercavia|Delalara]] to the capital and the legions.

The net-masters are a dozen families who have held the town's boats and grounds for generations, and they sell together. Each winter they meet in their hall by the beach and agree the price of the salt fish they will release, and the factors pay it. In a month of black cloths the barrels in the sheds are worth twice as much.

The town has one rule older than its charter. A man the sea takes belongs to the sea, and his death is not asked after: the Priest of Storms gives his name to the water from the Weather Stone, his widow takes his share of the boat, and nobody speaks of how he went.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Volkemara is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provincvld|Province of Salkalon]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The council is the net-masters, and its business is the grounds: which boats fish where, and what a boat that fishes another's ground owes. The Priest of Storms is not of the council, but no council meets on a black morning without him.

Quarrels on the beach go to the net-master whose boat is in them, and the net-masters settle them among themselves. A thief of fish is made to carry baskets up the beach for a month for the man he robbed. Nothing in Volkemara reaches the provincial courts if the net-masters can help it.

## The Present {#present}

The net-masters keep the grounds to themselves, and the grounds are being pressed. A boat with no eye on her stem fishes the town's grounds by night. The salt boat from Thremuzara is a week overdue and the catch is spoiling in the sheds. A net-master drowned in the autumn gales, the town keeps its rule that the sea's dead are not asked after, and his widow has refused his share of the boat.

## Where a Party Lodges

The [Painted Eye]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, at the head of the beach, is the town's inn, kept by the widow of a net-master, and its rooms smell of salt and smoke whatever the season. Factors' agents rent the rooms above the curing-sheds for the winter. A party willing to row can be taken onto a boat short of a man and paid a share.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The salt boat.** The salt boat from Thremuzara has not come in for a week, the catch is spoiling in the sheds, and the boat's master sent no word. The net-masters hire a party to go down the coast and learn why.
- **The stranger's boat.** A boat with no eye on her stem has been fishing the town's grounds by night, and the net-masters want her found and her owner named.
- **The widow's share.** A net-master was drowned in the autumn gales, and his widow has refused to take his share of the boat. She asks a party, quietly, to find out who was with him the night he went out.

# The Truth of Volkemara {#truth .secret}

The sea did not take [Tholmaros Iltheris]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. He was the youngest of the net-masters and the only one who had sold anything without the others: in the summer he agreed to sell his boats and his share of the grounds to the [Venthelis]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} shipping house of Delalara, which wanted a fishery of its own to supply its ships. The Venthelis would have brought decked boats from Delalara onto the town's grounds, and their catch would have gone to the house's factors at the house's price, outside the net-masters' agreement. The other net-masters asked him to tear the contract up, and he would not.

On the first night of the autumn gales three net-masters rowed out with him to bring in his nets, and came back without him. The next morning the Priest of Storms gave his name to the water from the Weather Stone, as the custom requires, and the town did not ask. The priest does not know what happened in the boat. He knows that the three men came to his door with the dead man's name before their boat was drawn up on the beach, and he has decided not to wonder.

The widow, [[being-nizelenilth|Nizelen Iltheris]], has the contract. It was signed and sealed before her husband died, and under it the Venthelis house owns his boats and his grounds from the day the money is paid. She has not told the house that she has it, because the net-masters have offered her twice his share to burn it, and because she wants to know which of the three held him under before she decides.
