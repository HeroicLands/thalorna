## Overview {#overview}

Thremuzara is one of [[place-salkhalon|Salkalon]]'s four principal fishing towns, a town of about six thousand built beside the broad coastal salt-pans that give it its name. Salt and salt-fish are its livelihood. It cures the catch of half the lesser villages along this stretch of coast, and its salt feeds the curing-sheds from [[place-volkhemara|Volkemara]] to [[place-braldvik|Bralvik]].

A woman who has worked the curing-houses since girlhood shows a new hand the line of barrels and says why the work comes to Thremuzara: "Every village on this coast has fish. We have the salt, so they bring the fish to us." Control of the salt-pans makes Thremuzara's handful of pan-owning families quietly powerful far beyond the town's modest size.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The pans are seen before the town: a flat country of shallow square basins along the shore, mile after mile of them, divided by low banks of clay and fed by a single channel from the sea. In summer the basins are every color from gray-green to pink to white as the water thickens, and the light off them hurts the eyes at noon. The salt is raked into heaps along the banks, each heap as tall as a man and capped with reed thatch against the rain, and the heaps run in lines toward the town like the tents of an army.

The town stands on a low rise at the landward edge of the pans, a cluster of flat roofs round a harbor too shallow for anything bigger than a coasting boat. The curing-houses line the harbor, and the boats of the lesser villages come in every morning to unload into them. Above the curing-houses, on the highest ground, stand the five houses of the pan-owning families, each with a walled yard where its salt is weighed and stored, and each with a tower from which its steward can see the whole of its pans.

## What Thremuzara Lives By

The pans work from late spring to the first autumn rains. The sea is let into the outer basins through a sluice at high tide and moved inward from basin to basin as the sun thickens it, and in the innermost basins the salt forms in a crust that the rakers draw off with wooden rakes every few days. The rakers are hired men and slaves, and in high summer they work bare-legged in the brine from dawn until the heat stops them. The salt is the town's to sell to the whole coast, and the boats that bring fish in go home with salt.

The fishing villages bring what they cannot cure themselves. The curing-houses split and salt it, pack it in barrels with Thremuzara salt and sell it on through the factors of [[place-mercavia|Delalara]], and the village takes a share of the price by custom. A village that sells its fish elsewhere pays more for its salt the next season, which is why few do.

The salt is taxed at the heap. Each spring a clerk of the province's tax-farmer, the _tolar_ of [[affiliation-tolvan|the Tolvan]], walks the banks and counts the heaps of every family, and the family pays by the count. The families build their heaps tall.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

Thremuzara is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provincvld|Province of Salkalon]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors), and the five pan-owning families fill both. The first of them is the [Brazemen]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a provincial family named in the old way for its estate, whose mistress, [[being-dorandabraz|Doranda Brazemen]], has run the largest pans on the coast for twenty years. She sets the price of salt each spring, the other four families follow her, and the villages pay it.

Each family keeps order on its own pans, with stewards, overseers and watchmen, and a raker who steals salt is beaten by the overseer and dismissed. A quarrel between villages over the curing-houses goes to the _tavemoi_. The province's cohort is never seen here, and the town's council prefers it so.

## The Present {#present}

The pan families hold the coast's salt and mean to keep it. Doranda Brazemen sets the price each spring, and a lesser village has begun selling its fish to a Delalara house that brings its own salt by sea, which the five families want stopped. The tax-farmer's clerk has found a heap that is salt outside and clay inside. A raker who ran from the Brazemen pans in the spring is posted as a runaway slave and swears he was born free.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The [Salt Barrel]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, by the harbor, is where the village boatmen drink while their catch is weighed, and it has four rooms upstairs for factors' agents and the clerk who counts the heaps. A party with business with one of the families is lodged in that family's house, and is shown the pans from the tower whether or not it asks.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The tall heap.** The tax-farmer's clerk has found a heap that is salt on the outside and clay inside. He wants a party to learn which family built it, quietly, so that he can be paid to forget it.
- **The village that sold elsewhere.** One of the lesser villages has begun selling its fish to a Delalara house that brings its own salt by sea. The pan families want the house's salt-ship stopped, and the village wants protection from the pan families.
- **The raker who ran.** A raker escaped from the Brazemen pans in the spring, and the family has posted a reward for him as a runaway slave. He reaches the party first and swears he was born free.

# The Truth of Thremuzara {#truth .secret}

Some of the slaves on the pans were born free. The pans need more hands every summer than the town can hire, and slaves are dear, so for six years Doranda Brazemen has bought rakers from the slaver [Devorin]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who works from a hidden dock at Delalara and sells cheaply because he does not buy. His men take sailors' children, runaways and strangers off the waterfront of Delalara and the lesser harbors, and he sells them with bills of sale drawn as debt-slavery, sealed by a notary in Delalara who is paid to seal anything. A man sold that way can be freed by any court that hears him, so the Brazemen overseers keep the bought rakers on the outermost pans, where no visitor goes, and never let them into the town.

The other four families have bought from Devorin too, fewer and more carefully. They know where the Brazemen rakers come from because they know where their own come from, and none of them can name the trade without naming itself. The raker who ran is one of Devorin's, a Delalara boy taken from the quays at twelve, and he can name the dock.

The salt boat that supplies Volkemara is late because its master saw a boat of Devorin's unloading at the outer pans by night, and the Brazemen stewards have kept him in Thremuzara since, as a guest, until they decide what to do with him.
