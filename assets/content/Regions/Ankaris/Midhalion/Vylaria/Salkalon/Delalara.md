## Overview

Delalara is the busiest commercial harbor still under imperial control. With about fifty thousand people it is the largest city of [[affiliation-provincvld|Salkalon]] and its provincial capital, the trading face the province shows to the sea.

A Taruzelian factor who has kept a counting-room in its foreign quarter for twenty years describes the quays to a newly arrived partner: "Merchantmen two deep along the wharves, warehouses full of goods bound inland and abroad, and our quarter and the Helionite one a short walk apart. The shipping houses of Salkalon keep their counting-rooms here, and since the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]]' secession squeezed their eastern trade they have been loud about wanting it back."

Delalara is the most cosmopolitan city outside the capital, and the loudest voice in the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|empire]] for engagement with [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] rather than enmity. Its foreign quarters hold Taruzelian and [[place-helionis|Helionite]] factors. The provincial _therkos_ (governor) sits here, but real power is shared with the merchant houses whose money the province runs on.

## Arriving

Most people arrive by sea, and a ship's first sight of Delalara is the [Mole Light]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a square stone tower at the end of the [Long Mole]{.name data-type="place/structure"} that keeps a fire burning in an iron basket every night of the sailing year. The mole runs out from the shore for half a mile in a long arm of fitted blocks, and it shelters a harbor that holds two hundred hulls in a good season. Inside it the water is crowded and dirty, loud with oars and gulls, and every ship that enters takes a harbor boat to its berth. Merchantmen lie two deep along the quays as the factor says, the inner hull made fast to the stone and the outer one to her, and a crew whose ship is outside crosses the other's deck with every bale.

The quays run for a mile round the curve of the bay, and the city rises behind them in tiers of pale stone and red tile up a slope of low hills. Along the waterfront stand the warehouses, long and windowless, each with a crane on its roof and a shipping house's mark over its door, and the smell of the harbor is tar, fish, wet rope and pepper. In the middle of the quays stands the [Customs House]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a hall of gray stone with an arcade open to the water. Every cask, bale and jar that crosses the wharf passes under that arcade, where the clerks of the customs read the manifest, open what they please and chalk a mark on what they have taxed. A ship's master learns on his first visit that nothing leaves the quay without the chalk.

By land the city is reached on the imperial road from [[place-orzara|Orzara]], which comes down through the vineyards of the hills and enters by the [Orzara Gate]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, and by the coast road from [[place-castamar|Kastamar]]. The landward city is plainer than the waterfront and older. Its forum stands on the first rise above the harbor, with the [Governor's House]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on its north side, the provincial temple of the [[affiliation-imperialcult|Imperial Cult]] facing it, and along its east side the [Colonnade]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a double row of columns where the shipping houses meet every morning to buy and sell cargoes, ships and shares in voyages. The temples of [[affiliation-olvenia|Ólvenia]] and [[affiliation-pozeras|Pozeras]] stand together at the colonnade's end, joined by a single porch, and a captain sailing east makes his offering at both on the same morning.

The two foreign quarters lie on either side of the harbor's eastern end, where the quays are oldest. The [Eastern Quarter]{.name data-type="place/feature"} is the Taruzelians': a few streets of tall counting-houses round a small walled harbor of their own, with Taruzeli on the shop signs and the house marks of the islands on the doors. The [Helionite Quarter]{.name data-type="place/feature"} is a short walk west, round a square with a fountain and a lecture hall, and its factors deal in oil, books and the hire of captains. On the quays between them the language is [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]], the dock-talk of the whole sea, and a Delalara porter who speaks nothing else can unload a ship from any harbor of it.

## What Delalara Lives By

Delalara lives by carrying other people's goods and taxing them on the way. The wine and oil of [[place-hylen|Hilen]], the wool and leather of the estate country, the steel, glass and finished goods of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]] and the salt fish of the coast come down to its warehouses by road and coastal boat, and go out in its ships. What comes in is the trade of the sea: the grain of the western kingdoms, Helionite oil and manuscripts, and from the east the spices, cloth, silk and Khelathi goods that reach the islands of Taruzel by caravan and ferry. The shipping houses own the ships, hire the crews, buy the cargoes and sell them on, and most of the city works for them as clerks, porters, coopers, rope-makers, ship-chandlers, sailmakers, pilots and the women who mend nets and sails on the quays.

The houses are a commoner trade grown rich, and the richest of them carry the propertied order's rank and sit in the town's council. They raise money for a voyage by selling shares in it at the Colonnade, a tenth of a cargo here and a twentieth there, so that a single ship bound east may belong to forty people. They insure one another against loss by the sea-loan, money lent on a ship and her cargo and repaid with high interest only if she comes home, and a loss reported in the Colonnade can ruin a dozen households by noon. The moneylenders of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] keep a chapterhouse behind the Colonnade and clear the houses' notes, and the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] keeps a counting house in the Eastern Quarter that does the same in Taruzelian credit.

The eastern trade is the city's whole argument. Before the war of independence Delalara's ships sailed to the Taruzelian harbors as to any other port of the empire, and paid nothing there but the imperial customs. Now every Delalara hull in a Taruzelian harbor pays the harbormaster's dues on her tonnage, a pilot's fee by her draft, and, if she goes on through the islands, the price of a place in a convoy under the [[place-straitfortress|Strait Fortress]] of [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]]. A shipping house reckons that the Taruzelian dues take a fifth of the profit of every eastern voyage, and it pays them because the goods of the east reach the sea through the islands and nowhere else. The houses keep a book of dues at the Colonnade, in which every captain home from the east enters what he paid and to whom, and they carry its totals to the Governor's House each year as their case for a trade treaty with the Confederation.

That case is what [[place-orzara|Orzara]] calls collaboration. To the Senate, [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], a Delalara house that pays a Taruzelian harbormaster is paying the rebels' navy, and a house that sells Vylarian goods in Taruzel is feeding the province that broke the empire's sea power. The houses answer that the dues are paid either way, and that a treaty would lower them. The admirals at [[place-castamar|Kastamar]] answer that the only treaty worth making is the one signed after a battle. Nothing moves the argument, and every year the houses' petition goes up the road to Orzara and the levy the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]] still draft for Taruzel comes down it.

The Taruzelians of the Eastern Quarter live in the middle of this by waiver. Since the war each Taruzelian factor in Delalara holds a waiver from the _therkos_, renewed every spring, that lets him keep his house, his clerks and his harbor in the city, and a factor whose waiver is refused has a month to leave. The houses depend on the factors for credit, news and introductions in the islands, and the factors depend on the houses for cargo. Every spring the renewals are delayed by a week or two while the Governor's House considers them, and every spring the city watches to see whose is late.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The _therkos_ holds the province for the emperor, commanding its garrison, its revenue and its courts. The present _therkos_ is [[being-krvthsrvthnds|Korvathas Revothandis]], a _kurthos_ (senator) of an old house of the capital in his second year, sent to Salkalon by a Senate that wanted the province governed by a man with no ships of his own. He holds an audience every morning in the hall of the Governor's House and is careful to receive the shipping houses and the admirals' officers on alternate days. He has renewed every Taruzelian waiver so far, a fortnight late.

The province's officers keep their offices round the forum:

- **The _karezos_** (revenue officer) watches the imperial revenue for the emperor and reports past the _therkos_. In Delalara his business is the customs, which are collected for the treasury by a tax-farmer, a _tolar_ of [[affiliation-tolvan|the Tolvan]] who has bid for the right to collect them. The present holder of the Delalara customs is [Solteras Tharozates]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, of a house raised from the tax-farmers within living memory. His clerks are the men in the Customs House arcade, and every chalk mark on the quay is money in his pocket above the sum he bid.
- **The _tarkelos_** (treasurer) keeps the provincial treasury in strongrooms under the Governor's House.
- **The _zoralus_** (keeper of the peace) commands the urban cohorts. One is quartered by the Orzara Gate and one on the quays, where it guards the Customs House and walks the waterfront at night.
- **The _vandraks_** (legion commander) commands the legion stationed in the province from its camp inland, and comes to the city on the state's days.
- **The _pozerus_** (warden of the roads) keeps the imperial road to Orzara and the coast road, and the post-house by the Orzara Gate.
- **The _zalvos_ of the province** presides over the cult's rites in the provincial temple. On the state's days the shipping houses make the offering before the Orzas's image in order of their rank, and a house that comes late to the incense is talked about at the Colonnade.

The fleet does not answer to the _therkos_. Its _vormeks_ (admiral) at Kastamar answers to Orzara, and the province's three powers, the merchants, the admiralty and the customs, meet in the Governor's House only when the _therkos_ asks them.

Delalara is also a chartered town, with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The shipping houses fill the council, and the _tavemoi_ are by custom the masters of two of them. The council keeps the harbor, appoints its harbor-master and his pilots, and charges the town's own berth-fees on every ship at the quays. The first voice in it is [Ornetha Venthelis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a widow of fifty who has run the [Venthelis]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} shipping house since her husband's ship was lost in the war, and who carries the book of dues to the Governor's House each year herself.

Order on the waterfront rests on the houses. Each house keeps quay-masters and watchmen for its own warehouses and berths, and a theft from a house's warehouse is settled by its quay-master, who usually knows who did it before the owner knows what is missing. A fight between two crews goes to the harbor-master, who fines both ships. A stranger robbed on the quays goes to a _tavemos_'s bench in the forum. Only a riot, an attack on the Customs House or a crime against the foreign quarters brings the cohort, and it comes with clubs.

## The Present {#present}

Delalara trades with the enemy and is governed by a man chosen to have no ships. The _therkos_, Korvathas Revothandis, has renewed every Taruzelian waiver so far, a fortnight late, and one factor's waiver has not come this spring at all while three houses owe him money. Ornetha Venthelis is sending the houses' treaty petition to Orzara by a trusted hand, and the admirals' men at the Orzara Gate watch who carries it. The customs farmer, Solteras Tharozates, bid more than any _tolar_ before him and collects accordingly, and a Hilen wine merchant has paid twice on one cargo. A ship reported lost off the Tamzir Shoals has been seen in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] repainted, and the slaver Devorin works from a dock no harbor-master admits to.

## Where a Party Lodges

The inns of the quays are for sailors, and a party that wants to sleep goes up the hill. The [Two Anchors]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, on the street that climbs from the Customs House to the forum, is the inn of masters and supercargoes, and its common room is where a captain looks for a crew and a passenger looks for a ship. Its keeper lets rooms by the week and keeps the sailing-lists of every house in a book behind the counter. In the Helionite Quarter the house of a widow by the fountain lets rooms to scholars and travelers, serves the best food in the city and asks to know where her guests are going.

A party with business in the Eastern Quarter can lodge there, at a Taruzelian lodging-house within the quarter's walls, where the rooms are clean, the prices are written on the door in two scripts and the keeper reports every guest to the factors. Captain [[being-nebralenelth|Nebralen Elthezis]] of the _[Tempest's Mercy]{.name data-type="vehicle"}_ takes passengers when his holds are light and asks no more questions of them than they ask of him.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The cask taxed twice.** A wine merchant of Hilen has been charged the customs twice on the same cargo, once at the arcade and once at a warehouse door by men with the Customs House's chalk. He wants a party to find out whether the second set of clerks is Solteras Tharozates's or someone else's.
- **The late waiver.** One factor's waiver has not been renewed this spring, and his month is half gone. He asks a party to learn why from the Governor's House, and meanwhile to keep his ledgers safe from the three houses that owe him money.
- **The ship that was lost.** A Delalara ship reported lost off the [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]] last autumn ruined the forty shareholders of her voyage. A sailor back from [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] swears he saw her there in spring, repainted, under another name. The shareholders want her found, and the house that sold the sea-loans on her wants her to stay lost.
- **The hidden dock.** The slaver [Devorin]{.name data-type="being/npc"} works from a dock somewhere along the mile of quays that no harbor-master admits to. A sailor's child has gone missing from the waterfront, and her mother has heard that a party took his money last month.
- **The petition.** Ornetha Venthelis is sending the houses' petition for a trade treaty to Orzara by a trusted hand rather than by the post, because last year's reached the Senate a month late and opened. She hires a party to carry it, and the admirals' men at the Orzara Gate know the party's faces by morning.

# The Truth of Delalara {#truth .secret}

The harbor pays Taruzelian dues at home as well as abroad. When the Delalara customs came up for bidding three years ago, Solteras Tharozates bid more for them than any _tolar_ had ever offered, because a rival house of tax-farmers in Orzara meant to take them and his house's standing rested on keeping them. He did not have the money. He borrowed the whole of the bid from the Auric Compact's counting house in the Eastern Quarter, through a Helionite partner whose name stands on the contract, and pledged the customs themselves as the security. Every quarter, out of what his clerks chalk on the quay, the interest goes to the Compact, and every week the Compact's master of the counting house, [Lamuna Pilmuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, reads a fair copy of the customs ledger: every cargo that crosses the wharf, whose it is, what it is worth and where it is bound.

The Compact did not lend for the interest. It lent for the ledger, which tells the islands what the empire's busiest harbor ships, and for the leverage. If the Compact calls the loan, Tharozates cannot pay, his contract fails, and the province's largest revenue goes uncollected until the Tolvan finds another bidder, which in Salkalon would take a year. Lamuna has never threatened it. She has asked twice that a particular cargo be let through the arcade without being opened, and twice it was.

The _therkos_ does not know, and nor do the admirals, who would call the loan treason and be right in law. Ornetha Venthelis suspects. She has noticed that Tharozates's clerks never open the Eastern Quarter's goods, and she is trying to decide whether the knowledge is worth more to the houses as a weapon against the customs or as a secret kept for a Taruzelian banker who could ease the dues in the islands.
