## Overview

A shrine above the sea draws the faithful to Kurzalora, the altar height, a coastal pilgrimage town of about three thousand in [[place-salkhalon|Salkalon]]. It is quieter than the fishing towns and far quieter than the great ports, and it lives on the pilgrim trade: lodging-houses, votive-sellers, and the offerings left at the shrine.

A pilgrim who has made the walk up from the coast road to the shrine tells a first-timer to go in the morning, when the lodging-houses are still quiet and the climb is cool, and to buy the votive from the sellers at the top of the town rather than the bottom. The sellers at the bottom, she says, know exactly who is tired.

## Arriving

The coast road passes below the town at the foot of a steep green hill that ends in a cliff over the sea, and from the road the shrine is a white tower on the summit against the sky. The town climbs the landward slope in a single street of steps, with lodging-houses on either side, their doors painted with the god's lightning, and every few steps a votive-seller's stall hung with little ships, oars and anchors of tin, wood and wax. The climb takes an hour for a pilgrim and half that for the boys who carry water to the top.

At the summit stands the shrine of [[affiliation-brazemas|Brazemas]], the god of storms: an open court of white stone on the cliff edge, with no roof, because the god is worshipped under the sky, and an altar of rough black rock at its seaward end where the offerings are burned. Its walls are covered with votives: thousands of tin ships nailed up by sailors who came home, and here and there a carved plank from a boat that did not. Behind the court rises the [Vigil Tower]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, square and tall, older than the court, from whose top a man can see the sails of a day's run of coast in either direction on a clear day.

## What Kurzalora Lives By

Sailors climb to the height to make a vow before a voyage and to keep it after one, and the vows of the god of storms are binding: a sailor who promised a tin ship in a gale climbs with one when he is home, however far he has to come. The lodging-houses feed and house the pilgrims, the votive-sellers make what they offer, and the shrine takes a share of every offering for its upkeep. A shipping house of [[place-mercavia|Delalara]] that loses a ship sends a carved plank to the altar at its own cost, and the plank is burned with the house's name.

The town's great night is the [Thunderhead Vigil]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} at the start of spring, when the faithful of the whole coast climb to the height and wait on the cliff for the first storm of the year. When the first lightning strikes, the crowd cries out, and the Priest of Storms on the top of the Vigil Tower reads the strike for the sailing year. For three days before it the town holds five times its people, and the lodging-houses let floors, stairs and stables.

The shrine keeps one right of its own. A man who climbs to the altar and vows himself to the god's service is the god's for a year and a day, as an acolyte of the shrine, and no master, magistrate or officer may take him from the height while the year lasts. An old Orzas granted the right to the shrine, and the grant is cut on the altar stone.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Kurzalora is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provincvld|Province of Salkalon]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors), most of them keepers of lodging-houses. The town governs the street and the shrine governs the height. The shrine is kept by a Priest of Storms of the Faith of Brazemas, [Yalzema Kaldremis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a sailor's daughter of fifty who read the storms for the fleet at Kastamar before she was given the shrine, with a dozen acolytes under her.

The tower is the quarrel. The admiralty at [[place-castamar|Kastamar]] wants the Vigil Tower for a signal station, to watch the coast and pass word along it, and has asked for it three times in five years. Yalzema has refused each time, on the ground that the tower is the god's and his vigil is kept from its top. The admiral's officers say that a signal station would not stop the vigil. Yalzema says that marines on the height would stop something else, and does not say what.

## The Present {#present}

The admirals want the Vigil Tower, and the Priest of Storms will not give it. The admiralty at [[place-castamar|Kastamar]] has asked three times in five years for the tower as a signal station, and Yalzema Kaldremis has refused each time because the god's vigil is kept from its top. Now a party of marines and a surveyor has come to measure it with a letter of leave from the _therkos_, and Yalzema doubts that he ever signed it. A merchant captain who promised the god a silver ship in a gale has sent tin, and his crew will not sail with him.

## Where a Party Lodges

Every house on the street is a lodging-house, and a party has its choice of them outside the vigil. The [Tin Ship]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, at the top of the steps, is the best and dearest, and its windows look over the sea. The houses at the bottom are cheaper and their keepers sell votives too, at the prices the pilgrim warns of. A party that climbs to make a vow is offered bread and water at the shrine and a place to sleep in the lee of the court wall, for nothing.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The admiral's survey.** The admiralty has sent a party of marines and a surveyor to measure the Vigil Tower, with a letter from the _therkos_ (governor) granting them leave. Yalzema needs someone to read the letter and find out whether the _therkos_ ever signed it.
- **The plank with no name.** A carved plank has been left on the altar with no house's name on it, cut with a ship's name that no house in Delalara owns. The shrine wants to know whose loss it is before it burns it.
- **The vow unkept.** A merchant captain promised the god a silver ship in a gale and has come home and sent tin. His crew believe the next storm is his, and will not sail with him until a party gets the silver to the height.

# The Truth of Kurzalora {#truth .secret}

The height is a road out of the fleet. When the quarter is late at Kastamar and the oarsmen are paid in notes, some of them walk off the quay and up the coast road to Kurzalora, climb to the altar and vow themselves to the god. Under the grant they are the shrine's for a year and a day, and the marines who follow them can only stand at the bottom of the steps. For nine years Yalzema Kaldremis has not kept them for the year. Within a month of his vow each man goes down the landward side of the hill by night in a pilgrim's cloak, to a lodging-house in Delalara whose keeper finds him a berth on a merchant ship under another name. The shipping houses are always short of oarsmen, and the houses whose ships take the men pay the shrine a gift for each of them, which goes into the upkeep of the court.

Yalzema began it after a winter when the fleet hanged two deserters on the Kastamar mole. She reads storms, and she believes that a fleet that cannot pay its men has no right to keep them. The acolytes know, and so do the keepers of the lodging-houses at the bottom of the street, who watch the marines. The admiralty does not know where the men go, but it has counted how many have climbed the height and how few have come down it at the end of their year, and that is why it wants marines in the tower.
