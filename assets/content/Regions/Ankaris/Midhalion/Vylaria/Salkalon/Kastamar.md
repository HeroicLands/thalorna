## Overview

Kastamar is a working war-port of about twenty thousand people, the great naval city of [[place-salkhalon|Salkalon]] and the home of the imperial shipyards and of the command that keeps what remains of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]'s sea power. Its harbor is dry-docks and slipways, cordage-walks and timber-yards, marine barracks, and the offices of the officers of the Vormal, as the fleet is called in [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|the Vylarian military]].

A shipwright of the yards, showing a new apprentice the slips, makes the point about how the city lives: "Warships go down these ways when [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], the Senate, votes the money, and not before. When the money is late, the yard is quiet and so is every tavern in Kastamar." Where its sister-city [[place-mercavia|Delalara]] up the coast lives by trade, Kastamar lives by the fleet. Its fortunes rise and fall with imperial naval spending, and since the loss of [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] broke Vylarian supremacy in the eastern sea, that spending has been anxious and political. Discipline sets the city's tone, and a third of its people draw their bread, directly or not, from the navy.

## Arriving

The harbor of Kastamar is closed. Two moles of heavy stone reach toward each other across the mouth of a deep round bay and leave a gap a hundred yards wide between their towers, and at night a chain of iron is drawn across it from tower to tower. A merchantman comes in by day only, by the admiralty's leave, and is given a berth on the outer quay where the marines can see her. Inside the moles the bay is the fleet's, and the first thing a newcomer notices is how much of it is empty: a long quay built for sixty warships with fewer than twenty moored along it, their oars stowed and their decks swept.

The [Ship-Sheds]{.name data-type="place/structure"} line the inner shore, forty long halls of stone and timber built side by side down to the water, each roofed against the weather and open at the seaward end, each with a stone slipway inside it on which a galley is hauled up out of the sea to dry. A warship laid up in a shed keeps for years, and more than half the fleet is laid up. Behind the sheds stretch the yards: the timber-ponds where the trunks floated down from [[place-moktur|Moktur]] season under water, the sawpits, the [Ropewalk]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a covered alley a quarter of a mile long where the cordage for the whole fleet is twisted, and the pitch-houses, whose smoke hangs over the city on a still day.

The city climbs the slope above the yards in straight streets laid out like a legion's camp, with the marine barracks at the top and the [Admiralty]{.name data-type="place/structure"} below them, a square block of grey stone round a courtyard, from whose roof a signal mast talks to the watch-towers along the coast. On the wall beside the Admiralty gate hangs the [Roll of the Fleet]{.name data-type="miscgear"}, a board of painted wood on which every warship of the Vormal is lettered by name with her station beside it: at sea, at the quay, or in the sheds. The clerks repaint the stations each month, and on the first of the month the shipwrights' wives stand in front of the board to count the ships at sea, and so the work there will be.

## What Kastamar Lives By

Kastamar lives by building, repairing and manning warships, and by the money the empire sends to do it. The yards employ shipwrights, sawyers, caulkers, smiths, rope-makers, sailmakers and pitch-boilers, the admiralty employs clerks and storekeepers, and the fleet employs oarsmen, sailors and the marines who fight from its decks. The rest of the city lives on them: the taverns, the cookshops, the lodging-houses of the sailors' families, the moneylenders, and the temple of [[affiliation-brazemas|Brazemas]] on the harbor, where every crew makes its offering before it sails.

The fleet cannot pay for itself, and everyone in Kastamar knows it. The Senate votes the fleet's money each year and the treasury, [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]], sends it in quarters, and for twelve years the quarters have come late and short. A warship needs a hundred and seventy oarsmen, and the fleet hires its oarsmen by the season. When the quarter is late, the oarsmen are paid in the admiralty's notes, which the moneylenders of the waterfront buy at four-fifths of their face, and a ship whose crew will not take another note stays at the quay. The yards are paid the same way. The master shipwright keeps his men through a lean quarter by giving them work on the laid-up hulls, scraping and caulking ships that have no crews to sail them.

The war is the city's memory. The largest squadron the empire had ever sent into the eastern sea sailed from Kastamar twelve years ago and was destroyed at the [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]], with its admiral and most of its marines, and hardly a family on the slope above the yards lost no one. The names of the drowned are cut on the inner face of the eastern mole, and every year on the day of the battle the fleet's crews stand along it while the names are read.

The city keeps one more account. Each autumn a fishing boat from [[place-braldvik|Bralvik]] comes in under the moles, and its master hands the admiralty the [Boat-Bill]{.name data-type="lore"} for forty-one boats burned by a Kastamar squadron two centuries ago. The admiralty files it with the others and pays nothing.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Kastamar is a chartered town of the [[affiliation-provincvld|Province of Salkalon]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors), but the council governs the markets and the streets and little else. The harbor, the yards and most of the city's living belong to the fleet, and the fleet answers to Orzara, not to the _therkos_ (governor) at Delalara.

The fleet is commanded by its _vormeks_ (admiral), [Tharkas Gurimandis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a _kurthos_ (senator) of a house with a long naval line, who has held the command for five years. He is sixty, upright and patient, he lost a son at the Tamzir Shoals, and he wants the war with Taruzel resumed. He says so in every letter he sends to the Senate and in every audience he grants, and the officers who dine at his table say so after him. To the shipping houses of Delalara he is the man who would sink their trade for a battle. To his crews he is the admiral who gets them paid, sooner or later, which is more than the Senate does.

Under him the admiralty runs the city's life. Its clerks keep the Roll of the Fleet and the muster of every ship, its storekeepers issue the timber, cordage and pitch, and the master shipwright, [Kaldremos Holimandis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a commoner who began as a sawyer's boy in the yards, decides which ships are built, which are mended and which are left in the sheds. The marines keep order on the harbor and in the yards, and a theft from the yards is a crime against the fleet, judged by the admiral's officers and punished at the mast. In the town a quarrel between sailors goes to the bosuns of their ships, and only a fight between a sailor and a townsman reaches a _tavemos_.

## Politics {#present}

The admiral wants a war the fleet cannot pay for. Tharkas Gurimandis writes to the Senate for the war with Taruzel in every letter, and his officers say so after him. Half his warships lie laid up in the sheds for want of crews, and the oarsmen of the rest are paid in notes that a waterfront moneylender buys at half their face from men who believe the next quarter is not coming. A shipping house of Delalara offers to hire three laid-up warships with their crews to escort its convoys east, and the admiral will not lend the fleet to merchants. Short of oarsmen for the summer, he is weighing an offer to settle Bralvik's Boat-Bill in crews.

## Where a Party Lodges

Strangers are watched in Kastamar, and a party is asked its business at the gate and again at its inn. The [Slipway]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, below the Admiralty, is the inn of officers' guests, merchants with contracts to sell and anyone with a letter to the admiral, and its keeper is a retired bosun who reports each new guest to the marines without being asked. The sailors' lodging-houses along the waterfront are cheaper, noisier and full of men waiting for the quarter, and their talk is of the Senate, the merchants and the Taruzelians, in that order.

## History {#history}

Kastamar's history is the fleet's, and two of its days are still on its books.

The first is in 523, when a Malagna crew from [[lore-southrnfleets|the Southern Fleets]] sailed a captured galley through the western strait under imperial colors and came ashore in a deep bay up the coast ([[lore-bankedfire|The Winter of the Banked Fire]]). A Kastamar squadron fired the galley at her moorings, and the fire ran up the beach into the fishing village's forty-one boats. In 524 the survivors took terms from the _therkos_ of Salkalon, and twenty-eight of them went to the oars of the Kastamar fleet for seven years, northern crews rowing in the imperial fleet a generation before the Empire hired Nordmen by contract ([[lore-compactsignd|The Signing of the Compact]]). The village is [[place-braldvik|Bralvik]], and the bill for its boats has come to the admiralty every autumn since, in the same words.

The second is in 708. The islands of Taruzel rose in 707 ([[lore-taruzelwar|The Taruzelian War of Independence]]), and the next year a squadron of some forty warships, the largest the Empire had ever sent into the eastern sea, sailed from Kastamar to break the blockade of the island channels. It followed the Taruzelian flotilla into the [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]], took the sand on the falling tide and was destroyed there in two days, with its admiral and most of its marines ([[lore-tamzirbattle|The Battle of Tamzir Shoals]]). About 709 the Empire recognized the Confederation. Kastamar holds that Taruzelian pilots steered the leading ships onto the sand, and the Confederation holds that its captains chose the ground. The names of the drowned are cut on the inner face of the eastern mole, and since the war the fleet's money has come late and short.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The empty quay.** A shipping house of Delalara has offered to hire three of the laid-up warships, with their crews, to escort its convoys east. The admiral will not lend the fleet to merchants, and the crews would sail for anyone who paid them. A party is hired by one side to make the other's case at the waterfront taverns.
- **The notes.** A moneylender on the waterfront has been buying the admiralty's notes at half their face from oarsmen who believe the next quarter is not coming. Someone has told them so, and the admiral wants to know who.
- **The drowned name.** A widow has found her husband's name cut on the mole among the dead of the Tamzir Shoals, and a sailor home from Taruzel says he is alive and rowing in the islands. She asks a party to find out which is true before she remarries.
- **The answer to the bill.** The admiral is short of oarsmen for the summer and has begun to listen to the idea of settling Bralvik's Boat-Bill in crews. He sends a party to Bralvik to learn the town's price before he makes the offer.

# The Truth of Kastamar {#truth .secret}

The yard has been building warships for Taruzel. Three years ago the Senate voted the money for four new galleys and the treasury paid for two. Kaldremos Holimandis had already laid down all four, and the admiral would not see a yard full of men idle and half-built hulls rotting on the slips. Tharkas Gurimandis allowed the master shipwright to finish the two unpaid galleys and sell them, as hulls with no fittings, to a Helionite captain who said he was hiring out to the hunt for the Bethuan privateers, as Helionite captains often do. The price paid the yard for a year and the oarsmen for a quarter. The Roll of the Fleet carries the two ships in the sheds, under names they never bore.

The Helionite captain was buying for the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]. Both hulls sailed from Helionis to [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] within the season, were fitted out at the Naval Yards there, and now row in the League's convoys under the Strait Fortress, faster and stronger than anything the islands build, because they are Kastamar's design. A Taruzelian merchant captain lodging at the Slipway has recognized the lines of one of them, and has said so to a sailor who repeated it in a waterfront lodging-house.

The admiral does not know where his hulls went. Kaldremos suspects, because the Helionite paid in Taruzelian Solden and asked for spare oars of a pattern only the League uses, and he has said nothing, because the sale was his idea. If the Senate learned of it, the admiral who wants war with Taruzel would stand as the admiral who armed it, and the war party in Orzara would lose its best voice. The shipping houses of Delalara would pay a great deal to be the ones who tell the Senate.
