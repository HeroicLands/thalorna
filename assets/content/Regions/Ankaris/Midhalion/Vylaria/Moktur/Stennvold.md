## Overview {#overview}

Stennvold is a high mining-and-smelting town of about four thousand in the Mokturan heights, its furnaces working the ore that [[place-ferrovan|Zargonara]] and the lesser camps send down to it. Smoke hangs over its valley the year round.

A smelt-master, taking on a new hand, explains where the loyalty in the town lies: "The _therkos_ (governor) has the garrison, the tax-farmers have their ledgers, and I have the men who tend the furnaces. When the metal goes out under guard, it goes because they stayed at the fires." The workforce is half-[[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]], hard and insular, and loyal first to the smelt-masters who pay them. The metal that leaves Stennvold is among the most jealously taxed in the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]].

## Arriving {#arriving}

A traveler smells Stennvold before he sees it. The road from Zargonara comes over a saddle and down into a narrow valley full of a yellow-gray haze, and the town is inside the haze: a long street of stone houses beside a stream, and above it on the north slope the furnaces, a row of squat stone towers each with a bellows-house at its foot and a waterwheel turning in a leat behind it. The ore trains come down the slope to the roasting-beds above the furnaces, and the slag goes out below them into heaps that run down to the stream. The water below the town is the color of weak tea and nobody drinks it.

The furnace crews descend from the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] kindreds who came inside the province when its _therkoi_ bought the forest's War Chiefs, and like the people of [[place-pinnhald|Pinnhald]] they are Varokh still in speech and custom. They speak the border patois at the furnaces and Vilari at the counting-house, and they carve the totems of their kindreds on the lintels of their doors.

## What Stennvold Lives By

Stennvold smelts. Each furnace belongs to a smelt-master, who buys ore from the lessees of Zargonara, pays the furnace-men by the charge, and sells the metal to the province's buyers at a price the province sets. The furnaces burn charcoal, and the charcoal-burners of the forested slopes sell to them all year. Iron goes out as bars, copper as cakes, and silver as small stamped ingots in chests, and all three go down to [[place-tyrellan|Tirellan]] twice a month under guard.

The furnace-men work in crews of six, in shifts that keep a furnace burning for weeks at a time, and a crew is usually a family or a kindred. Their wages are good by the standard of the province and are paid in coin at the furnace door at the end of each charge. They are loyal to the smelt-master who pays them because he is the one who pays, and because the smelt-masters, most of them half-Varokh too, are the men who stand between the crews and the _therkos_'s officers.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

Stennvold is chartered under the province, with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors), and the smelt-masters fill the council. A clerk of the _karezos_ (revenue officer) keeps an office at the counting-house to weigh the metal before it leaves, and twenty of the _therkos_'s auxiliaries keep the road. The first of the smelt-masters, a broad, slow-spoken man named [[being-thalritherth|Thalrith ira Erthwald wur Stennvold]] who owns three furnaces, is the town's voice to Tirellan, and a dispute between crews goes to him before it goes to a magistrate.

## The Present {#present}

The furnaces must keep burning for the convoy, and the town is burying children. Three of the furnace crews' children died this winter of the cough the old women call the smelter's breath, and a crew's mother believes a rival kindred has cursed them. A smelt-master has let one of his furnaces go cold and will not say why, and the province's buyer wants it lit before the next convoy. The charcoal-burners on the slopes have been robbed three times this season, and the smelt-masters are short of fuel.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The [Bellows]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the inn at the upper end of the street, above the haze on most days, where the buyers from Tirellan stay. It brings its water down from a spring above the town in a covered pipe, and it charges for that.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The cough.** The children of the furnace crews have a cough every winter that the old women call the smelter's breath, and this winter three have died of it. A crew's mother, who believes it is a curse laid by a rival kindred, asks a party to find the curse and break it.
- **The cold furnace.** A smelt-master has let one of his furnaces go cold and will not say why, and the province's buyer at the counting-house wants it lit again before the next convoy. He hires a party to learn what is wrong.
- **The charcoal road.** Charcoal-burners on the slopes above the town have been robbed three times this season, and the smelt-masters are short of fuel. They want the road guarded and the thieves found.

# The Truth of Stennvold {#truth .secret}

The town is poisoning itself, and Thalrith knows it. The ore that comes down from Zargonara's old galleries is not the ore that came from the great vein. It is rich in silver and richer in a metal that goes off in the roasting-smoke and settles in the slag, and it has been coming for four years. The smoke over the valley carries it, and the stream below the slag heaps carries more. The cough that kills children in winter is the smoke. The cattle that graze the meadow below the town have been dying of a wasting sickness for three summers, and the herders blame the Varokh spirits of the stream.

Thalrith learned it from an assayer of the capital whom the lessees of Zargonara paid to test the new ore, and who told him plainly what roasting it would do. He has moved his own household's water to a spring above the furnaces and taken his grandchildren to relatives in [[place-pinnhald|Pinnhald]], and he has said nothing to the crews, because the lessees pay him to add bought silver to the melt and the crews would put out the fires if they knew what the old galleries' ore was doing to their children. The cold furnace is his: it is the one that roasts the worst of the new ore, and he could not bring himself to light it again.

If the crews learn the truth, they bank the furnaces, Stennvold's metal stops, and with it the province's silver. If they do not learn it, the town goes on burying its children.
