## Overview

Milanthos is a farming village on the rural circuit of [[place-tyrellan|Tirellan]] in [[place-moktur|Moktur]], and one of the places [[being-azendsdmtr|Azendas Damator]], Senior Priest of [[affiliation-lizelas|Lizelas]] in the Tirellan region, visits on his rounds. Its farmers leave part of their harvest at the temple, and they do it because they have seen the priest's blessing turn into a harvest that keeps them alive.

## Arriving

Milanthos is half a day's walk up a side valley from the Tirellan road, about four hundred people in stone farmhouses round a green, with the fields running up the slopes behind them in narrow walled strips. The village is walled like every place in the province, with a dry-stone wall the height of a man and a gate that is shut at night, and at its upper end stands the [Blockhouse]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a square stone tower with a timber gallery, built by the village's militia in the hunger raids for the people to shelter in. Beside the green is the village's shrine to Lizelas, a roofed altar under a lime tree, where the priest blesses the seed at spring plowing and the first sheaf at harvest.

## What Milanthos Lives By

Milanthos lives by barley, oats, sheep and a few cows. The fields are thin and the season is short, and a family that brings in a good harvest does so by working every hour of the summer light. The wool goes down to the fair at Tirellan, the grain is kept in the village, and the cheese is sold at the city market in autumn. At harvest each household sets aside the temple's share, a sheaf in ten, and carries it to the Blockhouse, where the priest receives it.

## The Elder

The village's voice is **Elder [[being-pirkelosholi|Pirkelos]] of Milanthos**, a former militia commander now in his seventies. Asked by a visitor why a village that counts every sheaf gives any of it to a priest, Pirkelos points at the barn and says that the priest's blessing fills it, and that he has watched it do so.

It was Pirkelos who first encouraged Azendas to take up healing, and when the Temple hierarchy in the capital questions the priest's methods, Milanthos is where Azendas finds shelter and Pirkelos is the one who provides the local political cover.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Milanthos is a village of the province with no charter of its own. Its taxes are collected by the revenue's men from Tirellan at the end of the harvest, its roads are the _pozerus_'s, and its quarrels go to Pirkelos and the heads of the oldest families, who sit on the bench under the lime tree. The village's militia is a list of every man who can carry a spear, kept by Pirkelos, and it musters at the Blockhouse when the smoke from the ridge says a war-band is out.

## Where a Party Lodges

Milanthos has no inn. A party is lodged by Pirkelos in the upper room of the Blockhouse, or by a family whose barn has space, and pays in coin, work or news from the road.

## Hooks

- **The auditor's visit.** An auditor of the revenue has come to Tirellan to learn why the temple's harvest receipts from the circuit are so small, and she is riding out to Milanthos next. Pirkelos wants a party to keep her company on the road, and to keep her from the Blockhouse.
- **The blight.** A blight has appeared in the barley on the upper strips, and the priest's blessing has not stopped it. Some of the farmers whisper that Lizelas has turned from him. Azendas asks a party to carry a sample of the blighted grain to someone who knows more than he does.
- **The muster.** The smoke on the ridge calls the militia out, and Pirkelos has eighteen men fit to carry a spear. A party in the village is asked to stand with them at the wall until the riders from Tirellan come.

# The Truth of Milanthos {#truth .secret}

The temple's share never leaves the village. For nine harvests the sheaves the households carry to the Blockhouse have stayed there, threshed and stored in sealed jars in the tower's cellar and in two pits dug under its floor, and the receipts Azendas sends to the temple in Tirellan record a fraction of them. The rest is a famine store for the whole circuit. Azendas and Pirkelos agreed it between them in the year the imperial road's last bridge below the provincial boundary fell, and the caravans that brought southern grain in a bad year stopped coming. In the wet summer three years ago the store fed four villages through the winter, and the families who ate from it believe that Lizelas sent the grain by her priest.

What the auditor's books show is that Milanthos gives the temple less than any village on the circuit and that its priest's blessings are worth the least. What is in the cellar is two winters' bread for a thousand people, taken from the temple and from the emperor's tithe on the temple's grain, and enough to have Azendas stripped of his rank and Pirkelos tried for theft. Every household in Milanthos knows, and none has told.
