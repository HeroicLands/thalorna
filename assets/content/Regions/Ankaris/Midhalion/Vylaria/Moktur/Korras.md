## Overview

Korras commands one of the principal passes through [[place-moktur|Moktur]]'s mountains, and the first thing a carter learns about it is the price of getting through. The town of about five thousand is a fortified toll-town grown fat on the customs levied on the overland trade between the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|empire]] and the lands beyond.

Its garrison and its toll-keepers answer to the military _therkos_ (governor). Control of Korras's tolls is a prize worth killing for in [[affiliation-provncmktr|Moktur]]'s rough politics, and more than one border lord has risen or fallen on who holds the gate. A carter who has paid at Korras for years advises a younger one to be civil to the toll-keepers and to remember the name of whoever is in command that season, because it changes.

## Arriving

The pass road comes up a gorge with a white river in the bottom of it, and for the last hour the cliffs close until a loaded mule can barely pass another. Then the gorge opens into a bowl of steep pasture, and Korras fills the bowl's far end. The town is built across the throat of the pass from cliff to cliff, a wall with the road running through a single gate under a square tower, and the town's houses climb the slopes on either side of the road behind it. There is no other way through. A traveler going north pays at the gate, and so does a traveler coming south.

The [Gate]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the town. Its tower carries the banner of whichever border lord holds the command that season beside the _therkos_'s grey, and the arch under it is wide enough for one wagon. Inside the arch, on the left, is the [Toll-house]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a hall with a counter, a great beam scale and an iron-bound chest behind a grille, and on its wall a painted board gives the dues on every load: so much a mule-load of furs, so much a wagon of iron, so much a head of cattle, salt by the sack. The board has been repainted so often that the old figures show through the new ones.

Above the gate, on the slopes, the high summer pastures run up to the snow, and the herding kindreds of the pass drive their sheep up to them in spring and down again before the first snow. Their steadings stand along the upper edge of the town, stone below and turf above.

## What Korras Lives By

Korras lives by the gate. Furs come south through it from the [[place-vandstein|Vandstein]] villages, wolf, marten, bear and fox, with honey, wax and amber, and iron tools, salt, cloth and Vylarian coin go north. The muleteers who carry them stop a night in the town each way, and the inns, stables, farriers and harness-makers along the road live on them. A trade in furs is done in the town itself, in the yards behind the inns, by buyers who would rather pay the toll once on finished bales than twice on raw skins.

The tolls go to [[place-tyrellan|Tirellan]] in coin, under guard, every month, and they pay the _therkos_'s auxiliaries. The commander of the gate keeps a share of what he collects, by long custom, and the share is why the border lords want the command.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The commander of the gate is named by the _therkos_ each spring and holds Korras until the next. The border lords ask for the command with gifts, with men lent to the _therkos_'s campaigns and with marriages, and a lord who holds it for one season has the coin to make a second request better than the first. A lord who is refused the command and takes it anyway has done so more than once, and the _therkos_ has confirmed him afterward more than once, when he held the gate firmly. The present commander is a younger son of a border house, with sixty of his own men and two hundred of the _therkos_'s auxiliaries on the wall.

The toll-keepers do not change with the command. They are clerks of the province, the same men year after year, and a new commander learns the gate from them. Korras has two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) and a small council of innkeepers and fur-buyers who keep the town's own peace and answer for its taxes, and a quarrel inside the wall goes to them. A quarrel at the gate goes to the commander.

## Where a Party Lodges

The inns stand along the road inside the gate, one against the next, and every one has a yard for mules. The [Marten]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the furriers' inn, warm, crowded and loud with bargaining in two tongues, and its keeper will tell a party what the toll is today before they ask. Outside the gate, on the south side, a carters' yard takes those who mean to pay at first light and be through before the queue forms.

## Hooks

- **The new banner.** A border lord who was refused the command this spring has sent word that he will come for the gate at midsummer. The present commander hires a party to stand on his wall and to find out how many men the other lord is bringing.
- **The painted board.** A muleteer swears he paid the dues shown on the board, and the toll-keepers swear he paid a third less, and his load is held at the gate until it is settled. He wants a party to read the board as it was painted last year.
- **The steading on the edge.** A herder of the upper pastures has been found dead below the cliff, and his kindred say he fell. His widow says he was pushed, and she will not say by whom until a party with no lord behind it comes up to hear her.

# The Truth of Korras {#truth .secret}

The gate does not close the pass. A herders' path leaves the upper pastures above the town, runs along a ledge under the cliff for a mile, and comes down into the gorge below the wall, out of sight of every tower. A man on foot can take it, and so can a sheep. A war-band can take it in single file by night in an hour, and every summer the Varokh bands that come over the march and strip the steads to the south take it, and are guided along it by the herding kindreds of the upper pasture.

The herders are half-Varokh and have cousins on the far side of the pass. A war-band guided by them passes their own steadings and leaves them alone, and takes its cattle from the valleys beyond, where the herders have no kin. It is a bargain for their survival, made by grandfathers and kept by grandsons, and no commander of the gate has known of it, because the commanders change each spring and the herders do not talk to strangers. The toll-keepers have suspected it for years and have said nothing, since a toll-keeper who accused the herders would need them to deny it at the commander's table.

The dead herder of the upper steadings was the one man among them who wanted to tell the gate. His widow knows why he died. The commander who learns of the path holds the knowledge that would make Korras what everyone believes it is, and he can only use it by fighting the kindred whose men are on the upper pasture above his wall.
