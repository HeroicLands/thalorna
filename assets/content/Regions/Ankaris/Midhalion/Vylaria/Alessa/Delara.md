## Overview

Delara is a busy market town of about 12,000 on one of the great roads into the capital, where the produce of the surrounding estates is gathered and sold on toward [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. Its inns and warehouses serve the constant traffic of carters and drovers, and its weekly market is among the largest in the heartland.

A drover on the capital run, advising a cousin who has never taken a herd to market, tells him where to rest: "Delara on market day, if you want to sell. Delara the day before, if you want to sleep. The inns on this road are never empty, and the warehouses are never idle, so book your bed and your stall before you are in sight of the gate, and be gone before the market is."

## Arriving

Delara is heard before it is seen: a mile out the road is loud with cattle, wheels and dogs, and the paving widens to let the herds pass the carts. The town is strung along the road for most of a mile with no wall, its inns facing the paving with wide gates into their yards, and behind them the drovers' pens and the long warehouses run back into the fields. A milestone of the imperial road stands in the middle of the market square and gives the distance to Orzara. Carters touch it for luck on the way in.

At the head of the square stands the [Weighhouse]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, an open hall on bronze-capped columns that is the oldest and finest building in the town. Under its roof, chained to a stone table, are Delara's standard measures: a set of bronze weights and grain-measures stamped with the province's mark, against which every scale and every bushel in the market is checked. The paving of the road outside it is worn into ruts deep enough to take a wheel, and the town has patched them with gravel for longer than the oldest carter remembers.

## What Delara Lives By

Delara lives by the road and the market. On the eve of market day the estates of the surrounding country send in their surplus, grain, cheese, wool, oil, wine and livestock, and on market day the factors of the capital's merchants and the stewards of its great houses buy it, weigh it at the Weighhouse and send it on. The town's own trades are the ones the traffic needs: innkeepers, ostlers, wheelwrights, farriers, harness-makers, warehousemen and the porters who load the carts. A posting-station of the imperial road keeps fresh horses for the couriers, and the post-riders change mounts here without stopping long enough to eat.

Market day is loud, crowded and quick, and by the evening the square is swept and the herds are on the road. The day before is the quiet one, when the inns are full of drovers asleep and the factors walk the pens to see what will be offered.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Delara is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]], with two _tavemoi_ elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ who answer in their own persons for the town's taxes. Most of the council keeps inns or warehouses. On market day one of the _tavemoi_ sits at the stone table in the Weighhouse and hears every quarrel about a weight or a price on the spot, and his ruling is final for anything under the value of a good horse. The market dues the town collects on every sale pay its taxes, and a good year on the road is a good year for the council.

The posting-station answers to the _pozerus_ (warden of the roads) at [[place-alyssar|Alessar]], not to the town, and its master keeps the road's own peace: he sends word ahead of bandits, broken bridges and plague, and the post-riders carry it.

## The Present {#present}

The road is good this year and the council is prosperous, and the capital has begun to complain. A buyer in Orzara has reweighed a cargo bought at Delara and found it light, and his factor means to learn which link of the chain cheats him before he buys again. A drover's herd from the hill country never reached the yards. A post-rider was found a mile down the road without his bag, and the master of the posting-station needs it back before the courier who sent it learns it is gone.

## Where a Party Lodges

A party lodges at one of the inns on the road, and books ahead as the drover says. The inns nearest the square cost the most and are the loudest. The ones at the ends of the town are cheaper and fill with drovers who would rather walk a mile than pay. Every inn keeps an ostler awake all night, because the road never stops.

# The Truth of Delara {#truth .secret}

Delara's standard weights are light. The bronze set chained to the table is not the one the province stamped: that set was stolen a generation ago, and the council of the day had a replacement cast by a founder in the capital, stamped with a copy of the province's mark, and said nothing. The new weights are short by a twentieth. Every sack, bushel and fleece weighed at Delara since counts for a twentieth more than it holds: the seller is paid for the difference, the buyer pays for it, and the town, which charges its dues by the measure, takes a twentieth more in dues than the goods would bear. Nobody in the market loses, so nobody in the market has complained. The loss lands in Orzara, where the buyers are too far away and too many to compare notes, and the extra dues are how the council has met its taxes in bad years without ruining its members.

The present council knows and has kept the secret because each of its members would answer in person for the whole of it. The buyer's factor in Orzara is the first to come close. The original weights still exist, in the cellar of the man whose grandfather stole them, and his family has been paid by the council every year since to keep them there.
