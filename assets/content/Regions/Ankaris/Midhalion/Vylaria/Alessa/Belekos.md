## Overview

Belekos is a city of wealthy merchant families, with opulent townhouses and manicured gardens that look down on the fertile river valleys supplying much of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]]'s grain. It makes nothing that anyone travels to buy. Its prosperity comes from controlling the agricultural surplus of the valleys and the trade that carries it away.

A grain factor of one of the great houses, walking a newly hired clerk through the city on his first morning, makes the point at the first gate he comes to: "You came in by the front door, and you are standing in the counting room. Here the family sleeps upstairs, the sacks are stored behind, and the ledger is read in the hall where the guests are received. A house in Belekos does not keep its business out of sight, and you should not be surprised to find how much of the family's life you are expected to keep out of the ledger."

## Arriving

The road comes up out of the valleys, past granaries the size of temples, and enters Belekos by a gate the great houses paid for, carved with their emblems in a row. The city is built on the slopes above the river, and its streets climb in terraces. The great houses stand along the upper terraces, each a long front of pale stone with a wide gate for carts in the middle and a garden behind, and each gate opens into a hall where the sacks are tallied under painted ceilings and colored windows. The lower city along the river is warehouses, wharves for the valley barges, the cooperages and the streets where the clerks, porters and carters live.

Belekos is clean, prosperous and new-looking, and it is the one city in Alessa where a stranger sees little decay. The houses rebuild their fronts every generation in the newest manner, and the glassworkers of the city, among them [[being-mentrkrmys|Mentra Keramyes]], are kept busy making colored windows for houses that buy art to be seen buying it. The city's arena, where the houses sponsor games on the state's days and keep gladiators of their own, is the only old building they have not replaced.

## The Great Houses

The mercantile families have accumulated wealth and influence over centuries, and they work from elaborate estates that blend palatial residence with warehousing and counting rooms. The line between home and enterprise is deliberately blurred.

The atmosphere is one of calculated sophistication. The merchant elite keep libraries, sponsor scholars and collect art, knowing that such patronage shows both refinement and power. Beneath the civility runs sharp competition. The houses keep up public courtesy and formal alliance networks, and meanwhile they maneuver constantly for advantage: favorable trade agreements, advantageous marriages, and a position to win imperial contracts. A family's fall from grace can be swift and absolute, and every house in the city has watched one happen.

Not every house is old. Some are families from elsewhere who came for the grain trade and stayed, such as the Helionite-descended Kalethos household of [[being-myrnklypss|Mirene Kalethos]], which settled in Belekos two generations ago and does well enough to be asked to the right gatherings and carefully enough to stay out of the houses' feuds.

## Hedging Against the Empire

Belekos feels the weight of the empire's decline differently from provincial cities like [[place-velysara|Velisara]]. Its merchants do not mourn lost grandeur. They are quietly relocating assets, establishing trading partnerships with other realms and hedging their bets against an empire that may not endure.

The factors say among themselves that true power is already shifting away from the imperial court, and that the merchants of cities like Belekos, not the _kurthoi_ (senators) in the capital, increasingly control the flow of goods and gold that sustains civilization. The great houses do not say it where a _kurthos_ can hear.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Belekos is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]], with two _tavemoi_ elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ answerable in their own persons for the city's taxes. Every seat on the council and both magistracies are held by the great houses or by men they sponsor, because a house in Belekos can afford the liability that ruins the councilors of poorer towns, and wants the seats for what they decide: the river dues, the warehouse leases and the city's bids for imperial grain contracts. The houses keep their own guards, who patrol the upper terraces at night, and the city's watch keeps the lower town and the wharves. A theft from a great house is a matter for the house, which settles it before the watch hears of it.

## Politics {#present}

The great houses rule Belekos and are quietly leaving it. They fill every council seat for what it decides, the river dues, the warehouse leases and the city's bids for imperial grain, and they buy art in public to be seen buying it while they move their money out of an empire they no longer trust to last. A house that fell last year left a townhouse, a warehouse and a gladiator under contract, and its creditors and its last son race to collect them. A glassworker is making a window of a rival house's broken emblem, to be set where the rival must pass it every morning. A clerk of a great house is offering to sell a ledger his master keeps apart from the one read in the hall.

## Where a Party Lodges

Inns stand by the gate and along the river, and the good ones are near the gate, where the factors of buyers from the capital and from abroad stay while they bargain. A stranger with a letter to one of the great houses is lodged in the house itself, in the guest rooms off the counting hall, and is expected to be grateful, discreet and seen at dinner. A guest of one house who calls on a rival house is remembered by both.

## History {#history}

The empire's histories record no event of Belekos's own. The city's history is its houses', which have built their wealth over centuries on the grain of the valleys below and the trade that carries it away. Each generation rebuilds its fronts in the newest manner, so the arena is the only old building the houses have not replaced. Not every house is old: the Helionite-descended Kalethos household came for the grain trade two generations ago and stayed.

The decline reached Belekos as a question of trust rather than of loss ([[lore-empirdclne|The Decline of the Vilérian Empire]]). As the far provinces went their own way and the Empire drew back to its core, the great houses began quietly moving their money and their partnerships out of an empire they no longer trust to last.

# The Truth of Belekos {#truth .secret}

The great houses have already left, on paper. Over the twelve years since the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] won its independence, the five largest houses have each opened accounts with banking houses of the Taruzelian cities and bought shares in Taruzelian ships through agents whose names are not their own. Each house keeps a second ledger of these holdings in its counting room, written by one trusted clerk in a cipher the houses share. Trading with Taruzel is no crime in law, and the Senate has never forgiven it either. The houses' gold now sits where the empire cannot tax or seize it, and the houses have agreed among themselves that if the empire falls, they will be Taruzelian partners before they are Vilérian subjects.

The cipher is the weak point. Every house uses the same one, so any clerk who sells one ledger has sold the key to all five, and the clerk offering his copy knows it.
