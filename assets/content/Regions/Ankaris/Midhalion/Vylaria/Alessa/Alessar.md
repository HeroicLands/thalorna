## Overview

Alessar is the capital of a province whose greatest city it does not govern. About 45,000 people live here, and it is the administrative seat of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]] proper: the [[affiliation-provinclys|Province of Alessa]] is held from Alessar by the _therkos_ (governor) the emperor appoints, while [[place-orzara|Orzara]], which stands within the province and is counted with it, is kept as a thing of its own.

A clerk on the _therkos_'s staff explains the arrangement to a newcomer sent to deliver a petition: "Everyone who writes to us wants the capital, and the capital is not ours. What is ours is everything else in Alessa—the towns, the roads, the estates, the tax rolls—and that is charge enough for one _therkos_."

## Arriving

Orzara lies one day east by road.

The road from Orzara reaches Alessar after a long day of farms and villas, and the city shows itself first as a line of white colonnades along a ridge, with the bronze roof of the [Governor's House]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at the top. The last mile is lined with tombs, since the dead lie outside the boundary along the road, and the newest are the grandest: a family that has done well in the provincial service buries its fathers where every petitioner walks past them.

The gate arch carries a list of every chartered town in Alessa, each with its distance in miles, and the city repaints the near ones every spring. Inside, the [Long Stair]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, a paved street of shallow steps between porticoes, climbs from the gate to the Governor's House. The porticoes are let to scribes, notaries, copyists and advocates, and their benches are full from first light. Behind them, a street back from the stair, the city is tenements, laundries and cookshops, and the aqueduct that feeds the upper fountains runs on one channel of its two. The second has been dry for as long as anyone in the laundries remembers.

At the top of the stair the Governor's House faces the [Hall of the Rolls]{.name data-type="place/structure"} across a paved square, with the provincial temple of the [[affiliation-imperialcult|Imperial Cult]] closing its third side. The bronze doors of the Governor's House are polished every morning. The Hall's roof leaks along the north gallery, and in a wet winter the clerks carry the oldest rolls down to the floor and work among them in their cloaks.

## What Alessar Lives By

Alessar lives by paper. Every lease of an imperial estate, every tax assessment, every road levy and every appeal from a town magistrate's bench in Alessa comes here to be written, heard, sealed or refused, and the city has grown up around the business of waiting for it. Its trades are the scribes on the stair, the advocates who argue before the _therkos_'s court, the clerks of the Hall, the inns that house the people waiting on all of them, and the cookshops that feed the inns. Moneylenders of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] keep tables in the lower porticoes, because a petitioner who has waited a month has usually run short.

The families of rank in the provincial service keep two houses, one here and one in the capital, and spend the assize seasons in this one. A son who wants a post in the provincial service starts at the morning call of a house in Alessar, and the satirist of [[lore-morningcall|the Morning Call]] puts "the third son of the senator from Alessar" in the queue at a patron's door in Orzara. Strangers come too. The [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the merchant tribe of the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Atarzád]], keep a walled office near the gate, where their factors buy the province's licenses for goods carried inland, and [[being-phltsklfts|Filezos Klethes]], a strategist the army exiled, lives in the city and sells his advice to whoever will pay for it.

The year turns on the two assizes, one after the spring sowing and one after the vintage, when the _therkos_'s court sits every day and the Hall revises the rolls. For a month each time Alessar doubles: petitioners sleep two to a bed, the advocates raise their fees, and the cookshops on the back streets stay open past midnight. Between the assizes the city is quiet, and its people say that is when a sensible man brings his business, because the clerks have time to read it.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The _therkos_ holds the province in the emperor's name, commanding its garrison, its revenue and its courts, and answers to him alone. The present _therkos_ is [[being-kldrmslthzs|Kaldremas Elthezis]], a _kurthos_ (senator) of an old house, who has held the province for eleven years. He is old, and for most of the past year he has kept to his villa in the hills at [[place-vennara|Vennara]]; his acts come down to Alessar sealed, and his freedman secretary, [[being-nethoren|Nethoren]], reads them from the steps of the Governor's House on the mornings after they arrive.

Below him the province's officers keep their own offices round the square, and each reports somewhere different:

- **The _karezos_** collects the emperor's revenue in Alessa and reports past the _therkos_ to the emperor, so the two men need not like each other, and seldom do.
- **The _tarkelos_** keeps the provincial treasury and its accounts in the vaults under the Hall of the Rolls.
- **The _zoralus_** commands the province's urban cohorts and keeps the peace. One cohort is quartered in a watch-house below the Governor's House, and at assize time its men walk the inns at night.
- **The _vandraks_** commands the legion stationed in the province, from its camp at [[place-corvinus|Korvenara]], and is the _therkos_'s deputy in the field.
- **The _pozerus_** keeps the imperial roads, their posts and their bridges. Every milestone in Alessa is cut to his office's pattern, and the levy that maintains them is assessed here. The levy for the north road is assessed with the rest, and the north road's bridge beyond [[place-skoraz|Soraks]] lies broken in its river.
- **The _zalvos_ of the province** presides in the provincial temple over the rites that bind Alessa to the capital, and on the state's days the town's magistrates, guilds and council make the offering before the Orzas's image there.

Alessar is also a chartered town in its own right, with two _tavemoi_ elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ who answer in their own persons for the taxes the town owes. They keep the markets, the fountains, the drains and the night watch of the lower town, and they are much smaller men beside the officers on the square. A shopkeeper robbed in the night goes first to his neighbors and his guild, then to a _tavemos_'s bench in the lower market, and only last to the _zoralus_'s cohort, which takes an interest in thieves who rob petitioners and very little in anyone else.

## The Present {#present}

Alessar governs Alessa without its governor. The _therkos_, Kaldremas Elthezis, has not been seen in the city since the spring; his acts come down from his villa at [[place-vennara|Vennara]] sealed, and Nethoren reads them from the steps the morning after they arrive. The _karezos_ reports past the _therkos_ as always, and the two men seldom agree. Petitioners crowd the inns, the board of the court's list is for sale at the innkeepers' tables, and water has reached a box of old citizen rolls under the gallery of the Hall. The north road's levy is assessed here with the rest, and the north road's bridge is still broken. Filezos Klethes buys suppers at the Two Seals for strangers who might carry a letter to [[place-velysara|Velisara]].

## Where a Party Lodges

Strangers lodge on [Petitioners' Row]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the street of inns that runs along the foot of the Long Stair. Every inn there keeps a painted board by its door listing the hearings the court has called that day, copied each morning by a boy sent up to the Governor's House, and a guest pays partly for the board. Beds go by the night between the assizes and by the session during them, and an inn full for the session still finds room for a guest who brings a patron's letter.

The best of the inns is the [Two Seals]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, where the advocates drink and where most of the business before the court is settled before it is heard. The cheapest are at the bottom of the row, beside the tombs, and are full of people whose cases have been waiting more than a year. Innkeepers on the Row say that a place on the court's list can be bought from the right clerk; the clerks say that the innkeepers sell it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The board.** A party's patron has a petition before the court, and it sits near the bottom of the board at the party's inn. The innkeeper offers to have it moved up for a fee, and a rival petitioner is offering the same innkeeper more to keep it where it is.
- **The rolls under the gallery.** Water has reached a box of old citizen rolls in the Hall, and a veteran's family finds its entry unreadable. What is not on the roll is not in the city, so the family hires a party to find the copy kept at the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]] in Orzara before the next dole is drawn.
- **The reading on the steps.** A lease the party's employer was sure of is refused in an act read from the steps of the Governor's House. The employer wants to know who in Vennara wrote it.
- **The strategist's offer.** Filezos Klethes buys a party a supper at the Two Seals and asks what they would charge to carry a sealed letter to [[place-velysara|Velisara]] without opening it.

# The Truth of Alessar {#truth .secret}

The _therkos_ has not sealed an act himself since the spring. Kaldremas Elthezis is failing in his villa at Vennara, clear in his mind on some days and lost on most, and his seal-ring is in Alessar, in the keeping of Nethoren, who seals the province's business with it and reads the acts from the steps as though they came down the Vennara road. Nethoren is careful and, by his own lights, honest: he seals what the _therkos_ would have sealed, and he refuses the bribes he is offered for anything else. He keeps a ledger of every act in his own hand, against the day he must answer for them.

In law his honesty makes no difference. An act sealed without the holder of the seal is a forgery, so every judgment, lease and assessment Alessar has issued since the spring can be challenged by anyone who proves that the _therkos_ never saw it. The _karezos_ knows, because his own assessments for the year went out under that seal, and he has reported nothing past the _therkos_ to the emperor, since the report would void his own work along with everyone else's. The _zalvos_ of the province suspects. The town does not.

When the _therkos_ dies, Orzara names a successor, the successor's staff reads the ledger, and every family that lost a case this year learns what it can reopen. Whoever finds the ledger first holds a year of the province's business in one book.
