## Overview

Come to Aravantara in the weeks after harvest and you will find a city that works like a scale. Wagons from a hundred aristocratic estates queue at its yards to have their grain, wine and oil weighed, taxed, and sent on toward the capital, and the whole of [[place-hylen|Hilen]]'s wealth passes through the town on its way to someone else. It is the administrative seat of the [[affiliation-provnchyln|province's administration]] and the clearing-house of the breadbasket, with twenty-eight thousand people living off the traffic.

The forums hold fewer _kurthoi_ (senators) than a province so full of senatorial families might lead you to expect. The voices belong to estate-factors and grain-brokers, and the city's calendar turns on the harvest rather than on any festival. The old landed families keep townhouses here. A broker tells a new clerk where the real conversation is: "The _therkos_ (governor) holds the audience, but the councils decide what is said at it, and the councils are the houses." Hilen's conservative temper, traditionalist and suspicious of merchants and mages alike, is nowhere stronger than in those councils.

## Arriving

Every road in the estate country ends at Aravantara, and in harvest weeks the last five miles of each are one slow line of wagons. A carter learns to judge his wait by the landmarks: at the last estate's boundary stone before the city he is a day out, and at the tombs he is a morning out. The tombs line the road for the final mile, since the dead lie outside the boundary, and the great houses of the province bury their masters there under roofs of carved stone, each with its house's name cut deep enough to read from a wagon seat.

The city stands on a low rise in the flat wheat country, inside a wall of brown stone the height of three men. The wall is old and sound, and it was never built against an army: its gates are wide enough for two wagons abreast, and the [Yards]{.name data-type="place/site"} lie outside it, between the wall and the road. They are a quarter of a mile of open sheds under tiled roofs, each shed hung with a great beam scale on a timber frame, and every scale is stamped with the province's mark and the year it was last tried. Beyond the sheds rise the granaries, long whitewashed halls on stone piers, with the eagle painted over the doors of the state granaries and the marks of the great houses over the rest. In the weeks after harvest the Yards work from first light to last, the air is thick with chaff, and the city's cats are the fattest in the empire.

Inside the gate the city is plainer than its wealth. The streets are straight, paved and clean, the houses are of the same brown stone as the wall, and nothing is painted that does not need to be. The main street runs from the gate to the forum, and halfway along it the [Street of the Houses]{.name data-type="place/feature"} turns off toward the higher ground. The townhouses of the old landed families stand along it behind blank walls, each with the cupboard of its ancestors' masks just inside a door that opens onto the street. In the week before the harvest, when the masters come in from their estates, the doors stand open and the street smells of beeswax.

The forum is the oldest thing in the city and the quietest. The [Governor's House]{.name data-type="place/structure"} closes its north side, a palace of pale marble built for a province with more business than Hilen keeps, its upper floor shut and its east wing let to the clerks of the revenue. The provincial temple of the [[affiliation-imperialcult|Imperial Cult]] faces it across the paving. On the long side stands the [Council House]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, whose front is a single slab of stone cut with the name of every man who has held an office of the town and the years he held it, and in front of it, at the center of the forum, stands the [Speakers' Stone]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, a block of dark stone a man's height square, worn hollow on top by centuries of speakers' feet. The colonnades round the forum were built for _kurthoi_ to stroll and argue in. Most mornings they hold grain-brokers, a few advocates and the boys of the rhetoric schools, declaiming to the pillars.

## What Aravantara Lives By

Aravantara lives by measuring what other people grow. A wagon that reaches the Yards is weighed on a beam scale under the eye of a yard clerk, and its load is entered on a tally, a strip of wood notched for the weight and split down its length so that the carter keeps one half and the clerk the other. Then the imperial revenue takes its share, in grain, at a shed of its own at the head of the Yards, and the rest goes into a house's granary, into a buyer's wagon, or into the state granaries to be carted on toward [[place-orzara|Orzara]] and the legions. Wine and oil are gauged by the cask and the amphora in a separate yard, and their seals are checked against a register of every press and vintner in the province.

The trades of the city are the trades of that traffic. Yard clerks and gaugers, granary-keepers and their cats, sack-makers, coopers, wheelwrights, carters' inns, and the brokers who buy and sell the split tallies before the grain they stand for has left the granary all live off the Yards. The factors of the capital's grain houses keep offices near the gate, and [[affiliation-mardovanes|House Mardovanes]] keeps the largest of them, where its freedman factor sees the house's Hilen purchases weighed before they go on to the state granaries. Moneylenders of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] advance coin against a tally at a twentieth of its value, and in the weeks before harvest, when the estates are short of hard coin, they do more business than anyone in the city.

The forum is where the capital's bread is priced. In the last weeks of the harvest the brokers meet in the colonnades each afternoon and trade the tallies of the season among themselves, and the price a tally fetches there on the day the Yards close is the price the state granaries pay for Hilen grain through the winter, and the price [[place-orzara|Orzara]]'s bakers pay for their flour. A broker who wants to know how the capital will eat in spring stands in the colonnades in autumn and listens.

The city's second trade is speech. Hilen's rhetoric masters teach in schools along the colonnades of the forum, and the sons of the province's houses learn the laudation there, the funeral oration that praises a house's masks one by one. The model they teach is [[lore-maskspraise|The Praise of the Masks]], which was spoken from the Speakers' Stone, and every schoolboy in the city knows its story of the magistrate who had the light weights of the Yards brought into the forum and broke them with a hammer in front of his own clients. The three broken weights are in a cupboard on the Street of the Houses. The yard clerks try their scales against the city's bronze standard, which is kept in the Council House and brought out to the Speakers' Stone whenever a weight is disputed.

The year has two seasons. From the harvest to the first rains the Yards never close, the population doubles with carters and tenants' wagons, and the masters of the houses are in their townhouses. For the rest of the year the masters are on their estates, the Yards weigh a few wagons a day, the forum belongs to the rhetoric schools, and the city is as quiet as a province town of half its size.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The _therkos_ holds the province for the emperor, commanding its garrison, its revenue and its courts, and answering to him alone. The present _therkos_ is [[being-lnthmsrvls|Linthimas Orvelis]], a _kurthos_ of an old house of the capital, appointed four years ago and the first in a generation with no estate in Hilen. He holds an audience every morning in the hall of the Governor's House, and the province's business is brought to him there: petitions, appeals from the town magistrates' benches, and disputes between estates over water, boundaries and inheritance. He is courteous, careful and much entertained on the Street of the Houses, and the houses agree among themselves what they will ask of him before they ask it.

The province's officers keep their offices round the forum and in the east wing of the Governor's House:

- **The _karezos_** (revenue officer) takes the emperor's share at the head of the Yards and reports past the _therkos_ to the emperor. His shed is the busiest building in the province for ten weeks of the year.
- **The _tarkelos_** (treasurer) keeps the provincial treasury and its accounts in strongrooms under the Governor's House.
- **The _zoralus_** (keeper of the peace) commands the province's urban cohorts. Two are quartered in Aravantara, and in harvest weeks one of them is spread along the Yards and the granary rows.
- **The _vandraks_** (legion commander) commands the legion stationed in the province, whose camp lies two miles out on the road to [[place-granaria|Kelborara]], and is the _therkos_'s deputy in the field. The legion eats Hilen grain drawn from the state granaries at the Yards, and its quartermasters are at the karezos's shed every day of the harvest.
- **The _pozerus_** (warden of the roads) keeps the imperial roads of the province, their posts and their bridges. In a province where everything moves by wagon, his levy is the tax the estates complain about most.
- **The _zalvos_ of the province** presides in the provincial temple over the rites that bind Hilen to the capital. Hilen is the cult's bastion, and on the state's days the masters of the houses make the offering before the Orzas's image in person and in order of precedence, with the whole city watching from the forum.

Aravantara is also a chartered town in its own right, with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for the taxes the town owes. The houses fill that council. Most of its seats are held by the stewards and clients of the great families, and a master who wants a thing done in the town sits in his own seat for the session. The broker's lesson in the Overview is the plain fact of the city: the _therkos_ rules, and he rules a province whose men of standing have settled their answer before his audience opens.

Order in the city rests on the houses first. A servant robbed, a tenant in a brawl or a carter cheated at an inn goes to his house's steward, and the steward settles it with the other house's steward, quietly and usually by nightfall. Only a stranger with no house behind him goes straight to a _tavemos_'s bench in the forum, and only a crime in the Yards brings the _zoralus_'s cohort, which hangs a granary thief on the day it catches him.

## The Present {#present}

Aravantara waits on the harvest and on the capital. The _therkos_, Linthimas Orvelis, suspects that every petition at his morning audience has been agreed on the Street of the Houses the night before, and he is the one man in the city with no house to ask. The yard clerks answer to the _karezos_, yet on one morning of last harvest every one of them refused the wagons of the [[affiliation-mardovanes|Mardovanes]] factor until noon, and the factor still does not know who gave the order. The houses watch the succession closely, because the capital's bread is weighed here and the old Orzas has adopted nobody. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s officer behind the forum has lost three letters to the province's posting-station this year.

## Where a Party Lodges

The carters' inns stand outside the wall along the road below the Yards. They are cheap, loud and full in harvest weeks, and empty enough the rest of the year that an innkeeper offers a party the whole upper floor for the price of a room. The best of them is the [Last Sheaf]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, which keeps a wheatsheaf standing uncut in a pot by its door all year in the province's custom for the dead, and whose yard can take a dozen wagons.

Inside the wall, the [Bronze Bushel]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on the forum is the inn of the brokers and the advocates, and the place where a stranger learns what the split tallies are fetching. A party carrying a patron's letter to one of the houses lodges on the Street of the Houses instead, as guests, and is fed, housed and watched with the correctness Hilen's houses pride themselves on. A guest of a house is that house's business for the length of his stay, and every other house on the street knows whose guest he is by supper.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The empty mask.** A master of one of the houses dies in harvest week, and the funeral needs men of the right height and build to wear the masks of his line. One of the masks is missing from the cupboard, and the heir hires a party to find it, quietly, before the procession forms at dawn.
- **The refused wagons.** On one morning of last harvest every yard clerk at the Yards refused the wagons of the Mardovanes factor until noon, and nobody has said who gave the order. The factor wants a party to find out before the house asks him why.
- **The audience.** Linthimas Orvelis suspects that every petition he hears has been agreed before it reaches him. He wants a party with no house behind it to dine on the Street of the Houses and tell him what is said at table.
- **The shuttered office.** The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] keeps one officer in Aravantara, in two rooms behind the forum whose shutters are closed more often than open. He asks a party to carry a sealed report to Orzara by hand, because the province's posting-station has lost three of his letters this year.

# The Truth of Aravantara {#truth .secret}

The houses have sworn to close the Yards. Two springs ago, with the reigning Orzas old and the succession in doubt, the masters of the great houses of Hilen met by night at the Speakers' Stone, one from each house, and swore before the _zalvos_ of the province and the image of the Orzas in the provincial temple that when he dies, no grain of Hilen is to be weighed at Aravantara for any claimant who brings a legion inside the capital's boundary. The Yards are to close, the wagons to stand in the road, and the granaries to stay shut until [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) and the legions have both accepted one Orzas. It is the province's horror of civil war written into an oath, and the masters call it the Compact of the Stone.

Every house of standing has sworn. The master of [[affiliation-ganothandis|House Ganothandis]] swore first, which is awkward for him, since his house has agreed to sell the next Olkassa harvests forward to the state granaries through House Mardovanes, and the compact would stop that grain as surely as anyone's. The list of the masters who swore, each name in its master's own hand, is kept by the _zalvos_ in a box under the Orzas's image in the provincial temple. The morning the Mardovanes wagons were refused at the Yards was a trial, ordered by three masters to learn whether the yard clerks would obey the houses over a capital buyer. The clerks obeyed.

The _therkos_ does not know. The _vandraks_ does, because his legion is fed from the same granaries and the houses needed to know which way he would march. He has told them that he means to keep his legion in its camp, and he has told nobody in the capital anything at all. Some of the masters believe that the threat alone keeps the claimants honest and that it need never be carried out. Others mean it. None of them has said aloud what every rhetoric schoolboy knows from the histories: the last time Hilen's grain stopped, the bread failed and Orzara burned for six days ([[lore-greatburnng|The Great Burning]]). The list in the box under the image names every house that would starve the capital to keep its legions apart, and every claimant to the throne would pay for it.
