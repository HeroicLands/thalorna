## Overview {#overview}

Polthara is a market town of about five thousand in the estate country of [[place-hylen|Hilen]], the breadbasket of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]]. The fields and vineyards around it belong to great houses and are worked by tenants, and on a market day the wagons that crowd its streets belong to their stewards.

That is the whole of Polthara's work: it is where the estate country's produce is weighed and sold on. A steward who has sold in its market for a few seasons says the town has no character of its own, which is how its buyers like it.

## Arriving {#arriving}

Polthara has no wall, no hill and no view. The road comes across the flat fields between rows of poplars and simply becomes a street, lined with sheds, stables and inns, and the street opens onto a market square paved with stone and large enough for two hundred wagons. Nothing in the town is older than a grandfather or newer than a father. The buildings are of plain brick under plain tiles, and most of them are let by the season to whoever needs them: a steward's storeroom one year, a buyer's office the next.

The one building with any pretension is the [Register House]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at the head of the square, a hall of dressed stone with a bronze bell in an arch over its door. The bell opens and closes the market. Inside, on a long table under the windows, lies the market register, a great book in which a clerk enters every sale made in the square: the seller, the buyer, the goods, the price and the day.

A mile out of the town on the north road, behind a long wall and an avenue of cypresses, stands the country house of the [Visbari]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a family of heralds who have served the imperial throne for generations. Its master, [[being-lrdmlnvsbr|Lord Aimilon Visbari]], an old herald of the [[affiliation-imprlhrlds|Imperial College of Heralds]], keeps a small school there: a handful of young men at a time, learning the arms, marks, liveries and precedence of every house in the empire. The pupils come into Polthara on market days to practice on the stewards' boards, and a steward whose mark they cannot name is a rare man.

On a market day the square is full by mid-morning. The wagons stand in rows by estate, each with its steward's mark on a board, and the buyers walk the rows tasting, weighing and arguing. By the evening bell the square is swept and the wagons are on the roads home, carrying the stewards and their masters' coin.

## What Polthara Lives By

Polthara lives by the market and by its register. The estates of the district send in what they have to sell, grain, wine, oil, wool, cheese, hides and livestock, and also what the great houses are getting rid of: old furniture, plate, harness, carriages and the contents of houses whose masters have died. A buyer who takes a thing home from Polthara takes a copy of its entry in the register, signed by the clerk and sealed by a _tavemos_ (magistrate), and that copy is good title to it before any court in the province. The buyers of the capital pay for that, because a thing bought at Polthara is bought clean, and nobody can come after it later claiming it was his.

The town's own trades are the market's: innkeepers, stablers, carters, the clerks of the Register House, and the scribes who copy entries for buyers. A circuit priest of [[affiliation-lizelas|the Faith of Lizelas]], [[being-rvthrbrmztr|Revothar Baremzator]], walks twelve tenant villages around the town in a fixed round and comes into Polthara on market days to buy medicines and carry messages. Beyond them, the town is where the district's people come for what they cannot find on their own estates: a physician, a scribe, a herald who knows the houses' arms, or an evening's entertainment.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

Polthara is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]], with two _tavemoi_ elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The register is the council's whole fortune: the town charges a fee on every entry and a larger one on every sealed copy, and the fees pay its taxes in most years. One _tavemos_ sits at the long table on every market day to seal the copies and settle disputes, and the other keeps the town between markets.

The present senior _tavemos_ is [Brazemos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a dealer in estate goods who has been elected seven years running and who knows every steward in the district by his mark. The market has its own watch of a dozen men, paid from the fees. Outside the town, on the roads through the wooded margins of the district, the stewards travel home in company, because bandits know when the market closes.

## The Present {#present}

Polthara sells a clean title, and the register's honor is the town's whole fortune. A buyer in the capital is being sued for a silver service he bought here, and the register's page for that day is gone. The bandits of the wooded margins know when the market closes, and the stewards ride home in company. Lord [[being-lrdmlnvsbr|Aimilon Visbari]] of the [[affiliation-imprlhrlds|Imperial College of Heralds]] keeps his school a mile up the north road, and his pupils read every steward's board in the square, so the old herald knows which house sells what in the district before its master does. Brazemos, senior _tavemos_ for seven years, seals the copies and settles the disputes.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The inns of Polthara line the road into the square and are full the night before a market and the night of it, and nearly empty between. The [Steward's Rest]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, nearest the Register House, is where the stewards sleep and where the buyers dine with them. The cheaper inns further out are cleaner than they need to be, and their keepers are glad of a party willing to stay a week, since a week holds only one market.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The road home.** A steward robbed on the road home from the market lost his master's coin and his wagon, and he asks a party to ride with him next market day, in case the bandits come back for the rest.
- **The torn page.** A buyer in the capital has been sued for a silver service he bought at Polthara, and when his agent goes to the Register House for proof, the page is gone. The buyer hires a party to find the clerk who kept that day's register.
- **The dead man's furniture.** A widow recognizes her late husband's writing-desk in a sale at Polthara, sold as the goods of an estate she has never heard of. She wants to know how it got there.

# The Truth of Polthara {#truth .secret}

The register washes stolen goods. The bandits of the wooded margins rob wagons on the district's roads and houses on its estates, and what they take comes back into Polthara on market day in the wagon of a steward whose master lives in the capital and has not visited his estate in years. The steward's mark is genuine, so the goods are entered in the register as that estate's surplus, sold, sealed and carried away with good title by buyers who are glad not to ask. A writing-desk taken from a house in one district is sold clean in the next, and the owner has no claim on it.

Brazemos runs it. He chooses which wagons the bandits rob, because the register tells him what every steward sold and so what every steward is carrying home, and he takes a share of every theft and of every sale. The steward whose mark carries the goods takes another share. The torn page recorded a sale Brazemos had not expected anyone to trace, and the clerk who kept the register that day has been sent to a cousin's farm in the south with a purse and instructions to stay there.

The register is the town's fortune and its honor at once. If it is shown in a court that a Polthara copy can carry stolen goods, every buyer in the capital who holds one has a title worth nothing, and the market that has no character of its own loses the one thing it sells.

Lord Aimilon Visbari knows. His pupils read the stewards' boards every market day, and one of them noticed two years ago that a capital master's mark was coming to market twice a month from an estate that had not been worked in a decade. Aimilon has let the ring run, because the register tells him what every house in the district buys and sells, and a register that Brazemos keeps for thieves is a register Brazemos does not suspect anyone else of reading.
