## Overview

Kelborara is where [[place-hylen|Hilen]]'s bread is counted. The chief grain-market of central Hilen, a town of seven thousand, is built around its granaries and weighing-yards, and the harvest of a hundred estates is gathered there, measured, and sent on toward the capital and the legions.

A weighing-yard clerk tells you he knows the state of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] before [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] (the Senate) does. In a good year the town overflows with sacked grain and there is nowhere to stand. In a lean year the granaries are empty, and that is the first warning that the empire's bread is short.

## Arriving

Kelborara shows above the wheat from ten miles off as a line of pale roofs, and the roofs are the granaries. There are more than sixty of them, long halls of whitewashed stone raised on squat piers so that rats cannot climb in, set in rows like the barracks of a camp, and the town's houses fill the lanes between the rows. The road from [[place-aravantia|Aravantara]] comes in past the legion's camp and enters the town between the two oldest granaries, which carry the eagle over their doors and are the property of the state.

In the middle of the town is the [Counting Floor]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a square of beaten earth the size of a parade ground with a roof on columns over half of it. Here the wagons are unloaded, the sacks are weighed on rows of beam scales, and the measurers call the weights aloud to the clerks at their tables. The noise of it carries all over the town in harvest weeks. The dust carries further, and a visitor's clothes are white with chaff by noon.

The granaries tell the town's history in their roofs. The eastern rows are newly tiled, bought and kept by the great houses, with each house's mark painted over its doors. The western rows are the state's, and several of them stand open to the sky, their tiles gone and their piers grown over with poppies, because the state built them for harvests larger than any it has stored in living memory.

## What Kelborara Lives By

The town lives by weighing, storing, sacking and carting grain. The estates of central Hilen send in their harvest after the reaping, and the grain is weighed on the Counting Floor, stored in the granaries, and sold to the factors of the capital's grain houses or carted on to the state granaries at Aravantara and the camps of the legions, and some of it goes north to the forts of [[place-moktur|Moktur]], which grow too little grain to feed their garrisons. The town's own trades follow from that work: measurers, granary-keepers, sack-makers, carters, wheelwrights and the cats, which the granary-keepers breed and sell to one another.

The heaviest work belongs to the sack-carriers, some four hundred men who unload the wagons, carry the sacks into the granaries and carry them out again. They belong to a fraternity with a hall beside the Counting Floor, and most of them were born in the tenant villages round the town and came in when their families' share of the harvest would not feed them all. A carrier is paid by the sack. In a good year he eats well and sends coin home, and in a lean year he stands idle on the Floor with the rest.

A granary-keeper turns the grain once a month all winter, airs the halls on dry days and burns sulphur in them in spring against the weevil, and a granary that loses a fifth of its grain to rot or vermin by spring is accounted well kept. The keepers say that the grain in a granary is never the same grain twice: what comes out in spring is lighter than what went in at harvest, and the clerks enter the difference as shrinkage.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Kelborara is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for the town's taxes. The council is made up of granary-keepers and the stewards of the houses whose granaries stand in the eastern rows. The _tavemoi_ keep the town's own standard measures in a locked chest on the Counting Floor and hear every dispute over a weight there, on the spot.

The state granaries answer to the _karezos_ (revenue officer) at Aravantara, whose clerks keep their books, and a detachment of the province's urban cohorts guards them. The detachment is twenty men under one officer, quartered in a granary of the western rows with a roof still on it. The sack-carriers' fraternity keeps order among its own members, and a carrier who steals from a granary is handed to the cohort by his brothers before the cohort can come for him.

## The Present {#present}

Kelborara counts the empire's bread, and its granaries are its politics. The houses' granaries in the eastern rows are full of grain bought for the capital, the tenant villages around the town are short in a lean year, and the sack-carriers who carry both are villagers' sons. The capital's factors hear that the granaries hold less than the clerks report, and one of them is paying to have the sacks counted. A roofless state granary in the western rows burned on a windless night though it held nothing that could burn, and the cohort's officer has not learned who was inside it. A carrier's younger brother has been missing since midsummer, and the fraternity's hall says nothing.

## Where a Party Lodges

Kelborara has four carters' inns round the Counting Floor, full and loud in harvest weeks and half empty the rest of the year. A party that cannot find a bed at harvest time can rent a granary-keeper's loft above one of the halls, which is dry, cheap and smells of wheat. The town's one good inn, the [Measure]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, is kept for the factors of the grain houses and charges for its quiet.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The fire in the western rows.** A roofless state granary catches fire on a windless night, though it holds nothing that can burn. The cohort's officer wants to know who was inside it.
- **The lean-year price.** A factor in Orzara hears that the Kelborara granaries are lower this year than the clerks have reported and hires a party to count the sacks in three of them, without the keepers knowing.
- **The carrier's brother.** A sack-carrier asks a party to find his younger brother, who left the tenant village for the town in spring and has not been seen on the Counting Floor or in the fraternity's hall since midsummer.

# The Truth of Kelborara {#truth .secret}

The sack-carriers are arming. The fraternity's hall is the meeting-place of a rising, planned by a measurer named [Varelos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who was born in a tenant village outside the town and has called weights on the Counting Floor for twenty years. His plan is simple and has the fraternity's officers behind it: in the next hungry year, when the tenant villages are short and the houses' granaries in the eastern rows still hold the grain sold forward to the capital, the carriers are to seize the granaries, break them open and send the grain home to the villages.

The weapons are in the state granary that burned. Bills, spears and a few old legionary swords have been carried in among sacks of chaff over two years and stored under the floor of a roofless hall in the western rows, where nobody but the cohort ever walks. The fire was an accident by lamplight, set by two carriers moving a load at night, and it scorched the floor without reaching what is under it. The missing brother joined the rising in spring, argued against it at midsummer, and is buried under the same floor.

Varelos is no fool. He knows the urban cohort's twenty men can be held in their own granary for a night, and he knows the legion's camp is a day's march away on the Aravantara road. What he has not reckoned with is that the houses of the eastern rows have sworn among themselves at Aravantara to close their granaries in the succession, and a closed granary in a hungry year is the very thing his rising is waiting for.
