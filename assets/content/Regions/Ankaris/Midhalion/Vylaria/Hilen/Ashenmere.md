## Overview {#overview}

Ashenmere is where [[place-hylen|Hilen]]'s estates run out. A town of about four thousand on the march of Hilen, the breadbasket of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], it is the province's border town, and a carter coming in from the edge of the province passes the last estate's boundary stones a few miles before its gate.

An estate steward from the interior, sent to Ashenmere with a wagon of tenants' goods, would call it a foreign posting. Inland the lord is whoever owns the land, and every road leads to a manor. On the march the roads lead out of the province as well as into it, and the town is where the two kinds of traffic meet.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The march lies on the north-eastern edge of the province, where the wheat country rises into wooded hills and the hills climb on into the forested uplands of the [[place-nartumkngdm|Nartun Kingdom]]. The imperial road comes up from the estates through the last fields, crosses a belt of rough common pasture, and reaches Ashenmere on a low shoulder of ground above a stream. The town has a wall, the only wall between here and [[place-aravantia|Aravantara]], of gray stone patched with brick, and a gate with a tower over it that the town keeps manned.

The stream below the town is the border. It is a dozen paces wide and fordable most of the year, and a stone bridge of three arches crosses it a quarter of a mile below the gate. At the near end of the bridge stands the imperial [Customs House]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a squat building with a barrier across the road and a yard behind it where wagons are searched. At the far end, on the kingdom's bank, a pair of Nartunese priests' men keep a hut and a barrier of their own and stop nothing that is not carrying a priest's displeasure.

Two buildings face each other across the water above the bridge. On the Hilen bank is a forge-shrine of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], a stone smithy with its fire burning day and night under a tiled roof and an anvil carved over its door, where a Hammer-Priest blesses the forge-work of the march and shoes the customs-post's horses. On the kingdom's bank, directly opposite, is a chapel of the [[affiliation-sacredflame|Order of the Sacred Flame]], the knights of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]], a plain hall of whitewashed stone with a lamp in its window that is never let go out. In the evenings the forge-fire and the chapel lamp burn opposite one another across the stream, and the people of Ashenmere call the stretch of water between them the Two Lights.

## What Ashenmere Lives By

The two kinds of traffic of the Overview are grain going up and timber coming down. Wagons from the Hilen estates carry grain, wine and oil across the bridge into the kingdom, whose forested uplands grow little wheat. Wagons and pack-trains from the kingdom bring down charcoal, timber, wooden goods and the work of Nartunese woodcrafters, which sell well on the estates. Every load crosses the bridge, and every load pays the emperor's customs at the Customs House on the way in or out.

The town lives on serving both. It has a horse and mule market, where the Nartunese pack-trains change animals, wheelwrights and harness-makers, inns for carters and drovers, the warehouses of grain dealers who buy on the estates and sell across the stream, and the agents of timber merchants who buy across the stream and sell on the estates. A few families of the town have kin on both banks, and the town's speech carries words of the kingdom's old-fashioned Vilari that a man from Aravantara does not use.

Pilgrims cross too. Devout Vilérians who find the empire's temples too easy come up the road to cross into the kingdom, and they cross under the eyes of the Order's chapel. Nartunese priests who come down to preach on the estates cross under the eyes of the Forge-priest.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

Ashenmere is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The council is made up of innkeepers, grain dealers and horse-dealers, and it keeps the wall, the gate and the town's night watch.

The Customs House is not the town's. Its officer and his clerks collect the emperor's customs for the _karezos_ (revenue officer) at Aravantara and answer to him alone. The present officer, [[being-ornthstaloth|Ornethos Talothates]], is a freedman of the revenue who has held the post for nine years, and he keeps a detachment of eight men of the province's urban cohorts to search the wagons and guard the bridge. He is reckoned honest by the carters, which on a border means that his clerks take the customs and nothing besides. A smuggler caught at the bridge loses his load to the revenue and is held in the town's jail until the _tavemoi_ fine him. A Nartunese subject who breaks the empire's law on the Hilen bank is handed back across the bridge to the priests' men, who take him home to be judged there.

## The Present {#present}

The Two Lights are the march's quarrel. The Hammer-Priest of the forge-shrine, Huraden, holds the Black Flame to be the corruption of his own god's fire, and the knights of the chapel on the kingdom's bank serve a kingdom that broke with the empire over the same faith. Nartunese priests come down to preach on the estates of the march, and the stewards who would rather they did not are the masters' men. Tenant households on the march have left their cottages in the night, hearths warm and nothing taken, and the stewards cannot say whether they ran across the stream. At the bridge the customs officer, Ornethos, has found a false bottom in a grain dealer's wagon that the dealer swears he never built.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The inns of Ashenmere stand inside the gate along the road, and each favors one trade: the drovers, the grain carters, or the Nartunese pack-masters, who keep to their own inn and their own table. The [Bridge Lamp]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, at the top of the road down to the bridge, has the best rooms in the town and windows that look across the stream at the Two Lights. A party bound into the kingdom takes the advice of its innkeeper on what not to carry across, and the innkeeper is right more often than not.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The empty house.** A family of tenants on an estate near the march is gone from its cottage one morning, the hearth still warm and nothing taken. The steward wants to know whether they ran across the stream, and why.
- **The preacher's escort.** A Nartunese priest means to preach on the estates of the march and hires a party to see him safely to the villages and back to the bridge. Several stewards would rather he did not arrive.
- **The searched wagon.** A grain dealer's wagon is held at the Customs House for a false bottom that the dealer swears he never built. He wants a party to find out who built it and what it was meant to carry across.

# The Truth of Ashenmere {#truth .secret}

The knights cross the stream by night. Men of the Order of the Sacred Flame ford the border above the bridge, out of sight of the Customs House, and take worshippers of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] out of the villages of the march: a smith who keeps the black flame in his hearth, a family that went down to the Night of Ashes in a port of Salkalon, a tenant overheard at a gathering in a barn. They carry them back across the water and up into the kingdom to be judged by the kingdom's priests, whose high priesthood broke with the empire over that faith. The empire does not proscribe the Black Flame, and every one of the people taken is a subject of the emperor taken from imperial ground by a foreign order of knights.

The forge-shrine points them out. Its Hammer-Priest, [Huraden]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds the Black Flame to be the corruption of his own god's fire and has kept a list for six years of every household on the march that he believes keeps it, and when the chapel lamp on the far bank is shuttered and shown three times after dark, he sends the next name across. The empty cottage is the eleventh household taken. The families of the march believe the takings are the work of slavers out of the hills and keep their doors barred.

Ornethos knows. A patrol of his cohort met the knights at the ford two winters ago and was told, by a knight who spoke the old Vilari of the kingdom, to go home; Ornethos reported nothing past his own door, because he is devout and because the knights were right about the smith. If the _karezos_, the _therkos_ or the Senate learns that the kingdom's knights are taking imperial subjects across the border, the empire has a quarrel with Nartun it cannot ignore, and the Two Lights become the place where it starts.
