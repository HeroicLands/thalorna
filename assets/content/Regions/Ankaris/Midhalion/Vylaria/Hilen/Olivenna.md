## Overview

You smell Olivenna before you see it. The town of five thousand lies in [[place-hylen|Hilen]]'s olive country and is built of presses and oil-merchants, and its sealed amphorae of olive oil travel across the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]].

Its prosperity rises and falls with the olive harvest and with the price of oil in distant markets. The press-owning families guard their groves and their pressing-rights jealously, and a stranger who asks whose press is the best will be told, in each house, that it is that house's.

## Arriving

The road to Olivenna runs for its last miles through groves of olive trees older than any house in the province, their trunks split and twisted into hollow columns a man can stand inside, still bearing. Low walls of stone divide the groves, and on each gate is painted the mark of the press its olives go to. The town itself lies in a shallow bowl among the groves, a cluster of tiled roofs round a market square, and behind it rises a hill of a strange reddish color that turns out at closer range to be broken pottery: the sherds of every amphora that cracked in the kilns or on the road, heaped up by the town's potters for longer than anyone has kept count.

The smell the Overview promises is of crushed olives, warm oil and the dried cakes of pressed pulp that the town burns as fuel, and in the pressing season it is in every room. Each press-house is a long hall built round its mill, a great stone basin in which a pair of upright millstones is turned by mules, and its press, a beam of oak as long as the hall is wide, weighted at one end with a block of stone. The press-houses of the five old families stand round the square, each with its mark carved over the door.

## What Olivenna Lives By

The olives ripen in late autumn, and the tenant families of the groves beat them down from the branches with long poles onto cloths spread under the trees. Through the winter the presses run day and night. The olives are crushed in the mill, the paste is spread on mats of woven rush and stacked under the beam, and the oil runs into settling-vats, where it is skimmed, graded and drawn off into amphorae. The first oil, from the lightest pressing, is sold to the capital and the temples. The heavy oil from the last pressing goes for lamps and soap, and the pulp is dried for fuel.

The press-owning families hold the pressing-rights of the groves around the town. A tenant of a grove owes his olives to the press whose mark is on his gate and pays a share of the oil for the pressing, and a tenant caught carrying olives to another house's press loses his tenancy. The potters of Olivenna make the amphorae, and an amphora sealed at Olivenna carries a clay stopper stamped with the mark of the press and the town. The oil-merchants buy the sealed amphorae and send them by wagon to the coast, and from there across the sea.

The town keeps a shrine of [[affiliation-lizelas|the Faith of Lizelas]] at the edge of the oldest grove, and on the first night of the pressing every press-house sends the shrine a lamp filled with its first oil.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

Olivenna is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The council is the five press families and the merchants who buy from them, and the two _tavemoi_ are by custom always a press-owner and a merchant. Their court sits in the square on market days and hears cases of pressing-rights, stolen olives and broken seals, which make up most of the town's law.

The families keep their own order. Each press-house has its overseers, its pressing gangs and its watchmen, and the watchmen walk the grove walls at night in the weeks before the harvest. A thief of olives taken in the groves is brought to the press-house whose mark is on the gate, and the overseer decides whether the _tavemoi_ hear of him.

## The Present {#present}

The five press families quarrel over their seal and their tenants. Amphorae with Olivenna stoppers are selling in the capital full of other oil, each family suspects another, and the council is looking for whose stamp was copied. A grove tenant lost his tenancy for carrying his olives to another house's press after his own refused them. A well-spoken man from the capital goes from press-house to press-house asking after a hand of fifty or so taken on since last winter, and the families answer him politely and tell him nothing.

## Where a Party Lodges

The [Lamp]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, on the square, is the town's inn for merchants and travelers, and it lights every room with the heavy oil of the last pressing, which smokes. The merchants' agents who come in for the season rent rooms over the press-houses. In the pressing season a party willing to work can be taken on at any press-house for the winter, fed and given a bed in the loft over the mill, and the families ask few questions of a hand who can lift his share of the mats.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The stranger at the presses.** A well-spoken man from the capital goes from press-house to press-house asking whether any family has taken on a hand of fifty or so, with soft hands and good teeth, since last winter. He offers a party a share of something large to help him look.
- **The broken seal.** Amphorae with Olivenna stoppers are being sold in the capital full of oil from somewhere else, and the five families each suspect another. The council hires a party to find whose stamp was copied.
- **The tenant's olives.** A grove tenant carried his olives to another house's press because his own press refused them, and lost his tenancy for it. He asks a party to find out why his press turned him away.

# The Truth of Olivenna {#truth .secret}

One of the press-houses is hiding a proscribed man. [Huradas Pramizis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a _kurthos_ (senator) of an old house of the capital, was named in a proscription two winters ago for a letter promising his support to one of the claimants in the succession while the reigning Orzas still lives. His property is forfeit to the treasury and his life to whoever takes it, and the price on his head would buy a press-house. He left the capital the night the list was posted and walked south in a carter's tunic.

He is in the loft over the mill of the [Pirkelen]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} press, the oldest of the five, working the mats under the name of Zenothen. The Pirkelen were clients of his house for six generations, and their master believes that a client who gives up his patron's son to the headhunters has no honor left to keep. The whole press-house knows who he is. The other four families do not, and the tenant turned away from the Pirkelen press was turned away because his brother is a watchman of a rival house, and the overseer would not have him in the hall.

The stranger asking at the presses is a hunter of the proscribed, working for the share the law gives to whoever collects. He knows that Huradas went south and that his house had clients in Hilen's oil country. He does not know which press, and he is patient.
