## Overview

The first thing a newcomer learns in Kervalara is where the imperial forest begins, because the wardens of the town will show him the line and explain what happens to a man found on the wrong side of it. The crown reserves the great game-forest from [[place-hylen|Hilen]]'s farmland, and Kervalara, a town of two and a half thousand wardens, houndsmen and lodge-keepers, stands at its edge.

The town swells when the court comes to hunt and drowses the rest of the year. Poaching the imperial deer is a serious crime, and the foresters of Kervalara are as much game-wardens and law-keepers as woodsmen. A warden walking a new man along the boundary puts the standing quarrel in a sentence: "Behind us, tenants who are hungry. Ahead, deer that belong to the emperor. Our work is to stand in between and keep both of them alive." The tension between the reserved forest and the tenantry around it is a standing feature of local life, and around Kervalara the penalty for poaching is the rope.

## Arriving

The road to Kervalara runs through tenant fields to the very edge of the trees, and the edge is a made thing: a ditch, a bank behind it, and on the bank a paling of split oak stakes taller than a man, painted at every hundredth stake with the eagle. The town lies along the outside of the paling for half a mile, a single long street of timber houses with the forest at their backs, and the gate into the forest stands at the street's head, guarded day and night by a warden with a horn.

In places along the road the paling stands in the open, a long way out from the first trees, with stubble and the stumps of old field-walls inside it and young oaks planted in rows among them. A tenant's cottage stands roofless inside the line beside one of those stretches. The wardens plant the oaks, and the deer graze the stubble.

At the head of the street stand the kennels, the stables and the [Imperial Lodge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a great timber hall on a stone footing with antlers over its doors by the hundred. Its hall is shut and dust-sheeted for most of the year, and its keepers air it on fine days. On the green outside the forest gate stands the gallows, which is not taken down between hangings.

## What Kervalara Lives By

The town lives on the crown's wages and the court's visits. The wardens patrol the forest and the paling, count the deer, keep the rides cut and the coverts thick, and plant oaks where the paling has been carried outward. The houndsmen breed and keep the imperial hounds, and the lodge-keepers keep the Lodge and its stables ready for a court that comes, in most years, for a few weeks in autumn. When it comes, Kervalara has a thousand more mouths for a month, and every family in the town earns its year in that month: beaters, grooms, cooks, laundresses, and the tenants of the villages round about, who are paid in coin and venison to drive the deer.

The reserve takes more land as the court asks for more deer. Every few years an order comes down to move the paling, the wardens carry it out into the fields, and the tenants whose fields lie inside the new line are moved to holdings further out or to none. The order comes from the capital, and the tenants blame the wardens, who carry it out.

The town's other trade is the law. Poachers, wood-thieves and trespassers taken by the wardens are brought to the forest court, which sits in a room of the Lodge, and the forest magistrate who keeps it is known across the province as a hanging judge. A tenant taken with a deer is hanged on the green within the week. A tenant taken with firewood is fined, flogged or sent to the legions.

The tenants of the villages round the forest keep a shrine of [[affiliation-lizelas|the Faith of Lizelas]] at the edge of their fields, where they pray for the harvest and leave bread for those hanged on the green.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The forest is the crown's and is ruled by its officers. The head warden, [[being-greltharvolk|Grelthar Volkemates]], has kept the forest for sixteen years and commands some two hundred wardens and houndsmen; he answers to the household of the Orzas in the capital, and the province's _therkos_ (governor) at [[place-aravantia|Aravantara]] has no say in the forest at all. The forest magistrate is appointed from the capital in the same way and sits in the Lodge.

Kervalara itself is too small to hold a charter and is governed by the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]] directly, through a headman the town chooses and the _therkos_ confirms. In practice it is the head warden's town. The wardens keep the peace on the street as well as in the forest, and a quarrel in an inn is settled by whichever warden is drinking there.

## The Present {#present}

The paling is moving again, and the villages are refusing. The order to carry it outward comes from the household of the Orzas, the head warden, Grelthar, carries it out, and the tenants whose fields it takes blame him. The forest magistrate in the Lodge hangs a tenant taken with a deer within the week, and a boy of fourteen waits on the green for a hind while his village raises a purse for a judge nobody has seen take one. The court comes to hunt this autumn after three years away, and several villages have refused to send beaters. A tenant whose cottage the paling took last spring went into the forest and has not come out.

## Where a Party Lodges

Kervalara has two inns. The [Horn]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, by the forest gate, is the wardens' inn, and a stranger who drinks there is asked his business before his first cup is finished. The [Paling]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, at the far end of the street, takes carters and the tenants' carriers and is cheaper and friendlier. When the court comes, every bed in the town is let to its servants, and a party without a place in the court's train sleeps in the villages outside.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The roofless cottage.** A tenant whose cottage the paling took last spring has gone into the forest and not come out. His wife asks a party to find him before the wardens do.
- **The hunt.** The court comes this autumn after three years away, and the head warden is short of beaters, since several villages have refused to drive. He hires a party to drive the deer and to watch the beaters.
- **The magistrate's sentence.** A boy of fourteen is to be hanged on the green for a hind. His village has raised a purse, and the forest magistrate is said to take one, but nobody in the village has met a man who paid him.

# The Truth of Kervalara {#truth .secret}

The head warden keeps a god in the forest. Deep in the reserve, half a day from the paling along rides no stranger is shown, is a grove of oaks older than the empire, and in it an altar of rough stone where offerings of wild meat are left to [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]], the Wild Beast, who holds the wilderness no law reaches. Grelthar is its shrine-keeper, one of the hidden keepers of [[affiliation-karnavos|the Faith of Karnavos]] who serve the god of disorder in secret while serving more orderly gods in public, and he took the shrine from the head warden before him.

At the turning of each season Grelthar leads a party into the forest by night: a dozen wardens, the oldest houndsmen, and the headmen of the villages whose fields the paling has taken. They hunt one stag with no hounds and no horns, kill it at the altar and eat it in the grove, and for that night the emperor's law is not kept in the emperor's forest. The stag of the grove is the only deer in the reserve for which no man has ever hanged, and the villages that send a headman to the grove have never had a man taken by the wardens.

It is a bargain as much as a rite. The villages keep quiet and their men keep out of the forest, the wardens hang only the poachers from villages that do not send a headman, and the head warden keeps the peace along a paling that takes the tenants' fields every few years. The villages that refused to beat for the court this year are villages whose headmen have stopped coming to the grove. The forest magistrate knows nothing of any of it. If the household of the Orzas learns that its head warden keeps an altar to the god of lawlessness in the emperor's own forest and feeds it the emperor's deer, the hanging judge has a warden to hang.
