## Overview {#overview}

Messora is quiet for most of the year and loud at harvest. It is a harvest-market town of about four thousand serving the estate country of southern [[place-hylen|Hilen]], and when the tenant villages bring in their grain, the estate-factors settle the season's accounts in its market square.

The great houses of the district keep storehouses and counting-rooms here, which is why the square matters. A factor who has spent twenty seasons at Messora tells a young colleague to arrive early and stay late: the grain is only half of what changes hands in those weeks, and the other half is paper.

## Arriving {#arriving}

Out of season Messora looks like a town waiting for something. Its square is far too large for it, an expanse of worn paving surrounded on all four sides by an arcade, and under the arcade stand some forty stone tables, each the height of a writing-desk and each carved on its front with the mark of a house. Behind the arcade the doors of the counting-rooms are shut and barred, and behind those the storehouses run back in rows. The town proper, its houses, its two temples and its inns, sits round the square's corners and is smaller than the square.

At harvest the square fills. The tenant villages come in by wagon and on foot, each behind its reeve, and stand in line at their house's table while the factor's clerks call each household's name, read what it owes and what it has brought, and enter the difference. Wagons of grain go round the back to the storehouses. Notaries set up folding desks between the arcade's pillars, moneylenders set up beside them, and the noise under the arcade carries to the fields.

## What Messora Lives By

Messora lives by the settling. A tenant household in the southern estates holds its land on a share: it owes its house a part of every harvest, a number of days' work, and whatever it borrowed in seed, coin or bread over the winter. Once a year all of it is reckoned at the house's table in Messora. A good harvest clears the debt. A bad one carries it forward, and a household that carries debt for three years running is likely to lose its holding, its sons to the house's labor gangs, or both.

The paper of the factor's lesson is everything written at the tables and the notaries' desks in those weeks: leases renewed or ended, debts carried forward, labor contracted, marriages between tenant households that need their houses' leave, and the sales of land. More of those sales are made each year. The district holds a few free farmers, families who own their fields outright and owe no house anything, and a free farmer who has had two bad harvests in a row comes to Messora at the settling and sells his fields to the house next door, which pays in coin and offers him the same land back as a tenancy. The great houses of the south hold more of the district every year in this way.

Between harvests the town lives on the counting-rooms. Each house keeps a freedman and two or three clerks there all year, minding the storehouses, keeping the accounts and the copies of every lease and will the house has made. The town's own trades are small: carters, inns, a smith, the notaries and a shrine of [[affiliation-olvenia|the Faith of Olvenia]], where the tenant households give thanks or ask for help, depending on the year.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

Messora is a chartered town under the [[affiliation-provnchyln|Province of Hilen]], with two _tavemoi_ (magistrates) elected each year and a council of _kurthaloi_ (councilors) who answer in their own persons for its taxes. The council is made up almost entirely of the freedmen who keep the houses' counting-rooms, which means that the great houses govern the town through their own former slaves. The _tavemoi_ hear disputes arising at the tables during the settling, sitting at a table of their own in the middle of the square.

Order among the tenants belongs to the houses and their reeves. A tenant who quarrels with a factor's clerk at the table is answered by his own reeve before anyone else, and a tenant who brawls in the square at harvest is taken home by his village and dealt with there.

## The Present {#present}

The settling is where Hilen's land changes hands. The freedmen who keep the houses' counting-rooms sit on the council and read the figures at the stone tables, and a tenant who cannot read them carries his debt into the next year. Free farmers sell their fields to the houses at the settling, and one of them swears he signed something else and that the notary who drew his deed has left the district. A freedman who keeps a counting-room wants a strongbox in his room opened before witnesses from no house.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

Messora has three inns, all on the square's corners. Out of season a party has its choice of rooms. At harvest every bed is taken weeks ahead by the factors and their clerks, and latecomers sleep in the storehouse lofts at a price the freedmen set and pocket. The [Tally]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the largest of the inns, keeps a table in its yard where the factors dine together on the last night of the settling and compare the season.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The carried debt.** A tenant household that has carried its debt for three years asks a party to stand with it at the house's table this year, because the household believes the clerk's figures are wrong and has no one to read them.
- **The free farmer's fields.** A free farmer sold his fields to a house at last year's settling and swears he signed something else. The notary who drew the deed has left the district.
- **The freedman's request.** A freedman who keeps a counting-room asks a party to witness the opening of a strongbox in his room, with no one from his house present, and will not say what is in it.

# The Truth of Messora {#truth .secret}

A counting-room in Messora holds a will that has never been read. Three years ago [Nizelas Yormalen]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, master of the [Yormalen]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a provincial house named in the old way for its estate of [Yormalenna]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} in the southern district, died at the estate, and the will read at his funeral left the estate whole to his nephew, [Revothas Yormalen]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who holds it now. That will was made eight years before the master died. In the last winter of his life he made another and gave it, sealed, to the freedman who keeps the house's counting-room at Messora, [[being-linthimspir|Linthimos Pirkelis]], with orders to keep it until it was asked for.

The later will also leaves the estate to the nephew, with one change: it frees every slave of the estate at the master's death, some three hundred people, and leaves each family the holding it works as a tenancy at a fixed share. Nobody asked for it. The nephew was told by a clerk that a second will might exist and burned the copy his uncle kept at the estate, and he has never thought to look in Messora.

Linthimos has kept it for three years. He was the old master's freedman, and he owes the nephew, his patron now, the duty every freedman owes his patron's house, so producing the will betrays his patron and keeping it betrays the man who freed him. He has decided at last to have it opened before witnesses who belong to no house, so that it cannot be buried again. He has not decided what to do after that, and three hundred people on the Yormalen estate do not know that they are free.
