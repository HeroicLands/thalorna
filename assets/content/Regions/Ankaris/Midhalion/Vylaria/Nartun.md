## Overview

Nartun is a city built up a hill with its gods at the top and its king below them. About sixty thousand people live in it, which makes it among the largest cities of the region, and it is the holy city of the [[place-nartumkngdm|Nartun Kingdom]] and the seat of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]], the independent Vilari-speaking realm ruled under its priestly aristocracy. The Holy Mother, the high priestess who anoints the kingdom's kings, has her seat here, and so does the king. It is the city whose high priesthood withdrew from imperial obedience about 224 AF ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]).

A priest-noble's secretary, meeting a newly arrived scholar at the gate, gives him one piece of advice before anything else: "Do not ask which clerk handles the temple's business and which the crown's. Here the same men answer for both, and the question tells everyone you are a foreigner before you have found your lodging."

## Arriving

The imperial road climbs to Nartun from the Hilen border at [[place-ashenmere|Ashenmere]], five days' carting through forest, and it is the only paved road in the kingdom. In its last miles it is lined with pilgrims' hostels, wayside shrines and the stalls of candle-sellers, and it comes out of the trees at the foot of a long wooded height with the city laid over it.

The walls are gray stone, and the main gate is a deep arch under a tall block of the same stone: the chapter house of the [[affiliation-sacredflame|Order of the Sacred Flame]], with a lamp in the window over the arch that is never let go out. Knights in white surcoats with the lamp on the breast keep the gate, and they ask a stranger his business, his faith and how long he means to stay.

Inside, the city climbs in three levels. **The lower city**, behind the gate, is timber houses three and four stories high with carved gables, the market square, the guildhalls and the inns, and it smells of woodsmoke and sawdust. **The temple quarter** above it rings the hill with twelve temples, one for each of the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian pantheon]], each with its school, its treasury and its houses of priests. On a terrace above the temples stands the king's hall, long and low, timber on a stone footing, with the royal banner over its door. At the summit, over everything, stands the [Mother's Hall]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the great temple where the Holy Mother sits and where every king of Nartun is anointed and crowned. Its roof of pale shingle shows from the road a day before the city does.

## The Temples and Their Schools

The twelve temples own most of the city and most of the kingdom, and their business fills the temple quarter from dawn: priors up from the estates with their accounts, barons come to renew a lease of forest, petitioners, pilgrims and novices. Every rite in them is said in the kingdom's ritual dialect, the oldest form of [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari]] still spoken, and every dedication on their walls is cut in [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon]] runes.

Each temple keeps a school. In the mornings the temple quarter is full of children with slates, the sons and daughters of carters and joiners as well as priest-nobles, learning to read the prayers and to reckon a weight of silver. Boys and girls meant for the priesthood stay on to learn the ritual dialect by heart and the runes stroke by stroke, because a priest who cannot read them cannot serve.

## Trade and the Guilds

The lower city lives on the forest and the pilgrims. [The Woodworkers' Guild of Nartun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} keeps its hall on the market square, and its masters (carvers, turners, joiners and the charcoal-burners whose work the guild judges) take the temples' commissions for altar-screens, panels and choir-stalls and sell the rest down the road to Hilen. The [Alchemist Guild of Nartun]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} works in a street of brick furnaces under the north wall, making pitch, potash, lye, dyes and medicines from what the forest gives, and answers to the priesthood for every license it holds.

Every coin in the city is weighed. The [House of Scales]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, the treasury of the temple quarter, stands at the top of the market square, a stone hall with brass scales on long tables and a priest-noble, the Keeper of the Scales, over them. A merchant up from Hilen brings his imperial silver to the House of Scales, has it weighed by a sworn weigher (_thradinos_), and leaves with a sealed tally of its weight that the city's shops accept as coin. Prices in the market are chalked as weights of silver.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order

The city belongs to the temples and the crown together. The Holy Mother and the high priests govern the temple quarter and everything above it, the king's officers govern the lower city, and the Order of the Sacred Flame holds the walls and gates for both. Most quarrels never reach either: each street of the lower city chooses a street-warden, who settles a theft or a brawl between neighbors and calls the king's sergeants only when that fails. A burgled shopkeeper goes to his street-warden first and to the king's court second. A blasphemer, a heretic or an unlicensed mage goes before a temple examiner, whose court sits in the temple quarter and answers to the high priesthood alone.

## Politics {#present}

The holy city waits on a judgment. The lower city says the high priesthood will sit in judgment on the king, [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]], before winter, and [[being-vrthlszrkms|Varthelas Zerkemis]] of Sélmoros is counting votes for it. The Holy Mother, [[being-ilvnssrkls|Ilvenas Sarkelis]], sits in the Mother's Hall above the king's, and the city reads every audience she gives as a sign. The Master of the Order of the Sacred Flame, [[being-sldrsplvrn|Saldoros Pelvaren]], holds the gates and answers to her. At the House of Scales a run of imperial Ardin has come up the road a twentieth light, and the Keeper wants to know where it is clipped.

## Where a Party Lodges

Pilgrims lodge in the temples' hostels along the holy road and inside the lower city, which are cheap, clean and strict: no weapons past the gate of the temple quarter, no meat on fast days, and the doors shut at the evening rite. Merchants and carters lodge at the inns on the market square, where the best is the [Charcoal Wagon]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, kept by a family of carters with kin in Ashenmere. Its innkeeper can say which temple's business is going badly, which baron is behind with his rent, and which knights drink in his back room.

## History {#history}

Nartun was the oldest sacred city of the Vilérian heartland long before it was a kingdom's seat. About 220 the faith of the Forge-Lord split ([[lore-ustaranschism|The Ústaran Schism]]), and when the Orzas Karomfos would not proscribe the Black Flame, the city's high priesthood withdrew from imperial obedience about 224 ([[lore-nartumrefuse|Nartun Refuses the Empire]]). It anointed [Daronos]{.name data-type="being/npc"} king, the first to take the throne from the priesthood's hand, and raised the Order of the Sacred Flame to hold the walls. The legions reached the city in the autumn, found the gate shut and the knights on the walls above it, and turned back at the first winter.

Every king since has been anointed and crowned in the Mother's Hall at the summit, and the kings the high priesthood deposed had the oil wiped from their foreheads in the same hall. The present king, [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]], was anointed there eleven years ago, after the Holy Mother refused his elder cousin for a winter.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The judgment.** A rumor in the lower city says the high priesthood will sit in judgment on the king before winter. A priest-noble house hires a party to learn which way the high priests mean to vote, and the king's own men want the same answer.
- **The light silver.** The House of Scales finds that a run of imperial Ardin coming up the road weighs light by a twentieth. The Keeper of the Scales wants to know who is clipping the coin, and whether it is happening north or south of the border stream.
- **The pilgrim's escort.** A devout widow from Hilen wants to be escorted up the holy road to the Mother's Hall to ask the Holy Mother's blessing on a marriage her family has forbidden. Her family has sent riders after her.

# The Truth of Nartun {#truth .secret}

The chapter house over the gate holds prisoners. Below its stables is an undercroft of cells, and in them are men and women of the Hilen march whom the knights of the Ashenmere chapel carried across the border stream by night as worshippers of the Black Flame. A temple examiner judges each one in closed session in the chapter house. Those who renounce the Black Flame are sent to work out a penance on a temple-estate in the uplands, under a new name, and their families in Hilen never hear of them again. Those who refuse stay in the undercroft.

The Holy Mother signs every warrant. The king has not been told, and the high priests who know keep it from him because he would stop it: a single imperial subject found in the undercroft would give the empire the quarrel with Nartun it has wanted for generations, and the king knows the legions turned back the first time only because winter came early. The rumor of the judgment in the lower city is half right. Several high priests want a king who would let the takings go on if he were told, and the king has a cousin who would.
