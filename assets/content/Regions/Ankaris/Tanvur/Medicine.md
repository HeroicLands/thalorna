A Yātvōr master takes a new apprentice's wrist in her fingers on the first morning and tells him to be quiet while she reads it. "Three pulses at each wrist," she says, "and every one of them tells you which part of this body has wandered out of its place. Your work is to put it back." Medicine in the [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Empire of Tānvür]] belongs to the Yātvōr (healers), the apothecaries and physicians of the [[lore-zathuk|Zāthük]] caste, and it rests on one idea: a body is sorted the way the world is sorted, and sickness is a body falling out of order.

## The Healers' Standing

The Yātvōr are Zāthük by caste and are held in a respect far above it. They have none of the political weight of the [[lore-tanthei|Tānthëi]] or the [[lore-venduk|Vëndük]], and they do not need it: everyone falls ill, from the poorest peasant to the Son of Heaven, and everyone needs a healer who understands how a body is put together.

## What a Healer Believes

The Yātvōr work within the cosmology of the [[lore-celestlrdr|Celestial Order]]. They teach that a body is a small copy of the Great Sorting: its elements, organs and flows of Lëi are each assigned to a proper place and held in proper relation. Illness is a breach in that classification—an element shifted out of its domain, a flow of Lëi turned into the wrong channel, a boundary between two of the body's domains worn thin. Four principles follow from it.

**Hëk and Lëng.** Health is the right balance of the two flows that govern the cosmos, Hëk and Lëng, within the body (see [[lore-phlsphylvr|Philosophy of Lëivōr]]). When one spills into the other's territory, the patient sickens, and treatment sorts them back.

**The five elements.** Each of the material domains—Air, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water—governs particular organs. A healer diagnoses by finding which domain is disturbed, then prescribes a remedy classed under the element that needs strengthening, or one that checks an element that has overreached.

**Body and spirit together.** Body and spirit are two sortings of one person, and the Yātvōr treat both: with meditation to settle the flow of Lëi, with petitions to the celestial bureaus asking that the patient's classification be restored, and with rites that set the patient back in step with the cosmic order.

**The Mandate of Health.** As the Emperor's [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]] keeps the empire in order, the healer's Mandate of Health keeps its people in order. Every person is classified to do a work—fight, study, farm—and a sickness that stops the work is a fault in the classification. The Yātvōr keep bodies sorted so that each person can do what the Great Sorting assigned.

## The Herb-Masters

The _Yātzāt_ (herb-masters) are the apothecaries. They gather, grow and prepare herbs, minerals and animal parts, and they class each one by its element, its quality of Lëi and its effect on the body's order. An herb-master's shop is shelved floor to ceiling with jars of dried leaf, root and ground stone, ranged by element, and a remedy is compounded for one patient's constitution and one patient's breach.

## The Physicians

The _Zhëkyāt_ (life-givers) are the physicians, who diagnose and prescribe. They learn four methods:

- **Pulse reading**, which follows the flow of Lëi through the body's channels.
- **Needling**, which turns Lëi by pricking the points where the body's internal domains meet.
- **Burning**, the heat of a smoldering herb laid on the skin to give the Fire domain to a weakened part.
- **Cupping**, which draws out Lëi that has pooled in the wrong domain.

A Zhëkyāt is valued above all for knowing the channels that carry Lëi between the body's domains, and how a disturbance in one runs on into the next. The best of them serve noble households as physicians to the Warlords and their families. The highest are called to the Emperor and the [[lore-zhekzhos|Zhëkzhös]], where the stakes are greatest: sickness in the imperial family is read as a sign that the empire's own classification may be failing.

## When the Cause Is a Spirit

Some sickness comes from the spirit side. An ancestor withdraws his advocacy, a debit on the moral ledger falls due as a fever, and the body pays for what the ledger owes. In such cases the Yātvōr treat the symptoms while a [[lore-ancstrwrshp|Lëigürt]] treats the cause, petitioning the ancestors, filing an appeal with the right bureau, or performing the cleansing rites that clear the debit. The two work together most of all at festivals and rites of passage, where a sick participant is held to put the whole rite at risk.

## Public Health

The Yātvōr answer for the health of whole communities: clean water, the quarantine and treatment of epidemics, and a watch on the health of every district. They work under the Warlords, and a Warlord counts his people's health as a measure of his own government, since a province swept by plague is a province whose classification is failing, and the court reads it so.

When an epidemic strikes, the Tānthëi are called to say whether it has a cosmic cause: the displeasure of a bureau, a breach in the boundary between the sorted world and the Hürnlëi, or a failure in the district's spiritual upkeep. The response then joins all three: the Yātvōr's remedies, the Tānthëi's diagnosis and the Lëigürt's rites.

## Training and Rank

A healer apprentices under a master for many years. The training joins the study of the medical texts, which are ordered by the five elements, with practice at the master's side in diagnosis and treatment. A Yātvōr rises through four ranks:

- _Thëizhëit_ (apprentices) learn the principles of the body's classification, prepare simple remedies and assist in minor treatments.
- _Yātgīrk_ (practitioners) have finished their apprenticeship. They keep their own shops or serve as household physicians.
- _Dākyāt_ (master physicians) have won wide respect for their skill. They treat high nobles and teach.
- _Vōryāt_ (grand healers) are held to understand the bond between the body's order and the cosmic order nearly completely. They attend the Emperor and the Warlords, and the Tānthëi consult them where health and classification meet.

The Tānvüri draw no line between health of the body and health of the spirit. Both are the same order, and both are kept the same way: by keeping things in their places.
