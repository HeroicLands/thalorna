**Merchants and Usurers**

The Shükrën comprise merchants, traders, moneylenders, and others who engage in commerce and finance but do not create goods themselves. Though they occupy a lower caste position than artisans, successful Shükrën can accumulate extraordinary wealth through trade. However, this freedom to prosper is heavily regulated and constrained by strict imperial law designed to prevent merchants from translating economic power into political influence.

## Roles and Responsibilities

### Commerce and Trade

Shükrën buy and sell goods created by [[lore-zathuk|Zāthük]] artisans, moving products between regions and connecting producers with consumers. They manage supply chains, negotiate prices, and facilitate the flow of goods throughout the empire.

### Finance and Lending

Some Shükrën engage in moneylending, offering credit to those in need. This practice, while legal under strict supervision, generates substantial profit but remains heavily regulated to prevent exploitation.

## Rights and Restrictions

### Rights

- **Right to Wealth**: Permitted to accumulate wealth through commerce and finance; financial gain is legally protected
- **Right to Travel**: Free movement within the empire; may hire [[lore-zathuk|Vüsrën]] guards for protection during travel
- **Right to Contract**: Permitted to enter into binding commercial agreements with other merchants and clients

### Restrictions

- **Obligation to Pay Taxes**: Subject to taxation at rates significantly higher than other castes; failure to pay taxes results in wealth confiscation, imprisonment, or execution
- **Prohibition of Exploitation**: Strictly forbidden from exploiting [[lore-nokvur|Nōkvür]] or lower castes; usury, unfair practices, and fraud result in fines, imprisonment, or execution
- **No Political Authority**: Absolutely forbidden from involvement in military affairs or political governance; any attempt to influence state policy is prosecuted as treason
- **Marriage Restriction**: NOT eligible for legal marriage; may engage in concubinage but have no family rights
- **No Personal Names**: Identified by merchant house names and business titles rather than personal names

## Commercial Regulations

### Contract and Trade Law

The Tëng Lüt contains extensive commercial law regulating Shükrën conduct:

- All significant contracts must be witnessed and recorded
- Prices for essential goods (food, medicine) are monitored and price-gouging is forbidden
- Fraud, deliberate overcharging, and misrepresentation are serious crimes
- Bankruptcy does not void debts; the merchant's assets are seized and distributed to creditors

### Taxation

Shükrën face a two-tier tax structure:

1. **Standard Commerce Tax**: Percentage of profits from all trade activities
2. **Wealth Tax**: Annual tax on accumulated holdings; deliberately hiding wealth to avoid taxation results in doubled penalties

Tax evasion is prosecuted with particular severity, as it directly threatens imperial revenue and is viewed as rebellion against [[lore-zheklung|Zhëklüng]] authority.

### Prohibition Against Political Involvement

The empire maintains strict prohibition against merchants wielding political power. This restriction is born from historical experience—merchant oligarchies and guild councils have attempted to rival [[lore-vushok|Vüshōk]] authority in other lands. Tānvür law eliminates such possibility through absolute prohibition.

Any Shükrën discovered conspiring to influence state policy, attempting to create a merchant council with governmental authority, or negotiating directly with [[lore-zheklung|Zhëklüng]] on matters of state is executed for treason. Their family and business associates may also face punishment.

## Enforcement and Social Standing

[[lore-vushok|Vüshōk]] administrators actively monitor Shükrën activities to ensure compliance with commercial law. Regular audits, surprise inspections, and informant networks keep merchants under constant oversight. Those who prove reliably compliant gain some degree of trust and may see their tax rates lightened.

Despite their wealth and economic importance, Shükrën occupy a lower social position than [[lore-zathuk|Zāthük]]. They are often viewed with suspicion and distrust—necessary to the empire's function but potentially dangerous if unchecked. Successful merchants may purchase luxury goods and employ retainers, but they cannot purchase social status or political authority.
