The Zhëkzhös are the people in [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]] who are never asked what they are, because the question has only one answer: the Emperor, his immediate family, and the Warlords (_Vüszhük_) who hold the provinces in his name. A clerk at the frontier way-station, told to describe the first caste to a classified foreigner, does it in a sentence. They are the administrative lineage of the mortal plane. Their authority comes from the celestial bureaucracy's designation of their family as the rightful administrators of the sorted world, and from nothing so unreliable as divine blood.

## The Son of Heaven

The Emperor is the **Tëngzhëi** (Son of Heaven), the celestial bureaucracy's appointed agent in the mortal world. His line holds the **Tëngvōr**, the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]]: an appointment that ties the Emperor's own Lëi to the Lëi of the empire. When he is sick the harvests fail, and when his household is in disorder the provinces rebel. He is not a god, and the Tānthëi are careful to say so. He is the administrator of the sorted world, and an administrator who falls out of order puts what he administers out of order with him.

## Who Decides

The Emperor's authority over state, law and war is absolute, and in practice he governs through three bodies:

- **the throne itself**, the Emperor and his household, whose decree is final;
- **the Warlords** (_Vüszhük_), Zhëkzhös of the imperial blood who hold the provinces and exercise the military, administrative and judicial power of the throne within them, with the [[lore-vushok|Vüshōk]] serving under them;
- **the [[lore-venduk|Vëndük]]**, the chattel sorcerers, whose counsel shapes strategy, ritual and the spiritual health of the realm.

Every final decision in governance, war and the law of faith belongs to the Zhëkzhös, and the Warlords are the ones who make most of them. The history of the empire is largely the history of how far a given Emperor could make his Warlords obey.

## Rights and Duties

The [[lore-castelaws|Caste Laws]] give the first caste three rights:

- **Final authority**: the last word in governance, law and war.
- **The appointment**: the sole right to decide matters of state, war and religion, by the celestial bureaucracy's designation.
- **The Vëndük**: ownership of the chattel sorcerers, bound to the Zhëkzhös from the day they are claimed.

And they hold it to three duties:

- **Defense**: the protection of the empire and all its people.
- **Order**: the preservation of the classification and of harmony with heaven.
- **Marriage within the caste**: a Zhëkzhös marries only another Zhëkzhös, by a match the clan council arranges.

## Names and the Number Nine

A Zhëkzhös may be granted a personal name for great service, as a Vüshōk or a Tānthëi may, and the names given to the first caste speak of dragons, of heaven's authority and of the Mandate. [[lore-personlnms|Personal Names]] describes the grant.

Nine is the number of the throne. Rites of the Emperor and his caste are reckoned in nines—nine steps to the dais, nine bows, nine dishes at a coronation banquet—because nine is held to be the fullest expression of the Tëngvōr. [[lore-numerology|Numerology]] explains the rest of the reckoning.

## The Elder Races

A [[lore-flksinale|Sinalo]] or [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár]] is classified Zhëkzhös only rarely: the lord of an Elder enclave, or the head of a house bound to a Warlord by a strategic alliance. Those who hold the rank manage their people's dealings with the empire, and their position lasts only as long as the politics of the court allow.
