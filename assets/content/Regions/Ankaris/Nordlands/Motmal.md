At [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]]'s great hov of [[affiliation-motefnir|Mótefnir]] a craftsman who swears his first work speaks half of a poem before he sets it on the shelf. **Motmál**, the Mould's Speech, is the hov's own account of how [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]], the Maker, pressed the first two of the [[lore-gnumborinn|Gnúmborinn]] out of the clay where [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrimthur]]'s flesh met the meltwater, cast as an exchange on the strand: [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]] comes down to breathe the fit into the drying figures, finds the Maker still at work on a heel, and the two trade question for answer until the figures have names. The hov's gudi speaks Odvar's lines and the telling between them, the newly sworn craftsman speaks the Maker's, learned by heart over the winter before his swearing, so that the first thing a maker says before the altar is that he is finished with what he made, and the Hofgudja speaks the last lines to him. The poem is in the speech-meter, and the hov holds it in memory only: no rune carries it, and the Hofgudja checks a swearer's lines against her own. One thing in it belongs to this hov alone: that the names of the first two were Odvar's giving. The door account, that death came in through the name he gave the woman, is what every hov of the Maker teaches, and [[place-odholm|Odholm]]'s rune-hall rejects both. Odholm agrees that the clay was a dead giant's and differs in teaching that people carry his death in them, and it sings no naming. The hov answers that the tongue keeps its account for it, since _dvern_, a birth, is built on _dvarn_, the door that is never barred.
