At [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]]'s great hov of [[affiliation-motefnir|Mótefnir]] a craftsman who swears his first work speaks half of a poem before he sets it on the shelf. **Motmál**, the Mould's Speech, is the hov's own account of how [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]], the Maker, pressed the first two of the [[lore-gnumborinn|Gnúmborinn]] out of the clay where [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrimthur]]'s flesh met the meltwater, cast as an exchange on the strand: [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]] comes down to breathe the fit into the drying figures, finds the Maker still at work on a heel, and the two trade question for answer until the figures have names. The hov's gudi speaks Odvar's lines and the telling between them, the newly sworn craftsman speaks the Maker's, learned by heart over the winter before his swearing, so that the first thing a maker says before the altar is that he is finished with what he made, and the Hofgudja speaks the last lines to him. The poem is in the speech-meter, and the hov holds it in memory only: no rune carries it, and the Hofgudja checks a swearer's lines against her own. One thing in it belongs to this hov alone: that the names of the first two were Odvar's giving. The door account, that death came in through the name he gave the woman, is what every hov of the Maker teaches, and [[place-odholm|Odholm]]'s rune-hall rejects both. Odholm agrees that the clay was a dead giant's and differs in teaching that people carry his death in them, and it sings no naming. The hov answers that the tongue keeps its account for it, since _dvern_, a birth, is built on _dvarn_, the door that is never barred.

::: {.verse form=speech-poem lang=en work=motmal}
The ground was there,/
and the gray sea on it,/
and the weather went over both, with nobody under./
Nobody walked it./
Nothing had a name yet./
The fells stood where the marks had said fell.

Where the flesh of the fallen/
met the foot of the rime/
the ground gave under a hand./
Two lay on the strand,/
two, and the tide out,/
drying, and dun, in the wind off the melt.

One knelt by the nearer,/
a knee in the wet,/
a thumb at the heel, smoothing the heel./
Odvar came down/
over the wrack-line./
Two birds were with him; both sat on the weed.

_Odvar said:_

What is the clay/
under your knuckles, Maker?/
I have had my hand in it. It is cold./
Say what it was./
I see what it is:/
a thing set to dry, with nothing in it yet.

_Mótefnir said:_

The giant. Here,/
where the ground was his gut,/
the flesh ran thin and the melt ran through./
Cold, because he is dead./
Clay keeps the cold/
of what it was cut from. Keep your hand in it.

_Odvar said:_

Is he in them,/
the one it was cut from?/
Does a pot remember the hill it was dug out of?

_Mótefnir said:_

The cold is in them./
Cold is not a man./
A pot off a hill is a pot. The hill is a hill./
He was dead when you cut./
The dead stay where they lie./
What I took was ground. The ground is not him.

_Odvar said:_

I came to breathe./
Are they dry enough for breath?/
There is color in the cheek that the clay never had.

_Mótefnir said:_

Dry enough for yours./
Do it when you like./
What the others bring, they bring. I made the pot./
I am at the heel./
The heel is not your part./
Breathe where the breath goes. Keep off the feet.

_Odvar said:_

Why on the strand,/
where the sea can come at them?/
Why not by a fire, where clay dries fast?

_Mótefnir said:_

Fire cracks it./
A pot fired wet/
splits down the thumb-line and leaks at the seam./
Wind dries it slow,/
and slow is sound./
The sea stops there. I marked where it stops.

I marked the cuts, too,/
before any of you cut:/
where the fells would come, and where the fields./
You had the blade./
The hammer had the bone./
Nobody cut until the marks were made.

_Odvar said:_

Where is the mould/
they were pressed in, Maker?/
I would see the shape before I see the thing.

_Mótefnir said:_

In the sea. I sank it./
It had served. A mould serves once./
A mould that is kept is a mould used twice./
The shape is there. It lies,/
the two of them, there./
The mould is in the sea, and the sea does not give back.

_Odvar said:_

There is your thumb in the hip./
There is your thumb in the throat./
You have left yourself all over them.

_Mótefnir said:_

My thumb is in everything./
Where the thumb is, I stopped./
There is no thing of mine without a thumb in it somewhere.

_Odvar said:_

Why two?/
One would have served./
You never made two of a thing before now.

_Mótefnir said:_

Once is all I make./
Once, and I go./
Everything I have made is the one of it./
Two can make a third,/
and the third a fourth./
That is the one thing I put in. No other thing of mine has it.

_Odvar said:_

This one is hollow/
where the other is not./
You have left a room in her.

_Mótefnir said:_

A pot is hollow./
A pot that is not is a stone./
The room is for the third. The third is not my work.

_Odvar said:_

What else have you made?/
I have met some of it./
Say what they were. Say what they are.

_Mótefnir said:_

A thing for the fords,/
that stands in the flow/
and is not moved by water or by men./
Gray as the river-stone,/
it grows no older./
It is standing there now. I was finished with it.

A thing for the high dales/
with wings too heavy for it,/
that drags them up the scree and sleeps on the snow./
It eats what climbs./
Its eyes are a goat's./
It has not flown yet. I was finished with it.

A thing to ward a pass./
The pass fell in./
The scree is where it was. The thing is watching./
Nobody told it./
Nobody needed to./
It will watch the scree. I was finished with it.

A thing to hunt one thing./
It hunted it down and ate it./
It is hunting still. It does not know what for./
It is good at it./
It was good at once./
Most of them were. I was finished with it.

A thing of parts/
that were never paired,/
the hind of a seal, the shoulders of a bear,/
that has not settled/
whether it is a sea thing/
or a land thing, and lies at the tide-line, settling.

_Odvar said:_

I did not ask for all of them.

_Mótefnir said:_

You asked what else./
Else is a long word./
The clay is wet yet. I have time.

A thing with no tail./
It has not told me/
what it thinks of that. It has nothing to say it with./
I was finished with it./
A thing that eats stone./
The grit is what it leaves. I was finished with it.

A thing for the ice-edge/
that is all ears and no eyes./
It hears a seal breathe through a hand of ice./
It is quick in the dark./
It is quicker than the ones with eyes./
It has not missed yet. I was finished with it.

A thing to carry./
It carries still/
what I laid on it, and I have lost what for./
Nobody unloads it./
Nobody could./
It goes on carrying. I was finished with it.

One that was wrong/
where the thumb went first./
I made it whole. It is out there, whole, and wrong./
Its head is on backward./
It sees where it has been./
It has not walked into anything yet. I was finished with it.

_Odvar said:_

Where is the bench?/
Where do you do this work/
when there is no strand, and nobody near?

_Mótefnir said:_

My hands are wet./
When they are dry, ask./
Hand me that shell. The heel wants a shell-edge.

The All-Father bent/
and brought him the shell./
Nobody before had asked him for anything so small.

_Mótefnir said:_

Hold the foot./
Hold it off the sand./
A heel dried in sand comes up with sand in it.

Odvar held the foot./
He held it a long while./
The birds on the weed watched the water go out.

_Odvar said:_

What are they for?/
When they are dry, what will they do?/
Everything you make is made for a thing.

_Mótefnir said:_

I do not know./
I did not finish them./
A thing finished does what it was finished for./
I have smoothed the heel./
The hands I leave rough./
What is rough they will work. They have the hands for it.

_Odvar said:_

They will walk, then.

_Mótefnir said:_

The heel is for that./
That is what a heel is for./
I would not smooth a heel on a thing meant to stay.

_Odvar said:_

What will they eat/
when they are up and walking?/
There is nothing on this strand but weed and wrack.

_Mótefnir said:_

What the ground grows./
What the gray sea gives./
I made them with mouths. The rest is theirs to find./
Breathe on them./
I am about done./
The heel is good now. Look at the heel.

_Odvar said:_

When they are dry/
and the breath is down in them,/
what do you call them? Say it over them.

_Mótefnir said:_

I do not name./
I am finished with them.

Odvar stood over the man./
Out went the water./
The birds on the weed were where they had been.

_Odvar said:_

This one is [hrandmil]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=nord}:/
a thing held down,/
set down first, and not for moving./
He was first on the strand./
He is first of them./
What is set down does not go looking for a place.

This one is [dvarnrinna]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=nord}:/
a door never barred,/
for the children will come in by her./
Leave it open./
Let nobody latch it./
A hand on the post, and a foot over the sill.

_Mótefnir said:_

Barred in which direction?

Odvar did not answer./
Odvar knew the ways out./
He knew what a word does once it is past the teeth.

He bent to the man/
and breathed, and the clay/
took the breath in and kept it, and was not clay./
He bent to the woman./
The birds on the weed/
shifted, and settled, and said nothing either.

The Maker got up/
out of the mud,/
wiped both hands on the moss, and went up the strand./
He did not look back./
He had not looked back/
at anything he made. He did not begin then.

The man sat up first./
He was slow at it./
He looked at his hands, and at hers, and at his./
She sat up after./
She looked at the sea./
The tide had turned. It was coming back up the strand.

_The Hofgudja says:_

He took nothing of theirs./
So the hov takes this:/
the first thing your hands made, and holds it for him./
Leave it on the shelf./
Nobody here lifts it./
Go out by the door. It is not barred.
