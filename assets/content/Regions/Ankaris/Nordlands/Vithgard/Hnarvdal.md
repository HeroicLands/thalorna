## Overview

"Send to Hnarvdal," a tally-keeper there says to the king's reeve who has come up to the valley because two jarls cannot agree how many winters have passed since an oath was sworn. "A skald will give you a year that suits his patron. The post gives you the year." The settlement's elders hold the kingdom's oldest reckoning-post, notched every midwinter for, by the valley's own reckoning, longer than [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] has had kings to date years by, and a dispute anywhere in the kingdom over how many years have passed since an oath or a debt was sworn is settled by sending to Hnarvdal and not by trusting a skald's memory.

The name is the notch cut to count a year off a tally-stick, and the valley has kept that function literally. Two hundred and fifty people live in it, sheltered from the coast's worse weather by the hills on either side, and counting the dark months is more than custom here: [[being-althyndeldskr|Althynda Eldskari]]'s chandlery supplies the tallow lights that get the valley through a polar night long enough that the reckoning-post's notch is the one thing anyone fully trusts to say when it ends. No hersvald keeps a hall in the valley itself. Hnarvdal answers to the one at [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]], a journey the valley's own elders make only when the post cannot settle a question.

The valley's quarrel with the wider kingdom is the reckoning-post's authority against the crown's. A king's reeve has more than once wanted a year dated differently than Hnarvdal's notch says it should be, and the valley has never given way, because a kingdom that cannot agree what year it is loses more than Hnarvdal does by insisting.

## The Fell above the Valley

Hnarvdal's fell is the setting of the kingdom's best-known tale of a seer. **Völva Aldrhildr**, the story says, vanished into the fell as a child and came back seven winters later with older words and a seer's knowledge. Some say hidden folk kept her and that a hearth-woman in the mountain taught her. The tale explains why Vithgard seeks her sight; it does not establish a public path into the fell. [[lore-vthgdhstry|Histories and Legends of Vithgard]] tells it.
