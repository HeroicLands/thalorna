## Overview

Hnarvdal takes its name from the notch cut to count a year off a tally-stick, and the valley has kept that function literally: the settlement's elders hold the kingdom's oldest reckoning-post, notched every midwinter for, by the valley's own reckoning, longer than **Vithgard** has had kings to date years by, and a dispute anywhere in the kingdom over how many years have passed since an oath or a debt was sworn is settled by sending to Hnarvdal rather than trusting a skald's memory.

Two hundred and fifty people live in the valley, sheltered from the coast's worse weather by the hills on either side, and counting the dark months is more than custom here—**Althynda Eldskari**'s chandlery supplies the tallow lights that get the valley through a polar night long enough that the reckoning-post's notch is the one thing anyone fully trusts to say when it ends. No Hersvald keeps a hall in the valley itself; Hnarvdal answers to the one at [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]], a journey the valley's own elders make only when the post itself cannot settle a question.

The valley's quarrel with the wider kingdom is the reckoning-post's authority against the crown's: a king's reeve has more than once wanted a year dated differently than Hnarvdal's notch says it should be, and the valley has never once given way, because a kingdom that cannot agree what year it is loses more than Hnarvdal does by insisting.
