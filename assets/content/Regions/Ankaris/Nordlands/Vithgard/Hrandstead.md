## Overview

"Read the contract aloud before you sign, and have the clerk read it back," a market clerk at Hrandstead tells the Norgaad trader who has taken a stall for the first time. "Here the jarl's garrison enforces what is written, and nobody has to like it." The trader reads it aloud, and the clerk reads it back.

Hrandstead sprawls across a plateau in the heart of [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]], a fortified settlement of roughly 1,000 souls that is the seat of its own province and one of the kingdom's principal strongholds. Wild bears roam the forests around it, and the noble line that has held power here for three generations, the **Bjarnaett**, takes its name from them, its banner showing a rampant bear worked in silver thread. A massive timber fortress with stone foundations dominates the settlement, its walls twice as tall as a man and its watchtowers visible for leagues across the plateau.

Hrandstead is first and foremost a military strongpoint, though time and prosperity have given it the amenities of a true town. The harbor, reached by the deep rivers that cut through Vithgard's interior, links the settlement to maritime trade, and merchant vessels bring goods from across the northern kingdoms. Workshops, tanneries and smithies ring the town, where the tools of war are forged and maintained. Regular markets draw traders from as far as [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]] and the frozen wastes to the north, and the town has a reputation for fair dealing and strong enforcement of contract.

The jarl's hall is the administrative and ceremonial heart of Hrandstead, where the jarl of the Bjarnaett holds court and settles the province's disputes. The settlement is a refuge during harsh winters and a mustering point for the province's defense, and its garrison of professional soldiers is regarded as one of the finest in the north. The rule here is martial strength tempered by pragmatic law: the law is enforced, but not with the capricious cruelty of some northern strongholds.

The Hrandstead ting also holds a judgment that outlasts the living. It keeps the empty farms of [[place-isdal|Ísdal]] for their dead, on the word of a [[lore-swornhandrnk|Sworn Hand]], until the last owner is laid.
