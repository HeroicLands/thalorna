## Overview

Dvarnvík's name is a door that is never barred, and the port has made a policy of the image: any ship that can reach it in weather is given berth and water regardless of its flag, a custom older than any living Harbour-reeve's memory, and nobody now knows why a kingdom this far into the ice ever decided an open door served it better than a guarded one. Four hundred and fifty people live by that custom now, a port busier than its size alone would suggest.

The [[lore-hersvaldrnk|Hersvald]] of the port district answers for keeping that door open even to ships **Vithgard** has reason to distrust, which is why **Thraldorv Vörnheim** earns steady work as a bodyguard to the merchants who do business with crews the Hersvald would rather not have vouched for personally. The town's craftwork runs unusually fine for its size—**Thalmthann Sólvargr** blows glass from sand brought north as ballast, and **Flarnthýra Steinblót** throws pottery glazed with minerals no other Vithgard settlement bothers importing—because an open door brings in trade goods a closed one never would.

Dvarnvík's recurring argument is the Hersvald's own custom turned against him: a door that will not bar a stranger cannot easily bar a debtor either, and more than one trading dispute here has ended with a ship gone before the ting could rule on what it owed.
