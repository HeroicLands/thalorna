[[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]] keeps long winter memories in its courts and in the work of its seers. A ruling can outlive
the witnesses who first heard it; a tale can change while the place it names remains.

## The Kingdom's Past

Vithgard's crown has been with the [Thalmidrok]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} at [[place-thrunborg|Thrunborg]] since the acclamations, and its law has been kept at [[place-logstead|Logstead]] since [[affiliation-eidhond|the Order of the Sworn Hand]] raised its mother-hall there about 330. The kingdom's hunger was as old as its kings: [[lore-threewinters|the Three Winters]] of 512 to 514 fell on Vithgard as hard as on [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]], and the kin-fell story is told in both. Its long dark gave it the two judgments below, the emptying of [[place-isdal|Isdal]] in 630 and the burning at [[place-askholm|Askholm]] in 679, both in the memory of men whose grandfathers saw them.

King [Tvarnthann]{.name data-type="being/npc"} died in the winter dark of 694 with two sons by two wives and no heir acclaimed, and the levies fought seven summers until a Sworn Hand brought the brothers to Logstead in 701; [[lore-vthgrdsccwrs|the Vithgard Succession Wars]] tells it. [Snirvmil]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is king at Thrunborg, [Glirvorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"} holds [[place-hrandstead|Hrandstead]] for his life, and the kingdom is quiet in the way a hall is quiet before a funeral.

## The Burning at Askholm

In 679, forty-one years ago, after a winter of failed whaling, a ting at [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]] accused eleven people of
working the winds. Seven women, two plateau men who had sold wind-knots and two Dvarnvík men accused
of _ergi_ were burned on the skerry called [[place-askholm|Askholm]]. Velva [Aldurhildur]{.name data-type="being/npc"} was accused but not tried. A
[[lore-swornhandrnk|Sworn Hand]] ruled that foretelling alone was no working against a named person.

The ruling survives as a precedent, while the deaths remain a grievance. Some plateau clans avoid
Dvarnvík. Descendants of the accused keep their own accounts of who spoke against whom. Repeating the
Hand's judgment protects a seer's prophecy from being treated as proof of sorcery; it does not
vindicate the burning of those who were tried.

## The Emptying of Isdal

In 630, ninety years ago, sickness went up [[place-isdal|Isdal]] in the polar dark. One child survived, remembered as the
[[place-isdal|Loft-Girl]]. The [[place-hrandstead|Hrandstead]] ting held the empty farms for the dead on a Sworn Hand's word until their
last owner was laid. The ruling remains a claim against descendants who want to reclaim the dale.
The dead have not all been laid, and its farms stand empty.

In a separate telling the sickness is a woman called the [[place-isdal|Grey Walker]], carrying a sieve and a
sack. Some say she served [[lore-nahilddty|Nahild]]; others say she gives a face to disease and
winter. The emptying and the ting's judgment are remembered history. The Walker's identity is not
known.

## The Fell-Taken of Hnarvdal

Velva Aldurhildur's story says she disappeared into the [[place-hnarvdal|Hnarvdal]] fell as a child and came back seven
winters later with older words and a seer's knowledge. Some say hidden folk kept her and that a
hearth-woman in the mountain taught her. The tale explains why Vithgard seeks her sight. It does not
establish a public path into the fell or prove that every missing child can be called back.

## The Ride over Hringstead

At neutral [[place-hringstead|Hringstead]], on the Nordheim–[[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] border, people tell of a high ride on [[lore-jol|Jel]]'s ninth
night. The tale says it once descended after a killing under [The Hall's Truce]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} and carried off the killer.
The Truce's living keepers need no supernatural verdict to forbid a murder there. Whether the ride
passed overhead, or answered that particular breach, remains a matter of belief.
