## Overview

Swear nothing lightly at Logstead. An oath made here is made before the hov of [[lore-eidgardty|Eidgar]], and the north holds the breaking of such an oath the gravest offense its faith knows. That is why the two brothers who had fought seven summers for [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]]'s crown were brought here in 701 to swear their truce ([[lore-vthgrdsccwrs|The Vithgard Succession Wars]]). The kingdom holds its own ting at this law-stead, and the hov of Eidgar swears its blet into the same oath the assembly swears over it. Ask anyone on the road in why the place matters and you get the same answer in fewer words: the offering and the law are bound together at one hearth here, and nowhere else in the north binds them so tightly.

The name says what the place is held from: _log-_, the law as it is recited, set before the generic for a farmstead.

## The Hov and the Hall

Two bodies keep one roof at Logstead and serve one god, and you do well to learn the difference before speaking to either. The **Hofgudi of Logstead** keeps the hov of the [[affiliation-eidgar|Faith of Eidgar]]: he consecrates it, ordains the gudar who serve under him, and keeps the ting that can be kept. [[affiliation-eidhond|The Order of the Sworn Hand]] keeps its mother-hall beside him, and its knights ride out to where the ting cannot reach. "A knight enforces a ruling with his own sword," the Hofgudi tells visitors. "I bind the oath the ruling rests on."

The order has kept its mother-hall here since its founding about 330, and its whole governance sits here. The [Council of Hands]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, twelve Elder Hands drawn from the five kingdoms, meets at Logstead every quarter, examines candidates in a final personal interview, tries a Hand accused of a broken ruling, and elects the order's chair for life. That chair is the [[affiliation-eidhond|Voice of Logstead]], _Logstadar Mal_ in the north's own speech, and the present Voice is [Knilthvir Logstadar]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a man in his late sixties whose judgments are quoted in halls that have never seen him. His wand of office is wrapped in black leather, and a traveler who sees a black-wrapped wand at a ting has seen the order's highest authority in person.

## What a Visitor Meets

Logstead lives at the pace of its courts. When the ting sits, the stead fills with claimants, witnesses and their kin, and every bench in every hall is spoken for. When the Council of Hands meets, the white surcoats of Sworn Hands from all five kingdoms crowd the yard of the mother-hall. Between sittings it is a working stead like any other, where the talk at the hearth still turns on precedent.
