## Overview

Flarnbrekka takes its name from the flakes of stone frost splits off an exposed slope, which is also a fair description of the settlement's livelihood: two hundred and fifty people work a scree-scarred mountainside for the silver ore running through it, prying loose with fire and cold water what frost has already half-loosened for them. [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]]'s mines are the richest in the north, and Flarnbrekka is one of the smaller camps that feeds the kingdom's reputation for them rather than one of the great workings.

No Hersvald keeps a permanent hall at this altitude; the district's officer comes up from the valley twice a year to weigh the settlement's ore against what the king's smiths at [[place-asgarthul|Asgarthul]] are owed, and between those visits Flarnbrekka runs on the same council of elders a mining camp has always used to settle a dispute over a vein two parties both claim to have found first. [[being-dagmrskaald|Dagmar the Skaald]], who has made Flarnbrekka's cold hospitality a theme of his verse for longer than most of the settlement can remember, keeps the camp's winters bearable in the way only a storyteller can.

The frost that splits stone for the settlement to work also closes its one road for months at a stretch, and Flarnbrekka's real argument is with the calendar rather than with any neighbor: ore mined in a good season is worth nothing until the pass opens enough to carry it down.
