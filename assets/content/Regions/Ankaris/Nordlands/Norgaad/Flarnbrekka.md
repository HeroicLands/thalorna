## Overview

The order of work at Flarnbrekka is fire first, then water, then wait, because the frost has already done half of it and cleverness only undoes that. Two hundred and fifty people work a scree-scarred mountainside for the silver ore running through it, prying loose with fire and cold water what the frost has half-loosened. The settlement takes its name from the same frost: _flarn-_ is a flake of stone split off by it. [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]]'s mines are the richest in the north, and Flarnbrekka is one of the smaller camps that feeds the kingdom's reputation for them, not one of the great workings.

## The Camp

No hersvald keeps a permanent hall at this altitude. The district's officer comes up from the valley twice a year to weigh the camp's ore against what the king's smiths at [[place-asgarthul|Asgarthul]] are owed. Between his visits Flarnbrekka runs on the council of elders a mining camp has always used to settle a dispute over a vein that two parties both claim to have found first. [[being-dagmrskaald|Dagmar the Skaald]] has made the camp's cold hospitality a theme of his verse for longer than most of the settlement can remember, and he keeps its winters bearable in the way only a storyteller can.

Flarnbrekka's real argument is with the calendar, not with any neighbor. The frost that splits stone for the settlement also closes its one road for months at a stretch, and ore mined in a good season is worth nothing until the pass opens enough to carry it down. A tale of the camp, in which nine dead kin return after a sickness and sit silent at the hearth until a [[affiliation-eidhond|Sworn Hand]] hears them at the door, is told in [[lore-nrgadhstry|Histories and Legends of Norgaad]].
