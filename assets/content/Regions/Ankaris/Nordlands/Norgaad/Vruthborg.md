## Overview

High in the [[place-flarnfjall|Flarnfjall]], above a pass that snow closes for half the year, a fortress of dry-laid stone stands on a spur over the mouths of its own mines. It is **Vruthborg**: _vruth-_, the temper iron takes from the fire, in its deep grade, with _-borg_, a stronghold on a height. The iron and silver that come out of the rock under it are some of the best in [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]], and the fortress was built to hold them: a curtain wall with a gatehouse, a hall, a barracks range for the jarl's drengir and miners, a treasury cut into the rock, and a foundry whose furnace was the pride of the district.

Nobody has come down from Vruthborg in a long time. The chimney still smokes.

## The Jarl

Vruthborg was the odel of **Brildorv Braldendik**, a jarl of the interior who held the workings and the pass from the king and paid his tally of iron and silver to the king's smiths at [[place-asgarthul|Asgarthul]]. He was a smith before he was a jarl, and he never stopped going down to the foundry. In his last winters the forge-cough took his breath, and he had no heir of his body. His odel would pass by the ting to a kinsman he despised, and his forge to whoever the king sent.

Herders on the last summer pasture say a guest went up the pass road that winter, a stranger on foot, and did not come down again. Nobody below the spur can say who he was. The household has not been seen since that winter, and the jarl has not come down to the ting.

## What Shows from the Pass

The fortress keeps its chimney smoking through the worst of the winter, and from the pass road below it looks like a hold in good order. Close to, it is not.

- **The gate is warm.** A traveler who climbs the switchback track finds the gate unbarred and its iron warm under the hand, in snow that freezes a man's breath on his beard.
- **The hammer.** From the gateway a hammer can be heard in the foundry, striking slowly, a long count between strokes, and the stones of the gateway tremble with each one. After each stroke comes a hoarse cry from inside the foundry, too regular to be a man in pain.
- **The watch.** On the wall-walk and in the courtyard stand the household's drengir, spaced as a watch is spaced, rimed with ice from boots to helm, and every one faces the foundry.
- **The heat.** Each stroke sends a breath of warm air across the yard that melts the snow on the cobbles for a moment before the wind freezes it again.

:::secret {title="The cry after each stroke"}
The cry is the jarl's voice, calling a name. At the gate the words do not carry, and it sounds like a scream; closer, at the fourth or fifth stroke, it is plainly a name from the household, called the way a man calls a name across a yard at first light.
:::

:::secret {title="Why the watch faces the foundry"}
The watch stands at its morning posts, waiting for its lord's word. Each one rose when the jarl called its name and went where the roll sends it, and between strokes it stands there and the frost takes it.
:::

## What Is Said of It

- **In the valley** they call Brildorv the Ashen Jarl, and they say he burned his own household in his furnace to buy his life from the forge-cough. The district's men say the smoke from the chimney is the household still burning.
- **The district's officer** comes up from the valley to weigh the ore of the mining camps against the king's tally, as he does at [[place-flarnbrekka|Flarnbrekka]], and Vruthborg's tally has not come down since that winter. He has not gone up to ask why.
- **The miners of [[place-skulfheim|Skulfheim]]** knock on their timbers and listen before they enter a gallery. They say the Vruthborg workings knock back every time.
- **The north** credits any stranger who teaches a dying man a way not to die to the [[affiliation-nghtwghtcncl|Nightwight Council]], and in the district they say the guest was one of theirs.
- **Travelers on the pass road** say that whoever climbs to the warm gate does not come down, and they pass under the spur quickly.

## Reaching It

By the pass road through the Flarnfjall, a day's climb above the last summer pasture, and then by a switchback track up the spur to the gatehouse. The workings under the fortress run through the mountain, and an old haulage tunnel carries ore down from them to a sledge-road on the far side of the pass, below the snow line. Late in the year, snow closes the pass road behind a party that goes up.

# The Truth of Vruthborg {#truth .secret}

**The guest's promise.** The guest who came up the pass taught Brildorv a binding and sold it to him as a cure. On the guest's word the jarl had his smith, **Knurvmil**, build a sealed chamber of runed iron under the furnace, its doors cut with runes the smith did not know, and set an anvil in the heart of the furnace itself. On one night of deep winter the jarl was to fire the furnace harder than it had ever been fired, climb into the heat with his hammer, and strike until the iron took the binding. In the morning, the guest said, the cough would be gone and his household would wake whole around him. At the guest's asking the jarl cut the names of his household on a stave, in his own hand, so that the binding would know whom to wake.

**The night.** The jarl fired the great firing as he was told. The flues from the foundry ran under the hall and the barracks, as he had laid them in his good winters so that his household slept warm, and that evening the guest had their dampers opened against the cold. The firing went through the flues into the floors, and before morning the sleepers were charred in their beds. The jarl, in the heart of the furnace, did not see it, and nobody has told him. The guest went down the haulage tunnel before the morning, and the staves record nothing of him after.

**The jarl now.** Brildorv is in the furnace, at the guest's anvil, his body held whole in the heat by the binding, and he believes it is still that one night. He strikes, and with each stroke he calls a name from the household roll, in the order of the stave, as he called the roll in the yard every morning of his life. He believes the cough has left him, as the guest promised. It has: he has not drawn breath since that night. He speaks to anyone at the furnace doors as a courteous host, offers them the fortress's silver, and asks them to stay until his people wake.

**The dead.** The household are _helthrall_, [[lore-undead|Helspawn]] of the driven kind, bound by [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] through the binding. Each stroke sends heat through the flues and the stone, and every dead thing in the fortress quickens with it and stiffens as it ebbs. The one whose name is called rises and goes to the post the morning roll sends it to: the watch to the walls and the yard, the stokers to the furnace, the miners toward the workings, where the cold stops them. That is why bunks in the barracks stand empty with their blankets thrown back. A risen dead thing strikes down whatever stands between it and its post, and the watch turns on strangers within the walls, as a living watch would.

**What feeds the binding.** The binding is renewed by every life burned in the chamber, and it feeds through the stokers, not through the jarl. Six dead stokers keep the furnace: two at the feed-chute, two at the bellows, two at the doors. When a traveler comes within the walls, the watch takes him and the stokers carry him down the stair behind the furnace and chain him to a ring in the chamber, and the strokes quicken toward a heat that burns him. The jarl does not know what the stokers carry down the stair. He believes they are his living men at the morning heat.

**The household now.**

- **The frozen watch,** twenty drengir, stands in the courtyard and on the wall-walk at its morning posts.
- **The barracks dead,** miners and servants, lie in their bunks where they burned, except those already called.
- **The stokers** work the furnace and leave their stations for nothing but a traveler to carry down.
- **Krildinda,** a miner, is alive. She was at work in the deep galleries that night, where the cold keeps the heat out, and came up at morning to find the floors hot and the household dead. She has lived in the old workings since, coming up for the gatehouse stores between strokes, and she believes what the valley says of the jarl. Her name is on the roll.
- **A traveler,** the last the watch took off the pass, is chained in the binding chamber, alive, praying.

**Stopping the strokes.** The jarl stops striking for a while whenever his belief is broken: a dead body of his household carried to the furnace doors where he can see it, a living voice answering its name to the roll, the last stave read back to him with what became of each name on it. Each of these stops the strokes, and with them the heat, for a time; then the binding drives his arm again. A jarl who has been told answers as himself, and asks what became of the guest.

**Breaking the binding.** Iron heated in the chamber and quenched cracks like glass, and Knurvmil proved it on a test-bar now lying split in the smithy. Above the foundry stands a quench-dam of snowmelt with a sluice onto the casting floor, built at the guest's order for a purpose the staves do not give. Cold water let in while the furnace is at its hottest floods the casting floor and the chamber stair, the runed iron cracks, the doors split, and the furnace dies; every dead thing in Vruthborg stops where it stands. The jarl's body, carried out through a gap cut in the foundry wall as the [[lore-nrdlndsfnrl|rite]] requires, does not rise again, and neither do the household carried out the same way.

**The cold and the ways out.** The heat never reaches the old workings under the fortress, and any dead thing that follows a traveler there slows, stops and stands rimed in the gallery. The workings come up at the gatehouse cellar, at an ore-hoist under the foundry, and at the foot of the quench-dam's stair. The hoist shaft carries the sound of the chamber: the hammer, the traveler praying, and the jarl's voice counting the strokes and calling the names. The haulage tunnel runs from the lowest gallery under the pass to the sledge-road below the snow line.

**The treasury.** The vault behind the hall holds silver ingots stamped with the jarl's mark, his gold arm-rings, and a rack of weapons from Knurvmil's forge. Those cut with the smith's mark and the year-rune of the jarl's good winters were made before the guest came. The later ones were tempered in the chamber, stay warm to the touch, and are the blades the watch carries. The vault's key hangs on the jarl's belt in the fire.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **"The Ashen Jarl burned his household to buy his life"** is false. He fired the furnace for a cure, and his own flues killed them on a night he believes is still going on.
- **"The smoke is the household still burning"** is false. It is the furnace, and the household is in the yard, the barracks and the foundry.
- **"The workings knock back"** is true. Krildinda answers a knock in the miners' way, to learn whether whoever knocks is alive.
- **"The guest did not come down"** is false. He left by the haulage tunnel before the morning.
- **"The guest was the Nightwight Council's"** is the north's guess. The runes on the chamber doors are all the trail there is.
- **"Whoever climbs to the warm gate does not come down"** is true. The watch takes him to the stokers.

> [!WARNING]
> The jarl's ignorance is the secret of the fortress. Play him from the first as a host who expects his people to wake, never as a man who knows what he has done; a jarl who gloats or bargains for fuel gives back the valley's telling, and the truth with it.
