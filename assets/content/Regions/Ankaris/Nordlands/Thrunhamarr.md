Thrúnhamarr is the hammer of [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrúnvald]], depicted as powerful enough to crush mountains and summon thunder and lightning. The [[affiliation-thrunvald|faith]] treats it as both the god’s weapon against the forces pressing on the ordered world and a tool of blessing and protection, joining battle and the sanctifying of ships in one symbol. The hammer stone represents that strength and protection. In the saga of [[being-skrldmylstrmbrn|Skrildmýl Stormborn]], [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]] is the mountain where the god is said to have struck the earth with his hammer.

## The Hammer Held

A coastal telling joins the hammer's battle and blessing in one refrain, carrying its praise from the harbor to the god's final encounter.

: The Hammer Held

```poetry {form=epic lang=en}
Hear the hammer before praise:
clouds closing their doors, the long shore holding its breath,
a gull hung white for a heartbeat over dark water,
then thunder rolling the hills like stones in a hand.
Thrúnhamarr, sing the weight that a shelter must bear.

The Thunderer stands where the ordered world thins to foam,
where walls meet the strength pressing inward.
Behind him are roofs, and the smoke of ordinary suppers;
before him the hunger that gives no name to its taking.
Lift, hammer; remember the lives sheltered under your shadow.

The singers make mountains crumble beneath that terrible striking.
They give every flash a bright road through the black,
and every road ends where the great hand closes again.
We hear in the blow the refusal of the defender:
thus far the dark, and here the arm that answers.

No ear has heard that battle entire.
Our rafters would break with the breadth of its sounding;
our tongues know the thunder that reaches the coast before dawn.
We fashion from listening the words that our children can carry,
and set its weight in song.

Lower the singing; let sea wind enter.
A ship waits below, with her ribs smelling sweetly of timber.
Hands move along rope, testing each strand before parting;
the tide gathers strength without caring whose sons are aboard.
The hammer is named, and the blessing goes over her prow.

The weapon whose stroke lays mountains open,
held in the words of the godi above working sailors.
Their shoulders bend to the burden of leaving;
the god's great strength is invoked for the crossing before them.
Hold, hammer; remember the lives committed to water.

The shell at the hof has the curve of a listening ear.
The hammer stone keeps in its silence the shape of protection.
One tells of the sea that swallows vessels,
one of the arm raised against what would swallow the world.
Between them the faithful stand, small as sparks beside iron.

The boat slips away; the blessing travels in memory.
An oarsman feels wood rasp into his palm.
A woman ashore watches dark water close over the wake;
a child learns the distance of waving.
Their names are the freight that the thunderous song must carry.

Sing also the reavers who ask for the Thunderer's favor,
the warriors whose hammer blows answer the charge of their foes.
The sea takes their boasting and breaks it to salt on their lips;
the bright shield tarnishes under the spray of the crossing.
Strength has a burden beyond the loud hour of its showing.

The hammer lifts in a hand weary of lifting.
The watcher may stand with cold rain threading through wool.
The heavy trial measures the distance a body can carry;
the vigil makes darkness a thing faced one breath at a time.
Hold, hammer; remember the arm beneath your great emblem.

At Thrumufjall the singers turn their faces toward stone.
There, says the saga, the Thunderer struck at the earth;
there Skrildmýl climbed with his people through winter's white anger.
The same name rang over the mountain and over the harbor,
a weight borne upward, a strength asked down from the sky.

Hear how that journey puts feet beneath the great legend:
the stride of the leader, the stumbling that others must steady,
the wind taking speech till a mouth has to labor for prayer.
What mountain could teach us the measure of such a weapon?
Its shadow spreads; the climb is step by step.

The god has the strength to shatter the summit;
the people beneath have the strength to keep hold of each other.
Let neither be lost in the thunder the storyteller summons.
When heaven is loud, hear the breathing that rises against it.
Lift, hammer; remember the mortal weight of the asking.

And now let the hall grow still for the farthest encounter.
Aldarlok waits at the edge of the song like banked fire.
The World-Wyrm faces the Thunderer; venom waits with its teeth.
The hammer rises against the thing that will kill its wielder.
What hand is worthier than one that lifts knowing?

Heimsormr falls in the telling; the god walks nine steps.
Let the boast stop there, at the limit the priesthood keeps.
No tenth stride comes galloping out of a singer's desire;
no louder acclaim changes venom to rain on his beard.
Hold, hammer; remember the price of the victory sung.

We watch the hearth where embers settle.
The cup passes between warm living hands.
Outside, the waves keep striking the dark foot of the coast.
Inside, the song gives the dead their weight in our hearing,
and lends to our hands what a hand may yet carry.

Tomorrow a ship will need blessing before she can sail.
Tomorrow a roof needs mending before rain.
Tomorrow the guard will grow tired while the darkness grows longer.
The hammer's great thunder goes into these small acts of holding,
like strength in a beam that no guest at the feast ever sees.

Speak Thrúnhamarr clearly; give the word room in your breathing.
The name is no charm for forgetting the work of the crossing.
Let rope answer hand, let the lookout keep watching the water,
let those who are strong take their share of the burden beside them.
The god's great arm is praised when the shelter still stands.

We have sung of the mountain; we have sung of the vessel.
We have set the last steps by the first strokes of rowing.
The shell hears the sea, and the stone bears the hammer's likeness.
What joins them is held in the pause before thunder returns:
the living world, fragile, defended, and dear to its keepers.

Lift for the threatened; hold for the hands that grow weary.
Strike where the hunger comes crushing against the world's boundary.
Bless the ship sailing out under a sky without promises.
Thrúnhamarr, carry our song where our voices cannot follow.
Remember the shelter. Remember the hands. Let the hammer be held.
```
