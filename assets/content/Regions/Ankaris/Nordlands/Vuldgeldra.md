Among the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] of [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]], the **Vuldgeldra** is the praise-poem the Hirvskáld [Hliskvir]{.name data-type="being/npc"} made for **Jarl [Krildorv Kraldaruk]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, who holds one of the five [[affiliation-kingdmnrgd|Norgaad provinces]] whose jarls sit at [[place-domsey|Domsey]], for a winter in which the jarl's fjords went hungry and nothing happened that a _geldra_ ordinarily sings. It was first given at the close of [[lore-sumarmal|Somarmál]], after the kings came home from Domsey and its skalds had begun reciting, and [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|the Skalds' Circle]] has carried it as standing repertoire since the [[lore-skaldating|Skaldating]] after: it is the piece an apprentice's own _geldra_ is measured against, and the Circle teaches it as the model of the truth of the recital. The text below is the repertoire text as one Stóraldstjori teaches it to apprentices at the Skaldating, with his gloss of the kennings between the parts; the gloss is his, and other masters read one or two of the _hulmur_ otherwise.

Sit. This is the one your own _geldra_ will be measured against, so you hear it first and not last. Six syllables to the line, two lines bound by their onsets, a chime inside the line and nothing at its end, and the clauses laid across one another so that a hall has to work to get the sense out. A _geldra_ is paid with a ring, and a ring should buy something a thrall cannot follow.

I will not take the names apart for you. Krildorv is a name and [Kraldaruk]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} is a clan, and a stem is glossed when a stranger asks and never rendered. Everything else in it I will take apart, except one stanza.

He names the kind of poem in the first line by its price, and what he will not sing in the third and fourth. The cloak's raven is a Hirvskáld, from the brooch he wears. The reddened sail is a raid, and a small [[place-vigvoll|Vigvoll]] is a battle, because every battle is the last one, smaller. The wolf's long turn at the chain is winter: in the telling, [[lore-vetrulfur|Vetrúlfur]] is chained until the end, and winter is in his name. "Notch by notch" is the tally-stick, and the _hnerv_ is built on the same stem.

The thin flesh is the fields. In the telling, [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrimthur]] had little flesh on him, and what there was went thin over the bone when the Ten of the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian Pantheon]] cut him, so a furrow's bone is the rock under a field and "the thin flesh gave little" is a bad harvest. The blade-bone is the shoulder read at the [[lore-veturnetur|Veturnetur]] blet. The steward's stick is the buvordur's tally of the pits, and it said worse than the bones, which is Hliskvir telling you the bones were kind. The giver is [[lore-solrundty|Solrún]], whose gold is given away. She takes her portion whole in a hungry year, and he wants you to know nobody cut it.

Stanza three you will work out yourselves. I am not asked about it, and I do not answer.

A ting called in snow with no sword and no feud is a ting called for one thing. "Loud, the lock of a jarl" is no _hulma_. The whole district hears a jarl's pit opened.

The fire's kept coal is the hearth. The hand that stayed is the scale. In the telling, [[lore-eidgardty|Eidgar]] paid a hand to the wolf's chain, and what the other hand holds is the iron scale, so the hand that stayed is the thing in it. A master whose apprentices sit on the bench across from you teaches that it is the sword, because the god is carved holding either. He has never stood at a rationing. Nobody brings a sword to the floor to weigh barley. The Healer's gold is [[lore-frodvindty|Frodvin]]'s barley.

The sixth stanza has no kenning in it. He put none there. A rationing is a plain thing and he gives it to you plain, and then he cuts the first notch. The notch comes before you know what it counts. That is what a notch is: you cut it when the year falls, and you count it later. "His bench on Domsey bare" is a _tingsveld_, a stone bench with the holder's mark cut in its back, and his stood empty at the assembly that year. Apprentices ask me every year what the moss stands for. It stands for moss. The island is wet.

The giant's spilled cold is the sea, from his blood, and where the blood meets the flesh is the tide-line, the sea on the soil, which is the strand the law names. The fat hill is the whale, and he never says whale, because the word is small and the thing was not. The law-mouth is the Lawspeaker, and the sixth is the sixth rule as Norgaad's Lawspeaker counts [[lore-nrdlndswhal|the law of the stranded whale]]: in a hungry winter the jarl shares it, and the strand is repaid from the next good season. The jarl's own words stand unbroken in the seventh and eighth lines. Flat light is the oil, which is the stem's own sense and barely a _hulma_. Nobody carved the jaw: no gatepost stands at [[place-gnaldurfjord|Gnaldurfjord]] for this one.

The cold's hard hem is the ice-edge, where the sealers go in the hungry weeks. "Twice the floes groaned" is two deaths. Gnaldurfjord is named for the sound its ice makes, and every coast in the north hears that sound as somebody dying. Sing it inland and it is a line about ice. He has the two by name, and says so, and keeps them out. A _geldra_ is bought and a _hnura_ is not, and the nine are named in the household's _hnura_, which the Circle does not teach.

[[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]]'s floor is the sea again, and he has two words for it. When it gives, it is the giant's cold. When it is sailed, or it takes, it is the Thunderer's. The shingle crying is a keel, from the sound a keel makes going off and coming on, and the keel's own word is built on it. The south's yellow is grain from [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]]. The ring off his arm went for the buying.

The seventh year's boat is the king's boat come for his jarls, and the grant moves: a king who moves the grant moves the seat with it. He knew it and let the boat go. Hliskvir was not on Domsey. "They tell me" is how he says so. The king's answer is a look at the law-rock, and he gives you no more because he had no more.

The hall roofed in bone is the whale's ribs where the gale had the roof. "Fewer" is the first time he tells you anyone died at the door-seat.

The sixteenth stanza is the debt entered. A debt said at a feast and carried by a skald is in the hall's [[lore-kinhalcrdt|standing credit]] from that night, so a debt said is a howe, and nobody digs in it: digging in a howe is theft from the dead and the line together. He has said it at two Jels so far.

The cliff is the Three Winters. The tellers have gray heads going over it, and some skalds give the five kings' oath as history. Hliskvir will not; he cuts no notch he did not see. The one-eyed's birds are [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]]'s ravens, which wheel over the cliffs at Gnaldurfjord, and the brooch on a Hirvskáld's cloak.

By the wall is dead: a hall cuts a gap in its own wall to carry the dead out and closes it behind them, and the door is for the living. The stones on the headland are the drowned men's, cut with runes so they do not ride with the [[lore-jol|Jel-Ride]]. One came in by the door: a birth is a door in the teaching of the hovs of the [[affiliation-motefnir|Faith of Mótefnir]], which is the teaching of Hliskvir's hall, and [[place-odholm|Odholm]] would have given you [[lore-gnumborinn|the giant in the clay]] instead. No voice and no fire is a thrall, buried without a pyre, with a tool put in by a master who thought well of him. Where the sickness-dead sit is the hovs' quarrel, and a skald who settles it for pay recites what he does not know.

The ninth you have had since stanza three. I said you would work it out. "Fed first" is the oath's word. "The bowl was full" is Hliskvir's.

He ended on "Give the ring, or do not," before the jarl and the jarl's hall, in the spring after the jarl's mother went out by the wall. Krildorv gave the ring, with one ring fewer on his arm to give it from. No master has been asked to soften the close, and I have said what I will say about it.
