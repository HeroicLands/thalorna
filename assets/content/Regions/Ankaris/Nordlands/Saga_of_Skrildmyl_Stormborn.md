Among the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]], this is one telling of [[being-skrldmylstrmbrn|Skrildmýl Stormborn]] leading his people through winter storms to [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]]. Whether he once walked the north or belongs to its songs, the skalds praise his endurance and the clearing they attribute to [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrúnvald]]. The Winter Witch claims the storms as her own work; her claim travels beside the tale, unanswered.

: Saga of Skrildmýl Stormborn {#saga}

```poetry {form=epic lang=en}
Hear now, hearth keepers,
hold still your cups.
Snow shakes the shutters;
sparks climb the smoke.
Bench boards bear us;
burden bearers are sung.
Skrildmýl, storm tested,
stands in our telling.

Hammer hard his schooling,
harder the shield work.
Fathers taught him fighting;
folk needed more than blows.
Fire must be tended;
food must reach the frail.
Thrúnvald heard their homage;
thunder hallowed their hope.

Blue eyed the bearer,
broad backed the chieftain.
Braids bound his hair;
bright sparks stirred its ends.
Silver shaped the thunderbolt,
securing his heavy cloak.
Hammer hung at hand;
his shield sheltered his kin.

Battle bore his bellow;
bold men braced beside him.
Lightning leapt from iron;
lines of fighters faltered.
Still, the sterner struggle
sat with him at supper:
people placed their welfare
upon his watching shoulders.

Winter worried the roof beams;
wind worried the door.
Snow swallowed the pathways;
sleet smote the walls.
Bread baskets grew lighter;
breath whitened by beds.
Fear filled the benches;
fearful eyes sought their leader.

Thunder, where is your keeping?
Tell us why roofs fail.
Children crouch by embers;
cold crawls beneath their cloaks.
Old hands shake with hunger;
our hearth cannot hold out.
Voices varied in asking;
one dread drove their words.

Skrildmýl sat and listened;
silence steadied his heart.
Faith faces a testing;
folk must hold together.
Thrumufjall towers beyond us;
there the hammer struck earth.
We shall seek that summit,
speaking the god's name there.

The hammer had honored battles;
here no enemy showed.
Winter wielded no weapon
that iron could break.
He bore a greater burden:
the belief of frightened folk.
Their eyes asked for assurance;
his heart answered with faith.

High stood the hammer mountain;
hidden stood its crown.
No eye knew the pathway;
no ease softened the choice.
His people packed their burdens;
prayer went with provisions.
Skrildmýl shouldered the weather;
steps followed where he stood.

Snow struck their faces;
speech froze in their mouths.
Wind whipped their clothing;
white darkness closed around.
Shield could shelter no hillside;
shoulders could turn no sky.
Still he strode before them;
still they stayed beside him.

Down in the drifted village,
doors had groaned like ships.
Here, high above the houses,
hope must walk on feet.
No hall could hold them;
no hearth warmed their hands.
They carried their common courage
where comfort could not come.

Keep close, called the chieftain;
keep courage for the climb.
Feet found uncertain footing;
fingers failed at their loads.
Breath burned within bodies;
bitter air took its toll.
Strength shared the hardship;
strong arms steadied the weary.

Rank won no refuge;
rich cloth froze like rags.
Young knees shook with effort;
years weighed upon the old.
Each pace asked a choosing;
each pause threatened the march.
He chose where they could see him;
hope held their steps together.

Sing steadfast the leader;
sing also those who followed.
Many made that mountain road;
many bore its mortal cost.
Trust travels behind a chieftain;
terror travels there too.
A hard heart may hurry;
a heavy duty must hear.

Higher, harsher the mountain;
harder each breath to draw.
Home hid beneath the snowfall;
hearth light lived in memory.
Thrúnhamarr had struck here;
the telling held that hope.
Skrildmýl stopped at the summit;
snow streamed across his shield.

Thrúnvald, thunder keeper,
take heed of these voices.
Hammer lord, hear us;
here your people stand.
Flesh has fashioned our offering;
faith has brought us uphill.
Strength should shelter the living;
shield those who honor you.

No warmer world awaits us;
no other lives lie stored.
Our children need their mornings;
our kin need their hearths.
Hear the hammer mountain;
hear the hungry mouths.
The cry climbed beyond them;
clouds crowded his words.

Still stood the mountain;
still hung the unanswered breath.
Then wind withdrew its fury;
white veils fell apart.
Clouds cleared from the summit;
cold sunlight struck the snow.
Faces found their neighbors;
fear loosened its hold.

Light lay on the living;
long shadows marked their feet.
Hands held their burdens;
hardship had not departed.
Yet the sky yielded brightness;
yet the storm fell still.
They spoke the thunderer's name;
their speech grew strong again.

The mark of the god's hammer
mattered more than stone.
Memory made the mountain
a meeting place for prayer.
The peak received their voices;
the people received the light.
What passed between earth and heaven
passes through our mouths.

Downward now the journey;
daylight showed the slopes.
Bodies bent with weariness;
breath still misted the air.
They had seen their leader calling;
he had seen them endure.
Neither forgot the other
when the road led toward home.

What wealth won the chieftain?
Warm faces beneath their hoods.
What hammer homeward came?
His iron, worn and faithful.
What shield saved the people?
Shared courage upon the climb.
The singer sets these answers
beside the benches tonight.

Another voice claims the weather;
another will names its work.
The Winter Witch waits elsewhere,
words sharp beneath her warning.
Hers, she says, were the blizzards;
her challenge haunts the north.
The tale leaves that terror
turning outside the door.

Wide runs his wish for union;
war worn clans need peace.
Jarls guard their own grandeur;
jealous hearts reckon losses.
Hnirvmýl's wisdom once bound allies;
his hope remembers that bond.
Skrildmýl seeks a shared purpose;
songs cannot swear its success.

Many mouths need feeding;
many roofs need keeping.
A ruler's ring is weighty;
responsibility wears the hand.
He knows the need of others;
night brings him troubled thoughts.
Sing courage in his keeping;
sing care beneath that courage.

Pour mead among your neighbors;
pass the cup to the frail.
Let the hall hold warmth;
let hungry guests find bread.
The mountain stands far from us;
mercy stands within reach.
Shoulder the small day's burdens;
share strength before you boast.

Snow shakes the shutters;
sparks climb the smoke.
Keep close through the winter;
keep courage for the climb.
When another's footing falters,
find room beside your shoulder.
Skrildmýl, storm tested,
stands in our telling.
```
