Among the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]], this is one telling of [[being-skrldmylstrmbrn|Skrildmýl Stormborn]] leading his people through winter storms to [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]]. Whether he walks the north as a living hero or belongs to its songs, the skalds praise his endurance and the clearing they attribute to [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrúnvald]]. The Winter Witch claims the storms as her own work; her claim travels beside the tale, unanswered.

: Saga of Skrildmýl Stormborn {#saga}

```poetry {form=epic lang=en}
Draw close, you keepers of the northern hearth;
let sleet beat harmless on the outer door.
The bench remembers burdens laid upon it,
and mouths remember those who bore them first.
Tonight we lend the storm a human voice:
Skrildmýl, hammer bearer, keeper of his clan.
Hear what the winter carried down to us.

His fathers taught him how a shield receives
what flesh must never learn to welcome home.
His mothers taught him how the fire is fed
when hungry hands have emptied every basket.
Under the thunder god he learned to stand,
and standing learned that strength must shelter others.
The hammer rose; the waiting shield stayed wide.

He grew into the weight of watching faces,
blue eyes beneath the weather of his years.
Long braided hair caught sparks in bitter air;
a silver thunderbolt held fast his cloak.
Men heard his battle shout across the field
and said the heavens answered in its echo.
Yet greater burdens waited at his hearth.

For winter came with teeth against the rafters.
The roofs bowed low; the paths forgot their names.
Wind drove the snow through every little opening;
the folded cloak grew stiff beside the bed.
Beyond the walls the storm ground ridge on ridge,
and fear went softly round the gathered benches,
asking whether their protector had departed.

Who dares to call a god across such weather?
Who dares to tell a frightened child to sleep?
They turned to him whose hammer spoke in battle,
whose hands had steadied shields against the charge.
Now hands were needed underneath the living;
now courage needed something else to strike.
He listened while the oldest voices trembled.

The thunder tests our keeping, said the chieftain.
Let no one cast another from the fire.
The mountain bears the memory of his hammer;
there we shall carry all our shaken faith.
Thrumufjall stood hidden past the whiteness,
its summit lost to any watching eye.
He named it, and the hall became a road.

No marching boast could warm that bitter morning.
No banner made the frozen footing sure.
Their breath went out like offerings surrendered;
the wind returned no portion to their mouths.
Skrildmýl set his shoulder toward the mountain.
Behind him walked the people in his keeping,
with everything they feared between their ribs.

The blizzard filled the hollows of their footsteps.
It scoured the little comfort from their speech.
A fur cloak could not clothe the whole procession;
a shield could not turn back the northern sky.
Still he went forward, facing what they faced,
and where their courage thinned he gave his voice:
Keep close; the mountain waits beyond this whiteness.

The storm had no regard for rank or household.
It struck the strong and weak with equal spite.
The breath that called the thunder god grew ragged;
the fingers gripping burdens ceased to feel.
Then every pace became a thing to choose,
again, again, against the crawling cold.
Their leader chose it where their eyes could see.

We sing his steadfastness; remember also
how many lives lay heavy on that stride.
A chieftain's certainty can cut like iron
when other feet must follow where he goes.
The song gives him the faith to cross the mountain;
it gives the frightened people their endurance.
Together they ascend its hidden shoulders.

The higher ground grew harsher underfoot.
The hammer's mountain took their breath in payment.
Their homes had vanished even from imagining;
there was the snow, the wind, the next ascent.
Skrildmýl lifted up his weathered face.
His voice went out beyond the others' hearing,
then thunder seemed to gather in his chest.

Thrúnvald, hear the people who have followed!
Hear those whose bodies make this prayer with mine!
Your hammer struck the earth upon this mountain;
let strength protect the hands that honor you.
We have no warmer country in our keeping,
no other lives to spend against the cold.
Remember us beneath your storming heavens.

So runs the cry the singers give their hero.
So hangs the silence underneath that cry.
The clouds drew back; the driven snow fell quiet.
The sky unclosed above the mountain's crown.
Light found the faces lifted from their hoods,
and every cheek seemed strange to every neighbor:
a living face where fear had seen a grave.

They stood beneath the clearing of the heavens,
their breath still white, their bodies aching still.
No sunlight could undo the road behind them;
no blessing made their winter burdens small.
But those who thought the thunder had forsaken
his faithful saw the mountain bathed in brightness,
and spoke the god's name with returning breath.

What hammer did the chieftain carry homeward?
The iron in his hand, the song replies.
What shield did he hold fast against the darkness?
The people who had held beside him there.
What treasure did he gather from the mountain?
The light upon the faces of his kin.
For this the benches make his name a shelter.

And still another voice goes through the snow,
claiming the storm was wrought by hidden malice.
The winter witch lays claim to all that terror;
her challenge waits beyond the telling's warmth.
We cannot weigh her words upon this hearth.
We sing the road, the summit, and the clearing;
we leave the quarrel living in the wind.

His longing reaches further than one village:
the clans divided by their ancient anger,
the jarls who count a neighbor's gain as loss.
He dreams a common purpose strong enough
to carry many households through their winter.
The storm has taught our song this harder question:
whose strength will bear the weight of being followed?

Pour mead, and let the cup pass among you.
Give warmth to those who came through lesser storms.
The mountain cannot stand inside our houses,
but hands can make a shelter each evening.
Remember him when someone needs your shoulder;
remember them who climbed beside their leader.
The fire sinks low; the outer door holds fast.
Carry the song where winter gathers next.
```
