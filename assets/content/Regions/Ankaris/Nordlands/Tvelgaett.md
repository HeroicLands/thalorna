The last names a skald speaks at [[lore-jol|Jól]] belong to gods nobody prays to. When the telling of [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] has burned both worlds and the hall has gone quiet, the skald names the ones who walk out of the ash: the **Tvelgaett**, the remnant-kin—_tvelg-_, a remnant, and _-aett_, a kin reckoned together. They are the gods' heirs, and the next age is theirs.

## Why There Are Heirs

The [[affiliation-asguardian|Ten]] were kindled from the remnant of the age before this one, and the [[lore-aldarhringr|Aldarhringr]], the ring of ages, teaches that every age ends the same way. So the Ten have heirs as surely as a jarl does, and for the same reason: what a house leaves is how it goes on. The Ten do not cross into the new age themselves, and the hofs never soften that. "The heirs are not the Ten come back," a Hofgodi at [[place-odholm|Ódholm]] tells a newcomer who has asked. "They are what the Ten leave. You will meet the same thing at every grave-ale you ever drink."

The heirs keep no hofs, take no blót and confer no standing. Nobody in this age serves them, because they have nothing yet to be served for; their age has not begun. They are spoken of, and only at the end of the telling.

## The Three Every Hall Names

**Ulthvar** is [[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]]'s son, and the silent one. His name is the ward of the far limit, and he speaks no word in any telling until the last day. When [[lore-vetrulfr|Vetrúlfr]] the Winter-Wolf takes his father, Ulthvar goes to the wolf, sets his foot in the lower jaw and his hands to the upper, and tears the mouth that swallowed wisdom; then he walks out of the fire with nothing to say. The rune-halls read him as the answer to the bargain his father made at [[lore-minnir|Minnir]]'s well: Ódvar paid an eye to learn his death, and his son is what that death could not take.

**Hamarberi** is [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrúnvald]]'s daughter, the hammer-bearer. When the Thunderer has struck [[lore-heimsormr|Heimsormr]], walked his nine steps and fallen, [[lore-thrunhamarr|Thrúnhamarr]] lies on the field where he dropped it, and she is the one who lifts it. The coastal hofs tell that she carries it out of the fire still warm, and that the first thunder of the new age is the hammer set down on new ground.

**Grönvin** is [[lore-frodvindty|Fródvin]]'s heir, the green friend, and the least warlike of the three. Fródvin goes down to [[lore-svartbrandrdty|Svartbrandr]] and the harvest is left with no hand to bring it home; Grönvin is the first shoot through the ash after the fire has gone out, and the tellings make the first morning of the new age hers. The hofs of Fródvin speak her name over a field sown after a burning.

## The Heirs the Halls Dispute

Beyond those three, every faith claims an heir of its own and no two tellings agree. [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]]'s hofs refuse to name one; their Hofgydja at [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] says only that "a world kindled out of the coals will need everything in it made again, and somebody has to be left who knows how," and leaves the newcomer to decide whether she means an heir or a craftsman. Sólrún's priestesses hold that the honored dead she gathered are her heirs, and that the remnant who rise from the ash are hers. Nobody claims an heir for [[lore-nahilddty|Náhild]], and the question is not asked aloud.

## What the Heirs Teach

The heirs give the north its idea of what a death is for. A man's heir drinks the grave-ale before taking the high seat, and the Tvelgaett are the same custom told at the size of the world: the Ten fall, their heirs inherit, and nothing of the old age crosses except what it left in the hands of the next. The hofs read the lesson as a duty rather than a promise. A god who dies at Aldarlok leaves an heir; a person who dies in this age leaves an oath kept or broken, a craft taught or lost, and a name the skalds will or will not say.
