## Overview

"You are not here to win," a fort-rotation sergeant tells the bóndi who has just walked in from his farm, still carrying his own bread. "You are here to be standing on the wall when the man beside you is not." Nine hundred people live behind Tvalgard's timber-and-earth rampart, on the last defensible ridge before the burned ground that marks the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukar]] frontier, and the rotation that fills the wall is why the town fills and empties with the seasons.

Tvalgard's own name calls it the lesser of a pair, and nobody here disputes the judgment. Where [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]] and [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]] built their walls for a court's dignity as much as a kingdom's defense, Tvalgard built for the second reason only. The king holds his hall here because the frontier is where a Targudian king has to be seen, not because the hall claims to rival its sister capitals.

The fort-duty every [[lore-bondirnk|bóndi]] in the kingdom owes in rotation is mustered through Tvalgard. A free farmer arrives to stand his weeks on the wall, trades news and salt with the Lawspeaker's circuit while he is here, and goes home again to a farm his neighbors have minded in his absence. The arrangement gives nearly every household in [[place-targud|Targud]] combat experience, since nobody in the kingdom reaches middle age without having stood that wall at least once.

A garrison town needs what a market town does not, and Tvalgard has it. [[being-knlthnskrldmkh|Knilthann Skraldumakh]] sings the sagas that keep a rotating levy from forgetting what it is fighting for. [[being-anthrvasgrnd|Anthorv Ásgrind]] earns his bread making a frightened muster laugh at itself. [[being-thlmslddrknt|Thilmselda Drekanótt]]'s hall feeds and houses whichever shift of bóndi is standing its weeks this season.

## The Sword Behind the Seat

The hall is said to hold **Vrellbrandr**, the blood-brand, behind the king's seat, its hilt and scabbard bound together with nine knots that are checked each morning. [[lore-trgdahstry|Histories and Legends of Targud]] gives the tale and the two ways skalds read it.

## What the Town Argues About

Tvalgard's one argument with itself is whether a border town this grim can also be a capital. The [[affiliation-kingdmtrgd|Kingdom of Targud]] has answered it the only way it knows how: by making the wall the capital's whole point and building nothing around it that does not serve that wall.
