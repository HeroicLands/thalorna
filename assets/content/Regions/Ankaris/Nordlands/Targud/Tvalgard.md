## Overview

Tvalgard's own name calls it the lesser of a pair, and nobody among the nine hundred people living behind its timber-and-earth rampart disputes the judgment: where **Knalthstead** and **Gnarthborg** built their walls for a court's dignity as much as a kingdom's defense, Tvalgard built for the second reason only, on the last defensible ridge before the burned ground that marks the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukar]] frontier. The king holds his hall here because the frontier is where a Targudian king has to be seen, not because the hall itself claims to rival its sister capitals.

The fort-duty every [[lore-bondirnk|bóndi]] in the kingdom owes in rotation is mustered through Tvalgard, and the town fills and empties with each turning of that duty—a free farmer arrives to stand his weeks on the wall, trades news and salt with the Lawspeaker's circuit while he is here, and goes home again to a farm his neighbors have minded in his absence. The arrangement is what gives nearly every household in **Targud** combat experience, since nobody in the kingdom reaches middle age without having stood that wall at least once.

What a garrison town like Tvalgard needs that a market town does not, it has: **Knilthann Skraldumakh** sings the sagas that keep a rotating levy from forgetting what it is fighting for, **Anthorv Ásgrind** earns his bread making a frightened muster laugh at itself, and **Thilmselda Drekanótt**'s hall feeds and houses whichever shift of bóndi is standing its weeks this season. Tvalgard's one argument with itself is whether a border town this grim can also be a capital, and the [[affiliation-kingdmtrgd|Kingdom of Targud]] has answered it the only way it knows how: by making the wall the capital's whole point and building nothing around it that does not serve that wall.
