## Overview

The watch at [[place-tvalgard|Tvalgard]] has one rule about Dvulgvangr that it gives every bundi on his first rotation: cross it by noon, or do not cross it. The ford lies on the burned ground east of the ridge, a day's walk out, where the river comes shallow over gravel below the rise that [[place-nulthgard|Nulthgard]] stands on, and the old road to the eastern districts goes through it because there is nowhere else to go through. Its name is the sleep a man takes standing, on watch, _dvulg-_, before the generic for a field, and it was given after.

## The Three Days

In 613 the Grukar horde that had burned the east for nine summers reached the royal hall, and the king's hirv and the bundi of the eastern districts held this ford for three days so that the steadings behind it could empty west. About eight hundred of them died on it, and most were carried off by the Grukar, who take their dead and their enemies' alike, so that no pyre was lit for them and no grave-ale was drunk. [[lore-burningeast|The Burning of the East]] tells it; [[being-knlthnskrldmkh|Knilthann Skraldumak]], who sings every last stand in Targud's memory, does not.

## The Vrellvoll

Dvulgvangr is a _vrellvoll_, a blood-field, of the kind the north calls the dread, and it is thin and dangerous. What the field returns is what its defenders felt in their last hours, and it returns it two ways. A Nordman who stands on the ford at dusk finds himself unable to leave his post until dawn, a steadiness he did not choose, standing in the shallows with his spear grounded and his eyes on the far bank while the cold comes up through his boots. Grukar who cross it break and run, and the watch has seen a raiding party of forty turn in the water and go back the way it came with nothing behind it. Men who have spent a night there say the post beside them was manned, and that the man in it did not answer and did not need to.

On any night of the year presences walk the gravel, and in the thaw, when the river runs high and brown over the ford, things come through it that can harm the living. Two bundi of the rotation have been found at the water's edge in the morning in the present king's reign, standing.

Nobody keeps the field. The Lawspeaker's circuit never sits within sight of it, the fort-captains do not take a muster across it after noon, and a bundi who must cross goes by day, goes quickly and does not look at the far bank until he is on it.

## What a Party Finds

- A crossing at dusk that cannot be left before dawn, with a Grukar raid coming down the old road behind it and the field choosing which side it steadies.
- An old bundi of the eastern districts who wants to drink the grave-ale for his great-grandfather on the field itself, with the reckoning spoken, which is the one rite that might end it, and who needs someone to hold the ford while he does.
- The king's ride to the howes at Nulthgard, which crosses the ford twice.
