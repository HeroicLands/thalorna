## Overview

From the ford the rise looks like any other low hill on the burned ground until the eye finds the posts. Nulthgard was the hall of the kings of the [[affiliation-kingdmtrgd|Kingdom of Targud]] from the kingdom's acclamation until 613, and what stands on the rise now is its shell: a ring of charred hall-posts taller than a man, the ends of the roof-beams still socketed in some of them, the hearth-stones in the middle under a century of nettles, and the line of the stockade as a bank in the turf. Its name is the last hour of a watch, _nulth-_, before the generic for an enclosed yard and its buildings, and the kings who held it took it to mean that the hall was the kingdom's last watch before the east. In 613 it was.

## The Four Howes

Below the hall on the river side stand four howes, each with its stone and its names, and every king of Targud from the acclamation to the father of [Skruldmil II]{.name data-type="being/npc"} lies in one of them, because the [Driskaruk]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} opened a howe again for each king who died and laid him with his forebears. When the hall burned the howes were left where they stood, and the line the kingdom holds now runs a day's walk to the west of them, so the kings of the Driskaruk lie on the wrong side of their own frontier. Once in his reign a king of Targud rides out from [[place-tvalgard|Tvalgard]] with his hirv to pour the grave-ale at the four howes, and comes back past the ford at [[place-dvulgvangr|Dvulgvangr]] before dusk if he has timed it well. The ride is the only royal rite in the north performed on ground the crown does not hold.

## What Is There Now

The [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar Folk]] do not camp on the rise. The watch's patrols say that the horde that burned the hall never used it, and that nothing has used it since but foxes and the crows, which is the kind of thing the watch says about ground it does not understand. Robbers have been at the howes, and one of the four was found a few years ago opened from the inside, with the stone pushed outward and nothing taken; the watch set the stone back without going in, and the king's ride that year poured ale at three.

## What a Party Finds

- The king's ride is due in the present reign and the escort is short, because a hirv on the burned ground for two days is a hirv not on the wall.
- A howe opened from inside, with a king's grave-goods still in it and the stone pushed outward, and a Lawspeaker who would like someone other than the king to go down and look.
- The hearth-stones of the first royal hall, which a Driskaruk claimant would give a great deal to have carried to Tvalgard and set under the present hall's fire.
