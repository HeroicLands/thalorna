"Everything up here that matters comes and goes by water," a Knalthstead skipstjóri (shipmaster) tells the southern factor who has bought passage on his ship's first run of the season. "The roads are fjords. The fields are a strip of barley at the head of each one. The rest is rock, ice and forest, and the people who live in it have been reading the weather off that rock for longer than any of your cities have kept books."

The Nordlands are the frozen north of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]: jagged mountains, deep fjords, boreal forest and glacier-fed rivers, and the homeland of the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]]. Five kinship-kingdoms share one tongue, one pantheon and one seafaring life, and the Nordlands are among the most cohesive regions on the continent; no imperial authority has ever ruled here. An outsider remembers them for their longships, which carry the north's trade south in one season and its raiders in the next.

## The Land

The Nordlands occupy northwestern Ankaris, with [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldía]] to the southwest, [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]] and [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] to the south and east, and the open northern ocean off every coast. The coast decides everything else. Glaciers carved it into granite and basalt as a saw-toothed line of thousands of natural harbors, and the fjords run so far inland that each one is a sheltered road for a fleet. A ship goes where a cart cannot, and every settlement of any size stands where a keel can reach it.

Behind the fjords the land climbs into mountain tracts of wind-scoured stone and ice. Few people travel or live there; the most reclusive clans keep to high plateaus where the caribou migrate. People on the coast tell of [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] holds hidden in those mountains, and the tales are rumors, not neighbors anyone trades with. South and east of the mountains the ground settles into boreal forest of spruce and pine, cut by glacier-fed rivers that run south toward [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] and east toward the frozen interior. Off the northwest coast a chain of volcanic islands breaks the weather. Their hot springs and fertile volcanic soils make them coveted holdings, however far out they lie.

The climate is brutal by any Ankarian measure. Winter lasts for months and draws a near-total darkness over the far north; summer is short, flooded with light and violently alive. "You learn the land's moods young," the skipstjóri says, "or you do not get old."

## Five Kingdoms on Rock and Ice

About two million people live in the Nordlands, along the coasts and the inland rivers. The mountain interior is nearly empty, and much of the country stays empty even by northern standards.

Nordheim is pre-eminent. It is more than half again as populous as Norgaad, the next kingdom, and that one fact gives northern politics its shape. Nordheim holds [[place-domsey|Dómsey]], the island of the septennial assembly, and its king usually convenes it. Norgaad, at the center, brokers between west and east, and its Lawspeaker can call a **Great Moot** at Asgarthul when a kingdom wants a meeting between the septennial assemblies. Malagna faces the northern ocean and trades furthest abroad. Targud lives with one hand on a weapon against the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukar]] on its eastern border. Vithgard, small and scattered along the western coast, sends its whale-oil to lamps across the north.

Between Nordheim and Vithgard lies [[place-hringstead|Hringstead]], neutral ground guaranteed by all five kingdoms. The mercenary [[affiliation-malldbndlg|Málalidabandalag]] keeps its Hall there, and the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] counts it as the place where it was sworn, five generations before any present kingdom.

## What the Sea Feeds

Fjord, mountain and ice do not grow food. The arable is a scatter of thin fields in sheltered valley bottoms, barley and oats in a summer that is over almost as soon as it starts, and it has never been enough. The region lives on the _sea_: sealing, fishing, whale ivory, furs, amber, and the ships that carry all of it. [[lore-nrdlndswhal|The Whale Strand]] and [[lore-nrdlndsseal|The Ice-Edge and the Rookery]] follow the whale and the seal from the hunt to the lamp. The ships are the economy, and always have been.

The longships are the north's great craft. They draw little enough water to run up any fjord or river and are seaworthy enough to cross open ocean. They carry amber, furs, whale ivory and iron south to the markets of Mídhalión and come home with wine, grain, silk and silver. When trade falls short, the same ships carry raiders, and the Nordmen draw no sharp line between the two voyages.

## Why They Raid

The Nordmen give one answer when a southerner asks, and they give it without embarrassment: arithmetic. A people whose land cannot feed them must take food, or land, or both, from a country that can. Two million people on rock and ice have done so along every coast within reach for as long as anyone has kept a record, and the [[lore-nrdlndsraid|raid]] has its own customs of crew, share and muster.

The same arithmetic explains [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]]. Sixty years ago Nordmen crossed to [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] and took its north-east, and what they took was the _plain_, the one large body of good arable on that island, now worked by a hundred and twenty thousand thralls. Seen from the Nordlands it is the most rational thing anyone up there has ever done. Seen from the plain, it is a conquest, and the thralls' children still work land the conquerors hold.

## Clans, Halls and the Ting

The Nordmen are overwhelmingly human and fiercely independent. They live in clans led by _jarls_, who rule through strength, generosity and the reputation their ancestors earned, and the clans gather into the five kingdoms, each with its own king and its own customs. What they share outweighs what divides them: all speak [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], all worship the Ten of the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian Pantheon]], and all recognize the _ting_, the open assembly where disputes are settled, laws proclaimed and alliances made.

A visitor sees how old this country is before anyone tells him. Hall-posts carried from a family's former hall stand in the walls of its new one; the howes hold the old dead, and carved stones on the headlands name in runes the ones the sea kept; and at [[lore-sumarmal|Sumarmál]] neighbors walk their boundary stones together and strike each one so that both households can say where the line lies. [[place-horgsvangr|Hörgsvangr]], an altar-field in the Nordheim interior, is said to be centuries older than any present kingdom. The skalds recite genealogies that run back past every one of them.

The ting is the nearest thing the Nordlands have to a common institution. Every seventh year the five kings and their principal jarls meet on Dómsey for the **King of All Clans**, to settle quarrels between kingdoms, give judgments beyond any one kingdom's writ, and decide war and peace with the neighbors. The assembly keeps no officers and collects no revenue; its weight is the rulers gathered on the island and what follows for a crown that refuses them.

Personal honor comes first. A Nordman's reputation is the most valuable thing he owns, and an insult or a betrayal can begin a blood feud that runs for generations. The same culture allows a real rise: a clever or brave commoner who earns a jarl's notice can climb, and a weak jarl's grown children can find themselves ruled by a cousin who proved more capable. Women hold more autonomy here than in most Ankarian cultures. They manage estates while the ships are away, own property in their own right, can initiate divorce, and some take up arms as shield-maidens whose skill earns the respect any warrior's does. The [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordheimn]] culture note follows what a person owes and is owed.

## The Ten

The Asguardian Pantheon is the living faith of the Nordlands. Its gods—[[affiliation-odvar|Ódvar]] the All-Father, [[affiliation-thrunvald|Thrúnvald]] the Thunderer, [[affiliation-bjartr|Bjartr]] of light and dreams, [[affiliation-frodvin|Fródvin]] and [[affiliation-solrun|Sólrún]] of the harvest and of love, [[affiliation-eidgar|Eidgar]] of oath and honorable combat, dread [[affiliation-nahild|Náhild]] of the underworld, and the rest—are near, jealous and demanding neighbors. Every child in the north learns that the world was cut from a giant's body and that a reckoning comes at [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]], the close of an age in the [[lore-aldarhringr|ring of ages]]. Every Nordman hopes to be remembered as one who stood well when it came.

Worship has no center. There is no pontiff, no primacy of one temple, no Ordo. The faith runs through _hofs_ (communal halls), the _godar_ and _gydjur_ (priests and priestesses) who keep them and often lead their districts as well, and the _blót_, the seasonal offering and shared feast that binds a clan to its gods and to itself. Four great festivals mark the year: [[lore-jol|Jól]] at midwinter, Sumarmál at spring, [[lore-midsumar|Midsumar]] at the summer solstice, and [[lore-vetrnaetr|Vetrnaetr]] as winter returns.

Beside the priesthood go the wandering _völvur_, seeresses who travel from settlement to settlement practicing _seidr_ (trance and spirit-walking) and _rúnagaldr_ (rune-magic). The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] has almost no reach here. The völvur were practicing their craft for centuries before the Ordo existed, and the north distrusts foreign institutions on principle.

## Kings on Sufferance

The Nordlands are five sovereign kingdoms in a loose confederation. Nordheim is first among equals, but every kingdom guards its sovereignty. A jarl who reaches for power beyond his own jarldom meets the same reflexive resistance the Aurèldíans keep for would-be emperors.

Succession is hereditary in name and qualified by the ting in fact. A king whose clans reject him cannot rule, and the assembly has deposed kings more than once. Outsiders take this for chaos. It is a system that asks a king to show his competence in public, constantly, and the skipstjóri puts it in a trader's terms: "A king up here is like a ship's master. The crew follows him while he brings them home."

## Trade and the Wider World

The Nordlands are woven into Ankaris's trade whatever their reputation. Nordmen longships reach as far south as the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], and Nordmen mercenaries serve in armies from [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] to [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]]. The region exports amber, furs, whale ivory, iron, smoked fish and slaves, and imports wine, fine cloth, grain, silver and the luxuries only a warmer country grows. Commerce in coin runs on [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian money]], since the north strikes none of its own; larger dealings run on [[lore-bartercnmy|barter]] and [[lore-kinhalcrdt|hall credit]].

Aelwyth is the closest trading partner. The two face each other across some three hundred miles of open sea, and they share a family of gods through the Aelwythan syncretism of the Twelve and the Asguardians, and centuries of intermarriage, feud and quiet alliance.

## The Neighbors

To the south and west lies [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldía]]: trading partner, theological rival, occasional victim of raids, and party to the Aelwythan syncretism that sets the Asguardian gods in an Aurèldían landscape. To the south, the frontier with [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]] is a long, wooded contest between Nordmen settlers and the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] tribes of the deep forest, where who built the last stockade matters more than any formal rule. To the east is [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarhölm]], home of the Grukar. The two peoples share a climate and a martial temper and nothing else. Grukar raids on the coastal settlements are a perennial problem, and Nordmen punitive expeditions into Grukar country are the recurring answer.

Far to the south, Mídhalión is the great market. Nordmen traders and mercenaries are a familiar sight in every Vylarian port, and a good share of the north's silver is earned under southern skies.
