"Ten gods sit at the table," a godi of a coastal hof tells the newcomers who ask what the old liturgy means by _Ásvinir_, "and the old words name the ones who mean to stand up from it." The word is the archaic liturgical name for the defending kin of the gods, and the old liturgies use it where a newcomer would expect a list of the Ten.

What makes the Ásvinir kin is common cause, not harmony. They stand against [[lore-nahilddty|Náhild]], against [[lore-svartbrandrdty|Svartbrandr]] and against the corruption both of them spread, and they do so while quarreling among themselves, keeping favorites and failing as gods fail. The name therefore sorts the [[affiliation-asguardian|Ten]] rather than listing them: it covers the defending kin and leaves out Náhild and Svartbrandr, who belong to the pantheon without belonging to the kin that stands against them.

## The Older Name on the Coast

On the coasts the liturgy has a rival in memory. In the coastal tellings the Ásvinir largely displaced the older tradition of **Hafvald**, whose name survives in place-names, in coastal observances and in the [[miscgear-hornnjordur|Horn of Hafvald]]. A coastal godi will point out the place-names and the Horn and then stop, because the sagas leave open whether that elder sea-power was a god or a spirit.
