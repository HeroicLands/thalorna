**Hvelmsnerv**, the Whale-Prophecy, is the name a [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]] hall gave to the _snerv_ (foretelling) that **Völva Aldrhildr** spoke from its high seat at [[lore-vetrnaetr|Vetrnaetr]], on the first night of the last winter in which [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]]'s whaling failed. The name keeps the question the host asked and nothing of the answer he was given. [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] halls along the whale-coast recite it while the bones of the slaughter-blót are read, in four-beat stanzas of uneven length that close on her _hnerv_ (refrain) about the hall's own fire. She passes over the fire and the wolf of [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] in a handful of lines, as a thing the hall's skald already has at [[lore-jol|Jól]], and spends her length on what the [[lore-aldarhringr|ring of ages]] leaves: a coal banked in the empty _gnúm_, the clay pot a household keeps waiting for its next death, and the three heirs of the [[lore-tvelgaett|Tvelgaett]] who inherit a morning in which nobody says a name from this age.

The text is the recital the hall's skald gives after the ting, as he gave it there under the truth of the recital. The ting sat at Dvarnvík in the spring, when the strand had stayed empty through the dark months, and it accused eleven people of working the winds; [[lore-vthgdhstry|the Vithgard histories]] keep the judgment. A [[affiliation-eidhond|Sworn Hand]] ruled there that foretelling alone is no working against a named person, and under that ruling the piece is still sung. The ting struck two kinds of name from the recital, the names the völva spoke down the benches and the one she named as the carrier of the pot, and the skald keeps them struck; those are the lacunae. Seven women, two plateau men who had sold wind-knots and two Dvarnvík men accused of _ergi_ were burned on the skerry of **Askholm**. Aldrhildr was accused and never tried. The whales did not come in.

```poetry {form=snerv lang=en}
I am the hall's skald. I had the bench by the door.
I had the end of the age at Jól that year, as I have it now.
What I give you is hers. The ting has heard it once,
and I give it the second time as I gave it the first.

The ships were up. The strand was bare.
The giant's blood lay gray to the skerries with nothing in it.
The blót was done at the Winter Nights,
the beasts hung bled in the smoke of the roof,
and the bones lay by in a bowl, unread.
That was the night. I say it as it was.

Twelve boats had gone out to the run
and the run went by them, far out, going south,
backs in the swell like islands moving,
and no line made fast, and the autumn went.
The cauldrons stood on the beach with rain in them.
The harpoons hung in the roof with their marks turned out,
so a man could read whose iron had taken nothing.
The jaw that stands at the gate is from a better year,
and there are children in this hall who were born under it
and have never seen the like.
The barley was low and the tallow was lower.
The lamps had last year's oil in them,
and the host had counted it by the week.
A hall with no oil lights itself with tallow
and smells of it. There were forty of us.

She came in with the dark, on foot,
down the [[place-hnarvdal|Hnarvdal]] road, with a sealskin bag,
and she had not been sent for. The host
got up from the high seat and she sat in it.
That is the custom. She did not thank him.

She ate first. The host's wife served her
the blót-meat, and barley, and the ale,
and she ate as a woman eats who has walked all day,
and when the bowl was empty she held it out,
and it was filled, and she ate that.
The hall watched her eat. Nobody spoke.
A child asked what she was for. His mother
put a hand over his mouth, and the völva
looked at the child, and went on eating.
Then she wiped the bowl with the heel of the loaf
and set it down and said, "Ask."

The host asked one thing. He had said it over
in his head all day, and it came out short.
"Will the whale come in before the ice?"

She said, "No."

The host looked at his wife. His wife looked at the pit.
The hall said nothing for a while.
The fire ticked. Somebody's dog got up
and turned around twice and lay down again.

Then she went on without being asked,
and this is the part the hall remembers,
and I say it as she said it, and no shorter.

No. Not this winter. The run is past.
What went by you going south will not turn.
You will eat the seal, and then the dogs,
and then you will go to the jarl's door
and he will open it and count you in,
and some of you will not need counting by then.
There is your winter. You asked, and you have it.
Now I will tell you the rest of it,
because I have walked all day for this,
and there is nothing else in this house to do.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

The host put driftwood on. Two pieces.

Your skald has the end of the age at Jól.
He has it whole and he has it in order
and he is fed for it, and I will not sing it for him.
The gate opens. You know that.
The chain goes. You have heard it.
Nine steps, and a hammer on the ground.
A hound and a hand go down together.
The field goes down to the fire,
the fire takes what is left, and the ash falls last.
Nine steps. I have never seen why it wants a hundred lines.

I was not there, and nor was he.
He has it from his master, who had it from his.
I have it from somewhere else,
and it comes to the same ash.
What he does not have, because it is not sung,
is what the ash is for. Sit still.

A man got up to go out, and she said, "Sit still,"
and he sat.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

None of us moved. The driftwood caught.
"Fill that," she said. She meant the cup. It was filled.

There is a hall. It is not this one.
It is a hall like this one, on a coast,
whalebone in the roof, a pit under the floor,
and a woman in it. She is not a jarl's wife.
She is what you are.
She has no name that I will say tonight.

She hears it coming. The dogs hear it first.
The sea goes flat. The sky stands still.
There is a smell on the wind like a rendering-fire
with nothing in the cauldron.
She has a man at sea and two at the ice-edge,
and she does not think about them, or I was not shown it.
She does not run. Where would a woman run to,
with the world alight at both ends of the road?

She goes to the hearth. She rakes the gray.
One coal is red, and she rolls it clear,
the one you would keep, if there were a night.
She takes down the pot from the shelf by the door.
You know the pot. Every hall keeps one waiting.
Yours is up there. It has nothing in it yet.
It is for the next of you the fire takes,
and it was for her, and she knew that.
She scoops the hearth-ash in with two fingers
and makes a bed in it, the way you bank a coal,
and sets the coal in, and covers it over,
and puts the lid on, and ties the lid down with thong.

Then she does the things. She does them in order.
She puts the lamp out, because oil is oil.
She takes the sheaf down off the roof and brings it.
It was for the riders. There will be no riders.
She hangs the barley-scoop back on its peg.
She bars the door.
She takes the pot up in both arms like a loaf,
she lifts the turf out of the wall where the dead go out,
and she goes out through it, and she does not close it.

She goes down into the pit, the barley-pit,
empty, it being the end of the age,
and sets the pot in the corner where the floor is coldest,
the sheaf beside it, and sits down by them
with her back to the wall.
And the fire comes over the roof of the world,
and she does not come up again.

She did no more. I have told you all of it.
It is not much. It took less time than I have taken.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

The child who had asked what she was for
got up and put a piece of bone on the fire.
It was whale. It burned badly, and it burned.

It is green first. It is green before it is anything.
The ash lies a hand deep on all that was,
wet, for the first thing the new sky does is rain,
and it rains on the ash a long time, and the days go uncounted,
and out of the corner of the pit where the floor was coldest,
through the lid, through the thong, through the ash of her hearth,
a shoot comes up. That is [[lore-tvelgaett|Grönvin]]. That is her name.
She has never heard of [[lore-frodvindty|the Healer]].
She comes up through the ash because the warmth was there,
and does not know whose warmth it was, and does not ask.

Then the hammer. It lay where it fell,
nine steps from the one who dropped it,
and it lay in the fire the whole time and did not melt,
and when the ash was wet enough to walk on
a woman walked on it and picked it up.
Hamarberi. She was not looking for it. It was there.
It was still warm. She carried it a long way,
and when she was tired she set it down on the new ground,
and the new ground had its first thunder, a small one,
and the rain stopped for a while.

And the one who walks. Aldvar, [[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]]'s son,
who tore the wolf's jaw open and said nothing about it,
walks the first morning with his hands at his sides,
and he says none of the old names. Not his father's.
Not the wolf's. Not this hall's, not the host's, not mine.
He passes the pit and does not know it was a pit.
He passes the shoot and steps around it, which is something.
And that is all of it: a shoot, a hammer,
and a man who steps around the shoot.

There are people in it. Do not ask me where from.
The hofs have their answers, and the hofs are ash.
There are people in it, and there is weather, and ground,
and a woman is cutting turf for a roof
before the rain comes back,
and she will put a sheaf on it, and she will not know why.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

The host said, "Go on." His voice was not steady.
He was not a man whose voice was unsteady.
I say it as it was.

Then she did the thing the ting heard of.
She leaned out of the high seat and looked down the benches
one by one, the way the host counts stores,
and she named them. I will not say the names.
The ting took them out of my mouth and I have kept them out.
This is the shape of what she said, and it was said to each.

[…]
Your name is […]. Your mother gave it you,
and nobody in that morning will say it. Not once.
[…] the boat you took to [[place-knarvik|Knarvík]] for her ribs,
and the whale you did not take,
and the three winters you fed your father's brother
when he could not feed himself, and the oath you kept
at […]. None of it. Not a word of it. The shoot
does not know it. The hammer does not know it.
The one who walks does not know it,
and he is the one who would.
[…] your daughter's keys […] and the cask of oil
you still owe at […]
And mine. They gave me Aldrhildr at my first high seat,
and nobody will say it there either. Nobody. Not once.

The host said, "What is a name for, then?"
He said it the way a man asks the price of a thing
he has already made up his mind to buy.

You were not listening. Listen now.
The pot is carried. Somebody carries it.
Not there. Here. In this age, in a hall like this one,
down into a pit, in both arms, like a loaf.
The coal does not know who banked it.
[…] will carry it. I have seen the hands. […]
Now read the bones. I have said what I came to say,
and I want the hearth.
I am an old woman and I walked all day.

She did not ask whether the fire was low.
It was.

The host's wife read the bones. The reading is the hall's,
and the ting did not ask me for it, and I did not give it.
She slept by the hearth with the sealskin bag under her head,
and the dog that had turned around lay against her back.
She was gone before light. The host had let her out
and barred the door behind her and gone back to his bed,
and he did not say which way she went, and nobody asked him.
The spoon she ate with is up there, by the pot,
and nobody eats with it.

The whale did not come in. The ice closed in the dark,
and we ate the seal, and then we did what she had said,
and the jarl counted us in at his door,
and some of us did not need counting by then.
In the spring the ting sat at Dvarnvík and I was called,
and I said it as she had said it, no shorter and no longer.
The rest is the ting's, and the skerry's, and not mine.
The fire is low.
```
