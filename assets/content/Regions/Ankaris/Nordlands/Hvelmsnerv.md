**Hvelmsnerv**, the Whale-Prophecy, is the name a [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]] hall gave to the _snerv_ (foretelling) that Velva [Aldurhildur]{.name data-type="being/npc"} spoke from its high seat at [[lore-veturnetur|Veturnetur]], on the first night of the last winter in which [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]]'s whaling failed. The name keeps the question the host asked and nothing of the answer he was given. [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] halls along the whale-coast recite it while the bones of the slaughter-blet are read, in four-beat stanzas of uneven length that close on her _hnerv_ (refrain) about the hall's own fire. She passes over the fire and the wolf of [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] in a handful of lines, as a thing the hall's skald already has at [[lore-jol|Jel]], and spends her length on what [[lore-aldarhringur|Aldarhringur]], the ring of ages, leaves: a coal banked in the empty _gnum_, the clay pot a household keeps waiting for its next death, and the three heirs of the [[lore-tvelgaett|Tvelgaett]] who inherit a morning in which nobody says a name from this age.

The text is the recital the hall's skald gives after the ting, as he gave it there under the truth of the recital. The ting sat at Dvarnvík in the spring, when the strand had stayed empty through the dark months, and it accused eleven people of working the winds; [[lore-vthgdhstry|the Vithgard histories]] keep the judgment. A [[lore-swornhandrnk|Sworn Hand]] ruled there that foretelling alone is no working against a named person, and under that ruling the piece is still sung. The ting struck two kinds of name from the recital, the names the velva spoke down the benches and the one she named as the carrier of the pot, and the skald keeps them struck; those are the lacunae. Seven women, two plateau men who had sold wind-knots and two Dvarnvík men accused of _ergi_ were burned on the skerry of [[place-askholm|Askholm]]. Aldurhildur was accused and never tried. The whales did not come in.

I am the hall's skald. I had the bench by the door./
I had the end of the age at Jel that year, as I have it now./
What I give you is hers. The ting has heard it once,/

The ships were up. The strand was bare./
The giant's blood lay gray to the skerries with nothing in it./
The blet was done at the Winter Nights,/

That was the night. I say it as it was.

Twelve boats had gone out to the run/

backs in the swell like islands moving,/

The cauldrons stood on the beach with rain in them./
The harpoons hung in the roof with their marks turned out,/

and have never seen the like./
The barley was low and the tallow was lower./
The lamps had last year's oil in them,/
and the host had counted it by the week./
A hall with no oil lights itself with tallow/
and smells of it. There were forty of us.

She came in with the dark, on foot,/
down the [[place-hnarvdal|Hnarvdal]] road, with a sealskin bag,/
and she had not been sent for. The host/
got up from the high seat and she sat in it./
That is the custom. She did not thank him.

She ate first. The host's wife served her/
the blet-meat, and barley, and the ale,/
and she ate as a woman eats who has walked all day,/
and when the bowl was empty she held it out,/
and it was filled, and she ate that./
The hall watched her eat. Nobody spoke./

"Will the whale come in before the ice?"

The host looked at his wife. His wife looked at the pit./
The hall said nothing for a while./
The fire ticked. Somebody's dog got up/
and turned around twice and lay down again.

Then she went on without being asked,/

and I say it as she said it, and no shorter.

No. Not this winter. The run is past./
What went by you going south will not turn./
You will eat the seal, and then the dogs,/
and then you will go to the jarl's door/

There is your winter. You asked, and you have it./
Now I will tell you the rest of it,/

I was not there, and nor was he./
He has it from his master, who had it from his./
I have it from somewhere else,/
and it comes to the same ash./
What he does not have, because it is not sung,/

A man got up to go out, and she said, "Sit still,"/
and he sat.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

None of us moved. The driftwood caught./
"Fill that," she said. She meant the cup. It was filled.

There is a hall. It is not this one./
It is a hall like this one, on a coast,/
whalebone in the roof, a pit under the floor,/

She is what you are./

Then the hammer. It lay where it fell,/

It was still warm. She carried it a long way,/

And the one who walks. [aldvar]{.name data-type="lore/deity" lang=nord}, [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]]'s son,/

and he says none of the old names. Not his father's./
Not the wolf's. Not this hall's, not the host's, not mine./
He passes the pit and does not know it was a pit./
He passes the shoot and steps around it, which is something./
And that is all of it: a shoot, a hammer,/
and a man who steps around the shoot.

There are people in it. Do not ask me where from./
The hovs have their answers, and the hovs are ash./
There are people in it, and there is weather, and ground,/
and a woman is cutting turf for a roof/
before the rain comes back,/
and she will put a sheaf on it, and she will not know why.

The fire is low. Shall I go on?

The host said, "Go on." His voice was not steady./
He was not a man whose voice was unsteady./
I say it as it was.

Then she did the thing the ting heard of./
She leaned out of the high seat and looked down the benches/
one by one, the way the host counts stores,/
and she named them. I will not say the names./
The ting took them out of my mouth and I have kept them out./
This is the shape of what she said, and it was said to each.

[…]/
Your name is […]. Your mother gave it you,/
and nobody in that morning will say it. Not once./
[…] the boat you took to [[place-knarvik|Knarvík]] for her ribs,/
and the whale you did not take,/
and the three winters you fed your father's brother/
when he could not feed himself, and the oath you kept/
at […]. None of it. Not a word of it. The shoot/
does not know it. The hammer does not know it./
The one who walks does not know it,/
and he is the one who would./
[…] your daughter's keys […] and the cask of oil/
you still owe at […]/
And mine. They gave me Aldurhildur at my first high seat,/
and nobody will say it there either. Nobody. Not once.

The host said, "What is a name for, then?"/
He said it the way a man asks the price of a thing/
he has already made up his mind to buy.

You were not listening. Listen now./
The pot is carried. Somebody carries it./
Not there. Here. In this age, in a hall like this one,/
down into a pit, in both arms, like a loaf./
The coal does not know who banked it./
[…] will carry it. I have seen the hands. […]/
Now read the bones. I have said what I came to say,/
and I want the hearth./
I am an old woman and I walked all day.

She did not ask whether the fire was low./
It was.

The host's wife read the bones. The reading is the hall's,/
and the ting did not ask me for it, and I did not give it./
She slept by the hearth with the sealskin bag under her head,/
and the dog that had turned around lay against her back./
She was gone before light. The host had let her out/
and barred the door behind her and gone back to his bed,/
and he did not say which way she went, and nobody asked him./
The spoon she ate with is up there, by the pot,/
and nobody eats with it.

The whale did not come in. The ice closed in the dark,/
and we ate the seal, and then we did what she had said,/
and the jarl counted us in at his door,/
and some of us did not need counting by then./
In the spring the ting sat at Dvarnvík and I was called,/
and I said it as she had said it, no shorter and no longer./
The rest is the ting's, and the skerry's, and not mine./
The fire is low.
