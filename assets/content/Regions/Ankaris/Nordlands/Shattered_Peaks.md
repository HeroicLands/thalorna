The Shattered Peaks are a compact massif in the mountain interior of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], within the [[affiliation-kingdmnrgd|Kingdom of Norgaad]]. They are small—about five leagues across—but no stretch of country on [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] packs more broken stone into so little ground, and the Nordmen who live around them treat the five leagues as a wall rather than a distance.

## Geography

The massif stands in the high Kjaldar country of central Norgaad, above the pass road that climbs from [[place-steinmark|Steinmark]] toward the mining valleys beyond. The name is literal. Frost works the dark stone of the Peaks the way it works no other rock in the north, splitting it along its grain so that every summit stands as a cluster of leaning spires and toppled slabs, and the skyline from any side reads as something broken rather than built. Below the spires the slopes are scree—loose, sharp, and moving—and below the scree lie hanging glaciers that calve into the cirques with a crack that carries for miles.

There is one practicable crossing: a notched saddle on the western face, reached by a scramble over scree, that opens onto a high, wind-scoured plateau and drops again by a gully on the east. A strong party clears it in three days of summer daylight. Every other line through the Peaks ends at a cliff, a crevasse, or a slope that gives way underfoot. Weather decides the rest. Cloud closes the saddle without warning, snow lies in the gullies through midsummer, and the wind on the plateau can pin a party in its tents for days.

## Who Dwells There

Nobody lives in the Shattered Peaks. The clans of the surrounding plateau—herders and hunters, the most reclusive in Norgaad—graze caribou on the lower shoulders in summer and hunt the goats that keep to the spires, and they are the only people who know the saddle route well enough to guide a stranger over it. They do not go higher than the grazing, and they do not stay past the first snow.

## Mithral and the Expeditions

Norgaad's miners, who work the richest iron and silver seams in the north, insist that the broken stone of the Peaks carries **mithral**. The claim has never been proved, and the massif's reputation rests on that gap: the rumor is rich enough to draw expeditions and the country is hard enough to lose them. Parties climb in from Steinmark with hired guides, sledges, and tents built for extreme cold, and a fair number are never heard of again. The Peaks hide things as readily as they swallow people—a cache set in the right cleft is safe from anyone who does not already know the cleft—and the same rumor that brings prospectors brings those with reasons of their own to reach a place nobody else can.

## How the Nordmen See Them

The Nordmen do not think the Shattered Peaks are cursed. They think they are honest: a place that makes no promises and kills the unready without malice. A Norgaadian who has crossed the saddle says so, and is believed; one who claims to have brought mithral out of the spires is not, and is asked to show it.
