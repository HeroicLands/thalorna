"They go out at dawn and they come back at dusk, and that is not a figure of speech," a rune-caster at [[place-odholm|Ódholm]] tells a newcomer to the hall. He means **Hugvin**, the raven of thought, who flies with [[lore-munvin|Munvin]], the raven of memory, across the whole world each day to gather knowledge and news for [[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]]. The [[affiliation-odvar|Faith of Ódvar]] depicts the pair in flight, and the raven feather, the sign of the faith, stands for both of them at once.

The rune-hall holds two readings of the birds, and the newcomer learns the first one first. On the literal reading, thought and memory are made bird-shaped: Hugvin leaves at dawn, Munvin beside him, and both return in the evening with what a god who cannot walk among people has no other way to hear. Ordained acolytes are taught the second reading after ordination. The birds, they learn, are a teaching figure for the unordained, and a rune-caster who waits on a raven's report has stopped doing the work the runes exist for.

[[being-hnirvmylwise|Hnirvmýl the Wise]] claims to hear Hugvin's whisper in the wind, guiding him toward hidden knowledge and lost lore. The claim is his own.
