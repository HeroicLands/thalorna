## Overview

Málstead takes its name from suit and speech at law, and six hundred people have built a town around exactly that: when [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]]'s own ting is not sitting, a dispute from the western farms is heard here instead, at a stone marked for the purpose in the market square, before whichever Lawspeaker is riding circuit that season. The arrangement saves a farmer a day's travel each way and has made Málstead the second-busiest market in the kingdom by default rather than by any resource of its own.

A [[lore-hersvaldrnk|Hersvald]] holds the surrounding district and keeps his hall at the town's edge rather than its center, a deliberate distance that lets Málstead's law-speaking keep the appearance of independence from any one man's authority. Domestic service and the ordinary trades of a market town employ most of the six hundred—[[being-dvlgthyrskrldmkh|Dvilgthýra Skraldumakh]] among the households that keep a merchant's hall running, [[being-hlskthnvtrdmr|Hliskthann Vetrdómr]] thatching roofs against a coast that gets more rain than [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]]'s—and the market itself draws farmers, trappers and the Hersvald's own levied men in about equal number on a fair day.

Málstead's one recurring argument is whether a verdict given here binds as firmly as one given at Gnarthborg, and the honest answer—yes, until a losing party appeals it to the king's own ting—satisfies nobody enough to stop the question being asked.
