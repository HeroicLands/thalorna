## Overview

Málstead's market square holds a stone marked for the purpose, and when [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]]'s own ting is not sitting, a dispute from the western farms is heard there before whichever Lawspeaker is riding circuit that season. "Bring bread for a week," a suitor who has waited at the stone since the last market day tells a newcomer. "The Lawspeaker rides a circuit, and the circuit takes as long as it takes." The arrangement saves a farmer a day's travel each way and has made Málstead the second-busiest market in the kingdom by default, since the town has no resource of its own.

The name is suit and speech at law, and six hundred people have built a town around exactly that. A hersvald holds the surrounding district and keeps his hall at the town's edge instead of its center, a deliberate distance that lets Málstead's law-speaking keep the appearance of independence from any one man's authority. Domestic service and the ordinary trades of a market town employ most of the six hundred. [[being-dvlgthyrskrldmkh|Dvilgthýra Skraldumakh]] is among the households that keep a merchant's hall running, and [[being-hlskthnvtrdmr|Hliskthann Vetrdómr]] thatches roofs against a coast that gets more rain than [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]]'s. The market itself draws farmers, trappers and the hersvald's own levied men in about equal number on a fair day.

Málstead's one recurring argument is whether a verdict given here binds as firmly as one given at Gnarthborg. The honest answer is yes, until a losing party appeals it to the king's own ting, and it satisfies nobody enough to stop the question being asked.

## The Wergild

The quarrel the kingdom's two great clans remember differently is called the Málstead wergild. A priest of [[affiliation-frodvin|Fródvin]] walked between [[place-braldheim|Braldheim]] and Málstead to seek a peace between the [[affiliation-grimvar|Grímvar]] and the [[affiliation-hrafnvar|Hrafnvar]], and both jarls marked a split oath-stave. [[lore-mlgnahstry|Histories and Legends of Malagna]] tells both sides.
