## Overview

A boat caught out in weather that would wreck it anywhere else on the coast can run for Hvalgvík and find calm water the moment it rounds the headland. The village sits in a bay that gives shelter whatever direction the wind turns, which is the plainest reason two hundred people fish from it and not from the exposed coves further up the coast, and its standing with passing fleets rests on that one fact of geography.

The **Hersvald of the coastal district** counts Hvalgvík among his smaller holdings, and the village's only standing obligation to him is the yeoman infantry its free men owe in the kingdom's turn of duty. [[being-gnldrthnslvrgr|Gnaldrthann Sólvargr]] is among them, called up with the district's levy rather than kept as a permanent garrison. [[being-hrndrndrknt|Hrandrinna Drekanótt]] thatches the houses that shelter behind the same headland the bay does, using reed cut from the marsh at its inner end, and crosses each season to thatch the roofs of [[place-glarvey|Glarvey]].

A sheltering bay draws more boats in a storm than it can always feed afterward. Hvalgvík's one real tension is the custom that decides how long a wind-bound stranger's crew may eat at a village hearth before they are expected to work for the privilege.

## Nine Nights Down

The village tells a tale as a warning. A woman of Hvalgvík went down after her dead husband and brought him home, and the tale says a Green Warden ended what she had brought back. It is a lesson about trying to reclaim someone from [[affiliation-nahild|Náhild]]. [[lore-mlgnahstry|Histories and Legends of Malagna]] keeps it.
