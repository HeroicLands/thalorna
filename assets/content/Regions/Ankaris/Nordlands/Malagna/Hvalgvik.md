## Overview

Hvalgvík sits in a bay that gives shelter whatever direction the wind turns, which is the plainest reason two hundred people fish from it rather than from any of the exposed coves further up the coast. A boat caught out in weather that would wreck it elsewhere can run for Hvalgvík and find calm water the moment it rounds the headland, and the village's standing with passing fleets rests entirely on that one fact of its geography.

The **Hersvald of the coastal district** counts Hvalgvík among his smaller holdings, and the village's only standing obligation to him is the yeoman infantry its free men owe in the kingdom's turn of duty—**Gnaldrthann Sólvargr** among them, called up with the district's levy rather than kept as a permanent garrison. **Hrandrinna Drekanótt** thatches the houses that shelter behind the same headland the bay does, using reed cut from the marsh at its inner end.

A sheltering bay draws more boats in a storm than it can always feed afterward, and Hvalgvík's one real tension is the custom that decides how long a wind-bound stranger's crew may eat at a village hearth before they are expected to work for the privilege.
