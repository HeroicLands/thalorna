## Overview

Gnarthborg's name is a jaw that will not open, and the kingdom built on it has a reputation to match: the finest shipyards in the north stand in its harbor, and the clans that work them do not give up a hull's lines, a keel's proportions, or a trade secret for any price a stranger has yet offered. Twelve hundred people live within the fortified town, second only to [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]] among the settlements of this account, and [[being-knghlrthni|King Hlurthann III]] holds his hall here, acclaimed at the ting like every king before him and answerable to the same assembly for every arm-ring he is handed back.

[[place-malagna|Malagna]]'s western footing shows plainly on Gnarthborg's wharves: [[place-elavendre|Élavendrian]] and [[place-aelwyth|Aelwythan]] traders keep standing arrangements with its merchant households that a more insular eastern kingdom would never tolerate, and the town's hersvaldar and Harbour-reeves spend as much of their working lives brokering a toll dispute with a foreign factor as they do mustering men for the king. [[being-snrvthnvtrdmr|Snurvthann Vetrdómr]] thatches the halls that house this traffic, ordinary work that a town this size needs in quantity no smaller settlement does.

The jaw that will not open cuts both ways. Gnarthborg's shipwrights guard their craft from outsiders, but the same closed hand has made the town cautious of the king himself: a crown that leans too hard on one clan's yards risks a strike it has no way to compel, since a shipwright's skill is the one thing in the kingdom a Hersvald's levy cannot muster by force. A northern king is not required to rule Gnarthborg the way he rules the rest of Malagna, and Hlurthann III does not try.
