## Overview

A cold hearth in Braldheim is a question before it is anything else. The three hundred and fifty people who live scattered through its homesteads read a cold hearth as a sign that the house inside has failed, and a neighbor who finds one is obliged to ask why before asking anything else. Braldheim's name is the fire a household banks at night so that it does not die, and Malagnans know the settlement for keeping that custom harder than most.

No hersvald or landvördr keeps a permanent seat at Braldheim. The district's officers ride a circuit out of [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]] and stop twice a season, which suits a settlement built on homesteads and not on a market square. [[being-glrvrnstrmrt|Glirvrinna Stormrót]] sews the tents and hide-coverings that keep a charcoal-burner's camp dry through the autumn cutting, and most of Braldheim's working year runs on the same charcoal that feeds Gnarthborg's forges: wood cut, banked and burned down slow through the settlement's own long nights before the carts take it to the shipyards.

The one quarrel Braldheim takes to the ting more than any other settlement its size is a boundary dispute over cutting rights in the forest behind it. A charcoal camp that burns another household's stand of trees has taken something that does not grow back before the next winter needs it.

## The Howe

The [[affiliation-grimvar|Grímvar]] keep [[miscgear-crwnwyrm|the Crown of the Wyrm]] in a howe at Braldheim, and a priest of [[affiliation-frodvin|Fródvin]] walked between Braldheim and [[place-malstead|Málstead]] to seek peace between the Grímvar and the [[affiliation-hrafnvar|Hrafnvar]]. [[lore-mlgnahstry|Histories and Legends of Malagna]] tells the story of the howe and the wergild.
