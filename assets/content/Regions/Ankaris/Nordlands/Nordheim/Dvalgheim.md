## Overview

A king's rider on the inland road out of [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]] measures his journey by Dvalgheim. He changes horses there, hands his charge to a fresh watchman, and rides on with a sentry's half-sleep still in his head, which is what the place is named for: the stem is the doze a sentry keeps while standing his watch. Dvalgheim is a relief-post, not a farming village, and the three hundred people who live there make their living from the riders and watchmen who pass through, so they keep a stranger's hours more than a farmer's.

No hersvald or jarl keeps a hall at Dvalgheim worth the name. The landvördr who collects the king's dues in this district stops only twice a year, and between his visits the settlement runs the way a bóndi's own stead runs, on custom and the say of whoever is oldest in the room. [[being-glrvrvstnblt|Glarvorv Steinblót]]'s workshop turns resins into the kind of goods a courier can carry small and sell dear at the far end of the road, a trade that would starve a larger town. [[being-rlthyrdrknt|Ralthýra Drekanótt]] begs at the relief-post's door, because travelers passing through stop longer and give more freely than people with somewhere to be.

The one argument Dvalgheim has needed to settle is who owes the watch when the relief-post itself is short-handed. The ting has ruled on it more than once, and the ruling stands: a traveler asleep on the road because Dvalgheim's watch failed is a debt the whole settlement answers for together.
