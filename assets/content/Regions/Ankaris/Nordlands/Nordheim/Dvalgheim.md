## Overview

Dvalgheim takes its name from the half-sleep a sentry keeps standing his watch, and the home built on that stem is a relief-post rather than a farming village: the king's riders between [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]] and the inland holdings change horses and watchmen here, and the three hundred people who live by that trade keep stranger's hours more than a farmer's.

No Hersvald or Jarl keeps a hall at Dvalgheim worth the name; the Landvördr who collects the king's dues in this district stops only twice a year, and the settlement runs itself the way a bóndi's own stead runs itself, on custom and the say of whoever is oldest in the room. [[being-glrvrvstnblt|Glarvorv Steinblót]]'s workshop turns the resins a courier can carry small and sell dear at the far end of the road into a trade that would starve a larger town, and [[being-rlthyrdrknt|Ralthýra Drekanótt]] begs at the relief-post's door because travelers passing through stop longer and give more freely than people with somewhere to be.

The one argument Dvalgheim has ever needed to settle is who owes the watch when the relief-post itself is short-handed, and the ting has ruled on it more than once: a traveler asleep on the road because Dvalgheim's watch failed is a debt the whole settlement answers for together.
