## Overview

The hersvald's horn at Raltholm is not blown to be heard; it is blown so the farthest farm knows whose horn it is. The levy of Raltholm is called from the holm itself, and a farmstead three fjords away that cannot see a beacon can still hear the call carried over the water on a clear day. The name is the reason: Raltholm is named for how far a shout carries over flat water on a still morning, and the hersvald who holds the district has made that fact an institution.

Seven hundred people answer to the horn. They work scattered farmsteads and the holm's own waterfront, and they are not packed behind a wall, because Raltholm has never needed [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]]'s ramparts to hold what it has.

## The District

The [[lore-hersvaldrnk|hersvald]] leads those farmsteads to the muster and speaks for them at the ting, and he keeps his hall on the holm. A Lawspeaker riding circuit out of Knalthstead stops here often enough that a dispute can wait for him instead of traveling to the capital.

The district feeds itself and the fleet both. [[being-nlthyndeldskr|Nulthynda Eldskari]] works the inland fields each summer, and [[being-krldmylvtrdmr|Kraldmýl Vetrdómr]] cuts the timber a longship needs. A standing complement of [[lore-bondirnk|bóndi]] owe the fort-watch and the archery a frontier district expects of its free men, and [[being-ilthvrblthl|Ilthvir Blóthöll]] is one of them, a yeoman archer by the duty his land carries and not by trade. [[being-nlthslddrtgr|Nilthselda Dróttgar]] keeps the apothecary that a muster-point this size cannot do without, treating the hersvald's men as readily as a farmer's burned hand.

Raltholm's grievance is the ordinary one of a district asked to send men and grain both. A hard winter that costs the horn its answer costs the hersvald his standing as surely as a lost battle would, because the men who answer the horn are the same men whose consent keeps him in his hall.
