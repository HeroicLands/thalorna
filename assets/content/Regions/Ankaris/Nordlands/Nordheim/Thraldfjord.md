## Overview

At Thraldfjord flat water is the dangerous kind, since rough water tells a fisher what it means to do. The fjord's surface lies silent and flat while a current moves hard beneath it, and every fisher among the two hundred who live here learns to read the difference before anyone lets a child out on it alone. The village is named for that current: _thrald-_ is the pull of a current under calm water.

The drowning the fjord has caused over the generations is recorded nowhere but in which households will not marry into which others. [[being-knlthvrstrmrt|Knulthvir Stormrót]]'s standing dispute over a drowned kinsman's nets has gone to the district's Lawspeaker twice without a ruling either side accepts.

## The Village

Thraldfjord lives on what the fjord and the forest behind it give up. [[being-gnrthrvsnrvrkh|Gnarthorv Snarvarukh]] and [[being-svlthyrslvrgr|Svulthýra Sólvargr]] hunt and trap the hinterland, and [[being-brldthnvtrdmr|Braldthann Vetrdómr]] sews the tents the trappers carry into it. [[being-knrvrjrnskl|Knarvir Járnskel]] keeps the small rite that asks the fjord's current for a fair season, a practice the godi of [[affiliation-thrunvald|Thrúnvald]]'s hof tolerates without naming it faith. [[place-wildwoodmnr|Wildwood Manor]] stands above the village, and its chief huntsman is Gnarthorv.

No hersvald keeps a hall here. Thraldfjord answers to the one at [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]], a half-day's row away, and sends its wergild and its disputes there when its own elders cannot settle them. A ship-grave at Thraldfjord is said to hold a captain of Nordheim's founding age ([[lore-nrdhmhstry|Histories and Legends of Nordheim]] has the family's telling).

What the village has never had is a season it could fully trust, and that is the whole of its character: good catches come, but nobody here plans a winter on the assumption that they will.
