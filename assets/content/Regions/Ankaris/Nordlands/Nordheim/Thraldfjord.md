## Overview

Thraldfjord takes its name from a current that moves hard beneath water lying flat and silent on the surface, and every fisher among the two hundred who live here learns to read the difference before anyone lets a child out on it alone. The drowning the fjord has caused over the generations is recorded nowhere but in which households will not marry into which others, and [[being-knlthvrstrmrt|Knulthvir Stormrót]]'s standing dispute over a drowned kinsman's nets has gone to the district's Lawspeaker twice without a ruling either side accepts.

The village lives on what the fjord and the forest behind it give up: [[being-gnrthrvsnrvrkh|Gnarthorv Snarvarukh]] and [[being-svlthyrslvrgr|Svulthýra Sólvargr]] hunt and trap the hinterland, [[being-brldthnvtrdmr|Braldthann Vetrdómr]] sews the tents the trappers carry into it, and [[being-knrvrjrnskl|Knarvir Járnskel]] keeps the small rite that asks the fjord's current for a fair season, a practice the godi of [[affiliation-thrunvald|Thrúnvald]]'s hof tolerates without naming it faith. No Hersvald keeps a hall here—Thraldfjord answers to the one at [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]], a half-day's row distant, and sends its wergild and its disputes there when its own elders cannot settle them.

What the village has never had is a season it could fully trust, and that is the whole of its character: good catches come, but nobody here plans a winter on the assumption that they will.
