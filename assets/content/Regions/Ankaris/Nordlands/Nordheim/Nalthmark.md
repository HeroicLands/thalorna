## Overview

Nalthmark sits where [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]]'s claim runs out: the stem names a limit rather than a place, and the mark itself is the last reckoned boundary before the land turns to country no Hersvald's writ reaches and no bóndi has fenced. Two hundred and fifty people hold the frontier farms and the single palisaded yard that gives them somewhere to stand together when the plateau clans or worse come down out of the high country, and [[being-vrldrvdrtgr|Vruldorv Dróttgar]] is one of several who stand a paid watch on that yard rather than farm it.

The settlement answers to the [[lore-hersvaldrnk|Hersvald]] of the wider district, who visits seldom enough that Nalthmark settles most of its own disputes by custom before troubling him with them, and a farmer here like [[being-krldrvdrtgr|Kruldorv Dróttgar]] reckons a bad season not against the market at [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]] but against whether the mark holds its line for one more year. [[being-hvrnvrstrmrt|Hvurnvir Stormrót]] fishes the cold water below the settlement when the weather allows it, which is not every year, and the margin between a catch and a hungry winter is thinner here than anywhere closer to [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]].

Nalthmark's argument with its own Hersvald is the frontier's oldest one: a district that measures its worth by what it holds against the wild has to be paid for holding it, and a Hersvald counting silver against three other demands does not always agree how much that is worth.
