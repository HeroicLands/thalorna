## Overview

Nobody lives on Dómsey, and nobody may. The island lies in [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]]'s waters at the heart of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], a low hump of turf and grey rock a long morning's row from the nearest strand, and for six years in every seven the only feet on it belong to the sheep the [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]] grazes there and the herdsman who rows out to count them. In the seventh year, at [[lore-sumarmal|Sumarmál]], the kings of all five kingdoms and their principal jarls land on its strand for the **King of All Clans**, the one assembly whose judgments reach beyond any single kingdom's writ. The island's name says what it is for: _dóms-_, the judgment's, and _-ey_, an island.

A Lawspeaker who has recited at three of those assemblies puts the island's first rule to a newcomer before the boat touches: "Leave your sword in the boat. Everything else about Dómsey follows from that."

## The Hallowing

Before any king steps ashore, the **Landsgodi** hallows the ground. The office belongs to the King of All Clans and to no single faith: the Landsgodi is the priest of the land who speaks for the [[affiliation-asguardian|Ten]] with one voice, and on Dómsey that is the whole of the work. He walks the bounds of the ting-ground and strikes each _hrend_ (boundary stone) as he passes it, as a frontier bóndi walks his own stones at Sumarmál, and when the last stone is struck the _tingfridr_ (ting-peace) is on the island. From that moment until the Landsgodi lifts it, a blow struck inside the stones is an offense against the gods and the five crowns together, and no wergild settles it.

## The Seats

The ting-ground is a ring of seats set in the turf around a single outcrop, the law-rock, where the Lawspeakers stand to recite. Each seat is a _tingsveld_: a stone bench with its holder's mark cut into the back. There are twenty-seven of them, and the count has not changed since the island was first hallowed.

The five kings sit facing the law-rock, Nordheim's at the center because Nordheim holds the island and its king usually convenes the assembly. Malagna's seat is held by [[being-knghlrthni|King Hlurthann III]]. Each kingdom's jarls sit in the row behind their own king.

**A jarl's seat follows the grant, not the man.** A seat belongs to a province, and the jarl who holds that province by the king's grant sits in it. A king who moves a grant moves the seat with it, which makes Dómsey one more place where a king's choice of jarls is watched. A seat whose holder does not come stays empty. Nobody sits in another man's tingsveld, and a king may not seat a favorite in an empty jarl's place.

The counts are the oldest quarrel on the island. Norgaad holds that five seats against Nordheim's seven undercounts a kingdom of four hundred thousand, and Vithgard holds that three seats, the same as Targud's, overlooks a kingdom that feeds half the north's lamps. Neither has carried the point, because changing the count would take the assembly's consent, and the assembly is the twenty-seven people the present count seats.

## How the Ting Is Held

Behind the ring stand the others who have business on the island and no seat: the Lawspeakers of the five kingdoms, the skalds of the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] who will carry the assembly's judgments home in their memory, the parties to whatever dispute has been brought, and their witnesses. Knights of the [[affiliation-eidhond|Order of the Sworn Hand]], who serve all five crowns and are sworn to none, keep the ting-peace inside the stones.

The assembly runs over several days in a fixed order. The Lawspeakers recite first, each the part of his kingdom's law the matters in hand will touch, so that every seat hears the law of the other four before it judges. Then the quarrels between kingdoms are heard, one at a time, each party speaking from below the law-rock. A judgment is given when the seats agree on it, and the assembly has no officers to enforce what it decides: its weight is the twenty-seven people who agreed and what follows for a crown that refuses them. Matters of war and peace with neighbors come last, after the quarrels inside the north are settled, on the old reasoning that five kingdoms quarreling among themselves cannot agree about anyone else.

When the last matter is done, the Landsgodi walks the stones the other way and lifts the peace, and the kings go back to their boats. The skalds have already started reciting.
