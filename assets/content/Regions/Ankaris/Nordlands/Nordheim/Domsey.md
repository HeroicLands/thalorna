## Overview

Nobody lives on Domsey, and nobody may. The island lies in [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]]'s waters at the heart of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], a low hump of turf and gray rock a long morning's row from the nearest strand, and for six years in every seven the only feet on it belong to the sheep the [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]] grazes there and the herdsman who rows out to count them. In the seventh year, at [[lore-sumarmal|Somarmál]], the kings of all five kingdoms and their principal jarls land on its strand for the **King of All Clans**, the one assembly whose judgments reach beyond any single kingdom's writ. The island's name says what it is for: _dóms-_, the judgment's, and _-ey_, an island.

A Lawspeaker who has recited at three of those assemblies puts the island's first rule to a newcomer before the boat touches: "Leave your sword in the boat. Everything else about Domsey follows from that."

## The Hallowing

Before any king steps ashore, the **Landsgudi** hallows the ground. The office belongs to the King of All Clans and to no single faith: the Landsgudi is the priest of the land who speaks for the [[affiliation-asguardian|Ten]] with one voice, and on Domsey that is the whole of the work. He walks the bounds of the ting-ground and strikes each _hrend_ (boundary stone) as he passes it, as a frontier bundi walks his own stones at Somarmál, and when the last stone is struck the _tingfridr_ (ting-peace) is on the island. From that moment until the Landsgudi lifts it, a blow struck inside the stones is an offense against the gods and the five crowns together, and no wergild settles it.

## The Seats

The ting-ground is a ring of seats set in the turf around a single outcrop, the law-rock, where the Lawspeakers stand to recite. Each seat is a _tingsveld_: a stone bench with its holder's mark cut into the back. There are twenty-seven of them, and the count has not changed since the island was first hallowed.

The five kings sit facing the law-rock, Nordheim's at the center because Nordheim holds the island and its king usually convenes the assembly. Nordheim's seat is held by King [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Hvarnorv]] and Malagna's by [[being-knghlrthni|King Hlurthann III]]. Each kingdom's jarls sit in the row behind their own king.

**A jarl's seat follows the grant, not the man.** A seat belongs to a province, and the jarl who holds that province by the king's grant sits in it. A king who moves a grant moves the seat with it, which makes Domsey one more place where a king's choice of jarls is watched. A seat whose holder does not come stays empty. Nobody sits in another man's tingsveld, and a king may not seat a favorite in an empty jarl's place.

The counts are the oldest quarrel on the island. Norgaad holds that five seats against Nordheim's seven undercounts a kingdom of four hundred thousand, and Vithgard holds that three seats, the same as Targud's, overlooks a kingdom that feeds half the north's lamps. Neither has carried the point, because changing the count would take the assembly's consent, and the assembly is the twenty-seven people the present count seats.

## How the Ting Is Held

Behind the ring stand the others who have business on the island and no seat: the Lawspeakers of the five kingdoms, the skalds of the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] who will carry the assembly's judgments home in their memory, the parties to whatever dispute has been brought, and their witnesses. Knights of the [[affiliation-eidhond|Order of the Sworn Hand]], who serve all five crowns and are sworn to none, keep the ting-peace inside the stones.

The assembly runs over several days in a fixed order. The Lawspeakers recite first, each the part of his kingdom's law the matters in hand will touch, so that every seat hears the law of the other four before it judges. Then the quarrels between kingdoms are heard, one at a time, each party speaking from below the law-rock. A judgment is given when the seats agree on it, and the assembly has no officers to enforce what it decides: its weight is the twenty-seven people who agreed and what follows for a crown that refuses them. Matters of war and peace with neighbors come last, after the quarrels inside the north are settled, on the old reasoning that five kingdoms quarreling among themselves cannot agree about anyone else.

When the last matter is done, the Landsgudi walks the stones the other way and lifts the peace, and the kings go back to their boats. The skalds have already started reciting.

## The Dead of the First Sitting

Everything above has one cause, and the Lawspeaker who tells a newcomer to leave his sword in the boat is telling him the cause without saying it. The five kings first met on this strand in 287, at the first sitting of the [[lore-domseycount|Domsey count]], and a quarrel over who sat nearest the law-rock became a killing that lasted an afternoon: nine jarls and about three hundred hirvmen died, Nordheim's King [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Svalthorv]] among them, and the four living kings and Svalthorv's son swore the ting-peace on a beach of their own dead. The swords in the boats, the hallowing, the twenty-seven seats and Nordheim's keeping of the island were all sworn that evening, and [[lore-nordfirstsit|The First Sitting on Domsey]] tells it.

What was not done that evening is why the island is empty. No grave-ale was ever drunk for the dead of 287, because to drink it is to speak the reckoning over it and the reckoning names which crown's hirv struck first, and four centuries of hall-credit compound on that question. The dead were never received, and Domsey is a _vrellvoll_, a blood-field, of the kind the north calls the silence. No bird lands on it. No insect sings on it in summer. No ewe has ever dropped a living lamb on it, which is why the kingdom's sheep are rowed off to lamb, and in the six empty years of seven the herdsman who counts them at dusk counts one figure more than he has sheep, once, and does not count again. The field runs thin, and it is held shut by the one thing that can hold a field for a set time: the Landsgudi's hallowing closes it for the days of a sitting, and lifting the peace opens it. A man who draws steel inside the stones at a sitting feels the dead of 287 stand up behind his seat, which is the reason no wergild settles a blow struck there and the reason the rule needs no guard to enforce it.

The Landsgudi pours one cup at the law-rock at each opening, "for the ones the count left out", and that is the whole of what the north does for them. A Malagnan claimant who offered to drink the grave-ale of 287 in his own name would be offering to reckon the debt before every king in the north, and a reckoning that found Nordheim's hirv drew first would put the island's keeping in question. A Nahild cell that meant to spill blood inside the stones at the sixty-third sitting would open the field with all five kings sitting on it.
