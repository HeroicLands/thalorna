## Overview

Follow the [[place-nalthmark|Nalthmark]] road down past the last fenced farms of [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] and you come to Hrindstead: a stockade of Nordheim settlers standing in an oak grove that the **Eichthúl**, a [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] clan, hold to be theirs. The settlement is twelve years old, and its name says what its founders meant by it: _hrind-_, a thing set down that will not be moved, before _-stead_, a farmstead. The thing set down is the settlers' own hall-posts, carried down the road from the halls they left and planted in the grove.

## The Land-Taking

The settlers made their claim in the way their faith teaches. They walked the bounds of the ground with fire and treated the rite as a claim their gods had witnessed; Nordheim remembers the whole affair as the **Nalthmark Land-Taking**. The Eichthúl answered by burning the first stockade. The settlers raised a second one on the same posts, and that is the Hrindstead a traveler sees today: new timber standing on old posts, inside a ring of oaks older than either people's claim to them.

## Two Claims to One Grove

To the settlers the grove is ground walked and witnessed. To the Eichthúl it is sacred ground, because a Varokhi clan's grove holds its shared identity, its oldest trees house powerful spirits, and no one takes from a protected grove without the shaman's consent. Each claim rests on what its people hold holy, and the two cannot both be satisfied. The fire-walking is part of the quarrel. It shows what the settlers believe, and it does not show that the grove was theirs to take.
