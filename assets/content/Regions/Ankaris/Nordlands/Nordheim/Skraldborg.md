## Overview

Skraldborg stands on a height that carries a warning cry further than any hill its size has a right to, and the fort was raised to use exactly that: a watchman here is heard on the water long before a longship rounds the headland, which has made Skraldborg the Nordheim coast's standing alarm for longer than anyone among its two hundred people can date.

The fort's own Hersvald holds no district beyond the height and the cove beneath it, and his whole claim to the office rests on keeping the watch fires laid and the horn-blower sober, a narrower charge than most hersvaldar carry but no less jealously held. The cove below is deep enough to hide a hull under repair from the water, which is why Glirvynda Hrafnsvald keeps her shipwright's yard there rather than at a more convenient landing—a damaged ship is a secret worth keeping from anyone who might profit by knowing a hall's fleet is short a vessel.

Skraldborg's tension is the one every watch-post eventually has: a cry raised too often for a sail that turns out friendly costs the Hersvald his hearing the one time it matters, and a cry raised too late is not survived to be argued about at all.
