[[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] remembers its past in judgments, household claims and songs. The stories of a grave or a
haunted road can preserve a warning without proving every deed attributed to the dead. The
[[doc-heroessgrd|Heroes of Asguard]] belong to that epic tradition: they may or may not have lived, and their adventures are
no record of Nordheim's law or institutions.

## The Kingdom's Past

[[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Nordheim]] counts its kings from the hall at [[place-knalthstead|Knalthstead]], where the [Knalthidrok]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} have held the crown since the hall-lords of the western fjords first raised one of their own over the rest. [Svalthorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the first king the ting acclaimed, about 270, died on the strand of [[place-domsey|Domsey]] at the first sitting in 287. His son [Snilthorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"} took the island's keeping in place of a wergild nobody else could pay, and every king of Nordheim since has convened the sittings by that right, as [[lore-nordfirstsit|the First Sitting on Domsey]] tells. A quarrel over the succession of the fifth king opened [[lore-nordkinwars|the Kin-Wars]] with [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Malagna]] about 420, and two generations later [Thrildorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"} held the Nordheim shore of [[place-skalfvatan|Skalfvatan]] while the Malagnan host went through the ice in 466.

[Bruldmil]{.name data-type="being/npc"} was king through [[lore-threewinters|the Three Winters]] of 512 to 514, and at the sitting of 518 he swore the oath told below. His son [Thraskmil]{.name data-type="being/npc"} sent the hulls south the next spring ([[lore-southrnfleets|The Southern Fleets]]), and the church silver in the royal howes at Knalthstead came home with them. [Vruthorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"} sent the fleets of three kingdoms over the water in 660 and made [[lore-stormveldtkn|the Taking of Stormveld]], and the jarls who hold that plain are Nordheim kin.

[Hvarnorv]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the present king, has reigned since 689. He is old, he has two grown grandsons and no named heir, and the sixty-third sitting is due at the next [[lore-sumarmal|Somarmál]], with the land-taking at [[place-hrindstead|Hrindstead]] still unsettled behind him.

## The Three Winters

In the winters of 512 to 514, three cruel winters ran together into one long famine, and [[lore-threewinters|the Three Winters]] tells the whole of it. Nordheim's interior
and [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] both claim the memory. Some tell of old people going over the
_svelthfell_, the death-fell above [[place-marvstead|Marvstead]], so younger kin might eat. Accounts differ on who went,
whether anyone was sent, and how much of the cliff story belongs to later telling. No known law
ordered it.

One version ends with five kings swearing at the [King of All Clans]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, the sitting of 518, that no hall should abandon its old
while another had bread. Whether they made that oath in those words, or whether the tale gives a form
to a duty older than the famine, remains disputed. The present obligation of kin and clan to feed and
shelter their own does not depend on proving the oath. Nor does the story establish the origin of
[[lore-kinhalcrdt|hall credit]].

## The Nalthmark Land-Taking

Twelve years ago, in 708, Nordheim settlers carried their old hall-posts down the [[place-nalthmark|Nalthmark]] road and planted
them at Hrindstead in a grove claimed by the [Eichthúl]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] clan. They walked bounds with fire and treated the
rite as a claim witnessed by their gods. The Eichthúl burned the first stockade; the settlers raised
another on the same posts. The grove's oaks remain a claim the two peoples cannot both satisfy. The
Nordmen's rite is part of the dispute, not proof that the land was theirs to take. [[lore-nalthmrktkng|The Nalthmark Land-Taking]] gives Nordheim's account.

## The Howe-Warden of Thraldfjord

At [[place-thraldfjord|Thraldfjord]], a ship-grave is said to hold a captain of Nordheim's founding age. A family story says
one descendant entered by the sea at low tide, took the dead man's sword and fought him in the dark.
The family keeps a sword it will not draw. The tale warns against disturbing a howe and taking what
was laid with the dead. Some call the captain a _[[lore-haugverdir|hrúmverdir]]_, a warden of his mound; the descent and
the fight belong to the family's telling.

## Voices on the Nalthmark Road

Travelers tell of a child crying between the last farm and the forest. The tale places the child in
the Three Winters and says its mother left it unnamed when food failed. Some call the voice a
_frostnár_, a lingering spirit, and say a name spoken by its mother's kin could lay it. Settlers and
[[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] each fear the sound and sometimes blame the other. Neither the cry nor the old mother's
choice should be turned into an established charge against a living family.

## The Night-Rider of Raltholm

The [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]] story begins with a hirvman's death, a thrall-woman accused of riding him in sleep, and
a ting that would not condemn her on a dream. She later walked into the fjord. People still turn shoes
outward and keep steel near the bed when they fear the _nottreid_. Others say that a person in the
hall wanted the hirvman dead and used the woman as an easy target. The telling leaves the sender, if
there was one, unnamed; it cannot undo the court's refusal to treat a dream as proof.

## The Mothers under the Stones

At [[place-horgsvangur|Horgsvangur]], women speak of the _hrúmsmodir_: mothers of the older clans laid beneath the stones. They
credit these dead with keeping a household's luck. The winter-night observance associated with them
is kept among women, beyond the public rites of the gudar. Men know that it is held but do not claim
to know what happens there. The belief says what the living ask of their foremothers; it does not
settle whether every misfortune is a dead woman's judgment.
