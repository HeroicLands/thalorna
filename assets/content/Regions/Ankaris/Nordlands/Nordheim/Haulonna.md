Near the edge of Thalmdal the one rule is that when the birdsong stops and the air turns cold and stale in the middle of summer, a hunter turns back and does not argue with his feet. The valley the old songs say no hunter could find twice is now the one nobody can avoid, because its blight creeps a little further in every bad year.

- **Type:** destroyed [[lore-flksinale|Sinalean]] enclave
- **Location:** the deep interior of the [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]], in the forested mountains between the high plateau and the glacial valleys
- **Status:** corrupted necropolis with no living inhabitants
- **Current Master:** [[being-hlskmylsvrtrnr|Hlusmil Svartrunar]]

## Overview

Thalmdal is what the Nordmen call Haulonna, a hidden enclave of the Sinale in the deep interior of Nordheim and one of the northernmost sanctuaries of the Firstborn anywhere in western Ankaris. For centuries it lay concealed in a forested mountain valley, shielded from human knowledge by wards older than the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]. The Nordmen who hunted the surrounding highlands sometimes spoke of a valley where the trees never lost their leaves, where the snow melted before it touched the ground, and where the birdsong was strange and beautiful, but nobody could find the way there twice, and most people took the stories for tall tales. They were accurate. Haulonna is Sinale for the hearth of the unfallen leaf, and the Nordmen were describing exactly what it says; Thalmdal, in their own tongue, is the hush that has lain over the valley since.

The enclave was small by Sinalean standards, a few hundred souls at its height, but it was ancient, founded in an age when the Sinale were more numerous and the world less crowded. Its people kept a sanctuary of contemplation and natural magic and tended a deep bond with the living forest. The trees of Haulonna were said to be among the oldest in the north, and their roots were woven into the enclave's wards in a living architecture where craft and nature could not be told apart.

## Destruction

The fall of Haulonna, in the Age of Halls when the north had hall-lords and no kings, is the best-known deed the songs of the [[doc-heroessgrd|Heroes of Asguard]] give to [[being-hlskmylsvrtrnr|Hlusmil Svartrunar]], a Nightwight necromancer (a [[being-tereb|tereb]], in the learned word) who served [[affiliation-nahild|Nahild]]. The goddess tasked him with destroying the enclave, whether as a strike against the living world, a test of her servant's power, or for reasons known only to the divine.

Under cover of night Hlusmil pierced the enclave's ancient wards, a feat that should have been impossible for a mortal, even a Helspawn one, and which suggests Nahild's direct intercession. Inside, he carved black runes into the earth itself and corrupted the natural energies that bound the sanctuary together. The corruption spread like a sickness: it twisted the ancient trees, poisoned the waters, and turned the wards that once protected the enclave into a cage for the people inside.

The Sinale fought. **Ansorudhe Lonvunto**, a guardian of great age and power, led the defense. Hlusmil's runic magic was ruinous to the natural and divine forces the Sinale drew upon, so that their own magic turned against them, corrupted and weaponized. In the last confrontation Ansorudhe faced Hlusmil directly, and the sorcerer, drawing on the full power of Nahild, shattered the Sinalo's star-staff, a blow that broke both the guardian and the last of the enclave's defenses.

Haulonna died that night. The ancient trees blackened and twisted, the clear waters turned dark, and the spirits of the fallen Sinale, scores of the Firstborn, were bound to the corrupted land by Hlusmil's runes and trapped in torment.

## Current State

What was a sanctuary of starlight and living wood is now a necropolis. The trees are dead but will not fall, and they stand as skeletal sentinels. The air is cold and stale in every season, and animals shun the place. Hlusmil has made the corrupted enclave his stronghold, drawing dark power from the imprisoned Sinalean spirits and the twisted remnants of their wards.

The corruption is not contained. It has crept outward since the Age of Halls, slowly and unevenly, tainting the surrounding forest and the water. Nordmen clans in the nearby highlands have noticed the blight in failing hunts, poisoned streams and livestock born wrong, though they do not know the cause. Travelers report stumbling into the valley's edges and fleeing from the wrongness they felt there.

## The Sinalean Response

Among the Sinale the fall of Haulonna is a wound that has not closed. An enclave was a place of ancient power and home to hundreds of the dwindling Firstborn, and its destruction has no precedent in the long memory of the Firstborn. That a human sorcerer accomplished it, even one empowered by a goddess, adds humiliation to grief. The Sinale measure time in centuries and their grudges run longer still, so the name Hlusmil Svartrunar is spoken in their remaining enclaves with a cold fury that will not fade for generations.

Sinalean agents, solitary, patient and implacable, have searched for a way to destroy Hlusmil and free the trapped spirits. The search is hard because his runes have so thoroughly perverted the enclave's own magic that a direct assault risks destroying the spirits it means to free. The Sinale do not act in haste, and they do not forget.

The spirit of Ansorudhe Lonvunto is believed to endure within the necropolis, resisting Hlusmil's control and working to undermine the sorcerer from within. If the reports are true, the fallen guardian may hold the key to unraveling the corruption, but reaching him means entering the heart of Hlusmil's domain.

## Significance

The destruction showed that Sinalean enclaves, long thought inviolable, can be found and broken. That has unsettled the Firstborn across Thalorna in ways human scholars can barely perceive. It has also drawn other powers: the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] is keenly interested in how Hlusmil pierced the wards, and certain factions within the Arcane Order would dearly love to acquire that knowledge, for defensive purposes, they claim.

Among the Nordmen the spreading blight is a growing concern. The highland clans know the hidden valley from the songs, and the older velvur hold that the songs tell the truth about what happened to whatever it once sheltered. Several have gone to look and have not returned.
