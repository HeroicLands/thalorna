## Overview

The ice-fishers of both shores teach their children one sound before any other: a boom under the ice in a marching beat, in the last weeks of winter, that means get off the lake and do not look back. Skalfvatn is a long cold lake lying along the border of [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] and [[place-malagna|Malagna]], in the hill country where the two kingdoms' howes face each other, and for six years in every seven it is an ordinary lake: pike and char under the ice, a Nordheim shore and a Malagnan one, and fishers from both who will sell each other bait. Its name is the shudder in ice before it gives, _skalf-_, before the generic for a lake, and the name is a warning that was ignored once.

## The Ice at Skalfvatn

In 466, in the last weeks of winter, King [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Gnirthorv]] of Malagna led his host across the frozen lake to take Nordheim's host from behind, against his own pilots' word that the ice was shuddering. King [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Thrildorv]] of Nordheim held the far shore, and the ice gave under the Malagnan center: about eleven hundred men and the king went through in the time it takes to tell it. Nordheim burned its own dead with full honors. The Malagnan drowned stayed under the ice, unburned and without grave-ale, and [[lore-nordkinwars|The Kin-Wars]] tells the battle and the peace that followed it.

## The Vrellvöll

The lake is a _vrellvöll_, a blood-field, of the kind the north calls a recurrence, and it runs moderate: harmless to a watcher who keeps off the ice, dangerous to anyone who answers a call from under it. In the last weeks of winter, when the lake ice shudders before it gives, the ice booms in a marching beat and ranks of light pass under it toward the Nordheim shore. Figures walk on ice too thin to bear them and are gone at a crack. Men who have slept within sight of the lake in that month dream the crossing from the middle of it, and wake with the cold of the water in them.

Neither shore fishes the anniversary month. Children of both learn the marching beat as the sound to run from, and a fisher who hears his own name called from under the ice goes home and does not go out again that winter. The field has never been settled, because settling it means drinking the grave-ale for eleven hundred Malagnan dead with the reckoning spoken over it, and the reckoning would say whether Nordheim's rune-workers had a hand in the ice, which Malagna asserts and no skald outside Malagna sings.

## The Headland Stones

The Malagnan shore keeps a ring of standing stones on the headland above the deepest water, eleven hundred of them, each cut with a drowned man's name by the kin who could not raise him a howe. One stone is blank. It is Gnirthorv's, for a king whose arm-ring was never brought up, and the [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Hlurthidrokh]], the royal line of Malagna, names itself from his son [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Hlurthann I]] rather than from the king under the ice.

## What Lies There

Gnirthorv's arm-ring lies under the lake, somewhere below the ring of stones. The man who brings it up holds a token of the oldest Hlurthidrokh claim there is, in a kingdom whose aging king has no acclaimed heir and two great clans on the edge of war. A Nordheim jarl who wants the blank stone cut and the grave-ale drunk at last would end the field, and would hand Malagna a wergild claim older than its present king on the day he did it. Both of those are reasons someone will go onto the ice in the anniversary month, and the lake has never yet let anyone who did come back with what he went for.
