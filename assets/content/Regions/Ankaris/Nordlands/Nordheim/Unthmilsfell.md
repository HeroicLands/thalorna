## Overview

The fell stands over the last stretch of the pass road that climbs from the farms below [[place-horgsvangur|Horgsvangur]] to the far side of the range. Its bald crown is turf over stone, and under the turf lies a ship. The herders of the upper steads call the hill by the name of the man buried in it: **Unthmilsfell**, the fell of [Unthmil]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. His own name is the far side of a pass, _unth-_, with a man's ending, and the hill is named for what he held.

Unthmil was a hall-lord in [The Age of Halls]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, before there were kings. He held the pass against whatever came up the far road, and he fell holding it. His heir fell at his side. The dead were carried up the fell, the ship was dragged to the crown and the turf was raised over it with the war-band laid around the keel. Nobody was left to drink the grave-ale. The hall that held the pass broke up, the line of its jarls ended, and the cup the heir was owed went undrunk. In the north's reckoning that leaves an empty high seat and a dead man still in it.

## The Howe

The mound is long rather than round, the shape of the ship under it. Three things mark it:

- **The gap.** A hole in the north flank, closed with turf, is where the dead were carried out of the hall. It is the way out and is not meant to be the way back.
- **The prow-post.** The carved head of the ship stands clear of the turf at the south end and faces down the road. It is old black oak, and in its brow a shard of black glass has been set. The glass was set by a living hand.
- **The cairns.** A line of waist-high cairns follows the road for half a day below the fell, one for each of the hall-lord's drott who fell in the pass. They are the toll-stones of the dead.

## What the Dead Do

At [[lore-veturnetur|Veturnetur]] the herders of the upper steads set a share of the slaughter-meat in the door-seat of the nearest hall, as the custom is for the honored old, and say the name of the man whose seat it was. The share is gone by morning. They have done it for as long as anyone remembers and speak of it as courtesy.

Travelers on the pass road describe the rest. After dark the cairns have men standing at them, grey with rime, eyes shut, in mail of a make nobody has seen. They do not move against a traveler who walks on. They ask one question in the old speech of the halls, _who holds the pass?_, and the traveler who answers with anything but an oath is not harmed on the spot and is not seen again at the next stead. The skalds of the district sing the pass as haunted and are careful to leave the verse unfinished.

The hall under the fell is lit. A herder who put his ear to the turf in the dark of the winter reports benches, a laid table and the sound of a feast, and the voice of a man calling the roll of the names of his war-band and getting answers.

## Whose Dead

The dead are [[lore-undead|undead]]: the war-band are hrúmverdir of the oldest kind, the _eidnár_ or oath-corpses, men bound to an oath past the death that should have released them, and the man in the high seat is something nearer a [[lore-nagengir|nágengir]] than the folk word allows. Every undead carries [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]], and the Shadow needs a way in. The undrunk grave-ale is the way. A dead man who is owed his reckoning and never receives it is held between the howe and the halls, and the black glass in the prow-post gives the Shadow something to grip.

The glass is not from the fell. The gudi of Horgsvangur know it by its look, the grey-black of the ice at [[place-dvilgvatan|Dvilgvatan]], and know what kind of hand sets it. The [[affiliation-nalok|Nalok]] work in secret to widen the reach of [[lore-ginnauga|Ginnauga]], the Eye of the Void, and a howe whose dead lack their ale is ground they can reach.

## Who Knows of It

- **The herders of the upper steads** keep the door-seat share and will not climb the fell after dark. They do not call the hall-lord a danger. They call him a guest who has not left.
- **The velvur of Horgsvangur** know the howe is wrong and have not moved against it. A wake on the fell is possible at Veturnetur and the leave for one is seldom given. A seeress who knows the fell is [Hrumrún]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who sits in the high seat of the altar-field's women and gives leave for a wake to the few she judges can bear what the dead ask.
- **The [[affiliation-ravenguard|Raven Guard]]** of [[place-odholm|Odholm]] count the howe among the holy places the north must defend, and they cannot spare the warriors to do it.
- **Traders on the pass road** pay a silver ring to the herders for guides who know which cairns to pass at noon.

## Reaching It

From the farms below Horgsvangur the pass road climbs for two days. In summer the way is merely steep. In winter the road is ice, and the cairns are the only marks of it. The fell is visible from the second day and is a bare brown hump with a black post at one end. The toll-stones begin half a day below it.
