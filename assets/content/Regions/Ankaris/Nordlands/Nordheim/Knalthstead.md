## Overview

"Do not count what the ring weighs," a clerk of the Hringvördr, the keeper of the rings the king gives, tells a newcomer standing at the back of the hall at Knalthstead. "Count who is watching when it is handed over." Knalthstead is the seat of the [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]], whose king holds the realm only as long as the ting goes on handing back his arm-ring, and the hall here is where that handing-back is watched most closely. A hringberi slighted at this hall is slighted in front of every clan with business here, which makes a king's generosity a public performance and not a private virtue.

Fifteen hundred people live inside and hard against the town's timber ramparts, more than in any other settlement of the kingdom. The wall is there because a capital cannot afford to lose what it keeps: the king's hall, the court where a níding is named and a wergild reckoned, and the gaol below it, where a man waits on the ting's word. The name is older than the hall. _Knalth-_ is the hard grade of the stem for an oath that needs no scribe to bind it, and a stead built on that word answers for every oath the kingdom swears in its own name.

## The Harbor and the Hall

A ship beaching at Knalthstead's quays meets a working harbor before it meets a market. Caulking sheds, cordage-walks and salting houses crowd the waterline, because the town's wealth is the fleet it keeps afloat, not coin it has never struck. No native currency moves here. [[lore-bartercnmy|Barter]] in furs, sealskin and iron tools and the running tallies of [[lore-kinhalcrdt|hall credit]] carry a season's trade until the next catch or raid settles the account.

The harbor-reeve who tolls the wharves and the landvördr who rides the king's dues out through the district work from the same hall as the Lawspeaker who recites the law at the ting and the Hirdstjóri who masters the muster. Three of the town's own households make their living from what that concentration of offices draws to one place. [[being-mrvrhrfnsvld|Murvir Hrafnsvald]] sets amber and silver for a court that must look the part. [[being-sklfvrasgrnd|Skilfvir Ásgrind]] sells what the forest yields to the kitchen that feeds the hall's hird. [[being-hrndmylsnrvrkh|Hrundmýl Snarvarukh]] holds the keys to the cell a níding occupies until the ting decides his case.
