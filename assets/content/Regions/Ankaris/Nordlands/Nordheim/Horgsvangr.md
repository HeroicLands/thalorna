## Overview

"The road is the first thing the place asks of you, and the last day of it is the hardest," a pilgrim who has made the journey to Hörgsvangr twice tells the newcomer beside her at the final farm. Hörgsvangr, the Altar-Field, lies in the interior of [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]], far from the coast and the merchant routes, and reaching it means crossing difficult ground that few outsiders choose to cross. The remoteness is deliberate.

It is the oldest inhabited settlement in Nordheim, and it predates the present kingdoms by centuries, perhaps millennia. About 300 people live there, in a settlement small and austere, built around a sacred grove and standing stones of unknown origin.

## The Priestesses

Hörgsvangr exists for ritual. An order of priestesses, the völvur who work seidr there, keeps a tradition that reaches back beyond anyone's records. They perform the great seasonal sacrifices, read omens in the flight of birds and the fall of runes, and conduct the deepest rituals of the Asguardian faith. What they sacrifice is not spoken of outside the settlement. Outsiders whisper of blood offerings, ritual combat and ceremonies under winter stars that seem to move as stars should not, and nobody who has visited will say whether the whispers are fact or fear.

Women of the older clans say that the mothers of their oldest lines lie beneath the stones, the _hrúmsmódir_, and credit them with keeping a household's luck. A winter-night observance connected with them is kept among women, apart from the public rites of the godar. Men know it is held and do not claim to know what is done.

## Who Comes

The settlement draws pilgrims of a particular kind: people who seek communion with the divine at the deepest level, who will endure hardship and lay their innermost selves open, and whose questions only a priestess of Hörgsvangr can answer. The word of the priestesses carries weight throughout Nordheim and beyond, far past what a settlement of 300 would command, and a district's hersvald will travel a long way to ask their counsel on a grave matter.
