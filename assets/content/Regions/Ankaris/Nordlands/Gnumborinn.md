"Everything he makes, he makes once," the Hofgydja at [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]]'s great hof of [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]] tells a newcomer. "Then he walks away from it. You are the only thing he ever made that could walk away from him in its turn and make more of itself. Think about what that cost." The north calls its people **Gnúmborinn**, the clay-born—_gnúm-_, a clay pot, and _-borinn_, one born of a thing—and the name is the whole account in two pieces.

## The Mold and the Clay

When the [[affiliation-asguardian|Ten]] had cut [[place-worldthlrn|Mannguard]] out of [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrímthur]]'s body in the [[lore-thursblot|Thursblót]], the world was ground, sea and weather with nobody on it. Mótefnir walked it. Where the giant's flesh lay against the meltwater coming off his rime the ground was clay, and the Maker pressed two figures out of it in his mold—the same mold his hofs carry as his sign beside an unfinished casting. He set them down on the strand to dry.

The first two have names, and the names are older than any hall. **Hrandmýl** is the man, named from _hrand_, a thing set down that will not be moved, because he was the first thing set down on the world and he stayed. **Dvarnrinna** is the woman, named from _dvarn_, a door that is never barred. Both names stand in the hard grade, the first of the three, and that is why the skalds teach that every hall's genealogy, counted back far enough, rings home to a hard-grade name: the first two were the first naming, and the grades have turned from them ever since.

## The Gifts

Clay set out to dry is a pot, not a person, and the tellings give the rest to the other gods. [[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]] gave them the fit—_ód_, the breath and the seer's fury that makes a body think and want. [[lore-solrundty|Sólrún]] gave them warmth, the color in a face and the heat in the blood. [[lore-frodvindty|Fródvin]] gave them growth, the hunger that makes a child become a man. The halls each add their own god to the list and quarrel about the order, but the three gifts every hall names are breath, warmth and growth.

Mótefnir's own gift is the one his hofs care about. He gave the clay-born the power to make more of themselves, which he gave to nothing else he ever made: his [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|Dreadspawn]] are each made once and none of them breeds. Then he did what he always does. He stopped, and walked away, and left them unfinished. "That is why you grow," the Hofgydja says. "He did not finish you. You are still being made, and you are the ones doing it."

## How Death Came In

Every hall teaches that the first two were made without death in them, and that it came in later. They do not agree on how, and the disagreement is old enough that a hof teaching one account names the other two.

**The door that swings both ways.** Mótefnir's hofs teach that death came in through Dvarnrinna's name. A thing that can make more of itself cannot also be kept for ever, or the world fills and nothing new has room. So the door that lets the child in is the same door that lets the old out, and it is never barred in either direction. The tongue keeps the account in its words: _dvern_, a birth, is built from _dvarn_, the door that is never barred, and the hofs of the Maker hold that the north named birth after the door because it knew which door death uses. Hrandmýl was the first through it. He grew old, as the made thing that grows must, and one evening he walked out of the door and did not come back.

**The giant in the clay.** [[place-odholm|Ódholm]]'s rune-hall teaches that the clay was Hrímthur's flesh, and Hrímthur was dead. People were pressed out of a dead giant, so they carry his death in them as the ground carries his wound, and every death since is the giant's death finishing its work in one more piece of him. The rune-priests do not find this grim. "You were made of something that was beaten," a rune-caster tells a newcomer. "It does not follow that you are."

**The unasked gift.** [[lore-nahilddty|Náhild]]'s hidden clergy teach that death was her gift to the clay-born, given after the others and without being asked for, and that she is the only god who gave people something they cannot refuse. No other hof will hear that account said aloud, and a household that repeats it is suspected of keeping her rites.

## The First Grave

Whichever account a hall keeps, it tells the same ending. When Hrandmýl was gone, Dvarnrinna raised the first howe over him, poured the first cup at it, and drank the first grave-ale—the _hrúmsminni_ every heir in the north still drinks before taking a dead man's seat. The north's whole manner of burying its dead, from the pyre to the howe, is held to start with her, and is set out in [[lore-nrdlndsfnrl|The Pyre, the Ship and the Howe]].
