Among the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] of [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]], the **Vyldgyldra** is the praise-poem the Hirdskáld **Hliskvir** made for **Jarl Krildorv Kraldarukh**, who holds one of the five Norgaad provinces whose jarls sit at [[place-domsey|Dómsey]], for a winter in which the jarl's fjords went hungry and nothing happened that a _gyldra_ ordinarily sings. It was first given at the close of [[lore-sumarmal|Sumarmál]], after the kings came home from Dómsey and its skalds had begun reciting, and the [[affiliation-skaldscrcl|Skalds' Circle]] has carried it as standing repertoire since the Skaldating after: it is the piece an apprentice's own _gyldra_ is measured against, and the Circle teaches it as the model of the truth of the recital. The text below is the repertoire text as one Stóraldstjóri teaches it to apprentices at the Skaldating, with his gloss of the kennings between the parts; the gloss is his, and other masters read one or two of the _hulmur_ otherwise.

Sit. This is the one your own _gyldra_ will be measured against, so you hear it first and not last. Six syllables to the line, two lines bound by their onsets, a chime inside the line and nothing at its end, and the clauses laid across one another so that a hall has to work to get the sense out. A _gyldra_ is paid with a ring, and a ring should buy something a thrall cannot follow.

I will not take the names apart for you. Krildorv is a name and Kraldarukh is a clan, and a stem is glossed when a stranger asks and never rendered. Everything else in it I will take apart, except one stanza.

: Vyldgyldra, the opening {#opening}

```poetry {form=gyldra lang=en}
Hear, hall, what a ring buys
of the cloak's raven, here:
no reddened sail, no small
[[place-vigvoll|Vígvöll]], no steel; the wolf's
long turn at the chain wrote
this one, and I wind it
notch by notch, where it fell.
Sit still. The count is long.

The thin flesh gave little
that fall; the furrow's bone
showed through the stubble, bare.
Bones burned black at the blót:
the blade-bone said, short year,
the steward's stick, shorter;
[[lore-solrundty|Sólrún]]'s share went whole in,
none stinted the giver.

The door-seat filled with gray,
fed first, as the oath says;
steward served them, then us,
the bench-holder ate last.
By the door, one would not.
The bowl went by her, full.
The wall was whole that night,
the turf-knife on its peg.

Jól came. The pits, counted
twice, came up the same twice.
He called the ting in snow —
no sword came, no feud came —
opened, with all eyes on,
the hall's own barley-pit;
Krildorv's own key turned first.
Loud, the lock of a jarl.
```

He names the kind of poem in the first line by its price, and what he will not sing in the third and fourth. The cloak's raven is a Hirdskáld, from the brooch he wears. The reddened sail is a raid, and a small Vígvöll is a battle, because every battle is the last one, smaller. The wolf's long turn at the chain is winter: [[lore-vetrulfr|Vetrúlfr]] is chained until the end, and winter is in his name. "Notch by notch" is the tally-stick, and the _hnerv_ is built on the same stem.

The thin flesh is the fields. [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrímthur]] had little flesh on him, and what there was went thin over the bone when the Ten cut him, so a furrow's bone is the rock under a field and "the thin flesh gave little" is a bad harvest. The blade-bone is the shoulder read at the [[lore-vetrnaetr|Vetrnaetr]] blót. The steward's stick is the búvördr's tally of the pits, and it said worse than the bones, which is Hliskvir telling you the bones were kind. The giver is Sólrún, whose gold is given away. She takes her portion whole in a hungry year, and he wants you to know nobody cut it.

Stanza three you will work out yourselves. I am not asked about it, and I do not answer.

A ting called in snow with no sword and no feud is a ting called for one thing. "Loud, the lock of a jarl" is no _hulma_. The whole district hears a jarl's pit opened.

: The notched part, first {#notches-first}

```poetry {form=gyldra lang=en}
He set, where the fire's kept
coal lit it, the hand that
stayed, iron, thumbless, on
the floor; need in one pan,
Healer's gold, the other;
mouths weighed, child and gray;
bóndi's bowl and jarl's bowl,
one measure, the pan said.

He ate what his men ate.
He ate it where they saw.
His wife kept the measures
on a stick by the door.
Nobody was fed twice.
Nobody was fed less.
His bench on Dómsey bare;
mark cut, moss on the mark.

From the giant's spilled cold
the ebb left a hill, fat,
at [[place-gnaldrfjord|Gnaldrfjord]], dark months,
where the floes grind their teeth;
the finder ran, knife sheathed,
to the strand's holder first;
the law was on the strand
before the first cut fell.

The law-mouth said the sixth,
the hungry rule, the jarl's,
and he shared by the pan
where the blood meets the flesh;
the strand's man stood to lose,
Krildorv stood for the strand:
"Mine the debt; the first good
year pays it, from my pit."

Flat light lay in the casks;
lamps burned, that had been dark;
the strips went on the racks,
the ribs went for rafters
where the gale had the roof.
Nobody carved the jaw.
His bench on Dómsey bare;
mark cut, moss on the mark.
```

The fire's kept coal is the hearth. The hand that stayed is the scale. [[lore-eidgardty|Eidgar]] paid a hand to the wolf's chain, and what the other hand holds is the iron scale, so the hand that stayed is the thing in it. A master whose apprentices sit on the bench across from you teaches that it is the sword, because the god is carved holding either. He has never stood at a rationing. Nobody brings a sword to the floor to weigh barley. The Healer's gold is [[lore-frodvindty|Fródvin]]'s barley.

The sixth stanza has no kenning in it. He put none there. A rationing is a plain thing and he gives it to you plain, and then he cuts the first notch. The notch comes before you know what it counts. That is what a notch is: you cut it when the year falls, and you count it later. "His bench on Dómsey bare" is a _tingsveld_, a stone bench with the holder's mark cut in its back, and his stood empty at the assembly that year. Apprentices ask me every year what the moss stands for. It stands for moss. The island is wet.

The giant's spilled cold is the sea, from his blood, and where the blood meets the flesh is the tide-line, the sea on the soil, which is the strand the law names. The fat hill is the whale, and he never says whale, because the word is small and the thing was not. The law-mouth is the Lawspeaker, and the sixth is the sixth rule as Norgaad's Lawspeaker counts [[lore-nrdlndswhal|the law of the stranded whale]]: in a hungry winter the jarl shares it, and the strand is repaid from the next good season. The jarl's own words stand unbroken in the seventh and eighth lines. Flat light is the oil, which is the stem's own sense and barely a _hulma_. Nobody carved the jaw: no gatepost stands at Gnaldrfjord for this one.

: The notched part, second {#notches-second}

```poetry {form=gyldra lang=en}
Seal-weeks: the cold's hard hem
gave once under the knives.
Each man's take was each man's;
most went into the pan.
Twice the floes groaned that month,
and I have those by name,
not for a ring; the notch
is cut here, and not named.

"No keel in Jól," the ship-
masters said, every one;
"[[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrúnvald]] has shut that floor" —
he heard each, and sent one
keel, crying off shingle,
south for the south's yellow,
flat light for the buying
and the ring off his arm.

The seventh year's boat came
ice-out for the bench-men;
the king's man said "Dómsey,"
and Krildorv, "My keel first" —
a grant moves; he knew that —
the boat went without him.
His bench on Dómsey bare;
mark cut, moss on the mark.

Twenty-six backs to stone,
law-rock, the five law-mouths;
Norgaad's row had a gap,
nobody's back filled in.
The king looked, they tell me,
and looked at the law-rock.
No sword inside the stones;
no word about the bench.

Then shingle cried again:
Half a hold. Half is grain.
The south had sold it dear,
the ring stayed in the south,
three strakes sprung, two men short —
the floes groaned for them too —
the grain went to the pan.
The pan had not been moved.
```

The cold's hard hem is the ice-edge, where the sealers go in the hungry weeks. "Twice the floes groaned" is two deaths. Gnaldrfjord is named for the sound its ice makes, and every coast in the north hears that sound as somebody dying. Sing it inland and it is a line about ice. He has the two by name, and says so, and keeps them out. A _gyldra_ is bought and a _hnúra_ is not, and the nine are named in the household's _hnúra_, which the Circle does not teach.

Thrúnvald's floor is the sea again, and he has two words for it. When it gives, it is the giant's cold. When it is sailed, or it takes, it is the Thunderer's. The shingle crying is a keel, from the sound a keel makes going off and coming on, and the keel's own word is built on it. The south's yellow is grain from [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]]. The ring off his arm went for the buying.

The seventh year's boat is the king's boat come for his jarls, and the grant moves: a king who moves the grant moves the seat with it. He knew it and let the boat go. Hliskvir was not on Dómsey. "They tell me" is how he says so. The king's answer is a look at the law-rock, and he gives you no more because he had no more.

: The notched part, third {#notches-third}

```poetry {form=gyldra lang=en}
Sumarmál: keels go down
crying to Thrúnvald's floor;
the hall stands roofed in bone,
the pit is not yet shut,
the old sit by the door,
fewer. Fed first. The oath.
His bench on Dómsey bare;
mark cut, moss on the mark.

Hear the debt, strand-holder:
it is in this, and said:
a hill of fat, weighed out,
owed from his first good year;
I will say it at Jól
until the strand says paid.
A debt said is a howe:
nobody digs in it.

The tellers have a cliff
in the Three Winters, gray
heads going over it.
They have it. I do not.
I have one door, one bowl,
one winter, and no cliff.
Whatever the kings swore,
this one kept it unsworn.

Hliskvir stood at the pan
each night, and took his bowl.
I wear my masters' marks,
cut in the arm's silver.
The one-eyed's birds were here;
they saw what I say now.
His bench on Dómsey bare;
mark cut, moss on the mark.
```

The hall roofed in bone is the whale's ribs where the gale had the roof. "Fewer" is the first time he tells you anyone died at the door-seat.

The sixteenth stanza is the debt entered. A debt said at a feast and carried by a skald is in the hall's [[lore-kinhalcrdt|standing credit]] from that night, so a debt said is a howe, and nobody digs in it: digging in a howe is theft from the dead and the line together. He has said it at two Jóls so far.

The cliff is the Three Winters. The tellers have gray heads going over it, and some skalds give the five kings' oath as history. Hliskvir will not; he cuts no notch he did not see. The one-eyed's birds are [[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]]'s ravens, which wheel over the cliffs at Gnaldrfjord, and the brooch on a Hirdskáld's cloak.

: The close {#close}

```poetry {form=gyldra lang=en}
Now the close. Cut these too.
Keels sit on the water,
the gray are at the door,
the pan is on the floor.
Nine went out by the wall
while the pan held even.
Nine notches. Hear them cut.
I will not say a name.

Two the shudder-edge took
when it gave, with the seals.
The floes said so. I heard.
Two, Thrúnvald's floor has kept;
two stones on the headland
cut, so they do not ride.
The grain they died for came.
It was weighed like the rest.

One came in by the door
in the dark months, and went
out by the wall at Jól,
light. A hand closed the gap.
One gray from the door-seat,
fed first, and full, and done.
One with no voice. No fire.
A tool went in with him.

One the sickness had first,
before the hunger could.
The pan had weighed her fair.
Fair did not hold, that year.
Where she sits I do not
sing. The hofs quarrel there.
Eight notches. One to cut.
The knife goes slow on it.

By the door, one would not.
The bowl went by her, full.
He set it down himself;
she set it by the next.
He had the pan brought in.
Need stood in the one pan.
The other pan stayed up.
No key turns a mother.

She went out by the wall
before the keel came home,
in the seat by the door,
fed first. The bowl was full.
Her son cut the wall gap.
Her son weighed out that day.
Nine notches. There it stands.
Give the ring, or do not.
```

By the wall is dead: a hall cuts a gap in its own wall to carry the dead out and closes it behind them, and the door is for the living. The stones on the headland are the drowned men's, cut with runes so they do not ride with the [[lore-jol|Jól-Ride]]. One came in by the door: a birth is a door in [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]]'s hofs' teaching, which is the teaching of Hliskvir's hall, and [[place-odholm|Ódholm]] would have given you [[lore-gnumborinn|the giant in the clay]] instead. No voice and no fire is a thrall, buried without a pyre, with a tool put in by a master who thought well of him. Where the sickness-dead sit is the hofs' quarrel, and a skald who settles it for pay recites what he does not know.

The ninth you have had since stanza three. I said you would work it out. "Fed first" is the oath's word. "The bowl was full" is Hliskvir's.

He ended on "Give the ring, or do not," before the jarl and the jarl's hall, in the spring after the jarl's mother went out by the wall. Krildorv gave the ring, with one ring fewer on his arm to give it from. No master has been asked to soften the close, and I have said what I will say about it.
