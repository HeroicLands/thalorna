**Skipskreld**, the Ship-Flyting, is the party-piece of the fishing havens along the [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] coast, and [[place-thraldfjord|Thraldfjord]] sings it on [[lore-midsumar|Midsomar]] bonfire night, after the low flames have been jumped and before anyone is too drunk to hold a line. Two singers each take a god, [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] and [[lore-velgrimurdty|Velgrímur]], and the haven takes the third part: the _hnerv_ (refrain) is "Row, then," shouted at whichever god has just lost an exchange, and shouted at both when nobody can tell. It is a _skreld_, a flyting, in the speech-meter the north keeps for the gods' own mouths—two short lines bound by one sound, then a long line that closes the thought—and the verse hangs on flat prose links that count the voyage in oar-strokes. The links are the haven's own and differ from one haven to the next; the opening link is given below in two versions, Thraldfjord's and [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]]'s, since the two are a half-day's row apart and will not agree on it. Where the song names the sea in other words it is the giant's blood, after the [[lore-thursblot|Thursblet]], and an oar is the pine that walks on it. [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]]'s Hofgudi does not allow the piece sung inside the hov, and says why: "The trickster wins too many of the exchanges, and a crew that has laughed at the right lines goes out remembering the wrong ones." [[place-malagna|Malagna]]'s ports sing their own version, in which Velgrímur wins more of them still. The one below is Thraldfjord's, as the haven's two best mouths have it.

At Thraldfjord they tell it so. Thrunvald and Velgrímur were crossing open water in a two-oared boat, bound for Thrumufjall from a shore the song does not name, and the wind fell at noon and did not get up again. Thrunvald could have had a wind. He had promised [[lore-frodvindty|Frodvin]] a still sky while the barley set, with his hand on the hammer, and the barley was setting. So the Thunderer took an oar, and Velgrímur did not.

At Raltholm they tell it otherwise. The two were not going anywhere; they were coming back, from a harbor Velgrímur had his reasons not to name, in a boat that was not theirs. The wind fell at noon all the same, the word to Frodvin stood all the same, and Thrunvald took an oar all the same. Raltholm adds that the boat leaked.

: Skipskreld {#skreld}

::: {.verse form=skreld lang=en work=skreld}
_Thrunvald said:_/
Take the oar, Velgrímur./
There is one on your side./
Two pines walk this water, and one of them is yours.

_Velgrímur said:_/
I see the oar./
I see the sea under it./
I have looked at both since noon, and neither one has moved.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Take the oar.

_Velgrímur said:_/
You are the sea's god./
Say a word to the sea./
Tell the giant's blood to get up and carry the boat.

_Thrunvald said:_/
The sky is sworn./
I swore it to Frodvin/
until his barley sets. I will not wake a wind.

_Velgrímur said:_/
Break it./
Who would know you broke it?/
Frodvin is in a field with both hands on a sick cow and his back to the sea.

_Thrunvald said:_/
The barley would.

_Velgrímur said:_/
Then wake yourself./
Wake the hammer-arm./
Put it on the pine and pull, and let the oath keep the sky.

._Row, then._
:::

thuv es asva na hafra ok hafskel./
marl eiv marlma tiv hafra‼/

thrunvald marl./

Thrunvald rowed. He pulled forty strokes on the steerboard side, and the boat came round until her bow faced the way she had come. He crossed the thwart and pulled forty on the other side, and she came round again. The headland they were making for stood where it had stood at noon, with the sun on it. Velgrímur sat in the stern with his feet up on the ballast and counted the strokes aloud.

_Thrunvald said:_/

_Velgrímur said:_/
That is your hammer/

the one stone, and it breaks heads with the other end of the swing.

The hull went out blessed/
from your hov in the spring,/

Seven men, and a calm./
Not a storm. Not a wind./

You held their names./
The hov held their mead./
Tell me what the blessing bought, and I will pull for that.

_Thrunvald said:_/
A blessing is not a plank./
I never priced it as one./
The hov says it. The gudi says it. Ask them what they sell.

_Velgrímur said:_/
Your faithful carry iron/
till their feet give out under it,/
a weighted hammer, and they call the falling a rite.

Carry this pine, then./
Carry it as far as the shore./
Drop in the shallows, Thunderer, and I will call it a crossing.

They stand all night for you,/
soaked to the skin, no roof,/
one man on a headland, keeping a stone from nothing.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Take the oar.

_Velgrímur said:_/
God of reavers./
Your reavers sail south/
and take the barley other men set, and you bless the hull that carries it.

You keep the [[affiliation-thrunvald|Reaver's Thanksgiving]]:/
reavers home with the take,/
a blet on the beach for it, and the hull blessed again for next year.

And you will not wake a wind/
where the barley wants one,/
because a herb-bundle asked you. Which field is it you serve?

_Thrunvald said:_/
The one I gave my word on.

_Velgrímur said:_/
Nine steps./
That is the song, is it not?/
You strike the wyrm and walk nine steps and lie down.

I have seen the nine steps./
I have seen them from a mead-bench/
to the yard behind [[place-solvangurhall|Solrún's hall]], and nobody made a saga of it.

Eight, and the knees go./
Nine, and the beard goes down/
in the midden by the byre. Shall I count them for you now?

._Row, then._
:::

The singer who takes Velgrímur's part names no haven for the drowned men. Thraldfjord supplies its own. Thrunvald rowed on. The boat had been taking water since noon, and at the hundred-and-twentieth stroke it was over the bottom boards, so he shipped the oar and took up the scoop and bailed. The scoop was whale-bone, and it was the only thing in the boat that Velgrímur had brought aboard; he did not say from where. For a while the strokes were not counted; at Thraldfjord the children count the scoops instead, and the singer lets them.

: Skipskreld, the bailing {#skreld-3}

::: {.verse form=skreld lang=en work=skreld-3}
_Velgrímur said:_/
Bail, then. Bail./
That is water that will bend to you./
It came aboard by itself, and you did not have to ask it.

_Thrunvald said:_/
I will bail. Then I will row./
And you will sit and talk,/
and talk is what you have, and I have been counting it.

Where is your hov?/
Name the harbor. Any one./
A hidden hov in every port, your gudja says, and no one has seen one.

So who would know/
whether you have any?/
A god whose hovs are hidden is a god whose hovs are nowhere.

Hidden, you say./
Hidden is a turd under snow./
It keeps until the thaw, and then the whole yard knows where it was.

._Row, then._

_Velgrímur said:_/
My hovs are in Malagna./
Go and find one of mine./
You would have to be let in, and no door in Malagna opens to a hammer.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Your faithful finish a siege/
with no fight in it./
They go in over the wall at night and open the gate from inside.

_Velgrímur said:_/
And yours sit outside it/
and sup on the farms around it/
until the gate comes open by itself. Mine is open by morning.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Your one rule./
Your gudja reads it plain:/
do not be the reason we are found. Everything after that is for sale.

_Velgrímur said:_/
It is one rule, and kept./
Count me one of yours/
that a crew has kept past the harbor mouth.

_Thrunvald said:_/
I was in [[place-gnarthborg|Gnarthborg]]./
You had gone through before me./
Two ships in the harbor, one bound north and one bound south.

You sold them my blessing./
You sold it twice, in my name,/
with a stone off somebody's horgur, and you took sealskins from both.

One blessing. Two ships./
Opposite ways out of one harbor./
That was my name on the stone. Tell the haven how it went.

_Velgrímur said:_/
Both came home.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Both came home./
That is the worst of it./
All Gnarthborg thinks the blessing is worth two skins now, and that it comes from you.

._Row, then._
:::

: Skipskreld, the twelve sagas {#skreld-4}

::: {.verse form=skreld lang=en work=skreld-4}
_Velgrímur said:_/
Twelve sagas, Thunderer./
Tell them over on your hands./
In six I pull you out of a thing you walked into with your eyes open.

In six I am the thing./
That is the half they sing./
Nobody sings the pulling-out. There is no hammer in it.

_Thrunvald said:_/
Six out, six in./
I will stand you the count./
And in all twelve, trickster, I was the one at the oar.

And [[lore-solrundty|Solrún]]'s tables./
You sat the dark months through,/
fed first and asked after, the way her hall does it, and you were never asked.

They marked it as mine./
Hall-credit, on my name,/
a winter of mead, and the skald stood up at [[lore-jol|Jel]] and said so.

And you said the skald was wrong./
Nobody says that./
Not to a skald, not at a feast, not with the ring on his arm.

_Velgrímur said:_/
He was wrong./
It was two winters./
He had the count short, and I will not be told I owe less than I do.

_Thrunvald said:_/
You shed like a snake/
and leave the skin in the straw/
of whichever hall fed you, for the thrall to find in the bedding.

You look in a dark glass/
to look at what you have done,/
and you like what is in the glass better than the daylight.

I know what you are./
Flat water, and a pull under it./
Every child on this coast is taught to read that before it is let out alone.

._Row, then._
:::

At Thraldfjord the haven shouts the refrain twice there, and the singer who has Thrunvald's part waits for it. Raltholm's singers leave out the snake and the glass and go straight from the skald to the flat water, and say that Thraldfjord put the two in to make the Thunderer sound quicker than he is. Then Thrunvald rowed again. He pulled on the steerboard side until the boat had come round once more and faced the headland, and he kept pulling, and the boat kept turning, and he kept on. Two hundred strokes. Velgrímur had stopped counting aloud.

legva arvi inn, thrunvald, thara‼/
vendri af skromrand ok sken ir‼/
hvorn es ir inn skip? hvorn halv thuv ir inn it?
:::

Thrunvald did not answer for a hundred strokes. Velgrímur counted them, and this time he counted under his breath. The haven goes quiet for the hundred; nobody counts them aloud, and a child who starts to is hushed. At the hundredth Thrunvald shipped his oar, and what he said is two lines and not three; no haven has ever made the third.

: Skipskreld, the two lines {#skreld-6}

::: {.verse form=skreld lang=en work=skreld-6}
_Thrunvald said:_/
Today you are in the boat./
Take the other oar.
:::

::: {.verse form=skreld lang=nord enc=nordthuravarkenc work=skreld-6}
thrunvald marl./
gludva hern es thuv inn skip, gesta./
nem andra arvi, nura‼
:::

Velgrímur took the other oar. They made shore at dark, and the strokes from there have never been counted. The haven has the last line.
