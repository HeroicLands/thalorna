**Skipskreld**, the Ship-Flyting, is the party-piece of the fishing havens along the [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] coast, and [[place-thraldfjord|Thraldfjord]] sings it on [[lore-midsumar|Midsomar]] bonfire night, after the low flames have been jumped and before anyone is too drunk to hold a line. Two singers each take a god, [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] and [[lore-velgrimurdty|Velgrímur]], and the haven takes the third part: the _hnerv_ (refrain) is "Row, then," shouted at whichever god has just lost an exchange, and shouted at both when nobody can tell. It is a _skreld_, a flyting, in the speech-meter the north keeps for the gods' own mouths—two short lines bound by one sound, then a long line that closes the thought—and the verse hangs on flat prose links that count the voyage in oar-strokes. The links are the haven's own and differ from one haven to the next; the opening link is given below in two versions, Thraldfjord's and [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]]'s, since the two are a half-day's row apart and will not agree on it. Where the song names the sea in other words it is the giant's blood, after the [[lore-thursblot|Thursblet]], and an oar is the pine that walks on it. [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]]'s Hofgudi does not allow the piece sung inside the hov, and says why: "The trickster wins too many of the exchanges, and a crew that has laughed at the right lines goes out remembering the wrong ones." [[place-malagna|Malagna]]'s ports sing their own version, in which Velgrímur wins more of them still. The one below is Thraldfjord's, as the haven's two best mouths have it.

```verse {form=prose lang=en cols=2 numstep=5}
1 [At Thraldfjord thenva marl likva hern. Thrunvald ok Velgrímur gang tvar berv vatna inn skip miv tvei arvi. Thenva gang tiv Thrumufjall af strandva na dirma nel nafm. Snirna stap at solmid ok nel risna genda. Thrunvald kan hav snirna. Hanv eidna hlusi himra tiv Frodvin inn setma na barnva miv hondi or hamri. Barnva setna nura. Darni Thrunvald nem arvi. Velgrímur nel nem.]{.nativefont lang=nord}
2 At [[place-thraldfjord|Thraldfjord]] they tell it this way. [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] and [[lore-velgrimurdty|Velgrímur]] crossed open water in a two-oared ship toward [[place-thrumufjall|Thrumufjall]], from a shore the lay does not name. Wind stopped at noon and did not rise again. [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] could have wind. He promised [[lore-frodvindty|Frodvin]] quiet sky while his barley set, hand upon hammer. The barley was setting. So [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] took an oar. [[lore-velgrimurdty|Velgrímur]] did not.
2 [At Thraldfjord thenva marl likva hern. Thrunvald ok Velgrímur gang tvar berv vatna inn skip miv tvei arvi. Thenva gang tiv Thrumufjall af strandva na dirma nel nafm. Snirna stap at solmid ok nel risna genda. Thrunvald kan hav snirna. Hanv eidna hlusi himra tiv Frodvin inn setma na barnva miv hondi or hamri. Barnva setna nura. Darni Thrunvald nem arvi. Velgrímur nel nem.]{lang=nord}

1 [At Raltholm thenva marl andra. Tvei nel gang tiv velri sta. Thenva komri bak af strandva na Velgrímur nel vril nafm firn hugdar na sjel. Skip nel es na thenva. Snirna stap at solmid likva samri. Gandma tiv Frodvin stavi likva samri. Thrunvald nem arvi likva samri. Raltholm marl skip lekva aukri.]{.nativefont lang=nord}
2 At [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]] they tell it differently. The two were going nowhere. They returned from a harbor [[lore-velgrimurdty|Velgrímur]] would not name for reasons of his own. The ship was not theirs. Wind stopped at noon just the same. The word to [[lore-frodvindty|Frodvin]] stood just the same. [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] took an oar just the same. [[place-raltholm|Raltholm]] says the ship leaked too.
2 [At Raltholm thenva marl andra. Tvei nel gang tiv velri sta. Thenva komri bak af strandva na Velgrímur nel vril nafm firn hugdar na sjel. Skip nel es na thenva. Snirna stap at solmid likva samri.
```
