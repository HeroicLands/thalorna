Put your hand flat on the ground of any hall in the north and, the hovs teach, you are touching a giant. That is not a figure of speech at a hov; it is the first fact of the faith. In their telling, when the Ten of the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian Pantheon]] had broken the giant-kin and shut the rest of them into the void, one of the kin lay dead on the field: [[lore-hrimthurspr|Hrimthur]], the Rime-Giant. The Ten cut the world out of him. The act has a name, the **Thursblet**—_thurs-_, a giant, and _-blet_, a sacrifice—and the name is a quarrel the north has never finished.

## The Cutting

The skalds give the making in the order a butcher works. The Ten opened the body and set it out. His bones became the stone of the fells and the bare rock under every field, and his flesh the soil laid over it, thin in the north because there was never much flesh on a rime-giant. His blood ran into the low places and became the salt sea, which is why the sea is cold and never still. The rime that coated him in life stayed where it lay and became the glaciers and the snowfields, and the meltwater at a glacier's foot is the giant still giving up his cold. The ground that came out of all this is [[place-worldthlrn|the World of Thalorna]], Mannguard, the world underfoot.

The tellings give each of the Ten a hand in the work and quarrel over whose hand came first. [[place-odholm|Odholm]] gives the first cut to [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]]; [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]]'s hovs say the hammer broke the bones before any blade was needed; [[lore-motefnirdty|Mótefnir]]'s hovs say nobody cut anything until the Maker had marked where the cuts should fall. Every hall agrees on the one point that matters to the pantheon. The cutting is the only work all ten of them ever did together, and every quarrel the sagas carry dates from after it.

## Why the Ten's Hovs Call It a Blet

A Hofgudi teaching a newcomer at a hov of the Ten explains the name by the rite. A blet is an offering that is shared rather than destroyed: the gods partake, the living partake and the dead partake, and the sharing binds all three together for as long as the meal lasts. The Thursblet, on this account, is the first blet and the pattern of every one since. The Ten took their portion as the halls of [[place-asguard|Asguard]], the living take theirs as the ground they farm and build on, and the dead take theirs as the howes they are laid in. "Every feast you will ever eat in the north is eaten off his body," the Hofgudi says, "and every blet you attend is the same meal, served again."

That is why the hovs of the Ten speak of the cutting with pride rather than unease. It is the pantheon's oldest claim and its proudest: the world exists because the Ten won, and a hov that remembers the cutting is remembering a victory.

## Why Holafell Refuses the Word

The adherents of the [[affiliation-hrimthur|Faith of Hrimthur]] call it the first cutting and nothing else. A blet, they say, is given willingly by a household that owns what it gives, and nobody asked the giant. Their rites are not poured on an altar but cut into the rock, because what was done to him was a cutting, and [[lore-renewalwound|the Renewal of the Wound]] cut at [[place-holafell|Holafell]] at the turn of every winter keeps that cut open on purpose. An Adherent there gives the dispute its shortest form: "They call it a feast because they ate. He calls it nothing, because he is the plate."

[[affiliation-thursborn|The Giant's Children]] stand closest to Holafell's reading. Their doctrine holds that the world's continued existence depends on the founding violence being renewed, and they fight in that conviction. The hovs of the Ten do not condemn either faith for its reading; they treat the Renewal as accurate and the word they refuse as Holafell's own business.

## What the Cutting Asks of You

A newcomer to the north meets the Thursblet in small things before anyone explains it. A hov of Hrimthur counts its gray chips, struck from its own floor, aloud against the roll of its dead at [The Naming of the Stone]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, and returns them to the floor they came from. A pilgrim pours meltwater taken at a glacier's foot over his hands before a rite at a horgr on high ground. At every blet of the Ten, the meal is shared three ways because the first one was. Each of these treats the ground as a body, which is what every hall in the north holds that it is.
