"A coast that misses its whales goes hungry before midwinter," an old harpooner at [[place-knarvik|Knarvík]] tells a newcomer who has signed on for his first run. "That is the whole of it. Everything else I tell you is how not to be the reason." The _hvelm_ (whale) is meat, fat, light and building timber to the northern coasts, and the hunt for it is the most valuable work a [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] household does in a year. A whaling winter that fails is remembered for generations: the last one in Vithgard ended in the burning on the skerry of [[place-askholm|Askholm]], when a ting at [[place-dvarnvik|Dvarnvík]] looked for someone to blame for the empty strand.

## Who Hunts

Whaling is coast work, and most of it is done in the west. [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]] lives by it more than any other kingdom, with [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]]'s western fjords, [[place-gnaldrfjord|Gnaldrfjord]] among them, close behind. [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]], [[place-malagna|Malagna]] and [[place-targud|Targud]] take whales where they find them and do not build their year around them.

A whale-boat is crewed by a household or a group of neighboring households, under a _skipstjóri_ (shipmaster) who answers for the boat in the kingdom's levy. A hall that keeps ships may put out several boats and share the catch with every household that manned an oar. Before a run, a Vithgard boat goes to the yards at Knarvík to have its ribs and strakes reinforced, because a hull that meets ice-pressure or a wounded whale's tail with a cheap repair in it opens.

## The Seasons and the Hunt

The great whales pass along the western coast twice a year, northward in spring and southward in autumn, and those are the hunting seasons. The crews row out to meet them with the _hvelmgeir_ (harpoon), a heavy spear on a line, and work a whale for hours until it tires. Every harpoon carries its owner's mark cut in runes on the socket, because the law turns on whose iron is in the whale.

In summer the smaller whales come into the fjords in pods, and a whole coast turns out for the drive. Boats spread across the mouth of the fjord and close behind the pod, the crews beating the water and throwing stones to turn it toward the shallows, and when the pod grounds, everyone on the strand goes in with knives. A drive feeds a district rather than a household, and it is shared by the strand's own reckoning: a portion to every household that sent a boat, a portion to the holder of the strand, and a portion set aside for the households with nobody to send.

## Blubber, Oil and Bone

A whale on the strand is flensed where it lies. The _flöm_ (blubber) comes off in long strips and is either eaten or rendered in iron cauldrons on the beach into _glerv_ (oil), which goes into casks. Glerv is the north's lamp-oil and its waterproofing, and Vithgard's is prized across the five kingdoms for both; the hall that lights its winter with its own oil has had a good season. The meat is eaten fresh, dried on racks or salted down for the dark months. The _merv_ (bone) builds rafters and fences on coasts with no timber to spare, and the jaws and ribs of great whales stand as gateposts in villages that have had a famous catch.

The ivory that carries Vithgard's name south comes mostly from walrus and narwhal rather than the great whales, and is worked at [[place-marvstead|Marvstead]], where the [[lore-hersvaldrnk|hersvald]] collects the dues that both ivory and whale-oil owe the crown.

## The Trade

What a coast does not eat or burn, it trades. Glerv, ivory and whalebone go south with the merchant ships to [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]], and they are the reason a Vithgard trader can buy grain in a year when the kingdom grew none. Inside the north most of it moves by [[lore-bartercnmy|barter]] and [[lore-kinhalcrdt|hall credit]] rather than coin: a cask of oil this winter against a share of a neighbor's barley next autumn, reckoned and remembered.

## The Law of the Stranded Whale

A whale that comes ashore dead is the most fought-over thing a beach can hold, and every kingdom keeps a law for it that its Lawspeaker recites at the ting. The law is the same in substance across the five kingdoms.

- **The strand is the owner.** A whale stranded on a shore belongs to the holder of that shore. Where the shore is held by nobody, it belongs to the district, and the hersvald answers for its sharing.
- **The finder is paid.** Whoever finds a stranded whale and reports it to the strand-holder before cutting it is owed a share, set by custom as the finder's portion.
- **The iron speaks.** If a harpoon with a readable mark is in the whale, the marked harpoon's owner is owed half of it, provided he claims it at the next ting. A harpoon nobody claims by then leaves the whole whale to the strand.
- **No knife before a witness.** Nobody cuts a stranded whale until the strand-holder or a witness for the landvördr has seen it. A whale cut in secret is stolen, whoever owned the beach.
- **A whale afloat belongs to the line.** A dead whale drifting at sea belongs to the first boat that makes a line fast to it, so long as that boat tows it ashore. A crew that cuts the line loose to save itself in weather gives up the claim.
- **In a failed winter, the jarl shares it.** When a district is hungry, a stranded whale is shared out like the jarl's opened stores, by need, and the strand-holder is repaid from the next good season.

Most quarrels the ting hears about whales are quarrels about the third rule: a mark worn past reading, a harpoon claimed by two crews, a beach where the high-water line moves.
