Far south of the last chain of wells, where a dry lake has left a floor of white salt wider than a day's ride, one dark thing stands up out of the glare. The **Tower of the Lightning-Marked** is a spire of black stone, taller than any fire-temple in the cities of [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]], split along one side and glazed over most of its height with a grey-green glass that the lightning has made of the sand. The tribes call the place **Qeshvadez**, the seat of lightning.

## What Stands There

The spire rises from a low mound at the middle of the **Pale Floor**, and every storm of the season finds it. Lightning strikes the tower on most nights when the sky holds any weather at all, and it strikes the same place: an iron spike at the summit, thick as a man's thigh, that the builders drove into the stone and nobody has been able to reach in living memory. Where the bolts have run down the walls the stone is fused into ropes and sheets of glass. Around the foot of the mound the flat is pocked with fulgurites, rooted tubes of glass that the sand has been turned into, some as long as a spear.

Inside, the tower is a single stair that winds up the wall around an empty core. Iron rungs and bands are set into every course, and in a storm the whole interior sings. Chambers open off the stair at intervals: a vestibule at the foot, a hall where the lineages sit when they come, a cell for fasting, a sealed room that the elders say was the cult-master's, and above it the open gallery under the iron spike. Nobody sleeps in the tower. Nobody lingers there after a bolt has struck, because the air stays charged for a long breath afterwards and a man's hair stands out from his head.

## The Master Who Went Into the Storm

The [[affiliation-strmcltsbhrms|Storm Cults of Bahramish]] are five lineages with no master over them, and the Council of Elders at the [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]] decides only what touches all five. The tower belongs to the story of a man who wanted otherwise. **Vahrun Marzuvan**, a storm-walker of the _ghuzmogar_ and a _ruzhogar_ in his own lineage, gathered the cults' youngest and boldest to the Pale Floor and taught them to call the lightning down on purpose, to a spike and a place, and to read the god's mood from where it fell. He took the title _Master of the Tower_ for himself. The Council did not confer it, and when he claimed to be a vessel of [[affiliation-bahramis|Báhramis]] without the assent of the gathered elders, they denounced him. He wore an iron crown he had forged himself, because the iron torc of a vessel was never given to him.

The elders of the [[affiliation-strmcltsbhrms|Lightning-Marked]] lineage, who were touched by the god in vision or by a bolt and so have the best claim to speak of lightning, tell the end in few words. A great storm came up out of a clear sky. The master climbed to the gallery alone. The bolt took the tower and the storm did not leave with it, and in the morning he was gone: no body, no ash, not so much as the crown. **The master never died. He went into the storm.** The elders say it the same way each time and decline to say what they mean by it. Mehrava, the senior elder of the lineage, refuses to soften what she reads in the sky above the Floor, and what she reads there she does not say to outsiders.

## The Salt Toll

Caravans cross the Pale Floor because it saves ten days on the road between the southern wells and the Amradi markets. They pay for it. Apprentices of the Lightning-Marked (_hamelogar_) sit in a tent at the northern edge of the flat and take a toll in salt, one load in twenty, and in return they read the sky for the crossing and name the day it is safe to go. A caravan that pays and crosses on the named day is rarely troubled. A caravan that refuses, or crosses on the wrong day, is said to be taken by the storm, and the dead are found in the flat with the glass rooted in their wounds.

The toll is a hospitality gift in the old sense, the same as a sheikh's, and the tribes see nothing wrong in it. Merchants of the walled cities see a good deal wrong in it and pay it all the same.

## The Kinless Watch

A day's ride west of the mound, on a rock that stands clear of the salt, an Amradi-funded fort keeps watch on the road. The garrison are **Kinless**, men cast out by their kin and claimed by none, hired with coin by the Kingdom because no tribe's feud can reach them and no sheikh's word can order them. They are owed no water and offer none, and the fort's cistern is guarded as closely as its gate. A Dunhari who comes to the Kinless Watch owes the garrison nothing and is owed nothing; a stranger who eats its salt is a guest for the stated days and no more, since the salt is the host's. The watch reports to an Amradi _ghedvader_ in [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]] and does not speak of what it sees at night on the flat.

## What the Tribes Say

- The sky above the tower is clear on the night after a bolt falls. Those who climb the mound at that hour say the stars are wrong.
- The iron spike hums on windless nights, in the tune of a voice-bearer's lament.
- A herder who watered his goats in the shadow of the tower came away with every animal blind in one eye and facing east.
- The storm cults do not deny any of it. They deny that anyone should go and see.

## Reaching It

From the Amradi shore the route runs south through the oasis towns and then by the long wells to the northern tent of the toll. From [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]] the road is nine days over open sand. A guide who knows the Floor charges more than one who does not, and one who knows it well will not take a traveller to the mound itself.

## Hooks

- **The crown.** A thief has heard that the master's iron crown went into the storm with him, and that lightning cannot melt it.
- **The watch.** A soldier is posted to the Kinless Watch, where nobody owes him water and the nights are loud.
- **The Promise.** A leader of the Circle is asked whether the Old Promise holds against a Dunhari who stands halfway out of the world.
