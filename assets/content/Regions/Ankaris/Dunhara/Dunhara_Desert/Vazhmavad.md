Vazhmavad is the weighing-place, an oasis of date palms on a gravel shelf where the poets of a third tribe sit when two tribes bring their recitations in dispute and a judgment must be made. The Dunhari keep their law in memory, and where two memories disagree the decision has to be made by someone qualified to judge which is sound. This is where they sit.

## The Weighing

Vazhmavad is not a settlement. Its wells are deep and good, its palms are old, and a ring of low stone benches stands under the largest of them where the judging poets sit to hear each side recite. A Truce-Warden's peace lies over it in the season of a hearing, and poet-apprentices learn the recitations of every tribe there by listening. It is the nearest thing the desert has to an archive: not a building but a gathering of memories, each one held by a living person.

## What Holds It

The poets are alive. Their eyes are open and they walk the paths between the palms, and they recite. They recite the genealogies of other people's lines, sentence after sentence, in even voices, and they do not know their own names. If they are asked who they are, they begin another line.

The oasis is held by [Melkhur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a poet by training and a sorcerer by choice. He speaks pleasantly and walks under the palms with his hands behind him. He is a thin, gentle-faced man in the dyed blue of a poet's sash. He takes a name as other men take a coin: by asking for it, and being given it. A tribe's genealogy goes, and with it the tribe's standing in the recitation, and with that the tribe's claim to wells and wives and herds. His raiders, who are called [the Unrecited]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"}, are men and women whose names he has taken. They have no kin to shame them and no line to disgrace, and they strike caravans on the southern circuit and leave the survivors unable to say who they were.

## The Hollow in the Well

The source of the sorcery is below the deepest well of the oasis. Melkhur found a hollow in the water, a place where the dark does not reflect, and the thing that lives in it takes what is given and returns nothing. It is [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]], the chaos from outside creation, and it is attracted to what Dunhari hold most dear. The poet found that it will accept a recitation as payment. The more he gives it, the more it gives him: sight into a man's line, speech in a dead voice, a word that unmakes an oath. He pays with other people's names. He does not know, or has chosen not to learn, what the hollow keeps of its own.

## What the Tribes Say

- Caravans on the southern circuit have not come in from the southern circuit, and the tribes that sent them cannot name their lost.
- A trader who passed through Vazhmavad and bought dates there came away speaking in the cadence of a man long dead.
- [[being-trvshshrdr|Torvash Shiradar]]'s poets have stopped sending disputes to the weighing-place, and have been unable to say why.
- The judging poets have stopped speaking of their own tribes at the gatherings.

## Reaching It

The oasis lies on the southern circuit, a few days beyond the long wells. Its location is known to every guide, and every guide now routes around it.

## Hooks

- **A claim lost.** A sheikh is asked to prove his line before the common council at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]] and cannot, because his poet came back from Vazhmavad empty.
- **A debt unwritten.** A merchant finds the kin-credit that carried half his trade has vanished from every memory but his own.
- **The Circle.** [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|The Circle]] has no mandate: the [Old Promise]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} bars a blade against a Dunhari while an outside enemy walks the land, and the Unrecited are Dunhari with their names removed.
