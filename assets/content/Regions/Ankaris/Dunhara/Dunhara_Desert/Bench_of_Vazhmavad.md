The **Bench of Vazhmavad** is the company of judging poets at [[place-vzhmvd|Vazhmavad]], the weighing-place, an oasis of date palms on the southern circuit of the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]]. When two tribes of the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] bring their recitations in dispute, the poets of a third tribe sit on the low stone benches under the largest palm and weigh which memory is sound. The bench is the only body the oasis has, and it is held now by one of its own poets, [Melkhur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.

## Who Decides

The poets still sit on the benches in their dyed blue sashes, and they still recite, sentence after sentence, in even voices. They have not weighed a dispute since Melkhur came to hold the oasis, and no tribe brings them one. Melkhur decides what the oasis does: who draws water from its wells, who eats its dates, who is received at his table under the largest palm, and who rides out with [the Unrecited]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} onto the southern circuit. The apprentices tend the palms and the wells and listen at the benches, as apprentices always have.

The Truce-Warden's staff stands planted at the edge of the oasis, and its peace holds. A guest under it is safe from every hand inside the palms. The Dunhara Tribes have no central authority to send against the oasis, and the [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|Warrior's Circle]] has no mandate against Dunhari. The bench answers to nobody, and neither does its holder.

## Meeting It

A party comes to Vazhmavad as guests. The poets do not stop reciting to greet them. Melkhur does: he walks out under the palms with his hands behind him, courteous and gentle, and invites them to bread and salt at his table. He asks their names. Every guide on the southern circuit now routes around the oasis.

## Hook

- **The first case in years.** A sheikh whose line is disputed brings his poet and his rival's to Vazhmavad, because no other bench will hear a case this old. Melkhur agrees to have it weighed, and the sheikh wants the party with him on the benches when the recitations begin.
