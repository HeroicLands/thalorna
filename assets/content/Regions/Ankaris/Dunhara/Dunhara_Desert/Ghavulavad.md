The caravan comes into Ghavûlâvad an hour before dusk, when the heat lets go of the sand. The town is a hollow of palms in a gravel plain in the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]], cool under the trees and smelling of wet earth, dung smoke and crushed dates, with black goat-hair tents running out from the water in long rows. It is the nearest thing the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] have to a capital: the oasis where the common council of the tribes meets, and where a caravan bound across the desert hires its guide.

The well is the town. Boys haul dripping leather buckets up from it from dawn to dark, and the order in which the herds come to water is set by the council's Water-Warden and never argued in his hearing. Around the well the market runs whenever a caravan is in: wool against salt, dates against iron, a camel against a season's silver. The tents belong to whichever tribes are camped there that season, and the guides of a dozen tribes wait in them for a caravan-master's offer.

Ghavûlâvad is home to [[being-shadznkmrz|Shâdezan Kamrûz]], the chieftain whom the storm cults recognize as a vessel of their god, and to the storm-shaman [[being-firavdprsk|Firâvâd Pâresak]], and petitioners come from across the desert to put their troubles before one or the other. When the common council sits, the tents spread far out across the gravel, and the Truce-Warden's peace holds over all of them.
