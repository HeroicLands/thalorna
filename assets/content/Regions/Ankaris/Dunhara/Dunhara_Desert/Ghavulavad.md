## Overview {#overview}

Ghavulavad is the oasis seat of the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]]: a hollow of palms round a single great well in a gravel plain of the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]], where the common council of the tribes meets and where a caravan bound across the desert hires its guide. It is the nearest thing the tribes have to a capital, and it is a town only because the well never fails. The families of the palm gardens live at the water the year round, and the black tents of whichever tribes are camped there that season run out from it in long rows.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The caravan comes into Ghavulavad an hour before dusk, when the heat lets go of the sand. The hollow is cool under the trees and smells of wet earth, dung smoke and crushed dates. Before the first palms a boy runs out from whichever tribe's tents claim the road that season, and the newcomers are brought to its fire and given bread and salt before they are brought to the water. Only then is anyone asked his name, his business and who sent him.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

The well is the town. Boys haul dripping leather buckets up from it from dawn to dark, and channels cut from its basin water the gardens in turn. Under the palms the gardens are walled plots no larger than a tent's floor, with dates overhead and barley, onions and fodder beneath, and the garden families sell their dates to every camp that comes in. Milk and meat come from the herds at the water. Bread is another matter: the hollow grows too little grain to feed itself, and the rest comes in from [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]] on the caravans, so the price of a sack of barley is the first thing anyone asks a caravan-master.

Around the well the market runs whenever a caravan is in: wool against salt, dates against iron, a camel against a season's silver. Its real trade is guides. The guides of a dozen tribes wait in the tents for a caravan-master's offer, and the fee a caravan pays goes to the guide's tribe, which spends it at the same market before it moves on.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

No sheikh holds Ghavulavad. The well belongs to all the tribes in common and is kept by the council's Water-Warden, who sets the order in which the herds come to water, and that order is never argued in his hearing. Between sittings of the council the garden families settle their own quarrels by their elders, and a quarrel between two camped tribes goes to the Lawkeeper if both sides agree to have it heard. When the council sits, the Truce-Warden walks the tent-lines, and his peace lies over every tent, so that tribes at feud camp within a spear's throw of each other and neither draws a blade.

## The Present {#present}

The council is sitting, and the tents spread far out across the gravel. The question before it is the Kingdom of Amradad's offer of grain and iron for escort, and the Amradi envoys camp at the edge of the gravel as guests, with salt and no seat. Disputes between tribes pile up before the Lawkeeper, because no guide takes a poet to [[place-vzhmvd|Vazhmavad]], and he hears more of them than one man can. When the council rises each evening the poets contend by the well, and the tribes call first for [[lore-blackstonesode|The Ode of the Black Stones]].

Ghavulavad is home to [[being-shadznkmrz|Shadezan Kamruz]], the chieftain whom the storm cults recognize as a vessel of their god, and to the storm-shaman [[being-firavdprsk|Firavad Paresak]], and petitioners come from across the desert to put their troubles before one or the other. Shadezan's riders hold more of the escort trade than any other confederation's, and the smaller tribes at the council watch whom he receives.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

There is no inn and no caravanserai. A stranger lodges as the guest of the tribe whose guide he has hired, in its tents and at its fire. Its salt makes him its charge for the stated days after he leaves, and its feuds become his manners: he does not share a fire with its enemies while he is its guest. A party that comes in without a guide is the Water-Warden's guest for one night, by custom, and finds a host by morning or camps outside the palms.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The guide's price.** A caravan-master needs a guide for the southern circuit, and every guide in the tents names a price for any road but that one.
- **The envoy's guard.** An Amradi envoy to the council wants guards who are not of any tribe, because he does not trust the salt of a tribe that is owed his Kingdom's blood.
