## Overview {#overview}

The Dunhara Desert Region is the open desert of the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]]: the sand seas, mesas and oases south and east of [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]] that the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] range and no king holds, and the country through which every caravan between the Vilérian shore and [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] must hire its way. A guide leading his first foreigners into it stops at the last well of the Amradi road, fills every skin twice, and tells them that from here on, the water belongs to someone. No line divides the desert on any map. What divides it is custom: the circuits each tribe rides through the year, and the oases each holds by right of long use.

## The Land {#the-land}

Great sand seas cover most of the country, dunes up to fifty meters high that walk slowly north on the prevailing wind, broken by rocky mesas, lone volcanic plugs and the pale floors of dry lakes. The oases are the fixed points of everything. Some are lush and permanent, like the hollow of palms at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]] or the spring at the [[place-oasistvrzvd|Oasis of Tevrazavad]] that has never failed; others fill only in a wet season, or lie at the end of a wadi that floods once in ten years. Every route across the desert is a chain of them, and a guide's worth is the number of links he knows.

The climate kills. A summer day can finish a man without water in hours, a winter night falls far below freezing, and a windstorm can bury a camp or strip the paint from a merchant's chests in minutes. The deep south sees almost no rain at all, and only the northern and western margins get enough winter rain to green the grazing for a month or two.

## The Tribes {#peoples}

The desert's people are the Dunhari of the tribes, living by lineage, household and the seasonal circuit. A **sheikh** leads each tribe by birth, by standing and by the consent of the elders, and the code of salt, feud and reputation binds them all. The poets keep the law: genealogy, history, precedent and the terms of every treaty are carried in verse by the tribal poets, and written records count for little. [[lore-dunhariclt|Dunhari]] gives the customs in full.

## Power {#who-holds-it}

No one rules the open desert. Perhaps a dozen great confederations and many smaller tribes share it, each with its sheikh, its warriors, its poets and its circuit, and they join against an outside threat and quarrel the rest of the time. Their common council meets at Ghavulavad, their [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|Warrior's Circle]] at the [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]], and the practitioners of the [[affiliation-strmcltsbzhrms|Storm Cults of Bazhrámish]] travel with every camp of consequence. The balance has held for centuries.

## The Grain Compact and the Southern Circuit {#present}

At every well this year the talk begins with the Kingdom. The tribes sell Amradad camels, sheep and goats and ride escort for its caravans, and take its grain and iron in return. The trade is cautious on both sides, and blood-debts from old raids keep some tribes out of it altogether. Every compact the Kingdom offers goes before the common council at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]], and the council argues each one from the beginning, because a tribe that is owed Amradi blood does not ride under a compact another tribe has sworn.

Then it turns south, to the circuit beyond the long wells, the most dangerous country in the desert. [The Unrecited]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} strike caravans and leave survivors who cannot say who they were, and guides give [[place-vzhmvd|the weighing-place at Vazhmavad]], where the raiders keep their camp, a wide berth. [[place-mezhvadez|The Dry Stair]] stands open in the dunes this spring, and the guides who know its dune are offered more than they can refuse. Herders at the edge of the [Pale Floor]{.name data-type="place/region"}, below [[place-twrlghtnmrkd|the Tower of the Lightning-Marked]], say a voice in its storm draws their own people out across the flat. None of it is the common council's business by custom. The [Old Promise]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} bars a Dunhari blade against a Dunhari while an outside enemy walks the land, and whether it lets the Warrior's Circle ride against any of this is the open question, so the southern tribes carry all of it to the Stone of Ranaz at the next season-turn.

## Roads and Trade {#roads-and-trade}

The desert lives on its herds and its roads. Camels, sheep and goats give milk, meat, wool and hides, the oases give dates, and grain comes in on the caravans, bought with herds sold north into Amradad and with the fees of the road. Three roads leave the desert by its edges. The Amradi road runs north-west into the Kingdom's country. The road to [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]] runs south-west, about twenty days, to the desert-march of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]]. [[place-marchroad|The March Road]] runs south-east, about thirty days, round the western end of the Grazian wall into Vedyara, and it carries grain, salt, horses, hides and dates in bulk for eight months of the year. Escort passes from tribe to tribe as a caravan crosses each territory, and each tribe takes its fee at the handover. The Lightning-Marked take a toll in salt at the Pale Floor, and the salt goes north with everything else.

## Dangers {#dangers}

Thirst is the first danger and the commonest killer, and the guides exist because of it. After thirst come people. The [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] of the Khazrin steppe raid the northern wells, and the Kinless, cast out by their own kin and owed nothing by anyone, take what they can from caravans that travel without a guide's protection. In the south the dead of the old vaults walk against the pastures in some years, and the Pale Floor kills caravans that cross it on the wrong day.
