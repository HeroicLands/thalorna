## Overview {#overview}

The Dunhara Region is the great southern desert of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] and the country of the Dunhari, one people living two ways: the settled [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] in the northwest, from the Vilérian shore inland, and the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] of the open desert, who range everything else and answer to no king. Sand seas, rocky mesas, dry riverbeds and hidden oases run from the eastern shore of the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]], opposite the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]], to the foothills below the western end of [[place-graznmntns|the Grazian wall]], where the desert gives way to the marches of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]].

About three million people live in it. Some two million are Amradi, subjects of the Shebarader, and most of them live in the oasis villages, along the wadis and in the herders' camps of the dry pasture around its towns and two cities: [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]], the seat of the court, and [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]], the toll fortress at the crossroads. About a million belong to the tribes.

The two halves share one blood, one faith and one tongue, and they live off each other. The caravan roads of the interior cross the tribes' country, pay their toll at Tahrodan and come down to the sea at [[place-valmakhend|Valmakhend]], and the Kingdom buys the tribes' herds and escorts with grain and iron. The Shebarader claims authority over every Dunhari people, and the tribes have never granted it. A sovereign kingdom beside independent tribes is the arrangement both live by, and it has held since the Kingdom was founded about 220 AF.

## Geography {#the-land}

The desert runs south and east from the dry scrublands behind the Vilérian shore into country that will kill the unprepared. Great sand seas fill most of it, dunes fifty meters high in places, walking slowly north on the prevailing wind, broken by rocky mesas, lone volcanic plugs and the pale floors of dry lakes. The oases are the fixed points of the whole country: some lush and permanent, some that fill only in a wet season, some at the end of long wadis that flood once a decade. Every caravan route is a chain of them.

The climate is brutal. A summer day can kill a man without water in a few hours, a winter night drops far below freezing, and a windstorm can bury a camp or strip the paint from a merchant's chests in minutes. The deep south sees almost no rain at all. The northwestern margin along the sea gets a thin winter rain, enough for a narrow belt of seasonal grass and the herds that graze it.

The region holds two countries. [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]], in the northwestern corner along the sea, is the country of walled cities, irrigated oases and the caravan roads between them. The [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]] is everything else: the open desert of the tribes, bounded by nothing but the customary circuits of the confederations and the oases each holds by right.

## Peoples {#peoples}

The Dunhari live by lineage, household and the seasonal circuit, the yearly round of wells and grazing each tribe has walked for generations. A tribe is led by a **sheikh** whose authority rests on three things together—his birth, his personal standing and the consent of the elders—and who is a man with a title once he loses any of them. Below him stand the warriors, whose standing comes from raid and caravan escort; the poet-scholars, who keep the law and the genealogies; and the herders, craftsmen, traders and camp-followers who are most of the tribe.

Three customs hold the desert together. **Salt**: a tribe must protect a stranger who has eaten its salt, against its own kin if need be and at the cost of war. **The feud**: a blood-feud may last generations, and every way of ending one—restitution in stock, intermarriage, a shared pilgrimage, a sheikh's formal apology to a sheikh—is set down precisely. **Reputation**: a shamed warrior keeps his life and loses everything that makes it worth having, and a shamed tribe may need decades before its sheikh can meet another's eye.

The poets are the Dunhara's great achievement. Genealogy, history, legal precedent and the terms of every treaty with other tribes and distant empires are kept in verse and handed down by the tribal poets, who are the keepers of law and identity. A poet can recite for days without repeating himself, and composing verse is a mark of nobility. Written records exist and count for little. [[lore-dunhariclt|Dunhari]] sets out the customs in full.

The settled people of Amradad are of Dunhari stock and have grown apart from their cousins. The tribes admire the wealth of the cities and despise the softness of a settled life, and the Amradi return both opinions. [[lore-dunhariclt#the-amradi|Dunhari]] sets out how the Amradi differ from the tribes.

A few [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]] halls are rumored in the mountain margins, and now and then a [[lore-flksenali|Senali traveler]] is reported. Nobody has counted either, and the stories about both are told around fires, not entered in any register.

## Faith

The Dunhari keep the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]], the faith of fire and light they share with the Khazrin tribes to the north and the Taruzeli to the west. [[lore-ahurdaendty|Ahurdaen]], held to be the god of order and harmony, is supreme; [[lore-bazhramisdty|Bazhrámish]], god of storms and protection, is the patron of warriors and caravan guards; [[lore-khorsadradty|Khórsadra]], god of knowledge and the eternal light, is revered by navigators and scholars; and [[lore-zarvanyadty|Zárvanya]], the giver of dreams and visions, is followed by the mystics who go alone into the deep desert to meet the divine.

The open desert has no temples and no temple priesthood; the great fire-temples and their priestly scholars belong to the cities of Amradad. Among the tribes, religious life is carried by people who travel with the camps: the practitioners of the [[affiliation-strmcltsbzhrms|Storm Cults of Bazhrámish]], who read the weather, call the rain and bless the camps, and the **desert mystics**, ascetics who fast and keep vigil in the hardest places the desert has. A mystic is no priest and holds no office; his authority is what he has survived. One who comes back from the deep desert with visions that prove true is housed, followed and obeyed for the rest of his life.

The holy places are features of the land: certain oases, rock formations, mesa-tops and caves no one has found the end of. Pilgrimage to them is central to the faith, and a Dunhari who has completed the whole round carries a prestige no other deed can earn. The greatest of them is the [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]], the hill-shrine where three tribal territories meet and the storm-cult elders gather at the season-turns.

## Power {#who-holds-it}

The desert has no single ruler. Perhaps a dozen great confederations and many smaller tribes share it, each with its sheikh, its warriors, its poets and its circuit. They join against an outside threat and quarrel among themselves the rest of the time, and the balance has held for centuries. The [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] describes the confederations and the councils where they meet.

The [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] is the only settled realm of any size. It is a true kingdom, with a hereditary Shebarader, a standing army and a court modeled on the old cities of the Ashaian faith, and it holds the northwestern corner from the Vilérian shore inland: its walled cities, their oases and the roads between them. Its wealth is out of all proportion to its land, and its influence runs past its borders through trade and through marriages with the sheikhs' houses.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] has no footing here. The tribes call it a Vilérian institution with no standing in their country, and the Arcane Order gave up trying to found chapters long ago. Magic in the Dunhara comes through faith and blood: the mystics, the poets held to be blessed, the storm-cult practitioners and the sheikhs' houses that claim a gift running in the line.

## Current Situation {#present}

The land's quarrels run along its seams. In Shebarakhend, four [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi]] princely houses keep their courts in exile on the Shebarader's courtesy, outspend the court's great houses and press the throne for privileges the great houses want refused. The Shebarader needs both sides: the great houses run his realm, and the Tellumi merchant-houses carry a large share of the trade his treasury taxes.

Between the Kingdom and the tribes the question is the compact. Amradi envoys offer grain and iron for escort on the roads into the Kingdom, and the common council of the tribes at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]] argues the offer at every sitting and binds nobody. The northern circuits, who eat Amradi bread every winter, want it sworn, and the tribes whose Keepers of the Feud carry a tally of blood the Amradi owe refuse to sit at a fire with the envoys. A treaty that holds at one well can be broken at the next, and [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]], where the herds come in, feels a broken one first.

The tribes' own authorities are stalled. The storm cults' Council of Elders is deadlocked, because [the Wind-Readers]{.name data-type="affiliation"} and [the Lightning-Marked]{.name data-type="affiliation"} each withhold assent from what the other proposes, over the salt toll on the [Pale Floor]{.name data-type="place/feature"} and the readings the Lightning-Marked keep to themselves. No ruling passes, and the summons the northern chieftains want for a [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] storm-shaman preaching in their camps waits with the rest. In the south, [The Unrecited]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} strike the caravans of the southern circuit, and herders at the edge of the Pale Floor say a voice in the storm draws their own people out across the flat. [[place-mezhvadez|The Dry Stair]] stands open in the dunes, and its grave-goods go to the buyers of Qarvelakhend and west through the caravans of [[affiliation-theashroad|the Ash Road]] to [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]]. The Road's boss, who rules it alone with the merchants of Kudumel as advisers, wants a guide who has been down the stair. Whether the [Old Promise]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} lets the [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|Warrior's Circle]] ride against any of it is the open question, and the southern tribes carry it to the Stone of Ranaz at the next season-turn.

## Trade {#roads-and-trade}

The Dunhari tribes hold the southern caravan routes that join [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] and the Vilérian Sea to Vedyara and, through further hands, to the far east. Spice, silk, jewels, fine cloth, medicinal herbs and the rare goods of the south travel west; iron, salt, wine, worked metal and the wares of the settled kingdoms travel east. A caravan carries the stock of several tribes under one escort, and responsibility for it passes from tribe to tribe as it crosses their lands, a system that works because the honor code makes the alternative unthinkable.

Amradad taxes the caravans that pass through its cities and spends the revenue on its army and its court. The sheikhs tax the caravans that cross their country too, and call it a hospitality gift; the merchants call it something else. A powerful sheikh's year of gifts can make him as rich as a minor Aureldian prince.

## Neighbors

**West** across the strait from the Amradi shore lie the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]]. The ties are old, close and commercial: the Taruzeli speak a sister tongue to Dunhari and keep the same faith, and the crossing from the Amradi coast to the Taruzelian ports is the main road between the desert and the Vilérian Sea.

**Northwest**, beyond the Kingdom's border, the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]]'s road through its [[place-southrnmrch|Southern March]] carries the caravan trade north into [[place-heladrgn|Hellád]].

**North** lies the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin Desert]]. The two deserts are cousins, the frontier between them is long and vague, and many caravan routes begin in one and end in the other with no clear sense of crossing anything.

**Southeast** lies Vedyara, reached by the march road that runs round the end of the Grazian wall—the only way into the subcontinent a loaded camel can take. The two peoples differ more than the Dunhari and the Taruzeli or the Khazrin do, and centuries of trade have taught them respect and a working understanding.

**Southwest** lies [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]], and above all the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], met by land across [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]], the desert march the empire garrisons and the western clans contest. Contact is ancient and constant: tolls, quarrels over wells and skirmishes at the march, and a sea trade carried by Taruzelian and Khelathi merchants on the Vilérian Sea.
