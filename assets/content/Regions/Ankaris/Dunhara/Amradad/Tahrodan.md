## Overview {#overview}

Tahrodan is the toll fortress at the crossroads of the caravan roads and the largest city of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], twenty thousand people inside walls that every caravan from the desert interior must pass on its way down to the port of [[place-valmakhend|Valmakhend]] and the merchant towns of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] beyond the strait. The roads from [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria]], from [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] and from the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] meet under its walls, and only one road leaves them for the sea. The city stands inland, on the road, and has no harbor of its own. Its oasis could feed a town of a quarter its size; the rest of its bread is bought with the tolls taken at its gates, which are one of the pillars of the Shebarader's treasury.

## History {#history}

The crossroads had an oasis and a caravanserai before it had a fortress. The Kingdom's first walls faced the desert edge and the shore, and in 315 [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]] came across the desert from the Vedyaran march and reached them from the east, the side nobody had built them to face. The host broke up at the walls when its orghun died. The Shebarader of the time raised the citadel over the meeting of the roads and walled the town around it, every wall facing a road, and set a toll-house inside its gate ([[lore-amradfndng|The Founding of Amradad]]).

The toll made the town. About 325 the [[affiliation-bhumipal|Bhumipal]] swore their first treaty with the Kingdom and began the forts of [[place-marchroad|the March Road]] ([[lore-bhmplraise|the Raising of Bhumipal]]), and the wagons of that road have come to Tahrodan since. The town grew into the largest city of the realm on the silver taken at its gate and on the grain that silver buys, and the fire-temples gathered estates in the oases around it whose endowments let a load under a temple's seal pass the gate untaxed.

## Current Situation {#present}

A temple's seal is worth more in Tahrodan than anywhere else in the realm. The fire-temples hold estates in the oases around Tahrodan, and by the right of their endowments what comes in under a temple's seal pays no toll. A priest will lend his seal to a bale of silk for a share of its price, the caravan-masters know which priests, and the toll-house loses more to sealed loads than to smugglers. The governor wants the seals opened and checked at the gate. The priests call it sacrilege to search an offering, and they hold the rites the governor must be seen to keep.

The tribes are the other edge. Tribal escorts bring the desert caravans to a well a day out, where the city's riders take them over, and the handover runs on old agreements and a fee paid in grain and iron. One tribe holds a blood-debt against a family in the garrison and refuses to hand over to any troop with that family's men in it, and the governor must choose between moving the men and moving the well.

## The Holder {#holder}

A governor, a _tagemader_, holds Tahrodan for the Shebarader with its garrison and its revenue, and he holds the richest governorship in the realm on the same terms as every other: by the Shebarader's grant, revocable at a word. He plays the great powers of the region against one another as carefully as the Shebarader does, because the city's wealth depends entirely on the road staying open, and he plays the temples and the toll-house against each other for the same reason.

## Garrison {#garrison}

The garrison is mounted. Its riders keep the roads clear of raiders for a day's ride in every direction, meet the tribal escorts at the handover wells and ride the caravans down toward the coast. The citadel holds the city's granaries, the toll silver and the barracks, and its walls face every road. A foot watch keeps the gates and the lower quarter at night, and it does the work nobody wants, such as standing guard while the priests burn out a dung-yard.

## Government, Faith and Trade {#government-faith-trade}

Every load is weighed, counted and sealed at the gate, and the clerks of the Yorvabun, the keeper of the realm's registers, keep the toll ledgers and send the audit to [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]]. Much of the toll silver never leaves the city. The governor buys grain with it from the wagons of [[place-marchroad|the March Road]], which bring Vedyaran grain and salt by the wagonload for eight months of the year, and from Amradi caravans coming up from the wadi towns, and the citadel granaries hold enough for a siege. The near oases send dates, vegetables and fodder, and the temples' estates send the rest of the city's fruit. What Tahrodan sends out is mostly what it takes: silver to the treasury, a safe road to the coast, and grain and iron to the tribes in payment for their escorts.

Its bazaars are as crowded and various as those of [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]] and [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]] across the strait, and less sociable. Tahrodan is a city goods pass through. A merchant comes in, pays his toll, sells or trades on, and leaves, and the market runs on brisk efficiency and not on long afternoons of bargaining over tea.

The fire-temples of the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian gods]] fill the city's central district, and their priests are a power in Tahrodan: they hold great estates in the surrounding oases and the spiritual authority that goes with the temples. The governor must be seen to keep the rites and honor the priests, and the priests need the governor's soldiers and his tolls, so neither can rule without the other, and no decision of weight is made in Tahrodan until both have been heard.

## Within the Walls {#within-the-walls}

The citadel stands over the meeting of the roads, and the governor's palace and the toll-house stand inside its gate. Below it the temple district holds the fire-temples and the priests' houses, and around that runs the bazaar, a street of covered halls between the two main gates. The lower quarter, by the eastern gate where the desert caravans come in, is caravanserais, stables, tanneries and dung-yards, and a colony of [[being-thornrat|thornrats]] lives in its drains and refuse-pits. The caravanserai-keepers burn their dung daily, and the caravan-masters shake out their baggage a day out on the road.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The sealed bales.** The toll-house's clerk believes one priest has lent his temple's seal to a whole caravan, and he cannot open the bales without the priests' consent. He needs strangers to travel with the caravan to the coast and learn what was under the seal and who sold it.
- **The handover well.** A tribal escort reaches the handover well and finds no riders waiting, only the troop's horses. The tribe that keeps the blood-debt is camped a day away, the garrison wants its men back, and the escort will not go on to the city without someone to hand the caravan to.
