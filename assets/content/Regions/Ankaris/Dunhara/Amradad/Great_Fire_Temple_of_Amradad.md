## Overview {#overview}

The Great Fire-Temple of Amradad is the temple of [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Ahurdaen]] in [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]], and the senior house of the whole [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian]] priesthood. Its High Shekhul is the formal head of every Ashaian priest from the Vilérian shore to the Khazrin, the Tellumi included, and in the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] his word on a matter of state religion or of portent weighs as much as the Grand Vashkader's. Its flame is never let out, and its glow over the palms is the first thing a caravan sees of the city at night.

A party comes here for one of three things: an omen read by the priests who read for the throne, a letter of standing from the senior house of the faith, or an audience with the man who convenes the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]].

## Founding {#founding}

The temple stands on the ground of the old fire-shrine of the oasis, which was there before any wall. Its keepers' records claim three thousand years of unbroken flame. When the confederation that founded the Kingdom settled at the springs, it built its war-leader's court on the high ground beside the shrine, and the shrine grew into the temple precinct below the palace, with the Great Fire-Temple at its heart and the lesser temples of the Twelve round it. The flame on the high altar is the shrine's flame, and the temple's fire-lineage begins with it rather than with a lamp carried from another fire.

## Current Situation {#present}

The temple is asking questions abroad. An envoy of its priests has crossed the strait to [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] to read the fire-lineage record of the [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]], the first such visit anyone there remembers, and in Shebarakhend the priests say only that the senior house reads the records it is entitled to read.

At home, the temple is the richest prize in the quarrel between the court's great houses and the Tellumi exile courts. Both endow it, the exile courts more lavishly, and every endowment buys a place nearer the altar in the processions and a hearing in the High Shekhul's rooms. The priests of [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]], whose temple seals the governor there wants opened at the gate, look to the Great Fire-Temple to defend them, and the High Shekhul has said nothing in public. The warbands of the [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] on the Kingdom's northern border are preached against at every rite as servants of a fire that only destroys.

## Who Rules It {#who-rules-it}

The High Shekhul, [Aravdán the Stargazer]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds the temple and answers for its flame. He is about sixty, an astronomer of reputation across the continent, and for three decades he has held the Tellumi exiles together as convenor of their Council. The Tellumi priesthood, the Shekhulukhem, answers to him, and the court treats him as a minister as much as a priest.

Under him the senior Delkoshekhs each keep a domain: the Delkoshekh of Astronomy keeps the record of the sky, the Delkoshekh of Law interprets the divine justice beneath the Shebarader's law, and the Delkoshekh of Cosmology teaches the priesthood and the educated laity. The Keeper of the Fire-Lineage holds the record of the flame's descent, on which the temple's standing rests. Roqeshekhs feed the flame in watches through the day and night.

## Lands and Income {#lands-and-income}

The temple lives on pious endowments: estates in the oases, water-shares, shops along the market and gifts of coin, each given by deed and administered through the court of the city's _vazhmader_. The great houses endow it to show their standing, and the exile courts endow it to buy theirs. Oil for its lamps comes from [[place-venzelakhend|Venzelakhend]], charcoal for its braziers on donkeys from the scrub hills, and the offerings of the faithful fill the rest.

## Visiting the House {#visiting}

Every prayer in the city begins with the kindling of a fire, and every household keeps a flame lit from a temple's. The outer courts are open to anyone who brings an offering for the fire. Caravan-masters spend the night before a long road there in prayer, and travelers with an omen to ask leave a gift with the Delkoshekhs and come back for the reading. The high altar's house is for the priests alone. The great days are the spring equinox, when the Festival of New Order renews every flame in the city from the high altar, and the opening of the Council's session, when the Tellumi princes walk to the temple in procession.

## The Grounds {#the-grounds}

The temple stands at the heart of the precinct, below the palace and above the garden quarter. Its fire-house is the oldest building in the city, low and windowless under a dome, with the high altar of [[affiliation-ataros|Ataros]] within, as in every fire-temple. Around it the newer halls hold the shrine of Ahurdaen, the rooms of the High Shekhul and the archive of the fire-lineage. On the roofs and terraces above stand the temple's observatories, the oldest and most elaborate in [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], where the Delkoshekhs map the planets and stars for signs of order or disorder. The [[place-shbracadmies|Academies of Shebarakhend]] crowd the edges of the precinct.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The envoy's errand.** The temple's envoy to Kudumel has written home that the Shekhul there wants to know who sent for him. Someone discreet is wanted to carry the next letter across the strait, and to watch the envoy.
- **An omen for a price.** A great house wants the Delkoshekh of Astronomy to read an unfavorable sky for a Tellumi court's marriage, and the Delkoshekh's own debts are the lever.
- **The seals of Tahrodan.** The governor of Tahrodan and the priests there both send pleaders to the High Shekhul, and each side wants proof of what the other's men carry under a temple's seal.
