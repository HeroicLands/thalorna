## Overview {#overview}

Shebarakhend is the oasis city of the Shebarader's court and the seat of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]]. It is not the largest city of [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad]], with fifteen thousand people to [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]]'s twenty, and it has no need to be: the Shebarader, his Grand Vashkader, the divans and the households of the great families live here, and the business of the whole realm comes through its gates. Its name means the seat of the court, and the court is what it lives by. It makes little for sale beyond its books and the Tellumi houses' wares, and it eats far more than its oasis grows; the realm's revenue pays for the difference.

The city is a place of gardens behind walls, of libraries, academies and fire-temples whose scholars set the standard of learning within the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]] for the desert, and of astrologers, since no great house of the court marries, travels or quarrels without consulting one. Four princely houses of the [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi]] keep their courts in exile here, and the money they spend is the city's loudest argument.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The road from Tahrodan comes in through the oasis villages, between palm groves planted in three tiers: dates above, apricots and pomegranates below them, beans and onions on the ground. Water runs beside the road in stone channels, and every channel has an owner. The first sight of the city is its wall over the palms, and at night the glow of the [[place-grtfiretmpl|Great Fire-Temple of Amradad]], whose flame is never let out.

A caravan pays its toll at Tahrodan and none here, but the market inspector's men stand at the caravan gate, read the toll-seal, weigh what is to be sold inside and take the market due. Anyone with business at court is sent on to the Gholvabun's clerks, who keep a list of petitioners that grows faster than it shrinks.

## Politics {#present}

Shebarakhend's politics are fought over houses, gardens and endowments, between the court's great houses and the exile courts. A great house of Amradad is a family whose men hold the great offices, and its standing is the Shebarader's grant: it comes with an appointment and goes with a word. The Tellumi princes hold their blood by genealogies that run back to the founding of their lines, and they have the trade of the strait and the donations of the Tellumi abroad to spend. When a great officer falls from favor his household sells its house and garden inside the walls, and the buyer is often a Tellumi court. The exile houses endow the academies and the temples more richly than the great houses can, lure the great houses' astrologers into their own service, and petition the Shebarader through every door in the palace for three privileges: their own Delkoshekhs to judge Tellumi cases at law, their own wardens on the gate of their quarter, and places for their princes in the order of the court beside his great officers.

The great houses answer in the divans, where they hold the offices the petitions pass through. The Shebarader grants a little and refuses most, because he needs the great houses to govern and the Tellumi trade to pay for it. The yearly session of the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]], on ground the Kingdom lends it, fills the city with the princes' parties and sharpens every quarrel for a month.

## Order and Law {#order-and-law}

Even the capital has a _tagemader_, who holds the city's garrison and revenue apart from the palace, and whose men keep the gates and the streets. The court guard keeps the palace and answers to the Shebarader alone. The city's _vazhmader_ judges by law and custom and administers the pious endowments of the temples and academies, so every rich gift to an academy passes through his court, and both sides court him. The _nuzgader_ inspects markets, weights and public morals, and is as resented here as in any town of the realm. Inside the Tellumi quarter the houses' Delkoshekhs arbitrate disputes among their own people by the codes of the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council]], but only where both parties consent; their rulings have no standing at law, and any Tellumi who refuses one takes his case to the vazhmader.

## What the City Lives By {#what-the-city-lives-by}

The oasis and the ring of villages around it give the city its dates, fruit, vegetables and fodder, and its water decides everything else; the Keeper of the Wells sits here, and his clerks know the hour of every channel. Bread comes from outside. The threshing floors of [[place-tazhinakhend|Tazhinakhend]] and the fields of [[place-sorvadakhend|Sorvadakhend]] send grain to the court's granaries, [[place-venzelakhend|Venzelakhend]] sends oil, and meat comes on the hoof from the herds the tribes sell at [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]]. Charcoal for the kitchens and the temple braziers comes in on donkeys from the scrub hills, where charcoal-burners such as [[being-gulrnhgzvn|Gulranah Gazvan]] work.

The city sends out what a court makes: appointments, judgments, the coin of the realm's revenue, and its learning. The academies copy books for the whole Ashaian world, the Tellumi houses sell instruments and date wine across the strait, and the astrologers of Shebarakhend take retainers far beyond the walls: the elders of Sorvadakhend keep [[being-fakhrdtmrn|Fakhrad Tamaran]] on retainer to read the seasons for their fields.

## Quarters {#quarters}

**The palace** stands on the highest ground of the oasis, with the courts of the divans around it, and its gardens are the largest in the city. **The temple precinct** below it holds the Great Fire-Temple and the lesser temples of the Twelve, and the [[place-shbracadmies|academies]] and libraries crowd its edges, where the priestly scholars teach theology, astronomy, mathematics and medicine. **The garden quarter** holds the walled houses of the great families, each with its water-share and its astrologer. **The Tellumi quarter** holds the four exile courts, each a household of a few hundred souls with its own genealogies, priests and ceremony; the court of the [[affiliation-hseshirvan|House of Shirvan]] keeps [[miscgear-shirvantab|the First Tablet of Shirvan]] there. **The market** runs along the inside of the caravan gate, and it sells to the court rather than to caravans.

## Faith and Festivals {#faith-and-festivals}

The [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]] is the established worship of the Kingdom, and its High Shekhul at the Great Fire-Temple, [Aravdán the Stargazer]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is as much a minister as a priest. He is an astronomer of wide reputation, he convenes the Council of the Princes, and both the great houses and the exile courts endow his temple. Every prayer in the city begins with the kindling of a fire, and every household keeps a flame lit from a temple's. The city's great occasions are the court's: an audience, a marriage timed by the astrologers, and the Council's session, when the Tellumi princes walk in procession to the Great Fire-Temple.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

The caravanserais inside the caravan gate take anyone who pays, and fill when the Council sits. A scholar with a letter to an academy may have a guest cell in its cloister. A party in the service of an exile house lodges in that house's court and is counted, by everyone watching, as its clients.

## History {#history}

Shebarakhend is where the Kingdom began, on ground far older than the Kingdom. The oasis had a settlement and a fire-shrine whose keepers' records claim three thousand years of unbroken flame. The confederation that founded Amradad about 220 AF wintered here before it settled, and when it stayed it walled the springs, cut the stone channels and built its war-leader's court on the highest ground beside the shrine, where the palace stands; the city took its name from that court ([[lore-amradfndng|The Founding of Amradad]]). The shrine grew into the temple precinct below the palace; the academies and the garden houses of the great families grew around it over the following centuries, and the court poets who set the Amradi registers worked here.

The Tellumi quarter is the city's newest. Between about 620 and 629 the four princely houses whose cities fell in the Breaking came here one after another, the House of Shirvan last, with the surviving tablets of its archive, and the Shebarader gave them ground in the city as guests of the throne ([[lore-exilecoming|The Exile Houses Come to Amradad]]). About 660 the High Shekhul of the Great Fire-Temple took up the convening of their Council, and its yearly session has filled the city since. Whenever a great officer fell from favor, a Tellumi court was ready to buy his house and garden, and the quarrel between the exile courts and the great houses grew out of those sales.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The charts that changed hands.** A great house's astrologer has gone over to one of the exile courts and taken the house's charts: every marriage, journey and quarrel it has timed for twenty years. The exile court denies having them, and the house cannot be seen to ask.
- **The garden at the vazhmader's court.** A disgraced officer's house and garden are to be sold through the vazhmader's court, and a Tellumi court means to buy them. A great house wants a flaw found in the deeds, the water-shares or the endowments, anything that stops the sale in the vazhmader's court.
