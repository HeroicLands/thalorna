## Overview

The Amradi say that their ancestors were the only Dunhari clever enough to stop walking. The **Kingdom of Amradad** is the great exception to the Dunhara's tribal patchwork: a settled, walled and comparatively rich kingdom built around a cluster of great oases in the northwestern Dunhara, between the eastern shore of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] and the open desert. Where the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] move with their herds and answer to no one above their sheikhs, Amradad has cities, walls, irrigation, a standing army, and a court as refined as any on the Vylarian coast. An outsider remembers it for the fire-temples of its cities and for the caravan tolls that pay for them.

## Character

Amradad was founded generations ago, when a powerful tribal confederation settled for good around the richest oases in the desert and began to build in stone. The kingdom it made holds the most valuable crossroads of the overland trade: the point where the roads from [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]], [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] meet on the coast facing the islands of [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Tarûzel]]. That crossroads has made the Shêbaradêr enormously rich and far more important than his thinly peopled land would suggest.

The court at [[place-shebarakhend|Shêbarakhend]] is a seat of learning and patronage. Scholars of theology, astronomy, mathematics and medicine gather in its libraries and academies, and every great house keeps a court astrologer to time its marriages, journeys and quarrels. The [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] faith finds its grandest expression here. The fire-temples of Amradad are the wonders of the desert, and the debates of their priestly scholars set the standard of Āsháian orthodoxy for the tribes and the Tarûzeli alike, whether or not either listens.

## Governance

The Shêbaradêr rules in his own right. His orders pass through the **Grand Vashkadêr**, who heads the administration, to the vashkadêrs of the treasury, the army, the post and the waters, and from them to a governor (_tâgemadêr_) in every city and oasis. A judge (_vazhmadêr_) sits in each city, ruling by law and custom and administering the pious endowments of the temples; a market inspector (_nûzgadêr_) checks weights and measures and public morals, and is the most resented man in any city. The **Keeper of the Wells** rules on the oases' water rights, and his rulings decide which caravans may cross. The **Master of the Post** keeps the courier roads, and with them most of what the Shêbaradêr knows.

The Shêbaradêr's power is real and it has limits. The priests of the fire-temples hold great landed wealth and spiritual authority, and in every city the governor and the high priest each need the other. The great caravan-masters are chartered by the Shêbaradêr and answer to him for their dues, and they know to the coin how much of his treasury they fill.

## Relations

The Shêbaradêr claims authority over every Dunhari people, and the tribes find the claim hilarious. In practice the two live together through trade agreements, marriages between the court and the sheikhs' houses, and carefully negotiated arrangements at the edge of the sown. The standing army is strong enough to hold the oases and too slow to catch anyone in the open desert, where the nomads go wherever they like.

Across the strait, relations with the Confederation of Tarûzelian States are warm. Relations with the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] have long been hostile, and when Tarûzel, a Vylarian colony, rose against the empire some twelve years ago, Amradad was among those who helped it break free.

## Commerce and Currency

The Kingdom uses [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian coin]]—elthêzâs, ankhrâ and bit—in its port cities and its administrative heart, where it is the main money of trade. The Vylarian paper script does not work here, since no chapter of the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]] operates in the Kingdom. Out along the desert roads, trade turns to [[lore-bartercnmy|barter]] and [[lore-kinhalcrdt|kin-credit]], as everywhere in the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]]. Tarûzelian coin from the secession trade is accepted in the principal trading cities, through changers who deal with the Tarûzelian treasury, the Merâzûnî.
