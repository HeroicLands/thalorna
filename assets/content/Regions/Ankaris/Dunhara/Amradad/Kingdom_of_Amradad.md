## Overview {#overview}

The Amradi say that their ancestors were the only Dunhari clever enough to stop walking. The Kingdom of Amradad is the great exception to the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara Region]]'s tribal patchwork: a settled, walled and comparatively rich kingdom built around a cluster of great oases in the northwestern Dunhara, between the eastern shore of the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]] and the open desert. Where the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]] move with their herds and answer to no one above their sheikhs, Amradad has cities, walls, irrigation, a standing army, and a court as refined as any on the Vilérian coast. An outsider remembers it for the fire-temples of its cities and for the caravan tolls that pay for them.

## Character

Amradad was founded about 220 AF, when a powerful tribal confederation settled for good around the richest oases in the desert and began to build in stone ([[lore-amradfndng|The Founding of Amradad]]). The kingdom it made holds the most valuable crossroads of the overland trade: the point where the roads from the [[place-byzariargn|Bizaria Region]], the [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]] meet at [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]] before going down to the coast facing the islands of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]]. That crossroads has made the Shebarader enormously rich and far more important than his thinly peopled land would suggest.

The court at [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]] is a seat of learning and patronage. Scholars of theology, astronomy, mathematics and medicine gather in its libraries and academies, and every great house keeps a court astrologer to time its marriages, journeys and quarrels. The [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian faith]] finds its grandest expression here. The fire-temples of Amradad are the wonders of the desert, and the debates of their priestly scholars set the standard of Ashaian orthodoxy for the tribes and the Taruzeli alike, whether or not either listens.

## Governance {#how-it-is-ruled}

The Shebarader rules in his own right. His orders pass through the **Grand Vashkader**, who heads the administration, to the vashkaders of the treasury, the army, the post and the waters, and from them to a governor (_tagemader_) in every city and oasis. A judge (_vazhmader_) sits in each city, ruling by law and custom and administering the pious endowments of the temples; a market inspector (_nuzgader_) checks weights and measures and public morals, and is the most resented man in any city. The **Keeper of the Wells** rules on the oases' water rights, and his rulings decide which caravans may cross. The **Master of the Post** keeps the courier roads, and with them most of what the Shebarader knows.

The Shebarader's power is real and it has limits. The priests of the fire-temples hold great landed wealth and spiritual authority, and in every city the governor and the high priest each need the other. The great caravan-masters are chartered by the Shebarader and answer to him for their dues, and they know to the coin how much of his treasury they fill.

## Current Situation {#present}

Four [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi]] princely houses keep their courts in exile at [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]] on the Shebarader's courtesy, and they pay for them out of the Tellumi trade across the strait and the donations of Tellumi working abroad. They press the throne for three privileges: that their own Delkoshekhs judge Tellumi cases at law, that their own wardens keep the gate of their quarter, and that their princes stand in the order of the court beside the Shebarader's great officers. The court's great houses resent being outspent in their own capital. Their standing is the Shebarader's grant, held by appointment and lost by a word, and they watch princes who rule nothing out-build them, out-endow them at the temples and hire away their astrologers. The Shebarader needs both sides. The great houses run his realm, and the Tellumi merchant-houses carry a large share of the trade his treasury taxes.

On the desert edge, trade with the tribes goes on beside the grudges. The tribes sell herds and escort caravans in exchange for Amradi grain and iron, blood-debts from past raids keep some tribes hostile, and every compact the Shebarader's envoys bring goes before the common council of the tribes at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]], where each sitting argues it again and binds nobody. A treaty that holds at one well can be broken at the next by a tribe that never agreed to it.

## Arms {#arms}

The **Ghedvader of the Host** commands the standing army in the field, and the army sits where the roads and the wells need it. A mounted garrison at Tahrodan keeps the caravan roads clear for a day's ride around the crossroads. The southern host at [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]], under a _ghedvader_ of its own, watches the desert edge and pays and supplies the [[place-kinlesswatch|Kinless Watch]], a fort on the road to the [[place-twrlghtnmrkd|Tower of the Lightning-Marked]] whose garrison are men cast out by their tribes. A guard of the court holds Shebarakhend, and every tagemader keeps a garrison of his town. In the far east each walled town of [[place-threeseas|the Three Seas]] keeps a garrison of the Host under its tagemader, most of them larger than any town guard in the west, against Khazrin steppe bands, the kinless of the desert and the [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] warbands that cross the northern border. The herding clans of the Three Seas pay the Shebarader's herd tax, in animals, at the town fairs. The army holds walls and wells well and moves slowly; it does not follow raiders into the open desert.

## Commerce and Currency {#commerce}

The Kingdom uses [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian coin]]—ethla, ankhra and bit—at the port of [[place-valmakhend|Valmakhend]], in its two cities and in the towns of the caravan roads, where it is the main money of trade. The Vilérian paper script does not work here, since no chapter of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] operates in the Kingdom. Out along the desert roads, trade turns to [[lore-bartercnmy|the Barter Economy]] and [[lore-kinhalcrdt|Kin and Hall Credit]], as everywhere in the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]]. [[lore-trzlncrncy|Taruzelian coin]] from the secession trade is accepted at Valmakhend and Tahrodan, through changers who deal with the Taruzelian treasury, the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]].

## History {#history}

The Kingdom is about five centuries old. A confederation of the northern circuits settled at the great oases behind the shore about 220 AF, walled the springs and made its war-leader the first Shebarader, and every standing in the realm has been the throne's grant since ([[lore-amradfndng|The Founding of Amradad]]). In 315 [[lore-hndrdbnnrs|the Storm of the Hundred Banners]] crossed the desert and reached the Kingdom's walls from the east, and broke up there; the Shebarader of the time raised the citadel of Tahrodan at the crossroads, and its tolls have paid for the court since. About 325 the [[affiliation-bhumipal|Bhumipal]] swore their first treaty with the Kingdom for the road to Vedyara ([[lore-bhmplraise|the Raising of Bhumipal]]), and it is renewed every generation.

About 618 a disputed succession held the throne in contest for four years, while the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Tribes of Atarzád]] began taking the Tellumi cities. The Kingdom sent them no host. The new Shebarader received the four princely houses of the lost cities as guests of the throne instead, and their exile courts have stood in Shebarakhend since about 629 ([[lore-exilecoming|The Exile Houses Come to Amradad]]). In 707 and 708 the Kingdom helped [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Taruzel]] across the strait in its rising against the Vilérian Empire ([[lore-taruzelwar|the Taruzelian War of Independence]]), and the islands have been its warm neighbors since.

## Relations {#relations}

The Shebarader claims authority over every Dunhari people, and the tribes find the claim hilarious. In practice the two live together through trade agreements, marriages between the court and the sheikhs' houses, and carefully negotiated arrangements at the edge of the sown. The standing army is strong enough to hold the oases and too slow to catch anyone in the open desert, where the nomads go wherever they like.

Across the strait, relations with the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] are warm. Relations with the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] are hostile, and Amradad was among those who helped Taruzel, a Vilérian colony, break free of the empire.

The Kingdom lends the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]] the ground it meets on once a year, and the High Shekhul of [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Ahurdaen]] at the [[place-grtfiretmpl|Great Fire-Temple of Amradad]] convenes it. The exile houses hold no place on the Kingdom's ladder. A Tellumi prince is a guest of the throne, received at court by the Shebarader's courtesy and by nothing he can claim; the Tellumi of the quarter stand at law as subjects and notables like any Amradi, and the houses' retinues are tolerated while they stay discreet, since the Kingdom's law forbids them any military activity. That gap between a prince's blood and his standing in Amradad is what the houses' petitions are about.

To the north, the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]]'s [[place-southrnmrch|Southern March]] is border country of customs posts and drovers, and the two keep the peace of neighbors who trade.

The Black Flame has no footing in the Kingdom: no temple and no convert among the Amradi. Its warbands cross the northern border from the hinterlands of Bizaria and Helionis, the fire-temples condemn it, and the Kingdom's garrisons fight its warbands wherever they meet them.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The officer on two payrolls.** A vashkader of one of the great houses is sure that an officer of the Gholvabun's household takes Tellumi silver to move the exile princes up the order of the court. He needs the officer and the purse found by people the Gholvabun's household does not know by sight.
- **The treaty in the post-bag.** A courier of the Master of the Post, carrying the Shebarader's terms for a herd treaty to the tribes, is a day overdue beyond Qarvelakhend. The Post wants the bag back before the sheikhs hear the terms from someone who has read them first.
