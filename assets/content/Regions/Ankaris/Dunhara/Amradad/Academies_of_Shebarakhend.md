## Overview {#overview}

The academies of [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]] are the schools and libraries that crowd the edges of the temple precinct below the palace, round the [[place-grtfiretmpl|Great Fire-Temple of Amradad]]. Priestly scholars teach theology, astronomy, mathematics and medicine there, and their debates set the standard of learning within the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]] for the desert and the lands beyond it. Their copyists supply books to the whole Ashaian world, and after the court itself they are what the city sends out.

A party comes for what only a seat of learning sells: a physician who has read every authority, a chart cast by an astronomer the court trusts, an old text found or copied, or a scholar's opinion that a judge or a priest will accept.

## Inside {#inside}

Each academy is a walled cloister round a courtyard and a cistern, with lecture halls on the ground floor, cells for the masters and students above, and a library behind a locked door. The oldest stand nearest the Great Fire-Temple; the newest stand on ground the exile courts bought along the precinct wall. Every academy keeps a copying room where scribes work in rows through the daylight hours, and a few keep a hospital court where the physicians teach at the bedside. Lectures are given in the morning, disputations in the afternoon, and anyone of standing may sit at the back of a disputation and listen.

## Who Is There {#who-is-there}

Each academy is a pious endowment, founded by a deed that names its purpose, its revenues and the qualifications of its master. The masters are Delkoshekhs of the [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Faith of Ahurdaen]], and many teach at the Great Fire-Temple as well, under its senior Delkoshekhs of Astronomy, Law and Cosmology. The students are Roqeshekhs reading for ordination, the sons of great houses learning enough to hold office, and the young priests the Tellumi exile courts send for training, whose course to ordination runs fifteen to twenty years. The court astrologers of the great houses come from these cloisters. [[being-fakhrdtmrn|Fakhrad Tamaran]], whom the elders of [[place-sorvadakhend|Sorvadakhend]] keep on retainer to read the seasons for their fields, learned the stars from his mother, outside them.

The Tellumi exile courts also keep a school of their own, where [[being-narvunakethr|Narvuna of Kethramír]] teaches law and theology, and it stands apart from the academies.

The city's _vazhmader_ administers every endowment, so every gift to an academy passes through his court. A scholar who arrives with a letter to an academy may be given a guest cell in its cloister for the length of his business.

## The Present {#present}

The academies are the quietest field of the quarrel between the court's great houses and the [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Tellumi]] exile courts, and the most expensive. The exile courts endow new chairs and new libraries more richly than the great houses can, name their own candidates for the masterships those endowments create, and lure the great houses' astrologers into their service with the charts they have kept. The great houses answer through the _vazhmader_'s court, where a flaw in a deed can hold a gift for a year, and through the divans, where the masters' privileges are confirmed.

The masters want something the exile courts will not give. The court of the [[affiliation-hseshirvan|House of Shirvan]] keeps [[miscgear-shirvantab|the First Tablet of Shirvan]] in the Tellumi quarter and shows it to nobody outside the house, and every master of theology in the precinct has petitioned to read it. The house endows the academies with one hand and refuses them with the other, and the masters it refuses are the ones the great houses court.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The chair that was bought.** A Tellumi court has endowed a chair of astronomy and named its own candidate, and an older master contests the deed in the _vazhmader_'s court. Each side wants the other's candidate shown to be unfit, by evidence that will stand.
- **A book that should not exist.** A copyist sells a party a copy of a text the masters say was never written, and three people want to know where the copyist found the original.
- **The physician's price.** The only physician in the city who has treated a rare desert fever teaches in an academy the great houses are starving of funds, and he wants his endowment saved before he leaves the cloister.
