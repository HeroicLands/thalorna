Ravenmoor stands on [[place-eilwith|Eilwìth]]'s south-eastern coast where the open moor that gives it its name breaks
off into a deep sheltered anchorage. Thirty-five hundred people, the seat of the
[[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]], and the seat of the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Synod]].

It is not the kingdom's busiest harbor—[[place-wraymouth|Wreimouth]] handles far more tonnage—but
it is the one every ship must enter. In the hall of the Synod the last king's chair stands at the head of the room, kept and never filled ([[lore-dunavarresynod|the Mages Take Dúnavarre]]).

## Politics {#present}

Ravenmoor is the Synod's town, and its politics are the Synod's. The closed session sits in the last king's hall under the Prime Marendos, [[being-velisndorrvl|Velisande Orravel]], and the realm's officers keep their offices around the harbor: the Warden of the Ports, [[being-taliravenvl|Talira Venavel]], at the inspection quay, the Justiciar, [[being-ardelvornvl|Ardel Voranvel]], in the temple court of [[affiliation-ardavon|Árdavon]], and the Bursar, [[being-halvrasenvl|Halvra Senavel]], in the counting-house beside the hall. This season the session is divided over the war on the [[affiliation-saelvri|Seilvri]]: the [[affiliation-justbladeordr|Order of the Just Blade]] asks for a final campaign into [[place-teilvari|Teilvari]], and the Bursar asks what it will cost ([[lore-saelvricrusade|The Seilvri Crusade]]). The Sekeros, [[being-torvinasmvrn|Torvin Asmavren]], spends his evenings in the harbor taverns, where the [[affiliation-umbralcourt|Umbral Court]] is said to keep its ears.

Ravenmoor keeps one of the island's five [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]] chapterhouses, in the same close as the Synod's hall, and the Synod's Kosthemoi are taught there.

## The Compulsory Call

The Synod **requires every vessel crossing between the mainland and Eilwìth to put in at Ravenmoor
first**: inbound before it may proceed to any other port on the island, outbound before it may leave for
the mainland. There are no exceptions worth the name, and the requirement applies to Ondarethan,
Aldorathi and Stormveld shipping exactly as it applies to foreigners. The Synod instituted the call in 660 AF, the year the Nordmen took the north of the island ([[lore-stormveldtkn|the Taking of Stormveld]]), in the name of a coast that had just been invaded.

The Synod's officers inspect cargo and manifests as customs officials anywhere would, looking for
prohibited goods in both directions. That much is unremarkable and, on the whole, welcome: Eilwìth's
other realms have no capacity to police their own coasts and quietly benefit from someone doing it.

What is not unremarkable is the second interest. **Anything magical is reported.** Relics, worked
items, curiosities of uncertain nature, and anything a sailor cannot explain are recorded, examined and
frequently detained—for study, for valuation, for safekeeping, according to which word the officer
chooses. Compensation is offered and is usually fair. Refusal is possible in principle.

Nothing about this is concealed. The Synod's stated reason is that unexamined magic entering or leaving
an island as fae-touched as Eilwìth is a danger to everyone, which is true, and not the whole truth: the
requirement means that no relic has left Eilwìth in living memory without the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Arcane Order]] seeing it first, and
no mainland working has arrived without the same.

## What Happens to Those Who Try It

The compulsory call is not enforced by ships, and the Synod has never in its history threatened anyone
over it. It does not need to. Its instrument is the thing every ruler on Eilwìth most wants to keep.

**Arcane Order advisors serve in almost every significant polity**, here and across the world. Their value is
inestimable and their rulers know it: a court mage's knowledge, judgment and power are advantages no
neighbor without one can match, and every person of consequence wants one. They are not neutral
scholars for hire. They are members of an order, and on Eilwìth that order answers to the **Synod at
Ravenmoor**, which is its central chantry for the whole island.

So the first sanction is simply **withdrawal**. Word goes out to the Arcane Order in the offending realm, and
its advisors take their leave—courteously, with proper notice, giving reasons that are impeccable and
transparently pretextual. Then no replacement is available. Not from Dúnavarre, not from the mainland,
not from anywhere, because the request is refused before it is made.

The ruler is left **shunned by the advisors he covets**, in full view of neighbors who still have
theirs. For most, that is the end of the matter; the arithmetic is not difficult.

A ruler who attempts to prevent his advisor's departure discovers that detaining a mage is a different
proposition from detaining a man, and that the Synod's courtesy has been a choice.

**If shunning does not produce compliance**, the misfortunes begin. A harvest fails where it had every
reason not to. A sickness moves through the stock, or the children, that no local physician recognizes.
A well goes bad; a season of weather arrives that the oldest farmers say they have never seen. None of
it is ever claimed, connected or admitted, and enquiries are met at Ravenmoor with grave and
genuine-seeming concern.

**And all of it is reversible.** There is a path back, it is well understood, and it is deliberately
humiliating: acknowledge the fault, pay restitution, and petition the Synod's good graces at whatever
length the Synod finds appropriate. When that is done the advisors return, the misfortunes cease, and
nothing is ever said about either. Twice in two centuries a realm has gone the whole distance. Both came
back. Neither has discussed it since.

## What It Costs the Island

The other realms dislike the compulsory call and comply with it, out of a combination of fear and
indifference. They are **afraid of Dúnavarre** and would rather not discover what testing the Synod
costs. And they do not much care: an insular people with no interest in the mainland gives up little by
letting someone else handle the mainland, and every Eilwìthan realm is far more exercised by what
happens on Eilwìth than by what arrives from beyond it.

[[affiliation-repblcndrth|Ondareth]] dislikes it most and most loudly—a trading republic on the far
side of the island, obliged to send its shipping the long way round to be searched by mages it despises—and its Senate has passed resolutions on the matter for sixty years without once refusing to comply.

**[[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]] is outside the arrangement altogether**, and by the Synod's
choice rather than in defiance of it. Its trade and its wars run north to the
[[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], not east to the Aureldian mainland, and Nordmen hulls come into the
north coast without troubling Ravenmoor.

The Synod does not stop them because it **does not care**. The compulsory call was never really about
customs; it is about the channel between Eilwìth and the Aureldian world—what knowledge crosses it,
what relics cross it, and who arrives by it. Northern traffic carries iron, timber, thralls' produce and
quarrels between jarls, none of which bears on any of that. So the Synod watches the eastern water
closely, ignores the northern water entirely, and has never troubled to explain the distinction to
anyone who resents it.

The practical effect is that the Synod sees the entire commerce of Eilwìth, knows every mainland visitor
before that visitor reaches anyone else, and can shape what the island learns of the world by choosing
what may cross. Of all the ways Eilwìth is kept insular, this is the only deliberate one.

## History {#history}

Ravenmoor was the seat of the kings of Dúnavarre, and their mage advisors kept their chantry beside the hall. Over three reigns the advisors took the kings' offices one by one, and when the last king was set aside about 490 AF the Synod went on governing from the same hall, with his chair kept empty at the head of the room ([[lore-dunavarresynod|The Mages Take Dúnavarre]]). The Synod made Ravenmoor the island's compulsory port of call in 660, and the war on the Seilvri has been ordered from its hall since about 694.
