**Brinwold** is the [[place-aldorathrgn|Aldorath Region]]'s upland country, rising west and north of [[place-aldorvale|the Aldorvale]]
into hills too high and thin-soiled for grain. Twenty-six thousand people, and a great many more sheep.

## The Wool

Aldorathi wool is one of the two Eilwìthan goods with a mainland reputation, and nearly all of it comes
off these hills. The flocks are large, the staple is long and fine, and the clip is gathered each summer
and driven down to [[place-ashford|Ashford]] to be graded and sold.

It is the kingdom's principal export by value and the foundation of whatever cash economy Aldorath has.
The vale grows the food; Brinwold earns the coin.

## Older Terms

The upland manors are held on lighter and older terms than the vale's—less labor service, heavier
rents in kind, and customs that predate the kingdom and have never been rewritten. Upland tenants are
correspondingly harder to command and appreciably freer, which the lowlands consider a standing
irregularity and the uplands consider the natural order.

They honor the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] and keep rather more besides, at the old stones above
[[place-brynhallow|Brinhallow]] and elsewhere, and their parish priests report them devout and leave it
there.

## Current Situation {#present}

The vale's lords want men from the uplands for the Wiindmarch's levy, and the upland tenants owe rents in kind and not service in arms. [[place-brynhallow|Brinhallow]]'s tenants have refused under their old terms, and the other hill parishes are waiting to see whether their lords press them. At the northern end of [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]], where Brinwold's hills come down to the water under the Sunderfells, the Brinwold manors and the reed-cutters of the free village of [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]] dispute the reed-beds and the fishing off them, and nets are cut every summer.
