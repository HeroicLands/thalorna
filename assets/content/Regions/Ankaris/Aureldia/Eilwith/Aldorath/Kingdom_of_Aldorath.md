## Overview

The Kingdom of Aldorath is the heartland kingdom of [[place-eilwith|Eilwìth]], occupying the fertile lowlands and gentle hills east of the great central lake, [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]]. Aldorath is the most storied of the island's realms—a feudal monarchy of knights and castles, ancient oaths and courtly intrigue, where the boundary between history and legend is hopelessly blurred. The king rules from a lakeside fortress, advised by a council of barons whose families have held their lands since the earliest human settlement; the first of its kings, [Drethar of Kingshollow]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, was one of them, crowned by his peers about 120 AF ([[lore-kingshlwcrwn|the Crowning at Kingshollow]]). The kingdom holds the land of the [[place-aldorathrgn|Aldorath Region]]: the lake's eastern shore and the country east of it to the sea, under the Ironfells, while the western shore is upland that no realm holds.

Its king is [[being-aldrikdrthng|Aldrik Drething]], of Drethar's line, and he reigns from [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]]. The tension that defines the kingdom's present is the one it was founded on: the crown was the barons' gift, and the king wants to settle who follows him before the barons can give it again.

## Character

Prophecy and questing are woven into the fabric of Aldorathian culture; knights ride out to fulfill visions, and the court seers wield as much influence as the military commanders. The kingdom's chronicles are as much mythology as history—tales of kings who drew a sword from a stone, of bargains struck with lake-spirits, and of a sacred vessel hidden in mist-shrouded ruins are told not as fables but as the deeds of ancestors whose bloodlines still rule.

## How It Is Ruled {#how-it-is-ruled}

The king keeps the high justice, the royal forest and the crown's own manors, makes war and peace, and appoints the officers of his household and his march. Everything beyond that passes through the barons. A muster, a levy, a campaign or a new due needs the consent of the council of barons, which the king calls to Dunmere and which sits under the Elder of the Council, and every baron sends men and dues from his own manors on his own terms. The barons' courts try their manors, and the king's assize at Dunmere hears the pleas of the crown and the barons' quarrels with each other.

The court seers read the mists of Lake Eilìssa before every great act: a campaign, a marriage, a crowning. The First Seer is heard first, and a king who acts against a clear reading has to explain himself to the council. Like every Eilwìthan court, Dunmere also keeps an advisor of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]], whom the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Synod]] can withdraw.

## Current Situation {#present}

Aldrik Drething was crowned at Kingshollow in 711 AF and has reigned since. He is a soldier king whose quarrel is with his own council.

- **The heir.** The king has asked the council of barons to accept his nine-year-old son at Kingshollow as king-to-be, while the king lives. No king of Aldorath has been accepted before his father's burial, and Godwald Storli holds the council against it: the crown is the barons' to give at each vacancy. The council sits at Dunmere at midsummer, and the king is buying voices among the barons of the Aldorvale with royal forest and marriages.
- **Midwinter.** The king has kept the last three midwinter feasts at Dunmere rather than at Kingshollow, and the Steward has stood at the hall door in his place when the tenants sang [[lore-kingswassail|the Wassail]]. The barons count each absence as a slight to the gift.
- **The seers.** Both the king and the Elder have asked the First Seer what the lake shows of the boy, and she has given neither of them a reading.
- **The march.** The barons' levies reach Kaerwiind late and short, and the Warden reports that he cannot hold [[place-sunderfells|the Sunderfells]] passes against [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]] and the forest margin against the [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] with the men he has. The barons answer that the king can have more men when he stops asking for their crown.
- **The lower shore.** [[affiliation-durrask|The Durrask]] of the Thornwold, across the lake's south-eastern tip, burned a mill on the kingdom's shore above it in the autumn, a morning's ride from Dunmere, and drove off the plough-oxen of three manors. The shore barons have asked the king for men, and the king has none to spare from the march. The barons have armed their tenants to watch the shore by night, against the Durrask and the [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] of [[place-aldermire|Aldermire]] alike.
- **The lake.** At the lake's northern end the reed-beds and their fishing are claimed by Aldorath's barons and by the free village of [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]], and nobody has drawn a line on the water.
- **The trade.** Every Aldorathi hull that sails for the mainland out of [[place-greywater|Greiwater]] calls at [[place-ravenmoor|Ravenmoor]] first under the Synod's requirement.

## Population and Society

Aldorath holds about **200,000** people, making it much the largest of Eilwìth's settled realms—half
again the size of the Jarldom and nearly three times [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]], a fact its
lords mention rather more often than their neighbors enjoy.

Its society is the most straightforwardly feudal on the island. **Four in five Aldorathi are serfs or
peasants on manorial estates**, bound to the land and to a lord, working demesne and holding strips in
the usual way; above them sit a landed aristocracy with real hereditary claims, a modest clergy and a
thin merchant class. There is no equivalent of Dúnavarre's Synod-appointed stewardship, and no
equivalent of Ondareth's civic politics.

[[place-dunmere|Dunmere]], the seat, holds about **20,000**—the only place in the kingdom that is
a city rather than a market town, and the only one where anyone lives who is not directly answerable to
a manor.

The arrangement makes Aldorath populous, agriculturally productive, militarily formidable on its own
ground, and slow: everything the crown wishes to do must pass through lords whose interests are their
estates. Dúnavarre's mages find this contemptible and have never been able to exploit it.

## The Senali Connection

The Aldorathian crown holds that it keeps an ancient and cautious relationship with the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] of [[place-hallowwood|Seirasto]], on the south coast beyond Dúnavarre, and the court counts it the most sustained contact between the elder race and any Eilwìthan polity. The kingdom believes the connection gives it a mystical legitimacy that its rivals cannot claim. The Senali neither confirm nor deny it; they do not explain themselves, and the Aldorathian court knows better than to press them. The forest is theirs, and nobody outside the court can say what the claim rests on.

## Commerce and Currency {#commerce}

Kingdom of Aldorath uses the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian currency]] (Solden / Ardin / Ogdel) as its standard currency, with full access to the paper-script system through moneylenders belonging to [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] and chartered under [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]]. Kingdom of Aldorath is a post-imperial commonwealth member—it participates fully in the trade and banking infrastructure of the Vilérian system but is not subject to imperial governance or imperial taxation. See [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian Currency]] for the full system.

## History {#history}

The eastern barons crowned Drethar of Kingshollow about 120 AF because they wanted a war-leader against the forest and a judge for their quarrels, and they crowned a man whose land they could ride across in a day ([[lore-kingshlwcrwn|the Crowning at Kingshollow]]). In the reign of [Tenral]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the court moved to the lakeside at Dunmere, and the kings kept the hall, the chapel and the graves at Kingshollow. [Réncaris]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a king of the third century, led the crown's first campaign against the Grukar, and a dozen more have followed it, each declared a success. Since the Nordmen took the Stormplain in 660 AF the passes over the Sunderfells have been the kingdom's frontier with [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]].

## Relations {#relations}

Aldorath's relationship with [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]] is competitive—the two realms have long vied for influence over the island's interior. Where Dúnavarre looks outward to the mainland and the wider world, Aldorath looks inward to the lake, the forests, and the ancient mysteries of the island itself. Relations with the [[affiliation-repblcndrth|Republic of Ondareth]], beyond the lake and the Highlands to the west, are generally cordial but distant; the republic's pragmatic civic culture has little patience for Aldorathian mysticism. The Nordmen of [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]] to the north-west are a persistent concern—raiding parties from the fjords have troubled Aldorath's northern borders for generations, while [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]], the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]] hold in [[place-ironfells|the Ironfells]] to the north, keeps apart from both realms. The Ironfells are unclaimed by any realm. The [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] are at war with the kingdom as with everyone: the nest at Hartfell raids the Wiindmarch forest, [[place-fernbank|Fernbank]] the old road south of Dunmere, and [[place-aldermire|Aldermire]] the lake's lower shore.
