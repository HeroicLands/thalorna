## Overview {#overview}

**Brinhallow** sits high in the [[place-aldorathrgn|Aldorath Region]]'s upland country, where the manorial arable gives out and the land
is good for sheep, stone and very little else. Eighteen hundred people, and a reputation in the lowlands
for being difficult.

## The Uplands

The hill manors are poorer than those of the plain and are held on older terms—lighter labor service,
heavier rents in kind, and customs that predate the kingdom and have never been rewritten because no
lord has thought the trouble worth it. Upland tenants are correspondingly harder to command and rather
freer than their lowland equivalents, a distinction both groups understand perfectly.

Wool, mutton, hill cheese and quarried stone go down to [[place-ashford|Ashford]]; nothing much comes
back up.

## The Observances

Brinhallow honors the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] and keeps something else as well, at the old
stones above the town, at the turnings of the year. The priest sent up from
[[place-dunmere|Dunmere]] attends both and reports the parish devout, which is true, and complete, which
it is not.

This is unremarkable in the Eilwìthan uplands, and it is one of the reasons lowland Aldorathi regard the
hill parishes as half-barbarian and the hill parishes regard the lowlands as soft.

## The Present {#present}

The march is short of men, and the barons of the vale have asked their upland manors for men for the Wiindmarch's levy. Brinhallow's tenants owe rent in kind and not service in arms, under customs older than the kingdom, and they have refused. The baron who holds the town has not decided whether to press them or to send the men from his lowland manors instead.
