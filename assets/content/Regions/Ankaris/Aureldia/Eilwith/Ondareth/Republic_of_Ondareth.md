## Overview

The Republic of Ondareth is a western republic governed by a senate of landed citizens and military officers. Ondareth's political culture is pragmatic and martial, with a strong citizen-soldier tradition that contrasts sharply with the courtly refinement of [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]] and the feudal romanticism of [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]]. The republic holds the coast of [[place-ondareth|Ondareth]] and the upland of [[place-cntrlhghlnds|the Central Highlands]] behind it, and it is governed from [[place-thornbury|Thornburi]] by the [[affiliation-senatondrth|Senate]].

## Character

The republic emerged from a military uprising against a tyrannical king several centuries ago ([[lore-ondarethfall|the Fall of the Last King of Ondareth]], about 440 AF), and its citizens take fierce pride in their self-governance. The Senate—composed of elected representatives from the landholding and military classes—debates policy with a directness that strikes the courtiers of Dúnavarre as vulgar and the barons of Aldorath as presumptuous. Ondarethans consider both attitudes proof of their neighbors' decadence.

Ondarethan legions—disciplined, well-drilled, and motivated by civic duty rather than feudal obligation—are the most professional fighting force on the island. Military service is both a civic obligation and the primary path to political advancement; a senator who has not served is a rarity and regarded with suspicion.

## How It Is Ruled {#how-it-is-ruled}

The [[affiliation-senatondrth|Senate]] governs. It sits at Thornburi under its First Senator, [[being-harwingrymr|Harwin Greimore]], votes the laws, the money, the levies and the wars, and chooses the magistrates for their year. The magistrates carry out what it votes, and answer to it when their year ends. The citizens vote in the assembly for the magistrates the Senate puts before them, and serve in the levy.

## Current Situation {#present}

- **The chair.** Harwin Greimore's three years as First Senator end with this year, and he wants a second term, which the Senate has never given. Kuthwald Ashden wants the chair and has put the Tyrant's Rock before the Senate again, because the houses at its foot have been bought in the name of one of Harwin's freedmen.
- **The frontier.** [[affiliation-bradhar|The Bradhar]] of the Peshtar forest burned two watch-burgs north of Kaermouth in the autumn. The Senate has voted Godstan Hardli another legion's levies and no money to feed them, and Wilrik Brownwell has made the levy the cause of the assembly.
- **The northern water.** Two jarls of [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]] took a Thornburi coaster off the Nordfells in the autumn under a pact between them. The shipping houses want the fleet sent north, and the treasury cannot pay for the fleet and the frontier both.
- **Veldareth.** Aldrun Somerli presses the Table of Veldareth to say whether its Speaker answers to the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Dúnavarre Synod]], and is buying copies of his letters to [[place-ravenmoor|Ravenmoor]].
- **The upland grazing.** [[affiliation-durrask|The Durrask]] of the Thornwold drove forty brood mares off the republic's eastern upland grazing in the summer, and the herdsmen who followed killed their War-Leader's brother at a ford. The levy has taken the upland breeders' young men north.
- **The highland nests.** The [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] of the Highlands' crags and ravines raid the upland farms and horse runs while the levy takes the herders north: [[place-kingsdelf|Kingsdelf]] under the Three Sisters, [[place-reedgill|Reedgill]] above the lake, Ravenkeld and Stoneholt, and from the Thornwold [[place-aldermire|Aldermire]].
- **The lake.** At the northern end of [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]] the reed-cutters of [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]], a free village that sells its fish at Stoniford, and the hill tenants of Aldorath's [[place-brynwold|Brinwold]] cut each other's nets over the reed-beds both claim, and Koldmere has asked the Stoniford senators to take its side at Thornburi.

## Relations

Ondareth has cultivated diplomatic channels with the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], sending diplomats there and trading military expertise for political recognition. Viléria has on occasion sent missions to Ondareth to keep its options open, and keeps no regular relations with it. Relations with [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]] are respectful but wary—the two polities share a border and compete for influence over the southern coast. The [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] nests in the Highlands' crags and ravines raid its upland farms, and no Senate has ever voted a campaign against them, because the losses fall on small breeders and not on senators. The [[place-pshtrwldrns|Peshtar tribes]] to the north are a chronic security concern, and Ondarethan legions regularly patrol the frontier. The Nordmen of [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]], beyond the forest, meet Ondareth only at sea, where their jarls take Ondarethan coasters when it suits them. [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]] lies across [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]] to the east, and the two share the lake without a line on it. The free city of [[place-veldareth|Veldareth]], on the coast between Ondareth and Dúnavarre, serves as a neutral meeting ground and center of learning.

## Commerce and Currency

Republic of Ondareth uses the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian currency]] (Solden / Ardin / Ogdel) as its standard currency, with full access to the paper-script system through moneylenders belonging to [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]] and chartered under [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]]. Republic of Ondareth is a post-imperial commonwealth member—it participates fully in the trade and banking infrastructure of the Vilérian system but is not subject to imperial governance or imperial taxation. See [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian Currency]] for the full system.

## Extent

Ondareth holds the **west and south-west** of [[place-eilwith|Eilwìth]], from the fog-bound western coast to its upland edge beside the Central Highlands, and about **150,000** people—the second-largest
polity on the island, twice the size of Dúnavarre, and the only part of
Eilwìth governed by anything resembling a civic constitution.

Its
merchants are able, its accounts are creative, and its neighbors deal with it warily and constantly.

## The Senate and the Model

Ondareth is not accidentally a republic. It is a republic **on purpose**, and the purpose is
[[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Viléria]].

Its constitution copies the Vilérian forms: a **Senate** whose members are styled **senators**,
magistracies with Vilérian names, a civic calendar of processions and assemblies, and public building in
a self-consciously Vilérian manner, executed in local stone by masons working from descriptions.
Senators wear the bordered mantle. The Senate house at [[place-thornbury|Thornburi]] has a portico.

The magistracies carry the Empire's own words, in their Vilari spelling. Two _tavoi_ (consuls) give the year its name and hold command in the field, a _vornos_ declares the law, a _theronos_ keeps the citizen roll, a _tarkelos_ answers for the treasury, a _peltros_ keeps the markets and the streets, a _lusos_ speaks for the citizens, and a Vandraks commands each legion in the field. A man who has held the year is _tavalen_ for life, and the propertied order that fills the magistracies is the _gulmenoi_. The words are borrowed for the standing they carry. Vilérian diplomats treat the borrowed titles with diplomatic courtesy; in private, Vilérians take them as a mild insult.

## Rubbish, and Vanity

The claim to comparison with Viléria is **unadulterated vanity and rubbish**, and it is worth being
plain about that, because Ondareth never is.

Nobody with any real knowledge of the mainland could mistake this for Viléria for the length of an
afternoon. The forms are copied without being understood: the magistracies bear Vilérian names attached
to offices that do nothing a Vilérian would recognize; the processions observe a calendar assembled from
hearsay; the mantles are the wrong cut and the wrong color; the Vilari of the Senate's orations is
archaic where it is not simply wrong, and delivered in an accent that no one has ever corrected because
no one present could. The portico at Thornburi is a portico in the way a child's drawing of a horse is
a horse.

What it resembles is not a republic. It is **children playacting at the behavior of adults**—with
total seriousness, immense ceremony, and no possible way of knowing how they look, because almost none of
them has seen the thing they are imitating.

That is the mechanism, and it is why the pretension is stable. Almost no Ondarethan beyond its diplomats has been to Viléria.
The mainland is a fortnight's dangerous sailing and a world away; what reaches the island is
second-hand, decades old, and filtered through people with an interest in flattering the Senate. There
is nobody in Thornburi able to say _that is not how it is done_, and there has not been for
generations; the Vilérians who could say it treat the titles as a courtesy and answer with courtesy.

Viléria knows Ondareth, because Ondareth sends diplomats to Viléria. Viléria has on occasion sent
missions to Ondareth to keep its options open, and has no regular relations with it.

## The Republic in Practice

And it is a **thoroughly corrupt** republic. Senatorial place is elective and is bought; the Senate is
genuinely deliberative and the deliberation is genuinely for sale; the courts work, for those able to
pay for them to work. None of this is hidden and very little of it is embarrassing—Ondareth's
political class regards the arrangement as the natural friction of self-government, and regards
Dúnavarre's incorruptible mage-administration as a tyranny that has found a
polite name.

But corruption is the mild description, and it flatters them.

A good number of Ondareth's senators are, plainly, **evil men**. They are wholly indifferent to the
people they govern, whose condition does not enter their calculations except as a supply of labor,
rents and votes. They are driven entirely by self-interest and prosecute it without restraint or
embarrassment. And they are **vain** past the point of caricature—ferociously jealous of precedence,
incapable of an unrewarded act, contemptuous of anyone who cannot injure them, and utterly persuaded of
their own greatness.

There are a dozen or so of the first rank, and the republic's politics is essentially the friction
between them. They ruin each other's clients, buy each other's magistrates, prosecute each other's
allies, and combine only when something threatens the arrangement that lets all of them prosper. Whether
the Senate ever does anything for the hundred and fifty thousand people of Ondareth depends entirely on
whether it happens to lie along the path of one of these men's ambitions.

Often enough it does not, and nothing is done, and this is understood by everyone as the weather.

## The Two Faiths

Ondareth's religious life is unlike anywhere else on Eilwìth, and both of its peculiarities follow
from the corruption rather than sitting beside it.

### The Black Flame

[[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]]—the faith devoted to the destructive aspect of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], suppressed across most of
the Aureldian world and reduced elsewhere to hidden shrines and secretive orders—has **enormous
influence** in Ondareth, and does not especially trouble to hide it.

It does not need to. A faith is suppressed by magistrates, and in Ondareth magistracies are bought. The
Flame buys them, as every other interest does, and by the same means; it holds shrines that are known
about, funds candidates who are known to be funded, and is prosecuted about as often as the shipping
houses are. Its doctrine—purification by fire, the strong over the weak—sits with unpleasant ease in
a republic where influence is openly for sale, and [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] has been
saying so for fifty years to no effect whatever.

The Flame's other western stronghold is across the water in [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]], where
[[place-harivrencity|Hárivren]]'s earl holds it openly. Whether the two are coordinated is a question
the Forge asks loudly and cannot answer.

### Sélmoros

The other great presence is the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]], whose god is known as the Silent Judge—and he is
prominent for precisely the opposite reason.

In a republic where the courts work for those who can pay them to work, the god who, in the faith's teaching, judges everyone
without exception and cannot be reached, bought, flattered or deferred is not an abstraction. He is the
only judge most Ondarethans believe they will ever face on equal terms with their betters, and they know it. The
Sélmoran clergy are correspondingly the one institution in the republic with unpurchased moral
authority: they take no part in elections, accept no civic office, and are the customary arbiters in
disputes that both parties genuinely want settled rather than won.

The result is that the two most powerful religious bodies in Ondareth are a suppressed cult of
domination and a death-cult of impartial judgment, and they are not allies. Both deal in death and they
mean opposite things by it. The republic's politics runs in the space between them, and its magistrates
are careful to be seen at both temples.

## History {#history}

A king ruled the western coast until about 440 AF, when [Tralkar]{.name data-type="being/npc"} taxed the carrying trade to ruin and hanged the captains who complained. The coast's levies and the highland farms rose under [Zalmen]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, threw his castle down at Thornburi, and the captains built the republic out of what travelers had told them of Viléria ([[lore-ondarethfall|the Fall of the Last King of Ondareth]]). A generation later the Senate voted a standing legion for the northern frontier and raised its camp at Kaermouth. Since the Nordmen took the north in 660 AF, the Jarldom's ships have shared the northern water with Ondareth's.
