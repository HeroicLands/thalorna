## Overview {#overview}

**Lake Eilìssa** is the great central lake of [[place-eilwith|Eilwìth]]: a highland lake at the eastern edge of [[place-cntrlhghlnds|the Central Highlands]], some hundred and five miles long and about twenty-seven across, on a long axis that runs from north-west to south-east. Its name echoes the island's own. The island believes it to be a nexus of mystical power, and its mists to be the place where the veil between the mortal world and the others is thinnest.

Its shores are divided. At the northern end [[place-sunderfells|the Sunderfells]] of [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|the Jarldom of Stormveld]] come down to the water. The eastern shore is the [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Kingdom of Aldorath]]'s, where the farmland of [[place-aldorvale|the Aldorvale]] and the hills of [[place-brynwold|Brinwold]] climb to meet it, and at the south-eastern tip, where Aldorath's shore meets [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]]'s, stands [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]], Aldorath's seat. The western shore is the upland of the Highlands, which no realm holds, wooded along the water and open grazing above it.

## The Land {#the-land}

The lake fills a long basin in the highland's eastern edge. The western shore is high and steep, wooded down to short gravel beaches, with the water deep a few strides out. The northern end is broken into bays and reed-beds below the moor and the mountains. The eastern shore is low and green, with manors, mills and fishing hamlets along it, and at the south-eastern tip the lake runs out into the river that drains east through the Aldorvale, along the border with Dúnavarre, and down to the tidal reach below [[place-greywater|Greiwater]]. The river carries the peat of the highland with it, and runs gray all the way to the sea.

The coastal fog does not climb to the lake, but the lake makes its own. On still evenings in autumn and spring a white mist rises off the water and lies on it until well after sunrise, and on some mornings it stands so thick that the far shore is gone and the boats keep within call of the land.

## Who Uses It {#who-uses-it}

The Aldorathi shore lives off the lake as much as off the land. Its hamlets fish it for trout, char and eel, cut its reeds for thatch, and run barges down the eastern shore to Dunmere with grain, wool and stone. Dunmere takes its water from the lake and its name from it; the Eilwìthi word for a lake is _mere_, and the city is the dark lake.

The western shore uses it less. The free steadings of the Highlands water cattle and horses there, and the reed-cutters of [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]] work the bays at the northern end.

Both shores keep the lake's observances. A household that has something to ask leaves bread or a coin at the water's margin at dusk, and the [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorathi]] court seers read the mists from the shore below Dunmere castle, as the kings' seers have since before the court came to the city.

## Dangers {#dangers}

The water is cold the whole year, and a boat that overturns far from shore loses its crew to the cold before the shore is reached.

The mists are the lake's greater danger. The kingdom's chronicles are full of knights who rode or rowed into the fog and came back changed, or did not come back at all, and the paths the mist opens lead to places that cannot be found by ordinary means. The shore folk keep their boats in sight of land when the mist is on the water, and a party that goes into it on purpose goes into one of the few places on Eilwìth where the island's reputation for fae power is true.

## What Is Told {#what-is-told}

The lake has a name and the shore does not say it after dark. The reeves of the Aldorathi manors teach it among [[lore-reevesthrees|the Reeve's Threes]]: "Three things not to be done after dark: to stand where the roads divide, to call the lake by name, and to count the standing stones." After sunset it is the Mere, or the water, and on the western shore, where the reeves' sayings carry less weight, the habit holds all the same.

The Aldorathi tell of bargains struck with lake-spirits by the first barons, and of a sacred vessel hidden in mist-shrouded ruins on a shore that is not always there. The tenants of [[place-kingsholow|Kingshollow]] call Dunmere the lake-town in [[lore-kingswassail|the Wassail at Kingshollow]], when they sing to a king come home from it with a lord at each hand.

## Converging Interests {#present}

The lake has no agreed line down its middle, and nobody has drawn one. At the northern end, where Brinwold's hills meet the moor under the Sunderfells, the free village of Koldmere and the Brinwold manors both claim the reed-beds and the fishing off them. The quarrel is carried on with cut nets and impounded boats rather than arms, and the [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] nest at [[place-reedgill|Reedgill]], in the wooded ravine above the beds, raids both sides' boats and cutters. Each year Koldmere asks the Ondarethan senators of [[place-stonyford|Stoniford]], who buy its fish, to speak for it at Thornbury, and the Brinwold barons carry their side to Dunmere, and nothing is settled.

On the kingdom's shore above Dunmere the manors watch by night since the Durrask burned a mill, and the Grukar of [[place-aldermire|Aldermire]], in the wooded bog above the south-western shore, have turned west onto the Highlands' grazing.

At Dunmere the court waits on the mists. King [[being-aldrikdrthng|Aldrik Drething]] has asked the council of barons to accept his son as king-to-be, and both the king and the barons who oppose him have asked [[being-elfrunthrnl|Elfrun Thornli]], the First Seer, what the lake shows of it. She has read the mists three times since the autumn and has given no reading to either side. The advisor that [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|the Order]] keeps at the court has asked to stand on the shore with her when she reads again, and she has refused him.
