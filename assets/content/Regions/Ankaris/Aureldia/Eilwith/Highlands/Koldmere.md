## Overview {#overview}

**Koldmere** is a village of about three hundred and fifty people on the north-western shore of [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]], in [[place-cntrlhghlnds|the Central Highlands]], which no realm holds. Its name is the cold lake. It cuts reeds, fishes the bays below the moor, and stands at the end of the lake where nobody has drawn a line on the water.

## The Village {#the-village}

Thirty-odd houses of turf and stone stand above a shingle beach, thatched with the lake's own reed, with boats drawn up below them and racks of drying fish and reed bundles along the shore. A shrine of four standing stones stands at the water's margin, where the village leaves bread at dusk when it has something to ask of the lake.

## The Holding {#the-holding}

The land is the villagers' own, held in small farms, with the moor above it grazed in common. The reed-beds and the fishing in the bays east of the village are the village's living, and it holds that they have always been its own.

## Who Leads It {#who-leads}

Koldmere chooses a reed-reeve each spring to set the cutting and keep the boats, and the reeve speaks for the village to anyone who comes asking, and to the Ondarethan senators at [[place-stonyford|Stoniford]], who buy its fish and its reed. [[being-elfithwgdn|Elfith Wigden]] has been chosen four springs running.

## The Failing or the Quarrel {#present}

The best reed-beds lie east of the village, across the lake's northern end under the Sunderfells, where the hill tenants of [[place-brynwold|Brinwold]] in [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]] claim them too. Each side cuts the other's nets and impounds the other's boats. This winter Brinwold's men took two Koldmere boats and the reed in them, and Elfith Wigden has asked the Stoniford senators to take the matter to [[place-thornbury|Thornburi]] and put the [[affiliation-repblcndrth|Republic of Ondareth]]'s weight behind the village. They have promised to, as they promise every year.

## What Is Seen from It {#what-is-seen}

The lake runs away south-east from the beach, farther than the eye reaches; [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]], at its far tip, is a hundred miles off. On a clear day the mountains of the Sunderfells stand over the reed-beds to the north-east, and the wooded western shore runs away south. When the mist is on the water, nothing shows at all, and the boats stay drawn up.

## The Cost of Reaching It {#cost-of-reaching-it}

A track runs to Koldmere from Stoniford across the open grazing, a long day on foot and easy riding. By water a boat from Brinwold's shore makes it in an hour, and that is half the quarrel.
