## Overview {#overview}

**Reedgill** is a steep, wooded ravine that runs down from the moor to the northern shore of [[place-eilissa|Lake Eilìssa]], between [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]] and the hills of [[place-brynwold|Brinwold]]. Its name is the reed ravine, for the beds at its foot. Alder, birch and rowan fill it, a stream runs down its floor, and a [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] nest lives in dens dug into its banks.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The nest holds the ravine and the moor's edge above it. Below it lie the reed-beds at the lake's northern end, under the Sunderfells, which the free village of Koldmere and [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]]'s Brinwold both claim, and the nest raids them as though they were its own: cutters on the beds, boats drawn up on the shingle, and the nets set in the bays.

## Layers {#history}

The ravine has been wooded for as long as the shore has been settled, because nobody could plow it or graze it. The nest dug into the banks where the soil is deep and the roots hold it, and it has been there as long as Koldmere has cut reed. The reed-cutters of both shores keep clear of the ravine's foot after dusk, and their boats keep to the open water when they pass it.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The reed-bed quarrel is at its worst. This winter Brinwold's men took two Koldmere boats and the reed in them, and in the autumn Koldmere lost a boat and two cutters on the beds and holds that Brinwold took those too. Each shore cuts the other's nets, and each finds its own cut nets in the morning. The reed-reeve of Koldmere, [[being-elfithwgdn|Elfith Wigden]], has asked the Ondarethan senators of [[place-stonyford|Stonyford]] to take the quarrel to Thornbury.

The cutters of both shores have seen a thin Grukar standing still in the shallows below the ravine at dusk, watching the beds, and call it [[being-stillwader|Stillwader]].

:::secret {title="The cut nets"}
The boat and the two cutters Koldmere lost in the autumn were taken by Reedgill's raiders, and half the cut nets on both shores are the nest's work, cut at night in the bays. Stillwader steers it: a shore watching the other shore does not watch the ravine.
:::

## Dangerous Neighbours {#dangerous-neighbours}

The two villages are each other's danger more than the nest's: a stranger with a boat on the beds is taken for the other shore's man. On the moor above, the Highlands' herdsmen ride past the ravine's head and do not go down.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

A narrow path climbs the stream, under trees close enough to touch on both sides. The dens are holes in the banks, roofed with roots, with the spoil thrown down to the stream, and the stench reaches the shore when the wind is off the moor. The spawn-chamber is dug deep under the largest bank, two-thirds of the way up, where the stream turns.

:::secret {title="In the dens"}
Cut nets, Koldmere's and Brinwold's both, line the dens. A party that finds them in the same den has something both villages need to see.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The lost boat.** Koldmere wants its boat and its two cutters found, and is sure they are at Brinwold.
- **The nets.** Each shore finds its own nets cut in the morning, and the reed-reeve of Koldmere pays for proof of who cuts them.
