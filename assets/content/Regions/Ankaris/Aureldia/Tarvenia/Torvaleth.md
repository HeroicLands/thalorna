## Overview

You smell Torvaleth before you see it. The road down the [Thalsira]{.name data-type="place/feature"} valley from [[place-ronetho|Ornetho]] runs between water meadows full of cattle, the pens outside the east gate hold a few thousand head on a market day, and the dung, the tannery smoke and the bawling reach the road long before the walls rise off the plain. Torvaleth is the capital of the [[affiliation-kingdmlnrk|Kingdom of Lyenrik]], a town of about 36,000 at the ford where the road from the mountains to the sea crosses the river, and it is the largest town in the [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia Region]] away from the coast; only the port of [[place-valthari|Valthari]] is bigger.

It is a brick town. The Thalsira carries a red clay down from the hills, and the brickworks along the riverbank have been firing it into _paltho_ (bricks) for as long as there has been a market here, so the walls, the granaries, the houses and the king's hall are all the same dull red, and the dust that blows off the brickyards in a dry summer turns every cart and every hem the same color. A Torvaleth carter carries the red on his boots wherever in the kingdom he drives.

## The Ford and the Market

Torvaleth is older than the kingdom that rules from it. The ford was a market before the legions came, a place where the herdsmen of the hills brought their cattle down to meet the grain farmers of the plain, and it stayed one through the occupation ([[lore-lowlandstaken|The Lowlands Taken]]). The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] governed the march from behind the stone walls of Ornetho and let the market at the ford go on paying. When the garrisons were driven out ([[lore-tarvfallsawy|Tarvénia Falls Away]]), the lords of the lowlands chose a king, and the king set his hall among the fields that fed him, at the ford.

The town still lives by the market. There are three of them, and each has its own ground:

- **The cattle market** fills the meadows outside the east gate every week. Drovers bring lean cattle down from [[place-asturath|Asturath]] in spring; the farmers of the plain fatten them on the water meadows through the summer and sell them on in autumn to the butchers of Valthari and the tanners downstream. The hides go to the tanneries below the town, and the tanneries are the reason nobody of standing lives on the downstream side.
- **The grain market** is held in the _emba_ (town square) under the royal granaries, and in a lean year it is the most watched place in the kingdom, because the price struck there is the price of bread from the mountains to the sea.
- **The wine market** opens in autumn, when the carts come down from [[place-enthara|Enthara]] in the eastern hills, and spends the winter in the cellars under the river wall.

A drover who has brought cattle down to Torvaleth for thirty years gives a newcomer the rule at the gate: "Count your beasts at the gate, and have the bailiff's man count them with you. The toll is paid on his count, and every dispute in that market starts with two men who counted differently and nobody who watched."

## The Harvest Fair

At the end of summer, when the threshing is done, Torvaleth holds the greatest fair in Tarvénia. For three weeks the town doubles. Merchants come up the river road from Valthari and across the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]] beyond it, from [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]] and from the Taruzelian ports, to buy the year's grain, cattle and wine in one place; mountain lords come down to buy horses and to be seen; companies looking for men set up their tables by the gate. The fair opens at the ford, where the priests of the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]], whom the farmers of the plain honor above every other faith, bless the first wagon of the harvest as it crosses, and it closes with the _arthauna_ (horse fair), the races and the trading of horses on the last three days.

The fair has its own officer, the Master of the Fair, who keeps its peace and its measures, takes its tolls and hears its quarrels on the day they arise. A merchant cheated at noon has an answer before dark, which is the whole of what a man three weeks from home wants from a court. Horses are judged by the _kornazdo_ (horse-judge), whose word on a horse's age and soundness settles a sale, and whose office is the one bribe every horse-dealer at the fair is suspected of having paid.

## The King's Hall

On the rise above the ford stands the _harka_ (king's hall) of the kings of Lyenrik, a long red-brick hall with a squat tower at each end, a dry ditch and no keep. [Zenoth Oltris]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, King of Lyenrik, keeps his court in it, and its lower chamber is the seat of the [Court of Pleas]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, where the cases between the lords of the kingdom are heard and where every appeal from a bailiff's court in Lyenrik ends.

The Court of Pleas writes everything down. Its clerks keep the plea rolls, which record every case it has heard since the kingdom was founded, and the [Roll of Houses]{.name data-type="lore"}, the written order of precedence of every noble house of Lyenrik, is kept in the same chamber under its own keeper. The habit is a legacy of the Empire, whose courts decided the lowland lords' disputes for four centuries, and the mountain lords mock it: a Tarvenan swears on a stone, and a Liénriki lord, they say, swears on a stone and then sends for a clerk. In Torvaleth the clerks are the busiest men at court, and a lord who wants a better place on the Roll of Houses spends years and a great deal of money finding a record that puts one there.

## Living There

Most of Torvaleth's people work the trades the markets feed: drovers, butchers, tanners and curriers; brickmakers; millers and bakers; carters and bargemen; the clerks and scribes of the court; the porters of the granaries. The great lords of the kingdom keep a _thegaimo_ (town house) on the streets below the hall, and a lord's standing in the town is measured by how near his door is to the king's.

Order in the streets is the neighbors' first and the crown's second. A shopkeeper whose stall is broken into in the night goes to the guild of his trade and to the men of his street, who usually know whose son it was; if they cannot settle it he goes to the bailiff's watch at the market gate, and only a matter worth a written plea goes up the hill. Torvaleth's walls are low, built of brick after the occupation, and the town has long since spread past them along the river; its real defense is the chain of watchtowers along the Thalsira, of which the tower over the river gate is one, and the host the king can raise from the richest farmland in the west.

The town's faith is the plain's. The temple of Lusinia stands at the ford beside the bridge-foot; [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] of gates and oaths has his shrine at the east gate, where the cattle come in and the oaths of sale are sworn; and the brickmakers keep the hearth festival of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] with their kilns.
