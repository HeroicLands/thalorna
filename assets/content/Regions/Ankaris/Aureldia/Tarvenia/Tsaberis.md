Tsaberis is the drove town of the high valleys north of [[place-lezkora|Lezkora]] in [[place-asturath|Asturath]], three thousand five hundred people in stone houses around a great walled sheepfold at the meeting of four drove roads. Its name is the old mountain word for a sheepfold, _tsaber_. In one week of every spring the town sends the flocks up to the summer meadows and the young men down to the coast. "Half went up and half went down" is the valleys' praise for a family that keeps the old way.

## The Fold and the Roads

The town is built around its fold. The wall is a man's height of dry stone, a quarter of a mile around, with a gate on each of the four drove roads, and in spring it holds the flocks of every valley that drives through. The houses stand outside it in rings, the oldest nearest the wall, and between them run lanes wide enough for a flock and no wider. The square in front of the north gates is where the market is held, and in its middle stands the town's _goitsa_ (oath stone), a standing stone taller than a man, black with the hands that have touched it.

North of the town the four roads become two and climb to the _galtsur_ (high meadows), which open in late spring when the snow goes off them. South of it the roads come together into one and go down toward the lowlands of [[place-leonrik|Lyenrik]] and the coast. The town lives on the traffic between the two.

## The Week of the Drive

The _izurka_ (summer drive) starts at Tsaberis on the day the shepherds' council names, when the high meadows are clear. For a week before it the flocks come in from the lower valleys, the fold fills, and the town fills with shepherds, dogs and the noise of bells. On the morning of the drive the flocks go out through the north gates in an order the council has fixed, valley by valley, each behind its bellwether.

The same week is the week the companies fill. A captain who means to take his company abroad that season comes to Tsaberis to make up its numbers, because the young men of every valley that drives through are in the town for the drive. He stands at the oath stone and reads out the season's terms, the contract he means to seek and its pay, and a man who wants to go with him puts his hand on the stone and gives his name, and the captain says the name back to him before the stone, and from that morning it is on the company's _therna_ (muster roll), which a captain carries in his head. The boys of a valley join their own company at its own stone at the [Feast of Thresholds]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}; Tsaberis is where a captain finds the men he still lacks. By the end of the week the companies go out of the south gates as the flocks go out of the north, and the town says goodbye to both.

The contract itself is signed later, at the coast, with whoever is paying. What is sworn at Tsaberis is the company: the captain's word to the men and theirs to him, before witnesses who will be there when the company comes home.

## Who Rules

Tsaberis belongs to the [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Kingdom of Asturath]], and the drove roads and the high meadows belong to its king, who lets the meadows to the valleys by custom older than his house. The king's Drove-Warden keeps the roads, settles quarrels between valleys over grazing and collects the crown's due, one beast in forty that goes up. The shepherds' council—one elder from each valley that drives through—sets the day of the drive and the order of the flocks, and the town's householders keep the fold and the market in an assembly of their own. The three bodies quarrel every spring and settle it before the drive.

## Living There

Outside the drive Tsaberis is a quiet market town. It makes _betsur_ (cheese) from the milk that comes down from the meadows through the summer, salts it in the cellars under the old houses and sells it at the autumn fair. It cards and spins the wool of the spring shearing. It keeps the drove roads in repair and the inns along them open. The women of the town run most of its holdings, because so many of its men are abroad, and the cheese trade is theirs almost entirely.

The town keeps the shrine of [[affiliation-menutsa|the Mother]] in a cave a mile above the north gates, where the shepherds leave cheese at the mouth on the way up and again on the way down. A _tsinga_ (wise-woman) lives below it and sees to the sick of the town and its flocks.

## Hooks

- **A contract read at the stone.** A captain has read out terms at the oath stone that are better than any company has been offered in years, and the older men will not put their hands on the stone.
- **The order of the flocks.** A valley that has driven fifth for a century has been placed last by the shepherds' council, and its elder says the vote was bought.
- **A name on the roll.** A man signed on at the stone under a name that is not his own, and the company is already on the road to the coast.
