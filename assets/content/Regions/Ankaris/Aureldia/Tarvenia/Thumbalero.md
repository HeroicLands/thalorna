## Overview {#overview}

**Thumbalero** is the pilgrims' path of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] in [[place-asturath|Asturath]]. It leaves the drove roads below [[place-tsaberis|Tsaberis]], climbs round the west side of the plateau of [[place-lezkora|Lezkora]], crosses the slope high above [[place-horngorge|the Horn Gorge]] and comes down to the foot of the cliff, where it joins the plateau road for the last climb to the gate. Its name is _thumba_ (a forge) and _valero_ (a road), the forge road. At the top of the climb is what the pilgrims come for: the fire in the temple on Lezkora's square, which the faith's priests say has burned since before the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] came west.

From Tsaberis to the foot of the cliff is two days on foot. The drovers and the cheese mules go up to the plateau by a shorter road over its north shoulder, and nobody uses the forge road who is not walking it on purpose. Two houses of the faith keep it: [[place-hrethumba|Hrethumba]] at the ford half a day out, and [[place-ithrakemtho|Ithrakemtho]] on the shoulder above the gorge.

## What Moves on It {#what-moves-on-it}

**Smiths with an ember.** A smith who means to light a new forge walks the path to the fire and carries an ember of it home in a clay pot packed with ash, so that the priest who consecrates the forge can light it from the god's own fire ([Blessing of the Anvil]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}). The pot is fed at each house on the way down.

**Pilgrims of the faith.** Smiths, apprentices at the end of their time, armorers of the companies and households with a vow to keep walk up in the season. A crossbowman going abroad under contract often keeps the [Vigil of the Forge]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} in the temple before he goes, and his valley's men walk up with him.

**The houses' mules.** Charcoal, Ardalis iron and bread go out to the two houses by mule from Tsaberis, and the houses' nails and mule-shoes come back.

**Shepherds and the curious.** Astúrathi shepherds cross the path by day with their flocks. Now and then a Vilérian antiquarian walks it to look down into the Horn Gorge.

## Logistics {#logistics}

The path is a mule's width for most of its length, paved with flat stones where the ground is wet and cut into steps where it is steep. The worst of it is the traverse above the gorge, half a day along a ledge with a wall of rock on one hand and the drop on the other, where a low parapet of dry stone stands at the bends. Springs run beside the path through the thaw and into summer, and dry up in a hot autumn.

There are no inns. The houses give a pilgrim bread, a place by the forge and a bench for one night, and take what he can give. A party on foot sleeps the first night at Hrethumba or in the open below the traverse, and the second at Ithrakemtho, and reaches the foot of the cliff by noon of the third day. From there the plateau road climbs the cliff in most of a morning.

## The Stations {#stations}

**The drove roads below Tsaberis.** The path leaves the south road a mile below the town at a wayside shrine (_nekai_) with a hammer cut on its stone.

**Hrethumba**, half a day out, at the ford of a stream that comes down off the plateau's west face. It is the lower house of the faith on the path, two acolytes and a forge, and the place where a smith going home feeds his ember for the last time.

**The traverse**, a day out, where the path runs along the ledge above the Horn Gorge. From the ledge a walker looks down on the gorge's upper end and on the cairn the Astúrathi raised there after the battle.

**Ithrakemtho**, a day and a half out, on the shoulder at the end of the traverse. A lamp burns in a niche of its outer wall, and pilgrims on the ledge walk toward it in the dusk.

**The foot of the cliff**, where the path comes down on the plateau road below the switchbacks.

## The Year on the Road {#year-on-the-road}

**Winter** shuts the traverse from the first heavy snow, and the houses are snowed in with their stores. **The thaw** is the dangerous season, because the meltwater loosens the stones above the ledge. The houses send word to Tsaberis when the traverse is clear, and pilgrims who wait for the word are wiser than the ones who do not. **Late spring and summer** are the pilgrims' season, and the [Lanterns of the Night]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} at Lezkora, at the spring equinox, opens it in a year when the snow goes early. **Autumn** brings fog off the gorge that sits on the traverse for days, and the houses light their lamps at noon.

## Who Patrols and Taxes It {#present}

Nobody taxes the forge road. It is the faith's path by custom, kept by its two houses, and no officer of the king of [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Asturath]] stands on it. The Drove-Warden at Tsaberis keeps the drove roads and not this one. The Warden of the Plateau Gate, [[being-thelvarougar|Thelvaro Ugarreta]], counts the pilgrims when they reach his gate as he counts everyone who climbs, and a pilgrim is counted as a pilgrim.

This spring more pilgrims than usual are on the path, and they are not the usual pilgrims. They are young men in pairs, in pilgrims' gray, who carry no ember pot, walk the traverse fast and do not know the words at the houses' fires. Every lord who comes up to Lezkora for an election passes the gate with twelve sworn men behind him and no more, and the lords of the lowlands have begun sending their men up one and two at a time. At Ithrakemtho the Hammer-Priest, [[being-volkemohilth|Volkemo Hiltheris]], counts the pairs who pass his door. He has told nobody what he counts.

## Dangers {#dangers}

- **The ledge.** A loose stone in the thaw, a wet step or a mule that shies can put a walker over the edge, and the drop is to the floor of the gorge.
- **The fog.** In autumn it comes up off the gorge and hides the parapet, and a walker who leaves the path in it can walk off the ledge.
- **The gorge.** On autumn nights the legion's horns sound the recall from the Horn Gorge below, and the houses tell every pilgrim never to go down into it and never to sleep in it. The path keeps to the ledge above.
- **The snow.** It comes early on the traverse, and a pilgrim caught there by the first heavy fall has a day's walk to either house.

## History {#history}

The path is older than the mule road over the plateau's shoulder, and older than anything written about Lezkora. The priests of the fire say pilgrims walked it before the Empire came west, and the houses' account is that it was the only way up to the fire for anyone who did not belong to the plateau. About 143 AF the legion that came up toward Lezkora died in the gorge below the traverse ([[lore-horngorgebtl|The Battle of the Horn Gorge]]), and the Astúrathi raised their cairn at its upper end, below the ledge. Ithrakemtho was raised on the shoulder at the end of the traverse in the generations after the battle, and Hrethumba at the ford later still. The path has had two houses since, and the faith has kept both open in every year of which the houses keep a record.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The pairs in gray.** Volkemo Hiltheris has counted more young men on the path this spring than the temple will bless in a year, and wants someone to learn whose men they are before he decides whether to tell the Warden of the Plateau Gate.
- **The cold forge.** The fire at Hrethumba went out in the winter and the lower house has no Hammer-Priest to light it again. Its acolytes want a priest brought down from Lezkora before a smith comes down the path with an ember to feed.
- **The antiquarian on the ledge.** A Vilérian scholar has hired a shepherd to take him down from the traverse into the Horn Gorge, and Ithrakemtho's acolytes want him stopped.
