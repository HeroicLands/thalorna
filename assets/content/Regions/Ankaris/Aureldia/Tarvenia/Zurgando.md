Zurgando is the high pass at the head of the valleys above [[place-kavrenath|Kávrenath]] in [[place-asturath|Asturath]], the short way between those valleys and the road down to the coast. Its name means "the cairn pass": the _zurga_ (cairn on a pass) and the _gando_ (pass). The cairns are the first thing a traveler sees of it: a line of them, each as tall as a man, standing thirty paces one from the next across the saddle and down both sides.

## The Way Over

The path climbs out of the highest valley along a stream, past the last houses and the last fences, through beech wood and then open pasture, and reaches the saddle in half a day. The saddle is bare rock and _kolzar_ (scree), half a mile across, with a small tarn on its south side that is frozen from autumn to late spring. The way down the far side is steeper and joins the valley road a day's walk below.

The pass is open from late spring to the middle of autumn. Snow closes it in winter, and in spring the snowmelt floods the stream below it. In autumn the _durka_ (fog in the passes) comes down and sits on the saddle for days at a time without moving, and a traveler caught in it cannot see from one cairn to the next. The valley people say a man who leaves the line of cairns in the fog does not come back to it, and they keep a stone shepherd's hut on each side of the saddle, roofed and stocked with firewood, for whoever has to wait the fog out.

The valley road goes round by Kávrenath, lower and longer, and stays open later in the year. Zurgando is the companies' road.

## The Stones

A man of the valleys going down to the coast under contract carries a stone up from his own stream and sets it on a cairn on the saddle. When he comes home over the pass he takes a stone off the same cairn, any stone, and carries it down to his house, and it goes on the threshold or into the wall. The stones on the saddle are the ones still owed. A house that has sent its men abroad for generations has a row of these stones in the wall beside its door, and a house whose man did not come home has a gap in the row.

A captain's stone goes on the topmost cairn, and he takes it down last, after his company is paid off.

The cairns grow in the years of a long war abroad and shrink in a peace, and their height in late autumn is the count of the valleys' men still away. Nobody takes a stone from a cairn he did not build on, and a stone that falls is put back on whichever cairn is nearest.

## The Dead Road

The old path down from the saddle along the stream, past every house in the valley, is an _arduts_ (dead road), and the valleys say the dead walk it on the night of the lanterns. The valleys tell [[lore-lightatfront|The Light at the Front]] of a crossbowman who came over Zurgando late with eleven dead men's pay in his pack and met the procession on it. The cave where the [Mother]{.name data-type="lore/deity"} is kept lies above the highest house, where the dead road turns up from the valley toward the saddle.

## Who Goes

- **Men of the valleys**, going down under contract in spring and coming home in autumn.
- **Captains** who know the pass and will take a company over it to save two days on the valley road.
- **Smugglers**, a few, who want a way to the coast that the fortress at Kávrenath does not watch.

:::secret {title="The stones that are never taken"}
Some stones on the topmost cairn belong to captains who did not come home, and a few belong to captains who did and left them there. A captain who breaks a contract abroad and is struck from every roll in the west may still come home over the pass, and some do, at night and in the fog. The valleys know which stones those are. The stones stay, and nobody in the valley will name the captain who left one.
:::
