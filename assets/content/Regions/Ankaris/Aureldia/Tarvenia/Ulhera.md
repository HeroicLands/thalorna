## Overview {#overview}

Ulhera is the farthest south a Tarvenan can stand without getting his feet wet. The town of about 9,000 sits at the mouth of a long, narrow inlet at the southern end of the coast of [[place-galvaren|Galvaren]], south of [[place-durvolka|Durvolka]], where a drowned river valley runs a day's sail inland between steep wooded sides and opens onto the sea between two bare points of rock. Its name comes from _ulhis_ (a fish), and the town is about one fish above all others: the cod of the Far Grounds, which its boats bring home split, salted and stacked in their holds after three weeks on the open sea.

Ulhera is smaller than Durvolka and rougher. It has one street along the water, a second up the hill behind it, a quay of tarred timber on stone piers, and a curing ground on the slope above the town where the split cod lies drying on racks of hazel and stone in every hour of sun the coast allows. In the drying season the only way to the upper town runs between the racks, and the smell of drying fish lies over the whole town.

## The Far Grounds

The cod boats are the town's pride. About sixty half-decked boats of eight to twelve men work out of Ulhera, and in spring and again in autumn they sail south, out of sight of land, to the shallow banks in the middle of the sea, two and three days' sail offshore that the town calls the [Far Grounds]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. There they fish by hand-line, split and salt the catch on deck, and come home when the hold is full or the salt is gone. A boat is out for three weeks if the fish are biting and five if they are not, and in a bad autumn one boat in ten does not come back.

A boy goes out as the cook at twelve and as a line-hand at fourteen. He takes a share of the catch from his first voyage, a half share at first, and a full share when the master says so in front of the crew. A master owns his boat, or owns most of it with the curing house that lent him the money, and he keeps his place in the town's roll of masters only while his boat sails each season.

The masters of Ulhera know the open water of the sea better than anyone in [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]], and their charts are worth more than their catches. Each master keeps his own book of soundings, currents and landmarks, writes it in his own shorthand, and leaves it to his heir. The best of them have been farther out than the Far Grounds, to waters the charts of [[place-valthari|Valthari]] leave blank, and come back with stories of islands of birds, of a current that runs the wrong way and of a shore none of them could find again. Most of the navigators and explorers the [[affiliation-kngdmglvrn|Kingdom of Galvaren]] is known for learned their trade on an Ulhera cod boat.

## The Town and Its Masters

The king's reeve holds the town for the crown, collects the dues on the catch and keeps the salt store, and he governs very little else. Ulhera is run in practice by its roll of masters, about sixty names written in a book that the reeve keeps and the masters correct. The roll elects two seats at the [Long Table]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} in Durvolka each autumn, and it settles the town's own quarrels in the masters' room above the salt store: a net fouled on another boat's line, a crew-man who left one master for another in the middle of a season, a share disputed after a death.

The present first master of the roll is [[being-korvaththal|Korvath Thaluris]], who has sailed thirty seasons, holds one of the town's seats at the Long Table and has been beyond the Far Grounds three times. He is the loudest voice at the Table against the crown's price for salt, and the king's council has learned to let him speak first and briefly.

The curing houses are the other power in the town. They lend the masters the money for boats and salt, buy the catch at the quay, dry it, and sell it south. Most are run by women, the widows and daughters of masters, and a curing-house mistress who holds the notes on six boats has more say in Ulhera than most of the masters she has lent to.

## Faith and the Drowned

Ulhera keeps the faiths of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] and holds to the [[affiliation-drownedroad|Drowned Road]] more strictly than the capital. Its harbor teller keeps the longest roll of the drowned on the coast, read aloud on the [Night of Lanterns]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} from a book that has been copied out fair four times. A boat that does not come home from the Far Grounds stays on the roll as out for a year and a day, and only then does the teller call it in. Until she does, the master's share and the crew's are held by the curing house, and the wives are not widows.

The priests of the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] bless the cod boats on the quay before each voyage, each master pays them for it, and each master also carries a smith's hammer in the boat against the fog.

## Trouble in Ulhera {#present}

- **The book of soundings.** Korvath Thaluris's old rival on the roll has died at sea, and his book of soundings, with the only chart of an island shore he claimed to have found beyond the Far Grounds, has gone missing from his widow's house. A Valthari shipping house has offered a sum for it, and the widow wants it back before anyone can sell it.
- **The salt runs out.** The autumn salt ship from the south is a month late, the crown's store at Ulhera is empty, and the boats cannot sail without it. The masters need salt from Durvolka, or a reason the Long Table will believe.
- **A crew-man on two rolls.** A line-hand signed with one master in spring and went out with another, and the first master's boat sailed short-handed and lost a man. The masters' room must decide whether the line-hand owes the dead man's share to his widow, and the line-hand has friends.
- **A boat out too long.** A cod boat is in its eleventh month out. The teller will call it in at the Night of Lanterns, and its master's young wife has been seen meeting a man at the drying racks after dark. Her husband's brother wants to know who he is before the roll is read.
