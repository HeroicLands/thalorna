## Overview {#overview}

Ithrakemtho is the upper house of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] on [[place-thumbalero|Thumbalero]], the pilgrims' path to [[place-lezkora|Lezkora]]. It stands on a shoulder of rock at the end of the traverse above [[place-horngorge|the Horn Gorge]], a day and a half out of [[place-tsaberis|Tsaberis]], and it is the last roof on the path before the foot of the cliff. Its name is _ithra_ (a lamp) and _kemtho_ (a wall), the lamp wall, for the lamp that burns in a niche of its outer wall over the path. A pilgrim on the ledge sees it from the last bend of the traverse and walks toward it in the dusk.

The house is a forge with a hall and a dormitory round it, kept by a Hammer-Priest and three acolytes. Pilgrims sleep there, smiths carrying an ember home feed it at the house's fire, and the house makes the nails and mule-shoes the path wears out.

## Founding {#founding}

The house's account is that a Hammer-Priest of the temple on Lezkora's square built the first hall on the shoulder in the generations after the legion died in the gorge, so that no pilgrim would have to sleep on the ledge above the dead. He lit the house's forge from the temple's fire, and the lamp in the wall from the forge. The acolytes are taught that the house's fire has not gone out since, and the lamp is trimmed by the youngest of them every dusk.

## History {#history}

The house has been rebuilt twice in its own record: once after a rockfall took the dormitory in a wet thaw, and once after a winter fire in the hall roof, when the forge was kept burning under a lean-to until the roof was rafted again. The temple at Lezkora has sent it a Hammer-Priest in every generation, and the house's book lists them by name. The present one, [[being-volkemohilth|Volkemo Hiltheris]], came to it from the temple thirty years ago.

## The Count at the Door {#present}

The path is busier this spring than Volkemo has known it. Pilgrims in pairs, young men in gray who carry no ember pot and do not know the words at the house's fire, have been sleeping on his benches and walking on at first light toward the foot of the cliff. Volkemo keeps the house's book and has begun writing them in it, two by two, with the day they passed.

The house's custom is that its door is open to anyone in pilgrims' gray, and the faith keeps out of the business of lords. The lords of the lowlands are sending their men up to Lezkora one and two at a time before an election, and a lord who comes up to the High Hall may bring twelve sworn men past the gate and no more. Volkemo knows what his book adds up to. He has not decided whether to send it to the Warden of the Plateau Gate, [[being-thelvarougar|Thelvaro Ugarreta]], which would make the house a party to the choosing of a High King, or to keep the door as the house has always kept it.

## Who Rules It {#who-rules-it}

The house belongs to the Sacred Forge and answers to the temple on Lezkora's square, whose Hammer-Priests send it its priest and its acolytes. Volkemo Hiltheris holds it as its Hammer-Priest. He says the rites at the forge, blesses the smiths who come through and keeps the book. His three acolytes are apprentices as much as novices: they work the bellows, trim the lamp, keep the cistern and carry the house's iron.

## Lands and Income {#lands-and-income}

The house has no lands. It lives on what pilgrims give, on the bread and charcoal the temple sends out by mule from Tsaberis, and on its own forge: nails, mule-shoes and the iron fittings of the path's parapet, which the house makes from Ardalis bar and sells at Tsaberis. The king of [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Asturath]] lets it take deadwood from the beech wood below the shoulder by a grant older than his house.

## Visiting the House {#visiting}

A pilgrim knocks at the gate under the lamp and is let into the yard. He lays any blade he carries on the rack inside the gate, because the house holds that iron brought to the forge is brought to be blessed and not to be drawn. He says the short prayer to [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]] over the house's hammer at the forge, eats the house's bread and sleeps one night on a bench. A smith with an ember feeds it from the forge and is given ash to pack it. A traveler who is not a pilgrim is let in the same way in bad weather and asked to say the prayer or keep silent while others say it.

## The Grounds {#the-grounds}

- **[1] The Lamp Wall:** the outer wall over the path, with the lamp in its niche at head height and the gate beneath it. The wall is dry stone, a man and a half high.
- **[2] The Yard:** a narrow court behind the gate, with the blade rack and a trough for the mules.
- **[3] The Forge:** the heart of the house, an open-fronted stone hearth under a slate roof at the back of the yard, with the anvil, the hammer the pilgrims pray over and the bellows the acolytes work.
- **[4] The Hall:** a long room against the rock, where the house eats and the book is kept in a chest by the hearth.
- **[5] The Dormitory:** the pilgrims' room, rebuilt after the rockfall, with benches along both walls for about twenty.
- **[6] The Terrace:** a ledge of rock behind the hall that looks down the length of the Horn Gorge to the cairn at its upper end. The acolytes are forbidden it after dark in autumn.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The book by the hearth.** Volkemo Hiltheris wants someone he trusts to read his count and tell him whose men the pairs in gray are.
- **The lamp trimmed late.** An acolyte left the lamp dark for an hour of a foggy autumn dusk, and a pilgrim on the traverse has not come in.
- **The ember that went cold.** A smith coming down from Lezkora reached the house with his pot cold, and asks whether a forge lit from Ithrakemtho's fire is lit from the god's.
