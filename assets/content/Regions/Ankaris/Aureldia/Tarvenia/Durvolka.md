## Overview

In Durvolka the fog comes first and the boats come second. The capital of the [[affiliation-kngdmglvrn|Kingdom of Galvaren]] is a harbor town of about 20,000 people on the deepest bay of the [[place-galvaren|Galvaren]] coast, halfway down the green, rain-soaked south-western shore of [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]]. Its name is plain Tarveni, _durka_ (fog) and _volkema_ (harbor), and the town has earned it: from late spring to the end of autumn a gray bank lies on the bay most mornings, and the herring boats go out through it by sound, steering on the bell at the harbor mouth and the shouts of the boat ahead.

About three hundred boats call Durvolka home. Most are open herring boats with a crew of six, which go out at dusk and come back at dawn; some forty are the half-decked cod boats that stay out on the Far Grounds for three weeks at a time; and two dozen are merchant ships that carry salt fish round the headland to [[place-valthari|Valthari]] and bring back salt, wine and oil. The king keeps six galleys in the basin under the castle. In the herring season the whole town smells of brine and woodsmoke, and while the catch is coming in a cart has no right of way on the waterfront.

## The Town

Durvolka climbs a steep, wet hillside from the water, and it is built of the gray granite of that hillside, roofed with slate, with every window on the seaward side small and every door set back from the weather. Five quarters make it.

[The Strand]{.name data-type="place/site"} is the waterfront: the fish market under its long slate roof, the curing houses where the cod is split, salted and stacked, the net lofts and the rope-walks, and the cellars under the market where the salt fish waits for a ship. The [[being-lrgspdr|large spiders]] of the coast live in those cellars and in the lofts, and the net-menders let them be, because they kill more rats than the cats do. The boatyards at the south end of the Strand build in oak and chestnut from the forests behind the town.

[The Masters' Row]{.name data-type="place/site"} runs along the first terrace above the Strand, a street of tall stone houses built by the shipmaster families with their profits. Its one public building is the [Masters' Hall]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, where the masters of the harbor meet each autumn to choose the three of their number who sit at the king's council for the coming year.

[The Headland]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the king's castle, a squat keep and a curtain wall on the rock at the north side of the bay mouth, with the galley basin cut into the rock beneath it. The council sits in its hall.

[The Lantern Stair]{.name data-type="place/site"} is a street of steps that climbs from the Strand to the top of the hill, where a passage grave older than the town stands among the gorse. The harbor teller of the [[affiliation-drownedroad|Drowned Road]] lives in the last house below the grave and keeps the roll of the harbor's drowned there. On a foggy night every house on the Stair has a lamp in its seaward window, and from the bay the street is a line of lights going up into the gray.

[The Lyenrik Gate]{.name data-type="place/site"} is the inland quarter at the top of the valley road, where the carts come down from [[place-leonrik|Lyenrik]] with grain, cattle and wine and go back loaded with salt fish. The king's salt-house stands here, beside the cattle market, because every grain of salt the curing houses use is bought from it.

## Who Governs {#who-rules}

The king governs the kingdom from the Headland, and the town through his officers. The [Long Table]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, the royal council, seats the landed lords of the inland valleys on one side and the elected shipmasters of the harbor towns on the other, with the king at its head; Durvolka's three masters sit there with the masters of [[place-ulhera|Ulhera]] and the lesser harbors. The present king is [[being-ganothervo|Ganoth Ervothandis]], who came to the throne in 712 AF and is a careful man with the crown's money.

The town has no council of its own. The Warden of the Strand holds the harbor for the king: he collects the dues on every hull and every basket of fish landed, keeps the bell and the harbor mouth, and hears the quarrels of the waterfront at a bench in the fish market every morning the boats are in. A man whose net is cut or whose catch is stolen goes to that bench, and the warden settles it there with the masters of both boats listening. A quarrel between two shipmasters goes to the Masters' Hall first, and to the Long Table only when the hall cannot settle it. A theft in the upper town goes to the warden's officer at the Lyenrik Gate.

## Living by the Water

The year turns on the fish. The herring come into the bay and along the coast from late summer into winter, and the open boats work them every night the weather allows. The cod boats go out to the Far Grounds in spring and again in autumn. In early summer the tuna run close along the headlands, and the whole town turns out for the [tuna traps]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}: long walls of net set from the Headland rocks, where the fish are drawn into the last chamber and killed with gaffs in a churning, bloody hour that the children watch from the castle wall.

Salt is the town's one great want. Galvaren makes none worth having, because its sun is too weak for salt pans, so every cask of herring and every stack of cod is cured with salt brought by sea from the southern coast. The crown buys it, the Keeper of the Salt sells it to the curing houses at the crown's price, and the price is the curing houses' standing grievance against the crown.

The inland road carries what the sea cannot. Lyenrik's grain feeds the town, its cattle come down to the Lyenrik Gate in autumn, and Durvolka salt fish goes up the same road to feed Lyenrik's villages through the winter.

## The Faith

Durvolka keeps the faiths of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], and the coast's older custom beside them. The priests of the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] bless the fleet with lamps each spring before the cod boats sail, and a shipmaster family sets up its household shrine to the [[affiliation-pozeras|Faith of Pozeras]] of voyages. The town's dead are buried by the priests of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] in the burial ground behind the Lyenrik Gate, all except the drowned. A body the sea gives back is carried up the Lantern Stair to the passage grave, the harbor teller says its name aloud at the door, and the priests do not argue the point in Durvolka, though some of them preach against it elsewhere.

## Trouble in Durvolka {#present}

- **A boat called in comes home.** The harbor teller called in a herring boat's crew of six at the last [Night of Lanterns]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and their shares and widows have passed under the law. On a foggy morning the boat comes in through the harbor mouth with four of the six aboard, and none of them will say where they have been for nearly two years.
- **The salt price.** The Keeper of the Salt has raised the crown's price for the second year running, and the curing-house mistresses of the Strand have begun to buy from a Valthari ship that anchors outside the bay at night. The warden wants the smugglers found, and the mistresses mean that he shall not find them.
- **A seat at the Long Table.** The Masters' Hall elects in autumn, and a shipmaster who lost two boats on the Far Grounds this year is standing for one of Durvolka's three seats against the king's own candidate. Somebody has been paying the herring crews to vote, which is unlawful, and somebody else has been threatening them, which is worse.
- **The spider in the grave.** The passage grave at the top of the Lantern Stair has lost its spider, and the teller will not say a drowned man in at the door, and the grave-keeper will not carry one, until the door is kept again. Three bodies wait in the teller's cellar, the families are at the door, and the nearest grave with a spider in it is two days' walk up the coast.
