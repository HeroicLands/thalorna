## Overview

Galvaren is the coast where [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]] runs out of land: the green, rain-soaked western shore of the region, held by the [[affiliation-kngdmglvrn|Kingdom of Galvaren]], where the hills come down through deep forests to a coast of drowned valleys and black cliffs and the open ocean begins. It is a country that fishes the western ocean and listens to its dead. Its fishing fleets are the largest in the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]], ranging far out for the seasonal runs of cod, herring and tuna, and its people take the fog, the old stones and the drowned more seriously than anyone else in Tarvénia.

About a million people live in Galvaren. A little more than half of them farm the inland valleys under the landed lords; the rest live on the coast, in the harbor towns and in a fishing village in nearly every cove.

## Weather

The ocean makes Galvaren's weather, and the ocean is cold. Rain falls on two days in three through most of the year, in long soft drizzles more often than in storms, and the land is green in every season. Winters are mild and wet, with snow only on the high ground and seldom for long. Summers are cool, and they bring the fog: when the warm air of the land meets the cold sea, a gray bank forms offshore and comes in over the coast most mornings from late spring to autumn, lying in the bays till noon and sometimes all day. The autumn and winter gales come off the ocean with nothing to break them, and they wreck more boats than anything else on the coast.

## The Coast

The coast is all inlets. Long, narrow drowned valleys run inland between steep wooded sides, a day's sail deep in places, so that a farm ten miles from the open sea may have a boat at the bottom of its fields. Between the inlets the land stands up in headlands and points of bare rock, and off them lie reefs, stacks and small islands of seal rock and gull.

Three places on that coast matter beyond their own parishes. [[place-durvolka|Durvolka]], the capital, stands on the deepest bay halfway down the coast. [[place-ulhera|Ulhera]], to the south at the mouth of the longest of the inlets, sends its cod boats out to the Far Grounds of the western ocean. North of Durvolka the green hills end in [[place-kaurema|Kaurema]], the Black Cliffs, eighteen miles of sheer dark rock with the drowned ruin the fishing towns call the sunken city at their foot, and [[place-ezkoba|Ezkoba]] in their one cleft keeps the lamp that brings the coast's boats home in fog.

The coast's living is fish. Herring come in along the shore from late summer into winter, cod are taken on the Far Grounds in spring and autumn, and the tuna run close along the headlands in early summer. Galvaren makes no salt worth having, because its sun is too weak for the pans, so every cask of herring and stack of cod is cured with salt brought by sea from the southern coast, and the crown that buys and sells that salt holds the coast's livelihood in its hand.

## The Forests and the Valleys

Behind the coast the land rises through forest. The coastal forests are oak and chestnut in the valley bottoms and on the lower slopes, with birch, holly and pine higher up, so thick and so wet that moss grows on every side of every trunk. They supply the oak for the boatyards, the chestnuts a poor family eats through the winter, and the bark for the tanneries, and they hold boar, deer and, in the deepest valleys, wolves.

Inland, where the forest opens, lie the valleys of the lords: small fields of oats, rye and barley, hay meadows, apple orchards and a great many cattle, worked by tenants who owe their lord labor and a share of the harvest. These valleys feed the coast, and their lords are the landed half of the king's [Long Table]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}. East of them the hills fall away into the lowlands of [[place-leonrik|Lienrik]], richer and drier country, and the one good road of the kingdom runs from Durvolka up the valley and over the hills into it, carrying salt fish east and grain, cattle and wine west.

## The Old Stones

The coast is crowded with stones older than the Tarveni. Standing stones stand singly or in rows on the headlands, leaning seaward; rings of stones stand on the clifftops and the high heath; and passage graves, low mounds of turf over a stone chamber with a narrow passage leading in, stand in nearly every parish of the coast and in many of the inland valleys too. Nobody knows who raised them. The people of the coast hold that the graves are where the mortal world thins, that their own drowned go home to them in the fog, and that a grave's spider keeps its door; that belief and the customs that go with it are the [[affiliation-drownedroad|Drowned Road]]. The [[being-lrgspdr|large spiders]] of the coast are commonest in the graves, and the cold lights that outsiders call [[being-vprwsp|vaporwisps]] are seen among the stones in fog.

## People and Speech

The Galváreni are seafarers, fishermen and storytellers, and the Tarvenans of the interior call them dreamers. They speak [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni]] in the coast's own way, swallowing their unstressed vowels and softening their clusters, so that the coast's speech is the hardest Tarveni in the kingdoms to follow. They keep the faiths of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], above all the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] of storms and the [[affiliation-pozeras|Faith of Pozeras]] of voyages, and they keep the Drowned Road beside them. Galváreni sailors are known across the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]] as fearless or foolhardy, and the kingdom produces more than its share of the navigators and sea captains who chart the western ocean.
