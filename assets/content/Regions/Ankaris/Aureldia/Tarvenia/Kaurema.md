## Overview

Kaurema is the black cliff, _kaure_ and _korema_ in the coast's Tarveni, and the fishermen of [[place-galvaren|Galvaren]] call it the Black Cliffs when they speak of it at all. North of [[place-durvolka|Durvolka]] the green hills of the coast end in a wall of dark rock that runs for about eighteen miles, three hundred feet high at its southern end and six hundred at its northern, sheer to the water along nearly all of it. The rock is a hard, close-grained stone that weathers black, and in rain, which is most days, it shines like wet slate.

A boat coming up the coast in clear weather sees the cliffs from a day off, a dark band under the green. In fog, which is more usual, it hears them first: the surf booming in the sea-caves, the seals on the rocks, and the bell that [[place-ezkoba|Ezkoba]] rings in its cove. Kaurema has one break along its whole length, the steep cleft where Ezkoba stands, and one landing, the cove beneath the town. A boat caught against the cliffs anywhere else in an onshore gale is lost, and the coast keeps a long list of the boats it has lost there.

## The Foot of the Cliffs

The sea has cut the base of Kaurema into a country of its own. Stacks and broken pillars stand off the cliff a few hundred yards out, the largest of them crowned with gulls. Low reefs of black rock run out from the foot, bare at low water and white with surf at high, and the gray seals haul out on them by the hundred in autumn to pup. Sea-caves open at the waterline every few hundred yards, some no deeper than a cart-shed, some running back into the dark farther than any boat has followed them, with the tide breathing in and out of their mouths.

Off the southern third of the cliffs, where the rock is lowest and the reefs run farthest out, between Ezkoba and Durvolka, lies the drowned ruin that the fishing towns call the sunken city: a few streets of fitted stone, uncovered at the lowest spring tides and under water the rest of the month, with stairs that go down from the streets into flooded passages under the cliff. The stonework is older than the Tarveni and older than the passage graves on the clifftop, and no story on the coast says who cut it. The harbor tellers of the [[affiliation-drownedroad|Drowned Road]] say the sea took the city for its pride. The fishermen set their nets wide of it, sail home before dark in the dark of the moon, and will not anchor within sight of it.

## The Top of the Cliffs

The clifftop is open heath, short turf and gorse, grazed by sheep and cut by the wind until nothing on it stands higher than a man except the stones. A line of standing stones runs along the cliff edge for most of its length, single stones a few hundred yards apart, leaning seaward, gray with lichen; at the northern end they gather into a ring of nineteen, and three passage graves stand inside it. Nobody on the coast walks between the stones in a fog, and the shepherds who graze the heath bring their flocks in at the first sight of a gray bank coming off the sea.

The cliff path from Ezkoba runs the length of the top, and it is the only road. In summer the gull and guillemot ledges are worked from it: the Ezkoba men go over the edge on ropes for eggs and young birds, and a good ledge is a family's property, passed down and quarreled over like a field.

## Dangers

The cliffs kill more people than anything that lives at their foot. A boat in an onshore wind has nowhere to land, the tide runs hard along the reefs, the edge of the heath is undercut in places and gives way without warning, and the fog comes in fast enough to lose a shepherd a hundred yards from the path. The coast also tells of what comes with the fog, the cold lights among the stones that the [[being-vprwsp|vaporwisps]] are blamed for, and of a long dark shape that boats coming home late have seen gliding along the cliff face against the stars.

# The Truth of Kaurema {#truth .secret}

The dark shape is the [[being-abyssdrk|abyssdrake]] that keeps the sunken city. It lairs in the flooded passages below the stairs, drives seals and boats out of the water round the ruin without eating them, and kills whatever goes down into the ruin itself. A party that goes into the streets at the lowest spring tide has about an hour before the sea comes back, and the drake comes with it.

The drake shuns strong light, and the Long Lamp at Ezkoba is why it is seen so seldom along the northern cliffs. Nobody on the coast knows this. The lamp-keepers keep the lamp for the boats and the tellers keep it for the drowned. If the lamp goes dark for a run of foggy nights, the drake hunts farther along the cliffs than anyone living has seen it, and the boats of Ezkoba begin to go missing in its cove.

The stonework of the sunken city has no explanation on the coast and needs none in play. What lies at the bottom of the stairs is the GM's to decide.
