"The Mother has two words. She has _yes_ and she has _no_. That's all of them." So the wise-women of [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]] begin the part of their winter tale that every child of the mountain villages knows. The Mother, _menutsa_, is the oldest faith of the valleys, kept under the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] and never in place of them. As the wise-women tell it, she is the mountains, she hears every word spoken on them, and every oath stone in the land is a piece of her.

## Overview {#overview}

The Mother has no temple and no priest. A mountain village keeps her in a cave (_ikorra_) and leaves its offerings (_itsarde_) at the mouth. It keeps its wise-woman (_tsinga_), who heals in the Mother's way. And it keeps its oath stone (_goitsa_), a standing stone older than any shrine, on which a Tarvenan swears the oath that matters. Her words, for the cave and the offering, the wise-woman and the stone, come from the old mountain speech that the [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni Language]] took in, and the oath stone itself is older than the tongue.

Nearly all of what the valleys hold about her is in one tale, [[lore-motheryesno|The Mother's Yes and No]], which the wise-women tell at the hearth in the long nights after the flocks are down.

## Belief {#belief}

The tale tells that the mountains are the Mother. She lay down before anybody, on her side with her back to the sea, and her shoulder is the high ridge, her hip the next, her knees the two snow peaks above the summer meadow. When [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] sat down to spin the world on her wheel the mountains were already there, and she spun the lowlands round them. The springs that come out of the rock are the Mother's, and the caves are where she keeps herself: she is kept in the caves because she keeps herself there, not because anyone keeps her.

She has two words. Everything that is so she answers _yes_, everything that is not she answers _no_, and a thing half so and half not she leaves unanswered. She cannot bear a lie. When one is told in the high country she stirs, and stones come off the crags on a still night. When the lie is a bad one she crosses the sky between the peaks as a fire, the light that goes over on a summer night, and then comes the hail. The valleys grant that the storm is [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]]'s, but the hail that falls on one meadow and not the next, on one man's barley and not his neighbor's, they hold is hers, looking for the mouth that lied.

The oath stone is a piece of her. The first one, by the tale, rolled out of the dark of her cave to the feet of two shepherds who had settled a quarrel over a high meadow before her, and they carried it out and set it on the line they had agreed. Every _goitsa_ since is her, which is why a Tarvenan puts his hand on it: whatever he says there, he says in her cave. A moved stone cannot be put back by the man who moved it, and only his son can set it right.

She lies still as long as somebody, in some valley, puts a hand on a stone each year and says a true thing and keeps it. On the day when no hand is on any stone she gets up, and the snow comes down every valley at once and over the lowlands to the sea.

## Worship and Customs {#worship}

The Mother's observance is plain, local and kept by the household.

- **The offering at the mouth.** Cheese, a bit of bread or a little pot of milk is left at the mouth of the cave, and nobody takes anything left there. The shepherds of [[place-tsaberis|Tsaberis]] leave cheese at their cave on the way up to the summer meadows and again on the way down. The miners of [[place-ardalis|Ardalis]] hold that a gallery in the iron hill is the Mother's before it is theirs, and a family leaves cheese and milk at the mouth of a new working before it cuts the first stone.
- **Going in.** Whoever has to ask her something leaves an offering and goes in, keeping four rules: speak plainly, and ask what can be answered _yes_ or _no_; never sit down; take nothing out, not a stone or a drop of the water; and come out facing her, walking backward to the light. At midsummer the old men of the valleys go in, and come out the same way.
- **The stone.** A Tarvenan swears on the stone, not on a document, when the matter is the one that matters. Where the priests of [[affiliation-ardavon|Árdavon]] have built a shrine near the stone, a valley swears before the god at the shrine and then walks to the stone and says it again with a hand on it. Once a year at the fair a householder puts his hand on the stone and says over his own bargain, whether the water, the boundary with the miller or the lease. The crossbow companies swear their contracts on their stones before they go down to the lowlands.
- **The dead.** A household leaves an offering at the cave mouth where the Mother is kept, as well as paying the village priest. The valleys above [[place-kavrenath|Kávrenath]] tell, in [[lore-lightatfront|The Light at the Front]], that the procession of the dead (_gatsa_) goes up the dead road and in at the mouth of the cave, into the Mother, and that a dead man received both by the god and by her is received no more than he deserves.
- **The hearth.** The household that swears on the stone also keeps the hearth fire (_karalzo_) and the household gods (_karalzis_), and the Mother's tale is a hearth tale, told by the fire in winter to the children and anyone else who stays up.

## The Wise-Women {#organization}

The Mother has no hierarchy. Her wise-women are the village healers, each a neighbor to everyone in her village, and a Tarvenan tolerates and often respects them for exactly that reason: she is answerable, and if she does harm the village knows where she sleeps. A wise-woman is paid by the household she serves. She sees to the sick of the village and of its flocks, sets bones and delivers children, teaches the herbs, lifts the evil eye (_udurts_) and makes a charm against the dead (_motsar_) for a house that wants one. A Tarvenan goes to her before he goes to a mage.

She asks the sick the Mother's way, in questions answered _yes_ or _no_: does it hurt here, when you breathe in, did you eat the mushrooms from the beech wood. The tale sets her against the town doctor, who asks a patient to tell him all about it and pays more attention to what he wrote than to the patient. The wise-women do not write.

## Caves and Stones {#houses}

A mountain village keeps its cave and its stone, and a few are known beyond their valleys.

- **The cave above Tsaberis**, a mile above the north gates of the drove town, where the shepherds leave their cheese. A wise-woman lives below it and sees to the sick of the town and its flocks.
- **The cave above [[place-zurgando|Zurgando]]**, above the highest house of the valley, where the dead road turns up toward the saddle of the pass.
- **The galleries of Ardalis**, the iron hill's workings, each the Mother's before it is opened.
- **The stone of Tsaberis**, taller than a man and black with the hands that have touched it, in the square before the north gates, where captains read out the season's terms and the men they hire give their names.
- **The stone of [[place-durgalis|Dúrgalis]]**, in the castle town's square, where the valley swore the contract against the hail told of in [[lore-adepthail|The Adept and the Hail]].
- **The stone of [[place-thorgila|Thórgila]]**, in the yard of the horse fair, where every sale at the fair is sworn.
- **The red stone of [[affiliation-goitsathal|Goitsa Thal]]**, in a meadow at the head of the valley above Kávrenath, on which the company's sworn men give their word.
- **The stone of [[place-lezkorahall|the High Hall]]** at [[place-lezkora|Lezkora]], a block of dark rock older than the building, before which the lords name their choice of High King.

## Politics {#present}

The Mother claims no quarrel with the gods, and her faithful keep both. The tale grants the priests every point: Lusinia spun the world, Brazemas makes the storm, and oaths are Árdavon's. The quarrel is the priests'. The priest of Árdavon at the head of a valley teaches that the god hears an oath the first time, and he does not like the walk from his shrine to the stone. The valleys walk it anyway, and the Mother's stone stands in the squares, the fair yards and the High Hall, the places where a Tarvenan's word is given.

Her rival is the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]. Its attempts to regulate arcane practice in Tarvénia meet resistance from polite refusal to open hostility, and many communities would sooner consult a village wise-woman than a mage the order sanctions. The hill farms under Dúrgalis tell their tale of the adept who sold them a contract against the hail as a comedy, because they hold the hail is the Mother's and comes for a lie.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

A party meets the Mother at the stone. A company hiring in Tsaberis, a sale at Thórgila's horse fair or any bargain a Tarvenan means to keep is sworn there, and a stranger who swears there is held to his word before the same witnesses. At Tsaberis this spring a captain has read out terms better than any company has been offered in years, and the older men will not put their hands on the stone. A party's sick or wounded go to the village wise-woman, who will ask questions they can answer _yes_ or _no_. A party that needs to ask the Mother something goes to the cave with an offering and keeps the four rules, and anyone who takes the cheese from a cave mouth has taken it from her.
