## Overview {#overview}

The **Academy of Philosophy and Rhetoric** is one of the three academies of [[place-aureliane|Aurelane]], with the [[affiliation-acadlaw|Academy of Law]] and the [[affiliation-acadmedcn|Academy of Medicine and Manuscripts]], and it is the one a Provénzian means when he says a son has gone to the academy. It teaches natural philosophy, mathematics and rhetoric, and rhetoric above all, because rhetoric is the skill the kingdom rewards most. Its students come from the houses of every chartered city and from across [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], and a family that can afford it puts a son through it whatever he means to do afterward.

## What It Controls {#what-it-controls}

The academy controls who may lecture under its name, and in Provénzia that name is worth a great deal. A man who has read rhetoric at Aurelane is heard differently in a council chamber, a court or a patron's hall. The academy holds a charter of the crown of the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provénzia]], as a chartered city does, which lets its masters choose their own Rector, admit whom they will and license their own readers. The crown names no master.

It teaches the forms of the kingdom's public speech: the oration, the pleading, the letter to a patron and the debate in song. [[lore-orderqstn|The Divided Question of the Order]] is taught here for Serdale's arguments and for the form of Solvima's judgment. Its masters keep the [Festival of Illumination]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} of [[affiliation-marendas|Marendas]] with public lectures, and the faith counts the academy among its works.

## Ranks {#ranks}

A **student** is enrolled on the academy's roll by a master and pays the masters whose lectures he hears. A **reader** is a student licensed to lecture on one book or one art under a master's eye. A **master** is a reader the masters have voted into their number; he teaches in his own right and votes in the choice of the Rector. A student or reader the Rector **sends down** loses the right to lecture under the academy's name, and the sending down is known at Jalnasdal within the month.

## Masters {#masters}

Rector
: [[being-nelamzsatr|Nelameza Sateris]], chosen by the masters for life. She keeps the roll and the charter, sends down the unruly and answers for the academy to the crown and to the city.

## History {#history}

The academy began as the schools of rhetoric of the provincial capital. When the Empire made Aurelane the capital of the province of the West after [[lore-tiravlenbattle|Tiravlen]], about 152 AF, the _therkoi_ needed clerks and advocates who could speak and write [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari]], and the schools of the basilica quarter trained them. In [[lore-charterwar|the War of the Charter]] the peace kept Aurelane's courts and its academies and gave the crown to the coast, and the first kings confirmed the academy's charter rather than move it. The court has been at [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] since, and the academy teaches the sons of the capital how to argue against it.

## The Present Quarrel {#present}

**The salt loan.** The crown's loan against the salt revenue has raised the Charter's oldest question, whether a levy the [[affiliation-councilpeers|Council of Peers]] never voted can be called something else. The crown's lawyers and the Peers' have both brought it before the high courts, and the academy's masters are writing orations for both sides. Nelameza has forbidden no one to write, and she has not said which side she favors. The academy's students are the sons of both parties, and their fathers pay the fees.

**The reformers.** [[being-kiramailthor|Kirama Iltoris]], the countess who leads the Council's reformers, reads the academy's writers on government and cites them in the Peers' hall. The traditionalists among the Peers say the academy teaches the sons of the coast to despise their fathers' charters, and one of them has asked the crown to name a master. The charter forbids it, and the masters have asked the Justiciar to say so.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The unsigned oration.** An oration for the Peers' side of the salt loan is circulating at Jalnasdal under no name, and the crown's lawyers want to know which master wrote it. The Rector does not want it known.
- **A son sent down.** A Peer's son has been sent down for debt, and his father wants the debt paid quietly and the son restored before the Council sits. The masters he owes do not.
