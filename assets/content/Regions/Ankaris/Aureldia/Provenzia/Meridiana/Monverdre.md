**Monvèrdre**—the green hill—sits in the uplands above the warm southern plain, at the point where
the settled, cultivated south runs out and the [[place-thuravelhills|Thúravel Hills]] begin.

It is a small town of perhaps two thousand two hundred, and it has two quite separate lives.

## The Summer Town

From late spring the southern gentry come up out of the coastal heat. Válaren and the southern ports
are magnificent and, in high summer, barely habitable; Monvèrdre is green, watered, and some ten
degrees kinder, and the families who can afford to leave have been leaving for it for centuries.

The town is accordingly far grander than its size warrants—villas along the ridge, a season of
entertainments, prices that triple in June—and for four months of the year it is where a good deal of
southern Provènzia's business is actually settled, over dinners, well away from the city.

## The Last Town

The other life is quieter and older. Monvèrdre is the last place with an inn, a market and a road
before the Thúravel Hills, and everyone going into that country outfits here: shepherds, foresters,
and—increasingly—scholars, antiquarians and agents of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]
looking for the [[place-shrinethuravelhills|shrine]].

The townspeople take their money and give directions of a quality that varies with how the visitor has
behaved. Monvèrdre has a good deal of hill blood in it, keeps one or two observances that its summer
visitors would find surprising, and is careful that they never do.
