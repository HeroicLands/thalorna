**Belpòrte** stands on the warm southern shore and is, with [[place-lunacorte|Lunacòrte]], one of the
two cities whose speech is **Còtière** – the prestige dialect of Provenzal, the form used in literature
and formal commerce, and the one carrying the deepest Vylarian influence.

Where Lunacòrte is a capital that happens to have a harbor, Belpòrte is a harbor and nothing else,
and it is the better port for it. Eighteen thousand people live off the water and the warehouses.

## The Working Port

Belpòrte handles what the kingdom actually ships in quantity: grain, oil, wine, salt out of
[[place-fiordaure|Fiòrdaure]], wool from the western march, timber, and the enormous re-export trade in
Vylarian manufactures moving inland. [[place-valarencity|Válaren]] takes the glamour and the luxury
freight; Belpòrte takes the tonnage.

Its merchant houses are consequently less grand and considerably more liquid than Válaren's, and the
two cities' factors regard each other with a rivalry that is entirely genuine and entirely commercial.
When credit tightens anywhere in southern Provènzia it tightens at Belpòrte first, because Belpòrte is
where the goods are.

## The Port Dialect

The **Parlàge dou Pòrt** – the pidgin-like register that multilingual merchants use among themselves,
mixing Vylari and Élavendri freely – is heard here more than anywhere else in the kingdom, and a good
deal of Belpòrte's daily business is conducted in a speech that is nobody's mother tongue.
