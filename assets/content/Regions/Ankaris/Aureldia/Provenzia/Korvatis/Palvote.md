**Palvote**—the green hill—sits in the uplands above the warm southern plain, at the point where
the settled, cultivated south runs out and the [[place-thuravelhills|Túravel Hills]] begin.

It is a small town of perhaps two thousand two hundred, and it has two quite separate lives.

## The Summer Town

From late spring the southern gentry come up out of the coastal heat. [[place-valarencity|Válaren]] and the southern ports
are magnificent and, in high summer, barely habitable; Palvote is green, watered, and some ten
degrees kinder, and the families who can afford to leave have been leaving for it for centuries.

The town is accordingly far grander than its size warrants—villas along the ridge, a season of
entertainments, prices that triple in June—and for four months of the year it is where a good deal of
business in the southern part of the [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia Region]] is actually settled, over dinners, well away from the city.

## The Last Town

The other life is quieter and older. Palvote is the last place with an inn, a market and a road
before the Túravel Hills, and everyone going into that country outfits here: shepherds, foresters,
and—increasingly—scholars, antiquarians and agents of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]
looking for the [[place-shrinethuravelhills|shrine]].

The townspeople take their money and give directions of a quality that varies with how the visitor has
behaved. Palvote has a good deal of hill blood in it, keeps one or two observances that its summer
visitors would find surprising, and is careful that they never do.

## The Present {#present}

The two lives of the town have begun to meet. This summer the villas on the ridge are full, and so are the inns below them: more scholars, antiquarians and agents of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] have outfitted at Palvote for the Túravel than in any year the innkeepers can remember. Some of them pay hill men to guide them, and the hill families have begun to ask which of their own are taking that money. The summer families have noticed the strangers and not the reason, and the townspeople mean to keep it that way. A guide who took a party into the hills in spring has not come down, and his family has not asked the town for help.
