Above the warm sandy coast of the south, in the richest and most comfortable country in [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]],
lies a field where nothing sings.

## Layers {#history}

Which battle made Kalvenza is not known. The south has been fought over by Tanevar kings, Vilérian legions and the estates' war against the _therkos_, and no record ties any of them to this ground. The district calls it a blood-field and has called it one for as long as anyone has written the name down.

What is recorded is the selling. The land has passed by sale several times, each time to a buyer from outside the region who meant to build on it, and each buyer's plan lapsed without a reason anyone in the district can give.

## The Silence

The **Blood-field of Kalvenza** is a [[lore-bloodfields|blood-field]] of the silent kind. No birds nest there. There are
no insects in the summer grass. Sound carries oddly—not muffled, exactly, but unaccompanied—and
the only noise on the field is weather and whatever the visitor brought with them.

Nothing on it is harmful. Nothing has ever been harmed there in living memory. Animals will graze it
without distress, and shepherds report that they simply prefer not to, and move on sooner than they
intended. So does everybody. People crossing Kalvenza arrive at the far side having walked faster than
they meant to, and are generally unable to say when they began hurrying.

## Its Position

What makes Kalvenza notable is where it is. The southern reaches are the warmest, wealthiest and most
settled part of the kingdom—ports, vineyards, villas, the whole sunlit prosperity that Provénzia is
known for abroad—and in the middle of it is a hundred acres that will not hold a bird.

The land is not built on, for all the buyers it has had, and that is the fact the district notices.
A field this comfortable would be villas and vines anywhere else on the coast.

## What Is There Now {#present}

It has been sold again. A merchant house of [[place-valarencity|Válaren]] bought the field last winter to plant vines, and its steward has hired laborers from the villages around it to clear the ground. The laborers come in the morning and are gone by noon, and none of them can say why they left. The steward is behind his season and his masters are asking why. The villages have stopped taking his money, and the priests of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]], who have never been asked to settle a field that harms nobody, have been asked by the steward whether they would.
