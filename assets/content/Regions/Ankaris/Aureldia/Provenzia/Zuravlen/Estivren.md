**Estivren** sits where the terraces give out and the hill pasture begins. It is the oldest settlement
in [[place-zuravlenrgn|Zûravlen]] — its name belongs to an older stratum than Mercadaure or Blavéra —
and it has never been much interested in peaches.

Use Estivren as the model of an **upland pastoral manor**, and as the reason the valley below works.

## The Manor

- **Demesne.** Little arable: a few fields of rye and oats, and a great deal of **hill pasture**, which
  is the manor's actual wealth.
- **The flocks.** Some four thousand head between the manor and its tenants. The flocks go up to the
  high summer grazing after lambing and come down in autumn, and the shepherds who take them are gone
  for months at a stretch and answer to nobody while they are.
- **Tenancies.** Around thirty holdings, several of them held by **shepherd families** on unusually
  favourable ancient terms that no lord of Estivren has ever managed to revise.
- **Banalities.** A mill and an oven, both small. More importantly, the manor operates the barony's
  principal **fulling** and wool-sorting sheds.
- **Cheese.** Ewe's-milk cheese, pressed in the uplands through the summer and brought down for the
  Mercadaure fair. It is the manor's steadiest coin.

## Folding Rights

The point of Estivren, so far as the rest of the valley is concerned. When the flocks come down in
autumn they must be folded somewhere, and where they are folded is where the dung goes — and the
orchard terraces below cannot bear without it.

Every folding arrangement in the barony is a contract with Estivren, renewed at the fair, priced by
what the orchards can bear to pay, and remembered bitterly. [[place-vergeval|Vergéval]] in particular
is entirely dependent and entirely aware of it. Estivren's lord is not a rich man and does not need to
be; he holds something that cannot be grown in a season.

## Officers and Custom

A **bailiff** and a **reeve**, and — uniquely in the barony — a **head shepherd** whose office is
customary, elective among the shepherd families, and effectively beyond the lord's power to fill. The
**court baron** sits twice a year, and half its business is grazing boundaries with holdings outside
Zûravlen altogether.

## Character

Estivren is conservative, self-sufficient and quietly contemptuous of the valley's obsession with a
fruit that fails. Its people keep older observances than the terraces do, its priest is elderly, and
it sends its share to the frost vigil with visible reluctance.
