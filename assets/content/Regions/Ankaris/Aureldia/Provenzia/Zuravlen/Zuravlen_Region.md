**Zûravlen** is a barony of the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provènzia]], in the
[[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]]—a short, cliff-bound coast and the long river valley behind
it, and the source of the finest peaches in Ankaris and some of its more highly regarded wine.

The barony is held of the Crown at [[place-lunacorte|Lunacorte]] by **Álegar alrí Zûravel**, who is
also Baron of Kývarel, and who sits at [[place-mercadaure|Mercadaure]]. His clan's prominent support of
the Order of the Burning Brand gives a small agricultural barony rather more weight in Provenzian
affairs than its size would suggest.

## The Region and the Barony

**Zûravlen** names two things, and Provenzians rarely bother to distinguish them.

The **region** is the whole of Provènzia's western seaboard—some three-quarters of a million people
along a long cliff coast and the valleys behind it, facing the cold ocean. It is the poorest and
hardest-used quarter of the kingdom: thin soils, few harbors, weather off the water, and none of the
Vylarian trade that made the south rich. Its living is fishing, salt, wool, timber and small mixed
farming, and its people are regarded in the south as dour and in the north as provincial.

The **barony** is one valley of it—Álegar alrí Zûravel's holding, some twelve thousand souls—and it
is why the region has the name at all. Fruit and wine out of that valley are known in every market in
Ankaris, and no other thing produced on the western coast is known anywhere. So the whole seaboard is
called after it, which the rest of the seaboard finds by turns convenient and irritating.

What follows describes the barony, which is what anyone means when they say Zûravlen.

## The Three Bands

Everything about Zûravlen follows from the fact that it is three quite different countries within half
a day's ride of each other, and that the middle one is where the money is.

**The coast.** Zûravlen faces **west**, onto the open ocean—not the warm
[[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] that laps Provènzia's southern shore, but the vast cold water beyond
the continent's western edge. Rocky cliffs stand above deep water and the wind off it is constant:
hard enough in winter to lay a man down, salt enough year-round to burn the leaves off anything
tender. Nothing worth exporting grows here. What the coast has is fish—and it has them in
extraordinary quantity, the cold water being far richer than the Vylarian—along with salt, and the
only harbor, at [[place-portvent|Portvent]], reached by a cut in the cliff.

**The valley.** Behind the coastal ridge the wind drops away and the land turns warm—hot, in high
summer, in a way the shore never is. The contrast is the barony's defining fact and is severe enough
to be strange: a cold sea on one side of a ridge and ripening peaches on the other, four miles apart. This is the sheltered middle country, terraced over four hundred
years into south-facing benches, watered by the channels of the _règa_, and given wholly to **peaches
and vines**. It is perhaps a tenth of the barony's ground and rather more than half its wealth.

**The uplands.** Above the terraces the soil thins into pasture, chestnut and scrub, running up to the
summer grazing.

The gradient is sharp enough that Zûravlen keeps two calendars and, in a sense, two peoples: the
valley reckons the year by blossom and vintage, and the coast reckons it by the weather.

## Water and Wind

The valley's wealth is made by **water**. A system of channels—the _règa_—carries river water along
the terrace lines to every orchard and vineyard in turn, and water-turns are allotted by custom,
recorded at Mercadaure, and the single most litigated property in the barony. A family's water-turn is
worth more than its house.

The coast's living is made by **wind**, and grudgingly. It drives the salt pans dry, it dries the fish,
and it keeps the harbor honest by making it unusable perhaps forty days a year. It also stops at the
ridge, which is the whole reason the barony is rich: the same wind that makes the shore useless for
fruit is what the valley is sheltered from.

### The Fog

Cold water against a hot land makes fog, and through the summer it comes in off the ocean most
mornings, drowns Portvent to the masts, climbs the cliff—and stops at the ridge. The valley watches
it arrive and go no further almost every day of the growing season.

Coastal Zûravlen therefore lives half the summer in a gray chill while the terraces four miles inland
are baking, which does more than anything else to keep the two halves of the barony from understanding
one another.

## The Peach

The trees are grafted, not grown from stone. Zûravlen stock is worked onto **almond root**, which
tolerates the dry stony ground of the upper terraces where peach root will not thrive, and the grafting
is done in winter by men whose families have done nothing else for generations. A tree comes into
bearing in its fourth year and is finished by its twentieth, so a third of the barony's orchards are
always young, and an orchard's age is the first thing any Zûravlen will tell you about it.

**Fresh fruit does not travel**—overland. A peach picked at Vergéval is worth a fortune in Válaren
and worthless four days later, which for most of the barony's history meant the fresh trade was a
prestige trade and nothing more. What left the valley in quantity was preserved:

- **Dried halves**, stoned and laid on cane trays in the drying yards, which keep a year and travel
  anywhere.
- **Fruit in honey**, sealed in crocks—the luxury export, and the reason Zûravlen honey is never sold
  outside the barony.
- **Kernels**, cracked from the stones and sold to confectioners and apothecaries for their bitter
  almond flavor.
- **Peach spirit**, distilled in small quantities under the apothecaries' eye, expensive and medicinal.

**Portvent has changed that**, and the barony has not finished arguing about it. A fast boat out of the
harbor reaches ports no rider could, and fresh Zûravlen fruit now appears on tables that had only ever
seen it dried. Every basket that leaves fresh is a basket that does not pay the baron's drying share—which is his largest single income—so the harbor and the yards are, quietly, in competition, and
everyone concerned is aware of who owns which.

## The Vine

Vines climb the same terraces the peaches do, generally above them where the ground is stoniest and the
fruit trees will not thrive, and Zûravlen wine is well regarded across Provènzia without ever quite
being fashionable in Lunacorte.

The vine is the barony's answer to its own worst problem. **Vintage falls in autumn, after the peach
harvest is in**, so the two crops share terraces and terracing without ever competing for hands at the
same moment; the wine keeps and travels, so it does not depend on the drying yards, the fast boats, or
anybody's goodwill; and a vine bears for a lifetime where a peach tree is finished in twenty years. In
a frost year—when the blossom is lost and there is no fruit at all—the vines usually come through,
because they break bud later than the peach does.

Zûravlen's orchardists regard the vines as insurance and say so. Its vignerons regard that as an
insult and have said so for four hundred years.

## The Rest of the Year

The peach bears for eight weeks. The barony eats for fifty-two, and an outsider who thinks Zûravlen is
an orchard has seen it in August.

- **Grain**—wheat and barley on the valley floor, which is what the barony actually lives on.
- **Wool and cheese**—flocks go up to the hill pastures for the summer and come down in autumn. Wool
  is the steadiest income in the barony and the most reliable, since it does not spoil and does not
  care about frost. Ewe's-milk cheese is pressed in the uplands and sold at the autumn fair.
- **Fish and salt**—the coastal living. Salt from the pans above Portvent goes to the fish, to the
  cheese, and increasingly into the preserving trades inland, which had bought it from outside for
  generations and resents having a local monopoly instead.
- **Honey and wax**—bee-boles beside every orchard. Nobody in Zûravlen will explain why beyond that
  it has always been done, and the honey is needed for the preserving in any case. The wax goes to the
  churches.
- **Olives** on the lower slopes, for local use rather than export.
- **Osier**—willow beds along the river, cut and woven into the baskets and trays the harvest is
  impossible without. Basketry is a winter trade and employs half the valley.
- **Chestnut** in the uplands, for flour, fodder and timber.

**The manure question** is the barony's permanent quarrel. Orchard terraces are hungry and must be
dunged; the dung is in the flocks; the flocks belong to men who are not orchardists. Every manor's
custom sets out where and when the sheep must be folded after they come down, and every generation
argues about it.

## The Frost Vigil

A late frost during blossom destroys the year, and everyone in the valley knows it. On nights when
frost threatens, the bells at Mercadaure are rung and the whole valley turns out to light **smudge
fires** along the terrace rows—damp straw and prunings, banked to smoke rather than burn—and to
keep them going until dawn. From the ridge the valley appears to be alight from end to end.

It is the sheltered ground that frosts. Still, clear nights let the cold air drain off the uplands and
pool on the valley floor, while the coast a few miles away—wind-stirred and warmed by the sea—scarcely frosts at all. So the vigil is the valley's business and not the shore's, and the people of
Portvent are not called to it and do not come. The valley has never entirely forgiven them for a
distinction neither side chose.

The vigil is a religious occasion as much as an agricultural one. Priests of
[[affiliation-florania|Flórania]] walk the rows through the night, and it is bad luck and worse manners
to refuse a call. Baron Álegar's people note, without ever quite saying it, how well the practice sits
with the Order of the Burning Brand.

## Faith

Provènzia is [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]], and Zûravlen's devotion falls naturally to two of
the Twelve: **[[affiliation-venusia|Vénusia]]**, the Bountiful One, whose symbol is a tree hung with
golden fruit and who is honored at harvest with the first-picked basket of every orchard; and
**[[affiliation-florania|Flórania]]**, the Nurturer, who owns the blossom and the vigil. A Zûravlen
will tell you the two goddesses divide the year between them, and that Vénusia gets the credit for what
Flórania saved.

Portvent keeps its own observances, which are for the weather and the sea and are not much discussed
inland.

## Settlements

- **[[place-mercadaure|Mercadaure]]**—the market town and the baron's seat, where the fruit is
  graded, packed and sold, and where the autumn fair is held.
- **[[place-portvent|Portvent]]**—the harbor at the foot of the cliff: fish, salt, and the fast
  boats that are quietly undoing the drying yards.
- **[[place-vergeval|Vergéval]]**—the great orchard manor of the upper terraces, and the richest and
  most anxious place in the barony.
- **[[place-blavera|Blavéra]]**—a mixed manor of the valley floor: grain, fold and a little fruit.
  The ordinary model, and the one most of Zûravlen actually resembles.
- **[[place-estivren|Estivren]]**—the upland sheep manor, oldest of the three, poorest in coin and
  least interested in peaches.

## Adventuring in Zûravlen

The barony is small, rich, and utterly dependent on a crop that can be destroyed in one cold night—which makes it unusually easy to hurt and unusually worth hurting. Water-turns, folding rights,
drying-yard shares, salt, the harbor cut and the grading table at Mercadaure are all worth killing
over on a small scale, and all of them are written down somewhere that could be altered. See
[[scenario-pechszrvln|The Peaches of Zûravlen]].
