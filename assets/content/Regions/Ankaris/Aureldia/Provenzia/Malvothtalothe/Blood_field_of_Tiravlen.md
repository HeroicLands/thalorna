On the shore of [[place-tiravellake|Lake Tiravel]], which gives it its name, lies the second of
the [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia Region]]'s named [[lore-bloodfields|blood-fields]]—and the one most people have actually seen,
since the lake road runs along its edge and nobody has ever thought to move the road.

## Layers {#history}

**The battle.** The legions broke the Tanevar kings on this shore about 152 AF, and the ones who ran went into the water ([[lore-tiravlenbattle|Tiravlen]]). The Vilérian dispatch that named the battle burned with the imperial archive, and the kings left no record of their own.

**The custom.** The villages have kept the anniversary as an observance for longer than any of them can count, and they call the ground by its name without saying whose battle it was.

## A Recurrence

Tiravlen replays, and it replays **over the water**, which is what makes it locally famous. The battle
that made it, [[lore-tiravlenbattle|Tiravlen]] (the breaking of the Tanevar kings) in about 152 AF, is known to the villages by the ground's name alone. On the
anniversary, the night of 9 [Karnavar]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, and on a scatter of other nights that follow no pattern anyone has established, lights
move out across the lake in ranks, and sound carries off the surface with the particular clarity that
water gives it—orders, horses, the noise of a line breaking—all of it perfectly audible from
villages a mile off and never from anywhere close enough to see.

Nothing has ever come ashore. The harm is a lesser field's harm, done at night and to the mind: a
household that keeps watch over the water on those nights sleeps badly for weeks, and the lakeside
villages remember people who watched too often and never came back to themselves.

## The Observance

The lakeside villages long ago stopped treating this as a crisis and turned it into a custom. On the
night of 9 Karnavar the boats are drawn up, the shutters go closed at dusk, and households sit the night out
together with the fire lit; in the morning there is a small offering at the water and the year goes
on. Priests of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] attend where they are invited and have never been
asked to perform the rite of settlement—the villages are firm that the field is not troublesome and
that the observance is theirs.

Visitors who insist on watching from the shore are not prevented. They are simply not accompanied.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The observance is in question for the first time in living memory. A fisherman's son watched from the water last year instead of keeping the night indoors, and he has not come back to himself. The village nearest the field wants him through this year's night in the old way, with the household around the fire and the offering at the water in the morning. Outsiders want other things: a priest of Sélmoros wants to perform the rite of settlement at last, and a Tanevar man from the hills wants to hear what the boy says when the lights come out over the water.
