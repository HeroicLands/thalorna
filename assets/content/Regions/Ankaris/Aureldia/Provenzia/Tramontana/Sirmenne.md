Deep in the borderlands between the [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]] and
[[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], in the wooded country of **[[place-tramontana|Tramontàna]]** below the Áelendan
mountains, stand the crumbling ruins of **Sirmennë**.

They predate any Provenzian settlement, and any Élavendren one, by centuries. What Sirmennë was built
for is not known, and the two peoples who care most about it do not agree. The name is Sinalë—the
hearth of the first stone—and the Áelendan use it too, having none of their own for the place.

## A Shared Site

Sirmennë is sacred to the [[affiliation-aelndntrbs|Áelendan Tribes]]—the reclusive human tribes who
are the original people of Élavendre and who hold the northern mountains whatever the maps say—and
also to the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] who dwell in the hidden forests nearby.

This is unusual to the point of being singular. The two peoples share very little, and the arrangement
at Sirmennë is not a partnership so much as a long-standing mutual restraint: each observes the site
in its own way, each acknowledges the other's right to be there, and neither has ever explained the
arrangement to an outsider who asked.

Neither crown administers it. Provènzia's claim is formally unambiguous and practically theoretical.

## The Withdrawal

Local legend holds that the Sinalë **withdrew** from Sirmennë, deeper into their enclaves, and left
things behind when they went—artifacts of considerable power, abandoned rather than removed.

Nothing has ever been produced to support this and the rumor has never weakened, which is the usual
condition of such stories and no argument against them. It is enough to bring a steady trickle of
treasure-seekers, scholars and agents of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] into country
where both the Áelendan and the Sinalë take a direct interest in visitors.

The Áelendan do not much distinguish between a looter and a licensed antiquarian. See
[[scenario-engmsrmnn|The Enigma of Sirmennë]].
