A battlefield of Provènzia remembered as a blood-field—ground where an engagement was fought at such
cost that the name stuck to the place rather than to the battle. Near
[[place-yharvalenhenge|Yhârvalen Henge]].

It lies in **[[place-tramontana|Tramontàna]]**, on the low country inland of the [[place-batarengulf|Gulf of Batáren]]—a day's march from where a fleet can put an army ashore, which is why an army was ever
brought to meet one there.

It is the worst of Provènzia's [[lore-bloodfields|blood-fields]], and the one every Provenzian can
name.

## A Withering

Most blood-fields are frightening and harmless. Yhârvalen is neither. It is a **withering**: the land
does not merely remember the battle, it dies of it. Crops fail, beasts sicken, the soil turns sour,
and—unlike every other resonance recorded—the harm does not stay on the field. It spreads.

For centuries it lay dormant, which the districts around it mistook for healing and which was in fact
only quiet. That has ended.

## The Henge

Yhârvalen is unusual in that the killing was not the whole of the injury. The Nordheim raids that swept
through northern Provènzia some two hundred years ago **threw down the henge** as well as the men, and
the standing opinion among those who study such things is that a blood-field made on the ruins of a
sacred circle is a different and worse proposition than one made in an open field—that the boundary
there was already answerable to the stones, and that breaking both at once left nothing to hold it.

Whether that is true is not established. It is, however, the only account that explains why this field
withers and the others do not.

## The Black Flame

The curse has been awakened, and it was awakened on purpose. The
[[affiliation-blackflame|Cult of the Black Flame]] has been rousing and magnifying it, drawing power
from the destruction—a blood-field being a permanent reservoir of exactly what that faith holds
sacred. The dying of the surrounding country is not incidental to their working. It is the working.
