## Overview {#overview}

The Council of Peers is the council of the greater nobility of the [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Kingdom of Provénzia]], and it sits with the king at [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]]. The [[lore-chrtrrghts|Charter of Rights]] binds the king to take its counsel, and in practice its counsel is required on three things: war, the great offices and the crown's grants of land and office. A levy needs the estates' consent, and the crown asks for it in the Council, where the deputies of the chartered cities come to give or refuse it. The Council has no word on the succession, which the Charter never settled.

Its present is the long contest with the crown over prerogative, taxation and appointment, which neither side has won, and the rivalry between the Earl of Degavel and the Count of Atavel, which most often leaves the Council without a majority.

## Who Sits {#who-sits}

Every earl, every count and every prince or princess of the blood who holds land of the crown directly is a Peer, summoned by right to every sitting. A baron who holds of an earl does not sit, so the Baron of Zuravlen is answered for in the Council by his overlord, the Earl of Hárivren. The great officers attend when the king summons them, and the Chancellor reads the king's business whether he is a Peer or not.

First Peer
: [[being-rutalplnvr|Rutal Pélanvri]], Earl of [[place-degavel|Degavel]], chosen by the Peers to call the sittings in the king's absence, keep the order of business and count the voices.

The Peers who decide the Council's majority are these:

- [[being-rutalplnvr|Rutal Pélanvri]], Earl of Degavel, the leading lord of the south and the head of one party.
- [[being-velzoreaitv|Velzore Aitevis]], Count of [[place-athavel|Atavel]], who also holds the great seal as Chancellor and heads the other party.
- [[being-avaarlzlvrn|Avaarl Zilavren]], Earl of Hárivren, who holds the western march and belongs to neither party.
- [[being-itrolaldrvn|Itrola Lédravren]], Princess of [[place-gwefavel|Gefavel]], who sits by right of her blood and whose petition is before the Council.
- [[being-kiramailthor|Kirama Iltoris]], a countess and the leader of the Council's reformist faction, who wants chartered rights extended to more towns.
- [[being-melvrelvns|Melvare Elvanis]], Count of [[place-ravenshold|Ravenshold]], who holds aloof from both parties.

## How It Decides {#how-it-decides}

The king summons the Council, and it sits in the Peers' hall at Jalnasdal for as long as his business takes. When the king is present he puts his business himself, through the Chancellor; when he is absent the First Peer calls the sitting and keeps the order. Each Peer speaks in order of precedence, the princes of the blood first, and the First Peer puts the question. A matter carries on the voices of a majority of the Peers present. When the two great parties are evenly matched and the uncommitted Peers abstain, nothing carries, and the king may not act on a matter the Charter puts to the Council.

A levy is decided differently. The Chancellor sets out what the crown asks and why, the deputies of the chartered cities are heard, and the Peers and the deputies together give the estates' consent or refuse it. The Peers choose the First Peer by the same voices at the year's first sitting.

## What It Is Fighting About {#present}

**The First Peer.** Rutal Pélanvri holds the office, and the Atavel party needs one more voice to take it from him at the year's first sitting. The Earl of Hárivren has not declared, and envoys from both parties have been to Hárivren City.

**The Gefavel petition.** Itrola Lédravren holds that [[being-kingvelarikiii|Vélarik III]] resumed her branch's grants by writ without the Council's counsel, which the Charter requires for the crown's grants, and asks the Council to declare the resumption void. [[being-kralenldrvn|Kralen Lédravren]] keeps the lands because they pay for his household, and the Chancellor, who would lose the seal if the petition succeeded in full, keeps the writ.

**The crown's money.** The frontier and the ships cost more than the crown takes. The king would rather borrow against the salt revenue than ask the Council for a levy, and the Peers whose kin sit in the coastal houses know exactly what that means.

**The reformers.** Kirama Iltoris and her faction press to extend chartered rights to more towns. Traditionalists among the Peers and in the old houses of the coast oppose every such extension, and the reformers use the minor nobles of the towns, [[being-arnelkrsvr|Arnela Kórisvar]] among them, to carry what they cannot champion themselves.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

Nobody petitions the Council directly. A petition reaches it through a Peer who will speak it, through the Chancellor if it concerns the crown's business, or through a city's deputies if it concerns a levy. A party meets the Council in the anterooms of the Peers' hall, where the Peers' households, the cities' deputies, the merchants and the ambassadors wait in the same rooms, and where a great deal is decided before any Peer stands up.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **One more voice.** [[being-velzoreaitv|The Count of Atavel]] needs one Peer to take the office of First Peer, and the [[being-avaarlzlvrn|Earl of Hárivren]] has not declared.
- **The writ.** [[being-itrolaldrvn|The Princess of Gefavel]] needs a sight of Vélarik III's writ of resumption before her petition is heard.
