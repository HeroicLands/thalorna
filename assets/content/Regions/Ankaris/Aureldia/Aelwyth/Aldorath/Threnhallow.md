## Overview

At the seaward margin of the [[place-greylevels|Greylevels]], past the last drained grazing and beyond the reach of the commissions' newer banks, a low mound stands up out of the reeds with a stone house on it. A causeway of laid stone runs out to it across the flats, and the tide covers the causeway twice a day. The house is a chapel with a squat bell-tower, a cloister of guest cells, a chapter room and a crypt cut down into the mound below the tide line. The Levels call it **Threnhallow**, the holy place of lament. Its roof is mostly sound. Nobody has kept it for years.

## The House

Threnhallow is a house of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]], the Silent Judge. The Levels lose people to the water every year: pilots on the flats, fowlers in the channels, children at the dyke-edge, sailors off ships that misjudged the bar below [[place-greywater|Greywater]]. Their bodies come ashore days or weeks later, far from any churchyard, and the house was built to receive them. Its brethren, a Threshold Keeper and a handful of Novices of the Silent Watch, gave the drowned their rites, buried them in the crypt, and kept the names.

The house kept the faith's silence. Its only voice was its bell, hung not in the tower but in a shaft above the crypt, low in the mound, so that it rang beneath the floor and beneath the tide. The brethren rang it once for each of the drowned brought in, for the dead to hear and not the living. The fowlers of the Levels, out on the flats at night, heard it through the water and through the boards of their boats, and took it as a sign that one of their own had been found.

The house kept a sluice as well. It sits at the outfall of an old drainage cut, and an endowment from the Levels' commission of sewers paid the brethren to work the sluice-gates at the ebb, keeping the cut clear and the inland grazing dry. The house's drainage plans, its sluice-book and the commission's endowment chest are all kept on the mound.

## What Happened Here

The commission's endowment failed in one of its long disputes, and the house could not pay for labour on the gates. The sea came back over the lower grazing, and more of the Levels drowned in a year than in ten before it. The Threshold Keeper of that time, **Keeper Leofwald**, had more dead in his crypt than brethren to bury them and no hands for the gates.

He altered the rite. The rite of the Silent Judge speaks the names of the dead so that the god guides them through his gate; Leofwald's rite spoke their names and bade them keep the house. The drowned rose in the crypt and worked the sluice-gates in the dark, and the grazing stayed dry for a season. What came through the opening he made was the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]], and it did not close behind him. When Leofwald died, his novices sang his own rite over him, as he had told them to, and he rose too.

The house has been empty of the living since. The bell rings beneath the tide, and not for the found. It rings before someone is taken.

## Who Knows, and What They Say

- **The fowlers and pilots of the Levels** know the bell and say that when it rings with no body brought in, somebody on the flats will not come home. They do not take the causeway after dark, and a pilot who must pass the mound at night ships his oars and drifts.
- **The commission of sewers** carries the house's endowment on its rolls as unpaid and in dispute, and its clerks argue over whose fault the sluice is.
- **The faith of the Silent Judge** lists Threnhallow among its houses and has not sent a Threshold Keeper to it in a generation. A priest of the faith asked about it says only that the house's rites are a matter for the faith.
- **The house's own guests' book,** kept in the chapter room, runs on for some years after the last brother signed it, in different hands, and then stops.

## Reaching It

The causeway runs out from the end of a sewer-bank, a long half-hour's walk across the flats, and is passable for some hours either side of low water. At high water the mound is an island, reached only by boat across channels that move from season to season. Pilots of the Levels know the channels and will take a party out for a fee, by day. A maintenance embankment of the commission runs from the mound's landward side back toward inhabited country, overgrown and broken in places, and passable on foot at any tide by anyone who knows it is there.

## Hooks

- **The endowment chest.** The commission of sewers wants its chest and its rolls back from the mound, to settle a dispute, and pays well enough to tempt somebody.
- **A pilot's son.** A pilot's son rowed a pair of travellers out to the mound at dusk and did not come back. His father heard the bell.
- **The reliquary.** A Levels family left a jewelled reliquary of the healers of [[affiliation-lusinia|Lúsinía]] in the house's keeping, and wants it brought home.
