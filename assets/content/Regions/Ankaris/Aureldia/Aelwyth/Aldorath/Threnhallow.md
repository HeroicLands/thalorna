## Overview

At the seaward margin of [[place-greylevels|The Greylevels]], past the last drained grazing and beyond the reach of the commissions' newer banks, a low mound stands up out of the reeds with a stone house on it. A causeway of laid stone runs out to it across the flats, and the tide covers the causeway twice a day. The house is a chapel with a squat bell-tower, a cloister of guest cells, a chapter room and a crypt cut down into the mound below the tide line. The Levels call it **Threnhallow**, the holy place of lament. Its roof is mostly sound. Nobody has kept it for years.

## The House

Threnhallow is a house of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]], whose god is known as the Silent Judge. The Levels lose people to the water every year: pilots on the flats, fowlers in the channels, children at the dike-edge, sailors off ships that misjudged the bar below [[place-greywater|Greywater]]. Their bodies come ashore days or weeks later, far from any churchyard, and the house was built to receive them. Its brethren, a Threshold Keeper and a handful of Novices of the Silent Watch, gave the drowned their rites, buried them in the crypt, and kept the names.

The house kept the faith's silence. Its only voice was its bell, hung not in the tower but in a shaft above the crypt, low in the mound, so that it rang beneath the floor and beneath the tide. The brethren rang it once for each of the drowned brought in, for the dead to hear and not the living. The fowlers of the Levels, out on the flats at night, heard it through the water and through the boards of their boats, and took it as a sign that one of their own had been found. A ladder runs from the bell-loft at the top of the tower down the shaft to the oak beam the bell hangs from.

Every room of the house has an iron drain-grate in its floor. At high water the sea stands in the drains, and at a spring tide it comes up through the joints of the chapel's flagstones.

The house's guests' book lies in the chapter room. Travelers who sheltered on the mound wrote their names and where they came from, and the brethren signed it in their own form, a name followed by the words "keepeth the house". The book runs on for years after the last brother signed it, in many different hands, most of them unsteady, and every entry after his is in the brethren's form. One name comes back among the later entries twelve times, [Leofwald]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s, in one hand that is a little less steady each time. The last leaves are nearly full.

:::secret {title="The guests' book"}
The later hands are the house's new keepers, each signing on as a brother of the house signs on entering. Every name after the last brother's is someone the drowned took, drowned in the crypt and raised, save Leofwald's own. The Keeper signs again each time he rises, and his twelve entries are his twelve risings, each in a newer and worse body than the last. The newest entry is the most recent taking, and its ink is fresh.
:::

## The Sluice

The house keeps a sluice as well. It sits at the outfall of an old drainage cut beneath the mound, worked by an iron wheel and gear at the far end of the crypt, and an endowment from the Levels' commission of sewers paid the brethren to work the gates at the ebb, keeping the cut clear and the inland grazing dry. The house's drainage plans are pinned in the chapter room, and its sluice-book and the commission's endowment chest are kept on the mound. The plans show the drains of every room running down to the crypt, the crypt emptying through the sluice, and a note in the margin that the gates must be opened at every ebb and shut before the flood or the cut backs up and the lower grazing floods.

The cut runs clear past the mound to the sea. Behind it, the lower grazing is dry, and the steadings on it show lamps at night.

:::secret {title="The sluice-wheel"}
The wheel is rusted on its rim and bright on its handles, and the gear is greased with tallow from the chapel's candles. The drowned work the gates at every ebb, and nobody living has turned the wheel since the last novice left the mound.

> [!TIP]
> Anyone who reaches the wheel at the ebb sees the handles before they see anything else. Describe the bright iron plainly and leave the reading to the players.

:::

## What the Levels Say

- **The fowlers and pilots of the Levels** know the bell and say that when it rings with no body brought in, somebody on the flats will not come home. They say the dead of the house come up out of the flats and call in the voices of those taken before, and that whoever answers is the next the bell rings for. They do not take the causeway after dark, and a pilot who must pass the mound at night ships his oars and drifts.
- **The Levels' telling of the house** is that the commission's endowment failed in one of its long disputes, the house could not pay for labor on its gates, and the sea came back over the lower grazing, so that more of the Levels drowned in a year than in ten before it. **Keeper Leofwald**, the Threshold Keeper of that time, had more dead in his crypt than brethren to bury them, and he altered the rite so that the drowned rose to work the gates. The telling says [[lore-theshadow|The Shadow]] came in by the opening he made, and that his novices raised him with his own rite when he died.
- **The Levels' telling of the Keeper since** is that he has been killed more than once: by a fowler's spear out on the flats, by a priest of the Silent Judge whom the commission paid to go out to the mound, and by the Levels' own people after a hard winter. They say the Keeper always comes back, and the water with him, and they have let him be since.
- **The graziers behind the cut** say the lower grazing has stayed dry since the house went quiet, and that the commission takes the credit for it.
- **The commission of sewers** carries the house's endowment on its rolls as unpaid and in dispute. Its clerks say the newer banks keep the lower grazing dry, and argue over whose fault the sluice is.
- **The faith of the Silent Judge** lists Threnhallow among its houses and has not sent a Threshold Keeper to it in a generation. A priest of the faith asked about it says only that the house's rites are a matter for the faith.

## Reaching It

The causeway runs out from the end of a sewer-bank, a long half-hour's walk across the flats, and is passable for some hours either side of low water. At high water the mound is an island, reached only by boat across channels that move from season to season. Pilots of the Levels know the channels and will take a party out for a fee, by day. A maintenance embankment of the commission runs from the mound's landward door back toward inhabited country, overgrown and broken in places, and passable on foot at any tide by anyone who knows it is there. From the embankment the outfall, the cut and the lower grazing behind it lie in plain view.

## Hooks

- **The endowment chest.** The commission of sewers wants its chest and its rolls back from the mound, to settle a dispute, and pays well enough to tempt somebody.
- **A pilot's son.** A pilot's son rowed a pair of travelers out to the mound at dusk and did not come back. His father heard the bell.
- **The reliquary.** A Levels family left a jeweled reliquary of the healers of the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] in the house's keeping, and wants it brought home.

# The Truth of Threnhallow {#truth .secret}

**The bad year.** The Levels' telling of the house is true as far as it goes. When the endowment failed and the sea came back over the lower grazing, Keeper Leofwald altered the rite of the Silent Judge. The true rite speaks the names of the dead so that, in the faith's teaching, the god guides them through his gate; Leofwald's rite speaks their names and bids them keep the house, in a call and an answer: "Who keepeth the house?" and "I keep it." The drowned rose in the crypt as [[being-damut|damutu]] and worked the sluice-gates in the dark, and the Shadow came through the opening he made and did not close behind him. When Leofwald died, his novices sang his own rite over him, as he had told them to, and he rose a [[being-tereb|tereb]]. The novices left the mound soon after, and the faith has sent nobody since.

**The gates, every ebb since.** The drowned did not stop when the bad year ended. They have worked the sluice at every ebb since Leofwald raised them: at the turn of the tide they leave whatever water they stand in and go down to the wheel, open the gates so that the cut runs out to the sea, and wind them shut and wedge them before the flood. The crypt and the house's drains empty through the sluice when the gates are open, and nowhere else. The lower grazing has stayed dry for a generation because of them, save in the months after each of Leofwald's deaths, and the commission's newer banks do not reach it.

**Why the bell rings.** Every damut wears out, and the house's drowned are no exception. The sea's own dead reach the mound days or weeks after they drowned, already far gone, and serve a few months before their hands will no longer grip the wheel. A living soul drowned in the crypt and raised at once by the altered rite rises with its wits, its strength and its hands, and keeps the gates for a year or more. So when a keeper wears past working, Leofwald rings the bell at the flood, and the drowned go out through the drains and along the water for a living one. The one they take is held under in the crypt, raised, and signs the guests' book as a new brother of the house. The drowned call through the drains in the voices of the brethren and of those taken before, and a living soul who answers them is counted among the keepers; the next bell is rung for that one.

**The drowned.** The house's drowned are bound to its water by the altered rite. They come only where the water comes: up the drains, through the flagstones at high tide, along the wet stone. Out of the water they cannot walk, and a body dragged clear of it falls still. Undriven by the bell they stand idle and slowly sink, and no gate is worked. Their palms are worn raw on the wheel's iron and rust-red to the wrist. The newest of them, freshly drowned, can pass for living in the dark and believes for a while that he still is.

**Leofwald.** The Keeper keeps his Hallowed Ground in the flooded crypt, and stands in the water there with the bell-rope in his hand. He is grave, gentle and patient, and speaks the older speech of the house. Close to, his face does not quite hold, and the courtesy slips. The face is his thirteenth: every body he has risen in has been a little worse than the one before. He believes he saved the Levels and is saving them still, and he is right on both counts. He cannot be talked out of the taking, which he holds is the price of the grazing, but he can be talked to, and he answers what he is asked about the gates truthfully and at length.

**Twelve deaths.** The Levels have destroyed Leofwald twelve times in the generation since the bad year, each time away from the crypt: a fowler's spear on the flats, a priest of the Silent Judge whom the commission paid, who caught him in the chapel and spoke the true rite over him, and the Levels' own people after a hard winter, among others. Each time, the drowned stood idle with nobody to ring for them, the gates stayed shut, and the next spring tide came in through the cut and over the lower grazing, and the grazing stayed under until he rose again in the crypt months later and the drowned went back down to the wheel. The Levels have let him be since the twelfth. His drowned brought each lost body home, and he laid each in a niche beside the crypt gate and closed it to the shoulder with mortar: twelve in a row, in order, one height and one build, the later ones the more ruined. Each time he rose he signed the guests' book again. He is on his thirteenth and last life and knows it, and he cannot afford a fight: he keeps his drowned and the deep water between himself and any threat, and he talks rather than risk himself.

**What breaking the house does.** The true rite of the Silent Judge, spoken over a damut, lays it down, and spoken over Leofwald with his name it unmakes him, for good. Destroyed now, by the rite or by any other means, he does not rise again. The true rite's last line is cut on the lintel of the crypt gate, and the struck lines in the vestry rite-book still read. Silencing the bell, unmaking Leofwald or laying the drowned down each ends the takings, and each stops the gates. Left unworked, the cut backs up within a few ebbs, and the next spring tide comes in through the gates and over the lower grazing, as it did in the bad year. A drained crypt strands the drowned on the mud, still, but somebody had to open the gates to drain it.

**The remedy.** The commission's endowment chest lies in a sealed niche of the crypt above the old flood line. It holds the deeds of the endowment, the rolls of the dispute and the coin of the years it was paid. Carried to the commission, the deeds settle what the commission owes the house, and the coin pays living hands for the gates while the commission argues about the rest.

**The ways in.** The vestry passage runs down a wet stair to the bell-chamber, where the bell hangs from its oak beam over the shaft into the crypt. The bell-loft ladder comes down the same shaft to the beam, so the rope can be cut from above without entering the crypt. The landward door opens on the maintenance embankment, which reaches dry ground at any tide. In niches above the old flood line, sealed with lead and wax, lie the house's silver vessels, the endowment chest and the reliquary of Lusinia's healers left in the house's keeping.

> [!TIP]
> Present the drowned as workers before they are hunters. Whenever the tide turns while they are in view, they break off whatever they are doing and go down toward the wheel, unhurried. Describe it each time and let the pattern build.

> [!WARNING]
> The fastest ways to end the takings are also the ways to flood the Levels. Let the party learn which is which before it acts, by what it sees at the turn, and let the consequence follow from what it knew when it chose.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The bell before a taking.** True. Leofwald rings it to send the drowned for a living keeper.
- **Whoever answers is next.** True. An answer is the altered rite's "I keep it", and it counts the one who gives it.
- **The Levels' telling of the house.** True, and short of the whole: the drowned did not work the gates for the bad year only. They have worked them every ebb since.
- **The commission's newer banks.** False. The banks do not reach the lower grazing, and the grazing is dry because of the sluice.
- **The Keeper always comes back, and the water with him.** True of his twelve deaths, and backward about the water: the sea came over the grazing while he was gone, because nothing drove the drowned to the gates, and went off it when he rose. False of the next death, which is final.
- **The graziers' remark.** True as observed. The house went quiet when the Levels stopped going out against the Keeper, and the gates have been worked every ebb since, by nobody living.
