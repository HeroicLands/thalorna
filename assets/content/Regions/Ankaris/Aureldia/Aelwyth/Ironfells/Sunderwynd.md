## Overview

The southern approach to the more easterly of the two [[place-ironfells|Ironfells]] passes climbs for a day through steep, sodden forest and then enters a gorge, a narrow cleft with a torrent at the bottom and a trail cut along one wall. At the gorge's upper end, on a crag that overhangs the trail, stands **Sunderwynd**: a square stone tower, a walled yard, a gatehouse and a barrack range with arrow-slits on the gorge side. The name is the gorge's, the split way between the hills. Every pack-pony that crosses the pass from the south goes under the post's walls, and the post collects the crown's toll on it.

Sunderwynd is an outpost of [[place-caerwynd|Caerwynd]], which watches the southern ends of the passes. Its garrison is a captain and a score of men, relieved by a patrol up from Caerwynd at intervals, and its life is weather, smugglers and the occasional skirmish with the jarl's men at the pass-head ([[place-sunderfells|the Sunderfells]]).

## The Post

The tower holds the captain's room and, above it, the signal platform; between the two, under the platform's timbers, is a low roof-room reached by a ladder and a trap in the captain's ceiling. The barrack range holds the men, the kitchen and the stores. Beneath the yard is a cistern, a vault on squat pillars cut into the crag, which takes the rain off the roofs and holds the post's water through a siege. A drainage tunnel runs from the cistern's low end through the crag and out onto the gorge face below the trail. Behind the cistern's far wall is the pay vault, which holds the toll and the garrison's silver; a captain walled it up during a raid from over the pass and died in the fighting. The daybooks record every search the garrison has made for its door, and none of them records finding it. An old mason's mark is cut on the cistern's far wall, and the same mark is on the tower's foundation stone.

:::secret {title="The pay vault's door"}
The door is a single dressed slab in the cistern's far wall, beside the mason's mark, set dry and faced with a skin of lime mortar so that it reads as newer stonework. A bar under its lower edge lifts it out. Broken through instead, the facing takes an hour of noisy work. Captain Aldmund Hardwick found it on the afternoon before he was taken, and the only record of it is the last line of his daybook, which ends mid-word.
:::

## The Thing in the Heights

Something lives in the post now. The garrison's last daybook calls it **the Hangman**, after what it does with the men it takes.

Its work shows from the gorge trail. A dead pine leans out over the drop below the crag, and men hang from its branches, out of reach of anything that walks. Inside the post, deep gouges run across the plaster and the beams of every ceiling, sets of long parallel scores as if something heavy hung there by hooks and dragged itself along, and there are none on any floor.

The daybook gives the rest, in the frightened hand of the post's captain. Nobody has seen the Hangman whole and lived to describe it calmly. It is long, much longer than a man, and its body bends at more joints than a body has. It moves across ceilings, up walls and between beams, and it does not touch the ground. It drops from overhead stonework, lifts a man by a rope or by its own reach, and is gone upward. It carries the men it takes to high places and hangs them there, in the dead pine above the gorge, from the tower's beams, from the cistern's vault, and it eats little of them.

The daybook says it dips them in the cistern first, and the writer did not know why.

:::secret {title="The dipping"}
The Hangman waters what it keeps. The men it hangs are mostly living. It dips a man in the cistern when it takes him, and each dusk after that it carries every one of them down, lowers him into the water to the chest and hangs him back in his place. The cold water wakes a man and keeps him alive. It feeds on each of them a little at a time, from the legs below the knee, and a man watered every dusk lasts some weeks. A hung man's boots are wet whenever he has been watered within the hour.
:::

It calls, the daybook says, in the voices of the men it has taken, in their own words, and it learns new voices quickly. On still evenings voices carry down the gorge from the crag.

:::secret {title="The voices"}
The Hangman has no voice. The voices from the heights are the taken, alive, hung by ropes under the arms and calling for help. The Hangman lets them call, because a man who goes to a voice in the heights comes within its reach. Each voice belongs to its own man, so it answers a question, and it says what that man sees from where he hangs: the snow starting on the pass, a lamp going out below. The garrison took that for learning.

> [!TIP]
> Play each voice as a man in pain who is trying to be brief: his name, where he hangs, what he can see, and a name he wants. Never let a voice repeat itself word for word. A voice that answers what it is asked is the truth of the place, and it should sound like it.

:::

It is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]], made and not born, and there is nothing else like it on Aelwyth. A band of dark metal is sunk into one of its limbs, grown over at the edges, cut with marks nobody at the post could read. What it was made for, who made it and why it came down to Sunderwynd are not known.

## Who Knows, and What They Say

- **Caerwynd** knows the post has sent no signal for some days and has sent a relief patrol up the gorge.
- **The smugglers** who cross the pass know that something took a string of ponies off the trail in the gorge a season ago, and have used the other pass since. They say the trail-men of the far side tell of a long thing in the high rocks that calls down in the voices of the men it took, and that nobody there goes up alone or answers a voice from above.
- **The Vardain of the valleys** below the range have a word for a made thing that walks where it should not, and they will not say it in the post's direction.

## Reaching It

The gorge trail is the only road to Sunderwynd. It is a day's climb from the last forest steading below, and the trail is cut into a wall of loose rock that comes down after heavy rain. Above the post the pass runs on into high country under snow for a third of the year. The post's drainage tunnel opens on the gorge face below the trail, and a climber who knows it is there can reach the trail from it, below any slide in the upper gorge.

## Hooks

- **The pay vault.** The toll and the garrison's silver lie walled up behind the cistern. Caerwynd's quartermaster wants them carried down, and so does anyone else who hears of them.
- **The relief patrol.** A patrol went up the gorge to relieve the post and has not signalled.
- **The captain's sword.** The captain who walled the vault carried a fine sword, a gift of the crown, and his family has asked for it.

# The Truth of Sunderwynd {#truth .secret}

**The Hangman.** It is what the daybook says in every particular but two: it has no voice, and most of what it hangs is alive. It keeps to the heights, moving across walls, ceilings, beams and the hollow places in the masonry, and it never touches the ground. On open ground with nothing above it to climb it does not come, so the open yard is safe at any hour. Brought down to the floor it is slow, clumsy and badly placed, its reach is short, and it can be killed. Its body is light for its size and smells of nothing. Who made it, and to what end, is not known to anyone living.

**What it keeps.** It hangs the men it takes by ropes under the arms, never by the neck, and waters them in the cistern every dusk. Its round takes the better part of an hour: the men in the cistern first, then the pine, then the tower, and while it is out on the crag face at the pine, the tower and the cistern are both unwatched. It feeds on the living a little at a time and eats little of the dead, which it leaves hanging. Anything that falls from its ropes it goes after at once, hook and limb, living or dead, because a man in the water is a man lost from its larder. A living man cut down without being lowered drops into the deep end of the cistern with his arms bound to his sides.

**Why the voices call.** It lets the hung call because a man who goes to a voice in the heights comes within reach. The voice is the bait, and the Hangman waits beside whoever is calling. Every voice in the post is a living man's, saying what he sees and what he wants.

**The hung now.** This is the post on the afternoon a rockslide closes the upper gorge below it, the day the relief patrol from [[place-caerwynd|Caerwynd]] reaches the gate.

- **Captain Aldmund Hardwick** hangs in the tower's roof-room under the signal platform, above the table where he was writing when he was taken, three nights before the slide. A rafter cracked as the rope took his weight. He is weak and lucid at dusk, after the watering, and he calls down the trap and through the platform's slits for **Kenric Brandley**, the one man of the garrison left on the ground, by name.
- **The dead pine** holds three. Two are men of the garrison taken on the wall in the first nights, dead and dry. The third is a patrolman of that relief, taken at the gate that afternoon and dipped within the hour, alive. When he wakes he calls for his sergeant, **Wilric**.
- **The cistern's vault** holds the rest: the garrison's dead, a smuggler and his pony, the relief patrol's sergeant Wilric and three of his men alive, and two men of the garrison taken in the last week, alive. One of the relief patrol broke his neck in the taking and hangs dead with the others.

**The garrison's end.** The Hangman came down out of the high country some weeks ago. It took men on watch on the wall first, then men from their bunks with their mates asleep in the next, and three nights before the slide it took the captain from his table in the tower with Kenric on the stair below. The lamp went over and a crack sounded in the dark, and the captain's chair was empty. That night his voice called from above for Kenric to come up and help. The captain's last entry, a single line saying he had found something at last, ends mid-word, written under the beams he now hangs from.

**Kenric.** He lies flat in the open yard every night, as the captain meant to order the men, and he has heard the captain call him by name for three nights. He believes the Hangman speaks in the captain's voice and learns from watching, and he has not answered. He does not know the captain is alive.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **"It calls in the voices of the men it has taken"** is false. The voices are the taken.
- **"It dips them first, for reasons nobody knows"** is the watering, and it happens every dusk, not once.
- **"It eats little of them"** is true, and it is true because it eats them slowly.
- **"It keeps to the heights and never touches the ground"** is exact.
- **The trail-men's rule, never answer a voice from above,** keeps a traveler alive and leaves the man calling to die.

> [!WARNING]
> The voices are the whole secret of the post. A GM who plays them as mockery, repetition or a lure in the thing's own mouth spends the reversal; a GM who has a voice answer a question before the party has heard the captain and seen the dripping boots spends it too early. Let the voices sound like men from the start, and let the party decide what they are.
