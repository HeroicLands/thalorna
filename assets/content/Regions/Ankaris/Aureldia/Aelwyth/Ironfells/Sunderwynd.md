## Overview

The southern approach to the more easterly of the two [[place-ironfells|Ironfells]] passes climbs for a day through steep, sodden forest and then enters a gorge, a narrow cleft with a torrent at the bottom and a trail cut along one wall. At the gorge's upper end, on a crag that overhangs the trail, stands **Sunderwynd**: a square stone tower, a walled yard, a gatehouse and a barrack range with arrow-slits on the gorge side. The name is the gorge's, the split way between the hills. Every pack-pony that crosses the pass from the south goes under the post's walls, and the post collects the crown's toll on it.

Sunderwynd is an outpost of [[place-caerwynd|Caerwynd]], which watches the southern ends of the passes. Its garrison is a captain and a score of men, relieved by a patrol up from Caerwynd at intervals, and its life is weather, smugglers and the occasional skirmish with the jarl's men at the pass-head ([[place-sunderfells|the Sunderfells]]).

## The Post

The tower holds the captain's room and the signal platform. The barrack range holds the men, the kitchen and the stores. Beneath the yard is a cistern, a vault on squat pillars cut into the crag, which takes the rain off the roofs and holds the post's water through a siege. A drainage tunnel runs from the cistern's low end through the crag and out onto the gorge face below the trail. Behind the cistern's far wall is the pay vault, which holds the toll and the garrison's silver; a captain walled it up during a raid from over the pass and died in the fighting, and nobody since has found its door.

## The Thing in the Heights

Something lives in the post now. The garrison's last daybook calls it **the Hangman**, after what it does with the dead.

Nobody has seen it whole and lived to describe it calmly. It is long, much longer than a man, and its body bends at more joints than a body has. It moves across ceilings, up walls and between beams, and it does not touch the ground. It drops from overhead stonework, lifts a man by a rope or by its own reach, and is gone upward. It carries the dead to high places and hangs them there, in the dead pine above the gorge, from the tower's beams, from the cistern's vault, and it eats little of them. It calls in the voices of the men it has taken, in their own words.

It is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]], made and not born, and there is nothing else like it on Aelwyth. A band of dark metal is sunk into one of its limbs, grown over at the edges, cut with marks nobody at the post could read. What it was made for, who made it and why it came down to Sunderwynd are not known.

## Who Knows, and What They Say

- **Caerwynd** knows the post has sent no signal for some days and has sent a relief patrol up the gorge.
- **The smugglers** who cross the pass know that something took a string of ponies off the trail in the gorge a season ago, and have used the other pass since. They say the trail-men of the far side tell of a long thing in the high rocks, and that nobody there goes up alone.
- **The Vardain of the valleys** below the range have a word for a made thing that walks where it should not, and they will not say it in the post's direction.

## Reaching It

The gorge trail is the only road to Sunderwynd. It is a day's climb from the last forest steading below, and the trail is cut into a wall of loose rock that comes down after heavy rain. Above the post the pass runs on into high country under snow for a third of the year. The post's drainage tunnel opens on the gorge face below the trail, and a climber who knows it is there can reach the trail from it, below any slide in the upper gorge.

## Hooks

- **The pay vault.** The toll and the garrison's silver lie walled up behind the cistern. Caerwynd's quartermaster wants them carried down, and so does anyone else who hears of them.
- **The relief patrol.** A patrol went up the gorge to relieve the post and has not signalled.
- **The captain's sword.** The captain who walled the vault carried a fine sword, a gift of the crown, and his family has asked for it.
