## Overview

Ezkoba is named for a lantern, the _ezkobe_ that a Tarvenan house sets on its threshold for its dead, and it is the lantern of the whole coast. The town of about 2,500 stands in the only break in [[place-kaurema|Kaurema]], the black sea-cliffs north of [[place-durvolka|Durvolka]]: a steep cleft about a third of the way up the cliffs from their southern end, where a stream comes down to the sea, with a stone cove at the bottom, a harbor wall built across its mouth, and the town stacked up both sides of the cleft in terraces of slate-roofed houses joined by stairs. On the clifftop at the seaward edge of the cleft stands the [Long Lamp]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, a squat round tower with a fire-basket in an iron cage on its roof.

Ezkoba is poor, proud and wet. It has no room for a curing ground, no farmland but the heath above it, and no road but the cliff path and the sea. It lives by its small boats, by the lobster and crab pots its women set along the reefs, by the bird ledges on the cliffs, and above all by the lamp.

## The Long Lamp

On every foggy night and every dark night from autumn to spring, the lamp-keepers of Ezkoba light the fire-basket on the tower and keep it burning till dawn. It burns seal oil and fish oil in iron pans, with driftwood and gorse under them, and it shows a yellow glare through fog that a boat a mile out can steer on when it can see nothing else. Six keepers serve it, two to a night, and the post passes in families; a keeper's daughter learns to trim the pans before she learns to row.

The lamp is paid for by the whole coast. Every boat that lands fish anywhere between Ezkoba and Durvolka pays the [lamp-due]{.name data-type="lore/law"}, one Bit a season, to the king's officer at its harbor, and the officer sends the money to Ezkoba with the oil. The fishermen pay it more willingly than any other due, because each of them can name a night the lamp brought him home. The Keeper of the Lamp, the senior of the six, answers to the crown for the money and to the town for the fire, and a keeper who lets the lamp go out on a foggy night is turned out of the post the next morning and does not get it back.

## The Teller of Ezkoba

The harbor teller of Ezkoba is the senior teller of the [[affiliation-drownedroad|Drowned Road]] along the whole coast. She lives in the house at the foot of the Long Lamp, and she keeps two rolls there: the roll of Ezkoba's own drowned, and the roll of the drowned of Kaurema, every boat and every man the cliffs have taken from any harbor, read aloud on the [Night of Lanterns]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} from the top of the tower while the lamp burns behind her. The tellers of the other harbors come to Ezkoba when they disagree, and what the teller of Ezkoba says about a disputed name is what the coast does.

The present teller is [Nelma Ularris]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a woman of sixty who lost her husband and both her brothers under the cliffs and has kept the roll for twenty-two years. She knows the name of every boat lost on Kaurema in her lifetime and most of those before it, and she knows which of the families who come to her at the Night of Lanterns have lied to her about how their men were lost.

## Life in the Cleft

Ezkoba's boats are small, open and handy, built to come in through the harbor mouth in a swell and to work the reefs under the cliffs where the bigger Durvolka boats will not go. They fish for pollack, bass and conger along the rocks, take herring in season, and bring the crab and lobster to Durvolka's market by sea when the weather allows. In summer the men go over the cliff edge on ropes for the eggs and young of the gulls and guillemots, and the families who hold the best ledges are the nearest thing the town has to a gentry.

The king's reeve at Ezkoba is a shipmaster's younger son sent from Durvolka, and he holds the harbor, collects the dues and keeps the oil store. The town's quarrels go to its own assembly of boat-owners, which meets on the harbor wall after the boats are in. The nearest priests are at Durvolka; a priest of the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] comes up by boat each spring to bless the fleet and the lamp together, and the teller stands beside him while he does it.

The passage graves inside the stone ring at the northern end of the cliffs belong to Ezkoba, and every drowned man the sea gives back along Kaurema is carried there along the cliff path, whatever harbor he sailed from. The coast holds that the cliffs keep the men they take, and that the road under Kaurema runs to Ezkoba's ring and not home.

## Trouble in Ezkoba

- **A false light.** Twice this autumn a boat has come onto the reefs south of the town steering on a yellow light that was not the Long Lamp. The keepers swear the lamp was lit, the teller has two new names for the roll, and somebody on the coast has learned that what a wreck under Kaurema brings ashore reaches the Warden of Wrecks only if nobody reaches it first.
- **The oil fails.** A bad seal year and a crown that is slow to send the oil have left the lamp with a month's fuel and the darkest months still to come. The Keeper of the Lamp needs oil from somewhere, and the only large store on the coast belongs to a Durvolka curing house that is owed money by the crown.
- **The teller's successor.** Nelma Ularris is failing, and she has not named who will keep the rolls after her. Two women want the post, one a teller's daughter and one a widow who has learned the rolls by heart, and the tellers of the other harbors are already taking sides.
- **The lowest tide of the year.** A spring tide in late summer will uncover more of the sunken city than anyone living has seen, and a young man of the town has a boat ready and a plan to go down the stairs. His mother wants him stopped. His friends want him helped.
