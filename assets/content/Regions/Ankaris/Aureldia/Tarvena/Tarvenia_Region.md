## Overview

Tarvénia lies to the west of [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]], a land of rugged mountains, rich coastal areas, and fiercely independent peoples. The landscape is as varied as its people — dense forests, golden beaches, and high, windswept peaks are home to small, isolated villages and proud city-states. The Tarvénans trace their lineage back to ancient warrior tribes, and their culture prizes strength, resilience, and an unshakeable sense of freedom.

## Geography

Tarvénia occupies the western peninsula of southern Ankaris, with the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] to its south and the open ocean to its west. The interior is dominated by a high central plateau ringed by mountain ranges, some of which are volcanic. The coasts are varied: the southern coast is warm, dry, and dotted with prosperous port cities; the western coast is wild and storm-battered, with towering sea cliffs; the northern border with [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] and [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] is marked by the **Toravar Range** — the great forested mountain belt that runs east-west across the upper peninsula and which the Áelendan tribes hold as sacred high country in the same way they hold the Cervaron Spine to the east.

The climate is dominated by the Vylarian Sea — long, dry summers and mild winters in the south, cooler and wetter conditions in the north. The land is ideal for agriculture, particularly vineyards, olive groves, and grain fields. Tarvénia is a major producer of trade goods, including high-quality wines, olive oil, and textiles.

## Culture and Society

The Tarvénans are fiercely independent and proud of their heritage. In contrast to the more refined and cultured people of [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]], they are known for their strength, resilience, and deeply entrenched sense of personal and communal freedom. Many of the great warriors and military leaders of Ankaris come from Tarvénia, and the region is home to skilled tacticians, mercenaries, and raiders.

Politically, Tarvénia is a patchwork of semi-autonomous regions united under the loose authority of the [[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Kingdom of Tarvénia]]. Three petty kingdoms — [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Astúrath]] (the mountainous heartland), [[affiliation-kngdmglvrn|Galváren]] (the misty western coast), and [[affiliation-kingdmlnrk|Léonrik]] (the fertile central lowlands) — share power with the [[affiliation-frctyvlthr|Free City of Valthári]] (a merchant port on the southern coast) and the [[affiliation-frtrtryskrth|Free Territory of Eskárath]] (an ancient highland people on the Élavendren border). They share a common language and a history of both collaboration and rivalry. The people of Tarvénia are united by their warrior culture and their defiance of outside rule — a trait that has made them difficult subjects for every empire that has tried to conquer them. Even the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] at its height struggled to hold Tarvénia.

Social structure varies by region. Coastal cities tend toward merchant oligarchies, while inland kingdoms are more traditionally feudal. Across Tarvénia, however, there is a strong tradition of communal assembly where freemen have a voice in local governance.

## Religion

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]] is the formal faith, but Tarvénan practice is inflected with strong local folk traditions. [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Jánus]] (order and honorable struggle) is especially revered among the warrior class, while [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Lúsinía]] (creation and earth) is honored by farmers and rural communities. [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Táranon]] (storms) has a significant following among seafarers and mountain communities.

The Tarvénans have a complex relationship with magic and the divine. While [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]] embraces arcane arts and mystical studies, Tarvénia has traditionally been more skeptical of magic, viewing it with suspicion or as a tool of the elite. That said, many cities hold ancient shrines to local gods and spirits, and the connection to the divine remains a vital part of the culture at the folk level. Hermit holy-men, wandering mendicants, and local shrine-keepers are more common than formal temple priests.

## Magic

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] has a limited presence in Tarvénia. The Tarvénan distrust of centralized authority extends to magical authority, and the Ordo's attempts to regulate arcane practice have been met with resistance ranging from polite refusal to outright hostility. Local magical traditions — hedge magic, herbal lore, and protective charms — persist alongside the Ordo's more formal practices, and many Tarvénan communities would sooner consult a village wise-woman than an Ordo-sanctioned mage.

## Relations

Tarvénia has begun to forge closer ties with [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia Region]] in recent years, especially in trade, but the two regions remain competitive rivals for dominance over the southern trade routes. Relations with the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] are distant — the Tarvénans broke free of Vylarian control long ago and have no desire to renew the relationship. [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] to the north is viewed with a mix of respect and wariness; the Élavendren are admired for their arts but considered overly mystical and soft.

Within Tarvénia itself, the **[[place-alndntrblnds#the-tarvenan-diaspora|Áelendan tribal enclaves]]** of the Toravar Range and the northern forested uplands are the most distinctive non-Tarvénan presence on the peninsula. Áelendan communities — Pelwar forest-dwellers who recognize the [[affiliation-theoldway|Old Way]] of the [[affiliation-firstgods|First Gods]] rather than the Aurèldían Twelve — occupy the high mountain country and the deepest forests, generally in territory the Tarvénan crown and the lowland feudal nobility have no use for. Relations are mostly cordial: the Áelendan leave the lowlands alone, the Tarvénans leave the high country alone, and a long-established practice of local accommodation between Áelendan Wardens and Tarvénan barons handles the periodic frictions over straying herds, logging concessions, road-surveys near sacred groves, and the occasional Aurèldían missionary who needs to be politely but firmly escorted out. Roughly once a generation a particularly stubborn baron meets a particularly unyielding clan and the result is a brief mountain-war that the crown of Tarvena prefers to settle quickly through Élavendri diplomatic intermediaries. No Tarvénan king has ever attempted to bring the Áelendan enclaves under formal authority, and none is expected to.

Tarvénan sailors and mercenaries are found across the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] and beyond. Their reputation as fierce fighters and skilled seafarers makes them valued (if sometimes unpredictable) allies and employees.

## Notable Features

- **The Mountain Kingdoms:** Isolated highland realms — especially [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Astúrath]] — where ancient traditions survive largely unchanged, and where the fiercest warriors are bred.
- **The Port Cities:** Prosperous trading hubs along the southern coast, above all the [[affiliation-frctyvlthr|Free City of Valthári]], where goods from across Mídhalión and beyond change hands.
- **The Mercenary Tradition:** Tarvénan sellswords are found in armies across Ankaris. Service abroad is seen as a rite of passage for young warriors.
- **The Eskárathi Highlands:** The [[affiliation-frtrtryskrth|Free Territory of Eskárath]] preserves a culture older than the Tarvéni migration itself, with its own language, laws, and fierce mountain traditions.
