## Overview

Lienrik is the bread of the [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia Region]]. It is the land of the [[affiliation-kingdmlnrk|Kingdom of Lienrik]], the lowlands of central and southern Tarvénia that fall from the mountains of [[place-asturath|Asturath]] to the sea, and it feeds the mountains, the free city and a good part of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] trade with its grain, its cattle and its wine. About a million people live there, and nearly all of them live by the land.

It is the richest farmland in the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]], and the easiest country in Tarvénia to cross. The ease has cost it. Lienrik is the one Tarvenan land an army can march through, and the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] held it for four centuries ([[lore-lowlandstaken|The Lowlands Taken]]); the walled towns, the fortified manors and the watchtowers that still stand across it are the marks that occupation left.

## The Land

The country runs about four days' ride from the foot of the mountains to the shore. In the north it begins in foothills under the peaks of Asturath, where [[place-ronetho|Ornetho]] stands at the mouth of the gorge. South of the city the land opens into a wide, gently falling plain of wheat and barley, cut by hedges, lanes and the walls of manors, with a fortified manor house to every few villages and a walled town about a day's ride from the next. Along the eastern side of the plain rise the eastern hills, low and dry, whose south faces carry the vineyards of [[place-enthara|Enthara]] and olive groves below them. To the west the plain climbs into the forests that mark the border with [[place-galvaren|Galvaren]]. In the south it ends in salt marsh, mudflat and shingle, a coast with no harbor worth the name; the harbor of that coast is [[place-valthari|Valthari]]'s.

The plain is made by one river. The [Thalsira]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the red river, rises in the mountains above Ornetho, comes out of its gorge under the city's walls and runs south through the whole length of Lienrik to the marsh below [[place-bruthena|Bruthena]]. Its name comes from the color of its water: _thal_ is red, and in spring the Thalsira runs the color of the clay it carries down from the hills. That clay is the wealth of the lowlands. The river spreads it over the water meadows every year, and the same clay, fired, builds every brick town of the plain.

## Weather and the Year

Lienrik has the weather of the Vylarian Sea: long, dry, hot summers, mild winters, and the rain from autumn to spring. The wheat is sown in autumn after the first rains and cut in early summer; the threshing runs through the heat, and the great harvest fair at [[place-torvaleth|Torvaleth]] follows it at summer's end. The vintage comes in early autumn, and the olives in winter.

The dangerous season is late spring. When the snow melts in the mountains of Asturath the Thalsira rises in the _mozkar_ (snowmelt flood), and in a heavy year it comes over its banks across the lower plain in a day. The villages along the river keep the flood banks in common, and the River Warden calls them out to the banks when the water rises; a village that skimped on its stretch of bank answers for it before the bailiff afterward. In autumn the river breeds fogs that lie on the plain until noon, and the carters of the river road travel in them by the sound of the bells on the lead oxen.

## The Towers

The chain of watchtowers runs the length of the Thalsira, from the tower over Ornetho's north gate to the castle tower at Bruthena, each within sight of the next and each with a beacon on its roof. The Empire built them to give warning of a rising in the mountains; when the garrisons were driven out ([[lore-tarvfallsawy|Tarvénia Falls Away]]) the Liénriki turned them to face the coast, against an empire that might come back by sea. A fire lit at Bruthena passes up the whole chain in a single night.

Families of towerkeepers hold the towers by inheritance, keep the beacons and the lamps, and take a measure of grain from every village in sight of their light, the light-due. The villages pay it and grumble, and from almost any field in Lienrik a gray tower stands against the sky.

## Roads and Neighbors

The [Tower Road]{.name data-type="place/structure"} is the old imperial road, and it is the spine of the country: from the passes down through Ornetho, along the Thalsira past the towers to Torvaleth, and on to the bridge at Bruthena and the last day's road to Valthari. Barges work the river between Torvaleth and Bruthena, carrying grain and wine down and salt and goods from the port back up. A hill road climbs east from Torvaleth to Enthara, and a forest road goes west to Galvaren.

Lienrik's neighbors are its markets and its rivals. Asturath sends down cattle, wool and iron through Ornetho and takes back grain, and its mountain lords regard the lowlanders as rich, soft and too fond of clerks. Galvaren over the western forests trades fish and timber for grain. Valthari buys most of what Lienrik sells to the world, at prices Valthari sets, and Lienrik pays Valthari's harbor for every cargo it ships; the two depend on each other entirely and like each other very little.

## The People

The Liénriki are the most settled of the Tarvenans and the most formal. They are farmers above all, tenants and freeholders under the lords of the manors, with the drovers, carters, bargemen and brickmakers of the river towns beside them. The lowland speech is the most regular of the dialects of [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni]], and the other Tarvenans call it the clerks' Tarveni, because the Liénriki write down what the rest of Tarvénia remembers: their courts keep rolls, their lords keep records of their lineage, and a Liénriki family is apt to know its descent for ten generations and be able to show the paper. The faith of the plain is the farmer's faith, and the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] holds the first place in every village of Lienrik.

Lienrik sends its sons abroad under contract like every other Tarvenan land, and the money they send home is spent on land. A man who comes back from ten years abroad buys a field, and a captain who comes back rich buys a manor.
