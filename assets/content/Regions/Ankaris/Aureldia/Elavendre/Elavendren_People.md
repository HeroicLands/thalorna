The **Élavendren** are the settled, courtly people of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]—the kingdom's
cities, its farms and its court—and the kin of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]], with whom they
share a common Pelwar ancestry. The two peoples are the two halves of one inheritance: the Élavendren
cultivated and literate, the Áelendan tribal and forest-dwelling. Both are unusually attuned to magic,
and that attunement is the thing an outsider notices first about either of them.

Their language, [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]], descends directly from [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] with simplifications
and absorbed outside influences—which is the relationship between the peoples in miniature. It is written in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]].

The Élavendren are the "modern" descendants of the Áelendan, and they hold them in great respect as an ancestor culture that keeps the old ways, though they do not always agree with them. They are much less conservative themselves, and have adapted to the new realities of their situation far more fluidly than the Áelendan; taking up the Semaron is one instance. Ancient texts in the region are Áelendi, not Élavendri.

## Faith

The Élavendren worship the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]], but their practice is
heavily inflected by older fae traditions, and no Élavendren would regard the two as being in
competition. The land is held to be a participant in human affairs rather than a backdrop to them—an attitude shared with the Áelendan, and one that shapes how the court deals with the interior.

## The Interior

That deference is not merely sentimental. Élavendre's mountains and deep forests hold the
[[lore-aralwen|aralwain]], the thin places where the material and spirit worlds lie close enough to
cross, and the crown recognizes the authority of the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] over
them. The Élavendren court, which prefers not to name such things precisely, calls them the thin
places and leaves their keeping to the people who have always kept them.
