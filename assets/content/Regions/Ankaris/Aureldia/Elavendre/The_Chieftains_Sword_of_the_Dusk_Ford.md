## Overview {#overview}

**The Chieftain's Sword** is a long sword of Nordmen make with a hilt wrapped in silver wire, the finest blade in the plunder heaped along the walls of the tally-house on the far bank of [[place-dskfrd|the Dusk Ford]]. Its owner's name is cut in runes along the fuller. He cannot read it, because he gave his name to the Toll-Keeper.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

Nobody holds it. It lies against the wall of the tally-house with the arm-rings, the hacked silver and the amber, and its chieftain sits on a bench in the nearest of the rotted ships on the mud below, one of the Forfeit-Men.

## Its Story {#its-story}

A Nordmen war-band came through the bog behind the [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shore to rob the far bank, in a generation when the beacon chain was young. At the first stone the Toll-Keeper asked each raider what he would give that he would not miss until he was across, and the chieftain gave his name. The raiders who cross pay their names and stay, and the sword stayed with him.

## What It Does {#what-it-does}

It is a fine blade, balanced and keen, and it has not rusted on the mud. It carries its owner's unpaid debt. Whoever lifts it takes the debt on, and the chieftain rises from his bench and walks to stand beside the one who took it, and the toll for the return is paid by two.

## Who Wants It {#who-wants-it}

Smugglers of the Kalipsa run tell of it over wine and would like a tenth. The chieftain, if his name were found, would want it back.

:::secret {title="The forfeit-seller"}
[[being-varlangrth|Varlan Gárath]], who sells forfeits to buyers on the Kalipsa run, knows what the sword's debt costs and would like someone else to pay it.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A hero's weapon.** A party that crosses the Ford for the hoard finds the sword the finest thing in it, and the Toll-Keeper tells anyone who asks exactly what its debt is ([[scenario-tlldskfrd|The Toll You Will Not Miss]]).
- **The name on the fuller.** A skald of the Nordlands will read the runes for a price, and the name is one the north still sings.
