The Áelendan Tribes have no central government of the [[place-alndntrblnds|Tribal Lands]]. Each tribal community governs itself through a **Council of Elders**, and coordination between tribes is handled through [[lore-seasnlcncls|seasonal councils]] and through runners who carry word between territories. The Crown of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]] recognizes this arrangement and treats the Áelendan as sovereign in their own land—the kingdom's courts will act against Élavendri who encroach, but they will not accept cases brought against the tribes themselves.

Sacred sites are a special category. Authority over them falls to the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]], whose jurisdiction is unchallenged within the sacred territory. Where the Wardens' charge applies, no other authority holds sway—not the tribal councils, not the Crown, not outside observers. The Wardens' judgment on trespass, desecration, or disturbance of a sacred site is final.

## The Araelwen {#present}

The tribes' gravest common charge is the [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]], the sacred thin places of their mountains and forests, which every tribe holds sacred and none admits an outsider to. A tribe whose valley holds one keeps its rules first among all its customs, sends its sons and daughters to the Wardens who watch it, and answers in council for any failure of the watch, as the tribe of the Lodge answered for [[lore-blacksnow|the Black Snow]]. The common council meets on the Araelwen more often than on anything else, because the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] comes up the valleys for them every season with hired guards, and because the crown that licenses the Order is the crown the tribes acknowledge.

The council's delegates argue and do not bind, and they are divided. Some tribes hold that the Wardens' warning and their judgment are enough. Others want the council to tell the crown plainly that a guard hired by the Order and taken on an Araelwen is dealt with as a desecrator, so that no lowland father can say his son was not told.

## The Tribes {#tribes}

A tribe is the clans of a valley or a range, and it takes the name of its oldest clan with the ending that means "those of". Seven hold the interior between the Lodge and the passes. Each keeps its own Council of Elders, its own lodge hearth and its own Kindred, and each sends delegates to the common council that meets at [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]], where [[being-khoradeldhrg|Khoradel]] of the Dhárgandi is its Speaker.

The [Dhárgandi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The tribe of the Lodge, whose hall was kindled from the Lodge's hearth. They hold that [Dhargan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who sat at the wood's edge, founded their line, and the chief who felled the birch in the Black Snow bore Dhargan's face ([[lore-blacksnow|The Black Snow]]). They send an ember back to the Lodge at each founding, send their young to its rites, and are heard first when it calls the tribes together. A Warden of the Dhárgandi keeps the watch at the Fen Door in Lísere.

The [Kháldendi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The line [[lore-theasking|The Asking]] calls cold-handed, descended from the man whose hands went numb on the birch. Their ground is the gorge country about [[place-longstair|the Long Stair]], and their craft is the carving the Stair's pilgrims buy. The lamb-line and the cold-handed line fell out for good over a pasture, and nine were buried. The Dhárgandi and the Kháldendi do not speak as kin, and the Keeper of the Feud reckons the blood between them.

The [Tírindi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The tribe of the outer margin of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]], along the envoys' road and the way to [[place-sourimael|Sóurimel]]. They are hunters, and many of the Wardens of the forest are of them, among them those who walk [[place-hntrsrng|the Hunter's Ring]] and escort travelers along the road below it. They seat a shaman at [[affiliation-mtslvrfrsts|the Moot]].

The [Thanandi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The tribe of the lower [[place-drenavrspn|Spine]] where the wood climbs toward it, nearest to [[place-owlhollow|the Owl Hollow]]. They keep its equinox rites and permit any Death-Speaker the asking without charge, on the principle that the answer is Óssenda's to give. They seat a shaman at the Moot.

The [Dulundi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The herders of the high meadow below [[place-sngngtrn|the Singing Tarn]], whose name is the word for the deep water. They keep their flocks below the line of cairns and bring them farther down in the evening, and the Keepers hold the list of those the water has taken.

The [Ronondi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The tribe of the valley across [[place-wndrngrdg|the Wandering Ridge]] from the Dulundi. The two valleys cross the ridge in summer by leave of the Wardens and the ridge's song, which every child of either learns.

The [Sáthandi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}
: The tribe of the eastern passes, where the Kervaron faces Viléria. They take the passage-gifts of the Vilérian merchants who cross with goods, grant safe conduct in return, and send the war-bands that cross east and always return ([[place-alndntrblnds|the Tribal Lands]]).

[[affiliation-mtslvrfrsts|The Moot of the Silver Forests]] is the gathering of the shamen of the three tribes whose ground meets the forest, the Dhárgandi, the Tírindi and the Thanandi. It advises the elders on what the wood asks and binds none of the tribes.

## Commerce and Currency

Within the tribal communities, substantial commerce runs on [[lore-kinhalcrdt|kin credit]]: value extended between members of an extended family against the security of the relationship, repaid in time, and held in memory and custom rather than in writing or coin. An Áelendan who trades in the Élavendren cities can deal with the guilds chartered under [[affiliation-tevran|the Tevran Maldor]] and the moneylenders of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], but credit earned there does not carry into the tribal economy, where the relationship is what counts. See [[lore-kinhalcrdt|Kin and Hall Credit]] for the system.

## History {#history}

The tribes reckon themselves from the band that came over [[place-nelgan|Nelgan]] in the snow ([[lore-nelgancross|The Crossing of Nelgan]]) and from the Pact first sung at the lowest pool of the Long Stair in the generation after ([[lore-lowestpoolpct|The Pact at the Lowest Pool]]). The first watch was set on the fog-hollow at the wood's edge in that generation. In a killing winter the watch was pulled and something came through, and the people went back to the wood's edge to ask again ([[lore-blacksnow|The Black Snow]]). When a war-band came over the pass with brands, the people stood at the trees, and the wood was given over to them ([[lore-firesnelgan|The Fires on Nelgan]]). The lowland houses came down out of the hills and crowned a king, who swore that the crown stops at the tree line ([[lore-firstcrown|The First Crowning of Élavendre]]), and when a later king opened workings above it, the Wardens closed them in a season ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]).
