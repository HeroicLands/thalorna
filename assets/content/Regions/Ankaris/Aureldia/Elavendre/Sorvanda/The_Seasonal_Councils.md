The Áelendan have no central government, and the nearest thing to one is the weather. At the turn of each season the delegates of the tribes come down to [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] for the great rite of the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"}, and the common council sits on the day of it. The sittings are the **Seasonal Councils**, and a tribe that has a quarrel with another, or a matter that touches more than one valley, brings it to the next.

## The Sitting

The sitting begins with the rite in the ceremonial hall, which holds the whole gathered community, and every delegate washes at the second pool before he enters ([[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]]). The **Speaker of the Council** keeps the order of speaking and the peace of the hall. Each tribe sends its **Delegate**, an Elder who argues for it and cannot bind it. The **Lawkeeper** is heard on the customs the tribes hold in common, and the **Keeper of the Feud** on blood owed and blood paid between kindreds ([[affiliation-aelndntrbs|Áelendan Tribes]]).

A council decides nothing for a tribe that has not agreed to it. What it reaches is carried home by the delegates and put before each Council of Elders, and a tribe that declines has broken no law, though its Delegate will be heard more briefly at the next. The **Host-Caller** is named only when a threat concerns every tribe, and then the council has called a muster.

## The Day After

On the day after each sitting the shamen of the three tribes whose ground meets the wood sit as [[affiliation-mtslvrfrsts|the Moot of the Silver Forests]], in the stone-walled hall beside the ceremonial one. The Wardens bring their reports to both: what the passes saw, what a watch lacked, who was turned back.

The **Truce-Warden** holds the seasonal market, which opens when the Moot rises. The tribes trade carved goods, wool and hides, and the peace of the market is the peace of the Lodge, and the Truce-Warden enforces it. A quarrel that began in a valley is not settled in the market and is not fought there either.

## The Four Turns

The Wardens change their watches at the turns, and the council hears what has been left short. The turns do not each have their own business, and the Wardens reckon them by what they have to cover.

**Spring** is the turn before the thaw, when the watch at [[place-talve|Talve]] is sat by Wardens who have been told what the hollow does as the ground softens, and the council counts how many it can spare.

**Summer** is the turn when the herds are up and the watches on the Spine are short of hands ([[place-hldhr|the Held Hour]]). The passes are open and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] comes up the valleys with hired guards, and the council spends more of its time on the Araelwen than on anything else.

**Autumn** is the turn at which relief comes down the valleys to the far watches, such as [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]] in Lísere, before the passes close.

**Winter** is the turn the council sits with the watches fixed and the passes shut, and the tribes' business is their own: the feud, the herds and the dead of the year. The Keepers hold the names of the year's dead.

## Meeting It

A party does not attend a council. It meets the council's result: a Warden who will take no guest through the pass this season, a delegate on the road home who asks an outsider to carry a word to a village shaman, a market at which the Truce-Warden turns a man out for a quarrel he did not start.
