The Owl Hollow is a steep wooded fold in the lower reaches of [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]], dark under its canopy at
midday and quiet in a way visitors remark on before they are told why. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]
named it for what is seated in it—[[lore-ossendaspr|Óssenda, the White Owl]]—and the tribes of the
surrounding country are careful speakers within it after dark.

## The Ground

The hollow runs perhaps a half-mile, narrow and steep-sided, with a spring at its head and a beck
running the length of it under the trees. The canopy closes overhead almost completely; the floor is
leaf-litter and moss and very little undergrowth, so the going is easy and the light is wrong all day.

The quiet is the thing everyone notices. It is not silence—there is water, and wind in the canopy—but the ordinary noise of a wood is thin here, and it stops altogether at dusk, when the owls begin.

## The Observances

- **The offering-stone** stands at the wood's edge on the upper path, where the trees begin. The
  standing offering is small and constant; nobody makes a ceremony of it, and nobody passing in
  neglects it.
- **The equinox rites** are the longer observance, kept twice a year by the nearest tribe, and they are
  the only occasions on which numbers come into the hollow at once.
- **After dark, one is careful.** Not silent—Óssenda is not owed silence—but careful. She is
  understood to be listening and to remember, and the Áelendan do not treat that as a figure of speech.

## The Asking

The Hollow's importance runs far past the tribe that keeps it. Óssenda is the Kindred a shaman goes to
**about the recently dead**—not to commend them onward, which is another spirit's office, but to ask
the narrower and more urgent question of _whether they have gone_.

**Death-Speakers of several tribes travel here** to ask it, sometimes a long way and usually in haste.
The nearest tribe permits this and charges nothing, on the settled principle that the answer is
Óssenda's to give and not theirs to sell—a position they have maintained through at least one
generation that could have made a great deal of money abandoning it.

The asking is done at night, at the beck, and the answer is not always given at once. Speakers have
waited three nights. Speakers have gone home without one.

## What Is Said Here

Because Óssenda listens and remembers, things are sometimes brought to the Hollow **to be said**—an
oath, an admission, an agreement between people with no other way to bind one another. There is no rite
for it and the tribes do not advertise it, but an undertaking made in the hollow after dark is
witnessed by something that will not forget it, and both parties know that.

The same property makes carelessness expensive. A boast, a threat, a lie told in the dark under those
trees is also remembered, and the Áelendan will tell you—without elaborating, and without being
asked twice—that this has mattered.

## Outsiders

The Hollow is deep in the [[place-alndntrblnds|Áelendan Tribal Lands]] and the
[[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] treat it as they treat any seated ground. Visitors are not
impossible; the tribes have brought outsiders here, for the asking, when someone's dead were involved.
Visitors who arrive on their own account are turned back at the upper path, and the offering-stone is
as far as most will ever get.
