## Overview {#overview}

**The Held Hour** is a corrie above the tree line on [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]], a deep bowl of the pale marble the Áelendan call _khalmon_, with a lip of broken rock all round it and one way down into it. From the lip the bowl is lit as if by a sun of the middle afternoon. The light inside does not change when the real sun moves, when cloud comes over or when night falls outside, and the shadows on the bowl's floor point the way they pointed when the Áelendan first saw them. It is an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] of the kind that disturbs time, and it is sacred ground of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]].

People go to it for the light. A traveler crossing the high country at night sees it from three valleys off, a bright cup in the dark of the range. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] wants it more than almost any other place on the Spine, because a place where the light stands still is a place where time can be measured against something.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] of the Spine hold it, and nobody else claims it. Nothing comes through the Held Hour, so its watch keeps the day and not the night: two Wardens sit at the lip where the one way down begins, with their backs to the light, and turn everyone away. A ring of upright mark-stones stands a long stone's throw outside the lip. It is the bound of the hallowed ground, the _ruthis_, which no blade crosses drawn.

No Áelendan goes down into the bowl. The herders of the high pasture keep their flocks below the ring, and a beast that strays inside it is let go.

## Layers {#history}

What made the bowl, or when its light first stood still, is not known to anyone.

**The Keepers' song.** The Tradition Keepers hold a short song of a herdsman who went down after a lamb in the first summers after the crossing, came back up before the stars, and was gray-haired, thin and silent from that evening on. The song gives the herdsman no name, and it is sung to children as the reason nobody goes down.

**The Order's reading.** The Order's theorists at the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} hold that the bowl is a reservoir of stored daylight, a kind of lamp left burning, and that its light could be drawn off and used. Its surveyors have measured the shadows from the lip with a sighting-rod, and the measurements agree with each other exactly, which the theorists take as proof.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The watch sits at the lip every day of the year, and it is short-handed in summer when the herds are up. Below the ring, in a fold out of the watch's sight, a party of the Order is camped with hired guards out of [[place-liranel|Liranel]]. It holds a license to survey the Spine from the crown and no leave from anyone who keeps it, and its scholar means to send a man down on a rope at the change of the watch, with a water-clock, and draw him up again.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbours}

- **The Wardens.** They warn once, at the ring, and then they do not warn.
- **Shardbeaks** perch on the mark-stones in sunlight and dive at whoever carries a working or a worked thing ([[being-shrdbk|Shardbeak]]).
- **The height.** The lip is broken and the one way down is steep, and a fall into the bowl is a fall into the Held Hour.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

**The ring.** Waist-high mark-stones of gray rock, upright and unworked, standing apart from one another. Inside them the grass is short and nothing is grazed.

**The lip.** A rim of broken marble, cold underfoot even in summer. The light from the bowl falls on a person who stands at the edge as warm afternoon sun, whatever the hour.

**The way down.** A cleft in the rim with a scramble of fallen blocks below it. The last block before the floor has a cairn on it, built by no Áelendan.

**The floor.** Smooth white stone, the shadows of the rim lying across it in their unchanging places, and a scatter of things on it: a sighting-rod, a coil of rope cut short, a tin cup, and a ring of stones round the ashes of a fire.

:::secret {title="The truth of the Held Hour"}
Time inside the bowl runs far faster than time outside. An hour on the lip is the better part of a week on the floor, and a day on the lip is close to a season. Because the light never moves, nobody inside can tell how long he has been there; hunger and sleep are the only clocks, and they are easily ignored. The herdsman of the Keepers' song spent years on the floor and came up the same evening.

The cairn on the last block and the fire-ring on the floor are the Order's. Its first party lowered a surveyor on a rope early on the morning before the party arrives, meaning to draw him up in an hour. The rope was cut from below, by the surveyor himself, when he had waited a month for it to move. He is alive on the floor, living on the last of his stores and a little rain that runs down the rock, thin, bearded and nearly mad, keeping a tally of his sleeps on the stone. His notes are the most exact record of an Araelwen the Order has ever had, and he knows it. On the lip, his colleagues have been waiting half a day.

A water-clock carried down runs at the bowl's pace and tells the bearer nothing he does not already feel. A clock left on the lip tells the truth.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The rope.** An Order scholar camped below the ring hires the party as guards, and their first task is to keep the watch busy for an hour while a man goes down.
- **The short watch.** The herds are up and the watch at the lip is one Warden short. She asks the party to sit the lip with her through a summer day and turn back whoever comes, the Order's camp included.
- **The notes.** Whoever brings the surveyor's notes out of the bowl can sell them at Péravel for a fortune, and the Wardens count them as something taken from an Araelwen.
