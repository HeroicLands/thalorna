**Controlled by:** [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]

## Overview {#overview}

**Ésalosa Lodge** is the principal settlement of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]—a ceremonial, political, and residential center tucked into a sheltered valley within [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]]. It is not a city in the Élavendri sense, nor a fortress in the lowland sense. It is something older and harder to describe: a permanent gathering place that has been continuously inhabited and maintained since the earliest Pelwar settlement of the interior, and that serves as the center of Áelendan collective life.

## Character

Ésalosa is built in harmony with its mountain setting rather than in opposition to it. The Lodge's structures—long timber halls, stone-walled councils, residential compounds, and the great central ceremonial hall—follow the contours of the valley floor and the surrounding slopes. Roofs are steep and moss-laid; walls are built of local stone and shaped timber; paths are stone and packed earth, kept to the natural flow of the land. Gardens and orchards occupy the terraced slopes above the main compounds, and the valley's streams feed both the communal water supply and a series of sacred pools that figure in Áelendan ritual.

The Lodge does not fortify itself against outside attack. Its defense is the mountains themselves, the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] who patrol the approaches, and the long-standing understanding that the lowland kingdom will not permit anyone to assault sacred territory. This arrangement has held for as long as the kingdom has existed.

## Function

Ésalosa serves several interrelated functions for the Áelendan:

Council seat
: The periodic great councils of Áelendan elders convene at Ésalosa. These are not permanent governing bodies; the Áelendan have no centralized government. The councils meet to decide matters that affect multiple tribes, to resolve disputes that local elders cannot settle, and to coordinate responses to outside pressures (of which the expansion of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] is the most significant current example).

Ceremonial center
: The great seasonal rituals of the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} are held at Ésalosa, drawing Áelendan from across the Drenavar Spine and the deeper forests. The ceremonial hall is the only structure in the Lodge large enough to hold the full gathered community, and it is used for little else.

Tradition-keeping
: The senior tradition-keepers—the memorizers who hold the Kindred Pact, the genealogies, and the ancient songs—gather at Ésalosa to teach, to consult, and to preserve the continuity of the oral tradition. The Lodge is effectively the living archive of Áelendan culture.

Warden coordination
: The senior [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] meet at Ésalosa to coordinate patrols across tribal territories, to share intelligence about intrusions, and to train new generations of Wardens. The Lodge is not the Wardens' headquarters in any formal sense, but it is the place where their leadership converges.

Hospitality to invited outsiders
: On rare occasions, the Áelendan extend invitations to outsiders—scholars of high reputation, envoys from the Sinale, or persons with some specific claim on Áelendan trust. Such guests are received at Ésalosa, under protocols that the Áelendan set.

## Access

Ésalosa is not accessible to casual travelers. The approaches are patrolled by Wardens, and no outsider reaches the Lodge without Áelendan invitation and escort. The last recorded unsanctioned attempt to approach the Lodge—a small Thordimalon (the Arcane Order) survey expedition some decades ago—is said to have ended at the outer ridge, though no official account of its fate has been published.

Visitors who are invited are guided through the mountain passes by Warden escorts and received at the Lodge under protocols of hospitality that are both generous and exacting. Guests are expected to observe the Kindred Pact's basic requirements—no weapons in the ceremonial areas, no writing in certain rooms, no approach to particular sites—and violations, even inadvertent, terminate the visit and are not forgiven.

## The Lodge and the Crown

No Élavendren monarch has ever set foot in Ésalosa without Áelendan invitation, and only a small number of monarchs have ever received such an invitation. Those who have are remembered with particular honor in Áelendan song. The first king of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]] came up by invitation and swore the Tree-Line oath at the Lodge ([[lore-firstcrown|The First Crowning of Élavendre]]); a later king who came uninvited was turned back at the outer ridge ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]). The crown recognizes the Lodge as the political seat of the Áelendan for purposes of the limited diplomatic intercourse that the two peoples conduct, and royal envoys who need to communicate with the Áelendan send their messages to Ésalosa through agreed channels.

## Within the Lodge {#keyed}

The Lodge lies in a fold of the high valley, oak and stone and moss, with no birch in it. A stream comes down the valley's head and runs the length of it through three pools, past the compounds and the terraces, and out at the lower end. The rooms below are keyed from the outer ridge inward, in the order a guest meets them.

### 1. The Outer Ridge

The valley is closed on its lower side by a long ridge of bare rock with one gap in it, and a path through the gap that a stranger does not find. Two Wardens keep the gap, with a fire-pit and a turf hut behind a boulder, and nobody passes without being seen. This is where the uninvited are turned back, a king in his time among them ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]), and where an Order survey ended some decades ago. A guest comes to it with a Warden escort, and is told at the gap what the Lodge asks of him.

### 2. The Guest Hall

A long oak hall of steep moss-laid roof, with a rack of pegs inside the door. Invited outsiders are lodged here, and the pegs are for their blades, which they hang before they go on. The hall has its own hearth, a small one, lit from the Lodge's great fire by a boy who carries an ember across the yard. A guest who sleeps here sleeps under Tena's notice and is told so.

### 3. The Great Hall

The central hall, and the only structure in the Lodge large enough for the gathered people. The hearthstone lies in the middle of the floor and the fire on it has not gone out since it was lit. This is the hearth of [[lore-tenaspr|Tena]], and no blade passes it, no oath sworn at it is broken without her notice, and no guest received at it is harmed. The elders' seats run along the long wall under the roof-beam, and the eldest's seat is the highest. A guest eats a broken loaf on the hearthstone and sits below the eldest. This is the hall where the elders meet the tribes on the great days, and the hall that made a chief Kinless at the hearth in the Black Snow ([[lore-blacksnow|The Black Snow]]).

A small niche at the hall's back holds the covered clay pots in which embers are kept for the tribes kindled from this fire. A tribe founding a new hall sends an ember back to the Lodge, and its pot stands here beside the others.

### 4. The Council House

A stone-walled house of one room, built against the slope with its floor sunk below the yard. The great councils of the elders sit here to decide what touches more than one tribe: a dispute the tribal elders cannot settle, a response to some pressure from outside, a punishment. The room has no hearth, because the elders sit in it to decide and not to eat. Runners from the tribes wait outside on a bench of split oak.

### 5. The Keepers' House

A long, low house at the upper end of the compounds, where the Tradition Keepers teach and consult. The Pact, the genealogies and the songs are held in mouths, and writing is forbidden in this house: a guest who carries a pen, a ledger or a notebook is asked to leave it at the door and is not asked twice. The Keeper's chosen child lives here, and is trained here for decades. Nobody else sleeps in the house.

### 6. The Wardens' Yard

A walled yard of beaten earth below the compounds, with kennels along one wall and a long hall on the other. The senior Wardens meet here to share what has been seen at the margins and to train the next generation: spear, dog, song and fire. It is not the order's headquarters, and it is where its leadership converges. A map of the Spine hangs inside the hall's door, and it is drawn on hide with charcoal, and nobody has made a copy.

### 7. The Compounds

The residential houses of the Lodge, each a courtyard of timber lodges round a household hearth, stepped up the valley side. About fifteen hundred people live here, and every household keeps its own hearth-kin. A guest does not go into a compound uninvited and is not shown the hearths.

### 8. The Terraces

Walled terraces above the compounds, with kitchen gardens on the lower and orchards on the upper. The orchards are old, and the apples are the Lodge's trade at the autumn fair at [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]]. A path along the highest terrace leads to the head of the stream.

### 9. The Source

The spring at the valley's head, in a hollow ringed by stone. The water rises through a crack in a flat gray slab and fills a basin no wider than a cartwheel, and from it the stream begins. Nobody fetches water here. The Tradition Keepers and the elders go to it at the solstices, and nobody else is permitted past the ring of stones.

### 10. The Washing Pool

The second pool, a wide stone-lined basin where the stream slows, with a bench round it. Everyone washes here before the great rites, and the Lodge's drinking water is drawn from the stream just above it. A guest is washed here before he is received in the Great Hall, and the washing is a part of the guest-right and no light matter.

### 11. The Tail Pool

The third pool, shallow and still, where the stream leaves the valley. Offerings are made here at first light, in silence: the things that people have made with their own hands and would miss, set on the stones at the edge. The offerings are gone by noon, and the Áelendan do not say who takes them.

## The Present {#present}

The great council meets at the turn of the season, and the senior Wardens have come in early. They count the places they cannot cover: an Order party camped below the Held Hour, another above the Singing Tarn, a survey on the Wandering Ridge that has lost a guard, a lone Warden at the Fen Door. A standing birch was cut at the Felled Stand three nights ago, and the Wardens of the Lodge have doubled their dawn walk. The Lodge knows who in the lowlands wants what, and it has not said.

A silver casket sent up for the council is overdue, and its envoy with it. The Lodge has not asked for help, and has let it be known that a hand offered in the right way would not be refused.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The overdue casket.** An envoy bearing a sealed casket for the great council is missing on the road, and the Lodge would welcome it found ([[scenario-lodgesilvbgh|The Lodge beneath the Silver Boughs]]).
- **An invitation.** A scholar of high reputation, or a party that has earned Áelendan trust, is invited up as a guest, and the invitation says nothing of the terms.
- **The cutter.** The Wardens at the Lodge want to know who cut the birch at the Felled Stand, and they want it asked outside the Lodge.
- **The word from the Door.** The lone Warden at the Fen Door has sent a message by the beacon families of Lísere, and nobody who can carry it up to the Lodge before the passes open.
