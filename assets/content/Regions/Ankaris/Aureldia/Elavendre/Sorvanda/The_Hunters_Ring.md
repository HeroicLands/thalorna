Where the envoys' road to [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] skirts the outer margin of the [[place-silvrfrsts|Silver Forests]], a long ring of standing stones runs through the trees on the uphill side of the road. The stones are waist-high, gray and grown with moss, and each is cut on its outward face with the same mark, a closed line with a single gap. Some are banded round the top with silver. The ring is too wide to see across: it encloses a fold of silver-barked wood, a stream, and a clearing at its heart that the moon finds when it finds nothing else. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] call it _Pálmirno_, a ring of standing stones, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] call it the **Hunter's Ring** when they speak of it to anyone else.

## The Hunter

The Wardens say the ring holds the ground of a fae being they name only _súldinno_, a hunter in the wood. It is not, they say, one of the seated spirits of the forest, and it does not ask, bargain or take a toll. It hunts. Within the stones it may take whatever walks there, and outside them it may take nothing at all, and the Wardens hold that it keeps that rule exactly.

Nobody living claims to have seen it plainly. Those who have come close describe a tall shape among the trunks at the edge of the eye, the sound of footsteps that keep pace with their own and stop when they stop, and a voice from above that belongs to someone they know. The Wardens teach that it takes one at a time, and that it takes the one who is alone: the man who steps aside to drink, the sentry at the far side of the fire, the sleeper behind a barred door.

:::secret {title="The voice from above"}
The hunter has no words of its own. It says only what its prey said while it lived, in its prey's own voice, and it says most often the last things its prey said, whoever they were said to. A voice in the ring is therefore a true record of a death, word for word, and that includes a death the hunter had no part in.

> [!TIP]
> Give the voice exact sentences and repeat them exactly, in the same words each time. It never answers a question, never uses a name its prey did not use, and never says anything new.

:::

The keepers' carvings and the Wardens' song both put a great tree at the ring's heart with figures hanging in its branches. The charcoal-burners of [[place-sourimael|Sóurimel]] say that what the hunter takes it carries there, and that some of what hangs in the tree is still alive.

:::secret {title="The hanging tree"}
The telling is true. The lower branches of the tree in the clearing are woven into cradles of bough and bark, and the taken hang in them, some living and silent, some dead, the oldest dry. The roots below are crowded with bones and with gear rotted to scraps, some of it still sound.
:::

It wears what it takes. A hunter caught in the ring is seen again, so the tellings run, walking at dusk in his own cloak and calling his companions by name, and a companion who goes to him is the next one hanging.

:::secret {title="What it does in a taken shape"}
In a taken shape the hunter walks its prey's last road again: from wherever the prey was taken back along the way the prey came, at dusk, to whoever the prey last spoke to, and it calls the prey's last words there. It takes the dead as readily as the living. A body left lying alone inside the line is the one alone, and the hunter wears it the next dusk as it wears any other.
:::

## The Stones and the Lodge

The Wardens say the stones are the bound of its ground. In their teaching a mark-stone standing upright closes the line on either side of it, and the space within arm's reach of an upright stone lies outside the hunt. The Wardens' word for such a bound is _thrima_, the bound of a hallowed place, and a Warden teaching the young says it as a rule: nothing walks alone in the ring, and nothing runs from it but toward a standing stone.

Who set the stones is not told. The Wardens' songs say only that they were set when the hunter was given its ground, and they do not say by whom or to what end. At the ring's lower edge, by the road, stands a long timber lodge with a stone hearth, built for the line of _thidharand_, the keepers of the boundary marks, who walked the ring each season, renewed the cut marks and kept the silver bright. The line ended, and the lodge stands empty, as the Áelendan say of a lodge whose kin are gone. The keepers carved the inside of its walls with the ring: every stone in its place, each with its mark, and the clearing at the center drawn as a tree with figures in it. A back room off the hall has one window, barred with oak, and a door barred from the inside.

The Wardens walk the ring's outside each season and do not go in. Lowland poachers, who know nothing of the stones, use the lodge as a hunting camp, since the ring holds deer that nobody else hunts.

## When the Stones Fall

A fallen stone, the Wardens say, opens the line. While the ring is whole the hunter's ground is the fold inside it and no more. When stones lie overturned the ground has no edge, and travelers caught near the ring tell what follows: a path walked out of the ring comes back to it, a blaze cut on a tree turns up ahead of the walker, and every way through the trees leads inward to the clearing. The Wardens hold that the hunter follows its prey outward as far as the opened line allows, and that the farther the gaps run the farther it walks.

Silver is the usual reason a stone falls. The bands round the stones' heads are worth prying off, and a thief who sets his bar under one finds the stone comes up with it.

## Who Knows, and What They Say

- **The Wardens** of the Silver Forests know the ring, its rule and its songs, and keep the songs' last verses to themselves. They escort travelers along the road below it and do not let them stop.
- **Sóurimel** tells its children that the deer in the stone ring belong to someone. Its charcoal-burners leave the road a mile short of the lodge and rejoin it a mile past.
- **Poachers out of the lowland** know the lodge as a good camp with fat deer near it and a run of bad luck that nobody explains.
- **The Bardic Colleges** have a song of a man followed home from the wood by his brother's voice. It is sung without its last verse.

## Reaching It

The ring lies a day's walk along the envoys' road from the last lowland steading, inside sacred territory, on a road the Wardens watch. A party traveling the road under Warden escort passes the lodge in daylight. A party traveling without leave meets a Warden before it meets the ring.

# The Truth of the Hunter's Ring {#truth .secret}

**The hunter.** The _súldinno_ is what the Wardens say it is, and its rules are exact. It takes one at a time, and only the one who is alone, living or dead. It cannot take what stands within arm's reach of an upright mark-stone, and it cannot cross a line of stones that is whole. It is very tall, long-limbed and thin, its joints bending where no joint should, and it wears the leather and hoods of its prey over a face that cannot be held in the eye. Blades find it, but it is gone before a second blow; wounding drives it off for a while, and nothing a traveler carries is known to kill it. The Wardens do not want it killed. Who gave it its ground, and why, is not known to anyone living, the Wardens included.

**The last verses.** The verses the Wardens keep to themselves are the ring's law as the Wardens use it. One who sheds kin-blood or Warden-blood on Warden ground, and is taken alive, is walked into the ring at dusk and left alone there, and the stones are the judgment. The Bardic Colleges' song of the man followed home by his brother's voice ends, in its unsung verse, with the brother's voice saying who killed him.

**The stones now.** A band of lowland poachers under **Vendrik Othral** robbed the envoy **Devrik Véldarin** on the road below the ring at dusk, three days before the Warden Sírgana comes looking, and took the silver casket he carried for the great council at Ésalosa. The envoy's escort, the Warden **Ástheren**, put an arrow into one of them, **Norven Halren**, in the fight. The band fled uphill through the ring, prying the silver bands off the stones as it went, and four stones lie overturned in a line where it passed. With the line open, the paths around the ring fold inward, and the hunter walks as far down as the road. The sack of pried bands is in the lodge; without the bands a set stone stands unbanded, and the carvings show every stone banded.

**The band.** The first night a poacher named **Torlan** stepped away from the band's fire and was taken. The band split: Vendrik and the rest went deeper into the ring with the casket, and Norven, wounded, held the lodge with the sack of silver. The hunter has taken the band one by one since. Vendrik's pack and the casket hang three branches up in the tree in the clearing; two of the thieves hang below them, one alive.

**The escort.** Ástheren kept the envoy and his two servants at a fire within reach of a standing stone on the ring's edge, and each servant was lost when he stepped from the fire. On the second dusk Ástheren went down to the lodge alone to bring the poachers to account and to take back the casket, leaving the envoy at the stone.

**What Norven did.** At dusk on that second day Norven had already watched Torlan walk back to the lodge in his own cloak, calling Norven by name. When a second figure came out of the dusk to the door, Norven held his boar-spear on it through the gap. Ástheren talked his way in. He said: "Put up the spear. I am Ástheren, of the Wardens, and I am living." At the hearth he said that he had walked the spring patrol with Sírgana of the Tírindi, and that the spring melt at the ford took her bow out of her hands. Then he turned his back to set the bar across the door, and Norven put the spear through him. Norven said, as he did it: "Stay off me. I watched it take Torlan, and Torlan came back." Ástheren fell against the door and said: "I was living." The spear-head broke off in the wound.

Norven dragged the body out by its cloak, and the cloak came away in his hands. He barred the door, hung the cloak by the hearth because he could not bring himself to open the door again to throw it out, sanded the floor white between the hearth and the door, and bound a spare head onto the spear, scraping the blood out of the shaft below it. The body lay alone outside, and the hunter took it.

**The envoy.** At the next dusk the hunter walked Ástheren's last road in Ástheren's shape: up to the envoy's fire, where Devrik wrote in his journal that Ástheren had come back for him smiling, and down again with the envoy behind it to the lodge door, where it called Ástheren's words through the boards. Norven did not open. Devrik, alone on the step, was taken, and his satchel lay on the step at dawn; Norven pulled it inside and pushed it under a bed. Devrik hangs in the clearing, alive and silent. Ástheren hangs there dead, the spear-head still in his back.

**Norven in the lodge.** Norven has been awake for two nights with an arrow wound in his side, the bar across the door and the spear across his knees. To anyone at the door he tells the story that covers the cloak: that Ástheren came to the door at dusk two nights ago already dead, sat at this hearth and went out again leaving his cloak, and that last night he came back with the envoy behind him and led the envoy into the trees. The parts about Torlan, the stones and the silver are true. He has told nobody what happened at the door, and the hunter tells it every dusk.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The rule of the one alone** is exact, and it holds for the dead.
- **The standing stone** is a true refuge, and the space within arm's reach of an upright stone is outside the hunt.
- **The voice of someone known** is never a trick of the hunter's own. It is the dead or taken person's own last words, said again.
- **The poachers' bad luck** is the hunter, and it is the stones they themselves pried up that let it down to the road.
- **The charcoal-burners' tree** is real, and some of what hangs in it lives.

> [!WARNING]
> Ástheren's last words are the one thing in the ring that tells what Norven did. A GM who has the hunter call them before the party has seen the cloak, the spear and the lodge floor spends the second reading of each.
