## Overview {#overview}

**The Felled Stand** is a ring of birch stumps at the margin of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]] nearest [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]]. The stumps are broad, cut flat and gray, with no shoot on any of them in all the years since the axes. The ring is kept clear: nothing is allowed to grow among them, and nobody sits on them. It is what is left of the stand cut in the winter of [[lore-blacksnow|the Black Snow]], and it is the reason no standing silver birch is felled in Áelendan country by anyone, for any reason.

It is sacred ground of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]], and a place they bring their own to see and nobody else.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] hold it. A Warden of the Lodge's watch walks the ring each dawn and clears it, and the young Wardens are brought to it in their first year, stood among the stumps and told the winter's account in the Tradition Keepers' words, which are not written down. The Stand lies between the Lodge and the fog-hollow [[place-talve|Talve]], and the Wardens treat the ground between as part of the watch.

The village of [[place-sourimael|Sóurimel]], inside the forests, keeps the birch law as strictly as the tribes do, and its charcoal-burners do not cut so much as a limb from a birch.

## Layers {#history}

Whoever cut the stand did so in the one winter the Áelendan hold up as the worst, and the tellings differ on what the cutting cost.

**The Áelendan account.** The people cut birch, left the smoke on the hollow and did not ask, and then went back to ask. The Stand is the place where the asking began ([[lore-theasking|The Asking]]).

**The Sinale account.** The people broke the law, came back and sat with their hands bare, and the Sinale said yes without telling them the cost ([[lore-flksinale|Sinale]]).

**The Order's reading.** A theorist of the [[affiliation-univperavel|University of Péravel]] holds that the stumps are dead from a fungus that took the roots when the trees came down, and that a graft from a living birch would sprout. No Warden will let him try.

## What Is There Now {#present}

A standing birch at the margin, a bowshot from the ring, was cut three nights ago, and the cut is clean. It was made with a saw, not an axe, by someone who knew the law and did it anyway. The Wardens of the Lodge's watch have not found who, and they have doubled the dawn walk. The young Wardens who were due to be brought to the ring this season have been kept back.

The Order is interested in the stumps. A fellow of the University has written to ask for a section of one, for the rings, and has had no answer from anyone who can give it.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbours}

- **The Wardens.** A person who cuts or burns birch in sight of the Stand is not warned.
- **The fog.** Talve lies beyond the ring, and on a spring dawn the fog comes up to the stumps and lies on them.
- **The margin.** The forest turns paths here as it does everywhere, and a traveler who walks into it for the sake of a look finds the road bending.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

**The ring.** A circle of broad stumps, each as wide as a cartwheel, set close, the ground inside bare and swept.

**The bark.** What remains on the stumps is dull. It is the one place in the forests where birch bark takes no light, even in a clear night's starlight.

**The dawn walk.** A Warden with a besom of birch twigs gathered from the ground, which is the one cutting the law permits, sweeping the ring and saying nothing.

**The margin trees.** Living birch stand close round the ring on three sides, their bark silver. The fourth side opens toward Talve, and the trees there stop.

:::secret {title="What the Wardens read at the Stand"}
The fog at Talve lies on the stumps in proportion to how thin the hollow is: the thicker it lies on the stumps, the nearer the door is to opening. The Wardens of the watch read the Stand each dawn for this and keep the rest of the world from it, because a stranger who sees fog on stumps sees only fog.

The cut birch is a test. Someone wants to learn what the Wardens do when the law is broken, and how far from the hollow their watch goes to do it. The cutter is not in the lowlands. He is an Áelendan, and he is looking for a gap in the watch.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A fresh stump.** Someone has cut a standing birch, and the Wardens want the saw.
- **The watch left for a night.** A Warden of the Lodge asks the party to sit the dawn walk with her through a short-handed week and to say afterward what they saw at the margin.
- **The section.** The Order's fellow pays to have a bit of stump brought to him, and the price rises each time he is refused.
