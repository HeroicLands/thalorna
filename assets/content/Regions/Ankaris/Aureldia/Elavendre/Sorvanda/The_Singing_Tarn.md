## Overview {#overview}

**The Singing Tarn** is a small, deep lake held in a corrie at the head of a high meadow on [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]], a _dulna_ in the Áelendan word, with a bare islet of gray stone at its center. The water is black and very clear. On still evenings, between the last sun on the peaks and full dark, voices sing from it. They sing in no tongue anyone knows, they are beautiful, and a listener who answers them, by singing back or by calling across the water, walks into the tarn and does not come out. It is an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] whose door lets in the sylphs and sirens of the pool, and it is sacred ground of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]].

People go to it for the song. The meadow below is the best summer grazing in that part of the range, and the herders who use it have lost children, dogs and men to the water. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] wants the song written down.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] hold the tarn and the upper meadow. Their watch at the tarn sits on the shore every evening of the open season with its back to the water, two Wardens with a dog between them, and when the singing begins they sing over it. The counter-song belongs to the Tradition Keepers, who teach it to the Wardens of the meadow and to nobody else. It is loud, plain and long, and it is sung until the tarn falls silent.

The herders of the meadow keep their flocks below a line of cairns a long way short of the shore, and in the evening they bring them farther down still. The Pact holds no bargain with the singers. They are owed nothing, and nothing is left for them.

## Layers {#history}

What first sang in the tarn, or when, is not known.

**The Keepers' account.** The Tradition Keepers hold that the singers are fae and not [[lore-thekindred|Kindred]]: they are not seated in the water as a river-mother is seated in a spring, and nobody has ever bargained with them. The Keepers keep a list of the people the tarn has taken, name by name, and recite it when the counter-song is taught.

**The herders' account.** The herders say that the drowned sing with the others, and that a mother who listens long enough hears her own child. The Keepers neither confirm this nor forbid it to be said.

**The Order's reading.** An Order theorist of the [[affiliation-univperavel|University of Péravel]] holds that the song is the wind across the corrie lip, shaped by the water's surface, and that the drownings are herders lost in the dark. His colleagues who have heard it do not hold this.

## What Is There Now {#present}

This summer the tarn took a herd boy from the meadow, who went up after a stray at dusk and answered. His mother sits at the shore each evening outside the Wardens' line, and the watch has twice carried her down. An Order scholar with hired guards is camped on the ridge above the corrie, waiting for a still night, with a clerk who writes music and a jar of wax for the guards' ears.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbours}

- **The water.** It is deep from the first step and cold enough to stop a swimmer's breath.
- **Shardbeaks** feed by the Araelwen-waters of the high meadows and perch on the meadow cairns ([[being-shrdbk|Shardbeak]]).
- **The Wardens,** who carry an outsider down from the shore once and do not carry him a second time.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

**The meadow cairns.** A line of low cairns across the meadow below the corrie. Above them the grass grows long, because nothing grazes it.

**The shore.** A narrow beach of gray shingle on the meadow side, and steep rock everywhere else. The watch's fire-pit stands well back from the water, facing away from it.

**The islet.** A hump of bare gray stone a long bowshot from the shore, smooth and without weed. On a clear night something pale is seen on it.

**The water.** Clear for a man's height and black below. Nothing lives in it that a herder has ever caught.

:::secret {title="The truth of the Singing Tarn"}
The singers take only one who answers. A listener who stays silent, however long and however near, is never taken, and the wax in a guard's ears is enough to keep a man from answering. The danger is that the song is answered without meaning to: a hum, a name called to a companion, a child's voice joining in.

Those who walk in are not drowned. They come up on the islet at the dark of the moon, standing in a ring, singing with the others in their own voices, and they go down again before first light. The herd boy is among them. The pale thing seen on the islet is the ring.

The counter-song does not drive the singers off. It gives the tarn a song it has no answer to, and it falls silent until the next evening. Nobody taken has ever been brought back, and nobody among the Áelendan has tried. The Keepers' list of the taken is a list of the living.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The song in ink.** The Order scholar on the ridge hires the party to guard his clerk through one still night at the shore, against the watch and against the tarn.
- **A mother at the water.** The herd boy's mother asks the party to stay with her at the shore one evening when the Wardens are elsewhere.
- **The counter-song.** A Warden of the meadow has fallen ill, and the one left on the shore needs someone to fetch a Tradition Keeper from [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] before the next still night, and someone to sit with her until the Keeper comes.
