## Overview {#overview}

**The King's Cut** is a line of sealed adits above the tree line in [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]], cut into pale stone, with spoil heaps tipped below them under the birch and a broken ore-road running down the mountain to [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]]. The first cut was made in white rock and found silver and gold. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] gave it the name it carries: it was the king's, and it was a cut in a mountain that was not his to open.

Nobody lives there and nothing is worked. People go to it with a map and a pack, hoping the old workings still hold what a king died for, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] turn them back.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The Wardens of the Spine hold it, and they hold it as they hold the rest of the mountain. They do not live at the Cut. They walk the road above Elanmere at the turn of each season and stand on the spoil heaps, and the first Warden a prospector meets takes his map of the old workings, reads it and hands it back. The map is accurate. It shows the cuts of a king who tried.

At the foot of the ore-road, where it reaches the lake, stand eleven rowans, set there by the diggers' mothers and wives. On the first night of the autumn fair a loaf is left on the stone among them, by someone nobody sees.

## Layers {#history}

The workings are the work of one season, opened by [Hóuradel]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the house of Kírethel when bad harvests left the crown unable to pay the silver that the treaty at Valdun asks, and closed by the Wardens before the season ended ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]). What the three tellings keep of it differs.

**The crown's account.** The king tested the mountains and found them closed, and the crown has not asked again except by arrangement.

**The Áelendan account.** The mountains were not the king's to open, and the Wardens closed them. They did not fight him. The tribes went home, and the guides of the interior went with them.

**The diggers' account.** The market singers keep the names of the people who went up and did not come down, and sing them where the ore-road comes to Elanmere ([[lore-roadsendlmnt|The Lament at the Road's End]]). Singers on the coast give more names than those at the lake, and the lake does not agree with the coast on whether the king's engineer is among them.

**The Order's reading.** The theorists of the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} hold that the lodes are as rich as the king believed and that a licensed survey could map them. The Wardens hold that a licensed survey is a surveyor.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The spoil heaps are being picked. Someone has been carrying silver-bearing rock down the mountain by night for a carter in [[place-lisere|Lísere]] who asks no questions, and the Wardens have seen the tracks and not yet the carriers. An Order prospecting party is in Elanmere with a map of the old workings that is better than the Wardens' own, drawn by someone who went above the tree line and came back.

The autumn fair is the time the Cut is spoken of most. The tribes release a small, closely watched quantity of worked silver at the lake on terms they set, and every person at the fair knows where the rest is.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbours}

- **The Wardens.** They say it once, on the spoil, and they do not say it again.
- **The adits.** The seals are stone and old, and a stone that has held for centuries does not announce that it is about to move.
- **The height.** The Cut lies above the tree line, where the weather comes without notice and a night out is a night's gamble.
- **The road.** The ore-road is broken, and the stretches that survive are laid with the king's stones over ground that has moved since.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

**The foot of the road.** Eleven rowans in a rough line at the lake shore, a flat stone among them with the crust of a loaf on it in autumn and nothing in other seasons.

**The ore-road.** A road of laid stone that begins well and goes to grass, broken at the streams and washed out at the turns. Wheel-ruts are still cut in its upper reaches.

**The spoil heaps.** Gray mounds under birch, grown with moss and young trees, and torn open here and there. Fresh rock lies in the open places.

**The first cut.** A square mouth in the white stone, filled to the lintel with a plug of laid rock. Marks of picks are on the walls beside it, clean as the day they were made.

**The deepest adit.** Higher than the rest, and sealed more thickly, with stone set in courses that were fitted and not piled. No pick-marks are on it.

:::secret {title="The truth of the deepest adit"}
The miners who did not come out are in the deepest adit. The Wardens did not wall them in alive: the diggers were dead when the seal was laid, and the Wardens sealed the cut over them with stone and with the burial song of the Pact, the song a tribe sings at a cairn, so that the adit is a grave and not a mine. The diggers who came down alive were told what the Wardens had done and were asked to say nothing, and they did not.

The deepest adit is also where the best silver is. Opening it for ore is the desecration of a grave, whatever the party meant by it, and the Wardens know the difference between a prospector and a robber of the dead. Whoever opens it disturbs a count the Lament does not agree on, and the Wardens keep the true one.
:::

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A map of the old workings.** A prospector at Elanmere hires guides with it, and the Wardens have the original.
- **The silver carter.** A carter in Lísere is paying for rock from the Cut, and a party sent to find the carriers meets the Wardens first.
- **The missing names.** A singer of the Minstrels' Guild at Elanmere pays to learn which names the coast sings that the lake does not, and who the extra ones were.
- **The deepest adit.** An Order scholar believes the deepest seal was set to hide the best lode, and hires guards to find out.
