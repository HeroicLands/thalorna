## Overview

The **Silver Forests** are an ancient woodland said to have been planted by the fae themselves, where the trees have bark that gleams like metal in moonlight. They lie deep in the interior of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], beyond the settled farmlands and the accessible foothills, in terrain that belongs to the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] and the wild. Even among the Áelendan, the Silver Forests are regarded with particular reverence—they are sacred territory of the highest order, protected by the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] with uncompromising strictness.

## Character

The forests take their name from the distinctive silvery sheen of their trees' bark, which catches moonlight and starlight in ways that ordinary wood does not. Travelers who have glimpsed the Silver Forests from a distance describe a shimmer that seems to hover between the branches—sometimes bright, sometimes dim, never entirely still. Whether this effect is a property of the trees themselves, a manifestation of the fae presence that lingers there, or something else entirely has never been determined by outside observers, because outside observers are almost never permitted close enough to study the phenomenon.

The forests are not large in the cartographic sense, but they are difficult to measure. Travelers who have attempted to circumambulate them report inconsistent journeys—a path that took three days in one direction required five in the other, or vice versa—and maps produced by different expeditions rarely agree. Áelendan tradition holds that the forests exist partly in the mortal world and partly elsewhere, and that they cannot be straightforwardly measured because their boundaries are not entirely spatial.

## Fae Presence

The Silver Forests are one of the thinnest places between the mortal world and the fae realm anywhere on the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. Beings that in other lands are glimpsed once in a lifetime and then doubted by the witness forever afterward are, in the Silver Forests, simply present. The Áelendan who enter the forests on sanctioned business know the protocols: what to speak, what to leave as offering, what paths to walk and which to avoid, what moonlight conditions indicate that it is time to turn back. These protocols are not improvisations; they are the codified result of many generations of experience, and they are not shared with outsiders.

## Relationship with the Sinale

The [[lore-flksinale|Sinale enclaves]] in the deep Élavendren forests are believed to have particular connection with the Silver Forests, though the nature of that connection is not something the Sinale have ever explained. Some Áelendan songs imply that the Silver Forests were grown by the Sinale in cooperation with the fae at the dawn of things; others speak of the forests as predating even the Sinale. Both versions may be true; the Áelendan do not consider these mutually exclusive.

## The Felled Stand

At the margin of the forests nearest [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] stands [The Felled Stand]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a ring of birch stumps that have never sprouted again, and the ring is kept clear. It is what is left of the stand cut in the Black Snow ([[lore-blacksnow|The Black Snow]]), and beyond it lies the fog-hollow the Wardens watch. Young Wardens are brought to see it, and nobody else is.

- **A fresh stump.** Someone has cut a standing birch, and the cut is clean.
- **The watch left for a night.** The fog-hollow has been unwatched, and something has already come through.

## Access

The Silver Forests are effectively closed to outsiders. The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] patrol their approaches with particular diligence, and trespass within the forests proper is treated as a violation of the most serious order. The crown of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]] has acknowledged this jurisdiction for as long as the kingdom has existed, and royal magistrates decline to hear complaints from those who enter unauthorized and fail to return.

In rare cases, the Áelendan have escorted specific individuals into outer portions of the forests—usually scholars of high reputation, envoys from the Sinale, or persons who have demonstrated some particular claim on Áelendan trust. Such visits are not something one arranges; they are something one is offered, on terms one does not set.
