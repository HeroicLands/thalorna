**Ceridwen's Hollow** sits in a deep fold of the [[place-brynavar|Brynavar]] hills, off the ridge roads
and out of sight of them, with perhaps two hundred and eighty people in it.

## Why It Is Still There

The Hollow's survival is entirely a matter of ground. Raiding parties out of the east move fast, take
what is on the road, and go; the Hollow is not on the road, is not visible from it, and is reached by a
track that a stranger will lose twice. Villages on better land have been burned four and five times in
the same period. The Hollow has been reached twice in three hundred years, and both occasions are still
talked about.

The consequence is a village that is old, continuous and unusually intact – its families, its customs
and its memory all older than the walls of [[place-valdun|Valdûn]].

## The Observances

The Hollow is named for a spring at its head, and what is done at that spring is not, strictly, part of
the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]] calendar. The village keeps the Twelve properly and keeps
something else besides, and has never seen a contradiction in this.

Priests sent up from the lowlands have generally reported the village devout and left it alone.
[[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] passing through the hills are received here in a way that suggests an
older acquaintance than either side discusses.

## Life

Sheep, ponies, oats and barley on the valley floor, charcoal off the slopes, and a great deal of
weaving through the winter. The Hollow's cloth is good and reaches Valdûn's fairs under other people's
names.
