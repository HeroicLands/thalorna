## Overview {#overview}

**Lámanis** lies among the orchards of the middle basin, a day's barge-haul above [[place-beravel|Péravel]], on a bend of [[place-beravriver|the Berav]] where the slope faces the afternoon sun. About three thousand two hundred people live there. The name is Élavendri, from _lámaine_ "an apple", and a visitor in the blossom weeks or at the pressing needs no more explanation of it than that.

The town makes cider, perry and the vinegar that every kitchen in the capital buys by the cask. It also keeps the oldest orchard in the basin, whose first trees the town says were planted by hand from stock the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] brought down, and at the foot of that orchard a well that the Grove Council's elders call holy.

## Arriving {#arriving}

The river road comes in between two long walls of apple trees, each tree numbered with a painted ring and each row with a name. The press-houses stand along the water with their wide doors open at the pressing, and the smell of crushed fruit reaches a barge a mile before it docks. The town's one bridge is a wooden footbridge, because the orchard-owners voted down a stone one for four generations rather than lose the trees on the bank.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

**The presses.** The press-houses work from the first windfalls to the first frost, and every one sells to the cider factors of Péravel through the town's cooperage, which makes the casks, brands them and guarantees the measure.

**The orchards,** held by families and by two houses of the middle nobility, who rent rows to tenants and take a share of the juice. A tenant who neglects his rows loses them to a neighbor at the winter assessment.

**The crown's levy.** The crown's butler holds the right in wine and its levy, and he has held the cider for some years, to the town's resentment. A levy-clerk lives in the best house on the quay and is spoken to with great courtesy by everyone and invited by no one.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The town is held of the crown and keeps its own court of the orchard-owners, which meets in the cooperage hall at the turning of the year to settle boundaries, damages and the assessment. The sheriff's reeve comes up from Péravel when the levy is in dispute and returns when it is paid. A grove-elder of the Grove Council tries cases in the old orchard and nowhere else, and the court sends him the quarrels that concern trees.

## The Present {#present}

The well at the foot of the old orchard has stopped running. It is a stone-lipped spring in a ring of ancient trees, and every household in Lámanis drew its first cupful of the year from it at the blossom festival. At midwinter the water sank, and by spring the basin was dry. The grove-elder says that the stone of the lip was taken: a party of University scholars lodged in the town in the autumn, measured the well, and left a day later with a long case on a mule. The elder has written to the court at Péravel and has been answered with an invitation to submit a petition, which is to say, to wait.

The town splits between those who want the stone back and those who do not want a quarrel with a University that buys its cider.

:::secret {title="The well-stone"}
The stone is in a chapterhouse at Péravel, entered in a catalog without the name of the well. The University's Keeper of the Collections recorded the origin in a private ledger that no one but she reads. The spring is not an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] and has no door or slow hour: it is sacred ground, kept for centuries by people who spoke to it, and it stopped because the stone it ran through was lifted out. Set back in its place by someone who sings the old words over it, it runs again within a night.
:::

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

**The Pressing Floor** is a tavern in a former press-house, warm and sticky and full in the season. **The Cooper's Rest** is a quieter house across from the cooperage, where the factors from Péravel stay and the talk is of prices. The orchards themselves are open to anyone who asks, and a bed in a loft is cheaper than either.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The stone from the well.** The grove-elder wants the [[miscgear-lamanisstone|lip-stone]] found and brought home, and will pay in kind, in introductions to the elders of the Grove Council.
- **The petition.** The levy-clerk wants the whole quarrel to go away before the autumn assessment, and would pay a party to persuade the elder to withdraw.
