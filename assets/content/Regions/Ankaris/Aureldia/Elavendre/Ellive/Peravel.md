**Péravel** stands on [[place-beravriver|the Berav]] some **fifteen miles up from the western sea**, at
the point where the tide stops and sea-going hulls must give way to barges. It is the capital of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]], the largest city in the kingdom at around fifty-five thousand
people, and the reason [[place-ellive|Éllive]] is arranged the way it is.

The city takes its name from the river, not the other way about.

## The Site

Fifteen miles is a deliberate distance. Far enough up that no raider under sail arrives without warning
and without a pilot; near enough that the tide does the work of bringing ships to the quays. Péravel has
never been sacked from the sea, which distinguishes it from most of the coast north and south of it.

The city is built on both banks and on the islands between, joined by the bridges Élavendre is famous
for—pale stone, and in the older quarters grown over with the trained living vine that visitors
always mention and residents never do.

## The University

The [[affiliation-univperavel|University of Péravel]] is the largest [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]] campus outside
the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], with four Zulen (fellowship) chapterhouses, and it makes the
capital the center of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s presence in the kingdom. The University's oldest court is the house the Empire's _felzos_ (legate) took after the treaty ([[lore-elvtribute|The Tribute of Élavendre]]).

It also makes Péravel the field on which the kingdom's central quarrel is fought.
[[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]], [[affiliation-groveconcl|the Grove Council]] and the Áelendan tradition all keep
establishments here, all dispute the Arcane Order's authority over Élavendre's supernatural life, and all
conduct that dispute—in the capital—through patronage, appointment and the placing of students
rather than through anything worse. The city's booksellers do very well out of the argument.

## Politics {#present}

The court and the University sit a mile apart and watch each other across the river. [[being-aurenthekir|Queen Áurenthe Kírethel]] reigns from the hall where every monarch is crowned, keeps the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] warm inside the walls and guided beyond them, and would like once in her reign to be asked up to [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa]]. Her heir, [[being-ielaraskir|Prince Yélaras]], was schooled at the University, keeps its fellows for friends, and says in company that the old blood and the Order's books are not enemies; his friends are planning a summer survey of the thin places, and he means to go with them. He means also to answer the bards who doubt him by having his line sung at the Elanmere fair, and [[being-kurthandlauv|Kurthand Láuvinnde]], Master of [[affiliation-clgline|the College of the Line]], has not said whether the College will sing it.

Across the river the Rector, [[being-thorenveldar|Thoren Véldarin]], holds the University's charter in trust for the crown and does not discuss what the fellows do beyond the tree line. The [[affiliation-groveconcl|Grove Council]] has petitioned the court about objects taken from groves and holy wells, the Keeper of the Collections, [[being-netharahalre|Néthara Halren]], keeps a catalog that records no provenance, and the Proctor at the gate of the Oldest Court has been told what to say about the petition. The University's Fitter of Expeditions, [[being-pelraorsel|Pelra Orsel]], settles the accounts of the guards she hires in the inns of the river quarter, and the bards sing in the same inns about the men who took the work and did not come back. The Royal Loremaster, [[being-aelithraenewyn|Áelithra Énevin]], holds a scroll that places the [[miscgear-heartofdhirikri|Heart of Dirikri]] in [[place-ukuaraelwen|Úkua-Araelwen]], and wants it fetched.

## The Economy

Everything the kingdom produces passes through. Wool and iron come down from
[[place-auldalon|Áuldalon]], the little metal and gemstone that [[place-sorvanda|Sórvanda]] releases comes
down with it, timber and dye come round the coast from [[place-lirve|Lirve]], and salt-fish comes
south from [[place-lisere|Lísere]].

What leaves is worth far more than what arrives, because Péravel is where it is worked: goldsmiths and
gem-cutters who have made an art of using almost no metal, instrument-makers, weavers, glasshouses, and
the copying and binding trades of the University quarter. The crown's steadiest revenue is the toll on
this traffic. Silver of the Sórvanda grain is for sale in the capital in larger lots than the tribes have released, and goldsmiths such as [[being-sheimarathol|Shéimara Thólmarnde]] are asked to work it without a word about its source.

## History {#history}

Péravel was the market of the Berav's lower houses before it was a capital, and those houses acclaimed the first king of the line of Orethan about 560 BF ([[lore-firstcrown|The First Crowning of Élavendre]]). He was crowned at Ésalosa; every monarch since has been crowned in the hall at Péravel, bareheaded through the Lay of the Tree Line. When the legions came into Áuldalon the city was never reached, and after the treaty at Valdun the Empire's _felzos_ took the house that is now the University's Oldest Court ([[lore-elvtribute|The Tribute of Élavendre]]). The University grew up around that house as the tributary channel opened the kingdom to the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Epistémion]], and the chapterhouses grew with it. When the line of Orethan ended, the Bardic Circle chose the [[affiliation-hsekirethel|House of Kírethel]], whose court the city has been since ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]). The city has never been sacked from the sea.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The prince's survey.** The queen wants to know where her son's friends mean to take him this summer, and wants it learned without the prince knowing she asked.
- **The petition.** A grove-elder's clerk asks the party to find one object in the chapterhouse collections and to prove where it came from.
- **Half in advance.** The Fitter of Expeditions hires guards at the river-quarter inns for a season in the interior, and will not name the ground.
