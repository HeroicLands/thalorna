## Overview {#overview}

**Ósselen** stands on a bank of firm clay in the tidal reach of [[place-beravriver|the Berav]], a few miles below [[place-beravel|Péravel]], where the river divides into channels and the reed beds begin. About six hundred and fifty people live there. The name is Élavendri, from _ossel_ "a reed", and the village is made of reed to the roof-beam: thatch, mats, baskets, hurdles, the floats of eel traps, and the withy fences that keep the tide out of the gardens.

## The Village {#the-village}

The houses stand on stilts of oak at the edge of the clay, and a boardwalk of split reed joins them. The cutting season runs from the first frost to the thaw, when the beds are dry enough to walk, and in those weeks the whole village is out in flat boats with long hooks. In summer the people fish eels, mend what the cutters broke, and sell mats to the barge-wrights of the river and to the capital's roofers.

A hedge-affiliate of the Grove Council, who is also the village's only midwife, keeps a reed-ring shrine on the highest ground, where each cutter leaves the first bundle of the year. Nobody asks what it is for, and nobody cuts the bed that surrounds it.

## The Holding {#the-holding}

The beds belong to the crown and are leased to the village for a rent paid in mats and eels at the winter assessment. The village divides them by lot each year, so that no family holds the best beds twice running. A family that loses its place at the lot keeps its boat and its hooks and is taken in by a neighbor until the next draw.

## Who Leads It {#who-leads}

The village is led by a reeve chosen at the autumn gathering, whose first duty is to keep the channel marks. Three stakes of black oak mark the safe way through the beds from the river to the village, and a reeve who lets one go loses the office. Disputes go to the sheriff at Péravel only when a body is involved.

## The Present {#present}

A body has come down on the ebb and fouled in the hurdles at the edge of the beds: a man in a hired guard's quilted coat, a strongbox-key on a cord at his neck, a black-fletched Áelendan arrow through the ribs. The reeve wants him buried before the sheriff's clerk comes down. The hedge-affiliate wants the arrow burned. The cutters want nobody to learn that the tide brought him in from the capital reach and not from the sea.

:::secret {title="The arrow"}
He was a guard hired at an inn in Péravel for a season in the interior, and he was killed on the road below [[place-gandelis|Gandelis]] by a man of his own company, not by a Warden. He had heard what the scholar he guarded meant to do at the margin and said he would not stand to it. The arrow is a Warden's, picked up from an earlier skirmish, and was driven in afterward so that his death would read as an Araelwen death: a thing that happens to guards who go where they are not supposed to. The body went into the river at the capital quays and the tide brought it here.

The strongbox-key opens a box in the hired guards' house at Péravel, which holds the scholar's contract and a second page nobody was supposed to read.
:::

## What Is Seen from It {#what-is-seen}

From the stilts the capital's pale bridges show on a clear day as a line of white on the water. At dusk the pilots' lanterns bob up the reach in a long string toward Péravel, and on a still night the sound of the sea comes up the channels like breath.

## The Cost of Reaching It {#cost-of-reaching-it}

By water, a flat boat from the Péravel quays in half a day on the ebb, and the pilot will want to be paid for the channel marks. By land, no road: the clay will not hold a cart, and the track along the dike is open to the sea at spring tides.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The reeve's burial.** The reeve will pay a party to take the body to Péravel in a boat without a flag, and to bury it in the guards' ground there under another name.
- **The key.** The strongbox-key is worth a hired guard's life to someone, and a party that carries it into the capital will be followed.
