## Overview {#overview}

**Sirlathen** stands on the north bank where [[place-beravriver|the Berav]] meets the western sea, a cluster of tarred houses on a spit of shingle, and its single business is bringing ships up to [[place-beravel|Péravel]]. About nine hundred people live there. The name is Élavendri, from _sirláth_ "a quay", which is all the village was when the first pilot's house was built on it.

No hull goes up the river without a pilot of Sirlathen. That is why [[place-beravel|Péravel]] has never been sacked from the sea.

## The Village {#the-village}

A pilots' fellowship runs the place from a long hall above the shingle, and the hall's roof carries a pole with a pennant for every hull in sight. A watch-tower of tarred timber stands on the highest dune, and a keeper sits in it through every daylight hour with a glass. The fellowship has a ship's cutter ready on the shingle at all times, and a pilot asleep in the hall in his oilskins.

Its women mend nets, and its men are pilots, boatmen or both. Every child of Sirlathen has learned the shoals before learning to read.

## The Holding {#the-holding}

The fellowship holds the right of pilotage under a crown charter, and pays for it with a fixed share of every fee to the treasury at Péravel. The right is not for sale, cannot be passed to an outsider, and descends in the house of the pilot who earned it. A house that fails to supply a pilot for three seasons running loses its place on the roll.

## Who Leads It {#who-leads}

The fellowship elects a Master Pilot for life, who assigns hulls, keeps the roll and answers for the channel. The crown's harbor-clerk lives in a stone house above the hall and counts every hull that enters, with its flag, tonnage and cargo, and files the count with the sheriff at Péravel. He has never been refused the count, and the pilots have never been offered a reason to refuse it.

## The Present {#present}

A deep-hulled trader out of Viléria has anchored off the bar with a request for a pilot, and eleven men on deck who are not sailors. The Master Pilot has been told by a fellow of the University, in a letter on the University's paper, to take the hull up on the night tide without a count and to land its passengers at the Péravel quays before dawn. The harbor-clerk has not been told. The Master Pilot has not decided whether he is being asked by the University or by the Order.

:::secret {title="The eleven"}
They are men of the Sekran's own household guard, in plain wool, and their contract is to join the University's hired guards on the next licensed expedition and to report on them. The Order does not trust the mercenaries it hires. It has been losing scholars in the interior, and it has decided that some of the men it pays to protect them are selling the Wardens the routes.

Their leader carries a sealed warrant to arrest any guard he finds carrying out an object from an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] on his own account.
:::

## What Is Seen from It {#what-is-seen}

From the dune the whole bar can be read at a glance: the white breakers, the green water that is the channel and the brown water that is not. Pilots say the channel moves a hand's breadth every winter, and the fellowship's roll of soundings is the most guarded book in the village.

## The Cost of Reaching It {#cost-of-reaching-it}

By water, the tide carries a barge down from Péravel in a morning, and a returning pilot's cutter goes up on the flood in a day. By land, a dike road along the north bank, which the sea closes at the highest spring tides.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The night tide.** The Master Pilot wants a party to be aboard the Vilérian hull when it comes up, as witnesses who are not the University's and not the crown's.
- **The harbor-clerk's count.** A harbor-clerk who has been told nothing asks the party to find out what the pilots are hiding from him, and offers the crown's good opinion for it.
