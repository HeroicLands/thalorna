## Overview {#overview}

**The Lip-Stone** is the ring of gray stone that stood at the mouth of the holy well in the oldest orchard of [[place-lamanis|Lámanis]], a day above [[place-beravel|Péravel]]. It is about as broad as a cartwheel and as thick as a hand, hollowed on its upper face like a shallow bowl and worn smooth by centuries of hands and cups. The spring rose through it. Every household of the town drew its first cupful of the year from it at the blossom festival.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The town does not know. A party of [[affiliation-univperavel|University]] scholars lodged at Lámanis in the autumn, measured the well, and left a day later with a long case on a mule. At midwinter the water sank, and by spring the basin was dry. The grove-elder of the orchard, [[being-gulmandelvan|Gulmand Élvannde]], says that the stone was taken, and he has written to the court at Péravel and been answered with an invitation to submit a petition.

:::secret {title="Where the stone is"}
The stone is in a chapterhouse collection at Péravel, entered in the catalog of the Keeper of the Collections, [[being-netharahalre|Néthara Halren]], as a bowl of worked stone, without the name of the well. The Keeper records its origin, the day it was lifted and the name of the guard who lifted it in a private ledger in a cipher of her own, and a copied leaf of that ledger is loose in the capital ([[scenario-kprscphr|The Keeper's Cipher]]).
:::

## Its Story {#its-story}

The town says the first trees of the oldest orchard were planted from stock the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] brought down, and that the well was kept before the orchard was. The grove-elders of the [[affiliation-groveconcl|Grove Council]] call it holy and sing over it at the blossom festival. Nobody in Lámanis remembers the stone being moved before the scholars came.

## What It Does {#what-it-does}

The well ran through it, and the well does not run without it. The grove-elder says so, and the dry basin agrees.

:::secret {title="The well and the stone"}
The spring is not an [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] and has no door or slow hour. It is sacred ground, kept for centuries by people who spoke to it, and it stopped because the stone it ran through was lifted out. Set back in its place by someone who sings the old words over it, the stone lets the well run again within a night. Gulmand knows the old words. He has not been asked to sing them. In a chapterhouse cellar the stone does nothing at all.
:::

## Who Wants It {#who-wants-it}

Gulmand wants it found and brought home, and he will pay in kind, in introductions to the elders of the Grove Council. The town is split between those who want it back and those who would rather not quarrel with a University that buys its cider. The crown's levy-clerk at Lámanis wants the whole quarrel gone before the autumn assessment. The Grove Council has petitioned the Oldest Court of the University about objects taken from groves and holy wells, and the stone is one of the things the petition means.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The stone from the well.** Gulmand hires a party to find the stone and bring it home before the blossom festival.
- **The case on the mule.** An innkeeper of Lámanis remembers the scholars' mule, and the carter who hired it out to them lives at Péravel.
