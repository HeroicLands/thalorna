## Overview {#overview}

**Kalvanel** lies in a fold of the foothills at the upper rim of the [[place-ellive|Éllive]] basin, where the last meadow runs into a belt of old birch and the Tribal Lands begin. About three hundred and eighty people live there. The name is Élavendri, from _kalve_ "a birch", and the white trunks come down to the back of the last house.

It is a village of a single office. Pilgrims come to Kalvanel to ask the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] for leave to see the outer margin of a sacred wood, and the village exists to lodge them, feed them and send word up the valley that they are waiting.

## The Village {#the-village}

The pilgrim-house is the largest building and is older than the village's charter: a long hall of oak with a hearth at either end and sleeping benches along the walls. Every house in Kalvanel owes it a share of firewood and bread, and each household takes a turn at keeping it, in a rota written on a board by the door.

A Tradition Keeper comes down from the hills when a pilgrim is waiting, usually within three days, and decides there whether the pilgrim may go up with an escort. Most are refused. The pilgrim-house does not say so in advance.

## The Holding {#the-holding}

The village was chartered by the crown on terms the Áelendan wrote: it lodges any pilgrim for three nights without charge, it sells no weapon and no strong drink within a mile of the hall, and it refuses its roofs and its mules to any company that comes under hire. The crown signed, and the village has kept the terms for longer than anyone's grandmother remembers.

## Who Leads It {#who-leads}

The keeper of the pilgrim-house leads Kalvanel, and the post passes by the house's own choice, not by the lord's. The present keeper is [[being-sarenasalth|Sárena Salthar]], a widow of fifty who has fed pilgrims for thirty years and has never failed to count them out as carefully as she counted them in.

## The Present {#present}

A young man has been in the pilgrim-house for two days, and no Keeper has come. He is plainly dressed, carries no weapon, speaks of the sacred country with the courtesy of a student, and writes at the long table in the evenings. He says he is a fellow of the University who has come alone to ask leave. A villager who saw him on the road says there were four men on mules behind him, who turned back at the last meadow.

:::secret {title="The pilgrim"}
He is a friend of [[being-ielaraskir|Prince Yélaras]], a fellow of the University and the leader of the summer survey the prince means to join. He has come ahead to find out whether a party that asks, and is refused, will be treated as a trespasser or as a guest. The four on the mules are the crown's, sent by the queen's chancery to see that he is not harmed.

He sincerely wants leave. If it is refused and he goes anyway, the Wardens will warn him once, and the prince's name will be in the mouth of whoever finds the body.
:::

## What Is Seen from It {#what-is-seen}

From the pilgrim-house door the birch belt shows as a pale wall under the hills, and above it the dark of the old forest begins. On still evenings the villagers hear what they say is a harp from the trees, and they do not follow it.

## The Cost of Reaching It {#cost-of-reaching-it}

A day's ride from [[place-gandelis|Gandelis]] by a track that the crown does not maintain, over a ridge that holds snow into spring. The pilgrim-house is open, and a stranger who is not a pilgrim is expected to say what he is.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The young fellow.** Sárena Salthar needs someone to sit with the young man until a Keeper comes, because she has other pilgrims to feed and does not want to be alone in the hall if the four mules return.
- **The mules.** Someone has to ask the four men why they turned back, and the village will not do it.
