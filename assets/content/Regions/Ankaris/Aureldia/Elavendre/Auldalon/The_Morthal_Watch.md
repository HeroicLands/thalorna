## Overview {#overview}

The **Morthal Watch** is the company that holds the bay road through [[place-morthalmrch|the Morthal March]]. Its Watchmen come from the March's own steadings and stand the posts in turns, its Riders are the crown's sworn men on hill ponies, and over both stands the **Warden of Morthal**, [[being-theirnmrlnth|Théironand Mórlineth]]. The Áelendan [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] have the same word in their name and hold the heights above the road, and the March's people say _the March-warden_ when they mean the crown's.

## How It Is Ruled {#how-it-is-ruled}

The crown names the Warden, who is a Captain of the Watch. He judges the Watch's quarrels, keeps its rolls and speaks for it to the crown. Under him each **Post-Serjeant** holds a post: a stone-footed tower of timber, a yard and a horn. The **Tally-Serjeant** keeps the post on the causeway and writes down everything that crosses.

The Watch keeps no seat. Its Warden rides the road between the posts with the rolls in his saddlebags, and the posts are wherever the road narrows.

A **Watchman** serves his weeks and goes home. A **Rider** serves for pay and rides the road and the valleys behind it. The worst sentence the Warden gives is to strike a man from the roll: he may not stand a post or draw the Watch's meal, and the steadings of the March are not bound to feed him.

## Current Situation {#present}

The Watch is stretched along a road that is longer than its roll. The Warden asked the crown for more Riders at the last council and was given a promise. In the meantime he has pulled Watchmen off the hill posts to keep the causeway manned, which leaves the valleys behind it to the Riders, who are few.

The Watch's other trouble is the tree line. Its Riders follow raiders to the line and stop, and the Warden has twice written to the Liaison to the Tribes asking what the Wardens do beyond it. The answer has been the same both times: the Wardens keep what is theirs.

## Arms {#arms}

The Watch is lightly armed and fast: spear, short bow and a hill pony, with the horn at each post. A horn at one post is answered by the next, and the sound runs the length of the road in the time it takes a rider to saddle. Where the road meets the shore, the horn is answered by the [[affiliation-beaconfamlys|Beacon Families]]' lamps, and the signal runs on along the coast. The Watch does not try to hold the causeway against a war-party. It slows the party there until the levy of the hills is called.

## History {#history}

The Watch was made from the steadings' own posts. After the Nordmen fleets burned the north shore the crown held the head of the bay with stone and found that the bay road was the other door, and it put the steadings' posts under a single Captain with a commission and a tally. Every Warden since has kept the crown's oath at the tree line, and the Watch's Riders learn it before they learn the road.

## Relations {#relations}

The Watch answers to the crown and takes its orders through the Captain of the [[affiliation-brdrmltia|Border Militia]]. The sheriff at [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]] reads its tally. The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] are its neighbors on the heights and its limit, and the two have had no dispute that either will name. The hill levy of [[place-valdun|Valdun]] is called when a war-party is past the causeway and does not think much of the Watch's tally.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The tally.** The Tally-Serjeant's count of the furs that crossed in autumn does not agree with the count at Sarmelis, and the Warden wants to know which of them is wrong before the sheriff does.
- **The pulled posts.** The hill posts have been thinned to keep the causeway, and a steading three valleys back asks the party to sit its night watch.
