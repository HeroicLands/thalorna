**Áuldalon**—_the hill country_—is [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]'s eastern march: some two hundred and forty thousand
people in rolling wooded hills between the [[place-ellive|Éllive]] basin and the borders beyond, rising
southward into the mountains of [[place-sorvanda|Sórvanda]].

It is good country and hard country at once. The hills are green, well watered and excellent for stock,
and they are also the road by which trouble arrives overland.

## The Raiding

Where [[place-lisere|Lísere]] is raided from the sea, Áuldalon is raided by land. Bands of
[[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] out of [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm]], and Varokh war-parties ranging
south out of [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]], come down through the eastern hills, many of them along [[place-trbtrd|the Tribute Road]]—not as armies, and
not seasonally enough to plan around comfortably, but often enough that no generation of Áuldalon has
been without it.

So the region is built for it. Towns are walled or ditched; steadings cluster; stock is grazed within a
day's drive of somewhere defensible; and the hill levy ([[affiliation-brdrmltia|the Border Militia]]) is the most experienced body of fighting men in
Élavendre and knows it. Áuldalon's opinion of the court at [[place-beravel|Péravel]] is that it is charming, cultured, and
has never once arrived in time.

## The Economy

- **Wool and cattle**, the region's staple and its bulk export. The great drove roads run west from the
  hills down to the Éllive markets, and the droving families are a power of their own.
- **Hill ponies**—small, sound and famously bad-tempered; Élavendre's baggage and border-patrol
  animal, sold across [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia]].
- **Iron.** Áuldalon has no gold and no gems, and unlike [[place-sorvanda|Sórvanda]] it is permitted to dig.
  Its iron is ordinary, plentiful and entirely uncontested, and it arms the kingdom.
- **Charcoal and timber** off the lower slopes, feeding the bloomeries.
- **Hides, horn and tallow**—the unglamorous half of a stock economy, and steady money.

The hills also take a quiet cut of the trade that crosses them: pack-trains from the east pay for
escort whether or not they wanted one.

## The Lost Cohort's Camp

Somewhere in the woods of the hills stands the ditched camp of the cohort that followed a path into them in the war of the tribute and was not seen again ([[lore-elvtribute|The Tribute of Élavendre]]). Hunters find it. A ditch, a bank and the lines of the tents are there under moss, and a hunter who goes back for a second look does not find the path he came by. The path to the camp is never the same path twice.

- **A helmet.** A hunter brings back an imperial helmet that has not rusted.
- **Antiquarians.** [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Arcane Order]] scholars want the camp, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] are unwilling to give it to them.

## Current Situation {#present}

The march is short of men in a season that needs them. The Order hires guards for the interior in the inns of [[place-valdun|Valdun]] at wages the crown does not match, and [[being-elenorthaurn|Captain Élenor Thaurnde]] of [[affiliation-brdrmltia|the Border Militia]] keeps the sworn company at strength only by moving serjeants from quiet posts to loud ones, while [[being-jeldormorlin|Jeldor Mórlineth]], the Hill-Captain, wants the hill levy led by men who know the ground and not folded into her plan. On the bay road the [[affiliation-morthalwtch|Morthal Watch]] has pulled its hill posts to keep the causeway manned, and its Riders chase raiders to the tree line and stop there with the raiders in sight.

The old war is stirring as well. A hunter has brought into Valdun an imperial helmet that has not rusted and will not say where in the woods he found it, and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]]'s antiquarians in the town are paying for his road to the lost cohort's camp. The drovers of the southern hills hold that only one place in the margin keeps iron bright, and they drive their flocks the long way round it.
