The Aureldian calendar has no name for the night, and the priests sent up from the lowlands to look at [[place-kelpasi|Kélpasi]] do not ask. The village sets a place at its cold spring, and the village calls it **the Spring Guest**.

## The Night

At the first thaw, when the spring at the head of the village first runs clear, every hearth of Kélpasi sends its _sorhand_ to the flat stones where the [[affiliation-thrglnklps|Thárgalon]] sits. The council does not sit that night. The Keeper of the Spring sets a bowl of the spring's water, a loaf and a place on the stones for a guest, and the hearth-heads sit round it in silence until the loaf is broken.

If an Áelendan has come down out of the hills in the days before, the place is his. He is washed at the spring, given the bread and the water, and asked nothing, and the village feeds him through the night. If no Áelendan has come, the loaf is left on the stone and the bowl is left beside the water, and in the morning both are gone, and the village does not say who took them.

## What It Does Not Say

Kélpasi has stayed out of the raiders' roads for three centuries, and the village does not say why the Spring Guest is kept. The Áelendan who pass through the hills are received in Kélpasi as people with an older acquaintance than either side discusses, and the Spring Guest is the one night of the year on which the acquaintance is shown.

The lowland priests have reported the village devout. The Speaker to the Lowlands has never been asked about the night and has never been given reason to volunteer it.

## What a Party Finds

A party that arrives in Kélpasi at the thaw is given the dry barn and bread and is asked, courteously, to keep to the barn until morning. A party that has an Áelendan in it, a Warden's guest or a shaman's, may be asked to sit the night. The village feeds it and says nothing.
