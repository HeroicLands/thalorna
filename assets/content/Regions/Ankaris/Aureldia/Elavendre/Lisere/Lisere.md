**Lísere** is [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]'s northern coast: a quarter of a million people along the southern shore of
[[place-liserebay|Lísere Bay]], from which the region takes its name.

The climate is **cold but temperate**—gray, wet, windy, and never quite as hard as its latitude
suggests, the sea keeping the frost off. The country behind the shore is low, boggy in places, and
thickly wooded; the coast itself is shingle and low headland rather than the cliffs of
[[place-lirve|Lirve]] to the southwest.

## Across the Water

The bay is the region's whole strategic problem. Sheltered water is not a frontier; it is a
crossing, and it is crossed, and at the head of the bay the shore runs on into Vristwald by land.

[[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]] lies on the far shore—wild forest country held by the Varokh
tribes, with large bands of [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] ranging through it out of
[[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm]] to its east and south. Neither is a kingdom that could invade
Élavendre. Both raid it constantly, and Lísere is where they land.

The consequence is a coast that is settled defensively: villages set back from the water, beacon
stations on every headland, boats drawn up where they can be got away, and a levy that musters
faster than any in the kingdom. Lísere regards [[place-ellive|Éllive]]'s complacency as its principal
export problem.

## The Nordmen

The bay is the nearer threat; the older one comes from the open sea.

**Nordmen** raiders out of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] have worked this coast for centuries, in
seagoing ships that have no need of the bay's shelter. Where a Varokh
war-party takes cattle and goes home, a Nordmen fleet arrives without warning, strikes anywhere on the
northern shore, and is gone before a levy can be raised.

There is accordingly **no warmth whatever between Élavendre and the Nordmen**, and there has not been
in living memory or in any memory the chroniclers preserve. It is the one foreign question on which the
courtly, conciliatory, endlessly diplomatic Élavendri have never been willing to be reasonable, and
Lísere is the reason: the north has been paying for that history for a very long time, and paid most of
it before [[place-beravel|Péravel]] took much notice. ([[lore-northshorefire|The Burning of the North Shore]])

## The Economy

- **Fish, and a great deal of it.** Cold northern water and a sheltered bay make the best fishery in
  Élavendre. Salt-fish and oil go south to Éllive and out through [[place-calypsacity|Kalipsa]].
- **Kalipsan transit.** Kalipsa lies some seventy-five miles off the coast
  to the north, just outside the mouth of the bay, and a great deal of Élavendre's northern trade is handled
  through it—including goods nobody wishes to declare.
- **Timber and pitch** from the coastal forest, for the shipyards.
- **Furs**, most of which are not Élavendren. They come across the bay in small boats, traded quietly
  with Varokh who would rather deal than raid, and the trade continues in seasons when the raiding does
  too.

## Places {#places}

**[[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]]** is the one harbor on the bay that takes ships, a town of fish, ship timber and the Kalipsan trade, and the seat of the crown's sheriff of Lísere. **[[place-durmelen|Durmelen]]** is the crown's fort at the head of the bay, where the Great Fire of the beacon chain stands and the shore's levy musters. **[[place-hendrel|Hendrel]]** and **[[place-harnasel|Harnasel]]** are beacon villages on the road, the families of the chain's southwest stretch. [[place-coastroad|The Coast Road]] joins them, a short way behind the shingle, from the border of [[place-lirve|Lirve]] to the fort.

Three bodies keep the shore: the [[affiliation-sarmelisconcl|Quay Council of Sarmelis]] holds the harbor, the [[affiliation-durmelengrsn|Garrison of Durmelen]] holds the fort, and the [[affiliation-beaconfamlys|Beacon Families]] hold the lamps.

## Olvalonke and the Fen Door {#present}

Behind the shore lies **[[place-olvalonke|Olvalonke]]**, the evening wood: wet, thick forest of alder, oak and birch over bog and black water, running back from the shingle toward the hills. Its deep parts are sacred ground of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]], and in its depth stands the one [[lore-aralwen|Araelwen]] the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] keep this far from the mountains: [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]], a cold cleft through which demons and fell things come on the dark nights of winter. A watch of two Wardens sits by it through every night, as the oldest watch sits on Talve, and comes down from the mountains by turns. The beacon families of the nearest stretch of shore sell the watch its bread and keep their own beacons lit all winter, raid or no raid.

The Door is not the coast's only uncanny place. [[place-dskfrd|The Dusk Ford]] behind the shingle is told to be a pact a beacon-keeper asked for, and nobody keeps it as the Wardens keep an Araelwen.

This winter the watch at the Door is down to one Warden, and no relief can come down before the passes open. An [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] scholar who heard as much at [[place-calypsacity|Kalipsa]] has hired guards on the coast and means to camp at the Door through the dark nights to see what comes through. He hires them on the quay at [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]], at wages that draw the best young hands from the boats, the levy at [[place-durmelen|Durmelen]] and the lamps of the [[affiliation-beaconfamlys|Beacon Families]].

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar at the Door.** The Order scholar hires the party on the Lísere coast to keep him at the Fen Door for a fortnight, and the Warden there is alone.
- **The relief.** The Wardens need word carried up to the mountains, through passes still closed, that the watch at the Fen Door is down to one.
- **The boom and the fire.** The sheriff at Sarmelis and the Castellan at Durmelen each want the other's levymen back from the Order's wages, and each will pay a party to find out who is paying them.
- **Smugglers' tracks.** A Kalipsan smuggling crew that runs through Olvalonke by night wants an escort past the part of the wood the coast avoids.
