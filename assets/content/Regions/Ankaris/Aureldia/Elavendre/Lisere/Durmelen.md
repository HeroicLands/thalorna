## Overview {#overview}

**Durmelen** is the crown's fort at the narrows of [[place-liserebay|Lísere Bay]]. It stands on the last rise of the Élavendren horn, where the two arms of the bay come nearest, and from its tower the water to the far horn is thirty miles. On a very clear morning the hills of [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]] show as a smudge on the northern edge of the sea. About 340 people live inside the wall and below it: the garrison, the families it brings, a smith, a chandler, and the boat-crews who keep the fort's cutters.

Its work is three plain tasks. It sees what crosses the narrows. It lights the Great Fire so that the whole [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shore knows. It holds the shore's muster until the levy is in.

## History {#history}

The first tower stood on the headland where the earliest Nordmen landings struck the shore. The chronicles at [[place-beravel|Péravel]] put the raising of the stone fort in the years after [[lore-southrnfleets|the Southern Fleets]] burned the north shore, when the crown swore it would make no treaty with the Nordmen. Every Castellan since has held it under that oath, and the fort's gate has never been opened to a Nordman who came under a flag.

The name is built on _dúrmera_, the Élavendri word for a wall: the wall at the end of the land.

## The Present {#present}

The fort is thin this winter. The Castellan has asked [[place-beravel|Péravel]] for another sixty men and has had an answer that says the matter is under consideration. The muster rolls show the shore's levy at its proper strength, but the best of the shore's young men are on the quays of [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]] hiring out as an [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] scholar's guards, at wages the crown does not match.

A Varokh boat under a green branch has lain on the beach in the lee of the point for four days. The Varokh in it are not raiders; they carry a woman who says she is a headman's kin and asks for the Castellan by name. The Castellan has not decided whether a crown fort that has refused every Nordman may hear a Varokh who comes unarmed, and the whole garrison is waiting to learn which way he turns.

## The Holder {#holder}

The Castellan is [[being-morenothral|Moren Othral]], a knight of fifty-odd winters whose father held the fort before him. He is careful, tired and a stickler for the muster roll. He knows that the Varokh furs reach Sarmelis with Kalipsan marks and has chosen not to ask, on the principle that a Varokh who trades is a Varokh who is not landing. He keeps the oath against the Nordmen to the letter and the shore's other raiders by the measure of what keeps his men alive.

## The Garrison {#garrison}

The [[affiliation-durmelengrsn|Garrison of Durmelen]] is a standing company of the crown's sworn men under a Castellan, with the levy of the shore's villages in turns beside it. A sworn man serves a term for pay and a share of the fort's stores; a levyman comes up from his village for his weeks, brings his own spear and a sack of meal, and stands the night watch with the sworn men. The fort keeps four long oared cutters hauled up in the lee, built to run down the shallow Varokh boats in a calm and useless against a Nordmen ship in a sea. A watch of two stands the Great Fire on the tower every night of the year.

## Government, Faith and Trade {#government-faith-trade}

The crown holds the fort, and the crown's law runs in it as in any royal fortress. The Castellan judges the garrison and settles what the fort's households bring him. A case between a soldier and a townsman of Sarmelis goes to the sheriff. A grove-elder from the nearest village comes up for the turning days to hallow the fire. The fort has no priest of its own and misses none.

Trade is a chandler's and a smith's: pitch, rope, nails, lamp oil, fish bought off the passing boats. What the fort wants it sends for to Sarmelis by the Coast Road, and a wagon takes two days.

## Within the Walls {#within-the-walls}

A wall of fitted beach stone rings the rise, twice the height of a man, with one gate on the road side and a postern to the boat-lee. Inside it stand the tower, a long hall that sleeps two shifts, the stores, a smithy and the Castellan's house, which is two rooms over a granary. The Great Fire is a basket of iron the width of a cart-wheel on the roof of the tower, laid ready with split pine and pitch. A well in the court is the only water, and a rock cistern under the hall holds the rain.

Below the wall, the lee holds the cutters on log slips and a huddle of boat-crews' houses. Nothing grows on the point but salt-grass.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The green branch.** The Castellan lets the Varokh woman speak, and what she says is a warning of a muster of boats across the bay. He asks the party to carry the message to the sheriff and to find out whether it is true before Péravel is told.
- **A thin watch.** The garrison is short two lamp-hands for the Great Fire. The Castellan will hire the party to stand the beacon watch for a month if they can be trusted with the iron basket and the signals.
- **The oath.** A Nordman sailor washed up on the point alive and asks for the Castellan's hospitality, and the garrison is divided on what the oath requires.
