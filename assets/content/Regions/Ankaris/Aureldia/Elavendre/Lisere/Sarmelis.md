## Overview {#overview}

**Sarmelis** is the one harbor on [[place-liserebay|Lísere Bay]] that takes ships and not only boats. It stands on the bay's southern shore on a bend of the shingle, behind a long stone mole and a headland that carries the town's beacon, and about 5,600 people live in it. Every other landing on the [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shore is a beach where boats are hauled up; Sarmelis has quays, a shipyard, smokehouses and a fish market that sets the price of salt-fish for the whole north. It is the seat of the crown's sheriff of Lísere, and the [[place-coastroad|Coast Road]] runs through its gates.

[[place-calypsacity|Kalipsa]] lies off the mouth of the bay, two days by [[place-kalipsarun|the Kalipsa Run]], and a good part of what Sarmelis lives on is Kalipsa's business done on Élavendren ground.

## Arriving {#arriving}

A traveler on the Coast Road smells Sarmelis before the town shows: woodsmoke, fish oil and brine. The road comes along the shingle past drying racks and net-sheds, then between the salt pans, and the first thing the eye holds is the Lamp Tower on the headland, a squat tower of black beach stone with an iron basket on its roof. Behind it the town climbs a low rise in tiers of tarred timber and shingle-stone, with the sheriff's hall at the top.

A wall of beach stone closes the landward side, with a road gate on the southwest and a gate to the headland path on the northeast. The harbor mouth lies between the end of the mole and the foot of the headland, and a boom of chained logs lies across it. The gatewarden at the road gate asks a stranger's business and takes the crown's road toll for any wagon. He does not search a wagon unless he has been paid to or told to.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

**Salt-fish and oil.** The cold water of the bay gives the best fishery in Élavendre. The boats go out before light and come in with herring, cod and the great flat fish the town calls plaice-kings. The catch is split on the quay, salted in pans along the shore, hung in the smokehouses or rendered into lamp oil. The rendering sheds stand at the north end of town for a reason, and the wind is watched.

**Ships.** Timber and pitch come in from the coastal forest, and the shipyards below the mole build cutters, fishing luggers and an occasional coaster for the Kalipsa trade. The ropewalk is the longest building in town.

**Kalipsa's cargo.** Sarmelis is where the north's goods change hulls. Kalipsan ships lie at the outer quay, and what goes aboard them is entered at the crown's tally-house only in part. A lot of the cargo is furs. The furs are not Élavendren; they come across the bay in small boats from [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] hands that would rather trade than raid, and by the time they reach the outer quay they carry a Kalipsan mark.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-sarmelisconcl|Quay Council of Sarmelis]] governs the quay, the market and the boom. Its Reeve of the Harbor gives the berths, sets the boom, and speaks to the crown's officers for the town. The crown's own law runs in the sheriff's hall: the **sheriff of Lísere**, [[being-saltenelarve|Salten Elarven]], tries the crown's pleas, holds the posse and collects the road toll and the duty on whatever is entered at the tally-house. A lieutenant of the Admiral keeps the crown's three cutters at the mole and answers to the Admiral for the coast.

The two offices are courteous with each other in public. The council holds that the quay is the town's and the tally-house is the crown's, and the sheriff holds that the quay is wherever a crown cargo lies. A quarrel in the market is settled by the council. A killing, a theft from a crown cargo or a raider found in the town goes to the sheriff.

## The Present {#present}

The town is short of hands. The levy of the shore muster at [[place-durmelen|Durmelen]] in the dark months, the boats need crews for the winter fishery, and an [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] scholar newly up from Kalipsa has taken rooms over a sail-loft on Salt Row and is paying guards' wages above the sheriff's rate. He means to camp for a fortnight of dark nights at [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]], which no stranger may enter and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] hold against everyone, and the men he is hiring are the men the boats and the levy want.

The Reeve of the Harbor resents the Order's gold and the sheriff resents the scholar. The beacon families of the shore, whose sons are the best of the hands, resent them both, because the Order's guards are bound for the one place on the coast the families keep their lamps lit against.

## Where a Party Lodges {#lodging}

**The Lamp and Oar** on the inner quay is the house of the boat-masters. It is loud, honest and half-owned by the council. **The Salt Row boarding-houses** let beds by the night to deckhands and carters, and every one of them lets the sail-loft's news travel. **The Hawser**, at the outer quay, takes Kalipsan captains and their factors, asks nothing and charges for it.

## History {#history}

The town began as a landing under the shingle where the boats of the first coast families were hauled out of the weather. The Nordmen fleets burned it more than once, and the stone wall, the mole and the boom were built each time on the foundation of what the fire left. The crown at [[place-beravel|Péravel]] has never forgiven the Nordmen the burnings, and Sarmelis keeps the boom shut on the first fire of a raid as an oath kept by habit.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar's guards.** The Order scholar needs eight guards by the first dark of the month and will pay in Kalipsan silver. A boat-master offers the party a better price to crew a lugger through the winter fishery and be at sea when the scholar sails.
- **The mark on the furs.** A tally-clerk at the crown's house has noticed that a bale of Kalipsan furs carries a Varokh knot under the mark, and wants someone outside the town to find out whose boat brought it across.
- **The boom.** The boom was closed last night on a false fire, and nobody on the headland will say who lit it.
