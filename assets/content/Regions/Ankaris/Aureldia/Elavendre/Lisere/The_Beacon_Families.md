## Overview {#overview}

**The Beacon Families** are the lines of the [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shore who keep the chain of beacons from headland to headland. Each line holds a station and a village behind it, and the heads of the lines sit together as a council that keeps the chain as one. [[place-hendrel|Hendrel]] and [[place-harnasel|Harnasel]] are two of them, and the crown holds the shore with their lamps as much as with its fort.

## How It Is Ruled {#how-it-is-ruled}

A **Head of a Line** is the oldest of a line's household. He keeps its station, holds its fuel and sits on the council. A **Lamp-Hand** is any member who has stood a night on a station and takes a place in its rota. A **Shoredweller** is a villager who does not stand a watch.

The council chooses a **Chain-Keeper**, who holds the roster of stations, the order of the signals and the tally of fires, and who speaks to the Castellan at [[place-durmelen|Durmelen]]. The **Reckoner of Fuel** counts the pine, the pitch and the oil that each station draws and settles what each village owes the chain. The **Speaker to the Crown** treats with the sheriff and the crown's clerks for the chain's right to fuel from the forest and its release from part of the levy.

To be put out of the chain is the heaviest sentence the council has. A man struck from every line's roll may not stand a beacon, draw fuel or sleep in a beacon-house, and the whole shore knows the name.

## Current Situation {#present}

The chain is under strain. The Order's gold at [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]] is pulling lamp-hands off the stations, and a winter beacon with one man on it is a beacon that a raid can darken. The Chain-Keeper, an old Head of a Line of the stretch toward Durmelen, has asked the council to forbid its lamp-hands to hire out, and the council is divided.

The council has a second trouble that it does not discuss. The stretch behind the [[place-dskfrd|Dusk Ford]] is raided less than the rest, and the stretch nearest [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]] keeps its lamps lit every night of the winter whether a raid is feared or not. The lines at Hendrel and Harnasel each ask the council, in their own way, that the chain be left as it is.

## Arms {#arms}

The chain has no arms but its lamps. A station is a stone hut, a stack of laid pine under an oiled hide, a pair of lamp-hands and a flat-stone path to the road. A fire at one station is answered at the next within the minute, and the signal runs from the Great Fire at Durmelen to the Lamp Tower at Sarmelis and on to the stations nearest Lirve. A lamp-hand carries a horn for fog.

## History {#history}

The first beacon-keepers were the families whose boats were raided first and who lit a fire on the nearest headland to warn the next stretch of shore. The fires grew into a line, and the line into a chain that every village on the shore keeps. The crown's fort at the narrows stands at the chain's head, and its Great Fire leads the lamps, which the crown learned to rely on before it learned how the families keep them.

## Relations {#relations}

The families answer the Great Fire at Durmelen and keep the garrison's signals; their relation to the Castellan is respectful and each side knows what the other can refuse. The [[affiliation-sarmelisconcl|Quay Council of Sarmelis]] hires their sons and supplies their pitch. The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] buy their bread at Harnasel. The families keep their own counsel on the Dusk Ford and on the Door, and the crown's sheriffs do not ask.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The dark chain.** The Chain-Keeper asks a party to ride the stations from Hendrel to Sarmelis and learn why three beacons failed to answer on the same night.
- **The Order's hire.** A Head of a Line whose three lamp-hands have signed with the Order scholar asks the party to stand a month's nights on his station, because he trusts no one from the shore.
