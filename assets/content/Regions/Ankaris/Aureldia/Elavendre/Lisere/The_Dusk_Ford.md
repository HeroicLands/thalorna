A day's walk inland from the [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shingle, where the coastal forest thins into reed and black water, a slow river crosses the old drove track between two beacon stations. By day the track runs into the water and stops, and the bank on the far side is a wall of reed. At the long dusk, between the last light and the moment the first star clears the western trees, a ford stands: a line of flat stones, pale in the failing light, leading across to a causeway that does not exist at any other hour. The coast calls it **the Dusk Ford**, and in [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] _Ólvarne_, evening as the wood reckons it.

## The Toll

A figure stands at the first stone. It is tall and thin, dressed in what looks like woven reed, with a face that sits oddly in the half-light and is never quite where the eye leaves it. Those who have crossed call it the [Toll-Keeper]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}. It does not bar the way and does not demand. It asks. It asks a traveler what he will give that he will not miss until he is across.

The answer is the toll, and it is real. A man who gives his mother's face finds, on the far bank, that he cannot call it to mind. A man who gives the name of his horse does not know the horse again. The Toll-Keeper does not take anything unoffered, and it accepts a refusal without anger: the stones sink, the water rises, and the traveler stands on the near bank with the reed behind him.

A crossing in each direction asks a toll. The return is heavier than the going, and heavier again by the weight of what a traveler carries across it.

## The Far Bank

The causeway ends at a low shore of grey mud and black stakes, and on the mud lie ships. They are Nordmen longships and Varokh river-boats, rotted to their ribs, with their oars shipped and their gear stowed. The crews sit on the benches. They are the **Forfeit-Men**: raiders who crossed to rob the far bank, were asked what they would give, and gave their names. A man who has paid his name cannot be named, cannot name himself, and cannot rest. They sit at the benches and wait for a roll to be called.

Behind the ships stands a long low house of stakes and reed, the **tally-house**. It holds the forfeits: a shelf of knotted cords, bone tokens, little coils of hair and braided straw, each tied to something the payers gave. The raiders' plunder lies in heaps along the walls. Nothing in the house is guarded but by the rule that what is taken from it carries its owner's unpaid debt.

## Why It Stands

The Ford was asked for. A beacon-keeper of the Lísere shore, in the generation after the first Nordmen landings, walked out to the reeds at dusk with a lantern and asked the thing that kept the water to stand between the raiders and the land. The Toll-Keeper answered in its fashion. It keeps a crossing that takes from any who cross, and it favors the line of the beacon-keeper who asked, whose kin cross free. The raiders who come through the bog behind the shore pay their names and stay.

Lísere does not speak of it. A beacon-station that keeps its light lit on the stretch of shore behind the Ford is raided less than its neighbors, and nobody wishes to know why.

## Who Knows, and What They Say

- **The beacon families** of the northern shore know the Ford exists and say only that the track is not to be crossed after the lamps are lit.
- **[[affiliation-groveconcl|A Grove Council elder]]** at the coast says the Ford is a pact kept and a pact asked for, and that it holds the shore as the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] hold the wood: by being what it is.
- **[[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]]** have a ballad of the Forfeit-Men, and the last verse is the one the bards do not sing.
- **Smugglers** of the [[place-calypsacity|Kalipsa]] trade use the track by daylight and the bog by night, and have lost three crews who used it at dusk.

## Reaching It

The track runs inland from the shingle between two beacon stations of [[place-lisere|Lísere]]. It is visible from the shore road as a gap in the reeds, and impassable until the sun is down. A traveler who arrives at dusk finds the stones already laid.

## Hooks

- **A bard's last verse.** A College bard who crossed in search of the ballad's ending has not come back, and his master pays well for the bard or the verse.
- **A raider's hoard.** The Forfeit-Men's plunder is real silver, real iron and real amber, and the ships' gear includes a chieftain's sword.
- **A name.** A boy of the shore lost his father to the Ford and has been forgotten by his own mother. He does not know why.
