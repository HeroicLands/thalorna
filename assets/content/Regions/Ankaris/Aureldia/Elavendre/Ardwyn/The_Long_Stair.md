Seven falls in succession where a headwater of the [[place-drenavrspn|Drenavar Spine]] drops through a
stepped gorge, each pool deeper and colder than the last. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] do not treat
the Stair as seven things. It is one water and one spirit—[[lore-aranwyspr|Aranwy]]—and the
offerings are made at the lowest pool, because that is as far as anyone is permitted to go by daylight.

## The Gorge

The gorge is narrow, wet and loud, cut into the rock over a length of rather less than two miles, and
in shadow for most of the day. The falls are not of a size to astonish anyone who has seen mountains
before; what makes the Stair remarkable is its regularity—seven drops, each into a plunge pool, each
pool deeper and colder than the one below it, stepping up the gorge as evenly as if it had been built.

Aranwy's power runs the length of it and **stops where the water slows**, at the tail of the lowest
pool. The Áelendan are precise about that boundary and expect visitors to be.

## The Offering

Made at the **lowest pool**, at **first light**, in **silence**, and consisting of something the giver
**made with their own hands and would rather have kept**.

Every clause of that is load-bearing. Aranwy is _appeased_ rather than venerated: she is neither
malicious nor kind, her requirements are specific, and she does not accept an approximation of them. A
thing bought for the purpose is not an offering. A thing made carelessly to discharge an obligation is
not an offering, and shamen who have dealt with her a long time hold that she is more interested in the
**making** than in the losing.

What is given is not retrieved. The pool is deep, the water is very cold, and nobody goes in after
anything.

The practical consequence is a small, constant craft-economy in the tribes about the Stair: carvings,
worked leather, small metalwork and turned wood, made slowly and well by people who intend to give them
away and do not want to.

## The Prohibitions

Aranwy's rules are few and absolute, and Áelendan children learn them in order:

- **Nobody climbs the Stair by daylight.** This is the first one taught.
- **No fishing above the third fall.** Below it, freely.
- **No swimming anywhere in the seven.** Crossing at the foot is permitted.

## The Daylight Question

The first prohibition is the one outsiders always ask about, because of how it is worded. It does not
forbid climbing. It forbids climbing **by daylight**, and the Áelendan have had a very long time to
notice this.

What the prohibition protects is not agreed upon. Some hold that the upper falls are simply Aranwy's
own and not to be overlooked; some that the wording is older than its reason and has been transmitted
intact past the point where anyone understood it; a few, quietly, that the phrasing is exact and means
what it says.

Aranwy has never been asked directly. Asking would require climbing.

## Outsiders

The Stair is deep in the [[place-alndntrblnds|Áelendan Tribal Lands]] and the
[[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] watch the approach. Lowlanders have been brought here—the Stair is
not secret, and the tribes are not ashamed of it—but always accompanied, always to the lowest pool,
and always with the offering explained beforehand rather than afterward. Visitors who arrive with a
gift they purchased are gently told to come back another year.
