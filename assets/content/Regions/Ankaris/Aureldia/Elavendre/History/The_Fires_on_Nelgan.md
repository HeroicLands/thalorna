The Áelendan mothers have a name that quiets a child. It is **Dûlthân**, the Deep Dark, and it is the name of the chief who came over [[place-nelgan|Nélgan]] with fire.

## The Fires

One spring there were fires on the pass at night. They belonged to the folk who had pushed the Áelendan's fathers west, late but come, for grass, for sheep and for a fight. A war-band of the host that was then taking the forest east of the mountains ([[lore-takingwood|the Taking of the Wood]]) had burned two valleys to the bark behind it, and the chief at its head was Dûlthân.

A Dhárgan led the Áelendan then, a descendant of the first, with the first one's name and face. He stood the people at the edge of the trees below the pass with their spears up and did not send the children past the line. He went forward alone with his hands bare, to where Haulera could hear, and asked her to take the children in. The people would stay outside and keep the fire away from the wood, he said, and they did not ask to come in.

Haulera had set this task to the first Dhárgan lives before and had not known what it would come to: to stand with fire behind them and ask, and not to drive. She turned the whole wood for the Áelendan's sake, which she had once refused to do, so that the paths ran straight for the Áelendan and in rings for the raiders. The children went into the wood and heard nothing of the people outside, no shout and no word, and the silence was worse than fire.

## The Brands

The raiders came at dusk with brands. The Áelendan held the edge and the first spears flew. The brands passed over the line and into the birch, and a stand of it caught.

The Sinalë do not fight, and no song puts out a fire. Haulera looked at the burning for a long time and raised her hand. She walked out through the fire, and the whole of the Sinalë came out of the wood after her into the light of the raiders' own fire, in sight of men who had never seen them. Half the raiders ran. The rest stood like stones, and the brands dropped.

Dûlthân stayed. He threw one brand at the nearest tree, and it caught. Dhárgan went up to it and took it out bare-handed, and it burned through his hand. Dûlthân's spear took him in the side. Dûlthân held the brand until it died in his own burned hand, and then sat down where the trees thin. Haulera looked at him, and that was all she did. He turned and ran, and his men ran after him. No man of them came back that year, or since.

## The Giving-Over

In the morning the Áelendan counted, and Dhárgan lay speared and breathing. The children came out of the wood, and the Sinalë came out with them. The people had not seen the Sinalë in the light before. They are by day too tall and too still, and not the gods the Áelendan had made of them.

The wounded Dhárgan was laid in the court of worked stone at [[place-ethalosse|Ethalossë]], where the last trees thin toward the pass. Haulera spoke aloud, so that all could hear, which the Sinalë do not do. She told them they had not earned the wood, that nobody earns a wood, that they had broken it and would break more and would still come back each time with their hands spread. She told them that was the reason for the gift: not that they had kept it, but that they wanted it and had no deeper want. The wood had never been hers, and she gave what was not hers to give, which is how a thing like this is given. The night was the Sinalë's, she said, and the day the Áelendan's. The wood woke to them. They were to be its first light.

That is the day the Áelendan were named, in the Sinalë tongue, first light. Dhárgan sat up in the court and said, "To keep it," and lay back down, and the wood passed to them. Haulera smiled. The Áelendan set the watch again that day with Dhárgan's sons and theirs, and they walk a wall that is not there.

Haulera told the people the name the Sinalë had kept since the first Dhárgan sat at the wood's edge. It was **Vilmë**, the one who sat. The Áelendan say it to children by the hearth and at his cairn.

## What It Left

- **The Wardens' wider charge.** The order that had begun with a fog-hollow keeps the passes as well as the groves from this day ([[affiliation-alndnwrdns|The Áelendan Wardens]]).
- **Ethalossë as the door.** The Sinalë deal with the world there and nowhere else, and only through Áelendan who carry what is said without adding to it ([[place-ethalosse|Ethalossë]]).
- **Dûlthân's name,** said to quiet a child, and behind it a grudge east of the mountains that the Áelendan expect to see paid.
- **Fire-scarred rock on the pass,** where the raiders camped, and a raider's iron that still turns up in the scree ([[place-nelgan|Nélgan]]).
- **A gift that is not earned.** The Áelendan hold the wood as given and not won, and say so first when an outsider asks by what right.

No blood-field marks the pass. A war-band's dead are not a host's.
