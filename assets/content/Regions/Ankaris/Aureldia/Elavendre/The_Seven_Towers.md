**The Seven Towers** is a [[lore-flksinale|Sinalean]] song held in [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]. The
[[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] count it among the songs first heard at [[place-aelirosse|Aelirosse]] and
carried out of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]] by people to whom it was given freely, and their
Tradition Keepers hold it with the tribal songs. [[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]] sing it to
the harp as a Sinalean gift, at three and four removes from any Sinale who ever sang it. Some Áelendan songs
set the enclave at the edge of what the Sinale call the deeper places, and the Tradition Keepers sing what
comes out of it as it was received, adding nothing.

The song gives itself no name and never says what city it mourns. The bards call it after the seven towers
of its first turning, and the only city it fits is [[lore-khazarturn|Khazartúrn]]. It calls the city's
people only "the makers." It names no people for the renegade: it breaks off at the word, and when it
begins again it is evening in the valley as the valley stands now. College bards count seven turnings before
the break and hear the seven towers in them; the Sinale have neither confirmed the reading nor corrected it.
The bards mark the break as a silence, and keep the last line where it stops.

::: {.verse form=elegy lang=en work=song}
The evening comes early to that valley, for its walls are high./
.The last light climbs the eastern face, and stands there, and is gone./
We knew it when it was only stone and water and the swifts,/
.when the world was otherwise, and the only voices under the stars/
were ours, and the voices of the ones your people learned to call the fae./
.What comes down to you as a story came to us as an evening./
We were there. We are here still. We remember it as it was./
Then the makers came, and set their hands against the cliff,/
.and we watched the first face dressed, and the face above it,/
and when the last was dressed we could not tell the work from the wall./
.Fault and weathering and stain, the strata running true:/
seven stood out from the rock, and they were the rock./
.We walked beneath them many seasons before we understood/
that they were towers, and that the mountain was a door.

Come in, they said, and we came in out of the starlight,/
.and found that the day had gone in before us, and was waiting./
They had cut down through the mountain's crown to let it fall,/
.and it fell in shafts as broad as rivers to the floors far under,/
and crossed them slowly from the morning to the evening,/
.as light crosses a clearing in the wood./
We have lived by the light of clearings longer than we say./
.We had not thought to find one under a mountain.

They had worked the stone until it forgot that it was stone./
.It ran on the pillars like spun glass, like water standing still./
The colonnades went on along the great ways past the reach of a voice,/
.and over the galleries were galleries, and over those the light./
We do not build. We have never wished to; the forest builds itself,/
.and what we make we sing, and what we sing does not fall down./
But we stood a long while in those ways and said nothing,/
.and among us there is no higher praise than that.

There was water in the heart of the mountain, running,/
.for no reason but that they could make it run./
Fountains stood in the thoroughfares, and the sound of them/
.went up the stairs ahead of us and followed us down again,/
so that in the deepest street there was a rain that was not falling,/
.and the children of the makers put their hands in it, as children do.

Over the market circles they had painted the open sky:/
.not the sky of any one day, but weather, cloud in motion,/
.the blue going pale toward the edges, as it goes./
The makers did their business under a heaven of their own/
.and glanced up at it now and then, as all folk do, to see that it would hold./
We had known the true sky longer than the makers had been a people./
.We looked up too. We did not find it wanting.

And there were birds./
.They came in at the shafts of light, and some of them stayed./
They nested in the high galleries, on the ledges of the vaults,/
.and sang there in the mornings, and nobody drove them out;/
.the makers stepped around their feathers on the stairs./
A hall that a bird will live in is not a room./
.We said so to the makers once. They said they knew.

Then a horde came up the valley in the dark,/
.[[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]], more than that valley had ever held of anything,/
and they came as one, which Grukar cannot do,/
.and they did not break, which Grukar always do,/
and all that was known of them was true, and it was nothing./
.By the morning there was no one in the mountain to be glad of the light./
They did not choose it. Nothing in them chose it. They were compelled./
.A renegade compelled them.

The evening comes early to that valley, for its walls are high./
.Seven stand out from the cliff, and they are the cliff again./
The mountain is mountain to its crown. No light goes down in shafts./
.If water runs in there, it does not run for us./
The birds are on the outer face now, as birds are everywhere,/
.and the birds that nest there now have never known a hall.

Ask us for the halls, and we will sing the halls till morning./
.Ask us for the light. Ask us for the water and the birds./
It was the finest thing that anyone has made,/
.and we were its guests, and we—
