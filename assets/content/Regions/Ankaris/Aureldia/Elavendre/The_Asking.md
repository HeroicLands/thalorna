**The Asking** is a [[lore-flksenali|Senalian]] song held in [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]. The
[[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan Tradition Keepers]] hold it with the tribal songs and count it, with
[[lore-seventowers|The Seven Towers]], among the songs first heard at [[place-aelirosse|Aelirosse]]
and carried out of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]] by people to whom it was given freely;
[[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Bardic Colleges]] sing it to the harp in nine turnings, each named for what
stands out in it. The song is the [[lore-flksenali|Senali Folk]]'s own account of the [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar band]] that came
over [[place-nelgan|the eastern pass]] into the interior ([[lore-nelgancross|The Crossing of Nelgan]]), of the herdsman [Dhargan]{.name data-type="being/npc"} who sat four days at the wood's edge and
asked, and of the generations after him: [[lore-lowestpoolpct|the Kindred Pact]] sung at the lowest pool of [[place-longstair|the Long Stair]],
the watch set at [[place-talve|the fog-hollow]], the chief made Kinless at the fire of [[lore-tenaspr|Tena, the Hearth of Ésalosa]] in the
[[lore-blacksnow|Black Snow]] year, [[lore-firesnelgan|the fires on the pass]], and the giving-over at the stone court of
[[place-ethalosse|Ethalosse]].

The [Dhárgandi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} hold that the Dhargan of the song is the founder of their line, and the [Kháldendi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} are the
line the song calls cold-handed. The Senali have confirmed neither claim and corrected neither, and the
Tradition Keepers sing the song as it was received, adding nothing. The Senali names in it, for the wood,
the pool, the clearing, the fog-hollow and the epithet the song gives Dhargan at its close, are sung as
the Senali say them, and are the only Senali words most Áelendan ever speak.

The Senali original uses first-syllable stresses, alliteration and answering lines. The translation beside it is in iambic tetrameter, rhymed ABBA.

: I. The Last Trees {#asking-1}

::: {.verse form=lay lang=en work=asking-1}
A child comes up the hill at night/
.and leaves a loaf beneath the birch./
.She does not wait. She does not search/
the dark for us. We stand in sight.

We watched her mother do the same,/
.and hers, and hers, back to a man/
.who sat here once. His face began/
again in hers. You keep his name.

We do not change. They do. This girl/
.will be a grandmother, then dead,/
.before the birch that takes her bread/
is old. That is, for them, the world.

Your kind were in the world when we/
.first came to it. You hunted. You/
.had fire, and words that changed, and few/
of you. We watched. You did not see.

The birch you hunted under then/
.has seeded, fallen, and come back/
.more times than you have kept the track/
of grandsons. We were here since then.

And we were fewer than before./
.We had a grief we do not sing./
.We had grown careful. Anything/
that came, we watched. We shut the door.

You ask us how it all began./
.With fire—not ours—a long way east,/
.a people driven west. The least/
of them, a herdsman, led the clan.

He kept the sheep, and nothing more,/
.and never led. He led you here./
.We watched him come, that first dark year,/
and did not open any door.

: II. The Road {#asking-2}

::: {.verse form=lay lang=en work=asking-2}
For lives on end your fathers' line/
.was pushed west, camp by camp, by men/
.who came out of the east. And then/
his turn. The last of all that line.

Far east, past every hill you know,/
.the grass ran on and met the sky./
.Your flocks were on it. Then the cry/
went up one night: the fires. The foe.

They came out of the east at night,/
.for grass, for sheep, and then for men./
.Your chiefs were dead by morning. Then/
a herdsman stood up in the light.

He swore no oath. He said, "Go west./
.Take children, and the seed. No more."/
.That was Dhargan. The night before/
he'd been a herdsman like the rest.

The river first. It was in spate./
.No boats. You roped the sheep. You roped/
.the children to the men. You hoped./
Then in. Some came out. Some came late.

Then Dhargan went back in for one/
.more child, and got her out. The brown/
.wild water took his wife. Gone down./
He stood. And all the rest went on.

Of her, that is the whole that's told./
.We keep the faces we have seen,/
.and hers has never come. That's been/
a hole, since then, in what we hold.

The plain was flat and long. The dead/
.ewes fed you for a while. And then/
.the men looked at the seed. The men/
were hungry. "Not that," Dhargan said.

He sat down on the sacks. One drew/
.a knife. And Dhargan did not rise./
.He said, "Eat this, and no one dies/
tonight. We walk, then, till we do.

There is no field without the seed./
.Eat sheep. Eat dogs. Eat me. Not that."/
.The knife went in its sheath. He sat/
down with the rest. And you agreed.

You ate the dogs. The mountains came/
.up out of what had passed for cloud/
.all summer—stone, and snow—and loud/
with wind. You went up all the same.

The pass was high. The snow came cold./
.Some sat to rest. They sit there yet./
.[Nela]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, his daughter, would not let/
the lamb go from her coat's warm fold.

And every ewe you have, you say,/
.comes down from that one lamb. At dawn/
.you stood up on the pass, and on/
the far side saw a wood, all grey.

And in the wood there moved a light/
.that was not fire, and was not sun./
.You all went down to it, each one,/
for there was nowhere else that night.

Below the pass, where trees grow thin,/
.a court of worked stone stood alone,/
.grass in its cracks, older than stone/
should be. You looked. And none went in.

That much is yours. But we were near./
.We stood among the last trees, still,/
.and watched you come down off the hill/
half-dead with cold. We stayed in here.

We were not friends. Not then. That took/
.a lost boy, and a winter's sleet,/
.and one man who would sit, not eat,/
four days outside, till we would look.

: III. The Silver Wood {#asking-3}

::: {.verse form=lay lang=en work=asking-3}
That night you stopped outside, and lit/
.a fire. Beyond the fire, the bark/
.took starlight, up into the dark,/
and all the hillside shone with it.

Your shaman, [Silor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, sang. The wood/
.did not reply. His songs were made/
.for wolf and rain and river, laid/
on grass-land. Here they did no good.

What lived in here was not the kind/
.of thing his craft could name or feed./
.The forms he had were made for need/
on open ground. He sang them blind.

The lights came down to you. A boy/
.got up and followed them, the way/
.you follow things at ten: to play./
The wood closed up behind the boy.

His mother ran in after. Round/
.and round she went in it all night./
.It put her out at the first light,/
outside the trees, on the cold ground.

He came out at the next full moon,/
.no thinner, and the same. He thought/
.he had been gone a breath. He sought/
his fire, and said, "It's cold. So soon?"

He said the lights had led him in/
.to where one tree stood in a clear/
.round place. It spoke. He did not hear/
a question in it. Just: "Begin."

That is the wood. It meant no ill./
.It meant no good. It is not kind,/
.nor cruel. It has, in parts, a mind./
It moves, and it is standing still.

One man cut birch to feed the fire./
.His hands went numb, and stayed that way./
.He lived. He had sons. To this day/
that line has cold hands, son and sire.

Then Dhargan went and knelt in snow/
.before the first tree. Silor said,/
."We do not kneel." He turned his head./
"Then what? You do not know this." "No."

We heard him. These were all his words:/
."You in the wood, whatever's there,/
.the road has used us up. We bear/
nothing but children, seed. No herds.

Take me, and leave them, and we'll serve/
.you, every year we live. Say how,/
.and we will do it, here, and now./
Believe it, and we will not swerve."

The wood said nothing. Nor did we./
.The prayer was wrong. Your [[affiliation-theoldway|Way]] does not/
.beg favors, and he knew it. What/
he did, he meant. And we could see.

He meant it more than most of you/
.have meant it since. And then you made/
.a ring of stones outside, and laid/
bread, milk, and blood: the plain's old due.

It had not asked. One night, alone,/
.he came down to the pool. And there/
.was one of us: [Lauva]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, whose care/
is water, standing on a stone.

He took her for the wood. He knelt,/
.and set down bread before her feet./
.She went away. She did not eat./
The wood went still. That's what he felt.

He took the stillness for a yes./
.He told it at the fire that way,/
.and so you sang it. We will say/
She went. That's all. No more, no less.

She ate the bread when he had gone,/
.and said that it was well made. Then/
.she came back in and told us. When/
she'd done, we talked. The talk went on.

: IV. The Watchers {#asking-4}

::: {.verse form=lay lang=en work=asking-4}
You did not ask. We did not say./
.We had seen wonder in your kind/
.before, and watched it fall behind/
the next bright thing, and pass away.

[Hauletta]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, eldest of us, said,/
."I knew halls once." She stopped. "These came/
.here pushed. A pushed thing does the same/
to what is next. And we're ahead.

Turn all the paths. Not round. Away,/
.and to the sea. The land runs out/
.a few days on. They'll farm. No doubt/
they'll fish. They need not come our way.

They'll come to it for what they lack,/
.and kneel, and we'll be what they kneel/
.to, and we'll feed them, and they'll feel/
fed, and kneel more. There's no way back."

Then Lauva spoke, who keeps the pool./
."He came down there, and knelt, and set/
.down bread, and asked for nothing. Yet/
you say they'll beg. He broke your rule.

He had not got much. What he'd got/
.he gave: himself. They're here. They'll stay/
.by us, Hauletta, either way./
The sea, you say? And after, what?"

Hauletta: "Yes." And Lauva: "No."/
.The rest of us said neither. We/
.would not drive you off to the sea,/
nor let you in, nor let you go.

We turned the paths in rings. The deer/
.went in to where you could not go./
.You hunted nothing in the snow./
You starved. You call it [[lore-nelgancross|the Lean Year]].

A girl died in your camp. Her bones/
.went under stone. Her brothers were/
.the first to take up fire for her,/
and go in with it, past the stones.

They came with fire to drive the deer/
.out to the spears. The brands went out./
.The wood turned them. They went about/
in rings till dark. And we were near.

One of them walked into a fog/
.that sat in a low hollow, white,/
.calling the deer. He called all night./
The fog kept him. No man, no dog.

You did not know that hollow yet,/
.nor what it was. You'd learn. The cost/
.was [Dranketh]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Ansel]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s brother. Lost./
Not dead. Not back. No trace. No debt.

One sat down in the snow, not old,/
.and laid his head down. Lauva stood/
.to go to him. Hauletta would/
not have it, and her hand took hold.

"Go out to him, and they will come/
.for what you brought, and come again,/
.and come again. We'll be, by then,/
the hand that feeds them. That's the sum.

They'll kneel to that, and stay the same,/
.and never rise." And Lauva stayed./
.The young one in the snow had laid/
his head down. Then a voice. His name.

A man was calling for his son./
.He came up with no fire, no spear,/
.his two hands open, calling clear,/
"Ansel!" The snow had Dhargan's son.

It was Hauletta's choice. She could/
.have let the paths stay turned. She let/
.them straighten. No one spoke. We set/
no word to it. We understood.

He met no turning. He went in/
.and got the young one up, and out,/
.and all the rest. He did not shout/
or thank. He stopped where trees grow thin.

He turned to the dark trees, and spoke:/
."I will come back. With no cup, no/
.fire, spear, nor bread. Alone." And so/
he went back down into the smoke.

Hauletta stood a long time there./
.Then: "Let him. We will see what's meant."/
.And that was all. And so we went/
back in. We left the paths laid bare.
