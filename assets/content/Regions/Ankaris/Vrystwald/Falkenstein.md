## Overview

Falkenstein holds a hill on the western forest's edge, close enough to the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] frontier that a Nordmal expedition has reached its slopes more than once and a Varokh counter-raid has answered from them just as often. Three hundred people live inside the fort's timber ring, more fortified than most **Vrystwald** villages its size because the ground it sits on is worth fighting over in a way a forest clearing further east is not: the hill commands the easiest crossing for leagues, and whoever holds it decides who moves between the two peoples' country and who does not.

A [[lore-chieftainrnk|Chieftain]] of sufficient war-standing keeps the fort manned year-round rather than seasonally, and **Frithgar Vithrund** serves him as a sworn huscarl, fed at his hall and bound to his person the way a man of proven loyalty is kept close on a contested border. The village's forges and workshops run busier than its farms: **Harmund Garwald** arms the warband, **Thragrim Rikhild** cuts the timber the palisade constantly needs replacing, and even **Skaldar Balthhelm**'s toys and **Armund Hildskorn**'s jests are made for children and men who spend more of the year under arms than most Varokh elsewhere do. **Edrigar Sundryth** works what little ore the hill itself yields, enough to keep Harmund's forge fed without making Falkenstein a mining village in its own right.

Falkenstein's argument is the frontier's oldest one, repeated in both directions: a Nordman who loses cattle to a Varokh raid and a Varokh who loses a cousin to a Nordmal expedition both call the other side the aggressor, and the hill fort has never once settled which account is owed.
