## Overview

Waldburg is a river crossing and a market. Furs, amber and captives taken on three frontiers pass through it on their way elsewhere, and eight hundred people live behind its timber palisade—the largest number gathered in one place in [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]]. It remains a village, but at a size that strains the direct rule of its three elders. The Other Chief must hear more bargains and kin debts than one person can settle promptly, while the War Chief must guard both the palisade and the visiting war-bands. Waldburg keeps the dove as its totem: a bird under whose sign rival traders may set a quarrel aside long enough to deal.

The crossing is also a place elders know how to reach when imminent danger requires several villages to act together. They may meet here, bring fragments of the **Sundered Talisman**, and acclaim a common Hárthúl for that response. Waldburg's own War Chief has no claim to that command by virtue of living here. Once the response ends, the visitors leave and the council is gone. No one can summon them back for ordinary business, and the market gives Waldburg no claim over any other village.

The Frithmund who holds the peace at Waldburg's seasonal market answers to no crown and commands no army. The custom is narrower and stronger: a killing at the market is answered at once by every tribe present, rather than by the dead person's kin alone. That immediate danger is the one matter on which rival war-bands can act together without promising to trust one another tomorrow. **Vithár Hildvith** forges the weapons a Varokh warrior trains his whole working life to use, **Wítharic Druthgar** builds the river-boats that carry furs and captives both down to the Nordmen buyers, and **Véthar Balthhelm**'s hall houses the visitors whom trade or danger brings to the crossing.

Waldburg has no treasury, no standing garrison and no wall around anything but the village itself, and that absence is not a weakness anyone here has had reason to correct: there is nothing at Waldburg worth an army's season to take, which is exactly what has kept it standing while armies that tried to take something else never left the forest again.
