## Overview

Waldburg carries the [[affiliation-vrystwldtrbs|Vrystwald Tribes]]' seat on its own name, and that is nearly the whole of what the word means: it is the village where the common council meets, where a [[lore-frodradrnk|War Chief]] of enough standing calls the seasonal moot, and where the **Sundered Talisman**'s fragments are laid together on the rare occasion every tribe agrees one crisis is worth a single Hárthúl—and nothing more than that, because a village is still what it is once the council disperses. Eight hundred people live behind its timber palisade, the largest number gathered in one place anywhere in **Vrystwald**, drawn here the way every larger Varokh settlement is drawn: a river crossing good enough that furs, amber and captives taken on three frontiers all pass through it on the way to somewhere else. Waldburg keeps the dove as its own totem, an odd claim for the largest gathering in Vrystwald until the reason for the gathering is remembered—every tribe that comes here comes to set its quarrel aside for the season, and a bird that holds a bond without a fight is the one every visiting war-band can agree to stand under, at least for as long as the moot lasts.

Waldburg's own triad answers to the logic its totem names: the Other Chief holds the strongest hand of the three, because a village whose whole purpose is the council needs its presiding seat more than it needs a war-band's captain, and the common council's day-to-day business is his to order even when the great-moot itself is not sitting. The village's own War Chief feels that imbalance sharpest in the one season it should matter least to him: when every tribe lays its fragment of the Sundered Talisman together and agrees one crisis is worth a single Hárthúl, the command that crisis raises up is never his by right however many years he has held Waldburg's own seat, because the confederation's Hárthúl is whoever the moot acclaims—and a Waldburg man can sit in his own hall and watch another man's banner answer for his own village's gate. The Frithmund who holds the peace at Waldburg's seasonal market answers to no crown and commands no army, only the custom that a killing at the market is answered by every tribe present rather than by the dead man's kin alone, which is the one law strong enough to let rival war-bands trade face to face. **Vithár Hildvith** forges the weapons a Varokh warrior trains his whole working life to use, **Wítharic Druthgar** builds the river-boats that carry furs and captives both down to the Nordmen buyers, and **Véthar Balthhelm**'s hall houses whatever War Chief or Athalthúl the season's business has brought to the village.

Waldburg has no treasury, no standing garrison and no wall around anything but the village itself, and that absence is not a weakness anyone here has had reason to correct: there is nothing at Waldburg worth an army's season to take, which is exactly what has kept it standing while armies that tried to take something else never left the forest again.
