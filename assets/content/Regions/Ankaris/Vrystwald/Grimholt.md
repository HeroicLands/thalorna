## Overview

Grimholt sits at a river landing good enough to have grown past what a **Vrystwald** clearing usually holds, and four hundred and fifty people live by exactly that: furs coming down from the northern taiga and grain-belt goods coming up from the [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthian]] frontier both change hands at Grimholt's wharves, and the village has fortified itself the way any landing worth raiding eventually has to. It remains, by the only measure that matters here, a village rather than a town—nothing about how it is governed changes with its size, and the same council of elders that would run a clearing of fifty runs this one of four hundred and fifty. Grimholt keeps the sturgeon as its totem, a fish that outlasts flood and drought by moving slowly and committing to nothing it cannot sustain, which describes the village's council about as well as it describes the river.

The women who work the landing set the prices and keep the reckonings, as they do at every Varokh trade site, and a War Chief is not the one a foreign merchant bargains with. That is the Other Chief's seat speaking as much as custom is: at Grimholt it is the strongest of the three, because a landing lives or dies by its reckonings before it lives or dies by its wall. The War Chief's seat is not far behind it—the wall still has to hold—but he answers for the palisade and the patrols rather than for what crosses the wharves, and the two seats have divided the village between them without much needing the third. The Shaman's seat is the quiet one: he keeps the sturgeon totem, patient and slow-moving as the fish itself, but a landing this busy has little patience for a banishment pronounced on spiritual grounds alone, and more than one man cast out of his own kindred elsewhere has found Grimholt's wharves willing to forget why, so long as his back holds up under a load—exactly the sentence a Shaman's word is supposed to be able to pass, and exactly the place where it may not stick. **Waldaric Ríkhild** sews the tent-cloth the river-traders carry, and the village's taverns and lodging-houses do the business a landing this size generates: **Theódris Sundrýth** begs at the wharves where a successful season leaves traders generous, and **Rádrún Vithrúnd** works a trade of her own among the crews who winter over waiting for the thaw.

Grimholt's one standing argument is with the tribes further up the same river, who hold that a landing fortified this well has started acting like something more than a village even if its council insists otherwise—a complaint the moot has heard and never once resolved, because resolving it would mean admitting a Varokh settlement can become a town.
