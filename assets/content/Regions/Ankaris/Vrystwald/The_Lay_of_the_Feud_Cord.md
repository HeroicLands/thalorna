At Óthholt, a village of [[place-edrwald|Edrwald]] whose households argue over an inherited grove, a kindred minded to refuse a settlement the Skathár has reckoned hears **Skathdreth drúnga**, the Lay of the Feud-Cord, before the three seats hear the refusal. The Other Chief calls for it. The reciter stands with a staff and strikes the floor on the long lines, and between the runs of stanzas the Skathár of the day reads out, flat and without the staff, the reckoning of **the Óthholt Feud** as her predecessors kept it: what was owed, what was offered, who refused, and how many knots. A Skathár of Óthholt learns that reckoning before she is trusted with a living cord, and it is the frame the stanzas hang in. The lay itself is held word for word, as a _drúnga_ is, and it is a lay of speeches: the killings are in the reckoning, and the verse carries what was said in the hall by the two kindreds, the **Grimskorn** and the **Hárvith**, and by the seats. Dómholt, where the Hárvith women went with their children, keeps a lay of the same feud that gives the killer a speech; Óthholt's gives him none. No stanza says what the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] hold about a price refused; the occasion says it. The lay stops where the reckoning stops, at the cord.

: The Vow at the Grove {#vow}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
Keep the door. Set the cup down. This is the lay of the cord,
the Óthholt lay, of two halls, and the knots tied between them,
as the one before me gave it, and the one before her.
Strike the floor for **Ármund**. Strike it twice over for the ones who came after him.

The seats are sitting. The cord is on its peg, and the Skathár has her hand on the reckoning.
Whoever came in here to refuse, keep your hand off the cord till this is done.
It is not long. It is three winters, and it goes quicker in the telling than it went.
The Skathár says her part between the runs, and the last of it is mine, and then the seats will hear you.

The grove stands between the two halls, where the birches crowd in on the oaks.
A woman brought it, married out of the Grimskorn into the Hárvith
three grandmothers back, and both halls have cut in it since,
and nobody had yet said which stump was whose, and the saying was coming.

Ármund was of the Grimskorn, and **Skathric** was his _frithwin_,
one winter younger, and he went where the other went.
They came to the _frath_ in one spring and took their true names in one spring,
and the band that took them in was nine young men, and the nine are counted here.

One had a split lip that never closed. One was the smith's second son.
One had carried a boar's tusk since he was twelve, and showed it.
Two were cousins out of the Grimskorn's own hall. Two were from the landing.
One was the loudest in any hall he stood in. One stood by doors, and was the ninth.

Ármund's deed for his name was a boar, a big one, in the beech mast, that had opened a dog.
He went in on it with a spear and the spear broke, and he finished it with the half.
Skathric's deed for his name was that he carried Ármund home from the boar,
from the beech mast to the gate, with the thigh opened, and the boar's head on top. Ármund would not leave the head.

So they were named in one spring, and the Shaman said the names, and the band struck for both.
Ármund got a name for the boar. Skathric got a name for the carrying.
That is how it was between them from the first, and the village knew it, and said it,
and nobody said then that a man who carries another from the beech mast to the gate will carry him farther than that.

They sat in the grove at the thaw, with the band around them,
with the drip off the birches, and the ice gone soft in the ruts,
and Ármund stood up first. He was always first to stand.
He spoke the _vóra_, and the nine heard it, and the grove heard it, and it is kept.

_Ármund said:_

If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you.
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground.
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it.
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

_Skathric said:_

If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you.
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground.
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it.
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

That was **the Vow at the Grove**, and it was a fine thing to hear.
The nine struck their spears on the roots and said they had heard it.
There was no Skathár there, and no seat, and nobody old,
and Ármund's _óthwa_ was not yet born, who sits at the end of this.

Twelve winters went over the grove, and both halls cut in it.
Ármund married a woman out of the Hárvith, and the grove came up at the wedding,
and was laughed down at the wedding, and came up again at the naming.
Skathric did not marry. He had said where he would be, and he was there.

There was a birch at the grove's edge, a good one, straight for twenty feet,
and Ármund put his mark on it in the autumn, and **Hildulf** of the Hárvith put his on it under Ármund's,
and in the spring each went up to fell it and found the other's mark, and the axes were already in their hands.
The birch is still standing. Nobody has felled it. The two marks have grown over, and the bark has closed on both.
```

**The reckoning of the Skathár.** Kept by **Edrarún**, Skathár of Óthholt, and by those after her. Ármund of the Grimskorn, a [[lore-druthmundrnk|Druthmund]], killed in the grove on the third day after the thaw by Hildulf of the Hárvith, with an axe, in daylight, the blow in front, over a birch both men claimed. Owed by the Hárvith: the full price of a man. Offered by the Hárvith at the first moon after, before the three seats, in the Grimskorn hall: forty winter pelts, marten and fox; a boat with four oars; an ox; an iron kettle; two [[lore-othmundrnk|Óthmund]], a man and his wife out of the south; and the Hárvith half of the grove, to Ármund's _óthwa_ **Árhilda**, five winters old. Reckoned by Edrarún as full. The _óthris_ of the Grimskorn spoke first, Skathric's mother and Ármund's.

: Before the Three Seats {#seats}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
_The Other Chief said:_

The Skathár has reckoned, and I have heard it, and I say it is full.
The kindred will say whether it takes it, here, before these seats, and nowhere else.

_His mother said:_

I have counted the stores since before you could count.
I count them now, and I will say what is in them, so the seats hear it.
Barley for the winter, if the winter is short. Hay for two cows, and we have three.
And one bench by the fire that no cow and no dead Hárvith is going to fill.

Forty pelts is a boat's load, and the boat is in the offer.
An ox breaks the clearing we have wanted broken for ten years.
The kettle is iron, and ours is cracked and leaks at the seam.
And the girl has a grove, the whole of it, with nobody left to argue a stump.

I bore Ármund, and I washed him, and I will wash him again for the pyre.
Do not tell me what he is worth. I know what he weighed.
Hildulf's mother knows what Hildulf weighs. She is sitting there.
She has offered me her ox, and I have looked at the ox, and the ox is sound.

You say the _vóra_. I heard of it. The whole village heard of it:
two boys in a wet grove, and nine boys banging spears on roots.
A vow is a thing a man gives. A price is a thing a kindred takes.
You gave your word. The kindred's word was never asked, and the kindred is me.

Your _frithwin_ is with the _wesk_, or he is not, and I do not know which,
and the Shaman does not know, and will not go and ask, and I would not let him.
The ancestors judge Ármund. They do not judge an ox.
Fill the byre. If they judge you for it, they will judge you fed.

What does a dead Hárvith give? Say it. I am listening.
No milk. No oars. No clearing broken. No girl with a grove.
He gives one bench emptied across the clearing to match ours,
and a knot in their cord to match the knot in ours, and two more hands to tie the next.

I have seen a cord go to the peg and stay there.
At Hildrýth they knotted over timber until the ones left cut a new clearing,
because the old one had nobody in it to cut. That is what a kept cord comes to.
A clearing with birches through the hearth-stones, and a cord on a peg with no wall.

Take it, Skathric. Take it before the seats, and let me untie it.
I will untie it with these hands, and I will say Ármund's name while I do it,
and I will say it every winter at the _eldskorn_ until I am a name said after his,
and your word stands, because the kindred took the price, and not you.

_Ármund's widow said:_

I am Hárvith-born, and I married across the clearing, and my kindred has killed my husband.
I will say that once, so the seats have heard a Hárvith woman say it in a Grimskorn hall.
My mother is sitting there with her ox. My brother is sitting there with his hands in his lap, and his hands did it.
And I have a girl of five on this bench who is both of you, whatever either hall does.

I take the price. Hear me. The girl's mother takes it, for the girl, before the seats.
If the kindred's word is its mistress's, then I have said mine, and it is on the reckoning.
If the kindred says the kindred's word is Skathric's, then I will hear it from him,
and I will know which hall my girl sleeps in by the end of this, and so will he.

_Hildulf's mother said:_

I have offered what I have offered, and the Skathár says it is full, and I say it is more than full.
The two from the south are my hands. They cut my wood. The boat is my boat, and I have had it nine years.
The ox is the one that broke our clearing, and there is no other.
I give them because Hildulf is mine, and I would sooner feed a Grimskorn girl than burn a Grimskorn son.

I will say one thing to the one who refuses, if he refuses.
I have ten in my hall who can hold a spear, and they are listening to you, not to me.
What you say to the seats you say to them, and they will say it back in their own hall,
and I will have to sit and hear it, as your mother is sitting and hearing you now.

_The War Chief said:_

I have the wall, and the band that holds it, and that is the whole of my charge.
I do not have your cord. I do not have the Hárvith cord. The Skathár has them.
I did not hear your vow. I was on the wall that spring; somebody was.
Nine heard it, and nine are of my band, and that is where it touches me.

A _vóra_ binds the one who says it. That is what a _vóra_ is.
It binds the ones who heard it to say they heard it, and to nothing else.
A witness is a mouth and not a spear. Say that back to me, you nine.
If a witness were a spear, a boy's boast would raise a band, and the wall be bare by midsummer.

I will say what I see. Seeing is my charge.
Nine men gone to the traplines with spears, hunting Hárvith, is nine men off the wall.
The Hárvith have ten who can hold a spear. That is ten more.
Nineteen off a wall of sixty, and the landing open, and the boats pulled up in plain sight of the bay.

I cannot make a kindred take a price. The seats have no such hand.
I cannot make the nine stay on the wall if they will go. I can only count them going.
I am counting. Eight. The ninth is standing by the door, and has not moved.
That one has the wall for his charge, and knows it, and I will remember which one he was.

_Edrarún said:_

I keep the cords. Here is the Grimskorn cord, and I have brought it, and here is the Hárvith.
Each has its knots from before any of you, and each has been untied as often as tied.
A cord is made to be untied. A cord that is only knotted is a rope,
and a rope is for hanging things from, and what hangs from this one will be your own.

I will say what a thing costs at Óthholt. It is in my head and nowhere else, and you should hear it once.
A hand, a boat. An eye, a boat and a winter's pelts. A tooth, a pelt; a front tooth, two.
A dog, a lamb, if it was a good dog. The Hárvith paid a lamb for a dog in my mother's time, and it was a bad dog, and they paid.
A man, what I have said. A Druthmund, what I have said. There is no price above it, and nobody has ever asked me for one.

I have untied this cord nineteen times in forty winters, and the Hárvith cord twenty-two.
A boat burned. A girl got with child and not married. A slave of theirs killed by a slave of ours over a goose.
Every one of them was a knot, and every one came out, and the two halls cut in the grove the next autumn.
And here is the Grimskorn cord with no knot in it this morning, and I am the one who will put one in, and I have the fingers for it.

I have reckoned the price. Hear it again, because a reckoning is said aloud or it is nothing.
Forty winter pelts, marten and fox. A boat with four oars. An ox. An iron kettle.
Two Óthmund, a man and his wife out of the south. The Hárvith half of the grove to the girl.
I have reckoned it full. I have reckoned prices for forty winters, and I have not reckoned a fuller.

You say you take no cow for him. The cow is not for him.
Nothing is for him. He is past taking. The cow is for the bench he left,
for the girl at it, for the _óthris_ who has to fill a bowl at it every night,
and for the winter after this one, which does not care what you swore in a wet grove.

I will hold this price if you refuse it. That is the custom at Óthholt, and I keep it.
The ox will eat my hay. The pelts will lie in my loft, and I will turn them against the moth.
The two from the south will work my clearing, and not know whose they are.
And it will be here, all of it, the day a Grimskorn hand reaches for it, if there is a Grimskorn hand.

_The Shaman said:_

I will say one thing, and it is the thing you have not asked, because you know better.
You want to know whether he holds you to it. Whether Ármund, where he is, holds you to it.
I will not go and ask him. That is the one question nobody carries to the _wesk_.
Nobody will ever tell you whether he holds you to it, and the keeping or the breaking is yours, without knowing.

The dead are not asked about blood. Not by me, not by anyone, not for you.
If you keep the vow, you keep it to a man who cannot release you.
If you take the price, you take it from a man who cannot forgive you.
Either way you go on without his word.

_Skathric said:_

I heard my mother. I heard the War Chief. I heard the Skathár and the Shaman.
I will answer each, and then I will say what I said in the grove, and then I am done talking.
Mother, the byre is yours, and the stores, and the girl, and I take none of them from you.
I take one thing from you, and it is the one thing you have not got, which is my word.

War Chief, the wall is yours. I was on it, and I will not be on it. Count me off it.
I did not swear to the wall. I swore before the band, and the band was nine, and they are here.
Skathár, your reckoning is full. I say it is full. Hear me say it: it is full.
A full price is still a price, and I said no price, and the saying was heard.

Shaman, I did not ask you. I will not ask you. I know what is not asked.
He cannot release me, you say. Then I am not released.
If he could speak and said take the ox, I would take the ox and be glad of it.
He cannot speak. So I have only what he heard me say, and he heard me say this.

You who were his wife: the girl sleeps in this hall. She is Grimskorn here, and she is fed here.
You may sleep where you like, and nobody will bar a door to you on either side of the clearing.
You who were Hildulf's mother: keep your ox. Keep your boat. I have no use for either.
Tell your ten what you like. They heard me, and I would say it again to them in their own hall, and I expect I will.

He was first to stand. He was first to stand in the grove, and first at the boar, and first at the birch,
and I was second, every time, and the second is where I stood, and I stood there because he was in front of it.
He is not in front of it now. There is nothing in front of me. I can see the whole clearing.
I would give the ox and the boat and the girl's grove to have something in front of me again, and that is not in the price.

If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you.
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground.
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it.
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

What is a _vóra_ worth in this hall after today? That is what I am asking the seats.
If I take forty pelts for it, then it is worth forty pelts, and every vow in Óthholt has a price.
Every man who swears before the band will be asked afterward what he will take instead,
and the number will go down the river with the pelts, and come back up with the grain.

I know what it costs. My mother counted it, and the War Chief counted it, and the Skathár counts for her living.
I am not a fool, and I am not a boy in a wet grove. I was a boy then. I am what I said then.
Knot it, Edrarún. Knot the Grimskorn cord, one, for Ármund.
And when you have knotted it, put it back on the peg where the girl can see it, so she knows what her father was worth.

_The nine said:_

Eight said: We heard it. We struck our spears on the roots. We hold him to it, and we go where he goes.
The ninth said: I heard it. I struck my spear on the roots. I hold him to it,
and I am holding the wall, because that is what I swore to afterward, before the same band,
and a man can keep two words if they do not meet, and I will keep this one until they meet.

_The Other Chief said:_

It is said, and it is heard. Knot it, Skathár.
The seats have no hand to lay on a kindred, and I lay none on him.
```

Taken by the _óthris_ of the Grimskorn and by Ármund's widow, for the girl. Refused by Skathric and by eight of the nine. The kindred's word divided. Held by the Skathár until it was one: the ox to Edrarún's byre, the pelts to her loft, the boat under her eaves, the kettle on her hearth, the two Óthmund to her clearing. The Grimskorn cord: knotted, one.

: The First Winter {#first}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
_Grema_ came, and the river was a road, and the landing was quiet.
Hildulf came down to the boats with two of his hall, to see the ice.
The two Óthmund out of the south were there, stacking the Skathár's oars,
and Skathric came down the bank alone, and the two of his hall were slow.

He did not call out. He did not run. He walked across the ice to him.
Hildulf had an axe at his belt and got it half out.
The blow was in front, and it was one blow, and it was enough.
The two from the south stood with the oars in their arms and watched, and nobody had told them whose they were.

Skathric stood over him and said the last line of it,
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it,
and there was nobody to hear it but the two from the south and the two who came late.
He went back up the bank. He did not look at the boats, which were in the price, and were not his.

The Hárvith went to the traplines in the next moon, two of them, the way they had gone every winter,
and four of the nine were at the trapline before them, at the third snare.
One of the two had a marten in his hand, and kept it in his hand.
The four left the snares set, and came home by the river, and it was the loudest one who told it.

Skathric's sister had married a man out of the river villages, who had come in to the Grimskorn hall,
a quiet man, good with a net, who had heard of the vow and had not been at it, and had nothing to do with it.
He went down to the ford to see to the weir, as he did, and the Hárvith were at the ford, as they had not been before.
His wife took his body back to his own people by the river road, and did not come back, and that was one fewer in the hall.
```

Hildulf of the Hárvith, killed at the landing in _grema_ by Skathric, in the open, the blow in front. Owed by the Grimskorn: the full price of a man. Offered by the _óthris_ of the Grimskorn: three cows. Refused by the Hárvith men, in Skathric's words. The Hárvith cord: knotted, one. Two of the Hárvith, at the trapline, by four of the nine. The Hárvith cord: three. Skathric's sister's husband, at the ford, by the Hárvith. The Grimskorn cord: two. At the thaw, offered by the _óthris_ of the Hárvith, the second time: the first price, still held, and nothing asked for the three on her own cord. Refused by Skathric. Refused by the Hárvith men, who had not been asked.

: The Hárvith Hall {#harvith}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
_The mistress of the Hárvith said to her sons:_

I have counted the stores, as the woman across the clearing counted hers, and I will say them.
Barley for the winter. Hay for the ox that is not in my byre. Pelts that are not in my loft.
A boat that is under another woman's eaves, and two pairs of hands that are cutting her wood.
And Hildulf's bench, which I have not moved, and which I am going to have to move, because I need the bench.

I have offered the price again, the same price, and asked nothing for Hildulf and the two from the trapline.
Nothing for three men, and the cord open, both cords, and there is a wall to stand on.
I am not asking you. I am telling you what I have offered, because the _óthris_ offers.
Say to me that you refuse it, so I know it was said in this hall and not only in theirs.

_The Hárvith men said:_

We refuse it. Hildulf lay on the ice with his axe half out of his belt.
They said their vow in a grove. We say ours in a hall, and the hall is enough.
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till we have it.
We did not swear it when we were boys. We swear it now, with Hildulf's mother listening, and she will have to hear it kept.

_The mistress of the Hárvith said:_

Then it is said in both halls, and the Skathár has it, and I have it.
I will keep the stores. I will keep the children in. I will keep the fire.
When there is nobody to keep them for, I will go to my sister at Dómholt,
and I will take the cord with me, and hang it in a hall that never heard of you.
```

The second winter. Three of the nine, at the Hárvith woodpile; two of the Hárvith with them. The Grimskorn cord: five. The Hárvith cord: five. Two of the Hárvith, at the landing, by Skathric and two of the nine. The Hárvith cord: seven. Two of the nine, on the river road. The Grimskorn cord: seven. A man killed going for a kindred is knotted on that kindred's cord: that is the rule, and it was kept. Ármund's widow took Árhilda across the clearing to the Hárvith hall at the fifth knot. Offered by the _óthris_ of the Hárvith, the third time: the first price, still held. Refused by Skathric.

: The Wall {#wall}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
The ninth stood on the wall, which was his charge, and watched the eight go out.
Eight on the river road at dusk, with spears, and no torch, going east.
He counted them going, as the War Chief had counted them, and the War Chief stood by him.
He counted them coming back at dawn, and it was easier, because it was five.

At Weskskald in the second winter both halls said their dead, as every hall does, at the same hour.
The Grimskorn _óthris_ said Ármund, and her husband, and her husband's mother, and back, as it is said,
and across the clearing the Hárvith said Hildulf, and the two from the trapline, and the two from the woodpile, and back,
and the girl at the Hárvith fire heard her uncle named as the hand, and her father named across the snow, and said nothing.

The landing again, in that _grema_, and the two from the south at the oars again,
and two of the Hárvith came down to see the ice, as Hildulf had,
and Skathric and two of the nine came down the bank, and it went as it had gone, and the two from the south watched it again.
The man from the south said something to his wife in their own tongue, and she did not answer him, and they went on stacking.

The five told it on the wall, the ninth asking, and he had the right.
The Hárvith woodpile stood outside their palisade, and the eight went to it in the dark to be there when the wood was fetched,
and the Hárvith knew it, and had seven at it, and no wood was fetched.
The split lip was one of the three. The smith's second son was one. The one with the tusk was the third, and the tusk went home with the five.

_The War Chief said to the ninth:_

You have the wall. I have the wall. Between us there is a wall and no band to speak of.
Count me the band that is left, and leave out the ones who are counted on a cord.

_The ninth said:_

Forty-two, and three of those are boys who took their names this spring, and one is you, and one is me.
Enough for the gate and the landing. Not enough for the river road. Nothing for the grove.
If anything comes up the bay this winter, it comes up the bay. I will be on the wall when it does.
And I will not go to the grove, because I said I would not, and five men have heard me say it, which is more than heard the other thing.

He stood on the wall through the second _grema_ and the wall was quiet.
Nothing came up the bay. Nothing came down the river. The wall was not tried.

Edrarún kept what she held. The ox ate her hay, and she turned the pelts in the loft against the moth,
and the boat lay under her eaves with the snow on it, and she had the two from the south tar it in the spring.
They asked her once, the man asking, whose they were, since they cut her wood and were not hers.
She said, The girl's, if there is a girl, and they went back to the wood, and did not ask again.

The girl crossed the clearing in the second winter, with her mother, to the Hárvith hall.
She was Hárvith on that side, and the Hárvith took her in and fed her at their fire,
and the cord that hung by that door had knots in it, and the first was for a man her uncle killed.
She was seven winters old, and she counted the knots.

Her mother took the cough in the third winter and did not get up from it.
The Hárvith women washed her and the Shaman came, and the Grimskorn _óthris_ stood at the edge of the clearing,
and the girl walked back across the snow by herself, because she was Grimskorn on that side,
and the Grimskorn hall had one woman in it and one fire, and nobody else was coming home to it.
```

The third winter. Skathric and the last three of the nine, and the last three Hárvith who could hold a spear, in the grove. Six dead there. Skathric found by the ninth at dusk, and dead before morning. The Grimskorn cord: eleven. The Hárvith cord: ten. Of the Hárvith at Óthholt: none. The women went to Dómholt and to Eichwald with the children, and the hall was unroofed for its timber. The Hárvith cord went to Dómholt with the _óthris_, as she had said.

: The Grove Again {#grove}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
In the third _grema_ the river was a road again, and there was nobody left to walk it but seven men.
Three Hárvith who could still hold a spear, and Skathric, and the three of the nine who were left,
and each side knew where the other would be. There was one place left, and it was the grove.
They went to it in daylight, from the two sides, and the birch with the two marks on it stood between them.

It is not told how long it took. Nobody was there who could tell it.
The crows came down at midday, and the ninth saw them from the wall, and knew what crows over the grove meant,
and did not go, because it was midday and he was on the wall, and the wall is left at dusk.
He left it at dusk. The War Chief took the gate from him without a word, and he went up through the snow.

Six lay in the grove, among the birch stumps that were the whole of the quarrel,
two of them under the marked birch, one Hárvith and one of the nine, with each other's spears in them,
and Skathric lay with his back against the oak Ármund had stood under, and was not dead.
The ninth knelt. He had kept the wall three winters, and the wall had been quiet, and this was what he had kept it for.

_Skathric said:_

Count them for me. I cannot see the far ones. Count them, and I will tell you if it is right.

_The ninth said:_

Hildulf, on the ice at the landing, with his axe half out. Theirs, and the first.
Two at the trapline, theirs. Your sister's man at the ford, ours.
Three at the woodpile, ours, and two there, theirs.
Two at the landing, theirs. Two on the river road, ours. Six here, three and three, and you, and I make that twenty.

_Skathric said:_

Twenty-one with Ármund. Yes. That is the number.
Say it to Edrarún. She will want the number before she wants anything.
Is there a Hárvith man left?

_The ninth said:_

None who can stand. The women go to Dómholt at the thaw, with the children.
There is a girl in your mother's hall, Ármund's, and she is theirs on the one side.

_Skathric said:_

Then she is the last of theirs that stays, and the last of ours, and she has both halls,
and I am not sorry, and you can say that in the hall, because it is the truth and I said it.
It was a fine thing to hear in the grove. It is kept. Tell them it is kept.
Carry me down. Not past their hall. Round it, and not past it.

He said nothing after that, and the ninth carried him down the long way,
round the grove, wide of the Hárvith hall, where a woman stood in the door and did not call out,
and across the clearing to the Grimskorn door, where there was a light.
The _óthris_ opened it, and looked at what was carried, and said, I have washed him once.
```

Skathric burned at the landing with his spear and his axe, the Shaman guiding it. The _óthris_ of the Grimskorn sat down by the fire after the pyre and did not eat, and died in that _grema_, and was laid in the mound with her kindred. Of the Grimskorn at Óthholt: Árhilda, eight winters old, in the hall, keeping the fire.

: The Knot {#knot}

```poetry {form=drúnga lang=en}
They built the pyre at the landing, where the ice had been that first winter, where the Grimskorn burn.
The women of the village raised the lament over him, the ones who were left, and that is theirs and is not told here.
The _óthris_ washed him, as she had said, and put the spear by him and the axe, and not the tusk,
which the five had brought to the hall, another man's, and it went to his mother, who had one son left, nine winters old.

She sat by the fire after the pyre and the girl brought her the bowl,
and she looked at the bowl, and put it on the bench, and said, Give it to the child.
She said it the next day and the day after, and the girl ate, and she did not.
She had said she would untie the cord with her hands, and the cord hung by the door, and she did not look at it.

They laid her in the mound at the edge of the clearing, under the snow, with her kindred.
The Shaman said over her what is said, and no more, because nobody knows more.
The girl stood at the mound in a coat that had been her grandmother's, and went back in,
and banked the fire, as she had been shown, and there was nobody else to do it.

Edrarún came across the clearing in the deep of that _grema_, and she was old,
older than when she tied the first knot, and she had tied all eleven, and that is the Skathár's hand.
Behind her came the ox, and the pelts on the ox in two bundles, and the kettle,
and the boat on the snow, dragged by the two from the south, who had worked three winters not knowing whose they were.

It was slow across the clearing. The ox went in to the knee and the keel of the boat cut a furrow,
and the breath of the ox and the breath of the two from the south stood in the air behind them,
and the girl saw it from the door, the whole of it coming, and did not know what it was,
and stood in the door with the cold coming past her. Nobody had told her to shut it, and nobody was coming to.

The hall had two benches and a fire and a girl. It had a cord on a peg by the door.
Edrarún stood inside the door and said the price, aloud, because it is said aloud or it is nothing.
Forty winter pelts, marten and fox. A boat with four oars. An ox. An iron kettle.
Two Óthmund, a man and his wife out of the south. The Hárvith half of the grove, to Ármund's _óthwa_.

_Edrarún said:_

Ten knots against ten, set off, as custom sets them. One knot left, the first.
The price for the first is here, and I have held it, and it is full, and I say it is full.
Who takes it?

_Árhilda said:_

There is nobody but me.

_Edrarún said:_

Then you. Hold the end.

The two from the south stood in the door, where they had been told to stand, with the snow on their shoulders.
The man looked at the girl on the bench, and at the old woman, and at the cord, and said nothing in any tongue.
The girl looked at them. She had been told they were in the price, and they were the only part of it that looked at her.
Nobody told them to come in to the fire. There was nobody there whose place it was to say it, and so it was not said.

Edrarún took the cord down off the peg and sat on the bench with it across her knees,
and the girl held the end, as she had been told, and the two from the south stood in the door.
The ten came out one by one, and some had been tied in a hurry, and came easily,
and some had been tied in a cold hall by a hand that shook, and had to be worked with a thumbnail.

The last was the first, the one for Ármund, tied at the first moon after the thaw
in a hall with three seats in it and a kindred standing and nine young men behind him,
and it had been pulled tight by every knot after it, three winters' worth of pulling,
and it did not want to come, and the old fingers worked it, and the girl held the end, and it came.
```
