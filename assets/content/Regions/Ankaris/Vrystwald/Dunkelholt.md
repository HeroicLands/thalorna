## Overview

North of the Vylarian passes [[lore-legionsroad|The Legions' Road]] drops off the [[place-vandstein|Vandstein]] highlands into a gorge, crosses its river on four stone piers the legions set, and climbs the far side into a stand of black pines. In the pines stands one of the forward posts the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] gave up: a square of mortared stone around a yard, a gatehouse, a granary on vaulted cellars, and a squat tower whose roof the Varokh burned. The ground is **Dunkelholt**, the dark copse, _dunkel-_ with the ground-closing _-holt_, and the name is older than anything that happens there now.

## The Guesthouse

For some winters the post has been a guesthouse. The gatehouse and the old barracks range are roofed again in bark and turf, a plank-and-rope span crosses the four piers, and a lamp burns in the window facing the road from dusk. A traveler coming down off the passes in bad weather finds smoke, a fire, a stable and dry beds a day short of anywhere else. The keeper is a man of [[place-vandgrund|Vandgrund]], and he charges in aurions or in furs and does not haggle.

The guest rooms are the barracks loft, eight beds up a stair from the dining room. The gatehouse bars are on the outside of the guest doors, and new iron hasps hold the shutters fastened from outside; the house says this is against the wind off the highland.

:::secret {title="The bars and the hasps"}
The bars and hasps keep guests in. The wind off the highland is real, and it is not what they are for.
:::

A stream runs through the yard in a stone channel the legions cut, past the stable and under the woodshed, where an iron grate in the floor drains the yard and water runs loud beneath it. An icehouse is dug into the bank behind the stable. The woodshed holds firewood, and a stack of planks and coils of rope enough to lay the span again.

:::secret {title="The woodshed grate"}
The grate is the head of the legions' sally passage, the one way into the cellars that the stair does not guard. The planks and rope are kept because the span is cut behind parties the house expects; it is laid again once they are inside.

> [!TIP]
> A party that lifts the grate finds a passage too low for anything large, with water running down it. Running water is what keeps the post's largest servant out of it.

:::

## What Folk Say

- **The villages of Vandstein** say a [[lore-varokhiclt|hrag]] hunts the old road north of the passes, one of the hungry spirits that take the weak beyond the totem's land. The tellings of the hrag hold that it will not cross running water. The Shaman of [[place-thrastein|Thrastein]] has told her people not to cross the gorge after dark.
- **[[affiliation-blckpnwlvs|The Blackpine Wolves]]** hunt travelers on the road farther south, and they turn back at the gorge. They say two of their own went across after a fur party and did not come back, and they blame the guesthouse.
- **Vandgrund's pass guides** send their parties to the guesthouse because it is the only roof on the road. Some of them say among themselves that few of those parties are heard of again at [[place-waldburg|Waldburg]].
- **Travelers who have stayed** speak well of the fire and the venison, of a keeper who opens the door before anyone knocks, and of a thin young woman of [[place-erthhaven|Erthhaven]] who carries the bowls and lights guests in from the yard with the lamp from the window. The keeper says she came up with a merchant party two summers ago and stayed when it left.

## Reaching It

By the Legions' Road from the passes, two days north of the last farms of Vandgrund, down into the gorge and across the span on the four piers. The span is the only crossing within a day's walk in either direction. Below it the river runs fast and deep between rock walls.

# The Truth of Dunkelholt {#truth .secret}

**The cellars.** The granary cellars run deeper than any granary needs, because the legions cut them as a strongroom and a last refuge. Long vaulted chambers lie under the granary. The innermost is an operating chamber, warded by a sign cut into its lintel that burns the hand that opens the door. It holds two tables with straps, racks of instruments, jars of preparations and a brazier. Off it, behind an iron door the legions hung, a strongroom holds the goods of every party that went down the stair, sorted by the house they came from, and a cedar chest of the mage's manuscripts. The sally passage drops from the woodshed grate, opens into the operating chamber through a second grate beneath the second table, and comes out at the river through the rock, carrying the post's runoff the whole way. A boat is kept at its foot.

**The mage.** The cellars are the workshop of **[Siremos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, a Vylarian mage of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] whose house struck his name from its rolls and whose work the [Sekran]{.name data-type="affiliation"} lists among the categorically forbidden arts. He came north because the forest has no law that can reach him, and he stays because the road brings him what he needs: travelers, taken apart for his workings, and their goods, sold south through Vandgrund to pay for materials. For some winters he mended his failing body with the bodies of travelers—a hand, an eye, a run of skin—and two summers ago that body failed past mending.

**[Sundrún yra Eichrúnd wur Erthhaven]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.** A mage of the forbidden arts can move out of his own living body into another living body, putting out the soul that was in it, and bind his former body as a construct. Two summers ago a merchant party came up the road with a young Varokh woman of Erthhaven in it, **Sundrún**, about twenty, sound and steady-handed, and Siremos moved into her. Her soul went out into the spirit realm with no rite said over it and no Shaman to call it home. The merchant party went down the stair, and its goods stand in a row of their own in the strongroom. Siremos lives above stairs as the thin young woman who carries the bowls and lights the guests in with the lamp from the window. At every working she stands at the head of the table and holds that lamp, and she cuts. His manuscripts run for years in an old man's crabbed hand and change two summers ago to her young, practiced one; the last working in the old hand is the taking itself.

**The old man in the cellar.** The body Siremos left died when he left it, and he worked it into a [[lore-golemcrtr|construct]] like the rest of his servants. A narrow old man sits by the brazier day and night, wearing a young man's hands, one eye blue and the other brown, his throat stitched where a fresh run of skin covers his ring of signs. He says four courteous sentences in order, the same four whatever is said to him, and begins again at the first when he reaches the last. He carries no key and no purse, and nothing in the cellar obeys him. He is a decoy. The Sekran's file describes an old Vylarian mage with a failing body, and a hunter who comes for Siremos finds exactly that, kills it, and carries the story south with the file closed.

**What she wants of a party.** Siremos wants one of two things from every party the road brings, and either serves her. A party strong enough to kill the old man and carry the tale south closes the Sekran's file, so she wants it armed, angry at the keeper and pointed at the cellar. A party too weak for that goes on the table: the [Choir]{.name data-type="being/creature"} needs mending, [Athalwen]{.name data-type="being/npc"} needs keeping, and the goods pay for materials. She tells such a party the truth—that the keeper cuts the bridge, that the Master buys travelers with the keeper's wife's life, that the legions' passage comes up behind the guard on the stair—because a true warning herds better than a lie, and she says "the Master" and "below" and lets the party place the old man there. She writes the drain route into the last pages of dead guests' day-books, in her own hand, and leaves them where guests will find them. The route ends under the second table, laid out with one set of instruments for each guest.

**The keeper and his wife.** The keeper is **[Othric yra Vandwald wur Vandgrund]{.name data-type="being/npc"}**, of a Vandgrund kindred that has guided parties over the passes for generations. His wife **Athalwen** was mauled by a bear on the terrace above Erthhaven, and Siremos keeps her alive in the cellar, her own body from the waist up and the rest of her somebody else's, which she cannot yet make walk. That is Othric's bargain: he keeps the guesthouse, takes word from his kin at Vandgrund of every party they guide north, cuts the span behind each one, lays it again afterward, and sends his guests down the stair, and in return his wife lives. He knows what Sundrún is and is afraid of her. He will not stand with his back to her, he sends her to the kitchen when she lingers among the guests, and he carries the cellar key on his belt; the strongroom key is on hers. Athalwen knows too, and she wants to die.

**The Choir.** The largest construct is the Choir, joined from several travelers, a head taller than a tall man, with mouths along its shoulders and throat, each saying again the last words it said when it was its own. It hunts the near bank of the river at night, keeps the house by day from the outer vault, and stops at running water: the yard stream, the runoff channel and the sally passage. It obeys the Master's voice, which is Sundrún's. When she walks out among the pines at night it stands still until she has passed, and when the old man orders it, it does not move. A construct does what it was last told for as long as its signs hold, so the Choir keeps the house on its last order after its maker is dead or gone. A ring of signs is cut around its middle; break the ring and every mouth closes at once, and it falls. Two smaller servants, joined from dogs and the parts of men, wait under the tables for her word.

**The tellings, weighed.**

- **The hrag.** What hunts the road is the Choir, a made thing and no spirit, and it leaves the tracks of a heavy body and none a spirit would leave. The tellings are right that it will not cross running water.
- **The Wolves' two men** went across and are in the Choir.
- **The guides' unease** is well founded. The parties that do not reach Waldburg went down the cellar stair, and the guides who send them are Othric's kin.
- **The old mage.** Travelers' tales, the Sekran's file and the house itself all put an old Vylarian mage with a failing body in the cellar, and he is there, empty. The mage himself serves supper.

> [!WARNING]
> The old man at the brazier is the most dangerous thing in the cellar to describe carelessly. He never improvises, and nothing in the cellar obeys him. A GM who lets him answer a question spends the clue that he is empty.

> [!TIP]
> Play Sundrún as the one person in the house on the guests' side. Every word she says is true, and her fear is real enough to pass: what she fears is a party that fails to go down the stair.
