## Overview {#overview}

The Dorumbe are the western tribe of the [[place-sthmrchsrgn|South Marches]], the marcher clans whose country runs along the foot of the highland spurs from the frontier of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]] eastward through the first of the forage belts. They keep sheep, goats and small sturdy horses, and cattle only along the streambeds that carry grass enough. Their seat is [[place-dorumbecamp|Dorumbe Camp]], a well-camp at the foot of the spurs.

They are of the same stock as the frontier clans of [[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] and speak a Marcher form of [[skill-okharclng|Okharik Language]]. A Dorumbe says his clan first and his well second.

## How It Is Led {#how-it-is-led}

The headman is [[being-mbareloddrmb|Mbarelo of the Dorumbe]], who holds the tribe by descent and consent together. The elders of the clans sit with him, and the Elder of the Wells holds the claims to water that the whole tribe's order rests on. Stock, wells and grazing pass from father to son, and a clan's head is a man. Women keep the household and the milk and do not lead; the [[lore-bethuanclt|Bethuan]] ordering of the sexes is to the Dorumbe a standing offense.

## Range, Wells and Grounds {#range}

The tribe waters at the wells of the western Marches, a scatter of rock-cisterns and springs where every one is named and claimed by a clan ([[place-longwells|the Long Wells]]). The rest of the tribe camps in clan groups at many such wells, among them [[place-thumbelcamp|Thumbel Camp]], Ngarali Well and Kwendo Camp. In the dry months the herds are brought up to the edge of the irrigated strips of Bethua, and in the wet weeks they spread south over the chaparral. [[place-marchesroad|The Marches Road]] runs through the tribe's grazing, and the western stages of [[place-khuqetzalu|Khuqet-Zalu]] stand on its wells.

## Relations {#relations}

The Dorumbe pay Bethua tribute in stock for the right to graze at the edge of its country, and the tribute is carried by the Tribute-Bearer to the frontier posts. It is tribute and not submission. They tolerate and hire [[affiliation-jcklsthmrchs|the Jackals of the South Marches]], who winter in the stages nearest Bethua, because the Jackals are a hard blade against the north and too dangerous to move against lightly. Their kin of the [[affiliation-hasembetribe|Hasembe Tribe]] hold the eastern country, and the two tribes meet at the long wells in the dry months.

## History {#history}

The Dorumbe water at wells that the sun-house of Au'Khelathu dug and walled for its tribute road, and each clan credits its wells to its own forefathers ([[lore-templetrib|the Tribute of the Temple-Cities]]). The Khelathi left, the clans stayed, and the wells became theirs.

## The Present Pressure {#present}

The tribute is late. The winter rains came short and the herds went into the dry months thin, so Mbarelo has sent word to the Hégissa of the southern cantons that the stock will come after the autumn drive and not before. Bethua wants it sooner. The Dorumbe riders have been called home for the autumn drive, and the elders of the wells are weighing whether to pay in stock, in grazing or in nothing until the grass returns.

## The Gathering Place {#gathering-place}

[[place-dorumbecamp|Dorumbe Camp]] is where the elders sit and where a stranger asks leave to water.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The late tribute.** The Tribute-Bearer needs an escort for stock he cannot afford to lose to the Jackals or to a Bethuan patrol.
- **A dispute at the wells.** Two clans claim the same cistern, and the Elder of the Wells wants a witness from outside the tribe.
- **Riders for the drive.** The Master of Riders is gathering the tribe's horsemen for the autumn drive and will rent the ones he can spare to a caravan that pays before it leaves the last well.
