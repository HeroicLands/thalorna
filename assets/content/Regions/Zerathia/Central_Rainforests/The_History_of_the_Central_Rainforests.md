The Central Rainforests have no king-list, no calendar and no founding. No outside power has ever ruled them, and no internal power has ever unified them. What the forest has instead is a set of places that remember and a set of stories that warn, and an outsider's history of it is the history of the outsiders who came to the margin.

## What the Forest Keeps

The forest peoples keep their past in their spirit-places, their manners and their feuds, and the feuds' origins are often mythological rather than historical. Four traditions hold the forest between them: the [[lore-luwandoclt|Luwando]] along the great rivers, the [[lore-kubindoclt|Kubindo]] in tribes each bound to one spirit-place, the [[lore-yesomaclt|Yesoma]], mobile foragers of the deep interior, and the [[lore-pewayeclt|Pewaye]] on the forest margins. None of them is a polity, and nothing stands above the tribe ([[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]]).

## The Court on the Black River

One story is known across the interior. There was a court on a black river, the spirit-place of a Kubindo tribe, bound to a great river-serpent. Its keeper made himself the tribe's leader, took helpers to keep the court's manners for him, and built a house of stone for the serpent. The serpent died of it, and the dead of that court still turn its wheels. The court is [[place-drwndzgrt|the Drowned Ziggurat]], and every Kubindo child knows the story before it knows its own court's manners. It is the reason the Kubindo hold that a keeper who becomes a leader has begun to ruin the place ([[lore-kubindoclt|the Court-keepers]]).

## The Silent Clearing

The forest also knows a place that belongs to no spirit. [[place-slntclrng|The Silent Clearing]] is a perfect circle where nothing grows and no bird calls, and generations of watchers have kept it: bell-posts of dark wood with the clappers cut away, a ring of earth burned every season, and paths renewed in ash.

## The Outsiders at the Margin

The powers of the north came to the rivers. The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] at its height projected commercial influence along the major rivers and abandoned any claim of sovereignty within a generation. The Empire of Au'Khelathu keeps a network of trading posts and occasional tributary relationships with the northernmost river-tribes, and has never tried to administer the interior. Khelathi merchants have kept river-post relationships with the northern forest peoples for centuries, and Okharik herders meet forest-edge peoples on their southern pastures in season ([[place-nrthrnfrtlrgn|Northern Fertile Region]]).

South of the forest the savannah peoples and the forest peoples trade through intermediaries and intermarry. The Sengala of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]] keep marriage exchanges with several frontier tribes ([[lore-nyalubahist|the History of the Nyáluba]]). Outside merchants who try to cut out the middleman end in disease, disappearance or the quiet insistence that the trade will stop.

## The Leopard King

In a valley that appears on no map a sorcerer who is said to take the leopard's shape leads a tribe that its neighbors feed with gifts out of fear. He is recognized for as long as the gifts keep coming, and the Kubindo expect for him the end they tell of the black river ([[place-lpdkngcrt|the Leopard King's Court]]).

## What It Left {#what-it-left}

- **No state.** No power has ever ruled the forest, and the forest peoples have no leader above the tribe.
- **A caution.** The Kubindo teach the story of the black river to every child.
- **Intermediaries.** The inherited prerogative of mediating trade stays with the tribes, clans and ritual specialists that hold it.
