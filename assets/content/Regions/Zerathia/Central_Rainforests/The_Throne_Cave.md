## Overview {#overview}

"Gifts to the Lower Terrace, the pelts to the Hall, and nothing past the stream," a gift-bearer says. "Nobody has been to the Cave who came back to say what the King's sleep looks like." **The Throne Cave** lies at the head of the valley of [[place-lpdkngcrt|the Leopard King's Court]] in the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], at the top of the three terraces the seat is built on. It is where the King sleeps by day, and no guest has been admitted.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The Leopard King holds it. The Court has no wall, and its hunters in leopard-skin cloaks and its ring of watch-posts in the trees stand between the cave and the neighboring tribes. The [Spotted Guard]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, the Court's own fighters, visit a tribe that stops sending its gift. The valley is the Court's own, and the neighboring tribes keep out of it unless they come with a gift.

## Layers {#history}

The Court says its bargains are struck under the notice of the leopard-guide, which the Sengala of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]] hold to be solitary and unseen, and the cave is where the claim lives: the King is said to walk the forest by night as a great spotted cat and return at dawn as a man. The neighboring tribes say that no one has seen the King and the leopard in the same place. The Sengala do not confirm the claim and do not deny it.

## What Is There Now {#present}

The cave is a closed door. The neighboring tribes send their gifts on their own schedules, and a tribe that stops sends no one to the cave and is visited by the Spotted Guard. A party that comes with a caravan sees the Lower Terrace and the Hall of Pelts and is told that the King is asleep. Whether a person sits at the head of the valley is the thing the tribes pay not to find out.

:::secret {title="The King's rite"}
The Leopard King once was a chieftain. He took the leopard's shape by a rite of his own making, which costs him a name for every change, and he has taken the shape so often that he forgets his own children. The Cord-Keeper, an old woman, ties each forgotten name into a cord so that the Court remembers what he cannot, and the cords hang in the high chamber of the cave. The cave opens as a crack in the rock behind a waterfall. The rite is sorcery, costly, rare and corrupting, and it is not the Shadow's work, though it opens the one who works it to the Shadow's kind.
:::
