Okharis keeps no founding date, and its account of itself begins with a flame. A kingdom of herds and rain became one realm under three temple-cities, and its history touches the empire and Bethua at the frontier.

## Rain and Herds

The country west of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethua]], beyond the South Marches from the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], needed no engineers. In the west the rains keep the grass green and the rivers running year-round, and the land grows barley, sorghum, flax, dates and figs without a canal. In the east the country dries to savanna, and the herds do the work of the water. The Okharik therefore never built the aqueducts, qanats and irrigation works that made the Khelathi and the Bethuans, and their civilization grew around cattle, sheep, goats and small sturdy horses, and around seasonal grazing cycles old enough to carry religious obligation ([[place-okharisrgn|Okharis Region]]).

The ground under the Flame is older still. The shamanic tradition that deals with the [[lore-zohira|zohira]], the demi-gods of rivers, storms, fertility, death and the hunt, is not a branch of the faith of [[affiliation-nkaruthar|Nkaru'thar]] but the older ground it stands on ([[affiliation-yenorzhrng|the Yenor Zohiring]]).

## The Three Flames

The faith of the Eternal Flame gave the kingdom its three fixed points. [[place-zarhanis|Zarhanis]], the Spirit Flame, is believed to be the birthplace of the religion and holds the largest temple complex and the flame in its deepest chamber ([[miscgear-emberfirst|The First Ember]]). [[place-kaljekor|Kaljekor]] is the flame of abundance and trade, and [[place-vuthraka|Vuthraka]] the flame of war and protection on the southern frontier. The cities are permanent centers of pilgrimage, council and craft, and the nomadic cycles turn around them.

The compact between the cities and the clans is the [Three-Flame Settlement]{.name data-type="lore/law"}: the cities provide religious legitimacy, metal-craft and entrepôt trade, and the clans provide the herds, the wool and leather, and the warriors. The kingship is hereditary over the three cities and the village tribes that owe them tribute ([[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]], [[lore-okharinclt|the Okharik]]).

## The Count and the Coin

Okharis counts its years from the Qet Telgu as a matter of course, as the empire does ([[lore-qettelgu|the Qet Telgu]]). It strikes no coin of its own. The temple-attested weight-pieces of [[lore-aukhlthcrncy|Au'Khelathu Currency]] pass in every purse, and credit runs through [[affiliation-crwntrdskhrs|the Crown Trades]], whose moneylenders keep correspondent accounts with the Gar-Hálzi. The count and the coin are the empire's, and the government is Okharis's own.

## The Tribute That Failed

The one recorded attempt to bring Okharis under the empire's hand came in the years of the sun-house of Magu-Thelqen, in the Restored Crown. The court sent an army and an embassy across the [[place-sthmrchsrgn|South Marches]] to make the eastern temple-cities pay tribute to the Gar-Au, dug wells and built a chain of forts for the road, and abandoned the whole when the house closed ([[lore-templetrib|the Tribute of the Temple-Cities]]). The kingdom never became a tributary. The temple-cities have treated a Khelathi embassy as a thing that wants something since, and the forts stand across the Marches as [[place-khuqetzalu|Khuqet-Zalu]].

## The Eastern Clans and Bethua

The frontier clans of the east pay tribute to the authorities of Bethua in exchange for grazing rights. Bethua won its independence from Pelagora about 615 AF with Khelathi gold and engineers ([[lore-bethuasep|the Separation of Bethua]]), and the patronage that war created is Bethua's own; Okharis keeps no patron.

## What It Left {#what-it-left}

- **An unaligned kingdom.** Okharis owes the empire nothing, trades with it freely and answers to no patron.
- **Two loyalties.** An Okharik belongs to a clan, which moves, and to a city, which does not.
- **A wariness.** The temple-cities receive each Khelathi embassy as a request.
