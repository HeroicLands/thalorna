## Overview {#overview}

"Without the sisters of the qanat the hinterland would be empty steppe," a Bethuan child is taught. The **Order of the Waters** (the _Qanatissai_, "sisters of the qanat") is the corps of engineer-priestesses of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]]: sworn women, half clergy and half engineers, who build and maintain the aqueducts, tunnel qanats, cisterns and canals that carry water to the fields and to the fountains of [[place-bethura|Bethura]]. The order sits within the sacred pillar of the realm, and its upkeep of the works employs tens of thousands of people across the interior year-round.

## Membership {#membership}

The sisters are drawn traditionally from the priestly caste. The order's expertise and its vocabulary are the most visibly Khelathi thing in Bethua, learned through the alliance that won the realm its freedom: an engineer-priestess of the order and a hymn-singing priestess of the same temple speak about water in two almost different languages, one in Khelathi jargon and the other in Helonik poetry. The tunnel-wells are dug by men, the diggers of the _katelun_, who work for the order for most of their lives. The order reads its line from Pédhoria, the sister passed over in front of the shore, who took the keeping of the vein under the fig and was the first of the sisters of the water ([[lore-headofroll|the Head of the Roll]]).

## Leaders {#leaders}

The chief engineer of the realm is the Izet-Mu, "Overseer of the Waters," a Khelathi loan-title feminized to Bethuan use that echoes the Gar-Hálzi Overseer of the Account. She answers to the Metrissa directly and sits in the [[affiliation-sacredcollege|Sacred College]] by right of office.

## What It Wants Now {#present}

The failing Khelathi flood makes the grain of Bethua's aqueduct-fed strips the shore's one reliable surplus, and the Council of Houses presses the Metrissa to sell it to the Khelathi patron while the Sacred College wants it kept in the cisterns; the Metrissa has not chosen. The order built the works that make the surplus and keeps the cisterns that would hold it, and the Izet-Mu sits in the College by right of office. The sisters want the strips watered whatever is decided, and they fear a sale that empties the cisterns before a dry year, which leaves the qanat-fed farms nothing to fall back on. They also fear that a patron who buys the grain will ask for the works behind it, and the order's Khelathi trade-vocabulary makes it the Bethuan body that patron already understands.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

A party meets the order at a qanat head or a cistern in the interior, where a sister of the order and her gang of diggers keep the works, and in Bethura, where the aqueducts feed the capital's own fountains.
