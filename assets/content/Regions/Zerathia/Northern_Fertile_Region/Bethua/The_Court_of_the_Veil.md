## Overview {#overview}

"To take the Veil is to be raised to the throne," a Hiora of the court says to a child who has asked about the purple. "To set it down is to abdicate or die." The **Court of the Veil** is the court of the Metrissa, whom common speech calls the Veiled Mother, the sovereign and High Priestess of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]]. It is the household and the instrument of the sovereign at [[place-bethura|Bethura]], where the two pillars of the state meet in her person, and the Purple Veil, a mantle and face-veil dyed in murex purple, is its regalia.

## Who Sits {#who-sits}

The court is the sovereign's household and officers. The reigning Metrissa is [[being-amithenakrvn|Amithena of House Kervanion]]. The scribal administration serves it, and it prizes discretion and merit above all other virtues: its clerks include the Grammatissai who keep the realm's most sensitive correspondence, and the Archivist, who keeps the genealogies on which every House's standing rests. Priestesses of the court who favor reform argue with those who do not.

## How It Decides {#how-it-decides}

The Metrissa's word is the law of the realm, and she rules through the Two Pillars, never above them. The court acts where neither the Sacred College nor the Council of Houses can: it watches the quays of the bay and the coast roads to the other orchard-ports most closely, and it commissions the fleet's privateers under the crown's Purple Warrant.

## The Present {#present}

The court marks the South Marches as its own, collects what tribute it can there, patrols the line it can hold and would rather not look past it. The exiles who ride south, the bound-men and the Houseless women, have produced the one thing Bethua fears above all others: a company of armed exiles who know the matriarchy's own ways of war and owe it nothing, the Jackals of the South Marches. The court would rather see them destroyed than admit how they came to be, and the Spadai the realm unmade are loose in the south with a grievance the court would rather not admit it made.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

A party meets the court through its discreet intermediaries, who engage a hunter or a company for the household's business, and through the Matriarchy's quays, where the court's agents watch whoever lands.
