## Overview {#overview}

"Do not make the Spear-Sisters decide whether the law is written for you," a factor says to a foreign man who arrives wearing a sword. The **Spear-Sisterhood** (the _Lorkhai_) is the professional warrior class of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]] and the backbone of its army, trained from youth in arms and tactics and in the [[skill-charissa|Kharessa]], the Grace, an unarmed open-hand and kicking style that turns the agility of Bethuan women into a weapon.

## Membership {#membership}

The Lorkhai are overwhelmingly women. The Kharessa is taught to every woman of the Lorkhai and widely among free Bethuan women besides, and its masters, the _Kharessai_, are figures of real prestige. The sworn eunuch-warriors, the [[affiliation-spadai|Spadai]], fight beside them. Companies of the Lorkhai also serve as marines on the fleet's war-galleys ([[affiliation-bethuanflt|the Bethuan Fleet]]).

## Leaders {#leaders}

A Lorkhissa commands a company of the Lorkhai. Above them the Stratégissa, the supreme war-leader, is appointed by the Metrissa and answers to her; she commands the army, the Lorkhai and the Spadai alike, and through the Naukrátissa the fleet besides. She attends the Sacred College and the Council of Houses when war is in question and votes in neither.

## What It Wants Now {#present}

The Sisterhood keeps the realm's order against its own men. No man may bear a weapon or armor on pain of death unless specifically authorized, martial training for men is forbidden and watched for, and the Lorkhai, with the Houses, watch the wards, the docks and the fields. It also keeps the line in the south: companies of the Lorkhai patrol from posts on the edge of the South Marches, where the Houseless have raised the armed bands the matriarchy fears. North of the line the canals run and a Dómissa's writ holds, and south of it nothing does.

## How a Party Meets It {#how-a-party-meets-it}

A party meets the Sisterhood at the head of a quay, where two women of the Lorkhai stand watch over the landing, and in the streets, where a foreign man with a weapon is asked to account for it. A woman who has been trained in the Kharessa is never to be mistaken for a defenseless one.
