## Overview {#overview}

Caravan guides name the ring of dry stone north of the road and say that it holds no water. They go round it and tell a new driver to do the same. **The Burned Wells** lie beside the first of the forts on the eastern road, in [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]]. They are a ring of wells filled with carcasses in the wake of a steppe host and never cleared.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

Nobody holds it. The garrison of the march, the Gar-Meglai under the Overseer of the March, keeps the forts, and the clans of the desert water where the wells still give water. Each names the other as the one who filled the Burned Wells.

## Layers {#history}

The host that struck the eastern road in 315 AF took the northern wells for water, and the ring beside the first forts was filled with carcasses in its wake ([[lore-eastroad|the Storm on the Eastern Road]]). The forts were walled in the decades after. Nobody has settled who did it, the host's riders or Dunhari raiders, and the question has a sting: the Laws of the Well forbid a Dunhari to foul water, so the garrison's charge against the clans is a charge that a clan broke the Laws.

## What Is There Now {#present}

A ring of dry wells and stone. The road bends round it, and a caravan driver who goes through is told by the guides to go round. The garrison and the clans each blame the other, and a party hired to clear the wells reopens the argument: whichever side the party serves, the other holds it spoiled the evidence. The water would be welcome. The wells of Khuqet-Miglet are the empire's road to the desert, and caravans want every well the march can give them.

## Dangerous Neighbours {#dangerous-neighbours}

The march is a garrisoned desert land-bridge held for the road that crosses it. The forts are close, and so are the Dunhari families who water beside them.
