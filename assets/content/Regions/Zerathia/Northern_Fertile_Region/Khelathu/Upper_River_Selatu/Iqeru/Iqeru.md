## Overview {#overview}

"We do not ask whether the sun will rise," a priest at the temple of [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]] says to a pilgrim in Iqeru. "Every night he goes down into the underworld and fights [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'athis]], the Devouring Shadow, and every morning he has won. We ask what we owe him for it."

Iqeru is the capital of [[affiliation-selatiqeru|the Selat of Iqeru]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 6,900 among irrigated wheatland. It holds the selat's chief temple of the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Its character is the selat's own: sun-temples and wide irrigated wheatland.

Uqa'a's demand on a life is a hard one, clarity, purpose and righteous intensity, and his cult holds the throne itself, because the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]] is understood as his earthly manifestation. The [[place-greattempluqaa|Great Temple of Uqa'a]] stands on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] at the capital; Iqeru keeps the selat's own chief temple.

The wheat outside the town is the [[place-fieldiqeru|Field at Iqeru]], where the war between [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]] and [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] ended in 1531 ST (580 BF) with the death of the Gar-Au [Zab'el'Psaqa III]{.name data-type="being/npc"} ([[lore-twothrones|the War of the Two Thrones]]). It is an ordinary battlefield: the dead were carried home to their tombs and rites, and the field is farmed like any other.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Iqeru is wheat-country with a temple at its center. The irrigated fields round the seat grow the grain, and the chief temple of Uqa'a holds more of the land than the villages do, so its granaries are the selat's reserve. About 6,900 people live in the seat on temple stipends and the tithe. A market town and a canal-head town serve the canals: [[place-zeliqeru|Zel-Iqeru]], about 8,300 people a day off, which sells the wheat, and [[place-zuiqeru|Zu-Iqeru]], about 6,800, a canal-head town where the main irrigation canal leaves the river. Iqeru feeds itself from its own wheatland and sells the surplus, and the temple vaults are the largest buyer. [[place-fieldiqeru|The Field at Iqeru]] lies in the wheat outside the walls.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Iqeru collects the render and holds the levy; the high priest of Uqa'a holds the tithe and the land. A farmer pays both and pays the priest first. The canal-heads are the selat's danger: a canal-gang that opens a gate out of turn can drown a neighbor's field or leave another dry, and the Canal-Warden's assize settles the quarrels.

## The Present {#present}

A low flood shortens the reach of the canals, and the farms at the end of the line go dry first. The farmers ask the temple of Uqa'a for seed-grain, and the priests give it against next harvest, which binds the farms to the temple a little harder each year. The temple granaries at Iqeru are lower than they were, and the Halzi'a's render reports a smaller crop than the court expected. The Measure explains most of it. The Halzi'a pays the throne from his own rolls and leaves the temple's stores alone.
