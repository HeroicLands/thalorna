## Overview {#overview}

"Set the first sheaf at the edge and say nothing," a farmer of [[place-iqeru|Iqeru]] tells his daughter at the start of harvest. "The field has had its due." **The Field at Iqeru** is the irrigated wheatland outside the town where the last battle of [[lore-twothrones|the War of the Two Thrones]] was fought, in the season before the harvest, with the canals full and the ground soft. It is an ordinary battlefield and the Khelathi say so: farmed like any other, with nothing left on it unread.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The field belongs to the wheat and to the farmers of the selat, under the Halzi'a of [[affiliation-selatiqeru|the Selat of Iqeru]] and the temple of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]] at the town. The temple keeps the one thing the field gave up: a basket of spearheads.

## Layers {#history}

The house of [Zu'Magetu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} held [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] against the upriver Gar-Au [Zab'el'Psaqa III]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, crowned at Lenen-Nezut, and the two armies went up and down the river for six years taking the granaries. At Iqeru the Gar-Au of Lenen-Nezut stood with his levies and Zu'Magetu with the delta chariots and the temple levies it had bought. By the end of the day the Gar-Au was dead, and with him the line of [[affiliation-gezeelanlaghu|Geze'el'Anlaghu]] as a line of Gar-Auu. The victor was crowned [Kuz'el'Qar I]{.name data-type="being/npc"} at Galezkara in 1531 ST (580 BF).

Iqeru is a temple town, and both armies kept the same rites. When the day ended the priests of Iqeru sent for the embalmers. The Lenen-Nezut dead went home upriver to their tombs, the delta dead went home to Galezkara, and every man who could be named was named and received. The dead who could not be named were read together at the temple of Uqa'a by the priests of Iqeru, and the field was farmed the next flood. Nothing was left on the ground unread, so the field is no blood-field. Its ghosts, if it has any, are the ordinary ones every old battlefield keeps, and the farmers who plow it do not mention them.

## What Is There Now {#present}

Wheat, and the custom of the first sheaf at the field's edge. A party passes the field on the road to Iqeru and meets the war only in the temple, where the basket of spearheads is shown to a visitor who asks. The field is why the upper river remembers that it crowned a Gar-Au once and could do it again, and the priesthood and the court remember it too. Lenen-Nezut's pride has a grievance in it against the capital, and the capital's against the town, and the granaries of the valley are thinning in a year when the selat governors hold back tribute from the capital.
