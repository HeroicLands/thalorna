## Overview {#overview}

Iku is where the eastern caravans arrive. It is the capital of [[affiliation-selatsltplgn|the Selat of Selat-Pelgun]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], and about 7,000 people live here, among the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the chief temple of the [[affiliation-pelgun|Faith of Pelgun]], the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls and the markets where the caravans unload. At harvest the temple fills with the selat's festival crowds for the harvest-festivals and the virility rites of the god.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Iku lives by the caravans. The eastern roads come down to it, and the caravan-yards at [[place-zelpelgun|Zel-Pelgun]], about 6,400 people beside the seat, outfit them, buying grain, water and fodder from the river farms before they set out. [[place-zupelgun|Zu-Pelgun]], about 4,300, a well-and-pasture town on the desert edge, waters the herds, and [[place-yathpelgun|Yath-Pelgun]], about 5,300, a day's ride on the river, brews and bakes for the harvest festivals. About 7,000 people live in Iku itself. The seat buys its bread from the river farms and sells what the caravans carry to the temple, the Halzi'a's court and the markets.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Selat-Pelgun holds court at the seat, beside the chief temple of the Faith of Pelgun. The tally of each caravan's tolls is read at the gate when it departs, so that no master can claim a lighter load later. The caravan-masters are known to the toll-keepers by name, and the Halzi'a's toll is the selat's chief revenue after the render. Desert raiders know the departure dates, and a caravan that does not return leaves a hole in the town's credit. The yards lend against the caravans, and a lender who calls in a debt early in a bad year finds the next caravan will not outfit with him. The temple of Pelgun holds the harvest festivals that fill the river villages, and the caravan-masters pay for a blessing before each departure. The town's order is the toll-keepers' and the temple's, and neither is gentle with a defaulter.

## The Present {#present}

The harvest is thinner this year. The caravans from the eastern roads still come down, carrying goods and no grain, and the villages sell them what they can spare. The festivals are shorter, the feasts smaller, and the people stretch what they have. The seat's markets buy what the caravans carry. The Halzi'a keeps the harvest grain in the temple of Pelgun to feed the festival-goers who still come, and he sends the throne his render in coin from the caravan tolls.
