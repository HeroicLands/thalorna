## Overview {#overview}

A family comes to Anzet with its dead, and the first thing the temple asks is what grade it can afford. "Three is a wash, a wrapping and a prayer," a Wazu of the temple tells them. "Two is a shorter course with cheaper oils. One takes seventy days, with natron, costly resins and an amulet at every joint. Choose what the house can bear, and nobody here will think less of you for it."

Anzet is the capital of [[affiliation-selatanztqlt|the Selat of Anzet-Qelt]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 5,700. It holds the selat's chief temple of the [[affiliation-hezmuiri|Faith of Hezmu'iri]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Its character is the selat's own: jackal-cult and desert-edge tombs, embalmers and tomb-guards.

[[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmu'iri]] is honored more universally than almost any other god for a plain reason, which is that nearly every human being will eventually need his priests. In Anzet the reason has a temple.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Anzet is the temple's house and the selat's ledger. The chief temple of Hezmu'iri takes in the fees of embalming and the offerings of the dead's families, and those fees pay the clerks, porters and lamp-makers of the town and the ration of the tomb-guards. The town grows little of its own. The estate-towns and the farmers of the flood-plain send it grain, and the two towns beside it depend on it in turn: [[place-zelanzet|Zel-Anzet]], an embalmers' town of about 5,700, and [[place-yathqelt|Yath-Qelt]], a tomb-guards' town of about 4,600, buy their bread with fees and stipends that pass through Anzet's granary. Of the selat's 320,000 people, only a few thousand live in the capital, and the town's strength is what the temple holds.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Anzet-Qelt keeps his court here, and the Thaz'Lekhau of Hezmu'iri speaks for the temple. The guards on the cliff are the jackal-cult's, reinforced from the Halzi'a's levy, and the two powers are rarely so close in interest. The scribal bureau holds the tax rolls. The ground near the tombs is the town's danger: thieves tunnel from the desert side, a stranger carrying digging tools is stopped at the edge of the necropolis, and a guard found selling a secret loses his hands.

## The Present {#present}

The flood has come low two years running, and the offerings brought to the temple of Hezmu'iri are fewer. The farmers who work the desert-edge tombs leave their fields for the pay of tomb-work, and the fields go unplanted. The guard posts are doubled because hunger makes thieves, and the temple's dole is smaller than the guards remember. The Halzi'a pays his tribute in the produce of the desert-edge tombs and enters the grain shortfall as the river's, and the Overseer's figures cannot easily dispute it. The jackal-cult's festivals are held with fewer offerings and more dread.
