## Overview {#overview}

"Come hungry, and leave your arguments at the gate," a flax-weaver tells a stranger on the morning of a feast at the temple of [[affiliation-thubai|Thuba'i]] in Gadju-Mulu. "The god takes it as an insult if you refuse his gifts, and the cooks take it worse."

Gadju-Mulu is the capital of [[affiliation-selatgadjuml|the Selat of Gadju-Mulu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 6,100 in the marshy flood-basin the selat is named for. It holds the selat's chief temple of the Faith of Thuba'i, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Thuba'i's temples keep wine vaults and granaries, and the granaries feed the dole, so the god of pleasure keeps grain as well as wine. The character of the town is the selat's: "Green Waters," a wet country of flax and waterfowl.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Gadju-Mulu feeds on the marsh and on its temple. The flax of the flood-basin, retted and spun in towns such as [[place-zmamulu|Zma-Mulu]] (about 6,500), the largest flax town, goes to the linen the valley is known for, and the traders who buy it bring grain back from the dry country. [[place-gargadju|Gar-Gadju]], a market town of about 5,400 on the dry rim, takes the basin's flax, fowl and fish and feeds the traders with the rim farms' grain. The seat itself buys bread from the rim and sells the temple's wine and its tax-rolls in return, and about 6,100 people live on that exchange.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Gadju-Mulu holds court and granaries here beside the temple's high priest, and the temple's feasts are the town's calendar. Order in the marsh is mostly a matter of boats: a boat-steerer who knows the channels is a person the selat's officers know by name. The standing water brings fever, and in a bad season the gates of the rim towns close to the basin. The [[place-drownstones|Drowned Stones]] lie in the southern marsh inside the [[affiliation-zeghetnelgu|Zeghet'Nelgu]]'s charter.

## The Present {#present}

The flood-basin is half dry this year. The villages that cut flax and take waterfowl have lost their wet ground and their reed, and the estate-towns round the seat work to reopen drained channels. The people are fed on fowl and fish from the remaining pools, and the granaries at the seat have fed more of the dole than in other years. The temple keeps its festivals, because its priests teach that gratitude does not wait for plenty, and the festivals have become a way of eating. The Halzi'a sends the throne the linen and what reed there is, and the waterfowl of the remaining pools stay in the selat.
