## Overview {#overview}

Every spring the flood goes down and takes the boundary stones with it, and the scribes of [[place-khelenetzu|Khelenet-Zu]] go out to put them back.

"Hold the cord and keep still while I sight along it," a land-survey scribe tells the boy carrying his rod, on a dyke above a field whose owner and whose neighbor have each walked out to watch. "The water leaves a hand of silt and no opinions about whose barley it was. All we have is last year's entry, so that is what we measure from."

Khelenet-Zu Selat is the land of [[affiliation-selatkhelntz|the Selat of Khelenet-Zu]], one of [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|the Upper River Selatu]] of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], and lies within the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]].

## Character

The land is the river valley at its working best: a green strip of flood-silt fields and villages between canals and dykes, with dry upland on either side where high desert and chaparral begin the moment the water stops. Estates and temple lands share the strip with the villages. What makes it Khelenet-Zu's is the paper that goes with the ground. Field after field here has a boundary of record, an owner of record and often a dispute of record, and the disputes go to the assize-courts at the capital.

## Economy

The selat runs on the flood, the harvest and the render like every [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] selat: grain to the granaries, labor to the canals and the works of the flood season, and a share of everything to the temples and the crown. What it adds for the empire is the work of its lawyer-priests and survey scribes, who settle the lines the flood has wiped out and carry the verdicts into the rolls.

## Notable Features

- Khelenet-Zu—the selat capital and the Halzi'a's seat
- The chief temple of the [[affiliation-qearet|Faith of Qe'aret]] and its estates
- The old assize-courts and the survey scribes' rolls they judge by

## Current Situation {#present}

After each flood the scribes of Khelenet-Zu walk the villages remeasuring the fields, and this year the work is slower and the quarrels longer. A farmer whose field is dry has a claim to a lower render, the neighbor whose field is wet disputes it, and the lawyer-priests of Qe'aret rule on each. The village headmen come to the assize-courts at the seat in numbers, and the courts sit late. The estate-towns pay their render in the sums the courts allow, which are small.
