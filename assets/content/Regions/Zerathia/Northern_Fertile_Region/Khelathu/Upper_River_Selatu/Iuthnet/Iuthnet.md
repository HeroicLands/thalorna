## Overview {#overview}

"Say what you came for, whether it is a child, a cure or only a blessing," a Wazu of the temple of [[affiliation-uznera|Uzn'era]] tells a pilgrim at the door in Iuthnet, "and the first priestess you meet will know where to send you."

Iuthnet is the capital of [[affiliation-selatiuthnet|the Selat of Iuthnet]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 8,000. It holds the selat's chief temple of the Faith of Uzn'era, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Its character is the selat's own: healing-temples and birth-shrines, the great cult of the mother-goddess.

Uzn'era's priestesses practice medicine and hold office up to the highest. Behind them stands the doctrine that creation needs the balanced partnership of a masculine and a feminine divine principle, which is why [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] women own property, initiate divorce and plead in the courts.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Iuthnet lives by births and cures. The chief temple of Uzn'era draws pilgrims from the whole river, and its wards and birth-shrines pay the town's wages. About 8,000 people live in the seat, and the selat's farms feed them. [[place-gariuthnet|Gar-Iuthnet]], about 5,400, a farm-and-market town on the river road, supplies the shrines with flour, oil and honey from its millers and honey-sellers. [[place-zeliuthnet|Zel-Iuthnet]], about 6,600, a healing-temple town upstream whose patients eat the barley of the nearby farms, and [[place-maguiuthnet|Magu-Iuthnet]], about 8,000, a birth-shrine town in a garden ring, are the shrine towns beside it. The seat buys bread from the farms and sells the pilgrims their lodging.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Iuthnet presides over the town's offices, and the estates round the seat are held by their own masters and mistresses. The priestesses of Uzn'era hold the town's moral authority. A death in the birth-shrines brings the temple's inquiry on the midwife, and a mother's death is a public matter. The danger is contagion: an outbreak inside the wards closes the gates, and the town is hungrier for it.

## The Present {#present}

The births continue in a hungry year, and the shrines are full. The villages bring their pregnant women to the temple for the grain the shrines give, and the estate-mistresses distribute it. The fields are poorer, and the people near the temple are fed while those far from it are not. The road to Iuthnet is crowded with families who have walked there. The Zabet'lutu send the estate grain to the birth-shrines first, and the Halzi'a's render is paid from what remains. The temple's stores are the seat's reserve, and they are drawn down.
