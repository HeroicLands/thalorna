## Overview {#overview}

"Come up after the lamps are out," a library-temple astronomer says to a visiting scribe who has asked how the year is kept in Gekhenu. "The records are downstairs, and they are accurate. The sky is up here, and it is the thing the records are accurate about."

Gekhenu is the capital of [[affiliation-selatgekhenu|the Selat of Gekhenu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 6,500 and the calendar-keeper of the middle river. It holds the selat's chief temple of the [[affiliation-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'ar]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Its library-temples and astronomers are the town's character, and they work under [[lore-rethsaardty|Reth'Sa'ar]], the ibis-headed scribe of the [[lore-eternallibrary|Eternal Library]], whose domain includes the reckoning of time.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Gekhenu lives by the calendar. The library-temples of Reth'Sa'ar keep the records and the astronomers' count, and the farmers below plant and render by it. About 6,500 people live in the seat, on the temple's stipends and on the estate-towns' grain. Two towns lie in its reach and sustain its trade: [[place-yathgekhenu|Yath-Gekhenu]], about 7,400 people downstream, a paper-and-ink town whose goods the temples buy, and [[place-lutgekhenu|Lut-Gekhenu]], about 6,100, an estate-town of the library-temples two days off, which grows the barley that the scribes eat. Gekhenu does not feed itself. It sells the astronomers' reckonings and the temples' copies and buys bread from the estate-towns.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Gekhenu sits beside the chief temple of Reth'Sa'ar and defers to the astronomers on when the river should rise. The scribal bureau keeps the tax rolls. A forged tally is the town's gravest crime, and a scribe caught with one is barred from the temple's schools. Fire is its quiet danger: the library-temples are full of reed and ink.

## The Present {#present}

By the temple's reckoning the river did not rise at its appointed time, and the astronomers say so on the roofs. The villages report the water's stage each week, and the estate-towns round the seat plant what the silt allows. The harvest is smaller, and the temple's stipends to the paper-and-ink towns are smaller with it. The astronomers' count is the one thing in the selat that is not short, which is why the Halzi'a's render reports cite it, and why a scribe at the Overseer's bureau at Anlagh-Zetun cannot easily set a figure against them. The render the Halzi'a sends the throne is on time, and the scribes' note beside it says why it is small.
