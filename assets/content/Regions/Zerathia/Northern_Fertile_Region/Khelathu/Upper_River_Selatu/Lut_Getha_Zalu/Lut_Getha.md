## Overview {#overview}

"The god shaped the world," a Lem'Nelgir (priest) of [[affiliation-psaqaru|Saq'aru]] tells an apprentice in the forecourt of a craft-temple, "and a smith does it again, small. Handle the metal accordingly."

Lut-Getha is the capital of [[affiliation-selatltgthzl|the Selat of Lut-Getha-Zalu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], who keeps his court and granaries beside the chief temple of the Faith of Saq'aru, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the smith-towns and villages. About 7,200 people live here. The empire's smiths work steel as a matter of course, so it is bronze and gold that Lut-Getha is known for: the metal of ceremony.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Lut-Getha lives by the forge. The chief temple of Saq'aru keeps the craft-temples' ledgers, and the nobility of the empire buys the ceremonial arms. About 7,200 people live in the seat. [[place-gargetha|Gar-Getha]], about 6,700, is the smithing town beside it; [[place-zelgetha|Zel-Getha]], about 5,500, a gold-and-bronze craft town on the river road; and [[place-yathgetha|Yath-Getha]], about 4,500, a charcoal-and-ore depot where the hill-roads meet the river. The smiths buy charcoal and ore from the hills and grain from the river farms, so the whole group buys its food by trade and its fuel from the hills.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Lut-Getha-Zalu keeps the tribute rolls and the levy, and each craft-temple keeps a ledger of every order. Apprentices are bound to a master for seven years, and every charcoal-yard keeps a night watch against fire. A smith who sells an unmarked blade loses his forge. A fire in the charcoal-yard can burn a street, and a mine collapse in the hills cuts the ore. The scribal bureau holds the tax rolls and the weights, and a standard weight is kept in the temple's forecourt against which every smith's scales are checked each season. The smiths belong to their craft-temples, which hold the forges in trust, and a master who quarrels with his temple loses his place at the furnace and his family's lodging with it.

## The Present {#present}

A short flood has raised the price of fuel, and the smiths work the ceremonial arms of the nobility at a slower rate. The estate-towns send their grain to feed the smiths, and the villages are poorer for it. The smith-towns' trade is the best on the upper river and holds in a hungry year, because the nobility's orders do not stop. The Halzi'a's render is short in grain and paid in bronze and gold.
