## Overview {#overview}

Chegu is the capital of an ancient royal selat. It is the seat of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of [[affiliation-selatzuger|the Selat of Zu-Ger]], "the Great Land," and about 7,600 people live here, among the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the chief temple of the [[affiliation-hezmuiri|Faith of Hezmu'iri]], the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls and the markets that serve the surrounding villages. The old burial-ground of the first dynasties lies beyond the cultivated land, and the temple of Hezmu'iri keeps watch over it ([[place-firsttablets|the Ground of the First Tablets]]).

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Chegu lives by the dead kings. The chief temple of Hezmu'iri keeps [[place-firsttablets|the Ground of the First Tablets]] on the desert edge, and the foundations that endow the tombs pay the town. About 7,600 people live in the seat. [[place-zelger|Zel-Ger]], about 7,400, is a mortuary-service town of priests, tomb-keepers and offering-bearers who buy their bread from the valley farms with the temple's stipend; [[place-lutger|Lut-Ger]], about 6,100, an estate-town with a granary the royal funerary estates fill; and [[place-yathger|Yath-Ger]], about 5,000, a river-landing where barges unload stone and timber for the burial-ground. The seat buys grain from the estates and sells the tombs' keeping.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Zu-Ger holds court beside the temple, and the foundations' stewards keep the accounts of every dead king who still has a claim. A tomb-robbery at the ancient burial-ground shames the whole selat, and the keepers answer for every missing object. The danger is a sunk barge: a sarcophagus lost in the river costs the landing its contract, and the crew its lives. A barge-master who has lost a load is not hired again on the river. The temple of Hezmu'iri keeps the tomb-keepers' rolls, and an offering-bearer who is dismissed has no other trade in the selat. The villages round Chegu are small and old, and they farm round the marker stones of the burial-ground as their grandfathers did.

## The Present {#present}

The throne's offerings to the first kings are paid in grain and oil, and the villages that carry them to the tombs report that the loads are smaller. The temple of Hezmu'iri holds the rest, and the stewards ask which foundation's dead are owed first. A foundation whose king is forgotten loses its grain, and the stewards do not forget him quickly. The estate-towns farm what the flood leaves, and the Halzi'a sends the tribute the selat owes and holds back nothing, with a respectful note about the unfinished rites.
