## Overview {#overview}

The stone leaves [[place-legirusi|Legirusi]] on the flood.

"Mind the lines," a barge pilot tells a passenger at a quarry landing in [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]], the season of inundation, while a gang walks a block down the ramp. "Any other month the river is a mile from this cliff. Now it laps the ramp, and a barge loaded by noon can float right up to the desert edge of the tomb or temple the block is cut for."

Legirusi Selat is the land of [[affiliation-selatlegirus|the Selat of Legirusi]], one of [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|the Upper River Selatu]] of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], and lies within the [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]].

## Character

The land is the river strip with the cliffs at its back. Flood-silt fields and villages lie below, and above them rise the flanking uplands where the empire's fine building stone is cut, with high desert and chaparral beyond the rim. The flood sets the calendar: while the fields are drowned and no farm work is possible, the farmers become quarry gangs and bargemen, and the blocks they cut go down to the temples and tombs that need them.

## Economy

Legirusi grows grain for its granaries and owes labor to the canals and a share of everything to the temples and the crown, like every [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] selat. What it adds for the empire is stone and the hands that shape it: quarrymen on the cliffs, master masons in the villages and the temple-workshops that carve the monuments.

## Notable Features

- Legirusi—the selat capital and the Halzi'a's seat
- The chief temple of the [[affiliation-psaqaru|Faith of Saq'aru]] and its estates
- The quarry cliffs and the temple-workshops that finish what they yield

## Current Situation {#present}

The villages of Legirusi are stone country: the quarries, the master masons and the temple-workshops fill the selat's calendar. The flood-season gangs that cut and haul stone are larger this year, because the villages' own fields are dry and the men have nothing else to do. The estate-towns send their sons to the quarries for the ration, and the stone-barges that go downriver ride lower than usual on the shallow river. The pay is food, and food is what the villages need.
