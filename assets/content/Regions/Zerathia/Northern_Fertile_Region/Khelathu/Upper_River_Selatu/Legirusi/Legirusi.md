## Overview {#overview}

You hear Legirusi before you see it: chisels, hundreds of them, from first light. The town is the capital of [[affiliation-selatlegirus|the Selat of Legirusi]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], who keeps his court and granaries here beside the chief temple of the [[affiliation-psaqaru|Faith of Saq'aru]] and its workshops, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the villages and estate-towns. About 7,400 people live here, and most of them work stone or feed those who do.

"Square it, then square it again," a master mason tells an apprentice in a temple yard. "A fault in a house for the living is mended in a season. A fault in a tomb lasts forever."

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Legirusi lives on stone. The chief temple of Saq'aru and its workshops take the blocks that the quarrymen cut, and the temples and tombs of the empire buy the carving. About 7,400 people live in the seat. [[place-zellegirusi|Zel-Legirusi]], about 7,000 people below the white cliffs, is the quarry town; [[place-yathlegirusi|Yath-Legirusi]], about 5,800, dresses and loads the blocks on the river bank; and [[place-lutlegirusi|Lut-Legirusi]], about 4,800, an estate-town with its own grain-lands and granary, houses the masters' families and the apprentices. The quarries lie in the white cliffs above Zel-Legirusi, and the temple's yards finish the blocks before they go downriver to the temples and tombs that ordered them. A block is marked with its quarry and its yard, so a flaw can be traced to the crew that cut it. The workshops buy their grain from the river farms, some by ferry from the opposite bank.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Legirusi holds the town's courts and the quarry corvée, and the master masons rule their yards. The masters' inspections are exacting, the fines for a spoiled block are heavy, and a mason who passes a flawed lintel is struck from the temple's rolls. A rockfall in the quarries kills a crew in an instant, and an overloaded barge sinks with a god's statue aboard.

## The Present {#present}

The flood-season gangs that cut and haul stone are larger this year, because the villages' own fields are dry and the men have nothing else to do. The estate-towns send their sons to the quarries for the ration, and the stone-barges ride lower than usual on the shallow river. The pay is food, and food is what the villages need. The Halzi'a's render is short in grain and full in stone. The treasury at the capital accepts the stone and does not say what it makes of the grain.
