## Overview {#overview}

Yath-Lemet is the smallest capital on the upper river, with about 6,000 people crowded between the cliff and the water. It is the seat of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of [[affiliation-selatyathlmt|the Selat of Yath-Lemet]], who keeps his court beside the chief temple of the [[affiliation-chelsur|Faith of Chelsur]], the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls and the markets that serve the gorge's few villages. The crocodile is sacred to the god and is also the beast that takes farmers and children along the river, and the temple teaches both facts together. When a beast takes someone, a culler licensed by [[affiliation-garnul|Gar'Nul]] answers, and the temple treats the cull as a religious act before it is a commercial one.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

The narrow valley feeds the town badly. The gorge has few villages, each of a few families on a strip of field, and no market town beside the seat. The town buys most of its bread from the selatu on either side of the gorge, by barge, and pays with the offerings of the chief temple of Chelsur and the tolls of the river traffic that must pass the narrows. It lives by trade and by the temple. The temple licenses the ferrymen who cross at the seat, and the license is the temple's to withdraw.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Yath-Lemet keeps his court beside the temple of Chelsur, and the scribal bureau keeps the tax rolls. The river-beasts are the order the gorge keeps: crocodiles and hippopotami live on the same banks the farmers use, and a ferryman who loses a passenger to them owes the temple a fee. The tolls are what the Halzi'a collects, and a barge-master who dodges them by night is fined the cargo. The narrowest reach of the gorge is a bottleneck, and a boat that grounds there blocks the river for those behind it until the temple's boatmen clear it.

## The Present {#present}

A low river leaves the farmers and the beasts in the same few pools. The farmers fetch water at dawn and keep watch for crocodiles, and the temple of Chelsur has more offerings than usual. The villages are each a few families who cannot leave, and the fields along the gorge are narrow and narrower. The barges that bring bread are fewer, because the neighboring selatu have less to send. The Halzi'a sends the whole of what he owes, a week late, with a clerk's note about the river.
