## Overview {#overview}

Magu-Thelqen was laid out all at once, and you can see it. The avenues run straight from the chief temple of the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]], the squares are evenly spaced, and the whole plan is the work of one zealous dynasty. It is largely empty. About 5,500 people live in a town drawn for more, and they keep the [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]]'s court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls and the markets that serve the surrounding villages in the part of the city that still has roofs. This is the capital of [[affiliation-selatmaguthn|the Selat of Magu-Thelqen]], a temple-city that is still inhabited, and a long walk shows how much of it is not.

The plan is the work of the house of [[affiliation-thelqenrelu|Thelqen'Relu]], the house of the sun, whose two Gar-Auu laid the city out from the chief temple of Uqa'a and ruled from it for a generation. The city began to empty when the great temples of the [[affiliation-qearet|Faith of Qe'aret]] and the [[affiliation-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'ar]] refused the house's doctrine of one sun behind every god and the court went home to [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] ([[lore-sunhouse|the Sun-House at Magu-Thelqen]]).

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Magu-Thelqen is a planned city with too few people for its plan. About 5,500 live in it, on the temple's gardens and the estates' grain. [[place-lutthelqen|Lut-Thelqen]], about 4,300 people at the edge of the old city, grows vegetables and figs on the temple's terraces and sells them to the capital's markets, and [[place-garthelqen|Gar-Thelqen]], about 5,200, a half-empty temple town a short ride away, lives on the open lots its people farm between the empty houses. The estates round the town grow the selat's grain. Magu-Thelqen feeds itself from its gardens and its estates and buys little.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Magu-Thelqen governs through the Legha'lutu of the estates, who hold the land. The empty quarters shelter fugitives, and a squatter in a house that has an owner may find the owner's agent at the door. The danger is the emptiness: a quarter with no watch is a refuge for anything.

## The Present {#present}

The quarters are half empty, and the people who live in them are of two kinds: the estate-dwellers whose families have always lived there, and the families from dry villages who have walked in. The Legha'lutu take the newcomers as bondsmen for their food. The estates' fields are poorer, and the estate-towns' stores are the selat's reserve. The empty temple-city has become a place to shelter. The Halzi'a lets the newcomers stay in the empty quarters and sends the throne a tithe of the estates' grain.
