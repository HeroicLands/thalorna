## Overview {#overview}

"Tell me where it hurts," a healer of [[affiliation-uznera|Uzn'era]] says to a traveler who has come to Gar-Lut with a fever, "and then tell me what you are holding on to. The goddess teaches that nothing is mended without something being let go, and the cure goes better when you have settled which."

Gar-Lut is the capital of [[affiliation-selatgarlut|the Selat of Gar-Lut]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a devout town of about 4,900. It holds the selat's chief temple of the Faith of Uzn'era, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. Its character is the selat's own, a small, devout place of healing-shrines and herb-gardens. Uzn'era's priestesses and priests heal the sick, and her faith holds that the body is sacred and its needs the goddess's gift.

## What the Town Lives By {#lives}

Gar-Lut lives by healing. The chief temple of Uzn'era keeps the shrines and their herb-gardens, and the sick who come to them pay in coin and in kind. The seat's own fields are small, and it eats from the estate-towns and from the nearer herb-towns: [[place-magulut|Magu-Lut]], about 3,900 people a short walk from the shrines, whose gardens grow what the healers use, and [[place-yathlut|Yath-Lut]], about 3,200, on a mound above the mineral spring the healers prize. The two sell herbs to the shrines and trade the surplus for grain. Gar-Lut has about 4,900 people, and its livelihood is healers' fees and the sale of plants. It does not feed itself from its gardens.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]] of Gar-Lut governs a small, devout selat and has little to do. The Canal-Warden rations the water by turns, and the temple takes its turn first. The priestesses of Uzn'era practice medicine and hold office to the highest, and the temple's word on a sickbed outweighs the Halzi'a's on most things. The danger is in the gardens: the herbs that heal also kill, and a priestess found to have swapped a dose is judged by her own order and then by the Halzi'a's court.

## The Present {#present}

The herb-gardens depend on the canals, and two low floods have shortened the water the Canal-Warden has to ration. The temple of Uzn'era is served first, then the village plots, and the garden-towns trade herbs for grain on worse terms every month. The healers are called to more sickbeds than the herb-gardens can supply, and the shrines buy what they lack from other selatu at the price of the day. The town is orderly, since the selat is small and its people are devout. The selat pays what it owes, which is little, and the Halzi'a's render reports no dispute with [[place-themeasure|The Measure]].
