## Overview {#overview}

**Zileti** (about 4,700 people) is the capital of [[affiliation-selatzuqeztu|the Selat of Zu-Qeztu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], the town of the western horse-country.

Despite the selat's name, the chief temple at Zileti belongs to [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]], the sun-god, and not to the war-god. The Halzi'a's court and granaries stand near it, the scribal bureau keeps the tax rolls, and the markets serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns and the pasture-country beyond. Raiders on this edge carry arms from the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]], and the watch has no account of how they come by them. A remount-clerk in the Halzi'a's stables keeps the tally of foals. "I write each one down when it drops and again when it is weaned," he says, "and the court only ever asks after the second."

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Zileti lives by horses and by the watch. [[place-zelzileti|Zel-Zileti]], a half-day west, is the horse-fair town where breeders, grooms and army buyers meet after the spring foaling. [[place-lutzileti|Lut-Zileti]], a short ride off on the canal that waters the pasture, is a stud-estate town breeding mounts for the chariot corps and the riders who are the selat's first line against raiders. Zileti's granaries take the barley the breeders buy from the valley farms and the hay of the estates, and its levies ride the edge of the margin where the irrigated fields give out.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of the selat holds Zileti as a hereditary military governor, from the house of [[affiliation-thaulgau|Tha'Ulgau]], the kin of the hill-nomad conquerors, settled on these pastures in 1671 ST (440 BF) ([[lore-hillhouse|the Closing of the Hill House]]) after [[lore-thaulgau|the Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]. The first of the house's dead lie in the grass west of the town ([[place-horsebarrows|the Horse Barrows]]), and the herders do not let their sheep graze the tops. The selat's levies and the watch answer to him.

## The Present {#present}

Walk out to the edge of the irrigated fields and the margin has moved: the green stops nearer the river than it did, and the herders have drawn their flocks in behind it. Thin stock stands at the fairs, and the watch feeds its troopers from the same stores that feed the stables. The remounts and foals owed to the chariot corps are late, and the Halzi'a does not say whether the herds can spare them.

Okharik traders with better western stock come to the town with offers, and his wells are open to their animals. He takes what is offered, as he takes the court's letters, and answers neither side. One sickness in a stable can cost a breeder a year's profit within the month, and a lost stud herd breaks a house in a season.
