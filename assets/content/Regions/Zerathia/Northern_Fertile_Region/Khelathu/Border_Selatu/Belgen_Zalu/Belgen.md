## Overview {#overview}

**Belgen** is the last city on the road south, and it is built like a fort. About 11,000 people live here, in the capital of [[affiliation-selatbelgnzl|the Selat of Belgen-Zalu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]].

A priest of [[affiliation-qeztu|Qeztu]] in the chief temple gives a newly posted officer the lay of it in a breath. "The god's house for the prayers, the Halzi'a's court for the judgments, the granaries for the winter, the bureau for the tally of who owes what," he says, "and the market, because the villages must sell somewhere, and so must the men who have been out on the road."

## Arriving {#arriving}

The road south thins the empire with every league: the villages are fewer and the forts closer together, until the last of them stands where the cultivable ground gives out. Belgen is the last place on it that has a market and a granary. South of the deepest fort the tribal lands begin, and the Halzi'a's patrols are the only Khelathi presence on the ground there. The villages near the last fort bar their gates earlier in the evening.

## Politics {#present}

Two low floods reach Belgen as a thinner supply of grain carried upriver from the valley. The Halzi'a reads the figures from the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapter's granary accounts, which tell him how long his forts can hold, and the answer is a short one. He sends a short render north, and late. His letters to the capital explain that a fort without stores is a fort without a garrison, and that the tribal lands to the south watch the patrols.

Under a weak throne he decides, and a throne that cannot keep the river is a throne at a distance. The selat's levies stay in its own forts and most of its taxes stay in its own treasury. The temple estates of Qeztu around the seat feed the garrison's families first.

## Order and Law {#order-and-law}

The Halzi'a of the selat holds the deepest fort on the southern march as a hereditary military governor. He has clerks as well, a steward for the revenue, the granaries and the labor levy, and a warden for the canals, but his authority rests on the soldiers he can put in the field, because nothing lies south of him except the tribal lands. Under a strong throne he reports to the capital. Fort-Commanders hold the southern forts, and an Overseer of Scribes keeps the tax rolls and the garrison pay.

## What the City Lives By {#what-the-city-lives-by}

Belgen lives by grain and by the garrison. The villages and estate-towns of the selat render grain to its granaries each harvest, and the garrison draws its share before the market sees any. Farmers on the cultivable strip plant what the canals still carry and sell nothing outside the selat. The market serves the villages and the men back from the road, and the temple estates of Qeztu hold much of the ground around the seat.
