## Overview {#overview}

**Iaqet-Leqa** (about 4,600 people) is the capital of [[affiliation-selatiaqetlq|the Selat of Iaqet-Leqa]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a garrison town of wells and quarry-roads behind the desert march of [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]].

The chief temple is the odd thing about it. A temple servant explains to a visitor who asks why a town of dry stone keeps the house of a river-beast's god: "[[lore-chelsurdty|Chelsur]] is the god of the eastern marsh, and of the wells out here, and of the gorge where the upper river pinches down to nothing. Wherever the water is worth fighting over, he is." Around the temple stand the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

"I mark the water on the cistern wall every dawn," a well-keeper tells a carter, "and the quarry gangs ask me the number before they ask for bread." Each quarry-road is a chain of wells, and a gang that misses one cannot reach the next. [[place-zelleqa|Zel-Leqa]], on the caravan track eastward, keeps deep sweet wells and a toll on water, shade and a safe night. [[place-yathiaqet|Yath-Iaqet]], in the hills north of the seat, cuts stone and turquoise for the temples, grows no food, and buys its grain, oil and beer from the river villages. The seat's granaries hold what the carts bring up from the valley, and its stores of water matter more than its stores of grain.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a is a garrison commander whose most important stores are water. He holds the wells, the quarry-roads and the garrison as a hereditary military governor. Fort-Commanders hold the well-forts along the roads, and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapter at the chief temple keeps the granary accounts.

## The Present {#present}

The wells hold through a short flood, and the grain does not. The quarry gangs are paid in grain, so the Halzi'a has shortened their hours to make the stores last, and the stone due to the crown sits at the quarry heads marked as awaiting transport. The valley road is open, but the carts that would haul the stone are the ones he needs to haul grain.

The temple of Chelsur offers rites for a river that will not rise, and he attends as a courtesy. Raiders from the sand-seas know every well, so Zel-Leqa keeps a watch and fears a poisoned cistern. A grain convoy that runs late starves the workings of Yath-Iaqet within the month.
