## Overview {#overview}

**Linmut** (about 4,500 people) is the capital of [[affiliation-selatlinmut|the Selat of Linmut]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a fortress-town on a river-island at the second cataract.

Approach it from the water and the walls come first. Behind them stand the chief temple of [[affiliation-wethur|Weth'ur]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns. "I cross all day, and the toll-house hears of every crossing," a ferryman at the landing says. Weth'ur, held to keep the passage from life to death orderly, is an apt patron for a town whose whole business is who may pass.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Linmut lives by the toll. The fortress commands the passage, and the toll is collected under its walls on every boat that stops. The island has room only for the garrison, so the ferry trade lodges on the mainland bank at [[place-yathlinmut|Yath-Linmut]], the landing town opposite, which is larger than the fortress it serves. Grain and vegetables from the east-bank farms cross to the island by ferry, and the garrison's pay and the toll feed the landing. The town's livelihood stops the day the garrison closes the river to travelers.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a commands the fortress and its levies as a hereditary military governor and collects the toll along with the selat's other taxes. A Caravan-Warden takes the caravan tolls at the landing road and the cataract pass, a toll-master of the Halzi'a's household keeps the toll-house, a Fort-Commander holds the walls, and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapter at the chief temple attests the weight-pieces. Tolls on the frontier run on weighed silver and gold.

## The Present {#present}

The toll-house learns the state of the river before the temple does: boatmen who run the rapids report the stage at the landing long before any priest reads the Measure. Boats from below the cataract carry less silt, and boats from upriver carry less ivory and gold because the southern road is thin. As the gateway of the south, the Halzi'a is the first governor who can tell what traffic on the whole river is doing, and he has known for two years that it is short.

The toll is revenue he can send or keep, and he enters a part of it against the fortress's upkeep. The capital wants this in writing; he sends the stage of the river and omits the sum. The cataract takes a ferry now and then, and the river is the only road the town has.

:::secret {title="The toll-house books"}
The part entered against the fortress's upkeep is larger than the upkeep costs. The Halzi'a keeps the difference, and leaves out the sum because the sum would show it.
:::

## History {#history}

The victory stela of [Kuz'Uqa]{.name data-type="being/npc"} at [[place-zelhalzi|Zel-Halzi]] counts the standard of [[affiliation-selatlinmut|the Selat of Linmut]] among the fourteen standards of the selatu taken up from the field below [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], where the [[affiliation-thaulgau|Tha'Ulgau]] destroyed the last army of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] in 1612 ST (499 BF) ([[lore-stelaquzuqa|The Stela of Kuz'Uqa at Zel-Halzi]]; [[lore-thaulgau|The Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]).
