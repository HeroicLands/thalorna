## Overview {#overview}

**Khuqet** (about 4,700 people) is the capital of [[affiliation-seltkhqtmntq|the Selat of Khuqet-Imntiqa]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], the oasis-capital of the far western desert march. It is not [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]], the garrisoned road in the northeast with no Halzi'a, no temple and no render; Khuqet has all three.

A clerk in the Halzi'a's household sorts the town for the drivers who come in off the trade-tracks. "There is the sun's temple, the Halzi'a's court, the bureau that keeps the tax rolls, the granaries and the markets. You will want the last two and be sent to the bureau for the third, and the temple will be there whether you go or not." The chief temple is that of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]], the sun-god.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Khuqet is a line of oasis-forts with a court at its head. The oasis-forts hold the wells, and the trade-tracks across the desert pass under their walls, so the town lives by tolls, by the grain tithe and by feeding what passes. [[place-zelimntiqa|Zel-Imntiqa]], a week of dry track west of the river, is the oasis market where drovers, salt-carriers and track-guides trade news and goods. [[place-lutimntiqa|Lut-Imntiqa]], two days along the oasis chain, is the estate-town of date and barley land whose stewards send dates and barley to the forts and buy cloth and iron. A steward out from the valley expects a harvest, and learns in the first season that this selat is a line of forts.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of the selat is a hereditary military governor, holding the wells, the tracks and the levies of the march. Fort-Commanders hold the oasis-forts, a Caravan-Warden takes the tolls at the wells, and the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] chapter at the chief temple attests the weight-pieces and keeps the granary accounts.

## The Present {#present}

The flood matters to Khuqet only through the grain tithe and the caravans. The tithe is late and light. The caravans are the news: they bring word from Bethua and beyond it that Okharik envoys are crossing the empire's western edge on their way to Galezkara, and that the houses at court are receiving them.

The Halzi'a charges the tolls, feeds the envoys and writes nothing down that his seat does not need. Two factions ask him what the riders said at the wells, and he answers each with what the other already knows. The oasis-forts keep their water, and the rest of the selat watches him keep it. A drought that drops the water table by a hand's breadth turns an oasis from a market into a refugee camp.
