## Overview {#overview}

"You have come in a busy season," a temple steward of [[affiliation-uznera|Uzn'era]] says to a pilgrim stepping from a ferry onto the landing at Yath-Leteru. "The boats are crowded, and the courtyard is full." The town is the capital of [[affiliation-selatseltltr|the Selat of Selat-Leteru]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], with about 4,000 people, the smallest seat of the ten delta selatu.

It holds the selat's chief temple of Uzn'era, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Yath-Leteru lives by pilgrims. They come by ferry to the landing, and the town sells them boat-fares, rooms and offerings, then sends them on to the marsh-temples and shrine-islands that the seat serves. [[place-garleteru|Gar-Leteru]] (about 3,500) is the pilgrims' lodging town on the mainland shore opposite the islands, and it fills when the seat's rooms are full. The few farming villages of the selat work the temple estates, which feed the pilgrims and the town from their granaries. The selat holds about 150,000 people, the fewest of the ten, and its islands and channels leave little ground for grain, so the temple owns much of the dry land.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of Selat-Leteru governs from the seat, a hereditary office held by a Zabu that commands the levies, collects the taxes and dispenses justice. The selat's levies are few. The temple of Uzn'era, goddess of fertility, renewal and healing, holds the offerings and the granaries, and the temple's steward settles the disputes of lodging and fares on the landing. Order is a matter of boats: a ferryman who overloads his craft or a lodging-keeper who overcharges a pilgrim answers to the temple's steward and then to the Halzi'a. The danger is the water, where a crowded ferry in a channel full of shrine-islands is the most likely way for a pilgrim to die.

## The Present {#present}

Yath-Leteru has few people and many shrines, and in a failing-flood year the shrines are its busiest trade. Pilgrims come to the marsh-temples to ask for renewal, and they bring offerings of grain that the temple estates store and feed them from. The Halzi'a's revenue is the pilgrims' boat-fares and lodgings and the temple estates' offerings, and both are up. He sends the throne a tribute in the incense and oils the shrines make, and he says that the pilgrims eat the grain. The smallest of the delta selatu is the one that has gained. The villagers eat more than they usually do and count the crowded islands a poor trade for their quiet. The temple courtyard, quiet in an ordinary season, is full.
