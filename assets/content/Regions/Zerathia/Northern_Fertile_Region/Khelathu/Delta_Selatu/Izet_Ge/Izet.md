## Overview {#overview}

Izet is the city where the delta's dates and wine are sold. At a stall near the temple gate, a wine-seller ladles a dark, sweet pour into a clay cup for a passing carter and names the plantation it came from. "Drink it slowly," she says. "It was made slowly." The city is the capital of [[affiliation-selatizetge|the Selat of Izet-Ge]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], with about 45,000 people.

It holds the selat's chief temple of [[affiliation-thubai|Thuba'i]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## Politics {#present}

Izet-Ge has dates and wine, and a hungry year is a good one for dates. The palms root deep and bear well, and a sack of dried dates feeds a village when a sack of wheat cannot be bought. The Halzi'a's strength is the vaults of Thuba'i, whose temples hold wine and granaries together. He fills the temple granaries with dates and with the grain he receives for the wine, and the vaults are full. The render he sends the throne is paid in sweet wine, which the court accepts at a price the delta's merchants find generous. A family with a palm-grove eats while a family with a wheat plot does not. The farmers who grow barley between the groves have less to sell, and the villages near the seat know that the vaults are full.

## Order and Law {#order-and-law}

The Halzi'a of Izet-Ge is a hereditary governor and a Zabu who commands the selat's levies, collects its taxes and dispenses its justice, as much merchant as lord. The Halzi'a's Steward keeps the revenue and the granaries, the Overseer of Scribes keeps the bureau that reckons the selat's trade for the treasury, and the Gar-Hálzi chapter of the temple attests the weights on which a plantation is paid. The temple's vaults are a working part of the economy and its priests are the city's other power. Its dangers are those of a city with full vaults in a hungry year: grain hoarders, the theft of vault stores, and the bitterness of the barley-farmers toward the plantations. The palm-climbers' work is a danger of its own, and a fall from a trunk with a loaded basket is a common death.

## What the City Lives By {#what-the-city-lives-by}

Izet lives by the date-palm plantations and the sweet wines of the delta, which the empire knows it for. The climbers cut the clusters, the estate-towns dry and press the dates, and the vintners make the wine that the city's cellars and the temple's vaults hold. The plantations grow dates and vines rather than grain, so the city's bread comes down the river from the upper selatu and is paid for with the sales. Away from the palms, the villages and temple estates of [[place-izetgenome|Izet-Ge]] grow grain on the usual terms, with a share of the harvest owed to the granaries and labor to the canals, and the scribes are the one reliable ladder out of the fields. The chief temple and its estates hold much of the palm country.

## History {#history}

The victory stela of [Kuz'Uqa]{.name data-type="being/npc"} at [[place-zelhalzi|Zel-Halzi]] counts the standard of [[affiliation-selatizetge|the Selat of Izet-Ge]] among the fourteen standards of the selatu taken up from the field below [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], where the [[affiliation-thaulgau|Tha'Ulgau]] destroyed the last army of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] in 1612 ST (499 BF) ([[lore-stelaquzuqa|The Stela of Kuz'Uqa at Zel-Halzi]]; [[lore-thaulgau|The Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]).
