## Overview {#overview}

Gar-Shebazet smells of cut green stems and wet clay. A paper-cutter at the edge of the market, with a pile of finished sheets weighted under a stone, explains it to a newcomer: "Everything here is either a reed or something that has just been done to one."

The town is the capital of [[affiliation-selatgrshbzt|the Selat of Gar-Shebazet]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], with about 8,900 people. It holds the selat's chief temple of [[affiliation-shebazet|Shebazet]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Gar-Shebazet makes the empire's paper. The marshes grow the papyrus, the cutters pole out for the stems, and the town's workshops split, press and dry them into sheets that the bureaus buy. [[place-luthawu|Lut-Thawu]] (about 7,800) is the papyrus-cutting town a day downstream. [[place-yathshebazet|Yath-Shebazet]] (about 9,400) is the reed-and-fowling town in the marshes, reached by punt. [[place-zeltelgu|Zel-Telgu]] (about 6,400) is the market town on the marsh edge where marsh villages sell fish and reeds and river farms sell grain and beer. The seat takes what all three bring and sells the finished paper upriver. It eats fowl and fish, and its bread comes from the farms inland.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of Gar-Shebazet governs from the seat, a hereditary office held by a Zabu that commands the levies, collects the taxes and dispenses justice. He answers to the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]] for the paper. The Overseer of Scribes keeps the tax-roll bureau, which is the selat's largest customer for its own sheets. The temple of Shebazet blesses the reed, holds estates in the marsh, and keeps a chapter of [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] that vouches for the pieces in which cutters are paid. The marsh is the town's danger: a punt that leaves the cut channels can be lost among identical reed-beds, and the beds belong to the temple's estates before they belong to the cutter.

## The Present {#present}

A low flood shrinks the marshes that grow the paper, and Gar-Shebazet feels it first. The papyrus beds come in thin, the cutters pole farther into the marsh for less, and the channels between the beds have silted. The fowlers do well, since the birds crowd into the pools that remain, but the villages eat fowl and fish and go short of bread. The Halzi'a answers for the paper to bureaus that count the empire's tribute, so a shortage reaches [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] quickly. He sends the reed in the quantity the beds allow and holds back the better stock for the temple of Shebazet. The scribes complain that the sheets are thin and ask for the whole thick stems the marsh no longer grows, and that complaint is the capital's only sign that the delta is short.
