## Overview {#overview}

"You will have noticed there are no children playing at the water's edge," a priest of [[affiliation-chelsur|Chelsur]] says to a visitor on the temple steps. "That is the god's doing and the god's gift, and we have never agreed on which." Lut-Chelsur is the capital of [[affiliation-selatltchlsr|the Selat of Lut-Chelsur]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 7,400 in the eastern marsh.

It holds the selat's chief temple of Chelsur, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

The town lives by the river-beasts and the cult of the crocodile-god. Hunters work the marsh, and the hides and beast-products they bring in are the selat's render. [[place-zmachelsur|Zma-Chelsur]] (about 9,700) is the hunters' landing in the eastern marsh, reached by boat from the seat. [[place-yathchelsur|Yath-Chelsur]] (about 7,900) is the mound-town of the crocodile-keepers, half a day away. The firmer ground near the seat grows grain under the temple's estates, and that grain and the hide trade are what the town eats and sells. The priests of the god disagree whether the crocodile is the god or his creature, and the answer decides whether a cull is a sacrilege that needs a dispensation or a service that needs only a rite.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of Lut-Chelsur governs from the seat, a hereditary office held by a Zabu that commands the levies, collects the taxes and dispenses justice. A cull is a religious act before it is a commercial one, so the Halzi'a licenses it, and the Gar-Hálzi chapter of the temple weighs the hides on which the licenses are paid. The temple protects the animals and its estates share in the hide render. The danger is the river itself: the sacred animal is also the one that takes farmers and children along the banks, and the town keeps children away from the water.

## The Present {#present}

In a low-water year the river-beasts crowd into the few pools that remain, and the god's own animals take more farmers and children than in an ordinary year. The priests protect the animals, the hunters kill the ones that have taken people, and the Halzi'a arbitrates. He grants the hunters leave to take more than the temple allows, and the temple estates lose their share of the hide render as a result. The selat's tribute in hides and beast-products is down, and he enters the shortfall as a lost season. The estate-towns near the seat have the temple's protection and the court to settle disputes. The outer marsh villages have neither, and they bring their quarrels to the Halzi'a rather than to the priests. He decides them by the harvest.
