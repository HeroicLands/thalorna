## Overview {#overview}

"You will smell the curing-houses long before you see the harbor," says a carter who loads at them, hauling a strap tight over a stack of salted barrels. "By the second day you stop noticing, and then you are one of us." The town is the capital of [[affiliation-selatgezazab|the Selat of Gezazabu]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], and its roughly 9,900 people live around a western delta port, its salt-fish and curing-houses, and the trade road to [[place-bethuargn|Bethua]].

It holds the selat's chief temple of [[affiliation-thubai|Thuba'i]], the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Boats go out at night and come back with the dawn tide, and by noon the fish is split and under salt in the curing-houses along the quay. The salt-fish feeds the coast and is the selat's render to the capital. The seat does not make all of it. [[place-zelmegit|Zel-Megit]] (about 9,900) is a ferry-and-curing town on the westernmost channel, [[place-yathzekri|Yath-Zekri]] (about 9,100) is a harbor town to the south on a mud-bar coast, and [[place-lutzechi|Lut-Zechi]] (about 8,600) is the inland estate-town of the salt-fish merchants.

The second livelihood is the trade road to Bethua. Carters load and unload at the seat, and the town sells them lodging, fodder and fish while they wait on the road's business. The town grows little of its own bread. Its grain comes from the estate-towns inland, whose fields draw on the river's tail, and from what the road brings.

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of Gezazabu governs from the seat. The office is hereditary and held by a Zabu, and it commands the selat's levies, collects its taxes and dispenses its justice. The Halzi'a's Steward keeps the revenue, the granaries and the corvée, and an Overseer of Scribes keeps the rolls. The Harbor-Master berths the boats and settles the quarrels of the waterfront. The Gar-Hálzi chapter attached to the temple of Thuba'i weighs the pieces in which a curing-house is paid and holds the granary accounts. The town's danger is the water: the mud-bar channels south of the port take a boat in every storm, and a pilot who knows them charges for the knowledge.

## The Present {#present}

The flood has come low two years running, and the road to Bethua has become the seat's business. Bethuan grain from the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]]'s aqueducts, which do not depend on the flood, already trickles east in the ordinary way of trade, and the seat meets each wagon, takes the Halzi'a's toll and passes the grain on to the delta's markets. The large sale waits on the Metrissa's choice. If she sells, the toll at Gezazabu is the price of the road. The Halzi'a sets the toll himself and has not raised it, which keeps the Trade-Houses and the delta's merchants friendly to him. His curing-houses sell to the capital at the price the capital offers, and the fish keeps the coast fed even as the fields inland thin. Farmers come to the seat in the evenings to ask what the road is carrying. Envoys from [[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] come along the same road and lodge at the temple of Thuba'i.
