## Overview {#overview}

"Begin at the water-stair," a priest of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]] tells a pilgrim fresh off a river-barge, "and look east at the hour the sun clears the reeds. That is the god arriving where his river divides." Khelaga is the capital of [[affiliation-selatkhelaga|the Selat of Khelaga]] and the seat of its [[affiliation-empireakhlth#titles-and-forms-of-address|Halzi'a]], a town of about 6,000 at the delta apex.

It holds the selat's chief temple of Uqa'a, the Halzi'a's court and granaries, the scribal bureau that keeps the tax rolls, and the markets that serve the surrounding villages and estate-towns.

## What the Town Lives By {#what-the-town-lives-by}

Khelaga lives by the division of the river. Every boat bound for the sea passes the apex, and the old customs-stations and forts that stand there count it, tax it and hold it if the Halzi'a's clerks are not satisfied. [[place-lutkhelaga|Lut-Khelaga]] (about 6,300), a short sail away, is the customs town of the fork. [[place-zelkhelaga|Zel-Khelaga]] (about 5,200) is the shrine-and-estate town upstream on the western channel, and its estates grow the barley and onions of the silt-islands. The seat itself is about the size of its customs town. It holds the temple, the court and the granaries, and its pilgrims, who come to see the river divide, fill its lodgings. The apex forts and stations date from the sea-raiders' going ([[lore-seahouse|the Closing of the Sea House]]).

## Who Rules and Keeps Order {#who-rules}

The Halzi'a of Khelaga governs from the seat, a hereditary office held by a Zabu that commands the levies, collects the taxes and dispenses justice. The Customs-Scribe enters every bale and jar that crosses the fork. The Dike-Warden keeps the channel-cuts and the Canal-Warden the sluices on which the estates' fields depend. The priests of Uqa'a keep the old shrines and watch the river's division. The danger lies down the western channel, where [[place-chariotfld|the Chariot Field]], the blood-field of the chariot corps broken by a Vilérian army in 377 AF, stands within the selat. Standing water along its bank stirs it, and a Reading held at the edge quiets it for one night only.

## The Present {#present}

The apex is where the river first shows how short the water is. The channels run low, the two forks carry less, and the ferries that cross them touch bottom. Barges bound for the ports are lightened or wait on the quay under the customs-stations' eye, and the Halzi'a collects customs on every one that passes and enters a barge as delayed when it is held at the fork. The old shrines of Uqa'a are full of petitioners who ask the river to rise, and the priests who watch the division report to the Halzi'a what they report to [[place-themeasure|the Measure]]. Low channels leave more water standing along the Chariot Field's bank, and the priests cannot spare the stores that pay for Readings while the granaries are short. The Halzi'a does not pay for one either.
