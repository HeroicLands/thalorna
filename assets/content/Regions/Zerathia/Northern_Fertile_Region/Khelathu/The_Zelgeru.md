"Count the sacks, and then ask whose sacks they are," a Lem'Nelgir who runs a temple's lands tells a new tally-boy at the estate gate. "The grain is the same grain a lord would hold. The owner is a god."

**The Zelgeru** is the name for the great temple-estates of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu]], each with its own granaries, workshops and tenant-villages. They work the land with the same grain and labor as any manor, but hold it as religious foundations rather than as a lord's demesne, and that is what lets their combined wealth rival the palace of the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]]. A temple holds its estates in its god's name, and the god's priests administer them.

## What an Estate Is

An estate is a temple's endowed land: a granary for the grain of its tenants, workshops for the temple's daily needs, and a ring of tenant-villages that owe their labor and a share of the harvest to the temple before they owe either to any secular official. The bondsmen, the empire's lowest standing above the outcast, are bound to an estate or a temple and hold nothing of their own. The estate-steward and his tally-scribes keep the rolls, and a Lem'Nelgir of the temple runs the lands. The temple of [[affiliation-shebazet|Shebazet]] and its estates hold much of the marsh in the Selat of Gar-Shebazet, and the [[place-greattempluqaa|Great Temple of Uqa'a]] owns estates across the [[affiliation-capitalselat|Capital Selat]] and beyond. [[place-garlegulu|Gar-Legulu]], east of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]], is a town built to run one such estate.

## Where the Estates Lie

The Zelgeru holds no single tract of ground. Its estates lie wherever the empire's irrigated grain-land will support a temple's granaries, from the delta marshes to the upper-river basins, and each is carved out of whichever selat surrounds it. One selat can therefore hold estates of several temples that answer to their gods and not to its Halzi'a. A tenant-village bound to one of the Zelgeru answers to its temple before it answers to any governor, and the difference shows the first time a village has to choose between a temple's claim and a governor's.

## Trade Abroad

The estates' western trade reaches [[place-orzara|Orzara City]] through their factor, [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]], who offers the Vilérian throne grain on credit and silver for its paper. The capital's shipping houses want the grain contract, while its grain dealers oppose the foreign supply.
