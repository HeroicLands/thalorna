## Overview {#overview}

A new apprentice at the Measure is made to read the cut cubits aloud from the bottom of the stair before he is given any other task. The keeper's rule for him is short: the number is read as the water stands, and it is not bent for the god-king or for anyone.

**The Measure** is the igelu-gauge of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]: a graduated stone stair and shaft descending to the river at the upstream point of [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], where the river first strikes the sacred ground. Its walls are cut with the mark of every cubit of the flood. The priests of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]] keep it, and the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] treat it as the one instrument in the empire that cannot be commanded.

## Inside {#inside}

The stair runs down the shaft to the water, and the cut cubits climb the wall beside it. As [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]], the Inundation, brings the river up, the Reader of the Measure goes down to the stair each day, reads where the water stands and carries the count to the [[place-greattempluqaa|Great Temple of Uqa'a]]. At the crest the reading is proclaimed to the city and the empire as the single most important number of the year, and the proclamation is held as a festival.

## Who Is There {#who-is-there}

The **Reader of the Measure** is the office of the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]] that reads the gauge daily through the inundation. A keeper and his apprentices stay at the gauge, and the royal guard watches the island's gates around it. On the night the number is proclaimed the burial clubs of Gar-Qethar read the court tale of a scribe who wrote the flood false ([[lore-qethgezerker|The Tale of Gezerker]]).

## The Present {#present}

The height of the flood foretells the harvest and the harvest sets the taxes: a full Measure means plenty and a heavy levy, a low one means dearth, and a failed one means famine. Two low crests in a row have made the reading the most watched number in the capital, because the granaries are thinning and the selat governors quietly hold back tribute. A party can stand at the stair at the crest, or be sent to confirm a reading a faction doubts. The falsifying of it is among the gravest crimes a Khelathi can name.

## History {#history}

A Gar-Au once bent the number. The last of [[affiliation-garzinelrelu|Gar-Zin'el'Relu]] hid a failing flood by having the Measure read by other hands, killing anyone who said the word _hunger_ aloud ([[lore-failfloods|the Failing Floods]]). After that, every later reading had to be checked against the wall. A conqueror who left the Measure alone found it left alone: the sea-raiders ruled through the existing bureaus ([[lore-seahouse|the Closing of the Sea House]]).
