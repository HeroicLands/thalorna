## Overview {#overview}

"Walk in from the quay and you walk back through the dynasties," a Lem'Nelgir of the temple tells a pilgrim at the river gate. "Every house that held the throne raised one gate, one hall or one forest of columns in front of the last. The god is at the far end, where the oldest builders left him."

The **Great Temple of Uqa'a** is the oldest and grandest sanctuary in the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] and the seat of its official doctrine, the temple against which every other measures itself. It stands on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], the island the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] hold to be the mound that first rose from the waters at [[lore-qettelgu|the Qet Telgu]], and it serves [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]], the Solar Flame. It is less a building than a city of stone: court behind court and pylon behind pylon, three thousand years of royal additions, with obelisks tipped in electrum that catch the first and last light and a vast rectangular sacred lake whose level rises and falls with the flood. At the heart lies a small, dark, ancient sanctuary where the god's image dwells, and only the Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a and the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]] may enter it.

## History {#history}

[[lore-judgfalseuq|The Judgment of the False Uqa'a]] was held here: the last Gar-Au of [[affiliation-garzinelrelu|Gar-Zin'el'Relu]] had seized the temple and its granaries and silenced the priests who named his blasphemy ([[lore-akhstruckhs|the Struck House]]), and the priesthood bound him and gave him over to the curse ([[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]]).

## The Present {#present}

The temple is where the throne's claim is kept up and where it can be undone. The priesthood validates the Gar-Au's divinity and the Gar-Au endows the temple, and each can ruin the other. When the flood fails the strain falls on both: the temple's priests read [[place-themeasure|The Measure]] and proclaim the number, and two low crests in a row put the proclamation, and the fiction behind it, under the eyes of the whole city. The succession is disputed between factions of the bureaucracy and the priesthood, and the failing flood makes the dispute dangerous.

A party reaches the temple by the island's gates, which the royal guard watches day and night. Priests, courtiers, scribes of the inner bureaus and those summoned before the throne pass them, and the crowd keeps to the far bank and the water's edge.

## Who Rules It {#who-rules-it}

The **Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a**, the High Priest, is after the Gar-Au the most powerful figure in the realm. The sitting Gar-Au chooses the Thaz'Lekhau in consultation with the outgoing holder, a succession the priesthood has more than once contested. Under the High Priest the offices the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]] names serve here: the Keeper of the Inner Sanctuary, the Keeper of the Observatory, the Reader of the Measure, the Bearer of the God's Image and the Keeper of the Solstice Rites. The Lem'Nelgir administer and teach, and the Wazu tend the sacred fires.

## Lands and Income {#lands-and-income}

The temple owns estates across [[affiliation-capitalselat|the Capital Selat]] and beyond, held on the terms of [[lore-zelgeru|the Zelgeru]], and employs thousands. It schools the empire's finest priests. In the adjoining precinct of [[affiliation-rethsaar|Reth'Sa'ar]] it keeps the master copies of the king-lists and the calendar, and the temple chronicle that opens with [[lore-firstoccasion|the First Occasion]].

## Visiting the House {#visiting}

The temple lies at the pivot of the great axis. [[place-waluqaa|Wal-Uqa'a]] enters across the [[place-bridgecomingfrth|Bridge of the Coming-Forth]], runs through the forecourts and leaves by the temple's western face onto the [[place-bridgegoingdwn|Bridge of the Going-Down]]. On the high festivals the image of Uqa'a is carried down the avenue and across the water to the island. At the midsummer [Festival of the Coming-Forth]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} the dawn breaks straight down the eastern road, and at the midwinter [Festival of the Going-Down]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} the sun sets straight into the necropolis. The law of the empire forbids any structure to break the view from the temple to the horizon along either road, and a building that creeps into the line is pulled down.

## The Grounds {#the-grounds}

At the flood the water laps the outer courts, and the temple stands alone above it as the first mound stood above the waters of creation. The city counts it the holiest sight of the year. The temple platforms are raised clear of the highest flood, and the sacred lake mirrors the pylons at whatever level the river stands.
