"The Halzi'a has the selat, and I have the estate," a Legha'lutu tells a tax-scribe who has come to count his sacks. "He answers to the throne and I answer to him, and the sacks are the same sacks either way."

**The Lutu** is the name for the landed estates of the noble houses of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]]. An estate is a manor, its fields and an attached village. Its ruler is a **Legha'lutu** if a man and a **Zabet'lutu** if a woman, and both are Zabu, nobles of a titled house. The estate-ruler answers to the Halzi'a of the selat the land lies in, and the Halzi'a answers to the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Au]].

## What an Estate Is

The estate is a house's land and the standing of its holder. The Zabu rank descends with the house, and the estate is the ground the household lives on: a manor, the fields around it and a village attached to it. Bondsmen are bound to an estate or a temple and hold nothing of their own. A Zabet'lutu holds the same authority in her own right.

The title always comes before the personal name, so a party greets the ruler of an estate as Legha'lutu or Zabet'lutu and then the name: Zabet'lutu Thaminra.

## How It Differs

The Lutu holds its land as a house holds it, where [[lore-zelgeru|the Zelgeru]] holds its land as a god's foundation. The estate lies inside a selat and under its Halzi'a, who commands the selat's army, collects its taxes and dispenses its justice. The throne grants estates, and land that yields nothing cannot carry one: the desert march of [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]] has none. A great house also endows [[lore-westernestates|a western estate]] for its dead, apart from the land it lives on.
