## Overview {#overview}

**Magu-Ithnet** is the Khelathi name for the lake that lies in a basin of the western desert, far from the sea and watered by the Zumelesh. The name means _the garden of the west_: _magu_ is a garden and _ithnet_ is the west. The lake measures about ninety-four miles from end to end and seventy-four across, some 4,100 square miles of water, and it is the one great body of fresh water in the empire that is not the river.

## The Land {#the-land}

The basin sits in high desert west of the river's black land. A canal leaves the Zumelesh and runs west across the desert to it, and the lake is held at its level by the sluices at the canal's mouth: when the river rises the gates open and the lake takes its share, and when the river falls they close. The lake holds a year's water and evens out a single poor flood, but two low floods in a row show at the sluices: the river crests below the gate-sill for days at a time, the lake draws down, and the drains at the estates' upper edge stand dry.

The eastern shore, on the canal side, is where the lake matters. Where the water has drawn back from the old basin edge, the empire has laid out reclaimed estates: ruled fields of wheat, flax and orchard, each one cut into the silt by drains that run back to the lake. The estates are the only farmland in the west that takes the river's flood at second hand, so their harvest falls later and less than the valley margin's in a poor year, but it falls all the same. Reed-beds and fish-weirs line the margin below them, and the open water holds carp-like river fish, wildfowl and a small population of crocodiles. The far western shore is reed-bed and salt-crusted flat, with fishers' camps and no harbor.

## Who Uses It {#who-uses-it}

The estates are held by temples and great houses of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|the Empire of Au'Khelathu]] and worked by tenant households brought out from the valley. Boatmen carry grain, fish and salted wildfowl down the canal to the river and onward to [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] and the delta ports. Reed-cutters and fishers camp on the far shore in the cool months. A canal warden's post at the sluices keeps the gate-tally and the boat-tolls.

## Dangers {#dangers}

A storm crosses the open water in an hour and the far shore offers only the fishers' camps in the reeds, with no harbor for a laden boat. The canal silts, and a stretch left unscoured runs shallow enough to ground a loaded barge. The desert beyond the estates holds no wells, and a party that leaves the shore on foot carries its own water.

## What Is Told {#what-is-told}

Boatmen call the lake _the mouth the river feeds_, and the estate stewards say it never dries. Fishers on the far shore keep a story of a drowned house beneath the water, whose lamps burn on still nights.

## Converging Interests {#present}

The temples and houses that hold estates on the shore want the canal gate open for as long as possible each flood, and after two low floods they want it open for every day the river clears the sill. The delta selatu that draw on the river downstream want it shut early, because the same short flood leaves their own canal-heads dry and they count every day the gate stands open as water taken from their fields. The canal wardens answer to the empire's water-engineers and keep the tally that both sides argue from. For a party the lake is a destination in its own right: a place to hire on as an estate guard, to carry a cargo that will not travel by the river road, or to ask after a tenant family that has stopped sending word.
