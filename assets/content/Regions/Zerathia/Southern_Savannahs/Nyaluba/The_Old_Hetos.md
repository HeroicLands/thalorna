## Overview {#overview}

"You know the Old Hetos," a Ngonzi elder says. "No, you don't. None of us do, unless we are Ngonzi." The **Old Hetos** are the dry-stone walls, raised platforms and oval enclosures of the southeastern highlands of the [[place-sthrnsvnhs|Southern Savannahs]], the work of a lost civilization that built in stone before the Nyáluba were a people. The Nyáluba do not call them ruins. They hold the Old Hetos to be inhabited by the spirits of those who built them.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The [[affiliation-ngonzi|Ngonzi]], the eagle-clan, hold them. They alone claim descent from the builders, they alone know the techniques the builders used, and they make the offerings that keep each Heto welcoming. Each Old Heto has its Guardian, a shaman of the eagle cult who holds that one site and no other, and the Guardian says no: entry to every Old Heto but Heto Lubemo is refused to anyone who is not Ngonzi, including to the Nyáluba of the other four clans. Heto Lubemo is the one exception, and only for the delegations of the five-clan council, which sit within its enclosure under the Ngonzi who keep it. The Lobwezi griots, pressed, say the builders may be the ancestors of some other people entirely, and whose dead they are is the longest-running dispute in Nyáluba country.

## Layers {#history}

The Old Hetos lie on the country's highest ground, in the cool uplands where the rivers run all year. Nobody living knows what the lesser enclosures and platforms around the great ones were for. A Kolembozi spirit-speaker, [Lusheya nu Kolembozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is said to have walked into one in her initiation and come out speaking the Stone Tongue, the speech the Ngonzi elders say was the builders' ([[lore-stonetongue|the Stone Tongue]]). The Ngonzi confirm the report, and the Lobwezi griots have entered it into the record of the Lubemo.

## What Is There Now {#present}

[[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]] is the one the Nyáluba use: an oval of dry-stone walls higher than a tall man, set on a raised platform at the edge of the escarpment, where the Ngonzi keep the offerings that keep the Lubemo welcome. The other Old Hetos stand quiet and guarded. This year the delegations gather at Heto Lubemo, and the Ngonzi are the hosts of a council whose Convener is at war. The Guardians of the other Old Hetos hold their walls closed against everyone who is not Ngonzi, the delegations included, and the Ngonzi say the stone-builders' dead are not a party to any war.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

A party that goes to an Old Heto uninvited meets its Guardian, who says no. At Heto Lubemo the delegations camp in apportioned grounds outside the enclosure, and the formal sessions are held within it.
