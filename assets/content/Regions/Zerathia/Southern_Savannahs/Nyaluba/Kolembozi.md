## Overview {#overview}

The **Kolembozi** are the crocodile-clan of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]], the river-people of the eastern wetlands. They are fewer than the Ruwozi, about three hundred and fifty thousand, and more influential than their numbers, because the great rivers that water all of Nyáluba country pass through their territory before they reach the sea. They fish and hunt in the wetlands, move through the channels in long reed canoes, and carry the most elaborate spirit-relationships of the five clans, since the river-spirits of their country are numerous and unforgiving.

## The Clan Now {#the-house-now}

Kolembozi life runs along the water: fish-camps that move with the river, landings where the canoes are drawn up, and the reaches of channel each household knows by name. The clan's standing in the confederation rests on its _zherami nyende_, the spirit-speakers, whom the other clans send for when a matter needs judgment, healing or a bargain with a difficult spirit. The senior of them is [[being-lusheyaklm|Lusheya nu Kolembozi]], who walked into an Old Heto at her initiation and came out speaking [[lore-stonetongue|the Stone Tongue]].

A Kolembozi who loses a relative to a crocodile names the crocodile, by name and by lineage, and treats the matter as a family affair to be settled and not avenged. [[lore-settlingfigs|The Settling at the Two Figs]] is the fire story of one such settling.

## Territory and Waters {#holdings}

The clan holds the eastern wetlands, where the rivers from the highlands spread and slow before the eastern coast: a maze of papyrus channels, oxbow lakes, hippopotamus pools and crocodile-haunted reaches. The paramount is [[being-nyabesnklm|Nyabesa nu Kolembozi]], and the paramount's great cattle-fold is [[place-nyangulovlg|Nyangulo]], at the edge of the wetlands.

The water is the clan's trade as well as its country. Kolembozi canoes carry fish, salt from a few coastal pans, papyrus and riverine goods up the eastern rivers to the highlands and the central plains, and the clan holds the river-trade as the Sengala hold the northern overland trade. Every clan's herds drink from rivers that pass through Kolembozi country.

## Quarrels and Debts {#present}

The Kolembozi are at war with the [[affiliation-ruwozi|Ruwozi]]. Every river that waters Nyáluban country passes through the wetlands before the sea, and the dry season left the channels low. The Kolembozi hold that the river's flow is the clan's to answer for and that the reeds, the fish-camps and the landings come first in a dry year. The Ruwozi say a channel was diverted to keep the water in the wetlands. The Reach-Elders say the channel was cut by hand and name no hand, and the Ruwozi accuse the clan of cutting it.

The Senior Spirit-Speaker, who is asked before the clan makes war, was asked, and the answer was war. The Master of the Canoes has withdrawn the reed-canoes from every landing that serves a Ruwozi herd, so the ferrying of cattle across the channels, which every clan relies on, has stopped for the Ruwozi alone. The Kolembozi paramount sits at [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]] across from a Convener who is a party to the war, and the clan asks of the other three what the Ruwozi ask: that they choose. The Lubemo does not vote, so the Kolembozi cannot be outnumbered, and no convergence can be reached over the Kolembozi paramount's objection.

The salt, fish and papyrus that go up the rivers to the Sengala and the highlands still move to the Sengala landings, and the clan keeps that trade open as the proof that it is the Ruwozi it is fighting and not the Pact.

## History as Told {#history}

The Kolembozi count themselves among the clans that swore the [Long Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} and do not say they were its first. Their own stories begin at the crossings: the girl taken by a crocodile, the settling between two lines before the Guardian and the griot, the brother who would not say the word. The river is older than the Pact.

## Arms {#arms}

The clan musters by age-set regiments of young men and young women, and its fighters are boatmen. They move in reed canoes through channels no other clan can read, strike from the papyrus and withdraw into it, and use the drums to coordinate scattered parties. A Kolembozi muster controls water, and in this country that is controlling the ground.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **A dry channel.** A landing that has fed a dozen households has no water in it. The Reach-Elder says the channel was diverted by hand, and will pay a party to find out whose.
- **The crocodile with a name.** A family has settled with a crocodile and now wants the settling witnessed, outside the clan, by a griot of another.
