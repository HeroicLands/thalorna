## Overview

The Southern Stair is the road over the southern side of [[place-klhrring|the Ring]] of [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]]. It leaves a river valley of the southern coast and climbs a long ridge to the crest, terrace above terrace, with the steps of the road and the walls of the terraces built as one work of stone. The Kaliharans call the ridge _avothani_ (high ridge), and the lineage Avothani that holds the ridge and the stair cut into it is named for it. From the crest the road goes down the inner face to the southern shore of [[place-klhrlake|the lake]] in [[place-klhrbowl|the Bowl]].

## The Lineage and the Road

The lineages of the valley work the lower terraces, and the Avothani hold everything above them: the upper terraces, the steps and the crest. Each household of the Avothani keeps a stretch of the stair and its walls as its own part of the Whole, and rebuilds what the rains bring down. A wind-stone stands on the crest at the head of the stair.

## What Is Seen from It

Looking down, the stair shows the whole southern coast: the valley it leaves, the terraces stepping down its sides, the green ridges on either hand and the sea beyond. From the crest the southern shore of the lake and its cleared land lie below on the other side. Along the crest to the west, one of the wind-stones stands bone-white and hard, a dead work that can be told from the stair a day's climb below.

## The Cost of Crossing

The stair is the longest climb of the three passes, steps the whole way, and it takes three days from the valley floor to the crest. Loads go up on porters' backs, since nothing else can manage the upper steps. In Deepwater the terrace walls slump and the stair is closed until the Avothani have rebuilt them.
