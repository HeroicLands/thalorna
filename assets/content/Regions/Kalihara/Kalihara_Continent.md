Kalihara is the island that is easier to reach than to leave. It lies in the western ocean, four hundred and fifty to seven hundred miles west of [[place-okharisrgn|Okháris]] and [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]], off the mouth of the western strait through which the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] opens on the ocean, and a ship that clears the strait with a fair wind behind her and settled weather raises the peaks of the Ring in about a week. Getting home again takes two or three times as long when it goes well, and the ports of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] reckon that one return voyage in eight does not come home at all. Beyond Kalihara there is only open water until the coasts of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]], which lie farther west still.

The island is some four hundred miles across and nearly as far from north to south, with a scatter of islets off its coasts, and it is a single country. Where [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] and [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]] are mosaics of cultures and empires, Kalihara carries one continuous civilization, one people, one language family and one philosophical tradition, all reaching back to an origin older than any other recorded history on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]], and it has faced inward for ten thousand years. Geographers who class it a continent do so for that reason and no other: in size it is a large island, and the Kaliharans call it nothing but the island.

## Geography

The coast is the only part of Kalihara a foreigner sees, and it is jungle to the waterline. Coastal lowlands of mangrove, palm and gardened village give way within a few miles to steep ridgelines under unbroken canopy, and overland travel into the interior is impractical for anyone who does not know the paths. Rivers come down the ridges to the sea on every side. The eastern river valleys carry the broadest terraces and the thickest settlement of the coast, and the four ports stand on the eastern shore and its islets; the western slopes fall more steeply to a harder coast.

The ridges climb to [[place-klhrring|the Ring]], a circle of tall volcanic mountains that closes the island on every side, forested to the treeline and bare along the crest, its peaks visible from far out at sea and wrapped in cloud for most of the year. Three passes cross the Ring, and in one place a gap breaks it, where the water of the interior goes out to the sea. Inside the Ring lies [[place-klhrbowl|the Bowl]], the island's interior, forested over most of its floor and terraced on the inner slopes of the mountains. At its center lies [[place-klhrlake|the lake]], and from the lake rises [[place-klhrmassif|the Massif]], a single mount standing in the water. The Bowl's cleared land lies along the eastern and southern shores of the lake; its western and northern reaches are unbroken forest. The oldest of the custodians' sanctuaries stands on the Massif, in the middle of the lake.

Forest covers most of the island, and the cleared land is the terraces and the gardened villages. Volcanic soil, terraced for thousands of years, feeds the island's three million people without any trade in staple goods.

The climate is hot and humid the year round, cooler on the heights, and governed by the monsoon, whose four turnings order the island's year: the **Dry-Turn** about the fourth month, when the rains end; **Stillwater**, the height of the dry season; the **Wet-Turn** about the seventh month, when the rains return; and **Deepwater**, the heart of the wet season late in the year, when travel in the interior stops. The jungle holds an extraordinary range of plants and animals, some of them found nowhere else on Thalorna, such as the [[being-mbrydct|Ember-eyed Cat]], the [[being-cnpyglr|Canopy Glider]], the [[being-slndtrtl|Island Turtle]] and the [[being-mngbhmth|Mangrove Behemoth]] of the eastern shore, with the trees and blooms of its [[lore-klhrflora|flora]], and Ankarian naturalists who have seen the specimens suspect that some were cultivated deliberately in a very distant age. They cannot test the suspicion, because the island explains nothing to them.

The waters around the island are the other half of its defenses. Currents off the Kaliharan coast set across the wind, reverse without warning and run hard over reefs that show nothing on the surface, and experienced sailors call them uncanny. No foreign master attempts the approaches without a Kaliharan pilot, and the pilots are not for hire to anyone the island has not chosen to admit. Terrain, jungle and sea together have kept Kalihara closed to every force it did not invite, and that fact has shaped the island's history as decisively as any act of policy.

## The Wind Out and the Long Way Back

For most of the year the prevailing wind over the western ocean blows from the continents toward Kalihara. A Provènzian master who has made the run eleven times puts it plainly to a new mate: "Out, the ship sails herself, if the wind holds and the weather keeps. Home is where you earn your pay." Outbound from Provènzia's ports, from Okháris, or from inside the Vylarian Sea by way of the western strait, the crossing runs straight out to sea and out of sight of land, and with a fair wind and settled weather it is about a week of downwind sailing.

The weather of that ocean is uncertain at the best of seasons, and a week is the crossing with luck. Ships are blown off their line, lie becalmed or are held back by foul weather, and a ship out of sight of land has no way to tell how far she has come or how far she has strayed. A ship only a little off her line passes the island without sighting it and sails on into an ocean with nothing in it until K'ich'chik. Some turn back while their water lasts; some are not heard of again. Masters bound for Kalihara wait in port for settled weather and a wind from the right quarter before they sail, and nobody sails for the island in Deepwater, when the monsoon brings the storms the island's works are said to turn aside from the interior and nothing turns aside at sea.

Nearing the island, a master watches for what Kalihara shows of itself. The cloud that stands over the Ring for most of the year is seen before the land beneath it, and the peaks themselves stand high enough to be raised far out. Closer in, the sea changes: the currents begin to set across the wind. On dark nights the [[being-rflntrn|Reef Lanterns]] glow over the reef off [[place-telvari|Telvári]], where the pilots keep their boats, and a ship that has found the island heaves to there and waits for one of them.

The return is the same wind in the face, and it is the dangerous half. A master going home has two choices. He can beat eastward against the wind, which is slow and wet, and takes two or three weeks of tacking across a long swell when the weather is kind. Or he can stand north, out of the steady wind, to find the westerly winds that blow toward the coasts of Provènzia, and between the two lies the **Doldrum Sea**: a wide band of dead air, oppressive heat and drifting current where a ship can lie motionless for weeks, her water going foul and her crew sickening, waiting for a wind that may be a month in coming. Becalmed ships drift half-crewed on its currents, and their bones are a recurring sight there. A master who crosses it and finds the westerly wind runs home before it and saves a week; the ones who do not are the one voyage in eight that the Mídhalión shipowner builds into his insurance rates as a fact of the trade. Either way home runs out of sight of land as the way out did, and a ship knows where she is only when she raises a coast her master knows.

So the world is welcome to visit Kalihara, and has good reason to think hard before it does. The asymmetry has kept the island's insularity practical for as long as there has been ocean shipping: it costs a foreigner more to go home than to arrive, and the island has never needed to go anywhere.

## Civilization

The rest of Thalorna knows Kalihara through its [[affiliation-kalihara|ports]] and the accounts of the merchants permitted to trade there, and what those merchants report is a civilization that does not trace itself to its own ancestors. The Kaliharans hold that the [[lore-theithari|Ithári]] ("Those Who Came Before") came to the island in the deepest prehistory, long before the Elder Races reached Thalorna; found scattered human bands living there; took them in; and taught them agriculture, medicine, governance, a vocabulary for all of it, and the understanding of the world as one living whole that everything Kaliharan has been built on since. In 7,300 BF the Ithári departed, abruptly and for reasons the Kaliharans either do not know or do not share.

The Kaliharans kept what they were given, and eighty centuries later they keep it still. That includes the [[affiliation-kalihara#the-ancient-structures|ancient structures]] the Ithári left standing across the interior, some of which continue to do their work in ways the Kaliharans themselves no longer wholly understand, and it includes [[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]] ("The Living Whole"), a tradition with no gods, no prayers and no offerings, in which a person observes the world and tends the part of it that is his. The custodians of both, the **Ithári'kaan**, are the island's institutional heart. The polity note [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] carries the councils, the structures and the ports in detail.

## The Elder Races on Kalihara

Kalihara is the one human civilization on Thalorna with a long relationship to an Elder Race, and the relationship ran the other way from everywhere else. When the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] first arrived on Thalorna, some seventy-five centuries before the founding of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], the Kaliharans were already thousands of years into their civilization, and the Ithári were still in the world. It was the Ithári the Sinalë met, once and by chance, and were politely declined by; the Sinalë came away without learning who they had met, and Kalihara, which was not party to the meeting, has never told them. When the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] followed two thousand years later, the Ithári had gone and the island stood alone. Each Elder Race in its turn asked the island for settlement. Each was received with courtesy, told that Kalihara was complete, and refused, and each was welcome to trade at the ports and did so. The interior was closed to both, and the answer has not been revised since.

Everywhere else on Thalorna the Elder Races held to a policy of non-interference with human development, and uplifted humanity in spite of it, by observation, inference and the occasional unauthorized individual; that argument is made in [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]]. On Kalihara the uplift was deliberate, it was done by someone else, and it is admitted. The Kaliharans did not need Sinalëan wisdom or Khazári craft. They had an older teaching, and they kept it.

## The Ports and the World

The island has never been disconnected from the world; it has decided exactly where the world may touch it. Four ports on the eastern coast and its islets serve as the interface. [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] is the greatest, a city of eighteen thousand with a foreign quarter, merchant palaces and an Aurèldían temple for the traders; [[place-nalurithan|Nalurithan]], [[place-vashuran|Vashurán]] and [[place-telvari|Telvári]] are smaller and more tightly held. Foreign merchants keep to designated quarters under the eyes of the harbor-wardens, goods move in and out, and information flows both ways through a careful filter. Centuries of Ankarian, Tarûzelian and Vedyaran contact have made the ports genuinely cosmopolitan, with mixed populations, foreign shrines and a culture quite distinct from the interior's.

Ankarian contact with Kalihara is commercially significant and culturally thin. A merchant who has traded there for twenty years has usually seen nothing of the island beyond the walls of the foreign quarter, and western scholarship on Kalihara is a patchwork of such accounts and the theories built on them. In the Kaliharan view most of those theories describe Ankarian assumptions, and the Kaliharans see no reason to correct them.
