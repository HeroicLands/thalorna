## Overview

Narivothei is a lineage village in a spring valley on the northern inner slope of [[place-klhrring|The Ring]], where [[place-klhrdpfrst|The Deep Forest]] climbs the foot of the mountains. The Kaliharans call the spring _narivothei_ (clear spring), and the lineage [Narivothei]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} that holds the valley is named for it. The village is a cluster of houses on stilts among tree-ferns, a footpath's length from the spring, and it is the first village of the island whose loss of its work has been counted by name.

## The Holding

The lineage Narivothei holds the valley, the spring and the paths down to the Deep Forest. The spring rises under a work of [[lore-theithari|The Ithari]], one of the tending-stones that stand beside a spring, a fishing ground or a terrace in every lineage village. The lineage tended it as its charge, under the kaan'vaar of the village, for as long as the village has a memory.

## The Failing

Two [Tenth-Year Walk]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}s ago the stone went dark. It stands by the spring as a smooth swelling gone bone-white and hard, and it can be told from across the valley. The spring still runs, but it runs muddier than it did, and after rain it runs brown. The kaan'vaar has performed the [Rite of Last Tending]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} over it as the custodians prescribe, and the stone has not changed. The village keeps its people and has lost its standing: the lineages round it reckon it poorer, and [[affiliation-councilisland|The Council of the Island]] has sent no custodian to answer the petition the Elder sent in the second year.

## What Is Seen from It

The valley is narrow and green, with the white stone at its head where the spring comes out of the rock. From the upper path the canopy of the Deep Forest runs away to the south, and on a clear day the lake shows through a gap in it.

## The Cost of Reaching It

Narivothei lies a long day from the shore of the lake by forest paths that belong to the forest lineages, and a stranger needs a lineage's word to walk them. The village does not turn away a visitor who comes with that word. It does not talk about the stone.
