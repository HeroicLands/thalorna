## Overview

The Deep Forest covers the western and northern reaches of [[place-klhrbowl|the Bowl]] of [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], from the reed-beds of [[place-klhrlake|the lake]] to the inner face of [[place-klhrring|the Ring]]. Nothing in it is cleared: the canopy closes overhead from the shore to the cliffs, and the streams that come down the inner slopes run to the lake under it. The Kaliharans call it _kuathivoel_ (deep forest), and the lineage Kuathivoel, the eldest of the forest lineages, is named for it.

## The Lineages

The forest is held in reaches, each by its own lineage, and the Kuathivoel hold the oldest stand, on the northern shore of the lake; the Maethukoa and others hold the reaches between it and the Ring. The forest lineages live by hunting and gathering, taking from their reaches what the Whole can spare and recording in their sílhari manuals what they take. The forest holds beasts and birds found nowhere else on the island, and no naturalist from abroad has seen them.

## What Is Seen from It

Inside the forest nothing is seen beyond the next trunks, and the light on the floor is green at noon. From the shore where the forest comes down to the water, [[place-klhrmassif|the Massif]] stands across the lake and the cleared land of [[place-klhrestrrc|the Eastern Terraces]] shows on the far side. From the inner face of the Ring the forest is a single green floor reaching to the lake.

## The Cost of Crossing

No road crosses the Deep Forest. The forest lineages keep paths through their own reaches and lead a traveler from one reach to the next, and a stranger off those paths is lost within a day. The crossing from [[place-klhrwstsddl|the Western Saddle]] to the lakeshore takes three or four days on foot. In Deepwater the streams flood the lower forest and the paths along them go under water.
