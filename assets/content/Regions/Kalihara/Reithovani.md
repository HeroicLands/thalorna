## Overview

Reithovani is a fishing village on the eastern coast of [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], a half-day south of [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]], where a beach of black volcanic sand runs between two headlands and the ridgeline comes down almost to the water. The Kaliharans call the sand _reithovani_ (black sand), and the lineage [Reithovani]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} that holds the beach and the reef is named for it. The village lies in a belt of palms behind the beach, its boats drawn up on the sand in the open.

## The Holding

The lineage Reithovani holds the beach, the reef and the spring behind the village. A work of [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]] stands on the reef side of the beach, one of the tending-stones that keep a fishing ground and a spring, and the village has fished the reef under it for as long as it has kept a record. The reef spear is the village's tool, and its catch goes to Ilovanu's coasters.

## The Failing

The fishing-stone has gone bone-white and hard. The reef gives less each season, and the spring behind the village is salt in the dry months. The village has stopped sending its catch to the coasters, and the coasters have stopped calling. Ilovanu has had no word from the village for months. After the stone went dark the kaan'vaar of the village, who holds the stone in her charge, did something that the custodians' procedures do not cover, and the village will not say what. The people who remain are quiet, and the boats do not go out at dawn.

## What Is Seen from It

The black beach and the white stone on the reef, bright against the dark sand. Out to sea the line of breakers shows on the reef.

## The Cost of Reaching It

Reithovani lies along the coast path south of Ilovanu, half a day's walk through palm and mangrove. The village has no quay, and a visitor comes up the sand to houses with their shutters closed.
