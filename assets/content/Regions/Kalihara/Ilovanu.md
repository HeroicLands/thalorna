## Overview

Ilovanu is where the world is allowed to touch [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], and the first thing a factor tells a new captain is where that permission ends: at the gate of the foreign quarter, at the harbor-warden's word, and at the last warehouse on the quay, beyond which the jungle begins and almost no foreigner has ever been invited. Eighteen thousand people live here, on the island's eastern coast, in a city of whitewashed warehouses and carved wooden balconies set between natural breakwaters and a wall of green ridgeline, and it is the seat of the [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] Council as well as the island's principal port.

The harbor is the city's reason. Two natural breakwaters shelter it from the swell, the prevailing wind brings ships to it from the mouth of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]]'s western strait in about a week when the weather is fair, and the currents off the coast bring nothing to it at all without a Kaliharan pilot at the helm. Through it passes the cardamom, the cloves and the colored stones that command extraordinary prices in [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], in [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Tarûzel]] and in the western kingdoms. Three great merchant families, the Kubasari, the Zazurari and the Yakodari, hold the warehouses, and the merchant councils that run the port's affairs meet in the [[affiliation-housefactors|House of Factors]] above the quay; the harbor-wardens who answer to the island's councils decide what those merchants may see, buy and carry. The city's motto, cut above the harbor-master's office, reads "Trade, Honor, and Profit," and the order is the Kaliharan order: the trade is permitted, the honor is required, and the profit is the foreigners' concern.

## The Two Cities

Ilovanu is two cities with one harbor. The **foreign quarter** holds the Vedyaran trading houses, the Tarûzelian captains' lodgings, the gem dealers' arcades, and an Aurèldían temple kept for the foreign population and for the Kaliharans of the port who have taken up that faith; its bazaars are dense with spice vendors and stone-cutters, each stall set to catch a merchant buying in bulk. A foreign merchant lives, trades, worships and dies inside its walls, and a man who has traded here for twenty years has seen nothing else of the island. The Kaliharan city climbs the slope behind it: gardened households, the quays where the island's own coasters land, the warden's halls, and the paths up into the ridges that a foreigner is not shown.

The population of the quarter is as mixed as any port on the Vylarian Sea: native Kaliharan families who have chosen the port over the interior, Vedyaran traders settled for generations, Tarûzelian ship captains, and adventurers from every realm. The harbor pidgin [[skill-valtarlng|Valtári]] is the language of the quay, and its Ilovanu dialect is the one the other ports measure themselves against. A port Kaliharan speaks it, [[skill-kalihrlng|Kalihári]] and usually [[skill-taruzelilng|Tarûzeli]], and is not admitted inland for all three.

## What a Visitor Needs to Know

- **The pilot comes to you.** No foreign hull enters without a Kaliharan pilot, taken aboard off the approaches, and the pilots answer to the Warden of the Approaches, not to the merchant houses.
- **The harbor-warden's inquiry stops everything.** A cargo held for inquiry stays held until the warden is satisfied, whatever the factor paid for it, and nothing moves it faster.
- **The household name is not yours.** A Kaliharan addressed by the short name her own house uses gives the full name back once, evenly, and the conversation turns formal for good.
- **There is no one to bribe for the interior.** The port has no guide inland to sell, and a merchant who asks for one has told the wardens something about himself.
- **Ilovanu has no enemies to be neutral between.** There is one power on the island and never has been another; the city's peace is the island's, and the quarrels of the realms across the water stop at the breakwater.
