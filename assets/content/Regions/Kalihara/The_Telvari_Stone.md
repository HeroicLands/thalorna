## Overview

The Telvari Stone stands on the islet of [[place-telvari|Telvari]], off the eastern approaches to [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], on the side of the islet away from the town and its boat harbor. It is the greatest of the current-stones, the works of the [[lore-theithari|Ithari]] that stand on the reefs and islets round the island, and it is [[lore-itharisul|ithasul]] like every work: one gray body that gives under the hand. The pilots' town is built beside it, and no foreigner is told so.

## What It Does

Near the current-stones the currents set across the wind and run wrong for anyone who does not know them, and the eastern approaches cannot be taken without a Kaliharan pilot. The Telvari Stone stands at the head of those approaches, off which a ship bound for [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] or any other port of the island heaves to and waits for her pilot.

## The Holding

Custodians formed at [[place-thalari|Thal'ari]] tend the stone, as they tend the current-stones of every coast. The pilots who keep their boats on Telvari answer to the Warden of the Approaches. A foreign ship waits for her pilot off the town, with the islet between her and the stone.

## The Failing

The current-stones have run intermittently for a generation, the Telvari Stone with them, and foreign captains have begun to make landfall on the eastern coast without a pilot. At [[place-vaelukoa|Vaelukoa]] a channel that held for a year sets differently in the next, and a pilot of the village lost a ship in a channel she had taken a hundred times.

Nothing in the stone shows it. It is as gray, and gives as much under the hand, as in the oldest account of it, and the Warden of the Approaches tells the Council that the approaches hold.

## What Is Seen from It

The stone looks out on the reefs of the approaches, where the broken water shows the currents setting across the swell. Westward on a clear day the coast of Kalihara lies along the horizon, with the cloud-wrapped crest of [[place-klhrring|the Ring]] standing above it.

## The Cost of Reaching It

Telvari is reached by sea, and the stone is a walk across the islet from the town. No foreigner is taken across, and none has seen the stone.
