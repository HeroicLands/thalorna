## Overview {#overview}

At dusk the swallows come in. They nest by the thousand under the eaves of the island's temples and in the cracks of the low cliffs along its shore, and for the last hour of every day they pour in over the harbor in dark, twisting clouds, so thick that the canoe-men on the beach duck their heads. Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik, the Island of Swallows, takes its name from them. It is an island port of about 30,000 people off the eastern coast of the [[place-neokbaatergn|Ne'ok Ba'ate Region]], one of the greatest city-states of the realm of [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], and it holds a wide archipelago of small islands around it.

## The Harbor

The town faces the mainland across a strait a morning's paddle wide, and its harbor is a long sheltered beach on the strait side with stone landing-stages built out into the shallows. The merchant families of the island keep their storehouses along the beach, each with its own landing, and their trading canoes go out from it along the coast in both directions. The island's trade is not only its own realm's. Ships of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] trade through it as a port that Ne'ok Ba'ate holds, and between Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik and the harbor of [[place-tzokilkul|the Walled City]] (Ts'okilkul) most of K'ankul's sea cargo passes: salt fish, bird feathers, exotic woods and luxury resins coming in, books, instruments and carved jade going out.

## The War Canoes

The island keeps its own war canoes, a small fleet of long dugouts with high prows, each paddled by twenty men and carrying a dozen warriors, and it is the only war fleet in the realm. The canoes patrol the archipelago, escort the merchant canoes along the coast, and answer to the island's ruler and no one else. The other cities of Ne'ok Ba'ate call on them in time of war, and pay for them.

## The Spring

Inland from the town, in a hollow among the trees, a spring rises into a round pool lined with cut stone, and a small temple of [[affiliation-xaqikhanal|Faith of Shaq'ik Ha'nal]], the goddess of fertility and the sacred pools, stands over it. Women come to the island from all over the heartland to bathe in it and ask for children, and the water priestesses who keep it are among the finest physicians on the coast. A pilgrim crosses from the mainland in a canoe the priestesses send, spends a night in the temple's hostel, bathes at dawn and goes home. The island's merchant families give the temple a share of every cargo, and the priestesses' word carries weight in the island's court.

## Who Governs

Ts'ilil Ne'ts'ik is ruled by an Ish'kanmah, a royal lady who holds it in her own right because the royal line of the island passes through its daughters. The present ruler is [[being-ishblnkkush|Ish'bal Ne'ok K'ushi]], a square, weathered woman of fifty-five who paddled in the war canoes as a girl and still goes out with them to inspect the archipelago. Kulmah hold the lesser islands for her, and the heads of the merchant families sit at her court. She sends tribute to the Ts'enmah of [[place-neokbaate2|Ne'ok Ba'ate]] in shell, salt and the services of her canoes, and she keeps a Ch'umts'om, an envoy, at his court.

The island's quarrel is with Ts'okilkul. The two ports compete for every K'ankul cargo, and two of the island's merchant canoes have broken on the reef off Ts'okilkul in the past two years. The island's canoe-masters say the reef has moved.

:::secret {title="The letters in the gourds"}
The war canoes carry more than escorts. Sealed gourds come out to the island from the Ts'enmah of Ne'ok Ba'ate by his own messengers, and the Ish'kanmah's canoes carry them on, unopened, to his envoys and his allies along the coast, outside the Speaking Road. She reads none of them, and she knows what they are. In return the Ts'enmah has promised to ask nothing of her canoes in the new round that she does not offer. The priestesses of the spring have noticed that the war canoes go out to sea on nights when there is nothing to escort.
:::
