## Overview

In the rainy months the great river of [[place-neokbaatergn|Ne'ok Ba'ate Region]] comes down from the highlands the color of brick, carrying the red clay of the volcanic slopes, and for half the year the water below the city is as red as its name. Ne' Ts'umul, Red Water, is a city of about 50,000 people on the bluffs above that river, one of the greatest city-states of [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]], and one of the few in the realm that keeps the full ceremonial life of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]]. It sprawls along the bluffs for most of a morning's walk, temple group after temple group, and between the groups stand the monuments that make it famous.

## The Monuments

The stones of Ne' Ts'umul are the tallest on the continent. Each is a single slab of red sandstone, carved on all four faces with the figure of a Ts'enmah and the count of his reign, and the tallest stands eight times the height of a man. They stand in lines across the great plaza and along the bluff, and the lines are sightlines. Standing at the foot of one and looking past the next, a reckoner sees the point on the far bank where the sun rises at the solstice, or where the bright star [K'inish'ilan]{.name data-type="place/celestial"} first shows before the dawn in its season. The court reckoners of the city keep the stone tables of those risings, and astronomers come from [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] to compare tables with them.

## The Stair of the Late Fire

The fire temple of [[affiliation-kayikchalix|Faith of K'ayik Ch'alish]] stands at the foot of the pyramid of the sun, and its stair carries [[lore-latefire|The Late Fire]], a text of twelve risers recording the Living Flame at which the sun did not reach the mirrors until the afternoon. A scribe of the temple reads it aloud to pilgrims on the day of each Living Flame and to anyone else for a fee, and pilgrims touch the seventh riser, the one that tells of the fire taking, which is worn hollow by their hands. On the summit of the pyramid of the sun burns the fire that does not go out, tended day and night by the fire-priests, and it goes dark only for the darkness of each Living Flame.

Below the bluffs, on planks over the riverbank, lives the river quarter, the households of fishermen and canoe-men who tell their own story of that Living Flame. On the day of every Living Flame someone from the quarter sits on the bottom step of the pyramid of the sun through the darkness, with an empty pot on their knees, and the priests let them. The next Living Flame falls at the winter solstice of 722, and the quarter is already arguing about who will sit.

## The Register of the Shut Temples

Ne' Ts'umul is the seat of the **Warden of the Ruins**, a builder-priest of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|Faith of P'iqal Kul'qat]] who keeps the register of the realm's binding temples: the temples of the storm-serpent that the serpent-keepers of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|Faith of P'uqil Ch'akun]] had built and later shut, in the forests and on the volcano slopes where the cities that kept them lost the road. Each entry records a temple, its seals, the codices lodged in it, its guardian and the guardian's state. The serpent-keepers of the city answer for the seal-stones of those temples, and the jade-dealers of the city's market know more about the serpent-keepers' errands than the serpent-keepers would like.

:::secret {title="The register"}
Most of the entries in the register were last written by a builder who looked into a sealed chamber, saw a still guardian and recorded it as dead. No one has looked again since. Each Warden has copied the last entry forward, and the register now calls dead every guardian of a temple nobody has visited for a round or more. [[place-kulqattzumul|The Red Steps]] (Kul'qat Ts'umul) is one of them, and it is not the only one.
:::

## Who Governs

A Ts'enmah rules Ne' Ts'umul by descent from his father or his brother, with Kulmah over its lesser seats and Ha'nalmah over the towns along the river. The city sends regular tribute to [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]], and its Ts'enmah keeps an envoy at the court there who is answered as if he were the Ts'enmah himself. The city's Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an is the strongest priest in the realm, and the Ts'enmah of Ne' Ts'umul rarely does anything the priesthood would refuse.
