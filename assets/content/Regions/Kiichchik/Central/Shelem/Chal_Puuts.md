## Overview

The wall of the mountain fills half the sky. It goes straight up out of the forest for nearly a thousand meters, bare pink rock streaked black where the water runs, and its top is flat and lost in cloud most mornings. A ribbon of water falls from the top and turns to mist long before it reaches the bottom. Ch'al Pu'uts, Beneath the Mesa, is a town of about 8,000 people at the foot of that _pu'uts_ (table mountain), on the eastern highlands of the [[place-xelemrgn|Shelem Region]], where the river that the waterfall feeds comes out of the forest. It is the last town of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]] before the highland peoples who keep their own leaders, and the place where the strangest goods of the jungle are sold.

## The Herbalists

The town belongs to its herbalists. Each of the old families of Ch'al Pu'uts keeps a household of initiates who know the plants of the forest and the mesa's lower slopes: the plants that ease a fever, stop a bowel or kill the worms of the gut, the plants that dye cloth and poison fish, and the vision-vine. The knowledge passes from master to initiate by oath, and an initiate swears in blood to keep it, so that what a family knows is a family's property. Buyers come to the town from the river towns and from the temples of the north, and they buy the plants in sealed jars at the herbalists' houses and are told nothing about where they grew.

The vision-vine is brewed in the town for the rites of the forest peoples and of Shelem's own priests, who drink it to see what the waking eye cannot. It goes north in sealed jars to the priests of the plateau who buy it, and it is the most valuable thing the town sells by weight after the plants of the mesa.

## What the Mesa Gives

The herbalists' most expensive goods are the plants they say come from the top of the mountain, where, they teach, things grow that grow nowhere else: a small red pitcher-flower whose juice stops bleeding, a gray moss that keeps a wound clean, an orchid that the plateau's nobles wear in their hair at weddings. These sell for their weight in jade, and every herbalist who sells one says that a member of his family climbed for it.

:::secret {title="The cleft"}
None of the herbalists of Ch'al Pu'uts has been to the top of the mountain. Halfway up the north face, hidden behind the fall of the waterfall's mist, a deep cleft opens in the wall, and its floor holds a hanging garden of the mesa's own plants, seeded from above and watered by the falls. One family reaches it by a ladder of lianas it renews each dry season, and sells the plants of the cleft to the others, who sell them on as their own climbing. The top of the mountain is as far beyond that family as it is beyond anyone else.
:::

## Who Governs

Ch'al Pu'uts is a council town, governed by the heads of its herbalist families and its master merchants together with the priests of its one temple. The council chooses a Ts'omeshmah each year, when the town renews its agreements with the river towns and sends its gifts north, and the speaker is nearly always the head of an herbalist family, because the merchants of the town have little to sell that the herbalists do not supply. The town keeps no levy. The highland peoples around it trade with it and owe it nothing.
