## Overview {#overview}

Shelem is loud. Before a canoe coming up the river rounds the last bend it hears the city: a screeching that rises and falls all day, from the macaw yards on the slopes below the temples, where several thousand birds are kept in pens of woven cane. Shelem, the seat of the realm of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]], the Land of Birds, is a city of about 60,000 people on a long ridge of high ground where [[place-hanaltzuma|the Misty River]] (Ha'nal Ts'uma) and [[place-hanalxilik|the Blue River]] (Ha'nal Shilik) meet, in the river-bound jungle on the eastern edge of the Kì'ìch'chik heartland. Where [[place-ixilankul2|Ish'ilankul]] is built on volcanic stone and [[place-kankul2|K'ankul]] on limestone, Shelem is built on water.

## The Ridge and the Flood

The ridge runs for most of a morning's walk between the two rivers, and the city keeps to it. The temple pyramid and the Ts'enmah's palace stand on its highest point, the wards run down its length on either side, and the landings line both shores below. In the dry season the rivers lie low between banks of mud, and the fields of the city's wards lie spread across the flats below the ridge. In the rains the rivers rise over the flats until the city is an island, and the fields are under water. The wards plant again on the silt when it goes down. The flood is the city's calendar as much as the priests' count is, and a household's whole year is set by it.

East of the city, on a clear day, a _pu'uts_ (table mountain) rises sheer out of the canopy, flat-topped and gray, the nearest of the mesas that stand over the jungle like islands.

## The Macaw Yards and the Feather Market

The macaw yards belong to the city's feather-working wards. The keepers raise the birds from the egg, feed them on maize and fruit, and pluck the long red, blue and yellow feathers twice a year, so that a bird gives feathers for twenty years. The feather-workers of the wards sort the plumes by color and length, tie them into bundles and work the best of them into headdresses, fans and cloaks on frames of cane. Macaw plumage and the feathers of hummingbirds are the city's own. Quetzal feathers come in by canoe from the cloud forests, where no one has ever kept the bird alive in a pen, and they are worth more than either.

The feather market fills the landings on the Ha'nal Ts'uma side of the ridge. Buyers from [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] and [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] come here for feathers and for jaguar hides, the mark of military rank and divine favor in every northern court, and a Ts'enmah of the north who commands a steady supply of both can outfit his warriors and honor his priests. Herbalists sell medicinal and visionary plants in sealed jars from the upper towns, and the highland slopes send cacao.

## The River Spirits

The priests of Shelem keep the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]] rites and the count of days, and they also honor the spirits of the rivers. The river dolphins guide canoes through bad water, and a canoe-man who meets one leaves it the first fish of his catch. The anaconda bridges the world of the living and the underworld, and the temple on the ridge keeps a great one in a walled pool at the foot of its stair, fed on the first day of each twenty-day month.

## Who Governs

Most of Shelem's river towns are governed by councils of merchant families and priests, as [[place-tsalukul|Ts'alukul]] and [[place-chalpuuts|Ch'al Pu'uts]] are. The seat is one of the few ruled by a Ts'enmah. He rules by descent, names his heir in a public rite, and holds the palace, the temple and the city's levy. The present one is [Ne' Puq'em Q'emul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a stout, cautious man of forty who has never left the city except by canoe. The heads of the city's merchant families, the P'ik'ulmah, own the canoes, the macaw yards and the trade, and he can do nothing that touches the trade without them.

Each year, when the trade agreements and the gifts with the north are renewed, the Ts'enmah and the P'ik'ulmah sit together with the envoys of Ish'ilankul and K'ankul, and the city's allegiance is bargained over again with its feathers. The [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]] reaches the city by river from the coast, three or four days from Ish'ilankul by way of K'ankul's coastal stations, and goes on upriver to Ts'alukul.

:::secret {title="The cedar chests"}
Every city on the continent wants new feather regalia for the [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]] of 722, and the price of plumes is already rising. The P'ik'ulmah have agreed among themselves, with the Ts'enmah's knowledge, to hold back a third of this year's plucking and the best of the quetzal feathers. The plumes are packed in sealed cedar chests in a storehouse on stilts at the downstream end of the ridge, to be sold in the last months before the fire at three times the present price. The storehouse has a leaking roof that nobody has noticed, and the chests at the bottom are already damp.
:::
