## Overview

The streets of Ishtal'hun glitter. Chips of black glass lie in the dust of every path, swept out of the knappers' yards and ground in by feet, and a newcomer learns on the first day to walk in sandals with thick soles. Ishtal'hun, the Place of Obsidian Blood, is a city of about 45,000 people on the eastern spine of the [[place-ixilankulrgn|Ish'ilankul Region]], built up the slopes of a valley under a long ridge of volcanic glass. It is one of the city-states of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]], and it makes two things that the whole plateau needs: obsidian, and warriors who take their enemies alive.

## The Glass

The ridge above the city is the best source of obsidian on the plateau. Its face is cut back in steps where generations of quarrymen have followed the glass into the hill, and on a sunny afternoon the fresh faces flash like water. The quarrymen bring the glass down in cores the size of a child, and the craft wards of the city work it. Each ward works one thing and pays its tribute in it: blades struck from the core in long, thin strips with a single blow; the ritual knives of the sun's altars; the edges set into the war-clubs of the captive-hunts; and mirrors, ground flat and polished for weeks until they show a face. The mirror-grinders' ward makes the great mirrors with which the Masters of the Living Flame draw the new fire, and every city the white roads reach has a mirror of Ishtal'hun on its pyramid.

## The Taking Field

Below the city, on a level terrace beside the river, lies a walled field of beaten earth, and on most days a hundred men are fighting in it with clubs of weighted wood and coils of rope. It is the taking field, where the captive-warriors of the plateau are made. The houses of youths of Ishtal'hun's wards train their own boys there, and the noble houses of other cities send younger sons to train beside them. The lesson is always the same. A captive is worth more than a corpse, so a warrior learns to disarm a man, throw him and bind him without breaking him.

The field also belongs to [[affiliation-tsumulob|the Blood-Workers]], the warrior-priests who carry the workings of the faith onto the battlefield. Their Takers learn there the set forms of the field workings and the taking of captives alive, and the **Master of Ishtal'hun**, who holds the training ground, judges when a Blood-Sworn is ready to march. A warrior from any city who has taken a captive cleanly may come to Ishtal'hun after the war month and ask him to be tested.

## Who Governs

Ishtal'hun is ruled as every city of the plateau is: by a Ts'enmah chosen from its royal line by the four P'eshalmah and confirmed by its Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an, with a Ch'ikalmah beside him. Its levy is the best trained on the plateau, and its Ts'enmah lends his captains to other Sun Lords for their campaigns, which earns him gifts and favors in every court. The Master of Ishtal'hun is a priest and holds no city office. He takes no orders from the Ts'enmah about the taking field, and the Ts'enmah knows better than to give any.

## The Mirrors for the Fire

The next [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]] falls in two winters, and every city that can afford it has ordered a new mirror for its pyramid. The mirror-grinders' ward has never had so many orders. Its yards are full of grinders working by lamplight, and envoys of distant cities come to watch their own mirror being polished and to make sure that no other city's mirror is ahead of it.

:::secret {title="The gray glass"}
The ward has taken more orders than the black glass of the lower quarry face can fill. Its masters are finishing some of the mirrors from the gray glass of the upper face, which looks the same when rough and takes a lesser polish. A mirror of gray glass throws a weaker spot of sunlight, and it takes much longer to light tinder at dawn than a black one. A Master of the Living Flame who tests his mirror against the noon sun before he accepts it can see the difference. The grinders know which cities' mirrors are gray, and are delivering those to the farthest cities first.
:::
