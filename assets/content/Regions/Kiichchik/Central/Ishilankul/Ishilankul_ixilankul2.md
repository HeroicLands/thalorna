## Overview {#overview}

Ish'ilankul goes dark at night as no other city on the plateau does. No fire burns on its temple summits after sunset. Each pyramid is crowned instead by a small square house of white plaster with narrow slots cut through its walls, and on every clear night a priest sits in each one with his eye to a slot and a tally-cord across his knees. Ish'ilankul, the Seat of the Stars, takes its name from those summit houses. It is a city of about 150,000 people in the central valleys of the [[place-ixilankulrgn|Ish'ilankul Region]], the largest on the plateau, and the seat of the realm of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]]. Its Ts'enmah is the Q'emulmah, the first of the Sun Lords, and the other cities of the plateau send their tribute here.

## The City

Four white roads enter the city, one through each of its four quarters, and each quarter climbs its own slope in terraces of white-plastered houses, ward above ward, every ward with its small temple platform and its house of youths. The roads run straight to the center, where the pyramid of the sun stands over the great plaza. The Q'emulmah's palace fills the plaza's northern side: a long front of red-painted columns on a raised terrace, with the courts of the lord's hall behind it and the treasury and the granaries behind those.

The market fills the plaza every day. A priest of the solar temple sits at each of its four entrances with a basket, and every trader who comes in pays him one part in twenty of what he carries, in kind or in cacao, before he sets out a mat. That is the priesthood's levy on all trade, and no market on the plateau is exempt from it.

## The Court of Tribute

Beside the palace lies a walled court of storehouses around a paved yard, and on the fixed days of the calendar it fills with tribute. Porters come in from the subject towns with bales of cloth and loads of maize, cacao and feathers, and envoys come from the other cities of the plateau with gifts that are tribute in all but name. The Ch'imelbal, the tribute stewards, stand on the steps with their painted rolls and call out each town's due as its porters set their loads down, and scribes tie a knot in a counting-cord for every load accepted. A load that falls short is set aside, and the town's steward is sent for. Jade from [[place-qokulqat|Q'o Kul'qat]] comes on its own day, by its own envoy, and is counted by no one but him.

## The Summit Houses

The summit houses belong to the astronomers of [[affiliation-ixilanchalix|Faith of Ish'ilan Ch'alish]], who keep the city's own sightings of the sun, the moon and the fixed stars. The day-tables by which the city's priests open and close the days come from [[place-kankul2|K'ankul]], computed by [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]], and the watchers check each new table against their own stones before the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an of the city issues it. The realm of Ish'ilankul holds the seat of the priesthood, and every one of its cities works on days reckoned in K'ankul.

On the morning of each new year the summit stairs are crowded with parents. Each brings a child with a head for figures to the steps of a summit house, the watchers set the children a first examination, and the best of them go to K'ankul to begin as Children of the Steps.

## Who Governs

The Ts'enmah of Ish'ilankul is chosen from its royal line by the four P'eshalmah, the lords of its quarters, and confirmed by the city's Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an, as every Sun Lord of the plateau is. The present one is [Nelush Puq'em Ish'bal]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a heavy, soft-spoken man of fifty who leads the tribute-armies himself and has never lost a battle in the field. His Ch'ikalmah, the Lord Within, holds the city when he is away, hears appeals, and keeps the granaries and the treasury.

He is the Q'emulmah because his city holds the respect of [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] (Q'emul Tq'an) and the great temples, and he holds that standing only for as long as the respect lasts. The Q'emul Tq'an sits at [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]], on the same plateau, and the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]] that carries every message in the realm is run by the priesthood from there. No cord reaches the Q'emulmah except through the priests' hands. An ambitious hierarch can engineer the fall of a Ts'enmah who collects too little tribute or loses a battle, and a strong Ts'enmah can try to put a manageable priest in the seat, but both struggles are conducted with ritual formality, because undermining the priesthood risks the anger of the gods.

The court is divided this year over [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]]. The captains of the captive-hunts and two of the four P'eshalmah want K'ankul's standing as a partner of the faith reduced to plain tribute before the next [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]], at the winter solstice of 722, when a new Q'emul Tq'an is chosen. The Ch'eshmah, the elders of the merchant guild, say that a realm which computes every city's days cannot be made to pay for them, and that a quarrel with K'ankul is a quarrel with the tables.

## Trade

Ish'ilankul is the greatest trading city of the heartland. Obsidian from the volcanic slopes goes out from it as ritual blades, mirrors and ceremonial objects. Its merchants process and distribute the cacao of the southern highlands and keep the recipes for the sacred preparations to themselves. Its weavers' wards dye cloth with cochineal and weave it with the seals of the Ts'enmah, and only the nobles of the tributary cities can pay what it costs. The most prestigious good in its market is the quetzal feather, worth more than jade or gold, and the Ch'eshmah keep a steady, limited supply of it flowing from the rainforests of [[place-xelemrgn|Shelem Region]].

:::secret {title="The full count"}
The tribute rolls show every subject town paying in full, and they are false. Three towns in the eastern valleys have been short in maize for two years running after poor rains, and a short count read out in the Court of Tribute would tell the temples that the Q'emulmah's hold is slipping, two years before the conclave. The Ch'ikalmah has the stewards enter those towns' dues as paid and makes up the difference from the city's own granaries at night. The granaries hold far less than the palace believes, and the city's own maize is enough for one bad dry season, not two. Two scribes of the Court of Tribute know, and one of them owes money in [[place-tsamash|Tsa'mash]].
:::
