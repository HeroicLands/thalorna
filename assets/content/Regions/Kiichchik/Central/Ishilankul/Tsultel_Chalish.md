## Overview {#overview}

The Ts'ultel Ch'alish, the Sacred Mountain, is the tallest pyramid on the [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]] and the center of [[place-tzomalchalix|the Sacred Foundation]] (Ts'omal Ch'alish), in the city of [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]]. It stands in nine tiers, each faced in fitted stone and plastered white, with a band of red along the lip of every tier, so that from the plaza it looks striped like a pot. It is about seventy meters high and two hundred and twenty paces along each side of its base. One stair climbs its eastern face, toward the sunrise, in 260 steps, and the priests teach that the nine tiers are the nine layers of the heavens the sun climbs each day.

From the summit rises the breath. Warm, damp and smelling of struck flint, it comes up out of a volcanic vent beneath the pyramid and leaves the summit as a thin white column, which stands straight up on a still morning and leans with the wind on any other. The priesthood teaches that it is the living breath of [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]], the creator.

## The Summit

At the top of the stair stands a roofless sanctuary of four walls. In the middle of its floor is a round mouth, as wide as a man is tall, ringed with a lip of green stone, and the breath comes up out of it. Only the Q'emul Tq'an, the Ch'alish Tq'an of the great temples and the most senior Ch'alishbal climb the stair, and the supreme rites of the [Celestial Recurrence]{.name data-type="lore/theology"} are performed at the mouth. They are performed standing. Pilgrims stand in the court at the foot of the stair, where the breath drifts down on the still days of the dry season, and breathe it.

The priests read the breath as they read the sky. A straight column is a good sign for whatever rite is being held under it. A plume that bends to the south is a bad one, and the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an of Kul'taq'an postpones any rite he can when it does.

:::secret {title="The breath at the mouth"}
The breath is heavier than air. On a still morning in the dry season it spills over the green lip and fills the sanctuary floor to the knee, and sometimes to the waist, before the sun warms it and it lifts away. A person standing breathes clean air above it. A person who kneels or lies down at the mouth breathes nothing else, falls into a sleep within a few breaths and dies within the hour unless someone drags him out by the feet.

The senior Ch'alishbal know this, and it is why the rites at the mouth are held standing. They do not explain it. Every few years a newly raised priest climbs for the first time and goes down on his face at the mouth in devotion, and the priesthood records each such death as the creator taking a priest to himself.

> [!TIP]
> A lamp set on the sanctuary floor burns low and then goes out where the breath lies. Describe the senior priests carrying their lamps on poles at chest height, and let the party see a dropped lamp gutter.

:::

## The Builders

The builder-priests of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|Faith of P'iqal Kul'qat]] keep the Sacred Mountain. Every year, in the dry months before the rains, they replaster one tier from top to bottom, working down from the summit on scaffolds of poles lashed to the stone, so that the whole pyramid is fresh every nine years. The builders say the Ts'ultel Ch'alish is the oldest pyramid in Kì'ìch'chik. The other branches of the priesthood say the same, and nobody counts the years.

At the foot of the stair, in the court, stands the hearth of the precinct. After each [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]], the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an chosen as Q'emul Tq'an lays the coal of his own city's new fire on it, and the others lay theirs on top.

:::secret {title="The mountain inside the mountain"}
The Ts'ultel Ch'alish is a casing. The builder-priests' register counts four older pyramids inside it, each built over the one before, and the innermost is a shrine of three small tiers raised directly over the mouth of the vent, with the shaft for the breath running up through all five. A passage from the third tier of the outer casing, closed by a slab cut with the builder's glyph, leads to the stair of the second pyramid, and from there a crawlway reaches the innermost shrine's summit. The builders open the slab once in every round, on the day after the Living Flame, to inspect the shaft, and they let no one else through it.
:::
