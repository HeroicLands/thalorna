## Overview

The lowlands of the [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul Region]] are flat, and Lishep'ikum makes its own height. Its temple groups stand on a single platform of rubble faced with cut stone, three times the height of a man and broad enough to hold four plazas, and from the top of its tallest pyramid the horizon is a clean line all the way round, with no hill to break it. Lishep'ikum, the Thrice-Built, is a city of about 40,000 people, one of the great city-states of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]]. Its name is a boast about that platform.

## The Platform

The platform has been raised three times. Each time the builders filled the courts of the old platform with rubble, buried its lower buildings and faced the new one over the old, so the lowest temples of the city lie inside it with their roofs still on. The tops of two old pyramids come up through the present platform as low mounds in its plazas, and the priests keep shrines on them. Raised walkways of white plaster join the temple groups across the top, so that a priest can walk from one end of the platform to the other without coming down.

The height is for the reckoners. From the platform's edge they sight the rising and setting points of the sun, the moon and the bright star [K'inish'ilan]{.name data-type="place/celestial"} against the bare horizon, and every building on top is turned to one of those points. The court reckoners of Lishep'ikum keep tables of the moon that the other cities of K'ankul copy.

## The City Below

Most of the city lives below the platform, in house compounds of pole and thatch on low stone footings, spread through the maize plots and the scrub for a morning's walk in every direction. There is no river. Every compound has a cistern cut into the rock, bottle-shaped and plastered inside, which fills in the rains and has to last through the dry months, and the platform's own drains feed a row of great cisterns at its foot that belong to the Ts'enmah. In a bad dry season the Ts'enmah's cisterns are the city's water, and he gives it out by the jar.

:::secret {title="The sweating wall"}
When the builders raised the platform the third time, they buried the drains of the second. The rain that falls on the eastern half of the platform no longer runs out through the cisterns but soaks down into the old fill, and every rainy season the eastern facing sweats water from between its stones for days after a storm. The builder-priests have found a long crack in the facing below the eastern temple group, and it is wider every year. They have told the Ts'enmah that the eastern group is sliding and that it could come down in a hard rainy season. He has told them to say nothing and to find a way to stop it.
:::

## Who Governs

A Ts'enmah rules Lishep'ikum by descent, with Kulmah over the lesser seats in the scrub around it and Ha'nalmah over its towns. His court reckoner keeps the city's stone tables, and every new calculation is announced as the Ts'enmah's own. Lishep'ikum and [[place-nechakalkul|Ne' Ch'akalkul]] are rivals in the reckoning of the sky, and each sends a reckoner to copy the other's newest tables every year.
