## Overview

East of the city the jungle opens onto a flat plain of pale limestone, and the plain is full of holes. There are dozens of them, round and sheer-sided, from a few paces across to the width of a plaza, and at the bottom of each lies still water, blue-green and clear enough to show the fish twenty meters down. Ne' Ch'akalkul, the Great Water Seat, stands along the western edge of that field of sacred pools, the _ch'uqil_. It is a city of about 60,000 people in the [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul Region]], one of the great city-states of [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]], and its pyramids look out over the pools to the eastern horizon.

## The Pools

Every pool in the field has a name and an owner. A few belong to the temples and the rest to priestly families of the city, who hold them by inheritance, keep a stair cut down the side of each, and treat their pool as a being with tastes of its own. One takes only offerings of jade, another refuses blood, and a third has to be spoken to before anyone draws water from it. The families harvest what their pools give: the salts and minerals that crust the rock at the water line, the pearls of stone that grow in the caves below, and the water itself, sealed in jars and sold to pilgrims as an offering. The priests of the city read reflections in the water at dawn, and the death-walkers of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] hold the Calling of the Dead at the largest pool at each dark of the moon, because the priesthood of K'ankul teaches that the pools are mouths of [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]], the underworld.

## The Reckoners

Ne' Ch'akalkul is famous for one star. [K'inish'ilan]{.name data-type="place/celestial"}, the bright star that shows only at the edges of the day, rises out of the plain beyond the pools in its season, and the city's pyramids are set so that a reckoner on the steps of the westernmost can see it clear the horizon above a carved post at the far side of the field. The court reckoners have watched it from that step for so long that their count of its cycle is the most exact on the continent, wrong by less than two days in a hundred years.

The present court reckoner is [[being-neishqemul|Ne' Ish Q'emul]], a scribe of the royal house in her fifties who has spent thirty years on the westernmost steps and walks with a stoop from it. Her tables are carved on a row of stone panels in the gallery at the pyramid's foot, and astronomers come from the other cities of K'ankul and from [[place-netsumul|Ne' Ts'umul]] to copy them, for a fee and a gift.

:::secret {title="The day the tables miss"}
Ne' Ish has found that the day-tables [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]] computed for the next round set the first dawn rising of K'inish'ilan after the [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]] one day late. Her own count is right and theirs is wrong. She has told the Ts'enmah and nobody else. The Ts'enmah means to announce the correction as his own discovery at the Living Flame of 722, in front of every city's envoys, and until then he wants it kept quiet so that the Star House cannot correct its tables first.
:::

## Who Governs

A Ts'enmah rules Ne' Ch'akalkul by descent and names his heir in a public rite while the heir is still a child. Kulmah hold the lesser seats in the jungle around the city, and the court reckoner and the keeper of the stones are both kin of the royal house. Like every city of K'ankul, Ne' Ch'akalkul competes with its neighbors in discoveries rather than in war, and its Ts'enmah measures his reign by the calculations he has announced.
