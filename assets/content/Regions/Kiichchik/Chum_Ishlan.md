# terran_analog: the chasqui runners of the Inka roads, with the shrine-to-shrine carrying of a pilgrim road; the name joins ch'um- (to speak) to ish'lan (a road) and copies no Maya or Quechua word
---

## The Speaking Road

A way-station of the Speaking Road is a stone hut beside the white road with a drum hung at its door, and two young men asleep or awake inside it at every hour. When the drum on the next rise sounds, one of them is on the road before the runner coming in has reached the door. He takes the cord, hears the spoken message once, and is gone. The runner who brought it sits down, drinks, and becomes the one who waits.

The **Ch'um Ish'lan**, the Speaking Road, is the relay that carries the word of [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|the Itsani Hierarchy]] across [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]]. Its way-stations stand about half a day's hard run apart on every great white road, from the plateau of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] to the last shrine at [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]], and along the river routes of [[affiliation-xelem|Shelem]] as far as [[place-tsalukul|Ts'alukul]]. Every cord it carries is knotted _k'otsel_, the cords that the [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]] needs for its count. The Road is two nets on the same roads, one fast and closed and one slow and open, and it quarrels with itself about which of them a message belongs to.

## The Fast Net

The fast net is the runners' road. Its **Ch'umbal**, the Voice Bearers, are ordained priests of [[affiliation-tzenultqan|Faith of Ts'en'ul Tq'an]], and they run in relays by day and night, so that a cord crosses the plateau between dawn and dusk and reaches the edge of the south in seven or eight days. Every cord on it goes to the central dispatch at [[place-kultaqan|Kul'taq'an]] or comes out of it, and on the long routes it passes through the dispatch halls of the great cities on the way.

The fast net carries the hierarchy's business: [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|the Star House]]'s day-tables and the astronomers' observations, the sentences of [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]], the ledgers of [[affiliation-tsumulob|the Blood-Workers]], the Wardens' accounts of the debt, the Dedicators' lists of captives, and the tribute tallies of every city. A Ts'enmah may send by it, but only through his city's Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an, and the priests decide when his cord runs and whether it runs at all.

**Who may read it.** Nobody on the road. The cords of the fast net are tied in the dispatch knots, and only a **Reader of the Cords** in a dispatch hall may untie one. The runner carries a cord he cannot read and a spoken message he has learned by heart, and he repeats the message only to the next runner and to the Reader at the end of the stage. The person a cord is addressed to hears it read aloud by a priest, and no Ts'enmah receives word from a distant province except from a priest's mouth.

**The law of the runner.** No lord may command, delay, inspect or hinder a runner on the Speaking Road, and a lord who does is given to the altar. A runner eats at any temple and sleeps at any shrine, and a house that refuses him water has refused the sun. A runner who drops, sells, opens or delays a cord is **Struck from the Road**, and one who opened a cord of the fast net goes to the altar as well.

## The Slow Net: the Carried Road

The slow net is the road priests'. Every road shrine of [[affiliation-xilanixlan|Faith of Shilan Ish'lan]] takes a message from anyone who pays the keeper, a few beans of cacao for each shrine it must pass. The road priests call it the _Ch'upalel Ish'lan_, the Carried Road, because nobody runs it. A **Road Shrine Keeper** knots the message for a sender who cannot read the cords, which is nearly everyone, or learns it by heart, or ties a sealed bundle to it, and he gives it to the next traveler bound for the next shrine: a pilgrim, a caravan's chaplain, a road priest walking his causeway, or a Trial Runner learning his stages. At the shrine where it is addressed the keeper reads it aloud to whoever comes to ask for it.

A letter on the Carried Road moves as fast as walkers move, and takes a month to go from Kul'taq'an to [[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]] where a cord on the fast net takes two days. Along the coast of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]] it goes by ship, from one shore shrine to the next.

**Who may read it.** Every road priest who touches it. The slow post is knotted in the plain cords that any road priest reads, and the branch's standing rule is that the roads belong to the god and what travels on them belongs to the roads. Each shrine keeper notes what passes through his shrine, and the **Keeper of the Traveler Rolls** gathers it into the branch's rolls with the registers of who traveled where. The rolls are sold, in portions, to kings, merchants and temples. A sealed bundle stays sealed. The cord that says who sent it, to whom and why does not.

## Which Net a Message Belongs To

The sun's branch holds that any message touching the count, a war, a succession or a closed day belongs to the fast net, wherever its sender took it. Its Readers sometimes take a letter off the Carried Road at a shrine near a dispatch hall, read it and send it on by runner, and the road priests call that theft. The road branch holds that whatever a sender pays a shrine to carry is the road's to carry, and that the sun's runners have no business at its shrines.

The quarrel is real because the two nets see different things. A letter that goes by the slow road is one the dispatch at Kul'taq'an did not read first, and lords who would never risk a cord on the fast net about their neighbors send it by the slow one, knowing that the road priests will read it and the sun's priests may not. A dispute over a particular letter goes to the Q'emul Tq'an, who rules on it alone and sets no rule for the next.

## Membership and Standing

Runners come from the road god's trading fairs, where the young men of the cities race, and a **Master of Runners** of the road branch takes those who win and those who finish without being told to. A Trial Runner carries the slow post for a season. If he keeps every message whole, he is ordained into the sun's service and posted to a way-station of the fast net. The road branch trains the runners and posts them, and the sun's branch owns them once they are ordained, and both branches remember it when they quarrel.

Readers of the Cords are scribes of the temple schools. A runner who grows old on the road becomes a Keeper of the Way-Station, and a Keeper may become a Reader only if he learns to read. Station keepers and runners are counted among the priesthood at the rank of an ordained priest, and no lord may touch them. Outside the Road they are young, poor and very fast, and most of them never see the dispatch hall at the end of their stage.

## Rivals

The Ts'enmah of [[affiliation-neokbaate|Ne'ok Ba'ate]] try from time to time to run messengers of their own, and the priesthood breaks each attempt, so that every Sun Lord of the isthmus knows that his cords pass through priests' hands. The merchant families of Shelem's river towns, [[affiliation-riverhouses|the River Houses]], keep canoe-men who are faster on the rivers than any runner, and they pay the Road's relay stations on the rivers, which is where the Road and the families meet. Beyond Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok the Road does not go, and south of it a message travels with whoever is brave enough to carry it, with no mandate and no law to protect him.

## What a Party Can Ask of It

- **Send by the Carried Road.** Anyone may pay a shrine keeper to carry a message to any shrine the roads reach. It is cheap, slow and read by every road priest on the way.
- **Send by the fast net.** Only through a priest. A party in the service of a Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an or of a body of the hierarchy may have a cord run for it; a party without a patron cannot.
- **Ask a station keeper the news.** A keeper knows which roads are cut and which runners came through in a hurry, and he is not forbidden to say so. What the cords said he does not know.
- **Carry a cord beyond the end of the Road.** The dispatch pays travelers bound south of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok to carry its cords to garrisons and expeditions where no runner goes, and a party that carries one comes back owed a favor by the priests of Kul'taq'an.
