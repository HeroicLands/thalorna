## Overview {#overview}

Nine posts stand in a ring on the paving before the south gate of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]], where a party gathers on the morning it leaves for [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]]. Eight of them are cut stone, dressed square and painted red, each a little taller than a man. The ninth is the one the gate counts first, and it is not stone. The priests call it Pek'ash Kulum, the Ninth Stone, and the parties who wait in the court call it the Ninth Post.

It is a hand's breadth taller than the others and rounder: one body with no seam, ribbed in long true curves that run into one another and close at the top in a smooth crown. It is an even gray from foot to crown, with no vein, no grain and no blemish anywhere. There is no straight edge on it and no square corner, and no ornament.

## What It Is Like

The Ninth Post is warm. The shelf the fortress stands on is high and the mornings are cold, and the post is as warm as a hand all year round. It gives a little under a palm, the way well-worked hide gives, and comes back. The eye says polished stone and the hand says something else, and the two never agree. Parties who have touched it once talk about that more than anything else in the court.

Nothing marks it. It has been touched by every traveler who has gone south through the gate for as long as the gate has kept its cord, and it shows no wear, no polish and no stain where the hands go. No chisel has ever cut it, no blow has dented it, and fire does not blacken it. It cannot be shifted or tipped. Nothing is written on it, and there is nothing to read there or copy.

The paving around it tells the opposite story. Every flagstone of the ring is scratched over with names, knots and small petitions, four centuries of them, one over another until the stone is a gray mat of scratches. The scratching runs right up to the post's foot and stops there as if cut off with a knife.

## The Touch

On the opened day, after the gate priest names the gods and the cord-keeper reads the party's name back from the cord, each traveler lays a palm flat on the Ninth Post before the gate opens. The cord-keeper knots the departure against it: he holds the cord to the post while each traveler's knot is tied. A traveler who will not touch it is not knotted, and a party with an unknotted member does not leave that day.

The gate priests teach that the Ninth Post is the gate's ninth keeper, the one who counts the leavers, and that what gives under a traveler's palm is the post taking his count. Most travelers touch it once without trouble. Some, perhaps one in five, take their hand away quickly and will not lay it there again. It does nothing else that anyone has seen.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The forty men.** One of [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] at the gate has demanded that the post be taken up so that its foot can be examined. The attempt was made before witnesses, with forty men, levers and a ramp cut into the paving, and the post did not move by a hair. The Severer says the failure settles nothing. The gate priest says it settles everything. Each wants the party, as outsiders who watched, to give its account to the Ch'alish Tq'an, and each wants it told differently.
- **The hand at night.** One of the Blood-Denied from the camp outside the north wall got into the court after dark and laid her hand on the post. The priests want to know who let her in and what she means to do, and she wants to go south with a party that will not report her.
- **The refusal.** A member of the party's own company, or of the party waiting beside it, touched the post on his first morning and will not touch it again. The opened day has come, and the cord-keeper will not knot him.

# The Truth of the Ninth Post {#truth .secret}

The Ninth Post was on the shelf before the fortress. The oldest course of the court's paving is laid to the curve of its foot, and the cords of the gate's founding speak of the ring of eight being raised around a gray post already standing there.

The post does not end at the paving. When the Severer's men cut their ramp, they dug beside the foot to the depth of two men and found the gray still going down, wider than it is above, with no end in sight. The pit was filled in and the paving relaid.

It is gray and it gives, so it is not dead. Nothing can be established about whether it does anything. A traveler who touches it is not changed by it in any way that can be found, and the travelers who refuse a second touch do no better or worse in the waste than those who do not. The gate priests' teaching is theirs alone, and nothing the post does confirms it or denies it.
