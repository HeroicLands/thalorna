## Overview

The Seven Houses, Nuq'el Ch'alish, are seven buildings strung a few days apart along a line that runs south from [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]] into [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]]. Each is a single long hall of fitted gray stone, roofless or roofed, with one doorway at its northern end and one at its southern, and no other opening. They stand alone in open country with no road between them. Every house has a rule a party must keep while it is inside, and a price the party pays to come out of the far door. A warrior of the southern cities who swears to pass all seven in order has sworn the boldest oath the waste allows.

## The Rules and the Prices

The returners' accounts knotted at the gate give each house its rule and its price, and on these the accounts agree more closely than on anything else in [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]].

## The House of Stillness

The seventh house has no rule anyone can name and nothing in it. It is a long, clean, empty hall, cool and quiet, with light coming in at both doors and a floor of swept stone. The returners' accounts say that a traveler who passes the first six houses walks into the seventh and sits down, because it is the first quiet place in weeks, and that most do not get up. The returners who came out of it say they rested for an hour and came out to find their water gone and their beards grown, and could not say how long it had been.

## What Is Said of Them

The death-walkers of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] hold that the Seven Houses are chambers of [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]], the passage below, standing in the open air, and that a soul which passes all seven living knows the way through the underworld before it dies. They recite the houses' rules to any party that tells them it means to try. The priests of the cities teach nothing about the houses at all, and nobody claims to know who built them.

# The Truth of the Seven Houses {#truth .secret}

The rules and the prices are exactly as the returners give them, and they hold for every party. A party that keeps a house's rule pays its price and comes out of the southern door. A party that breaks the rule pays the price again, doubled, before the far door lets it out.

> [!TIP]
> Each house is a short scene with one rule to keep. Tell the players the rule only if their characters bought a returner's account at the gate or were recited to by a death-walker; otherwise they learn it by breaking it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> In the House of Stillness, each character makes a test of will on sitting down. A failure costs one to six weeks, gone in what feels like an hour, with that much water and food spent. A character who refuses to sit, and walks straight through to the far door, pays nothing.
