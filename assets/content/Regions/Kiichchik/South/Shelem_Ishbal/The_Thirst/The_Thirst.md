## Overview

The Thirst, Ts'ikukal (the place of thirst), is the kindest country of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]], and the returners name it so because thirst is the worst of what it does. It runs south-west from [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]] for months along the western coast: a hot desert of gravel plains, dry lake beds and stony ranges, under a sky that is clear more often than anywhere else in the waste. A party that reaches it from the ash breathes easier for the first time since the gate.

## The Land

The Thirst looks like a desert anyone could cross. Creosote and low gray brush grow in an even scatter over the gravel, tall spiny trees with crooked arms stand alone on the slopes, and the ranges rise bare and pale out of the plain, one behind another. In the hollows lie dry lakes, white and flat as a floor. The days are very hot and the nights cold, and the wind blows every afternoon.

Water is the whole of travel here. The springs lie a week apart or more, and a party walks from one to the next carrying everything it drinks. Some springs run sweet, some run poison, and some are not where the last party found them. The returners keep the springs' places in their cords, and a party that buys a returner's account at the gate buys the springs above everything else in it.

Its landmarks:

- **[[place-farflat|The Far Flat]]**, the greatest of the dry lakes, where a mountain on the far shore never comes nearer.
- **[[place-thebruise|The Bruise]]**, a basin where the sky is purple every hour of the day and night.
- **[[place-playinghouse|The Playing House]]**, a ballcourt of red stone where challengers play for a boon, a life or a name.
- **[[place-walkinggroves|The Walking Groves]]**, groves of hollow trees around springs that come and go.
- **[[place-coldspine|The Cold Spine]]**, a low gray ridge, cold at noon, that the walkers of the plains will not cross.
- **[[place-oldpyramid|The Old Pyramid]]**, a crumbling pyramid among the southern ranges, its inner walls painted with columns of signs nobody in the south can read.
- **The last four of [[place-sevenhouses|the Seven Houses]]**, strung across the northern Thirst.

## Distances

The Thirst is where the waste's distances do their worst. The air is so clear that a range two weeks away looks like a day's walk, and it stays a day's walk while the water runs down. The returners say the clear air is not the whole of it: a party that walks toward a peak for three days sometimes finds the peak no nearer, and a party that turns back finds the spring it left the day before already a week behind. They steer by the sun and the stars, count their steps aloud, and pay out a knotted cord to know how far they have gone.

## The Sky

By day the sky over the Thirst is the white-blue of hot iron. At dusk it goes green for an hour, a clear deep green from the horizon to overhead, and then black, and the returners' accounts speak of nights when no moon rose though the count said one should. Storms stand on the southern horizon without ever arriving.

## Ways to Die

Thirst kills most, and the rest is the desert's ordinary cruelty at a greater scale: sun that blisters, nights cold enough to kill a sleeper who lies down wet, flash floods that fill a dry wash from a storm nobody saw. The scorpion-things are fewer here than in the ash, and bigger. The massive reptilian things, neither snake nor lizard, lie under the gravel in the heat of the day and come up at night. Things are seen at a distance on the plains that walk like men and are not men, and the returners say not to signal to them and not to wait for them.

## What the Returners Say

The death-walkers of the gate hold that the things walking on the plains are souls of the waste's dead, lost below and still trying to finish the crossing. The returners do not argue the point. They say only that the walkers do not come nearer if a party keeps walking, and come nearer if it stops.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Thirst quarrels over its springs. A party crosses it from spring to spring, the springs lie a week apart, and their places are kept in the returners' cords. The gate of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]] holds the accounts it knotted when each returner came back, and shares them only with the worthy and the desperate. The returners hold that what they found is theirs to sell, and sell their springs to parties in the gate's court for whatever a party will pay. Some springs run sweet, some poison and some are not where the last party found them, so every account sold is a quarrel waiting for the party that bought it to come back. The death-walkers of the gate quarrel with the returners as well: the death-walkers hold that the walkers on the plains are souls owed a recital, and the returners hold that a party owes them nothing but to keep moving.

## Hooks

- **The gate's own account.** The cord-keeper of Shelem Ch'alish has learned that a returner is selling, in the court, the account of the springs that the gate knotted from him on his return, and holds that the account is the gate's. He asks a party to buy it from the returner and bring it to him, and the returner wants that party to take him south again instead.
- **A spring that moved.** A party bought a returner's springs, went south, and came back with half its members, saying that the second spring was not there. Its captain wants the returner who sold them, and the returner wants a party to go back with him, find the spring and clear his name.
- **A recital on the plain.** A death-walker of the gate has vowed to recite the chambers below to the walkers on the plains, and wants a party to take her to them and wait while she does. The returners say that the walkers come nearer to a party that stops.
