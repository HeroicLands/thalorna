## Overview

The Far Flat, Nalum Sh'aqal, is a dry lake in the northern part of [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]], a floor of white salt so flat and so bright that a traveler on it walks with his eyes half shut. On its southern shore stands a single dark mountain, and from the northern shore it looks a day's walk away. Most parties that cross the Thirst cross the Flat, because going around it adds a fortnight and two springs that may not be there.

## Crossing

The salt is hard and easy walking, and that is the trap. A party sets out toward the mountain at dawn and walks all day, and at dusk the mountain is no nearer. It walks the next day and the next, and the mountain stays where it was, and the northern shore behind it is gone from sight. The returners' accounts say the crossing takes two days or ten, and nobody agrees which.

The returners have three rules for it, and they knot all three into the account a party buys at the gate:

- **Do not look at the mountain.** Walk with the eyes on the salt and the sun on the left shoulder in the morning.
- **Count.** Pay out a knotted cord, count every step aloud, and trust the count over the eyes.
- **Turn back on the third night.** A party that has not reached the far shore by the third dusk has as much water as it will need to walk home, and no more.

The salt is littered with the gear of parties that kept walking: water jars, a litter, a dead man sitting up with his eyes open, all of it bleached white.

# The Truth of the Far Flat {#truth .secret}

The Flat is two days across, measured in steps, and it is always two days for a party that keeps to the returners' rules. For a party that walks toward the mountain, watching it, the crossing lasts as long as it keeps watching: every day spent with eyes on the mountain adds a day of salt ahead. Nothing on the Flat marks where this begins or ends.

> [!TIP]
> Ask the players each morning on the Flat where their characters are looking. A party that counts steps and keeps its eyes down arrives on the second evening. A party that watches the mountain arrives one day later for each day it watched, and the GM tracks the water.
