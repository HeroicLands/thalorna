## Overview {#overview}

A week into [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]], on the gravel plains below the first of the stony ranges, a low ridge runs across the desert from north-east to south-west. It is no higher than a man's knee in most places and his waist in a few, and it is not rock. It is a row of long gray ribs, each running into the next with no seam, like the back of something lying down in the gravel. The returners call it the Cold Spine, and the priests' tongue has it as Ch'upum Ha'kanal, the back of the cold.

The ridge shows above the gravel in stretches of a few hundred paces to a day's walk, and between them it runs under the plain, with nothing on the surface to say where it goes. The longest stretch that shows is the one the returners know.

## What It Is Like

Everything in the Thirst is hot at noon, and the Cold Spine is cold: as cold as well water to the hand under the white sky, and no warmer at the end of the day. It is a smooth, even gray, and it gives a little under the palm, and comes back. No blade has marked it and nothing has chipped it. The returners have scratched their names and their counts of days on the stones of the plain beside it for as long as there have been returners, and the scratching stops at the gray.

## The Camp

The things that walk the plains of the Thirst, the shapes that walk like men and are not men, do not cross the Cold Spine. They come up to it on its north-west side and stand there, and on its south-east side a party can sleep. Every account knotted at the gate that reaches this far tells a party to make its camp against the south-east face of the longest stretch, as close to the gray as it can lie, and the returners' cords count the nights there without saying why.

For a generation that stretch has been growing shorter. The walkers step over the gray now near both its ends, where they never crossed before, and a camp that once lay safely at its southern end has moved twice toward the middle. The ridge itself looks exactly as it did.

## What the Awakeners Hold

[[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] hold that the Cold Spine is the back of [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]], the storm-serpent, lying asleep under the waste, and that the walkers keep away from it out of fear of what it will be when it wakes. They take its shortening as the serpent stirring. A few of their devoted go south through the gate each year to sleep one night with their backs against the gray, and come home, if they come home, to be honored in their cells.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The resurvey.** The returners' accounts at the gate give the length of the safe stretch, and the last two parties to come back say the accounts are wrong. The gate priests want the stretch paced and knotted afresh by a party that will come back with the cord.
- **The pilgrim.** An Awakener of the jungle cells asks to join a party bound for the Thirst, and says only that she means to sleep one night at the Cold Spine. The gate's priests do not know what she is, and a party that does has to decide whether to tell them.
- **The gap.** At the southern end of the stretch a party finds the walkers standing on the near side of a place where the ridge runs under the gravel, between it and its camp.

# The Truth of the Cold Spine {#truth .secret}

The Cold Spine is working badly. It is gray and gives under the hand along every stretch that shows, so it is not dead, but the stretch the walkers will not cross is shorter each season, with no change anywhere on the ridge to show for it.

What can be established is only what it does: the walkers of the plains do not cross the middle of the longest stretch, they cross its ends more freely each season, and the cold of the gray does not change with the sun. Where the ridge runs under the gravel, and whether the stretches that show belong to one body, cannot be established without digging that nobody in the Thirst can do. The Awakeners' teaching is theirs, and nothing at the ridge confirms or denies it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The shortening is a clock with no warning in it. A party that camps where the cords say finds the walkers at the camp's edge by midnight. A party that camps in the middle of the longest stretch sleeps safely, this season.
