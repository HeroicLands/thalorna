## Overview

The Old Pyramid, Kul'qat Sh'amik, stands on a gravel plain between two bare ranges in the south of [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]], a week's walk from the nearest spring the returners' cords record. From a day away it looks like one more pale hill. Closer, it is a pyramid of four low tiers of yellow sandstone, so worn that its corners have fallen into slopes of rubble and its faces are scoured into honeycomb by the wind. Of the temple on its top only two door-jambs stand. In a waste full of old things, the returners call this one the old one.

## The Pyramid

The blocks of the tiers are small, no bigger than a man can lift, and laid in courses with a mortar that has turned to sand and run out of the joints. Every block that has kept its place is cracked, and the cracks are full of blown grit. The stair that climbed the east face is a ramp of loose stone. Nothing is carved on the outside, or nothing the wind has left.

A doorway at ground level on the west face, half buried in rubble, opens on a passage that slopes down into the pyramid. Its lintel is a single slab, broken across and still holding. The passage is long enough that the light from the door is gone before it ends, and it ends in a square chamber with a low stone table against its far wall and nothing on the table but dust.

## The Walls

The walls of the passage and the chamber, and the chamber's ceiling, are plastered and painted from end to end. The painting is in red and black and a faded blue, in columns as narrow as a hand, and each column is a stack of small signs, one above another, from the ceiling to the floor. The signs are pictures: men and women sitting, birds, reeds, a hand, a sickle, a scoop, a net-float, and boats, many boats, rafts and laden rafts and boats with poles and sails, painted in a pyramid weeks from any river. They are not like the glyphs of the Itsani cities, and the scribes of the south who have seen them cannot read them.

Every column that can still be followed is built the same way. Near its top is a small seated figure, a man or a woman, a little below that a sign drawn inside an oval of knotted cord, and below the oval a pair of signs that ends the column. The returners say every column in the pyramid has its sitter, so that the walls are covered with people sitting down.

The plaster is failing. Wherever it has cracked it has come away in flakes, and the floor of the passage is a drift of painted flakes with signs on them. The lower walls of the passage are bare to the stone for long stretches, and in the chamber whole columns hang by a corner. A flake breaks at a touch.

## Dangers

The pyramid is coming down. The roof slabs of the passage have slipped against each other, and in two places the passage narrows to a crawl under a slab resting on rubble. The rubble slopes on the faces slide underfoot, and the doorway fills a little more after every windstorm. Inside, the air is still, hot and full of plaster dust, and a party that works in the chamber for an hour comes out coughing.

The pyramid has no water. The spring a week to the north is one of the Thirst's sweet ones in the returners' cords, and a party that does not find it there walks back to the last one it found.

## What the Returners Say

The returners bring back flakes from the passage floor and copies of the columns on bark paper, and the gate's storehouses buy both and send them north. Some returners scrape a whole sitter from the wall and carry it sewn into a cloak, as a charm against losing the road. Others say that carrying one off is how a party loses the road.

They also say to read nothing aloud in the chamber, to touch the walls as little as possible, and to leave by daylight, because the rubble at the doorway is the only way out and it moves.

# The Truth of the Old Pyramid {#truth .secret}

The signs that can be read are [[skill-khelthzscrpt|Khelathi-zethu Script]], the sacred writing of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|the Empire of Au'Khelathu]], written by the rules its scribes follow. Each column is a name in the style of a Khelathi tomb, where the _reth_, the written name for which [[lore-rethzethu|Reth'zethu]] is named, is set on every surface: a given name spelled in the sound signs with its vowels left out, closed by the seated man or the seated woman, and followed by a short formula of death. Two columns from the chamber, read in the [[doc-khelzethuenc|Khelathi Zethu Encoding]]:

:::

_[Zobagulu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a man, went to the West in the five days. His name endures. [Lenuret]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a woman, went to the West in the five days. Her name endures._

The sign in the oval of cord is the sign of the five days that belong to no month, the days that [[lore-khelathclndr|the Khelathi Calendar]] and [[lore-itsaniclndr|the Itsani Calendar]] both keep at the year's end. In Au'Khelathu the oval holds a Gar-Au's name and nothing else, and no Khelathi wall sets the five days inside it.

## What a Party Can Establish

**The names are Khelathi.** Zobagulu and Lenuret, and every other name a reader can spell out, are given names still given in Au'Khelathu. None of them carries a house or a place, and no column gives a regnal year, a god or a reign. Nothing in the readable signs dates them or ties them to anyone a Khelathi record mentions.

**The forms are older.** Each readable sign is drawn in one form only, plainer and squarer than the plainest of the variants the schools of [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]] teach, and the seated figures sit straighter than any on a wall in Au'Khelathu. A reader trained in the schools can set them beside every form he has been taught and finds them older than all of them, older than any inscription in Au'Khelathu. The plaster, the paint and the stone say nothing about age. The Thirst keeps paint and cord for ages and grinds stone down with its wind, and nothing near the pyramid gives a measure to compare it with.

**Some of the signs are not quite Zethu.** Among the readable signs stand others that a Khelathi reader almost knows. One is a seated figure like the sign of a man, with its face turned back over its shoulder toward the end of the column, though every sign that faces a direction faces the start of its line. Another is a boat like the laden raft with a second load stacked on the first. A third is a square of strokes set like a count, with a stroke too many for any number the schools write. Each stands where a sign of the formula would stand, and no reader can say what it adds. They are drawn in the same hand and the same paint as the readable signs around them.

**Where the writing came from is not something a party can establish.** As far as anyone knows, the Khelathi have never had contact with this continent, and the Khelathi are surer of it than anyone. No record in Au'Khelathu tells of a voyage here, and the [[affiliation-gewaatis|Faith of Gewa'atis]], which keeps the empire's knowledge of every route, keeps the river pilots and the caravan roads. A Khelathi reader reads part of the walls and cannot read the rest, and cannot say whether the writing came from the Khelathi or went to them. Whether these forms are the oldest Zethu, carried out of the river country before any wall there was written, or the forms Zethu itself came from, nothing in the pyramid decides.

> [!TIP]
> Reading a column takes both [[skill-khelthzscrpt|Khelathi-zethu]] and [[skill-khelathlng|Khelathi Language]], in its classical form. A character who has both reads the names and the formula with an ordinary test, and dates the forms as older than anything in the schools on a good one. No result reads the near-signs.

In Khelathi belief the reth is part of the soul, and a name struck from every surface is a person unmade. To a Khelathi character the walls are people, the flakes on the passage floor are pieces of people, and a sitter scraped off and sewn into a cloak is a dead man carried away from his name.

## Three Explanations

Three bodies of the continent hold an account of the walls, from copies and flakes that come north through the gate. None of the three has read a sign, and nothing in the pyramid confirms any of them.

**[[affiliation-ishilanshulum|The Star House]]** holds that the pyramid is the continent's own. Its reckoners have counted the signs on the copies the gate sends north and find the oval of cord at the same place in every column, as a day-sign stands in a table. They read the walls as a count kept by people who knew the five doors at the year's end as the Itsani know them, and they hold the writing older than the glyphs of the cities and native to this land.

**[[affiliation-shaqalob|The Severers]]** hold that the walls are a working, not a record. In their account the columns were painted on a closed day, outside the tables, to keep the dead they stand for from passing on, and whoever painted them worked outside the Ten. The order forbids any priest to copy a column or to speak a working inside the pyramid, and its Inspectors at [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]] break every painted flake they find in a returning band's packs.

**The death-walkers of [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]]** hold that the sitters are dead strangers. In their account people from beyond the edge of the known world came down into the waste, died there and lost their road through [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]], and their own kin painted the columns to call them home. The seated figures are waiting to be called. A death-walker at the gate recites over a painted flake that comes back through it as over a body.
