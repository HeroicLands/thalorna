## Overview

The Playing House, Ts'ulam Ch'alish, is a ballcourt in [[place-thethirst|the Thirst]], larger than the great court of any city in [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]]. Two long walls of red stone face each other across a sunken alley of packed earth, each wall sloping to a high bench, with a stone ring set high in the middle of each. The court stands alone on a gravel plain with nothing built near it, and no sand lies in the alley, though the wind blows across it every afternoon. Nobody sweeps it.

## The Players

At dusk the players come out onto the court. The returners' accounts describe them alike: tall, a head taller than a tall man, in the padded belts and hip-guards of the game, their faces painted or masked so that no one has seen them plainly. They do not speak a word of any tongue. They wait at the far end of the alley until someone steps down onto the earth at the near end, and then they play.

The southern cities call them the lords of the threshold, and no priest gives them any other name. [[affiliation-itsanihierarchy|The Itsani Hierarchy]] keeps an account of the first pair of challengers who played them and won, and of what the pair gave up to win, in the Sealed Chambers, and does not teach it.

## The Game

The game in the Playing House is the game of the cities' own courts, played with a heavy ball of black rubber kept off the ground with the hips, and with the same honor in it. A challenger states his stake aloud before the first throw, and there are three:

- **A boon.** A thing the challenger wants, named in a sentence. Returners who have won a boon come home with it, and do not say how.
- **A life.** The life of someone who is dying, or has just died, named by the challenger.
- **A name.** The challenger's own name, against the players' knowledge of it.

A challenger who loses pays his stake from his own side: a boon lost costs a thing of the same weight, a life lost costs his own, and a name lost is gone. In the southern cities a returner who lost his name at the Playing House is known only by his gate-name, and his family speaks of him as though he had died.

# The Truth of the Playing House {#truth .secret}

The players play fair and play well, and they hold to the stakes exactly. They never leave the court and never come onto it before dusk or after dawn. A party that camps beside the court is not troubled. The game goes to seven points, and a match lasts most of a night.

> [!TIP]
> Run the game as a contest of skill over several rounds, with the stake named before the first throw. A challenger with no skill in the ballgame can still win, but each point costs him blood and bruises: the ball is heavy, and the players do not soften a throw for a novice.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> A name lost at the Playing House is gone from everyone who knew it. The challenger's companions find they cannot say it, cannot recall it, and cannot read it on any cord. Only his gate-name, given after his return, sticks to him.
