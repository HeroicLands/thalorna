## Overview {#overview}

Mirror Mountain, Ha'kin Ts'ultel, is a low hill in [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]] covered in standing slabs of obsidian and mica, each taller than a pyramid's tier and polished on both faces like a mirror. They stand close together in no order, leaning a little, so that the ways between them twist and close, and from a distance the hill flashes in the sun like water. It lies a week south of [[place-ashstair|the Ash Stair]], and for a warrior of the southern cities it is the third and greatest of the oaths most men will swear.

## Walking the Mirrors

A traveler who walks in among the slabs sees himself in every face, and then, the returners say, sees himself dying. In one slab he drowns, in the next he burns, in the next he falls to a blade in the hand of a man he has not yet met, and every slab shows another death, in another place, at another age. The returners say they are all true: every death a man might die is there, and he walks among them until he chooses one.

The choosing is the whole of it. A warrior who has chosen walks out on the far side of the hill, and the slabs show him nothing more. In the southern cities such a warrior is held to be proof against every death but the one he chose, and he fights that way. Many warriors who come back from Mirror Mountain wear a shard of its glass at the throat. They do not tell what they chose, and it is the worst of manners to ask.

## Those Who Do Not Choose

A traveler who cannot choose does not come out. Parties wait at the foot of the hill for a companion for days, and hear him moving among the slabs and calling, and do not go in after him, because a man who goes in to fetch someone faces his own deaths too. The returners' accounts at the gate speak of voices on the hill that answer to the names of men who went in seasons ago.

# The Truth of Mirror Mountain {#truth .secret}

What the slabs show is what the returners say they show. Whether the choice binds anything is not established by anything a party can test: a warrior who chose death by drowning has never been seen to survive a blade that should have killed him, and has never been seen to die of one either, because the men who believe it fight like men who cannot be killed and are usually the last on the field.

> [!TIP]
> Let each player describe the death their character chooses, and keep it. A character who has chosen fights without fear, and the GM may grant a bonus to morale and to tests of nerve for the rest of that character's life. Never confirm or deny that the chosen death is the only one: the belief is what changes how the character plays.

> [!WARNING]
> A character who refuses to choose is lost among the slabs. Companions can bring him out only by walking in themselves, blindfolded, by the sound of his voice, and each rescuer who opens his eyes faces the choice himself.
