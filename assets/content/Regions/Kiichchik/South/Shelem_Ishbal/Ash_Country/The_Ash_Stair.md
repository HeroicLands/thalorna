## Overview {#overview}

The Ash Stair, Pu'talal K'aipuk (the stair of ash), is the first day in [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]]. Below the place where the guides of [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]] turn back, the high ground of the threshold breaks off in a long slope of gray ash terraces, each a few spans deep and a few hundred paces wide, falling a full day's walk to the floor of [[place-ashcountry|the Ash Country]]. From the top a party sees the whole of its first week laid out below it: the plain, the cones, the steam of [[place-boilingground|the Boiling Ground]], and the mountains standing pale to the west.

## The Descent

The terraces are soft. Each one gives a little under the foot and slides a little toward the next, and a party that tries to hurry starts a slide that carries the ash, and the party, down three or four terraces at once. The returners' advice is to go down slowly, in a single line, with each traveler stepping into the prints of the one ahead. A party that leaves the gate at first light reaches the foot of the Stair at dusk, if the wind holds.

The wind seldom holds. An ash-storm can rise off the plain below in the time it takes to eat, and the Stair catches the worst of it, because the wind climbs the slope and drops its ash on every terrace. A party caught on the Stair in a storm has two choices: to go on down blind, or to climb back up blind.

## The Standing Dead

The Stair is full of the dead. They stand in the ash on the lower terraces, upright, some roped together in lines, with the ash to their waists or their shoulders or over their heads, and the wind uncovers new ones every season and buries others. They are the travelers who stopped to wait out a storm on the Stair and were buried standing. Nobody digs them out. The returners say a party should walk past them without looking at their faces, and that the ones whose eyes are open are the ones to hurry past.

## Where Most Turn Back

More parties turn back on the Stair than anywhere else in the waste. A party sees the country below it, or loses its first member to a slide or a storm, and climbs back to the gate within the week. The cord-keeper knots its members home like any other returners, and the gate gives them their gate-names. In the southern cities everyone knows which gate-names were earned on the Stair, and a lord who swore his oath to its foot is not much honored at a rite.

# The Truth of the Ash Stair {#truth .secret}

The standing dead are only dead. Nothing on the Stair walks, and the open eyes are ash-glazed and blind. The returners' warning is about a sight that breaks a party's nerve, and it is a good warning: more parties turn back after a long look at the dead than after any storm.

> [!TIP]
> Run the descent as a test of patience. A party that goes slowly reaches the foot at dusk. A party that hurries starts a slide: each traveler makes a test of agility or is carried down several terraces and half buried, losing gear and taking bruises. An ash-storm on the Stair lasts from one to six hours, and a party that stops in it is buried to the waist within the hour and to the head in three.
