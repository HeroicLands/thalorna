## Overview

Every forge in Ne' Ki'ahkul is lit from the fire on the temple of [[lore-kayikchalixdty|K'ayik Ch'alish]], and a smith whose forge goes out does not relight it from a neighbor's. He walks up the temple stair with a covered jar, pays the fire-keepers, and carries the coal down again. Ne' Ki'ahkul is the forge town of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]]: about 12,000 people on the steep flank of a mountain that smokes, where the heat of the ground warms the workshops and the valleys below run with black glass. Its smiths make the steel blades and breastplates that the warriors of the south carry, and its stoneworkers cut the obsidian that the priests of every city use.

## The Mountain and the Town

The town is built in steps up the mountainside, its houses and workshops of dark local stone, set on foundations of the same rock. Steam rises from cracks in some of the higher streets, and the smiths of the upper terraces build their workshops over them for the warmth. The fire-keepers read the mountain: they watch the color of its smoke from the temple platform each dawn and announce what they see, and a dark plume puts the whole town on edge for the day.

Below the town, in the narrow valleys to the east, obsidian lies in flows like dark glass. The stoneworkers' kin groups quarry it in their labor turn, and blocks of it come up to the town on porters' backs.

## The Smith Kin

The smiths, the _kulishbal_, are organized by kin group, and each kin group owns its forges and teaches its own trade to its own children. A kin group that makes blades makes nothing else, and one that works gold for the temples does not touch iron. The secrets of the town's steel, _kul'iqal_, belong to six kin groups, who marry mostly among themselves and do not teach anyone outside them. The largest of the six is led by [[being-toshelneok|Toshel Ne'ok]], a small, scarred woman with no patience for visitors, whose kin group makes the breastplates of the realm's best warriors.

Rank in Ne' Ki'ahkul follows the forge. A master smith is honored above most highborn in the street, and an apprentice who spoils a blade is set back a year. The town is inward and plain-spoken, and it has less use for the market than [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]] downriver. The trains that carry its work to market are loaded by the town's own porters and taken down to the junction, and buyers from outside rarely climb to the forges themselves.

## Who Governs

Ne' Ki'ahkul has the realm's two halves, upper and lower, and the smiths' kin groups are split between them. The upper half holds the gold and silver workers and the obsidian cutters, who work mostly for the temples. The lower half holds the iron and steel smiths, who work mostly for the Ts'enmah. Over both stands a hereditary lord of four hundred households, a Pel'ushmah, whose house holds the office by inheritance and who answers to the Ts'enmah for its count and its labor.

The town's labor turn is paid in metal and stone, not in field work. Each kin group owes a set weight of finished steel or a set number of blades each year, and the inspectors who come up from the capital weigh it against the cords. The temples take the finest work as tribute: ceremonial vessels and masks for the regalia, and the ritual blades of obsidian, sharp enough to cut bone, that every temple of the faith uses at its altars.

## Mirrors

The obsidian cutters of Ne' Ki'ahkul make mirrors as well as blades. A good one is polished until a face in it is clearer than a face in still water, and the warriors of the south buy them, saying a mirror from Ne' Ki'ahkul shows a man his true face. The priests of [[place-ixkulchalix|Ishkul Ch'alish]] buy them by the dozen to set along the causeways of the sacred lake at dusk.
