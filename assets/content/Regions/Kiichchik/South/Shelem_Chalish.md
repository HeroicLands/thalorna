## Overview

The night before a party leaves, a death-walker sits with each of its members in a stone cell under the gate, and recites to the living what is recited to the dead: every chamber of [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]] in the order the soul will meet it, and every deception in the order it will be offered. Past the gate there is no road shrine and no priest to do it later. Shelem Ch'alish, the Holy Bird, is the last fortress-temple on the [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]] and the gate through which every party bound for the wastes of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]] goes out. About 900 people live in it, and in a good season as many again pass through, each of them waiting, paying and praying.

## The Gate

Shelem Ch'alish stands on a shelf of high ground at the southern edge of the [[place-qapteltzaklrgn|Q'aptel Tsa'kul Region]], with the mineral flats of the threshold behind it and the waste in front. Its walls close a single long court. At the north end is the gate a party comes in by, at the south end the gate it goes out by, and between them the pyramid-temple, the wells, the storehouses and the cells where travelers sleep. Watchtowers stand on the south wall, and from them the southern sky shows its colors: a sickly yellow by day, a bruised purple when a storm stands on the horizon, and on some nights a light that comes from no moon.

The fortress belongs to [[affiliation-qapteltzakl|Q'aptel Tsa'kul]] and is governed from its seat, [[place-qapteltzakl2|Q'aptel Tsa'kul]], whose Q'aptelmah sets the fee for every party that passes south. The gate's own high priest, the Ch'alish Tq'an of the fortress-temple, rules the court in his name. The present one is [[being-tsultelne|Ts'ultel Ne']], a lean, soft-spoken man who has never been through the south gate himself and says so to every captain who asks.

## The Working Day

The court wakes before dawn, when the gate priest climbs the pyramid and reads the day from the last table to reach the fortress. [[affiliation-ishilanshulum|The Star House]] computes no table for the gate itself. A hired runner brings a copy south from [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]] each season, and at the gate most of its days are marked as answers the house cannot vouch for. A party may leave only on a day the priests call opened to [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] or [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]], and such a day can be weeks away. So parties wait, and waiting is the fortress's trade.

While it waits, a party pays for everything. The **cord-keeper** knots its name, its members and its fee into the gate's _k'otsel_ on the day it arrives. The **keepers of the storehouses** sell it dried meat and tubers that keep for months, the miasma cloths that a traveler binds over his mouth on the flats, water jars, and amulets consecrated on the pyramid. The court charges for a cell, for water from the deep wells, and for a place at the evening rites. Blood is offered on the altars every dawn for the parties in the court, and each party pays for its share.

On the opened day the party is called to the south gate at first light. The gate priest names the gods, the cord-keeper reads its name back from the cord, and the gate opens for it alone. The **guides** of the fortress walk it across the last of the flats, by the paths that avoid the sinkholes and the boiling pools, and leave it at the place where the gate's towers drop below the horizon. They go no farther, and they come back the same day.

## The Cord at the Gate

The gate's _k'otsel_ is the oldest record the fortress keeps. Every party that leaves has its knot, and every member who comes back through the south gate has a second knot tied beside the first. The cord-keeper does not count the knots aloud, and the gate does not publish them.

A traveler who comes back is questioned at the gate before he is fed. The gate priests knot what he says into a cord of its own: where he went, what he saw, what killed his companions. These are the accounts the priests of Shelem Ch'alish are said to keep, and they share them only with the worthy and the desperate. Then the returner is given a fourth name, his _tq'anil_, the gate-name, which the priests choose and knot into the cord beside his second knot. In the southern cities a lord who bears a tq'anil stands ahead of others at every rite, and a returner is commonly raised to high office or kept at the edge of things, and seldom anywhere between.

## Who Lives Here

The permanent people of the fortress are its priests, who serve Ts'uqil Ish'bal and P'uqil Ch'akun, the bound servants who keep the cells and the kitchens, and the realm's kin groups, who live inside the walls and work the deep wells in their labor turn. The guides are their own families, who pass the paths from parent to child and marry among themselves. Everyone else is waiting to leave: warriors under oath, priests who have vowed to see the south, treasure-seekers, men and women under a doom, and bands of [[affiliation-tsumulob|the Blood-Workers]] on their way out of the garrison to the north.

Outside the north wall is a second, poorer camp. It belongs to the **Blood-Denied**, whom [[affiliation-shaqalob|the Severers]] have barred from offering blood, from the calendar and from the funerary rites. Nobody at the gate reads their rank, and nobody recites for them. They cannot wait for an opened day, because the calendar is closed to them, so they buy water and cloth at the storehouses and go out through the south gate on any morning they choose, with no priest at the gate to name the gods. Many of them hold that a death in the waste reaches the [Eternal Garden]{.name data-type="place/site"} of [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]] without any priest's help, and the priests of the gate say nothing either way.
