## Overview

At dawn the lake is a mirror, and the first light of the sun comes off it onto the face of the great pyramid, which was set on the shore to catch it. Ishkul Ch'alish, the Holy Silver Seat, is the temple-city of the sacred lake that fills the eastern reaches of the [[place-kuxiixbalrgn|K'ushi Ish'bal Region]]: about 14,000 people on its northern shore, nearly all of whom serve the temples in one way or another. The lake is held to be the eye through which the gods watch the world, and the priesthood owns it outright. Nobody fishes it, nobody crosses it on business, and nobody sets foot in a boat on it without a priest's blessing.

## The Lake and the City

The city faces the water. Viewing platforms, ritual bathing pools and boat landings line the shore, and stone causeways run out into the lake to temples standing in the shallows, which can be reached only on foot along the causeway or by one of the sacred boats. The boats are reed-built, painted, and never used for anything but the rites. The pilgrims' quarter lies behind the waterfront, and behind that the weavers' courts, the storehouses and the long walled pens where the temple herds are brought in to be counted.

Ishkul Ch'alish is quiet. Voices are kept low on the causeways, nobody shouts on the shore, and a pilgrim who quarrels in public at dusk is taken off the waterfront by the temple guards. The priests say the [[being-lmnshd|Luminshade]] that walk the lake at dawn and dusk turn dangerous when they are shown something ugly, and pilgrims are told never to answer one that speaks. Along the causeways at dusk the temple craftspeople set out polished obsidian mirrors, brought up from the forges of [[place-nekiahkul|Ne' Ki'ahkul]], and the luminshades are seen looking into them.

## Who Governs

In [[affiliation-kuxiixbal|K'ushi Ish'bal]] the priesthood's word prevails over the Ts'enmah's wherever the two differ, and at Ishkul Ch'alish there is nothing else. The city is ruled by the Ch'alish Tq'an of the lake temples, who names its two half-lords from among the temple's own servants and has them put out of office when he chooses. The present Ch'alish Tq'an is [[being-nelushishtem|Nelush Ishtem]], an old priest of [[lore-tzenultqandty|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]], famous for his memory and for never raising his voice. The water priestesses of [[lore-xaqikhanaldty|Shaq'ik Ha'nal]] keep the bathing pools and the rites of the shallows under him, and the two priesthoods divide the waters between them by rite and by night.

Most of the city's people are the temples' bound servants, taken out of their kin groups for life, whose children serve after them: boatmen, bath-keepers, cooks for the pilgrims' halls, weavers and herders. The rest are the merchants and craftspeople who sell to pilgrims, and they pay the temples for the right to trade on the shore.

## The Temple Herds

The temples of the plateau take the largest share of its herds and harvests, and most of that share comes to Ishkul Ch'alish. The lake temples own the greatest herds of llama and alpaca on the plateau, and their herders drive them across the grasslands in bands of a few hundred head, coming in to the pens at the city twice a year to be counted. The cord-keepers knot every animal, every birth and every death into the temple's _k'otsel_, and the inspectors who walk the province read those cords before anything else.

The wool from the temple herds is spun and woven in the weavers' courts, and their cloth goes north as the plateau's best export. The finest of it never leaves the city: it is woven for the rites, worn once on the pyramid and burned.

## The Southern Shore

The lake's southern shore is closed. Its reeds have died back over a long reach, the water there tastes bitter, and the priests have forbidden pilgrims to bathe on it or to take water from it. Boats do not land there. The water priestesses send a boat to the edge of the bitter reach at each turning of the moon's count, and the priestess in its bow tastes the water and reports to the Ch'alish Tq'an. What she reports is not told to the pilgrims.
