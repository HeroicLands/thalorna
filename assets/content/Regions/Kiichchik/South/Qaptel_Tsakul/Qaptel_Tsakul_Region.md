## Overview {#overview}

Q'aptel Tsa'kul Region is the land of [[affiliation-qapteltzakl|Q'aptel Tsa'kul]], and lies within [[place-southkiichchik|Southern Kì'ìch'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]].

Q'aptel Tsa'kul is the threshold—the last gasp of civilization before the world breaks. The name begins with _q'aptel_, "the border," and it is aptly given. Here, the ordered world of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani]], with its pyramid-cities and managed agriculture, gives way to something far darker and older. The land itself seems cursed or dying; toxic mineral flats spread across the earth like scars, geothermal vents hiss poisonous fumes, and the sky often glows sickly yellow or bruised purple with distant storms.

The only settlements in Q'aptel Tsa'kul are its two fortress-temples, waypoints for the doomed and the desperate: the realm's seat, [[place-qapteltzakl2|Q'aptel Tsa'kul]], and the gate of the south, [[place-xelemchalix|Shelem Ch'alish]], at the region's southern edge.

## Geography

Q'aptel Tsa'kul is a land of geological chaos. Vast stretches of barren rock are stained in sickly hues—ochre, rust, and green—by mineral deposits that seep from the ground. In some places, the earth itself boils; geothermal pools of scalding, mineral-rich water bubble up from depths unknown. These vents release acrid gases that sting the eyes and burn the lungs. Some brave (or foolish) alchemists believe that the waters of these vents contain alchemical properties of tremendous value—and so a few researchers and mystics venture here, seeking secrets in the very poisons of the earth.

The plateau gradually descends here toward the southern wastes, but the descent is chaotic—deep canyons cut through the rock, ancient lava flows have hardened into treacherous terrain, and sudden sinkholes can swallow a caravan whole. The scrublands are sparse and hostile; plant life is twisted and thorned, sometimes luminescent in the darkness. Storms gather constantly on the southern horizon, great roiling clouds taken as warnings from [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]].

The single body of water in the region—a mineral-laden lake in the north—is undrinkable and said to drain the life from those foolish enough to fall into it. All potable water must be carried in or carefully dug from deep wells.

## The Crossing

The region is landlocked and grows more hostile with every league south. A party comes down off the plateau from [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]] with a hired guide, stops at the seat to pay its fee, and goes on across the flats to Shelem Ch'alish, where it waits for an opened day and leaves through the south gate. The guides of the two fortresses know the paths that avoid the sinkholes and the boiling pools, and nobody else does. No one lives on the flats between the fortresses, and there is no well, post or shelter on them.

## Trade

Trade in Q'aptel Tsa'kul is minimal and specialized. The primary commodity is passage itself—merchants and adventurers heading south must pay the temple-fortresses for guides, provisions, and the blessing of the priests. The temples hoard supplies: dried foods that can survive the journey, special cloths that protect against the toxic miasmas, and amulets consecrated to provide spiritual protection.

Some rare minerals and alchemical substances are harvested from the poisoned lands themselves. Certain crystalline formations found in the toxic flats are ground into potent reagents used in alchemical work. Strange sulfurous compounds are collected and sold to masters of alchemy in the northern cities. However, the cost in lives and suffering to obtain these materials is enormous, and trade in them remains highly restricted by the priesthood.

Most true trade flows through the region in transit rather than originating there. The great fortresses of Q'aptel Tsa'kul serve as staging grounds and resting places, where supplies are purchased and prayers made before the southern crossing.

## The Country's Quarrel

Q'aptel Tsa'kul lives on the fee, and it quarrels with everyone who takes a party across the flats without its leave. The Q'aptelmah sets a fee for every party bound south, and the fortress-temples sell the guides, the water and the blessing. The guides are their own families, who pass the paths from parent to child. [[affiliation-wallsworn|The Wall-Sworn]] of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok know the same paths and guide parties down across the flats to the gate for a fee paid into their own garrison's storehouse, and the guide families call that their paths sold by soldiers of another realm. The outfitters of the plateau sell guides of their own, men who have crossed the flats once or twice, whom the Wall-Sworn call the quickest way onto the flats and the slowest way back. The flats themselves are worked without leave as well: the priesthood restricts the trade in the crystals and sulfur of the poisoned ground, and gatherers go out onto it for the alchemists of the north regardless.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The guides' complaint.** The head of a guide family of Shelem Ch'alish says that the Wall-Sworn have been walking parties past the seat without stopping for the fee. The Q'aptelmah's cord-keeper wants a party to cross the flats with a Wall-Sworn escort and see where it goes.
- **Crystals without leave.** Crystals ground from the toxic flats are on sale in the northern cities without the priesthood's leave. The Q'aptelmah asks a party to find the gatherers, who work the flats at night from a camp nobody has found.
- **No priest at the gate.** An old man of the Blood-Denied camp outside the north wall of Shelem Ch'alish is dying, wants to die in the waste and cannot walk the ash beyond the gate. He asks a party to carry him out through the south gate on a morning of his choosing. [[being-tsultelne|Ts'ultel Ne']], the gate's Ch'alish Tq'an, says nothing either way, and a party that does it goes out on a day no priest has opened.
