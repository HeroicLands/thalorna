## Overview

A white shrine stands on every headland of the coast north and south of Ts'alam Ne', each in sight of the next, and the ships of the port sail from one to the next and never lose sight of them. Out of sight of a shrine the day-tables give no answer, and no captain of Ts'alam Ne' takes a ship where the day cannot be read. Ts'alam Ne', Spring Water, is the great port of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]], a city of about 45,000 where the jungle rivers open into the western ocean, and the richest and most unruly city of the south.

## The Harbor

The city spreads over coastal hills and narrow peninsulas around a ring of sheltered harbor basins. It is built in tiers. The upper city on the hills holds the lord of the sea's palace, the temples and the houses of the great merchant families. The lower city runs along the waterfront: wharves, warehouses, rope-walks, boatyards, fish-drying racks and the crowded streets of sailors, porters and dock workers.

The harbor is always full. Fishing canoes go out before dawn and come back by noon. Long trading canoes carry metalwork, cacao, feathers and cloth along the coast to the other ports of the shore and bring back salt, dried fish, shell and goods from the cities to the north. The war canoes of the priesthood, painted and manned by temple crews, lie in their own basin under the temple of [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]] and go out to escort cargoes the temples own.

## The Lord of the Sea

Ts'alam Ne' is ruled for the Ts'enmah by a lord of the sea, the Ne'kalmah, who keeps the city's fleet and pays its tribute to the capital in metal, cacao and feathers. The fleet is his, and so are the cords that count it. The inspectors who walk the inland valleys without warning come to Ts'alam Ne' by appointment, and they count the warehouses the Ne'kalmah shows them. The arrangement suits both sides: the Ts'enmah gets his tribute in full, and the sea lords of the coast keep their ships and their own counsel.

The present Ne'kalmah is [[being-chakulish|Ch'akul Ish]], a tall, cheerful man who has sailed every reach of the coast from shrine to shrine, and who is said to keep a second set of cords that the inspectors never see.

Below him the city has the south's two halves. The upper half is the hill city and its kin groups of merchants and temple servants. The lower half is the waterfront, whose lord hears the disputes over cargo, wharf space and pay that fill most of his days, since the waterfront quarrels more than any other part of the south.

## The Shore Shrines

The shrines of the coast belong to the road priests of [[lore-xilanixlandty|Shilan Ish'lan]], and each is kept by a priest who calls the day at dawn, as the road shrines inland call it. A ship leaving Ts'alam Ne' reads its day at the harbor shrine and the next day at the next headland, and a captain who cannot see a shrine at dawn has lost the day and puts in to the nearest beach to wait. The open ocean beyond sight of the headlands is, for the people of the port, a place where there are no days. Fishermen work the waters inside the line of shrines, and nobody from Ts'alam Ne' goes out past it.

## Trade

Merchants from [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]] send goods down the river for the port to carry along the coast, and the port sends salt, fish and shell back up. Mobility through wealth is commoner in Ts'alam Ne' than anywhere else in the south: a fisherman's son who owns three trading canoes can build a house in the upper city, and many have. The priesthood still takes its levy from every cargo, and the temple crews of the war canoes keep a close watch on what comes in.
