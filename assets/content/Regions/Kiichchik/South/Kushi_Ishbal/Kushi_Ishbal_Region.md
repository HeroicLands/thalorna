## Overview

K'ushi Ish'bal Region is the land of [[affiliation-kuxiixbal|K'ushi Ish'bal]], and lies within [[place-southkiichchik|Southern Kì'ìch'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]].

K'ushi Ish'bal, the "Maize-Ear Jaguar," sits at a threshold—one foot planted firmly in the heights and civilization of the upper southern territories, the other pointing toward the harsh, forsaken wastes that lie beyond. The region is a frontier realm, vast and relatively thinly settled compared to the dense city-states of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]]. Here, the grasslands stretch in endless waves, broken by isolated highlands and the scattered outpost-cities of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani priesthood]].

This is a land of transition and warning. The great ceremonial roads that bind Kì'ìch'chik together grow less certain here, their magnificent stones sometimes cracked by forces that priests attribute to the anger of [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Ts'uqil Ish'bal]], goddess of death, or the chaotic wrath of [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]], master of storms.

## Geography

The dominant feature of K'ushi Ish'bal is an immense high plateau that dominates the landscape—the "altiplano," where cold winds sweep across sparse grasslands dotted with hardy shrubs and low vegetation. The air grows thin here; those from the lowlands gasp for breath on their first ascent. The plateau sits beneath an open sky where the heavens seem closer, and shamans and priests travel here to commune with celestial forces.

Great herds of llama and alpaca graze these grasslands, most of them the temples', driven across the plateau by herders bound to the temples and counted twice a year at the pens of the lake. Deep in the plateau lie hidden valleys where settlements cluster near freshwater springs and sheltered gorges. The great lake of [[place-ixkulchalix|Ishkul Ch'alish]] dominates the region's eastern reaches—a vast, sacred body of water said to be the eye of the world, where the boundary between the mortal and divine grows thin.

To the south and west, the plateau gradually descends, the grasses thicken into scrubland, and the temperature begins to rise. The transition is slow and uneasy—the land does not offer gentle gradations here, but rather abrupt drops, twisted rock formations, and signs of what the priests call the wound in the world: bitter springs, ground that will grow nothing, and stone stained ochre and green.

East of the lake the plateau breaks off above a lowland of slow rivers, which spreads every wet season into a great swamp, the last green country north of the wastes. The hunters and fishers of the eastern rim go down to its edges in the dry season and leave its deep reaches alone, for those belong to [[place-ilkaivesto|Ilkaivesto]], a conclave of the [[lore-flksenali|Senali Folk]].

## The Lake and the Towns

K'ushi Ish'bal has no coast. Its heart is the sacred lake, and the temple-city of [[place-ixkulchalix|Ishkul Ch'alish]] on its northern shore owns the water, the boats and the greatest herds of the plateau. The lake's southern shore is bitter, and the priests have closed it.

The realm's seat, [[place-kuxiixbal2|K'ushi Ish'bal]], stands on the open plateau. The garrison town of [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]] holds its southern lip, where the last cord of the Speaking Road is read. The plateau is bordered to the north by Ish'bal Tsa'ku and to the south by the threshold of [[place-qapteltzaklrgn|Q'aptel Tsa'kul Region]].

## Trade

Trade in K'ushi Ish'bal flows primarily north and south along the sacred roads, though the volume is far less than in the wealthier regions to the north. The pastoralist peoples herd llama and alpaca, producing fine wool that is woven into textiles and exported northward. The high grasslands produce hardy grain analogs, freeze-dried tubers, and medicinal plants said to cure altitude sickness and sharpen the mind.

The main trade goods flowing through K'ushi Ish'bal are provisions for travelers heading to and from the far south—dried meats, cold-weather cloaks, strong cords, and the dried leaf that climbers and travelers at altitude chew to breathe more easily. The great fortress-city of [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]] stands as the primary staging point for expeditions into the wastes, and merchants there profit enormously from outfitting southern-bound adventurers, priests, and warriors.

## The Country's Quarrel

K'ushi Ish'bal quarrels over what the temples keep. The priesthood's word prevails over the Ts'enmah's wherever the two differ, and the temples take the largest share of the plateau's herds and harvests into their treasuries and their rites. The Ts'enmah answers for the garrison towns and the expeditions, and the storehouses that outfit them are filled with what the temples leave. The temples keep the lake's secrets as well. Its southern shore has turned bitter over a long reach, the priests have closed it, and what the water priestesses report of it each month goes to the Ch'alish Tq'an of the lake temples and no farther. The kin groups settled in the garrison towns from the northern valleys ask whether the bitter ground is spreading, and nobody answers them. On the southern lip the quarrel is over men. [[affiliation-wallsworn|The Wall-Sworn]] take the Blood-Denied onto the walls under wall-names, and [[affiliation-shaqalob|The Severers]] at the gate of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok hold that the sentence still stands on every one of them.

## Hooks

- **The priestess in the bow.** The Ts'enmah of K'ushi Ish'bal wants to know what the water priestesses taste at the edge of the bitter reach, and [[being-nelushishtem|Nelush Ishtem]], Ch'alish Tq'an of the lake temples, will not tell him. He asks a party to learn it, from a priestess, a boatman or the water itself, without setting foot in a sacred boat unblessed.
- **The short storehouse.** [[being-tsulishkul|Tsul Ishkul]], Sh'ukelmah of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok, has more expeditions coming up the road this season than his storehouses can outfit, because the temples took their share of the herds first. He sends a party to Ishkul Ch'alish with his request for a herd from the lake temples, and with his count of cloaks to show that he asks for no more than he needs.
- **The brother on the wall.** A Severer at the gate of Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok has named a Wall-Sworn brother as a priest who fled the Blood-Denied sentence in the north, and wants him brought before the Speaker of the Sentence. [[being-shlmishtmtsl|Shelem Ishtem Tsul]], First of the Wall, refuses to say what name the brother set aside. Each asks the party to persuade the other.
