## Overview

Ish'bal Tsa'ku Region is the land of [[affiliation-ixbaltzaku|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]], and lies within [[place-southkiichchik|Southern Kì'ìch'chik]] on the [[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]].

Ish'bal Tsa'ku is the jewel of the southern Kì'ìch'chik territories—a vast realm of soaring mountains, verdant river valleys, and emerald jungle.

## Geography

The backbone of Ish'bal Tsa'ku is a mighty mountain range running north-to-south, splitting the region into distinct highland and lowland zones. The high peaks rival those of the far north, crowned with perpetual snows that feed rushing rivers and deep gorges. The highlands are stepped with terraces where small hardy grains, tubers and highland maize grow in walled rows, each valley's terraces divided among the temples, the Ts'enmah and the kin groups who work them. These peaks are studded with fortress-cities and ceremonial centers, their temples climbing ever skyward to commune with the celestial powers.

Westward and eastward from the mountains lie vast river valleys, where the climate grows warm and humid. Here, the jungle thrives—dense, verdant, and teeming with life. The rivers themselves are arteries of trade and travel; great canoes laden with goods and tributary goods flow between highland and lowland, linked by well-maintained _ish'lan ch'upul_ (white roads) that cut straight through the jungle canopy. These lowland zones produce cacao, exotic fruits, medicinal herbs, and the rare feathers of precious birds used in priestly regalia. The rivers themselves are sacred highways, and those who travel them must make offerings to [[lore-xilanixlandty|Shilan Ish'lan]], god of voyages.

The climate shifts from alpine cold in the high peaks to tropical warmth in the valleys. The region experiences two seasons clearly marked: the season of rains (aligned with _Ne'al_, the [[lore-itsaniclndr#tsen|Water]] month of the celestial calendar) and the season of growth. Sudden cloudbursts are common in the lowlands, and the rivers swell with fury during the rainy months.

## The Coast

The western frontier opens onto the ocean, and the coastal cities there are the realm's own, held for the Ts'enmah by lords of the sea who keep their own fleets. The greatest is the port of [[place-tzalamne|Ts'alam Ne']], where the jungle rivers meet the sea. A line of white shore shrines runs along the headlands north and south of it, each in sight of the next, and the ships of the coast sail along that line and never out of sight of it.

## Towns

[[place-ixbaltzaku2|Ish'bal Tsa'ku]], the realm's seat, stands in the mountains. The four towns below it each hold one part of the realm's work: the river junction at [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]], where the highland trains and the jungle canoes meet; the forges of [[place-nekiahkul|Ne' Ki'ahkul]] on the flank of a smoking mountain; the port of Ts'alam Ne'; and the fortress of [[place-tzalamtzltl|Ts'alam Ts'ultel]], which closes the highest pass through the range and tolls every load that crosses it.

## Trade

Ish'bal Tsa'ku is a nexus of wealth and commerce. The highlands export finely worked metals—gold and silver fashioned into ceremonial vessels, jewelry, and temple adornments—as well as steel blades and breastplates, iron and copper implements, and finished textiles of extraordinary quality. The valleys and lowlands produce cacao (bitter and precious), exotic fruits, jaguar and other fine pelts, medicinal herbs, and feathers of the quetzal and macaw. Rubber tapped from jungle trees provides a sticky substance used in ritual and practical applications.

Trade flows in multiple directions: from the highlands down to the coasts, from the coasts back inland, and northward along the great white roads toward [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] and the central heartland of Kì'ìch'chik. The most valuable cacao and rarest feathers flow north to feed the appetites of the great pyramid cities. In return, the southern cities receive textiles from the northern looms and the prestige of access to the central priesthood's most sacred rites. Obsidian goes the other way: the blades and mirrors cut at Ne' Ki'ahkul are sold in every city of the continent.

Markets in the great cities—particularly [[place-nepaxel|Ne' P'ashel]] and Ts'alam Ne'—bustle with merchant cries in a dozen dialects.

## The Country's Quarrel

Ish'bal Tsa'ku quarrels over who counts. The priesthood levies every cargo, the Ts'enmah's inspectors weigh every town's tribute against its cords, and each of the realm's working towns keeps a count it would rather nobody checked. At Ts'alam Ne' the Ne'kalmah, the lord of the sea, keeps the city's fleet and its cords himself, and the inspectors come to the coast by appointment and count what he shows them. [[affiliation-stormfleet|The Storm Fleet]], the temple's navy in the same harbor, counts what it sees, and its Captain holds that the Ne'kalmah's cords are not the only ones that count his ships. Inland, [[affiliation-steelkin|The Steel Kin]] of Ne' Ki'ahkul meet the capital's inspectors at the edge of their town and walk them from forge to forge, and the merchants of Ne' P'ashel want to buy steel at forges the smiths will not open to them. A piece of steel without a kin group's mark is steel that should have gone into the Ts'enmah's weight, and somebody in the south is selling it.

## Hooks

- **Proof for the temple.** [[being-kushiqaptel|K'ushi Q'aptel]], Captain of the Storm Fleet, pays for proof of any cargo that came into Ts'alam Ne' uncounted, and pays best for proof of one belonging to [[being-chakulish|Ch'akul Ish]], the Ne'kalmah. He asks a party to take passage on one of the Ne'kalmah's trading canoes and watch where it puts in before it reaches the harbor mouth.
- **Unmarked steel.** [[being-toshelneok|Toshel Ne'ok]], who speaks for the six kin groups of the forges, has seen unmarked steel on sale in the market of Ne' P'ashel. She pays a party to buy a piece, follow it back to whoever sold it, and bring her the seller's name before the inspectors hear of it.
- **The light load.** [[being-penqalqaptl|P'enqal Q'aptel]], Ts'ikmah of Ts'alam Ts'ultel, has a merchant whose loads were lighter at the pass than at the market below, and the merchant swears they were not. He asks a party to go down to the market, read its cords against the cords of the pass, and learn which count is false.
