## Ch'um Ts'ena (The Speaking of the Day)

"Nothing is worked on a day nobody has opened," the Master of Initiates tells each year's Tq'an'ik on the morning they are admitted. "You will learn the words for ten years. The words are the easy part." The rest of the training is the consequence of that warning.

**Ch'um Ts'ena**, the Speaking of the Day, is the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]] priesthood's account of how a working is made. Its name joins _ch'um-_, to speak, with _ts'ena_, the day. It is not a school beside the temple, and it has no mages of its own: it is what the one priesthood of [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik Continent]] knows about the power its rites carry, taught to every Ch'alishbal as part of the calendar. Its grammar is the calendar, its scale is the blood-ledger, and its proof is the arithmetic of the observatories.

### The Same Potential, Cut by Time

The traditions of the world agree that one undifferentiated power runs through everything and that practice is the art of reaching into it. They disagree about where its divisions lie. [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]]'s [[affiliation-zemorhodhis|the Six Ways]] cuts it by _substance_, into fire, water, air, earth, metal and spirit. The [[lore-khelunulekha|Khelunu Lekhau]] of Au'Khelathu cuts it by _function_, into the eight powers of the cosmic order.

The Itsani cut it by _time_. In their teaching the calendar is [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]]'s own language, written at the creation in the movements of the sky, and every moment is a place in that language. A working has a _when_ before it has a _what_. The same words, spoken by the same priest with the same blood, do one thing on a day the counts have opened to the god they address and nothing at all on a day they have not. The priesthood calls a working a _ch'umel_, a voice, because it is spoken into the day rather than cast upon the world.

The partition cuts across both of the others. A rain-calling on a pyramid peak draws on what an Order _marendos_ would have to call water and air together, and a binding of the serpent spans air, earth and spirit at once. By the Order's doctrine these are impure workings, several currents braided into one act and timed to the hour, and they cannot be cast. The question does not arise in practice. The Arcane Order has no presence on Ki'ich'chik, and the priesthood, which regards the gods of Ankaris as pale reflections of the Ten, regards Ankarian magic, where it has heard of it at all, as speech without a day.

### The Four Counts

A moment stands in four counts at once, and [[lore-itsaniclndr|the Itsani Calendar]] keeps them all. Each count gives a working something different.

| Count                                  | Its length                                      | What it gives a working                                                              |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| [[lore-itsaniclndr#tsen\|Ts'en]]       | The sun's year of 365 days                      | The season, and the solstices on which the greatest workings fall                    |
| [[lore-itsaniclndr#tqan\|Tq'an]]       | 260 days of twenty signs and thirteen numbers   | The day's sign, and the meetings of the planets that open a day to a god             |
| [[lore-itsaniclndr#tsuqal\|Ts'uqal]]   | The moon's month of thirty days                 | The phase: the dark for dreams and the dead, the full moon for the waters            |
| [[lore-itsaniclndr#ishilan\|Ish'ilan]] | The year of the fixed stars, and the age itself | The great festivals of the ages, and the year of the Fifth Age the working stands in |

The Tq'an is the count that decides. Each of the visible planets belongs to a god, and the planets keep time with the 260 days. Where a god's planet stands in its count, and which others it meets there, is what the **Reckoner of Conjunctions** reads when he issues the days. The other three counts narrow what the Tq'an allows: a rain-calling needs a day opened to the storm _and_ the dry season, and a dream-reading needs a day opened to the moon's goddess _and_ the dark of the moon.

### Opened and Closed

The city knows the Reckoner's tables as a list of open and forbidden days for sowing, building, marrying and war. The priesthood knows them as something finer. Each day the Reckoner issues is opened to one god or to several, or to none, and the lay list is that reading simplified: a day open for sowing is, in the temple's own tables, a day opened to [[lore-kanixhakandty|K'anish Ha'kan]].

On a day opened to its god, a working takes. On a day closed to it, the grammar has no sentence. The words can be said, the blood can be let and the incense burned, and nothing follows. A priest who works on a closed day has not failed in the way a clumsy priest fails. He has said something the language does not contain. The doctrine that follows is the framework's whole discipline: _nothing is improvised_. Every working is a set form, learned whole, spoken on its day, and a priest who changes a word to suit the moment has changed it into nothing. This is the framework's strength under strain, and its limit.

The five doors at the year's end are opened to no god. The **Master of the Liminal Days** governs a city that has, for five days, no voice at all: its priests keep the fires and speak no working into the days.

One rite stands outside the tables. The water-marking of a newborn is given the hour a child is born, whatever the day. The water priestesses of [[affiliation-xaqikhanal|Faith of Shaq'ik Ha'nal]] hold that a birth opens its own day and needs no Reckoner. The Reckoners hold that the water-marking is a rite and not a working, and needs no opened day because it works nothing. The two answers have never been reconciled, and the water is given either way.

The Reckoners publish their conclusions and never their workings. A day appears in the tables as opened or closed, never with the arithmetic that made it so, and a Ch'alishbal of any other branch learns the forms of his god's workings without ever learning how the days for them are found. The Reckoners themselves say that they read the days rather than open them, and that the goddess opened every day at the creation. The rest of the priesthood says "opened" all the same.

### The Ledger

A working spends blood. Blood given to the gods is the mortal share of the bargain that keeps the age running, and the **Warden of the Debt** keeps its account in bark-paper registers, entered against the celestial events each gift was meant to answer. At the close of each twenty-day month he announces whether the city stands in surplus or in deficit. That figure is the ceiling on what the city's priests may attempt.

A small working is paid from the priest's own body. Every rank of the priesthood practices autosacrifice, and a Ch'alishbal who calls rain on one field, binds one vow or blesses one caravan gives his tongue, his earlobe or his thumb and draws on nothing else. A great working is paid from the city's account. A storm brought onto a field, the serpent bound for a season, the sun held to its road at the winter solstice: these spend what the whole city has given, and in a city in deficit they cannot be attempted at all until the figure turns. That is why a deficit lengthens the festivals and brings the captives' sacrifices forward. The city is buying back its voice.

The deepest reserve is the captive. A warrior's vow over a captive he has taken, received by the **Dedicator of Captives**, assigns that captive to a coming rite, and the rite is a working waiting for its day. The sacred captive-hunts, the _ch'utik'at_, are in this reckoning the supply of the city's greatest workings. A warrior who kills where he could have taken has spent a working on the ground, and the temple counts it so.

Three of the Ten ask little or nothing in blood. [[lore-naliktzuqaldty|Nal'ik Ts'uqal]] takes cacao, flowers, incense and the spoken word; Shaq'ik Ha'nal takes a little blood and a great deal of carried water; [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]] prefers work. Their workings are paid in that coin, and none of them can draw a city into deficit.

### The Ten Workings

Each of the Ten has its kind of working, and each is a rite its faith already keeps, spoken on its day. The full form of each is described under its ritual.

[[lore-kayikchalixdty|K'ayik Ch'alish]] stands apart from the other nine, because every great working passes through his flame. An offering that is not burned has not been delivered, so the blood of the solstice, the jade of the serpent-binding and the first ear of the harvest all come to a fire in the end, and the fire-keepers' feeding is the act by which a working's price reaches the god it pays.

### Where the Count Is Kept

A day is not a fact of the sky alone. In the Reckoners' teaching a day has a position only where the priesthood keeps the count, and they keep it along the white roads. The reckonings travel as knotted _k'otsel_ cords on the [[affiliation-chumishlan|Ch'um Ish'lan]], the Speaking Road, and every road shrine speaks the day at dawn. The Reckoners compute their tables for the cities the cords reach, and for those cities only.

So the Reckoners' account of the framework's reach is an account of their tables. North of the great desert, where there are no white roads, the tables give no answer. At sea, out of sight of any shrine, they give none. South of [[place-qptlulneok|Q'aptel'ul Ne'ok]], where the last cord from the civilized world is decoded, they give fewer answers with every day's march. Of [[place-xelemixbalrgn|Shelem Ish'bal Region]] beyond the threshold they give none at all. The Reckoners do not say that a working fails in such places, and they do not say why the tables are silent. They say that the tables give no answer there, and they hold that the difference matters. A priest who goes past the end of the tables carries his words and his blood with him and no day to speak them into.

### What It Can Do, and What It Cannot

At an opened day, with a surplus behind it, the Speaking of the Day can bring rain onto a chosen field days before it is needed, quiet a season of storms and shaking ground, hold a vow so that breaking it costs the breaker's household, read tomorrow in a dream-temple with the reliability stated, bring a city's maize up true to its seed, raise a wall that stands under loads it should not carry, set a caravan on a road it will not lose, and guide the dead through [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]] so that none is lost. Over an army, the last of these matters most: soldiers who know they will be recited for go up a stair they would not otherwise climb.

It cannot work on a closed day. It cannot improvise. It cannot work where the count is not kept. It cannot spend what the city has not given, and it cannot hurry, because the day decides and not the priest. A Ts'enmah who means to make war waits on the same tables as a farmer who means to sow, and a war declared on a forbidden day is held lost before it is fought.

### The Blank in Every Table

Every table the Reckoners issue has one position it does not publish: the day the Fifth Age ends. The goddess is held to know it. The calculation of it exists, bound in serpent skin in the chambers of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|Faith of P'uqil Ch'akun]]'s binding temples, and the **Warden of the Sealed Chambers** guards it and shows it to almost no one. A Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an may order a prediction suppressed, and more than one has.

The priesthood teaches that no working can be spoken into that day, because no god will be listening. [[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil) teach that the age is already beyond saving, and they can demonstrably pull the recently dead back out of the labyrinth. The orthodox priesthood holds that whatever the Awakeners speak is spoken into no day its tables open, and that is why it counts theirs the one heresy it cannot answer by its own means: the framework has no sentence for what they do, and what they do works.
