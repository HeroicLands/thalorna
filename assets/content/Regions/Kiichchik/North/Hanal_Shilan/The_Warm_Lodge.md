## Overview

Below the bluff of [[place-hanalxilan2|Ha'nal Shi'lan]], where the two rivers meet, a low gray dome stands at the edge of the bottomland, above the reach of the spring floods but not far above it. It is about four paces high and twelve across, one body with no seam, ribbed and vaulted inside like the inside of a great shell. The town calls it the Warm Lodge. The [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]] who come down the rivers call it _tsiotarie kahnisa_, the spirit's lodge.

Its south side stands open in a smooth oval, taller than a man, whose edge runs into the wall with no post, no lintel and no sill. Every child on the two rivers can describe that oval. There is no door to it and nothing to close: no leaf, no hinge, no bar. Nobody living has seen the oval other than open, and nobody on either river knows whether it can close.

## What It Is Like

Outside, the Warm Lodge is a smooth, even gray, like river-stone with every vein and grain taken out of it, and it gives a little under a palm, like soft-tanned hide, and comes back. No flood has moved it, no ice has scarred it, and the town's children have never managed to scratch their names on it, though the bank around it is crowded with names cut into every boulder.

Inside it is warm. Not hot: warm, through a winter that freezes the rivers from bank to bank and kills sleepers caught out on the ice. The warmth comes from nowhere a visitor can find, from no wall and no floor more than another. After dark the inside is faintly lit, enough to see a face across the room by, with no flame anywhere. Nothing is written on the walls inside or out.

## The Winter Sittings

The [[affiliation-hanalxilanmktcncl|Ha'nal Shi'lan Market Council]] holds its winter sittings in the Warm Lodge, when the river is frozen, the canoes are drawn up and the town's quarrels have had a season to grow. The council house of bark and poles is built against the dome's south face, so that a councilor walks from the one into the other through the oval. The Family Heads and the Quarter Elders sit on mats along the curve of the wall, and the council fire is laid in the oval itself, for the smoke rather than the heat. A few old people of the town, by long custom, sleep inside through the worst of the cold.

The Maple People hold that a spirit keeps the lodge and warms it for guests who honor it. Their traders who winter in the town leave tobacco at the oval when they first go in each season, and speak quietly inside. The council pays the same courtesy, since many of its families are of Maple blood, and it suits a market town to agree with its best customers.

## Hooks

- **The notched stick.** Two old women who have slept nearest the wall for a decade have been measuring the oval against a notched stick. It is a hand's breadth narrower than it was three winters ago, and the far side of the lodge is no longer warm enough to sleep in. They have told nobody on the council. They tell the party, because the party will be gone in spring.
- **The guest.** A [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]] envoy has arrived in the town in the first snow and asks to winter in the Warm Lodge, as an honored guest is entitled to. The Maple People's traders say the spirit will not have him. The council cannot refuse a guest and cannot afford to offend its traders.
- **The cedar log.** A Family Head who has heard the women's measurement means to wedge a squared cedar log into the oval to hold it open. He wants strong backs, and quietly, before the council hears of it.

# The Truth of the Warm Lodge {#truth .secret}

The Warm Lodge is working badly. It is gray and gives under the hand all over, so it is not dead, but it is less warm each winter, and the oval is narrowing. The old women's stick is right: a hand's breadth in three winters. The lodge shows no change a party can find anywhere on it.

Nothing anyone does affects it. The Maple traders doubled their tobacco last winter, and the oval narrowed as much as in the winter before. A cedar log wedged in the oval holds nothing open: the gray closes round it as it narrows and the log stays where it was put, gripped, until it is pulled out. Whether the oval will close altogether, and when, cannot be established. Neither can whether anyone inside when it closes would be shut in.

> [!TIP]
> The lodge is a clock the town has not yet noticed. Its stakes are the town's: without the winter sittings in the warm, the council meets in a bark house in the coldest months, and the traders who winter at Ha'nal Shi'lan for its warmth may winter elsewhere.
