## Overview

Nearly every war canoe on the coast was cut on the Outer Islands (_yatsala_, the gathering of islands). They lie off the middle of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Kelwanuk Region]], a chain of large islands across a strait a day's paddle wide, and the cedars on them are the oldest and the tallest the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]] know. About 30,000 people live here, and the island houses build the seagoing canoes (_yakwis_) that the rest of the coast buys, raids in and is buried with.

## Geography

The strait between the islands and the mainland is shallow and short-tempered: a southeast gale in winter raises a sea in it that no canoe crosses, and the islands are cut off for weeks at a time. The islanders cross in summer, in the long calm mornings, and they cross better than anyone.

The islands themselves are mountains with forest to the summits. On the strait side the shores are low, with sand beaches and long shallow inlets where the villages stand. On the ocean side cliffs drop straight into the surf, and there are only a few coves, where the sea lions haul out. The island cedars grow huge and straight in the wet, and a single trunk can make a canoe that carries forty paddlers.

## Villages

The islands hold some twenty villages, on the sheltered beaches facing the strait.

- [[place-gwemlasis|Gwemlasis]], Thunderbird Beach, about 1,800 people: the Thunderbird House, the canoe builders, which sat first on the coast before the Eagle House.

## What Moves Through It

Canoes go out. A canoe builder of the islands chooses a cedar standing, roughs out the hull in the forest, and floats it down to the beach to finish, and a mainland chief pays a fortune in blankets for a war canoe and waits two years for it. The canoes cross the strait under the islanders' own paddles and are handed over on the buyer's beach.

Everything else comes in: grease from the inlets, because the islands have no eulachon river, and beads and copper as the price of the canoes. The islanders say the mainland cannot raid without them, and the mainland has never found an answer to it.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Thunderbird House sold war canoes to the Eagle House for the raids that made the Eagle House rich, and now it sells them to the Bear House of [[place-lamekis|Lamekis]]. The island chiefs argue among themselves whether to keep selling to every house that pays, or to sell only to the house they want to see sit first.
