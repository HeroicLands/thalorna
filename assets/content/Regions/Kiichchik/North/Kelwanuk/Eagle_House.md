## The Eagle House

The tallest crest pole on the Cedar People's coast stands before the largest plank house at [[place-tsilkasis|Eagle Beach]] (Tsilkasis), and an eagle with spread wings sits on top of it. Inside, the house's posts are carved with the first Tsilkas, who came down from the sky as an eagle and took off his mask on that beach. When the house gives a feast, its doors stand open for days and the gifts are piled where every guest can count them.

The **Eagle House**, the _tsilkasanuk_, is the noble house of Tsilkasis that sits first on the coast of the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]. Its chief holds the first seat, and it has kept that seat for four generations of chiefs by giving feasts no other house could answer. The south calls the whole nation the Eagle People after this house's poles.

## Membership

A house is its great plank building and the lineage that lives in it, with its own names, crests, fishing places and dances. The Eagle House holds its chief and his kin, its nobles, each holding one or more of its hereditary names and the seats and fishing places those names carry, its commoners, who are of the house but hold none of its names, and its slaves. A name passes when its holder names an heir before witnesses and the heir lifts it at a feast, and the house's names pass only to its own blood or to those it marries.

The chief is the ninth [[being-lukwahakwa|Lukwahakwa]], Copper Lifted. His nephew [[being-tuklaskapala|Tuklaskapala]], Returns from the Mountain, is the house's **Seat Holder**, who walks to the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]]'s council each summer with a Speaker to hold the coast's seat there.

## Standing

The chief of the house that sits first holds the first seat (_kenwatukwa_): he speaks first at any council of chiefs, is served first at any feast, calls the feast that decides war for the whole coast, and leads the houses that eat at it. That is the whole of his authority, and his house keeps it while its gifts are the greatest on the coast. Every midsummer the house sends a few big canoes north past the glaciers to the Ice-Edge People's Trading Shore, and the ivory and furs they bring home go into its feasts.

## Rivals

**The Bear House.** The [[affiliation-bearhouse|Bear House]] of [[place-lamekis|Bear Beach]] has given two feasts in four years that the Eagle House has not matched, and gives a third this winter. Lukwahakwa has broken one copper already, and his commoners have worked two summers at the weir and the rendering pits for the feast he has not yet given. If he cannot answer, the next feast of the coast seats the Bear House first.

**The Thunderbird House.** The house of [[place-gwemlasis|Thunderbird Beach]] sat first on the coast before the Eagle House climbed past it, and it sold the Eagle House the war canoes for the raids that made it rich. It sells them to the Bear House now.

**The Orca House.** The chief of [[place-malkisis|Orca Beach]] frees slaves at his feasts and calls each one a gift, and a gift that guests refuse cannot be answered. The Eagle House's guests have twice walked out before the blankets rather than accept one.

## What a Party Can Ask of It

- **A seat at the feast.** A guest of the Eagle House is seated in the order of seats and fed for days, and a stranger with a gift worth counting is seated somewhere.
- **Work toward the answering feast.** The house needs grease, blankets and copper before winter, and a party that brings any of them is remembered in the count.
- **Paid witness.** Every feast pays its witnesses (_nilkwamis_) to see and remember, and a party paid by the house to witness its feast has accepted its claim.
- **The road north.** The midsummer canoes to Trading Shore take a few passengers who can paddle.
- **A word at the compact.** The Seat Holder can promise nothing the first seat has not agreed, and he carries questions home faithfully.
