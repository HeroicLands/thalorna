# terran_analog: the mainland inlets and fjords of the British Columbia coast north of Vancouver Island and the Alaska panhandle, the country of the Tsimshian, Nisga'a, Haisla, Nuxalk and Tlingit; the eulachon rivers are the Nass and the Kitimat, and the Grease Trail is the Nuxalk grease trail inland from Bella Coola

## Overview

A canoe can paddle all day up one of the Long Inlets (_tuyala hapas_) and still have the sea under it. The inlets are the northern half of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Kelwanuk Region]], the country of the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Kelwanuk]]: long arms of salt water between walls of rock and cedar, each ending at a river mouth where a village stands on the beach. About 90,000 people live here, nearly half the Cedar People, and the house that sits first on the coast lives here, at [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]].

## Geography

The inlets run east from the open sea into the mountains, some of them for a hundred miles. Their walls rise straight out of the water to snowfields, waterfalls hang off them in every rain, and the water at their heads lies so deep and still that it is fresh on top and salt underneath. A wind blows down each inlet from the ice every morning and up from the sea every afternoon, and the canoes travel with it.

At the head of each inlet a river comes down from the mountains, and every river carries salmon. The greatest rivers also carry the eulachon (_hulis_), the small oil fish that comes in at the end of winter, and a river with a eulachon run makes its village rich, because every feast on the coast must give grease (_melum_). The villages of the eulachon rivers render it in pits and sell it in boxes to every house on the coast.

North of the last inlets the mountains come down to the sea as glaciers, and beyond them lies the low, treeless shore of the [Ice-Edge People]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}.

## Villages

Each inlet holds three or four villages, one at its head and the rest on beaches along its walls where a stream comes down. Two matter to the whole coast.

- [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]], Eagle Beach, about 3,000 people, at the head of a northern inlet: the Eagle House, which holds the first seat.
- [[place-mukwasis|Mukwasis]], Wolf Beach, about 1,200, at the head of the longest inlet, where the Grease Trail starts over the mountains.

## What Moves Through It

Grease moves first. The eulachon villages send it down the inlets in boxes to the outer coast, and up the [Grease Trail]{.name data-type="place/feature"} from Wolf Beach over the passes to the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Grass People]], who pay in robes and roots. Copper comes back the same way, a little at a time, from traders who had it from the east.

Every midsummer the Eagle House sends a few big canoes north past the glaciers to [Trading Shore]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, the southernmost village of the Ice-Edge People, with cedar boxes, grease and now and then a sheet of copper, and they come home with ivory, furs and gut parkas. The war canoes that go north for slaves every few years leave from the same beaches.

## The Country's Quarrel

The Long Inlets hold the first seat and want to keep it. The richest houses of the coast are now in the south, on the [[place-yumisrgn|Outer Beaches]], where the beads of the south come in, and the northern chiefs say the southern wealth is bought with other people's captives. The southern chiefs answer that the northern wealth is bought with the same, only longer ago.
