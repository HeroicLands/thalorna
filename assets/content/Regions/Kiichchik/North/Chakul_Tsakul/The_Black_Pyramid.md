## Overview

The Black Pyramid, Kul'qat P'uqan, stands at the closed end of the deepest box canyon in the central badlands of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul Region]], where the canyon walls rise sheer on three sides and the sky is a strip overhead. It is a pyramid of seven steep tiers built of a dense black stone, with a single stair up its face and a temple on its top, and it fills the end of the canyon from wall to wall. It is the lair and treasure house of the [[being-mtrfrdrg|Mature Fire Dragon]] that holds the badlands.

## The Canyon

The only way to the pyramid is up the canyon floor, a day's walk of gravel and boulders between red walls, with no side canyon that climbs out. The floor nearer the pyramid is swept bare, and then glazed. The last mile is a road of fused gravel, black and glassy, ridged where it ran and set, and the bones lie along it in drifts: bison, deer, pack dogs and men, cracked and scorched, with ash blown into the hollows among them.

## The Pyramid

The lower tiers keep their shape. Their blocks are fitted without mortar, so close that no blade goes between them, and each tier is faced with a band of carving: a row of masked heads in profile, all facing the stair, and above them a row of interlocking hooks. The upper tiers have run. Their corners are rounded and glossy, the carving on them is melted to ripples, and black glass hangs from the lip of each tier in frozen drips as long as a man's arm. The stair is worn in the middle by something far heavier than feet.

The temple on the top is a single long room with a doorway wide enough for the dragon to pass, its lintel cracked through and propped on fallen stone. The hoard lies inside and spills out of the doorway onto the top of the stair: copper bells and plates, cacao in rotten sacks, turquoise, shell, jade, worked stone and gold, and things from the caravans that the dragon took for their shine and no merchant would buy, mirrors and polished blades and a priest's ceremonial mask.

## Who Comes Here

Nobody comes to the Black Pyramid but thieves and those who come to kill the dragon, and the bones on the glazed road are most of them. The guides of the badlands take a party to the mouth of the canyon and no further. The traders who leave tribute at the foot of the dragon's mesa speak of the pyramid as the dragon's house and nothing else, and they know no name for it but the black one.

# The Truth of the Black Pyramid {#truth .secret}

The dragon sleeps in the temple on the top, on its hoard, and it knows every piece of it. It hunts by day and spends most nights in the temple, and it leaves the canyon for days at a time only in the season after it has fed well. It hears a party on the canyon floor long before the party sees the pyramid.

The temple is not the whole of the pyramid. A low doorway, half buried by fallen blocks at the foot of the stair, opens on a narrow passage running into the pyramid's heart, too small for the dragon to enter. It climbs by short stairs to three chambers, one above another, cool and dark and untouched by fire. They hold carved stone, painted jars sealed with pitch, and in the highest chamber a body laid at full length on a stone bench, under a cloak sewn with plates of green stone, its face covered with a mask of hammered gold.

> [!WARNING]
> The highest chamber lies directly under the temple floor, and the floor between them is cracked. Anything louder than a whisper in it reaches the dragon above.
