## Overview {#overview}

The Sleeping Pyramid, Kul'qat Ishan, lies in the sand seas of northern [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul Region]], under a dune. Most years nothing of it shows but a small square shrine of white stone sitting on a dune crest, with a doorway on each side, like a hut someone built on the sand. In some years the dune moves off it, and a pyramid of five smooth tiers stands in a hollow of the sand, plastered white and banded with red, with the shrine on its top. No two years show the same part of it.

## The Pyramid

When the sand is off it, the pyramid is a broad, low building, wider than it is tall, with steep plastered sides and a stair on its north face. Each tier has a row of doorways along its foot, square and low, opening into rooms. The plaster is whole wherever the sand has kept it and gone wherever the wind has reached it, and where it is whole it is painted: bands of red and black, and between them processions of figures in long cloaks walking toward the stair, carrying bowls, birds and bundles. The sand has polished the faces of the figures away.

The rooms of the lower tiers are full of sand to the ceiling. The rooms of the upper tiers are partly clear, and their walls are painted too, from floor to ceiling, with the same figures standing and sitting, larger than life, in rooms painted to look like rooms, with painted doorways leading to painted rooms beyond.

## Dangers

The sand seas themselves are the first danger: a week of dunes with no water, under a sun that heats the sand too hot to sleep on. The pyramid adds its own. The sand in the rooms is loose, and a party digging into a buried room brings the sand above it down on its own heads. A wind from the west moves a whole dune in a night, and a party camped in the hollow beside the pyramid wakes to a wall of sand sliding down on it, or does not wake. The rooms that are clear one day can be full the next.

## What Is Found There

The clear rooms hold what the sand has kept. Jars of painted clay stand along the walls, some empty and some full of dust that was once grain. There are mirrors of polished black stone the size of a hand, small figures of fired clay, and spindle whorls, and in one room a pile of seashells, though the nearest sea is weeks away. The northern traders who find the pyramid uncovered take what they can carry, and most of what reaches the markets of the north from the desert's ruins comes from its rooms.

The traders say the pyramid shows itself after a winter of hard west winds, and that a party should leave it before the wind turns.

# The Truth of the Sleeping Pyramid {#truth .secret}

The traders are right about the wind. A winter of hard west winds strips the pyramid to its third tier or lower, and the first spring storm from the east begins to bury it again. A party that finds it uncovered has a few weeks before the rooms start to fill.

The shrine on the top has a shaft in its floor, filled with sand, that runs straight down through the heart of the pyramid. A party that clears it reaches a sealed room at the level of the desert floor, dry and free of sand, whose walls and ceiling are painted black and scattered with thousands of small white points, like a night sky. The points fall in no pattern that any sky-watcher of the south can match to the stars. In the middle of the room a stone seat faces north, and on it lies a folded cloak of feathers, black and white, perfect, which falls to dust if it is lifted.

> [!WARNING]
> Clearing the shaft takes days of hauling sand up on ropes, and a sand-slide in the shaft buries whoever is working at its bottom.
