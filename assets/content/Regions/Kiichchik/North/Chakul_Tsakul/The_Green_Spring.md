## Overview {#overview}

The Green Spring, Ts'alam Ha'nak, lies in the western mesas of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul Region]], a round sink in the bare rock a hundred paces across and as deep as a tall tree. From the rim it is the only green thing for days in any direction: the tops of palms, the dark leaves of fig trees, and grass. A traveler who has walked a week on carried water looks down into it and sees shade, birds and a pool so clear that the stones on its bottom show.

## The Sink

The walls of the sink are lined with cut stone, laid in terraces like the seats of a great round court, nine of them from the rim to the floor. A stair cut into the stone winds down the wall from terrace to terrace, its treads worn into hollows in the middle. Each terrace has a row of square niches along its back wall, blackened at the top by old smoke, and the lowest terraces are grown over with grass and fern.

At the bottom, a square pool walled in fitted stone fills with water from below, cold and sweet, and spills through a cut in its wall into a channel that waters the trees and sinks into the floor. The palms grow tall enough to shade the whole floor at noon, the figs fruit, and swallows nest in the niches. It is cool down there, cooler than anywhere in the desert, and still. No wind reaches the floor.

## The Dead in the Shade

The floor of the sink is the best camp in Ch'akul Tsa'kul, and it is littered with the dead. They lie under the trees and along the pool's wall: coyotes, foxes, a pack dog still in its harness, and men, some of them only bones and some still in their blankets with their packs beside them. None of the dead carries a wound. The desert's carrion birds circle the sink at dawn, and the few that drop to the floor stay there.

The traders who cross the western mesas know the Green Spring. They go down by day, fill their skins at the pool and climb out before the sun is off the rim, and they camp on the rocks above, without fires, and drink what they carried up. A party that will not climb out at dusk, they say, is a party that wanted to lie down in the shade, and the Green Spring lets it.

## What Is Found There

The traders strip the dead of what they carry and take nothing from the stone. Under the grass of the lowest terraces, carved slabs lie fallen from the wall above them: rows of seated figures in tall headdresses, worn almost smooth, with their hands raised toward the pool. The fig trees drop their fruit into the water, and the fruit is good to eat.

# The Truth of the Green Spring {#truth .secret}

The water is sweet and wholesome, and the fruit is good. What kills is the air. After the sun leaves the floor, a heavy air without smell or color seeps up through the floor of the sink and the bottom of the pool, and it pools in the sink as water pools in a bowl. By midnight it fills the sink to the height of the third terrace, and by mid-morning it has drained away. A sleeper in it does not wake. A waking man in it grows dull and sleepy, sits down, and dies within the hour.

> [!TIP]
> A lamp or a torch set on the floor gutters and goes out long before anyone feels anything, and a bird roosting low falls silent and drops. Give a careful party those signs before the first sleeper.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that goes down after dark to pull out a companion who stayed below walks into the same air. A rope from the fourth terrace or higher reaches the floor, and a rescuer who holds his breath has the length of one breath to tie it.

The water and the shade are safe in daylight, from mid-morning until the sun leaves the floor. The fourth terrace and every one above it stay clear on every night of the year.
