## Overview

The Bead Coast (_shuq halaq_) is the shore of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]], the Shell-Bead People, on the western coast of [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]], south of the fjords of the [[place-kelwanukrgn|Kelwanuk Region]]. It is a long strip of beaches, river mouths and headlands between the sea and the coastal mountains, with a chain of islands a morning's paddle offshore. About 230,000 people live on it in some sixty towns, more people to the mile than anywhere else in the north, and every one of them can reach the sea before noon.

## Geography

The coast runs from northwest to southeast, and along its southern half it faces south across a channel to the islands, which shelter its water from the open ocean. Kelp grows in forests along the whole shore, so thick in places that a canoe must follow lanes through it, and the kelp holds fish, sea otters, seals and shellfish in numbers that feed the towns with little work. Tar seeps up through the sand and the rocks along the channel shore and floats ashore in black cakes. The Canoe Kin seal their canoes with it, the basket makers line their water bottles with it, and the drillers fix their beads with it.

The rains come in winter, and the hills behind the beaches are green from the first storm until spring. By midsummer the grass is dry and gold, the oaks stand dark in the folds of the hills, and fog comes in off the sea most mornings and burns off by noon. Snow never falls on the coast. The coastal mountains rise steeply behind the hills, and three or four passes lead over them to the [[place-tulishlumuqrgn|Oak Valleys]].

The islands are dry, windy and nearly treeless, and short of water. The largest, [Shuq Siwaq]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, Bead Island, holds [[place-shuqwil|Shuqwil]], the drilling town, and a dozen smaller island towns, all of them living by the bead.

At the northwest end of the channel the coast turns north at a high headland, [[place-hipalhalaq|Hipal Halaq]], the Crossing Shore, where the souls of the dead go down to the sea. North of it the shore is rougher and more exposed, and its towns are fewer. The last of them is [[place-tikoqhipay|Tikoq Hipay]], the North Door, beyond which the coast belongs to nobody until the Cedar People's villages begin.

## Towns

A town stands where a stream reaches the sea and a canoe can land: domed reed houses in rows behind the beach, a sweathouse, a dancing ground, the chief's storehouse and a feathered pole on the hill above. Most hold a few hundred people; the great towns of the channel shore hold several thousand. The largest is [[place-kulaywil|Kulaywil]], Canoe Place, the harbor of the Canoe Kin and the seat of the Sky Elder. [Pitashwil]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, Tar Place, east along the channel, sells the tar that every canoe needs. Shuqwil on Bead Island makes the beads. Tikoq Hipay, behind the only palisade on the coast, watches the north.

## Trade and Danger

The coast trades with itself first. Fish, seal meat, shell, tar and beads move between the towns by canoe, and the islands' beads come over to the mainland on every calm day from spring to autumn. Over the passes the coast trades beads, dried fish and shell to the Oak Valleys for acorns, seeds, deer meat and baskets. The [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]'s trading canoes come south each autumn, and the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]]'s traders come over the mountains each summer.

The danger comes from the north. The Cedar People's war canoes come down the coast in summer, and watchers on the headlands send up smoke when they are sighted. The northern towns pay the Northern Strand every spring, and the war canoes pass the coast by.
