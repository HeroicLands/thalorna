## The Canoe Kin

Behind a screen of reed mats in the yard at [[place-kulaywil|Canoe Place]], two or three plank canoes are always being built. Planks split from driftwood dry in the sun, apprentices drill seam holes along their edges, tar pots smoke, and in the middle of it a master sewer fits the bow planks with a care nobody outside the screen is allowed to see. A canoe sewn this way crosses to the islands in a morning. Nothing else on the coast can.

The **Canoe Kin**, _kulayash_ in their own tongue, are the hereditary brotherhood of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shell-Bead People]] that alone builds, owns and commands the plank canoes, the _kulay_. They are about two thousand men in the coast towns, and every one of them descends from [Shiyawkal]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sea Arriving, who sewed the first plank canoe on the beach at Kulaywil. Their house stands at the east end of the beach there, with the [Paddle of Shiyawkal]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} hung above its hearth.

## Membership

The brotherhood is born, not joined. A son of the Canoe Kin works in his father's crew as a boy, and at twelve he is presented to the brotherhood. To enter it he must sew one seam of two planks that holds water overnight and paddle to Bead Island and back in his father's crew. Then he swears on the Paddle of Shiyawkal that he will teach the sewing of a canoe to no one outside the brotherhood. A man with no sons may bring in his daughter's husband for a hundred strands, and that is the only door from outside.

A brother who owns a canoe is a canoe master, an _alkulay_, and commands a crew of eight to twelve, some of them brothers and some hired paddlers. The master sewers, the _alsulik_, are a few dozen canoe masters of long standing. Only they know how the bow and keel planks are shaped and fitted, every new canoe is built under one of them, and they sit together to judge a brother. A brother who teaches the art to an outsider is a **Broken Canoe**: his canoe is broken up on the beach, and no canoe of the brotherhood carries him or his family again, not to the islands and not across the strait at the end.

## The Store

The Canoe Kin keep the only common store on the coast. Every voyage pays a share of its cargo into it, and the **Keeper of the Store** pays from it, for life, any brother drowned, crippled or too old to paddle, and his widow and his children after him. Everyone else on the coast depends on kin and chief. A brother depends on the brotherhood, and that is a great part of why no chief commands it.

## Standing

The Canoe Kin answer to no chief. The chiefs buy their cargoes and their canoes, and the chiefs need them, because only a plank canoe reaches the islands where the beads are drilled. A chief who pushes the brotherhood too hard finds that no canoe lands on his beach that spring. A man outside the brotherhood who builds a plank canoe has it broken up on the beach by the brotherhood's men, and no chief stops them.

Every spring, when the winter storms end, the Canoe Kin open the sea road at the [First Crossing]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. They launch their canoes together at Kulaywil and race to the islands, and the first canoe to beach on Bead Island wins its crew a strand from every chief.

## Rivals

**The chiefs.** Every chief wants more of every cargo and a lower price for every canoe, and the brotherhood sells to all of them and belongs to none. Kulaywil's chief takes a bead in ten of every cargo that lands on her beach, and the canoe masters land there anyway, because Kulaywil buys more than anywhere else.

**The Cedar People.** The [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]]'s war canoes, each cut from a single cedar, come south in the summers for slaves, and their trading canoes come in the autumn for beads. The Canoe Kin carry the coast's beads north as the [Northern Strand]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, and one canoe master, [[being-sitiqnush|Sitiqnush]] of [[place-tikoqhipay|North Door]], sails north three times a year to buy captives back from the Bear House at [[place-lamekis|Bear Beach]].

## What a Party Can Ask of Them

- **A crossing.** A canoe master carries passengers and cargo to the islands and along the coast for strands, and he decides who comes aboard.
- **Wages at the paddle.** Anyone may paddle in a plank canoe for pay, and a party can work its passage.
- **A voyage north.** A party that wants to reach the Cedar People's beaches by sea needs a canoe master willing to take it, and Sitiqnush is the one who goes.
- **A canoe.** Only by buying one, from a canoe master, at his price. Nobody outside the brotherhood is taught to build one, and a party that tries to learn is in more danger than one that steals.
