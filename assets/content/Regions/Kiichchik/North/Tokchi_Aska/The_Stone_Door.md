## Overview {#overview}

**The Stone Door** is a cave mouth in a gray outcrop of limestone on the flank of a ridge in the [[place-poktaskorgn|Old Ranges]], two hours' climb above a hill town of the [[lore-tokchiliclt|Tokchili]] and its valley. Below the outcrop a seep comes out of the hillside and leaves the ground white with salt, and deer come to lick it at dusk in every season. The town's hunters wait for them there. The cave behind the lick is shut by a slab of the same gray stone, set so close in the mouth that from the lick it reads as part of the outcrop, and it turns on its edge when a man leans on it.

## Who Holds It {#who-holds-it}

The lick is the town's, and its hunters have shot over it for as long as the town has had a mound. The town's Mound Chief counts the cave as part of the lick. The town's clan mothers count it as nobody's and tell the young men to leave the door shut.

## Layers {#history}

The clan mothers' account is that the cave was a house before the town was a town, that the people who lived in it went away and did not say where, and that they left the door so that the cave stays theirs. They give no name to the people and say no more of them. The hunters add only that the door was there when their grandfathers' grandfathers first hunted the lick, and that it has never been seen to fall.

## What Is There Now {#present}

A war party is the trouble. At the end of summer a war party of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Maple People]] came down the valley from the north and caught three of the town's hunters at the lick. Two of them ran up to the cave, put their shoulders to the door and shut it behind them, and the war party sat below the outcrop for three days and then went back north with the third man. The two came out when it had gone. They say the cave runs back behind the door into a dry hall where a whole family could sleep, and that the door has a bar on the inside.

The Mound Chief wants the cave for the town's refuge when the war party comes back, as everyone in the valley expects it to before the chestnuts fall. The clan mothers say the door stays shut and the refuge goes where it has always gone, on the mound. The town is split down the middle, and the man the war party took is still the war party's.

## Dangerous Neighbors {#dangerous-neighbors}

The war parties of the Maple People come down the northern valleys of the ranges, and this one knows the lick and the cave now. Bears come to the salt as the deer do and are not afraid of a man alone at dusk. The outcrop is limestone, broken along its joints, and the ledges under the door shed stones in a hard frost.

## What a Party Finds {#what-a-party-finds}

A trodden lick of white mud at the foot of the outcrop, ringed with hunters' blinds of brush. A climb of twenty paces up broken ledges to the door, which stands in a mouth that follows a crack in the rock and is wider at the foot than at the head. The slab fills it exactly, turns on a pin of stone at its foot and its head, and has a socket on its inner face where a bar of wood or stone drops into slots on either side of the mouth. Behind it is a hall the length of a longhouse, dry, swept bare, its floor laid level on the rock, with a hearth at the back under a crack in the roof that draws smoke up into the hill. Along the walls stand stone racks at the height of a man's shoulder, cut from the rock's own beds. Beside the door, at the height of a man's eye, a long row of small cuts runs along a seam in the stone.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The refuge.** The Mound Chief pays a party to make the cave ready as the town's refuge before the war party returns, and to find out whether it has a second way in.
- **The man taken north.** The family of the third hunter want him back, and the war party has gone up the northern valleys toward [[place-hanalxilanrgn|Ha'nal Shi'lan]].
- **The clan mothers' word.** The clan mothers want someone with no stake in the town to look at the door and the cuts beside it and tell them who made them, before the Mound Chief opens the cave to every family in the valley.

# The Truth of the Stone Door {#truth .secret}

**The lodge.** The cave is **Ghalz-khupás**, "the searching of beasts", a hunting lodge of the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]], abandoned. The houses that crossed the water in the ships of the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] and afterward cut Balg-gunáv on the barrens, behind [[place-stonerooms|The Stone Rooms]], came ashore on the eastern coast, went up into the ranges, and wintered here while their finder read the ridges for a hold. A party with no farmers at a gate eats what it carries and what it can kill, and the lick brought the deer to the door. The ranges are old and worn round, and the finder found no face in them high enough for a hold, so in the thaw the houses went on north and west, a journey of more than a year, toward the copper of the far barrens, and the lodge was left swept and shut.

**The door and the cuts.** The door is a slab dressed to the mouth the rock already had, and it turns because it was set to turn; nothing in it has worn. The row of cuts beside it is a tally in the gate-row of [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]]: the houses that wintered, by count, and the days of the winter, with one cut more at the end for the day they went. No one in the ranges reads it.

**What is not there.** The hall holds nothing of value and goes no further than the hearth. It is a lodge and not a hold, so it has no deep, no refuge and no dead. A Khazari pin or bar-slot taken from it is worth more to a scholar in the south than to anyone in the valley.

> [!TIP]
> The cave is exactly what the two hunters say it is, a dry hall with one door that bars from inside, and that makes it a good refuge and a trap at once if the war party learns the town is in it. That these were the houses of Balg-gunáv, their landing on the eastern coast, their winter here, the finder's reading of the ranges and the tally beside the door are this note's own account; a game master may change them.
