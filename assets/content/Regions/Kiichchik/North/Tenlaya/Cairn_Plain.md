## Overview

Stand anywhere on the Cairn Plain and look west, and the mountains are there, a white wall along the edge of the sky, too far to reach in a week. Everything between you and them is short grass, buff and gray-green, rolling in long swells, and across it run lines of knee-high stone cairns, two lines at a time, a few paces apart at one end and a quarter-mile apart at the other, running for miles toward a break in the ground you cannot see until you stand on it. This is Wehi Oshte, the Cairn Plain, the high western grass of the [[affiliation-tenlaya|Tenlaya]], the Grass People, where the herds winter and the bands take them. About 90,000 people hunt it, all of them in bands, and none of them stays in one place for more than a season.

## The Land

The plain rises westward from the [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]] toward the mountains, and the higher it rises the shorter and drier the grass becomes. Rain is scarce, and most of it falls in hard summer storms that fill the coulees for an afternoon and leave them dry by evening. The rivers that cross the plain run east out of the mountains in deep, narrow valleys, cut below the level of the grass, and a band crossing the plain walks from one of them to the next.

Winter is the plain's danger and its gift. The wind off the mountains blows the snow from the high grass and leaves it bare, so that the herds come west to graze it through the cold months, and the warm wind that comes down the mountains in midwinter can melt a foot of snow in a night. It can also turn and freeze. A band caught in the open by a blizzard out of the north loses its old people and its dogs, and every band winters in a river valley, in the shelter of the cottonwoods, close to the herds and out of the wind.

## The Drive Lanes

The cairns (_wehi_) are what give the plain its name. They run in pairs of lines across the grass toward the drops along the river valleys, where the plain ends at a cliff, a cutbank or a steep coulee, and a herd running between them cannot see the edge until it is over it. Some lanes run for five miles, with hundreds of cairns along each line. Nobody knows who built the first of them. Every band that uses a lane restacks its fallen cairns each autumn, and nobody builds a new lane, because the old ones are where the herds go.

The greatest of the drops is [[place-kuntontoshe|Kunton Toshe]], Thunder Cliff, at the head of a river valley in the foothills, where four lanes converge on a single sandstone cliff and several bands drive together each autumn. Below it the bone bed is deeper than a man is tall, and the camp where the women butcher and dry the meat holds more people for a month than any village on the river.

## The Breathing Hole

In the low pine-covered hills along the western edge of the plain, under the foothills, is the [Breathing Hole]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, a small opening in the rock at the foot of a ridge from which a wind blows out in winter, warm enough to melt the snow around it, and into which the wind blows in summer. The bands say the people and the herds came up out of it into the light, and the oldest songs of the [[lore-nipanheshehin|Earth's Breath]] say it is the earth breathing. Bands camp near it on their way north and south and leave tobacco, robes and painted sticks at its mouth. Nobody goes far inside.

## The Summer Gathering

In early summer the bands of the Cairn Plain gather for the summer gathering (_hekawa_) at the [Cottonwood Bend]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a wide meadow inside a loop of the largest river of the plain, where one great cottonwood stands alone above the grass. The camp circle there is a mile around, with every band in its place and the door of every lodge facing the sunrise, and the pledge dance is held around a pole cut from a younger cottonwood within sight of the old one. The bands choose their hunt chiefs for the autumn here, settle the year's quarrels, and arrange which bands will drive together at Thunder Cliff.

## Borders and Neighbors

West, the plain ends at the foothills and the mountains, and the passes beyond lead to the [[place-potontayuntorgn|Root Fields]] and the western bands. East, it slopes down to the Village River and its villages, where the bands go each autumn to trade. North, the grass thins into the aspen parkland and the forest of the Spruce People. South, the plain runs on, drier and drier, to the mesa and canyon country of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]], and the southern bands go there to trade robes for cotton cloth and turquoise.

## What Moves Through It

The herds move through it first, and the bands follow them. Meat, fat, robes and hides go east to the villages each autumn. Mountain sheep horn, obsidian and dried roots come over the passes from the western bands. Robes and pemmican go south to the Turquoise People, and once in a while a southern good comes back: a macaw feather, a copper bell, a piece of turquoise strung on a society head's necklace.
