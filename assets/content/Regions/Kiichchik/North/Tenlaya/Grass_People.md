## Overview

The Tenlaya are the Grass People, about 300,000 people on the plains at the center of the north and in the dry country beyond the western mountains. They take their name from the grass (_tenla_), which feeds the herds that feed them. The south calls them _P'enqal Ch'akal_, after their grassland, and its traders the _Ts'okum Ts'omalil_, the Coyote People, after the coyotes painted on the shields of one of their warrior societies. Their ways are set out in the [[lore-tenlayaclt|Tenlaya]] culture note, and their tongue is the [[skill-tenlayalng|Tenlaya Language]].

The Grass People live two ways. The hunting bands (_chayu_) follow the bison herds on foot, with dogs to drag their lodge poles and goods on travois, and live in hide lodges (_keyan_) they strike and carry. The villages (_hesun_) stand on the river terraces, clusters of round earth lodges (_moshen_) inside a ditch and a palisade, above fields of corn, beans, squash and sunflowers on the bottoms. The two need each other: the villages trade corn for the bands' meat and robes, and every autumn the bands come in to trade. The bargain is old and often bitter, and it is kept by the peace of the [Corn Pipe]{.name data-type="miscgear"}, made after the bands burned a village of their own people in a hungry winter.

The nation holds four countries. The [[place-hesunshewanrgn|Village River]] is the valley of the villages, with [[place-tansheyawa|Tanshe Yawa]], Bison Ford, in its middle reach, [[place-kopenmokin|Kopen Mokin]], Flint Hill, upstream at the flint pits, and [[place-henushewan|Henu Shewan]], the Forks, downstream where the [[affiliation-tokchiaska|One Fire]]'s raids come. The [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]] is the high western grass of the drive lanes, where the bands drive the herds over [[place-kuntontoshe|Kunton Toshe]], Thunder Cliff, each autumn. The [[place-watotenlargn|Long Grass]] is the wet eastern prairie of the raiding bands, with the [Red Quarry]{.name data-type="place/site"} in its north-east. The [[place-potontayuntorgn|Root Fields]] lie beyond the mountains, where the western bands dig roots and take salmon at [[place-hiyespito|Hiye Spito]], Falling Water. Their faith is [[lore-nipanheshehin|Earth's Breath]].

No one on the plains rides. The bands take the herds by driving them, and the autumn drive (_yuchan_) is the greatest work of their year. Whole bands spend days easing a herd into a lane of stone cairns, then rise from behind the cairns and stampede it over a cliff or into a pound of logs. A good drive feeds a band through the winter; a herd started early scatters, and the band goes hungry.

## Who Leads

Leadership on the plains lasts as long as the work. A band's council of respected men and women names a hunt chief (_yuchanka_) for the season, and while a herd is being steered his word is law; when the season ends, so does his authority. A war leader (_yashenka_) leads a raid only while it is out, and his standing rises or falls with what it brings home. The council settles everything else, by talk and by gifts.

A village is governed by its council and by its lodge owners (_moshenka_), the women who raised its earth lodges and own its fields and cache pits. They decide who eats in a hard winter. Each village chooses a village speaker (_hoyanka_) to bargain with the bands at the autumn trading, and the price he sets in corn is the one the lodge owners will stand behind.

The warrior societies (_yewan_) hold the drives together. Each is a fellowship of proven fighters with its own rites, songs and regalia, led by a society head (_yewan potun_), and during a drive one society polices the camp. A hunter who goes out alone and starts the herd has his bow broken and his lodge cover cut, whatever his standing; if he takes it quietly, the society gives him new ones when the drive is over. The same societies guard the camps, scout ahead of a moving band and lead the raids.

Below the councils stand the keepers. A bundle keeper (_chekanka_) holds a band's or a village's holy bundle and its songs, and a drive does not start against his word. A year-robe keeper (_keshunka_) paints a picture for each winter on the band's robe and recites its history in order. Neither commands anyone, and every council listens to both.

## How Leaders Are Chosen

Nobody is born to an office, and nobody keeps one by right. A person rises by having a name (_chitenka_): when a family holds a giveaway for one of its own, a speaker recites what that person has done, the coups, the drives, the robes given away, the lodge raised, the robes quilled, and from then on the person may sit in council and speak there. A band's council is everyone with a name who chooses to come, and it decides by talking until nobody is left who objects. A person who still objects at the end may take his lodge to another band, and often does.

The council names the hunt chief (_yuchanka_) each summer, at the gathering, from the men who have led drives before and whose drives went well. A war leader (_yashenka_) is not named at all. He announces a raid, offers a pipe, and the young men who smoke it are his party, so a war leader nobody follows is not one. A society head (_yewan potun_) is chosen by the society's own members from among themselves, when the old head dies or gives the office up, and he gives a feast for the whole camp in thanks.

In the villages the offices pass through the women. An earth lodge, its fields, its cache pits and its stretch of flint bluff pass from a lodge owner (_moshenka_) to her daughter, or to her sister's daughter if she has none. The lodge owners of a village choose its village speaker (_hoyanka_) from the men of the village who have kin in the bands, because a speaker the bands do not trust cannot bargain with them. A bundle keeper (_chekanka_) chooses a successor, who pays the keeper for the bundle, songs and all, over years.

## Keeping the Law

Most of the law is kept by teasing. A man who boasts, hoards or bullies is mocked to his face by his joking relatives until he stops, and the whole camp hears it. Above that, the councils settle quarrels with gifts, and a killing among the people is settled with robes and a pipe sent to the dead person's kin.

The warrior societies (_yewan_) are the only force on the plains, and they act only for the drive, the camp and the trading. While a herd is being steered, the society on duty breaks the bow and cuts the lodge cover of anyone who starts it early. While a band is on the march, it keeps the stragglers up and the young men from going off on their own. During the autumn trading, a society from the bands sets its shields against the post at Tanshe Yawa and keeps the peace of the trading camp. Every morning the crier (_pichishika_) walks the camp and calls out what the council decided, and nobody can say he did not hear.

The worst a band can do to anyone is to make him an outcast (_tewiya_). It takes the whole council, and a family will spend everything it has in gifts to keep it from happening to one of its own.

## The Bands and the Villages Together

No band or village commands another. The bands of a region come together every summer at the summer gathering (_hekawa_), where the societies dance, marriages are made and quarrels between bands are settled. The villages keep their ties by marriage, by trade and by the bands, which move between them. When an enemy comes, the societies of the nearest bands answer, and the villages draw in behind their ditches.

In the [[affiliation-redstncmpct|Red Stone Compact]], the Grass People send whichever war leaders and society heads the bands agree on. They are the compact's scouts and its fastest fighters, and they have the least patience of any nation with the settled nations' talk; their delegates leave the council early every summer. The Red Quarry lies where their grass meets the Maple People's forests and the One Fire's valley, and the bands remember [Kanwesawi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the war leader killed on its ground by a One Fire noble, longer than the One Fire would like.

The delegates are chosen at the summer gatherings of the [[place-watotenlargn|Long Grass]] and the [[place-wehioshtergn|Cairn Plain]], one or two from each, and they walk to the quarry together. They can promise nothing for anyone. When a Pipe Bearer of the compact comes to the plains, his pipe is carried from gathering to gathering, and each band smokes it or does not. Three years ago, when a southern war party was seen at the edge of the desert, the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] sent their pipe. The bands of the Cairn Plain smoked it and sent warriors, two hundred of them, who walked south for twenty days and reached the Turquoise People's towns after the war party had turned back into the desert. The bands tell this as proof that they keep their word. The Turquoise People tell it as proof that the plains are too far away to help.

## Raiders

Raiding is how a young man of the plains makes a name, and a raiding party on foot may walk for a month. Parties cross the grass to take food, robes, captives and goods from the One Fire's western towns, the Maple People's western villages and other bands of their own people. A captive child is usually adopted. The other nations of the compact value the Grass People as scouts and fighters who move faster than any town can muster, and complain of them as neighbors. The towns they raid strike back at the river villages, which cannot move, and the villages' lodge owners say so at every autumn trading.

## This Year

Three troubles press on the Grass People at once. Last winter the One Fire burned a third of the lodges of the Forks and took thirty-one of its people, in answer to the Coyote band's raids, and the lodge owners of the Forks have said that they will not open their cache pits to the Coyote band at the autumn trading; the bundle keeper of Tanshe Yawa, who keeps the Corn Pipe, has not said whether the pipe allows it. The herds have been thin on the Cairn Plain for three summers, and its bands are hungry while the herds feed the raiders of the Long Grass. At Thunder Cliff, where half of last year's kill rotted below the drop, the bundle keeper of the Cairn band will not open the bundle for this autumn's drive until the bands give away what they wasted.

## Trade

Meat, fat, robes and hides go from the bands to the villages and on to the other nations; corn, beans, squash, sunflower cakes and the tobacco the village old men grow for every pipe on the plains go back. The bands trade with the Turquoise People for cotton cloth, turquoise and pottery, and through them reach the occasional southern good, a macaw feather or a copper bell, which a war leader wears and then gives away. Over the western mountains the bands trade robes to the Cedar People's summer traders for shell and dried salmon. Southern steel almost never reaches the plains.
