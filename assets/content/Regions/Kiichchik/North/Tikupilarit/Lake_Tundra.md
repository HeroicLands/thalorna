# terran_analog: the Yukon-Kuskokwim delta of western Alaska, with its thousands of lakes and its river villages, rising inland to the spruce forest of the Athabaskan country
---

## Overview

The Lake Tundra (_taluq napiq_) is the hinterland of the [[place-tikumanikrgn|Ice Coast]], a delta as wide as a small kingdom where two great rivers come down to the sea through hundreds of channels. It is the country of the river villages of the [[affiliation-tikupilarit|Tikupilarit]], the Ice-Edge People, in [[place-northkiichchik|Northern Ki'ich'chik]]. About 18,000 people live on it in some eighty villages, all of them on the riverbanks, because the river is the only road.

## Geography

The land is flat to the edge of sight and more water than ground. Lakes and ponds lie everywhere, thousands of them, joined by sloughs and channels, between low banks of tussock and sedge with dwarf willow along the water. A hunter in a kayak can cross the delta for days without leaving the water. In summer the ground thaws a hand's depth over the frozen layer beneath and turns to bog, and the mosquitoes come up from it in clouds. In winter the whole delta freezes and becomes a road for sleds.

The delta is rich in what comes and goes. Each spring the birds arrive from the south in numbers that darken the sky, geese, swans, ducks and cranes, and nest on every pond, and the villages eat eggs and birds for a month. In summer the salmon run up the rivers, and the villages catch and dry them at their weirs (_taqva_), fences of stakes and willow across the channels. In autumn the whitefish come, and in winter the people fish through the ice with traps for blackfish in the ponds. Seals follow the fish up the river mouths in spring, so that a river village hunts seals as the coast does. On the higher ground at the delta's edge, caribou cross in herds in spring and autumn.

Inland the land rises slowly. A few days' paddle up either river the willows grow taller, the first spruce stand along the banks, and the forest begins. The forest belongs to the hunters of the interior, who come down the rivers in spring to trade.

## Villages

A river village stands on the highest bank of its channel, a men's house and a ring of sod houses, with fish racks along the water and a weir across the channel below it. In summer most families move to fish camps along the river and come back when the ice forms. The best known is [[place-taqvamiq|Taqvamiq]], Weir Place, on a channel of the northern river, home of the woman the coast names when it says what a woman should be.

## Trade

The river villages trade dried fish, bird skins, caribou hides and grass mats down to the coast at the Messenger Feast, for seal oil, ivory and walrus hide, without which a river household cannot light its lamps or make its boat lines. In spring the forest hunters bring caribou hides, wolverine and marten fur and birch bark up to the edge of the tundra and trade them for oil and dried fish. Neither side speaks the other's tongue, and the trade is done with signs.
