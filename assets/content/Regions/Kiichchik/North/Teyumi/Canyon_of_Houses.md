## Overview

The Canyon of Houses (Hosa Oyala) is the country every town of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] calls its mother. It is a shallow canyon a long morning's walk from end to end, cut into the middle of a wide, dry basin of sage and grass at the heart of the [[place-teyumirgn|Teyumi Region]], and a dozen great houses stand along its floor and on its rims, more than anywhere else in the north. About 25,000 people live in the canyon and the basin around it and in the turquoise hills to the north. The canyon itself feeds fewer than it holds, and the rest of the north keeps it fed.

## Geography

The canyon's walls are buff sandstone, low enough to climb in most places, and its floor is a flat of sandy fields beside a wash that runs only after a storm. The great houses stand along the north wall, where the winter sun warms them. [[place-yaahosa|Yaahosa]], the Grandmother House, is the largest, and [[place-hepuhosa|Hepuhosa]], the Door House, faces it across the wash. Between them and around them stand smaller houses, chambers sunk into the open ground, and the great mounds of broken pottery from a hundred feasts.

The basin around the canyon is high, flat and open, cold in winter and hot in summer, with little in it but sage, saltbush, juniper on the rises and the straight roads (_ponu_). The roads leave the canyon in every direction, wider than any footpath needs to be, edged with low stone curbs and running straight over everything, so that a road meeting a cliff climbs it by a stairway cut into the rock. On the rises along them stand the signal stations, a ring of stone and a heap of ready wood.

North of the canyon rise the bare, pale turquoise hills, where the camp of [[place-teyupotama|Teyu Potama]] digs the stone.

## Living in the Canyon

The canyon is poor farming country, and everyone in it knows it. The fields on its floor flood after a storm and dry out between storms, and a check dam can only hold so much. The houses of the canyon are fed from three sources: their own fields, the corn the pilgrims carry in at the feast, and the work parties of the outlying towns, who come to build, dig turquoise and keep the roads, and bring their own food. A good harvest across the north fills the canyon's storerooms. A bad one empties them first.

The towns of the canyon keep the oldest rites, the oldest songs and the oldest dead, and the rest of the north defers to them on those things. They do not defer on anything else. A rain chief of a canyon house who tries to tell an outlying town how to plant is answered politely and ignored.

## The Feast

Each autumn after the harvest the roads fill with pilgrims walking into the canyon from every town, carrying corn, pots, cotton cloth and turquoise. For the days of the feast the canyon holds some twelve thousand people, camped along the wash in brush shelters, and the great houses feed them. The towns bring their quarrels, the rain chiefs and house heads of distant towns meet, marriages are arranged between clans who see each other only here, and the turns at the turquoise hills are named for the coming year. On the last day the pilgrims climb the mounds and break their pots.

This year's feast carries a quarrel the whole north is talking about. The rain chief of the Grandmother House has asked for a [Dry Search]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} at the feast, the first since the Year of Shut Doors, and the house head of the Door House has answered by feeding every hungry family that reaches her.
