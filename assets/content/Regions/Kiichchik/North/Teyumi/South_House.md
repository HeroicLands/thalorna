## Overview

A southern trader who survives the desert crossing comes up a dry wash, sees a wall of stone rooms four stories high against the canyon cliff, and has arrived. Kwatuhosa, the South House, is the southernmost great house of the [[affiliation-teyumi|Teyumi]], the Turquoise People, and the first on the routes out of [[place-chakultzklrgn|Ch'akul Tsa'kul]]. About 1,000 people live in it and in the smaller houses on the canyon floor around it. The south counts it as the seat of the whole nation, and calls it by the nation's name, _Ishtem Tsul_; the Turquoise People count it as one town among many, and the one that stands closest to the south.

## The House

The great house is a single block of several hundred rooms, terraced down toward a walled plaza. Its outer wall has no door at ground level, and its rooms are entered by ladders through their roofs. Its storerooms hold the canyon's corn. Round chambers are sunk into the plaza floor, entered by ladders through their roofs, and a straight road leaves the plaza northward over the mesa toward the canyons of the other great houses. Cages along the plaza wall hold scarlet macaws, kept for the feathers that the cloud dancers wear in the rain dances, and the screaming of the birds is the first sound a trader hears from the wash.

On the mesa above the house a signal fire stands ready. When a southern war party is sighted at the desert's edge, it is lit, and the next mesa answers within the hour.

## The Trade

The Keeper of the South Road (_kwatuyo_) lives here, a man of the house's great-house kin. Every trader and pilgrim out of the desert is brought to him, fed once, and asked what he carries. The Keeper decides what goes north and at what price. Cacao, macaw feathers and copper bells go north from this house, and turquoise goes south. The Keeper speaks enough of the southern tongue to bargain in it, and the traders learn only the few words of the Teyumi Language that the house teaches them.

What the traders say of the south is carried north along the roads, and the house hears first when a southern expedition is on the way. The other towns say that the South House has heard the south for so long that it has begun to sound like it. The house's own people say that somebody has to listen.

This winter the Keeper's monopoly has a crack in it. A southern trader came up out of the desert by a western route to [[place-kenuvahosa|Kenuvahosa]], the Canal House on the lowland canals, sold his cacao and copper bells there, and went home with turquoise without ever coming to the South House. The Keeper has sent a runner (_ponuyo_) to the Canal House to ask why, and the canal chief has answered that a guest who comes to the door is fed.
