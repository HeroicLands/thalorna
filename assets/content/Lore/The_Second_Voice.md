The **Second Voice** is the most dangerous object known to have existed on Thalorna. It has been missing
for a very long time, and the two peoples who know what it is have spent that time not discussing it.

The [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] call it **Vrekhal**. The [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] do not name it aloud.

## Where It Came From

Unknown, and the silence on the point is total.

The [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] knew of it before Khazártúrn—that much is established, because their
failure to mention it is half the Khazári case against them. They have never said who made it, how it
was found, how long they had known, or what they understood it to be. They have equally never given the
renegade's name, said what became of him, or offered any account of what drove him.

Everything below is therefore reconstruction: what the relic evidently did, inferred from what it
demonstrably achieved.

## What It Does

A [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] tribe is held together by its Ahk. The spawner's chemical suppression keeps
rival proto-Ahks barren, and its authority over its own spawn is not obedience but something closer to
physiology. This is also the single constraint that keeps the Grukar a nuisance rather than a
catastrophe: **Ahks do not cooperate.** They cannot. Two tribes cannot combine, and so the Grukar have
never in their history fielded anything that could be called an army.

The Second Voice removes the constraint. It does not persuade Ahks or ally them. It **speaks over them**—reaching the hatched directly and displacing the spawner's claim, so that Grukar of any number of
tribes answer one will instead of many.

The result is not an alliance. It is a single mind operating tens of thousands of bodies that do not
tire, do not question, and were bred to fight.

What it does to the tribes themselves is worse than what it does to their victims. An Ahk cannot move
and cannot follow. It experiences its entire spawn walking away from it and does not stop existing
afterward.

## Khazártúrn

In **2427 BF** the Second Voice was taken up by a **Sinalë outlaw**, and used to destroy
[[lore-khazarturn|Khazártúrn]]—the greatest city the Khazári ever built, and at that date the greatest
work of any people on Thalorna.

It was not a war and not a siege. It was a single act of genocide against a city of thirty thousand,
carried out by an army that should not have been able to exist, and **it killed everyone**. The Khazári
afterward laid their dead in their own chambers, cleaned the city, and sealed it as a tomb.

## Why

He was not mad, which is the part his own people found hardest.

Khazártúrn could not feed itself—no hold can—and feeding a city of that size meant manufacturing an
agricultural hinterland around it and expanding it for twenty-seven centuries: forests cleared, bogs drained,
rivers diverted, whole uplands terraced and settled. It was the largest sustained alteration of the
living world anyone on Thalorna had ever attempted.

To a Sinalë, that is not engineering. It is a stone thing eating the world to keep itself alive, and
getting larger every century.

He was, narrowly, **not wrong about what was happening**. That is why his people's condemnation of him
carried an edge they could not put down: a good many Sinalë shared his diagnosis while being appalled by
his remedy, and that is not a thing one can say to the bereaved.

## The Sundering

The Khazári have never forgiven the Sinalë for Khazártúrn, and their case is not the simple one.

**The Sinalë knew the Second Voice existed and said nothing.** Not from malice—from indifference. A
weapon that commands Grukar is a weapon against cities, and the Sinalë do not build cities, so it did
not concern them. That unconcern was itself a failure to take another people's way of living seriously
enough to imagine what it had at stake, and it is the same blindness that later let one of their own
decide a Khazári city did not much signify.

**They dealt with him themselves.** It took a long time—an immortal with a Grukar army is not
concluded quickly—and the Khazári spent that time watching them not manage it. When it was finished
the Sinalë handed over nothing, explained nothing, and offered no accounting of any kind.

Both positions are defensible. _Your people knew, your people did it, and your people settled it in
private._ Against: _one outlaw, whom we destroyed; we owed you no reckoning._ Neither survives contact
with the other, and the two elder races have had effectively nothing to do with one another since.

It is the most closely guarded secret of both peoples, and each has its own reason. The Khazári guard
it because it is their humiliation. The Sinalë guard it because one of theirs did it, and because of
what they failed to say beforehand.

## The Crossing

The Sinalë recovered the Second Voice, and eventually the two peoples agreed the only thing they have
ever agreed since: it would be **held in trust on [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], by both races together**—the one place in the world where Sinalë and Khazári still shared a country, and therefore the only
ground on which neither would be trusted to hold it alone.

A convoy of **six ships** sailed from [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] carrying it.

It never arrived.

There was no storm of any note. No wreckage came ashore, then or since; no spar, no cargo, no body, on
any coast, in any year. Six hulls and every soul aboard them went off the water between Élavendre and
Aelwyth and left nothing whatever behind.

The crews and the escort were **above reproach**—chosen for exactly that, by people who understood
precisely what they were entrusting and to whom. This is the detail that has troubled everyone who has
ever been told it. It forecloses the comfortable explanation without offering another.

## Where It Is

Nobody knows, and both peoples have been looking for a very long time.

The possibilities are all bad. It is on the sea floor, in which case the sea floor is a place people
occasionally reach. It is on a shore somewhere, in the hands of someone who does not know what they
have. Or it is in the hands of someone who does.

The Khazári assume the worst, and their doctrine follows from it: **Grukar are ammunition.** The only
defense against a weapon that may still exist is that there is nothing within reach of a hold for it to
pick up. They kill Grukar on sight, everywhere, without exception—knowing perfectly well that the
Grukar were the Second Voice's first victims and chose none of it.
