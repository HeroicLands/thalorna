# terran_analog: Tarûzeli reads an Akkadian-and-Phoenician register of a Semitic maritime trade tongue. The roots coined here are made to the rules and are not the Akkadian, Phoenician, Arabic or Hebrew word for their meaning, and every name in the register is built from them.

A harbor notary at [[affiliation-yamuzel|Yamûzel]] greets every new clerk with the same exercise: she reads a contract aloud and asks which word in it is the price. None of them is. In Tarûzeli the price is a _pashînû_, a fair price, and that is only what two people agree to leave the table with; the contract rests on _mardû_, the credit of the names that sign it, and on the _qatûsh_, the holding-fast that each of them swears to. Everything a newcomer needs to know about the language of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Tarûzelian States]] is in that exercise: [[skill-taruzelilng|Tarûzeli]] is a tongue of bargains, and its words are built to say who holds what.

A Tarûzeli root names an act, and an ending says who stands in it. _Telm-_ is to bind in writing; _telmum_ is the notary who writes the bond, and _telmûsh_ is the contract itself. The same five endings are laid on every root, which is why a clerk who has met a word once can read its relatives, and why a stranger who learns the endings reads half a harbor sign on the first day. This page is the word-hoard of those roots: the stems the quay uses, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in the Tarûzelian lands.

The sound of Tarûzeli is rolling and full-voiced. Vowels come short or long, the long ones written with a circumflex, and a consonant doubled inside a word is held and pressed: _Sharrûsh_, _Zattûya_, _Ilammûnî_. Stress falls on the second-last syllable, and a word never opens on two consonants.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its _-am_ or _-ûsh_ form. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stem and ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Building words

Two moves make every built word on this page. **An ending on a stem:** the stem is bound, and each ending says what part the stem's act plays. **A name-ending on a root:** a person's name is a root with an ending the tongue keeps for names, and the family name is a root with a lineage ending; the endings stand under Making names.

## Words

### The bargain and the word

A Tarûzelian price is the end of a conversation, and the words show it. The seller opens with a _bashîmûsh_ that nobody expects to stand; the buyer answers with a _nibûrûsh_, a refusal that opens the real talk; and a price is settled only when both sides leave the table with a _pashînû_, a fair one. What holds the result is a name: _mardû_, the credit of the person who gave his word. A hired notary writes the _telmûsh_, but the bond a Tarûzeli trusts is the _qatûsh_, the thing a person holds fast to, and a guarantor is a _rumûkum_ who stands for someone else.

### Reckoning, credit and debt

A Tarûzeli cannot speak of money without saying who stands behind it. A debt is a _fusânûsh_ and the debtor a _fusânum_, but the third person in every loan is the _rumûkum_, the surety, because a Tarûzelian house lends against a name more readily than against a pledge. The words for profit and for justice come from the same stem family as the tide: _dammûsh_ is the sea giving and taking back, and _kûrrîm_ is the merchant's word for justice, the return of what is owed. A cheat is not a villain here but a person whose accounts have not come round.

### Hull, tide and knot

Tarûzeli has a noun for every part of a ship that a captain must pay for, and a verb for every motion of the water that a captain must survive. The tide is a _visîzûsh_ and a ship that works with it is _visîzan_; a harbor, _besîkû_, is named for what it does to the swell. A captain who owns his hull is a _jakûdum_, the characteristic figure of the coast, and the word has no more to say of his debts than that he is a person with a hull. The sea itself is _manî_, one word and no endings, because it is the one thing that does not belong to anyone.

### The house, the firm and the family

A Tarûzelian does not separate the family from the firm, and the vocabulary never did. The word _tarûzî_ means the houses of a line and the firms they keep, with no word to tell the two apart; _tarûzûnî_ is the whole company, kin and clerks and hired captains together. A marriage is a _teshûdûsh_, a joining of two houses that is also a merger, and an heir is a _qizîkum_ in the same breath as he is an apprentice and a hostage to the alliance. _Ilammû_, the honor of a house, is spoken of like a balance in the strongroom: something that can be spent, and that a careful house does not spend.

### Law, oath and the Confederation

Tarûzeli law is the custom of the quay set down in writing, and the vocabulary for it comes from the quay. A _dapûmûsh_ is an oath sworn before witnesses who have touched the clasped hands; a _khurûtum_ is a judge, which in most harbors means a retired captain who has settled enough disputes to be believed; a _sazûmûsh_ is a toll, and the person who keeps the gate is the nearest thing the coast has to a tax collector. _Zabûzû_, freedom, is the word the veterans of the war of independence use, and the root means to go unbound, which a Tarûzelian understands as having no debts to the wrong creditor.

### Doing and being

Almost every Tarûzeli verb has two faces. The root with _-am_ is the plain act, done as a matter of course: _qubâsam_, he counts. The same root with _-ûsh_ is the act as it settles, or the thing it leaves behind: _yutâlûsh_, a payment. An adjective is the root with _-an_, so a bargain is judged _latâran_ when it is honest, _lartan_ when it is dear and _temâdan_ when the goods are sound; those three words do most of a trader's judging.

## Making names

**A Tarûzeli person** carries a given name and a family name, the given name first: [[being-zbzmjkdn|Zabûzum Jakûdûnî]] is Zabûzum of the house of the hull. A given name is a root with a personal ending. A man's closes on _-um_, _-ûsh_, _-an_ or _-û_, and the ending carries what the parents hope of him: _Khurûtûsh_ is a dispute weighed and settled, _Tanâdan_ is full of holding fast. A woman's closes on _-a_ or _-ûya_, which run softer and carry fewer doubled consonants: _Belgina_, _Hushâdûya_. A family name is a root with _-î_, _-ûnî_ or _-ûm_, and says whose firm a person belongs to; the root names the trade or the standing the house was founded on, so _Jakûdûnî_ is the company of the hull and _Qizîkûnî_ the company of the inheritance, houses that outlast any man in them. The name is also a credit line: a person's _mardû_ is the worth of the whole name said aloud, and a Tarûzelian who changes either part of it has changed what a creditor can read.

Among friends a given name is cut to its stressed syllable with the vowel held long: _Zabûzum_ is _Bû_. A short name is used of a person already named in full, never in a contract.

**A place** is a root with _-un_, _-ar_, _-el_ or _-an_: _Sazûmun_ is where the toll is levied, _Besîkar_ where the harbor is kept, _Yamûzel_ the ground of the fleet, _Qudûmel_ the ground of arrival. The land itself is _Tarûzel_, the ground of the houses, built as `tarûz-` + `-el`; its people and its tongue take the reader's endings, Tarûzelian and Tarûzeli.

**A house or a body** is named for its founder or its work, with the lineage ending: the _Merâzûnî_, the Confederation's treasury, is the whole company of the ledger, built as `merâz-` + `-ûnî`. The _Vezâkûnî_, the Council of the Guild-Wardens, is the whole company of the warding, `vezâk-` + `-ûnî`, and its seat, the _Vezâkar_, is where the warding is kept; its overseers are the _Pazâsûnî_, the company of the lookouts. The Auric Compact is a banking cartel named in the reader's tongue for the gold it holds.
