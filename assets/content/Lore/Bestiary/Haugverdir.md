Two kinds of hrúmverdir lie in the northern howes, and the first question to ask of one is which kind it is. The goddess raises one kind and sends it against the living; the other keeps its own mound and its own tale, and nobody can tell you ahead of time what it wants.

_Hrúmverdir_, the howe-wardens, is a northern word for [[lore-undead|undead]] put to two uses. Both name a body that moves after death, and both name something touched by [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]], which binds every undead to every other across every belief and culture. The two uses keep their own meanings, and the word does not make one of the other.

## Náhild's Raised Dead

The [[affiliation-nahild|Faith of Náhild]] calls its mindless reanimated corpses hrúmverdir: [[being-damut|damutu]] raised by [[lore-nahilddty|Náhild]]'s dark magic, chiefly from the bodies of the weak, of cowards and of the dishonored. They serve as servants and soldiers in her legions, under the command of her favored [[lore-nagengir|nágengir]]. Náhild draws the power that raises them from [[lore-ginnauga|Ginnauga]], the Eye of the Void, a powerful source of the Shadow.

## The Dead in Their Mounds

In folk speech the word reaches further. Some in [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] call the captain in the ship-grave at [[place-thraldfjord|Thraldfjord]] a hrúmverdir, a warden of his own mound, after a family story of a descendant who entered the howe, took the dead man's sword and fought him in the dark. Such a warden belongs to its own grave and its own tale. Calling it a hrúmverdir does not make it Náhild's servant, does not say how it died, and does not say whether the learned would name it a tereb or a damut.

A presence that haunts a place without a body is a spirit and not one of the hrúmverdir. The [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordheimn]] customs for the restless dead, and the mediation of a local godi, gydja or völva, concern those presences as well as the walking dead.
