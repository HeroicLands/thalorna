On any quay from [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] to [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]], a ship's captain is a _kiromu_, the cargo is _zazuru_, and the price is a _pashin_. None of the three is a word of one people, and the tongue itself is named for the port of Valthári. _Kiromu_ is the Helonic _kýros_ worn down on the docks, and _zazuru_ and _pashin_ are the Tarûzeli _zazûrûsh_ and _pashînû_ with their endings dropped. [[skill-valtarlng|Valtári]], the harbor pidgin, is built this way throughout: a word of [[skill-taruzelilng|Tarûzeli]] or [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]], stripped of the endings that carry its grammar and of the sounds a stranger cannot make, and set down in a sentence that has no grammar to give it. This page is the word-hoard of that pidgin: the core words every port understands, where each came from, the sound rules a borrowed word is bent to, and how a trade name is made.

A Valtári word does not change. _Lokatu_ is to come, to go and to arrive, at any time and for any speaker, and _yakodu_ is one boat or many. What the parent tongues say with endings, the pidgin says with a separate word or leaves to the context: _tapelu_, now, and _olimu_, already, are the nearest it has to tenses, and a plural is the word said twice, _yakodu-yakodu_.

The sound of Valtári is plain and open. It keeps only the sounds that speakers of every tongue on the quay can make, so the rasps, the throat sounds and the long vowels of its parents are gone, and nearly every word closes on a vowel.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited. Valtári words take no endings in speech, so the form is the whole word.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief, and the word of the parent tongue it was taken from, where it is known.
- **Built from** is `—` for a word taken whole. A trade name names the word and the ending it is made of, joined by `+`, and the name is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Building words

**A borrowed word** is bent to the sounds above. Its long vowels are said short. Tarûzeli and Helonic _kh_ and _gh_ become _k_ and _g_, _q_ becomes _k_, _th_ and _dh_ become _t_ and _d_, _ph_ becomes _f_, _j_ becomes _y_, the rounded _ü_ becomes _i_, and _h_ is not said at all. The endings that carry the parent tongue's grammar fall away, and a word left closing on a consonant other than _n_, _l_ or _r_ takes a vowel after it, most often _-u_ in a Tarûzeli word and _-o_ in a Helonic one: the Tarûzeli _jakûdû_, a ship, is _yakodu_, and the Helonic _ánevir_, the wind, is _anevo_. A word in daily use is often clipped further, and the words of the Vylari family, of Nordmal and of the setting's other tongues are bent the same way.

**Two words side by side** make a compound phrase, the second naming what the first is: _bazunu-kerisu_ is a sail-hand, a sailmaker. **A word said twice** is a plural, or an emphasis.

**A trade name** is a word of the quay with one of the endings below. The ending takes the place of the word's closing vowel.

## Making names

**A trade name** is not a birth name. A trader carries the name of his own people at home and a Valtári name on the docks, and the [[skill-valtarlng|language note]] describes how the two are kept apart. The trade name is a word of the quay with a name ending: _Ushador_ is coin with the man's ending, _Ushadela_ the same word with the woman's, and _Bazunan_, sail with the ending for anyone, is carried by men and women alike. The word is chosen for the trade or for the luck it promises, so a sailmaker is often a _Bazunor_ and a moneychanger an _Ushador_. A word that closes on a consonant takes the ending after it: _pashin_, price, gives _Pashinela_.

**An epithet** sets a second pair of words after the trade name, naming the work: _Ushador bazunu-kerisu_ is Ushador the sail-hand, and _Kusadela ulma-dorasu_ is Kusadela the fish-dealer.

**A house, a crew or a trading firm** takes the ending _-ari_: _Yakodari_, the ship-people, is a name for a crew, and _Kubasari_, the reckoners, for a counting-house.
