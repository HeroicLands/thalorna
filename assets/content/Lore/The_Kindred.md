The **Kindred** are the servant spirits of the two primordial Powers of the [[affiliation-firstgods|The First Gods]]. They are the animal spirits, the place spirits, the water spirits, the stone spirits, the hearth spirits—the innumerable minor and intermediate beings who dwell in specific territories and whose presence constitutes the actual texture of Áelendan religious life.

Where the [[affiliation-firstgods#the-bright|Bright]] and the [[affiliation-firstgods#the-dark|Dark]] are great cosmic Powers too vast to receive direct petition, the Kindred are particular. Each has a name (or a title, or an epithet); each has a territory; each has a character; each has specific requirements. A tribe of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] does not maintain an abstract relationship with "nature spirits" as a category. It maintains named, specific, ongoing relationships with the individual Kindred whose territories overlap with the tribe's.

## Character

The Kindred are not gods in the sense that the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían]] deities are gods. They are not transcendent. They do not preside over domains of existence. They are spirits of the world, rooted in particular places or particular living lineages, and their power—which can be considerable—is local and specific.

A gray wolf-spirit who wards a particular ridge can do much on that ridge and very little beyond it. A river-mother whose domain is a specific stretch of a specific river can influence the waters, the fish, the fording, the drowning, and the spiritual life of the valley she runs through—and nothing else. The Kindred's power is great where it is anchored, and absent where it is not.

Kindred are individual beings with individual temperaments. A particular wolf-spirit may be generous and forgiving of human error; another, in an adjacent territory, may be strict and exacting. Áelendan shamen learn, by long apprenticeship, which Kindred the tribe deals with, what each of them requires, how each of them may be approached, and what each of them will not tolerate.

The Kindred are **not worshipped**, in the sense that the gods of the settled pantheons are worshipped. The Áelendan do not raise temples to them, do not pray to them for favor, do not regard them as transcendent beings to whom praise is owed. What the Áelendan do with the Kindred is keep relationships—and those relationships take three broad forms, which the tradition distinguishes carefully:

- **Venerate**—The benevolent Kindred are venerated: honored with offerings, sung of in the tribe's songs, thanked at the seasons for what they have permitted and provided. A good river-mother, a generous grove-keeper, a hearth-spirit who has watched over many generations of the lodge—these are venerated. Most of Áelendan religious life consists of such relationships.
- **Appease**—The severe, exacting, or easily offended Kindred are appeased: their specific demands are met, their protocols are followed without error, their offerings are rendered exactly as tradition requires. A stone-warder who keeps a difficult pass, a threshold-spirit who exacts a price at a crossing, a weather-kin who must be placated before a storm—these are appeased. Appeasement is negotiated, precise, and unforgiving of mistakes.
- **Ward against**—The dangerous, the capricious, and the outright hostile Kindred are warded against: avoided where possible, sung away where not, turned aside with charm, fire, and protective object when they intrude. Old powers of the deep caves, restless dead, certain fae-adjacent Kindred of the thin places, spirits whose enmity a tribe has earned across generations—these are warded against. One does not bargain with what one wards against.

A shaman's craft consists, in large part, of knowing which posture each of the tribe's Kindred requires, and which of the three postures a particular situation calls for.

## Categories

Áelendan tradition does not impose a single rigid taxonomy on the Kindred, but several broad types recur across tribes:

- **Animal-kin**—The spirit-kin of specific animal lineages. Not individual animals, but the ancestral or presiding spirits of a species or pack in a given territory. Wolf-spirits, bear-spirits, eagle-spirits, raven-spirits, stag-spirits, and many others. Hunt-rites are typically addressed to animal-kin.
- **Place-kin**—Spirits whose domain is a specific place rather than a lineage of living things. Grove-keepers of particular old-growth stands. Stone-warders of cairns, ridges, standing stones. Cave-spirits of the deep places. Threshold-spirits of the passes and crossings.
- **Water-kin**—Spirits of rivers, lakes, springs, and falls. Often addressed as "mothers" (the river-mothers) regardless of their specific character. Water-kin are consulted in matters of travel, crossing, drowning, and cleansing.
- **Weather-kin**—Spirits of storm, wind, snow, and fire. More mobile than the other Kindred, and often regarded as less reliable. Addressed in specific circumstances—a storm approaching, a fire to be kindled or quenched—rather than on a standing basis.
- **Hearth-kin**—Spirits of the household and of the tribal lodge. The keepers of the fire, the warders of the threshold, the guardians of sleeping children. Each Áelendan household has its own hearth-kin, and major tribal lodges (such as [[place-eshalshldg|Eshálosha]]) have powerful hearth-kin of long standing—the greatest of them [[lore-tanwenspr|Tanwen]]. Alone among the Kindred they depend on people: a hearth-kin comes into being where people keep returning and thins where they stop, so a permanent village holds one, a seasonal camp holds a thin one that must be woken on arrival, and a night's camp holds none.

A given Kindred may serve the [[affiliation-firstgods#the-bright|Bright]], the [[affiliation-firstgods#the-dark|Dark]], or both. The affiliations are not doctrinal. They are observed—a river-mother whose character is quickening and nurturing is understood to belong to the Bright; a stone-warder who keeps the long silence of a barrow belongs to the Dark. Some Kindred pass between the Powers with the turning of the seasons, which is simply what they are.

## The Named Kindred

Every river, every peak, every cliff, waterfall, grove and crossing has its Kindred. They are not
enumerated, and no shaman claims to know them all—a tribe knows the ones whose territory touches
its own, and that is the whole of anyone's knowledge.

A handful are known across the tribes and are named here. These are the Kindred a shaman is most
likely to hold a standing alliance with, and each is met through its own Spirit Power rather than

## Relationship with the Tribes

Each Áelendan tribe maintains a **pact** with each of the Kindred whose territory overlaps with the tribe's—a standing relationship of offering, ceremony, and restraint. These pacts are not written, because the Áelendan do not write them; they are held in the tribe's oral tradition and transmitted through the shamen.

A pact includes, at minimum: the Kindred's name (or the tribe's name for them), the territory the Kindred holds, the ceremonies the Kindred expects, the offerings the Kindred accepts, the times of the year at which the Kindred must be honored, and the conduct the Kindred forbids. A tribe that keeps its pacts prospers; a tribe that breaks them suffers in specific, attributable ways—a failed hunt, a drowning at a crossing, a stillbirth, a fire that takes a lodge. These outcomes are not regarded by the Áelendan as superstition. They are regarded as consequence.

Pacts are inherited across generations, and the maintenance of them is one of the central obligations of the tribe's shamen. When a tribe moves—through migration, war, or environmental change—new pacts must be formed with the Kindred of the new territory, and this is understood to be perilous work that can take generations to settle.

## Relationship with the Fae

The Áelendan distinguish carefully between the Kindred and the **fae** of the [[lore-faecourts|The Fae Courts]] and the [[place-silvrfrsts|The Silver Forests]]. The Kindred are of the mortal world; they are anchored in specific places; they can be known, named, and bargained with through the established protocols. The fae are something else—beings of the thin places, whose dealings with mortals follow rules the Áelendan understand only in part, and whose Courts lie beyond the ordinary reach of shamanic practice.

That said, the boundary is not absolute. Certain very old Kindred—the stone-warders of the deepest cairns, the river-mothers of the old springs, the grove-keepers of the Silver Forests—are understood by shamen to be fae-adjacent, or to have crossed the boundary long ago, or to have been fae-kin since before the Áelendan came to the land. These Kindred are approached with particular caution, and the protocols for dealing with them overlap with the protocols of the Kindred Pact.

## Outsider Interaction

Outsiders—travelers, traders, Ordo agents—do not have pacts with the Kindred and cannot form them. What outsiders can do is refrain from transgression: not desecrating a sacred site, not hunting what they have not been permitted to hunt, not disturbing what they have been warned not to disturb. Most Áelendan tribes are willing to extend this minimum reciprocity to respectful visitors. Those who transgress discover, sometimes slowly and sometimes quickly, that the Kindred take offense, and that the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] exist to prevent transgressions that the Kindred themselves would punish more harshly.
