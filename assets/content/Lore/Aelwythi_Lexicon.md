Aelwythi is the speech of Aelwyth, plain, direct and quick to take in foreign words, and the setting gives it in the reader's own language, in the older register set out on the [[skill-aelwthlng|Aelwythi Language]] page. Mainlanders call it coarse; Aelwythans hold that its plainness is honesty. Because the words are the reader's own, this page states no sound rules. It states what the island's names are made of: the small stock of elements that place names, given names and bynames are built from, the register of the names in use, and the names that stand outside the pattern.

The elements are the island's own, and the pattern they make is what keeps a new name from sounding as if it came from another coast. A new place, person or family on Aelwyth is built from the elements below, or it is a plain exception and is entered in the last table with the reason.

## How to read the tables

- **Element** is the word a name is built from, set in backticks.
- **Stands** says where in a name the element may stand. `first` opens a name or stands inside it, `last` closes it, `either` does both, and `link` is a joining letter that stands between two parts and never begins or ends a name.
- **Sense** is what the element means.
- **Example** is a registered name built with it.
- **Built from** joins the elements of a name with `+`. The parts, written one after another, spell the name. A letter that falls double where two parts meet is written once, so `hard` + `den` is Harden.

## Making names

**A place name is a compound of what the ground is and what stands on it.** One element says the quality or the owner, a second says the kind of ground: `ash` + `ford` is a crossing by the ash trees, `cold` + `beck` a cold stream, `stony` + `haugh` a stony river meadow. A joining `s` carries ownership, as in Kingshollow.

**A given name is two elements of the old stock set together,** one for the quality and one for the strength it promises: `leof` + `ric`, `ed` + `mund`. A feminine name often closes on `a`, or on `ith` or `rith`.

**A byname is a place, a calling or a descent.** Most are the name of a farm, a spring or a clearing: `chil` + `ton`, `barn` + `well`, `helm` + `s` + `ley`. A name ending in `ing` marks kin of the person it names, and one ending in `ling` marks the small or the young.
