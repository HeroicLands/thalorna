# terran_analog: Helonic reads classical Greek and its daughters read a Greek koine of the eastern trade road and a Romance-like island tongue with an Arabic-shaped loan stratum. The inherited stock is Greek-shaped (sôthira beside sophia, kôshamir beside kosmos) without being the Greek word; the coined roots here are made to the rules and are not the Greek, Turkish, Persian or Arabic word for their meaning. The particles and the article that stood as real Greek, Turkish and Arabic words are re-coined on this page.

A grammarian of the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Panepistēmion]] at [[affiliation-thyrenae|Thyrenae]] takes a merchant's son from [[place-byzaris|Byzaris]] as a pupil, and the first lesson is that the boy already speaks Helonic and has only to find out that he does. _Sôthira_ is wisdom in the academy, in a Byzaris counting-house and in a temple at [[place-bethura|Bethûra]]; what changes from one mouth to the next is how the word is said, and what stands around it. The Helonic family is one written stock and three spoken tongues: [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]], the old prestige speech of the schools and the stage; [[skill-byzarnlng|Byzarian]], the crossroads speech of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]], which clips the stock short and hangs eastern endings on it; and [[skill-bethunlng|Bethuan]], the island speech of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethûa]], which holds the vowels long and keeps two registers of the same grammar, one for the temple and one for the quay.

This is the word-hoard of the three: every stem the family is known to share, what each daughter does to it, the words each daughter has that the others lack, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in the three lands. It is laid out for the table. A Helonic word met in play can be taken apart here, and a name a GM needs for a Byzarian clerk or a Bethûan priestess can be built in a minute.

The three tongues sound nothing alike, which is the first thing a traveler notices and the last thing a grammarian admits:

- **Helonic** is musical and long-voweled. Every vowel comes short or long, the long ones written with a circumflex, and the rasp written _kh_ and its voiced partner _gh_ are the sounds the rest of the west cannot make. Stress falls on the second-last syllable unless an acute says otherwise. _Sôthira_, _kôshamir_, _Kálydrás_.
- **Byzarian** is clipped and percussive. The stops at the ends of its words are let go with a crack, which is why every house name closes on one, and _th_ and _dh_ have flattened to _t_ and _d_. It has a buzzing _z_ and _zh_ the others lack, and in the bazaar it swallows the end of a verb whole: _esh_ for _ésharin_. _Chrysandáhr_, _Siderákit_, _Ghaleníza_.
- **Bethuan** is slow and gliding. Vowels are held and run into one another, _sh_ has softened to _s_, and a word that closes on _-os_ in Helonic closes on a long _-ôs_ here. The feminine register pronounces every _th_ and every case ending; the masculine register drops the article to a breath, _'s_. _Sôtira_, _fótamir_, _Hiérissa_.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in the nominative, a verb in its infinitive, an adjective in its masculine. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stems and suffixes the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the joining and suffix tables make of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.
- **Tongue**, where a table has the column, says which tongue the row belongs to. A table without the column holds Helonic, the parent, and a Helonic word belongs to all three unless a daughter's table says otherwise.

## Building words

Three moves make every built word on this page, and a new word made the same way is as good a word of its tongue as any here.

**An ending on a stem.** The inherited stock keeps its stems bound and gives each a family of endings: _sôth-_ "wise" takes _-ira_ and gives _sôthira_, wisdom, takes _-arin_ and gives _sôtharin_, to hold together, takes _-ir_ and gives _sôthir_, a wise one. A stem is listed in its field with the class `stem`, and the words built on it stand beside it.

**Two words set together.** The first describes the second, and where a vowel meets a vowel the joining table decides what is written: _khrýsh-_ "gold" and _andáhr_ "lord" give _Chrysandáhr_, the gold-lord, a Byzarian man's name. Byzarian compounds freely; Helonic compounds only in poetry and in names.

**A name-ending on a root.** A person's name is a root with an ending the tongue reserves for names, and the daughters differ most here: the same root _Méril-_ is _Mérilos_ in a Helonic school roll, _Mérilash_ on a Byzarian contract and _Mérilia_ in a Bethûan temple register. The endings stand under Making names.

### Joining

Where the first word of a compound closes on a vowel and the second opens on the same vowel, the two are written as one long vowel; every other seam writes both words whole.

### From Helonic to Byzarian

| Step | Change     | Where    |
| ---- | ---------- | -------- |
| 1    | `th` > `t` | anywhere |
| 2    | `dh` > `d` | anywhere |
| 3    | `ph` > `f` | anywhere |
| 4    | `rh` > `r` | anywhere |

Byzarian lost the breathed consonants of the schools and kept the rasps: _th_ and _dh_ became plain _t_ and _d_, _ph_ became the plain _f_ the eastern tongues already had, and the breathed _rh_ that opens an old word became a plain _r_. Everything else the daughter did to the stock it did at the ends of words, where the bazaar swallows a verb's ending and says _esh_ for _ésharin_ and _danîzh_ for _danîzharin_; the clipped forms stand in the word tables as Byzarian words of their own, because the counting-house writes them.

### From Helonic to Bethuan

| Step | Change      | Where                |
| ---- | ----------- | -------------------- |
| 1    | `sh` > `s`  | anywhere             |
| 2    | `zh` > `s`  | anywhere             |
| 3    | `ph` > `f`  | anywhere             |
| 4    | `ss` > `s`  | anywhere             |
| 5    | `os` > `ôs` | at the end of a word |

Bethuan softened every hushing sound to a plain _s_ and kept every breathed one, which is why a hymn in the feminine register is thick with _th_ and empty of _sh_; it took the eastern _f_ as Byzarian did; a doubled _s_ fell together; and a word that closes on _-os_ holds the vowel long, so a Helonic man's name in _-os_ is heard on the island as _-ôs_ and, in a formal register, is recast on a woman's ending. The doubled _s_ is why _Wîssâros_ is written _Wîsâros_ in a Bethûan hand, and a temple roll recasts the name on a woman's ending as _Wîsârîa_. Three more habits are spoken and never written, so they stand in no table: _k_ and _g_ soften toward _ky_ and _gy_ before a front vowel, a short vowel lengthens before a cluster (_Jádhâros_ is said _Jâdhâros_), and a dental _s_ is sometimes heard as _th_ (_Dókhiron_ as _Thàbìron_).

## Words

### Thought, measure and the schools

A Helonic speaker has one word for wisdom and a dozen for ways of being wrong, because the academies spend their days naming errors. The stock the schools built is the stock the daughters inherited whole: a Byzarian factor who has never read a line of philosophy still calls a fair bargain _métharin_, a measure, and a Bethûan priestess who despises the counting-house still begins her judgments with _arkhîdhin_, the first principle.

## Making names

**A Helonic person** carries a given name and a clan name, the given name first: [[being-damsnghlrn|Dámàsûn Ghôlâron]] is Dámàsûn of the clan Ghôlâron. A man's given name closes on _-os_, _-on_, _-ês_ or _-is_; a woman's on _-a_, _-tha_, _-ra_ or _-îa_, and the women's names run longer and richer in vowels, which the Helonic peoples hold to be the graceful shape. A clan name is a root with _-ôs_, _-îdês_, _-ês_ or _-is_, and the root is most often an ancestor's name worn down: the clan Ghânîdês is the line of a Ghân nobody remembers. The names a Helionite child is given are chosen for how they will sound in the assembly, and a name is tested by saying it aloud across a room. Among friends a given name is cut to its first two syllables with the stress kept: _Dámàsûn_ is _Dáma_ at home, and the register lists such a short form where a note gives one.

**A Byzarian** carries a given name and a house name, and the tongue builds the two by different operations so that a clerk never has to ask which is which: a given name is a root with a personal ending and closes on a vowel or a continuant; a house name is a root with one of four house endings, _-ákit_ for the house of a trade, _-íkot_ for the house of a place, _-ídek_ for the house of a forebear and _-zát_ for the house of an eastern founder, and every one of them closes on a stop. [[being-chrysntssdrkt|Chrysántos Siderákit]] is Chrysántos of the iron-house. Three registers of given name are live at once: the old Helonic names in _-os_ and _-ês_ among the merchant dynasties, eastern roots with a Helonic or Byzarian ending among families with eastern marriages, and the hybrid names that are Byzarian proper, a Helonic root with an ending no other tongue has. A merchant known formally as _Chrysandáhr Nomismákit_ is _Chrysa_ in the bazaar: the trade name keeps the first two syllables of the given name and nothing else.

**A Bethûan** is named in the register she lives in. The feminine register gives a woman's name in _-a_, _-ia_ or _-ê_ and keeps every _th_; a priestess's name takes _-issa_, the office-ending that also makes the titles of the realm, and a high priestess alone takes _-ê_. A man's name in the practical register is the Helonic one with the island's sounds, _-os_ held long as _-ôs_; in a formal document the same man is entered under his mother's house. [[being-juthrjmthn|Júthâra Jâmîthôn]] is Júthâra of the house Jâmîthôn. The short name a Bethûan answers to is the given name's first two syllables with the first vowel held long: _Júthâra_ is _Jûtha_ to her sisters.

**A place** of the old stock is a root with _-on_, _-ea_, _-ia_ or _-os_: _Thyrenae_, _Pelagora_, _Kalydria_. A Byzarian town takes the schools' spelling and is most often a compound whose first word describes the second, or a root with _-ion_, the place of it: _Khrýshteíkhos_ is the gold wall, _Argýrteíkhos_ the silver one, _Karvashion_ the caravan-place. A Bethûan town is named in the feminine register, in _-a_: _Bethûra_, _Ashíra_, _Sehéla_.

**An office** is the stem of what the officer does with _-ir_ or _-or_: _árshir_, the one who presides; _strátimor_, the one who commands; _agóranir_, the one who wardens the market. Bethûa makes the same offices with _-issa_: a woman who commands is a _Stratégissa_, a woman who keeps the writ a _Grammatíssa_.
