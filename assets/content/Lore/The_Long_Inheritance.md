Both Elder Races came to [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]] and found humanity already here, living in scattered hunter-gatherer bands, and both decided—separately, and for reasons neither has ever fully explained to a human—to leave humanity to its own becoming. That policy is real. It was sincerely held, it has been maintained ever since, and both peoples believe to this day that they kept it.

They also uplifted us. The two statements are not in conflict, because non-interference is a description of intent and uplift is a description of outcome, and millennia of proximity settle the matter without anyone's permission. Humanity did not become what it is on its own. Neither was it taught. What happened lies between those, and has no clean name; the scholars who admit it at all call it the Long Inheritance.

## Four ways a thing passes

**Taught.** Rare, deliberate, and minimal. The clearest case is writing: the [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]] work-row given in reduced form to the Proto-Pelwar tribes who were [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari subjects]], from which every runic script and ultimately the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]] descends. Even here the Khazari deny that anything was uplifted—what was handed over was tally, mark, measure and oath, the minimum a bound laborer needs, and a means of keeping a count is a tool and not a civilization. Whether that distinction survives contact with what humanity did next is a question the Khazari decline to find interesting.

**Observed.** Most of the material inheritance came this way. Humans in Khazari service watched work being done and copied it—badly at first, then less badly. Human metallurgy, stonecraft, mining, surveying and engineering all show the same shape of history: a technique appearing suddenly and in crude form near a place where Khazari were working, and then improving for a thousand years by human effort alone. Nobody taught it. Nobody stopped it being seen.

**Inferred.** The subtlest of the four and by far the most consequential. A man who has seen a single-span stone bridge stand does not need to be told how it was built; he needs only to know that it can be. Knowing a thing is possible is most of the distance to doing it, and the Elder Races could not prevent humanity from _seeing_ what was possible without withdrawing from the world entirely. Much of what humanity credits to its own genius is genius spent on a problem someone else had already proved solvable.

**Direct.** Across all those millennia, individuals do as they please. A member of the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale Folk]] who taught one student because the student asked well; a Khazari exile who sold what a hold would not have sold; a smith with an heirless craft; a scholar who wanted an idea to survive them; and—not rarely—someone with a purpose of their own who needed a human able to do something humans could not yet do. These transmissions are unauthorized, undocumented, and responsible for a startling share of what a modern mage or engineer takes for granted.

## Two channels

The inheritance divides along the temperaments of the givers. From the **Khazari** came the material world: metal, stone, measure, structure, the letters. From the **Sinale** came the immaterial: song and the forms of poetry, the practice of keeping lore rather than merely remembering it, healing, and above all the idea that magic is a thing that can be _studied_—that the Aura answers to discipline rather than only to accident and terror. Whether any Sinale ever said so to a human is precisely the point in dispute.

The evidence is correspondingly lopsided. Material transmission leaves objects, and objects can be dated. The Sinale half leaves only resemblance—a mode of thought, a shape of practice—and resemblance proves nothing, which is why the Khazari contribution is grudgingly conceded across the learned world while the Sinale one is argued about endlessly and believed mostly by those who have met a Sinale.

## Still open

This is not a closed chapter. The Sinale maintain living relationships with a small number of human peoples—the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] above all, whose [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} tradition is unembarrassed about receiving from something older, and in whose bardic houses songs are still held that no human composed. Marriage, fosterage and long friendship all occur at that edge, rarely and quietly. What passes across it now passes as it always did: mostly by being seen.

In the west the Khazari keep almost nothing of the kind. No human scholar has been invited into a hold within memory, and the Khazari regard the question of what humanity owes them as settled, uninteresting, and closed.

## Tantsou, which did something else entirely

The [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] is the one place on Thalorna where the relationship is neither reverent nor withheld. Tantsou **filed** them. Both Elder Races live inside the imperial caste order, classified by function: most as [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]] artisans, the scholars among them as [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]], officers as [[lore-wohou|Wohou]], a very few as [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]] nobility. They hold the rights and the restrictions of their rank. They are subject to caste law. They accept it pragmatically, keep their own self-understanding inside their enclaves, and go on with their work.

The consequences are peculiar and mostly invisible to the rest of the world. Transmission in Tantsou is **continuous, formal and dull**: a Sinale scholar advises the human elite because advising is what a Tanchei does, and a Khazari engineer designs an imperial fortification because that is his office and he is paid for it. Nothing is smuggled, nothing is inferred from a distance, and nothing needs to be. Five centuries of that produces an imperial engineering and astronomical tradition with Elder Race work threaded through it so thoroughly that no Tantsou scholar could separate the strands, and none has ever seen a reason to try.

It is also, viewed from outside, a startling piece of arrogance. Every other human culture treats the Elder Races as older beings who withhold. Tantsou assigned them a rank in a system designed for humans and expects them to answer to it—and the cosmology that justifies the castes can categorize the Elder Races without being able to explain their magic at all, a gap the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] are aware of and do not raise. The empire's own answer to what humanity owes the Elder Races is that the question is void: they hold rank, they are compensated, the ledger balances. Meanwhile it watches their enclaves closely for any sign of dissent.

Whether this is the healthiest relationship any human polity has managed with the Elder Races, or the most complete failure to understand what they are, is a matter on which reasonable people differ, frequently at length, and never in Tantsou.

## The Kaliharan contrast

[[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] is the exception that clarifies the rule. There humanity was uplifted **deliberately**, by [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]], millennia before either Elder Race set foot on Thalorna, and the Kaliharans know it, say so, and organize their institutions around the fact. Elsewhere the same thing happened by accident and is denied by all parties—the Elder Races because they meant no such thing, and humanity because it would rather have done it alone.

The contrast runs deeper than the admission. Everything in the four modes above is partial: a technique seen and copied, a possibility glimpsed, a debt argued over for millennia. Kalihara was given a civilization entire—language, medicine, governance and a philosophy, arriving complete and with no clumsy ascent behind it—and given it by a people who were not an Elder Race, who were on Thalorna before either of them, and who raised humanity nowhere else on the world. The Ithari left in 7,300 BF; the Kaliharans have been keeping what was handed to them ever since, without ever having been told what most of it is for. Against that, the Long Inheritance is a slow argument between neighbors.

One caution about that comparison, which the learned world is in no position to make. Everything in the paragraph above rests on Kaliharan testimony, and Kaliharan testimony does not travel. The Ithari are named, remembered and accounted for on one island and nowhere else; the works they left in other countries stand there unexplained and unconnected, credited locally to gods, to the elder races, or to nobody. So the Kaliharan exception is not a case the scholars of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] weigh against their own and set aside—it is a case they have never heard, argued over an inheritance they cannot see the whole of. The full account, such as it is, is [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]].

## What it means to say aloud

The argument is politically loaded everywhere it is raised. [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] wishes to be original and finds the whole subject intolerable; the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] refuse the debt outright and take the suggestion of it as an insult; [[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Bardic Colleges]] concede the Sinale inheritance freely and are thought fanciful for it; and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] has an institutional interest in the answer, since a magical tradition that was inherited rather than discovered has a source that might still be consulted.
