The Celestial Order of [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tāntsou]] calls itself the _Téngwou Rong Léi_, the celestial court of a myriad spirits, and every piece of that name is a word a farmer uses: _téng_ is the sky over his field, _wou_ the magistrate's hall, _rong_ the count past counting, _léi_ the breath in his chest. [[skill-tantsoulng|Tāntsou]] builds its names by setting such words side by side, and the empire's castes, gods, bureaus, months and generals are all made this way. This page is the word-hoard of the tongue: the words, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in Tāntsou, and in the Celestial Order.

A Tāntsou word is a single syllable, and a compound reads modifier first: _zha_ is life and _zhò_ is the dragon, so _Zhazhò_, life-dragon, is the dragon-born caste of the Emperor's family. A name or a caste is written as one word; a title, an office or an institution is written as separate words, as in _Téng Gui_, the celestial minister.

The sound of Tāntsou is sung. Every syllable opens on a consonant, rests on an open vowel or a gliding pair of vowels, and may close on a soft _-n_ or _-ng_; no hard stop ends a word. Pitch carries meaning: each syllable has one of four tones, written as a mark on its vowel, and two words that differ only in tone are different words, as _wén_, the centre, and _wèn_, five.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the words the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly those words set side by side.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

### Letters

A syllable is an initial, a final and a tone. The letters below are the initials and the vowels, with the three tone marks each vowel may carry; the Finals table lists the vowel groups, with their optional closing consonant, that a syllable rests on. An initial is always written, so a compound splits at the next consonant that stands before a vowel.

| Letter                       | Kind              | Sounds like                                                                                    | Tongue |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| `b`, `d`, `g`                | consonant         | the plain stops, said with no puff of breath, as in _spin_, _stop_, _skin_; _g_ is always hard | all    |
| `k`, `t`                     | consonant         | the same stops said with a puff of breath, as in _kin_, _tin_                                  | all    |
| `f`, `h`, `l`, `m`, `s`, `y` | consonant         | _fine_, _hat_, _let_, _man_, _sit_, _yes_                                                      | all    |
| `n`                          | consonant         | _no_; also closes a syllable                                                                   | all    |
| `r`                          | consonant         | the _r_ of _rung_, never trilled                                                               | all    |
| `w`                          | consonant         | _we_                                                                                           | all    |
| `ch`, `j`                    | consonant         | _church_, _judge_                                                                              | all    |
| `ts`                         | consonant         | _cats_                                                                                         | all    |
| `sh`, `zh`                   | consonant         | _ship_, and the _s_ of _measure_                                                               | all    |
| `ng`                         | closing consonant | _sing_; it closes a syllable and never opens one                                               | all    |

There is no _v_, _th_, _q_, _x_, _c_ or _z_, and no rounded or central vowel.

A final is a vowel or a gliding pair of vowels, then _n_ or _ng_ or nothing. After _w_ the _u_ that opens _uo_ is dropped, so _w_ and _uo_ are written _wo_. These are the finals a syllable may rest on, written here without a tone mark.

### Tones

Tone is contrastive: the same letters in a different tone are a different word. The mark stands on the _a_, _e_ or _o_ of the final, and when it has none, on its last vowel. A syllable carries one mark at most, and a syllable with no mark is in the low tone.

| Tone       | Mark       | Contour               | Example                      |
| ---------- | ---------- | --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| High level | macron `ā` | held high             | _tān_ celestial, _wō_ sacred |
| Rising     | acute `á`  | from low to high      | _wén_ centre, _léi_ spirit   |
| Falling    | grave `à`  | from high to low      | _wèn_ five, _hò_ fire        |
| Low        | no mark    | low, falling a little | _wou_ court, _ha_ dark       |

Words that differ only in tone:

| Words          | Meanings        |
| -------------- | --------------- |
| _mòu_, _mou_   | wood, green     |
| _nou_, _nòu_   | field, bound    |
| _zhén_, _zhèn_ | boar, snow      |
| _chén_, _chèn_ | emperor, frost  |
| _wén_, _wèn_   | centre, five    |
| _wō_, _wò_     | sacred, warrior |
| _wou_, _wòu_   | court, tortoise |
| _hén_, _hèn_   | chaos, tiger    |

## Building words

One move makes nearly every built word on this page: **two words set side by side, the modifier first.** _Hò_ is fire and _yòu_ is the month, so _Hòyòu_ is the fire month. A handful of words close compounds so often that the [[skill-tantsoulng|language note]] lists them as suffixes: _-tsou_ for a people or group, _-wō_ and _-léi_ for a spirit, _-gui_ for an official, _-gèn_ for a master, _-zhuo_ for a lord, _-yòu_ for a month, _-juì_ for a festival, _-chài_ for a mountain or place, _-wou_ for a court and _-séi_ for a bureau. Each is the plain word, and each is set on its first word unchanged.

Nothing joins or alters the two words, each syllable keeps its own tone, and the tongue marks no plural: _rong_, ten thousand, set before a noun, gives the sense of countless.

## Making names

**A person** of Tāntsou carries the house name first and the given name after it. The house name is a single word, older than any record: [[skill-tantsoulng|Tāntsou]] lists _Tān_, _Wéi_, _Jing_, _Shòu_ and their like. Most people never hold a personal name; one granted for service is a compound of two to four words, a man's drawn from strength and the sky, a woman's from flowers, the moon and the stars. [[lore-personlnms|Personal Names]] gives the grant.

**A god, a caste or a place** is a compound written as one word: _Tānzhò_, the celestial dragon; _Wòhou_, the warrior-nobles; _Jénchài_, the nine mountains. **A title, an office or an institution** keeps its words apart: _Hò Séi_, the fire bureau; _Téng Gui_, the celestial minister.
