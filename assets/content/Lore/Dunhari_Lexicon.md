A caravan that crosses the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] meets two registers of one tongue. At the wells and in the black tents the tribes speak [[skill-dunharlng|Dunhari]] as the poets keep it, and the storm cults name their lineages in it: a _zevrôr_ reads the wind, a _belgôr_ calls the rain. In the walled cities of the [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate of Amradad]] the same words take the polish of the Amradi court, and the Sultan's officers carry them as titles: the _vazhmôr_ who judges a city and the _tâgemôr_ who governs it. This page is the word-hoard of both: the stems, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in the Dunhara and Amradad.

A Dunhari stem names an act or a thing, and an ending says what part it plays. _Vazhm-_ is to weigh a claim; _vazhmôr_ is the one who weighs it by office, the judge. The same handful of endings is laid on every stem, and a place is a stem with the ending that marks ground: _ghavûl-_ is to draw water, and _Ghavûlekh_ is the oasis where the tribes draw it.

The sound of Dunhari is open and flowing. Words open on a vowel or a single consonant, the rasping _kh_ and _gh_ fall anywhere, and long vowels, written with a circumflex, carry the weight of a line of verse. Stress falls on the first syllable or the second-last.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its present. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stem and the ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Building words

One move makes every built word on this page: **an ending on a stem.** The stem is bound, and each ending says what part the stem's act or thing plays. The clan endings _-an_, _-î_ and _-id_ are the ones the [[skill-dunharlng|language note]] gives for tribal names; the others make offices, kinds and places.

### Suffixes

| Suffix | Stem  | Makes                                            | Example                                    |
| ------ | ----- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------ |
| `-ôr`  | added | the one who does it, by office or by calling     | _vazhm-_ > _vazhmôr_, a judge              |
| `-î`   | added | of it, marked by it; a clan of secondary descent | _qeshv-_ > _qeshvî_, lightning-marked      |
| `-esh` | added | a company of them, a following                   | _vaduk-_ > _vadukesh_, the followers       |
| `-ekh` | added | the ground or the seat of it: a place            | _ghavûl-_ > _Ghavûlekh_, the drawing-place |
| `-an`  | added | a man of the clan, a clan of primary descent     | _barmand-_ > _Barmandan_                   |
| `-id`  | added | the descendants of a founder                     | _mazân-_ > _Mazânid_                       |

## Making names

**A Dunhari person** carries a given name and a clan name, the given name first: [[being-trvshshrdr|Torvâsh Shiradar]] is Torvâsh of the Shiradar. The clan name carries the endings the [[skill-dunharlng|language note]] gives, _-an_ for primary descent, _-î_ for a sub-clan and _-id_ for the descendants of a founder. Most given and clan names are older than the stems on this page, and the register shows `—` for them.

**A place** is a stem with _-ekh_, named for the work its first settlers did on the ground: [[place-ghavulekh|Ghavûlekh]], where the tribes draw water at the great well, and [[place-qarvelekh|Qarvelekh]], the walled city.

**An office** is a stem with _-ôr_, written alone: the Sultan's _vazhmôr_ and _tâgemôr_, the storm cults' _zevrôr_ and _rûzhôr_. The great offices of the court and the realm itself are named in the language of these pages.
