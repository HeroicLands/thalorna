A caravan that crosses the [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] meets two registers of one tongue. At the wells and in the black tents the tribes speak [[skill-dunharlng|Dunhari]] as the poets keep it, and the storm cults name their lineages in it: a _zevrogâr_ reads the wind, a _belgogâr_ calls the rain. In the walled cities of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]] the same words take the polish of the Amradi court, and the Shêbaradêr's officers carry them as titles: the _vazhmadêr_ who judges a city and the _tâgemadêr_ who governs it, the _gholvabûn_ who keeps the door. This page is the word-hoard of both: the stems, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in the Dunhara and Amradad.

A Dunhari stem names an act or a thing, and an ending says what part it plays. _Vazhm-_ is to weigh a claim; _vazhmadêr_ is the one who weighs it by office, the judge. The same handful of endings is laid on every stem, and a place is a stem with the ending that marks its kind of ground: _ghavûl-_ is to draw water, and _Ghavûlâvad_ is the oasis where the tribes draw it.

The sound of Dunhari is open and flowing. Words open on a vowel or a single consonant, the rasping _kh_ and _gh_ fall anywhere, and long vowels, written with a circumflex, carry the weight of a line of verse. Stress falls on the first syllable or the second-last.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its present. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stem and the ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Building words

One move makes every built word on this page: **an ending on a stem.** The stem is bound, and each ending says what part the stem's act or thing plays. The clan endings _-an_, _-î_ and _-id_ are the ones the [[skill-dunharlng|language note]] gives for tribal names; the others make offices, callings and places.

### Suffixes

| Suffix    | Stem  | Makes                                            | Example                                        |
| --------- | ----- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| `-adêr`   | added | the holder of an office, by grant                | _vazhm-_ > _vazhmadêr_, a judge                |
| `-abûn`   | added | the keeper of a thing, a door or a household     | _gholv-_ > _gholvabûn_, a chamberlain          |
| `-ogâr`   | added | the one who does it, by calling or rank          | _zevr-_ > _zevrogâr_, a wind-reader            |

| `-âvad`   | added | watered ground: an oasis or a spring             | _ghavûl-_ > _Ghavûlâvad_, the drawing-place    |

## Words

### The Shêbaradêr's court

Every office of the Amradi court is a stem with an ending that says how its holder stands to the act. _-adêr_ makes the one who holds an office by the Shêbaradêr's grant: the judge weighs claims, the governor holds a city in trust, the market inspector tests a measure, and the great officers bear the charges of state and lead the host. _-abûn_ makes the keeper: the registrar who enters a name or a sum in the roll, the chamberlain who keeps the threshold of the Shêbaradêr's rooms, and the principal wife who keeps the inner household. The seat of the court is named for the stem of sitting in state.

## Making names

**A Dunhari person** carries a given name and a clan name, the given name first: [[being-trvshshrdr|Torvâsh Shiradar]] is Torvâsh of the Shiradar. The clan name carries the endings the [[skill-dunharlng|language note]] gives, _-an_ for primary descent, _-î_ for a sub-clan and _-id_ for the descendants of a founder. Most given and clan names are older than the stems on this page, and the register shows `—` for them.

**A place** is a stem with an ending that marks its kind of ground, named for the work its first settlers did there: [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavûlâvad]], the oasis where the tribes draw water at the great well, [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]], the walled city, [[place-kherzulakhem|Kherzûlakhêm]], the hunters' camp, and [[place-twrlghtnmrkd|Qeshvadêz]], the lightning tower.

**An office** is a stem with _-adêr_, _-abûn_ or _-ogâr_, written alone: the Shêbaradêr's _vazhmadêr_ and _gholvabûn_, the storm cults' _zevrogâr_ and _rûzhogâr_. The great offices of the court and the realm itself are named in the language of these pages.
