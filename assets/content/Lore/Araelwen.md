An **Araelwen** is a thin place that the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] hold sacred: a site where the material world and the spirit world lie close enough that things pass between them, tied to the fae, and utterly forbidden to outsiders. The Araelwen are the reason [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] has the reputation it does, and they are the land's deepest mystery. Almost all the known ones lie in its mountains and deep forests, the Áelendan have lived beside them since before the tribes had a name, and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] wants nothing in the west so much as to study them.

Every Araelwen is dangerous. Inside one the ordinary rules of the world are negotiable. The fae are met there rather than elsewhere, [[lore-thekindred|the Kindred]] are close and attentive, and arcane power runs strongest. What people who come back describe is as often about time, distance and memory as about anything a sword would help with.

## Sacred Ground

The mountains and forests of Élavendre are full of places the Áelendan hold sacred, whether or not an Araelwen stands there. [[place-longstair|The Long Stair]] is sacred because the river-mother [[lore-salendaspr|Sálenda]] is seated there, [[place-owlhollow|the Owl Hollow]] because [[lore-ossendaspr|Óssenda]] is, and [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] because its hearth is [[lore-tenaspr|Tena]]'s. The grove-shrines, the mountain sanctuaries and the sacred springs of [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]] are sacred ground, and so is most of [[place-silvrfrsts|the Silver Forests]].

Not every sacred place is a thin place, and the Áelendan keep the two apart. Every Araelwen is sacred, and the thin places are a charge of their own within the sacred country. A thin place left unwatched is the one failure their tradition does not forgive in itself ([[lore-blacksnow|The Black Snow]]).

## The Kinds of Thin Place

No two Araelwen are alike, and the Áelendan sort them by what they do to whoever enters. Some are more than one kind at once.

### Places That Disturb Time

In some Araelwen time does not run as it runs outside. The best known is [[place-ukuaraelwen|Úkua-Araelwen]], a _slow_ place in the high forest of [[place-sathvothe|Satvote]], where a day inside can be a month outside. Others hold time in other ways: in [[place-hldhr|the Held Hour]], high on the Drenavar Spine, the light does not move.

The danger is the reckoning. A party that comes out of a slow place finds its kin older, its debts due and its news stale; one that comes out of another kind finds its food spent and its bodies worn by a time the world outside did not keep. The Áelendan do not camp in a place that disturbs time, and they do not let a sick person or a child be carried into one.

### Doors That Let In the Fae

Most Araelwen are doors. Some open onto the fae, and what comes through them is as various as the fae themselves:

Sentient trees
: trees that wake, move and judge, as in [[place-wkngstnd|the Waking Stand]] of the Silver Forests, and the mobile and hostile flora of Úkua-Araelwen.

Constructs
: things made rather than born, which walk out of [[place-wrghthllw|the Wrought Hollow]] in [[place-lirve|Lirve]]'s deep forest and go back into it.

Sylphs and sirens of the pools
: the singers of still water, met at [[place-sngngtrn|the Singing Tarn]] and in [[place-mallonna|Mallonna]], the deep pool that is a thin place in water.

Living landforms
: ground that is alive, as on [[place-wndrngrdg|the Wandering Ridge]], whose shoulder is never where it was the season before.

The danger is that the fae keep rules of their own, which the Áelendan understand only in part ([[lore-thekindred|The Kindred]]). A traveler who meets one of the fae at an Araelwen has met it on its own threshold.

### Doors That Let In Fell Things

Other Araelwen open onto worse: demons and fell creatures, things that do not belong on this side and do not go back of themselves. The oldest charge in the Áelendan keeping is one of these. [[place-talve|Talve]], the fog-hollow at the edge of the Silver Forests, kept Dhargan's son in the first generation, and in the killing winter of the Black Snow, when the watch was pulled from it, a thing of fog shaped like a stag came through it and killed at the Lodge. [[place-fndr|The Fen Door]] in the coastal forest of [[place-lisere|Lísere]] is another.

The danger is what comes through. A fell thing is turned back at the door or it is not turned back at all, and the Áelendan answer for every one that gets past their watch.

### Araelwen-Water

Some rivers are Araelwen along their length. An **Araelwen-water** runs partly in the material world and partly in the spirit world, and the two courses are not the same river: the banks do not match, and neither does the distance between two points on it.

These are held to be the strongest Araelwen of all. A place is a doorway that stays where it is put; a river is a road with one end in each world, and it goes somewhere.

## The Keeping

The Áelendan hold that they are the guardians of the Araelwen and of the fae and the numinous that cross at them, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] exist for these. The Wardens began as the watch Dhargan set on Talve, and the watch is still the shape of the keeping at a door: two sit by it through the night with spear and dog, turn what comes through by song, fire or stone, retire when it does not turn, and sing from the hall on the nights it wins. A place that disturbs time is kept from outside its bounds, by a watch that turns people away.

**The law.** An Araelwen is forbidden to every outsider, and that is the difference between it and the rest of the sacred country. Warden leave can take a stranger along a sacred road, or into the outer margin of a sacred wood; it never takes him into an Araelwen. A trespasser who blunders in is warned once and escorted out. One who returns, or who desecrates, is dealt with finally, and the Wardens' judgment on the ground is final over the tribal councils, the crown and everyone else. Taking anything out of an Araelwen is desecration, and so is cutting, digging or working the art there.

That authority is recognized by tribal custom and by the crown of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]], and it is older than either: it descends from the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"}, the compact between the Áelendan and the land itself. The royal magistrates do not hear a complaint from anyone hurt at an Araelwen, and their first question in any such case is whether the complainant was warned. The Áelendan recognize no borders, so the law runs wherever an Araelwen does: Úkua-Araelwen straddles the frontier with [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]], and neither crown administers it.

## The Order's Study

The Thordimalon regards the Araelwen as the most interesting arcane phenomena in the west, and nowhere else in the known world is the veil this thin over this much ground. It wants to map them, measure them, sort them into a system, and above all understand what they do: why time slows in one and stops in another, what comes through a door and from where, and how the art behaves where the world is thin. Its theorists hold that a phenomenon is a thing to be studied like any other. Its [[affiliation-sekran|Arcane Inquisition]] (the Sekran) takes a closer and less academic interest.

It goes about it by every road the kingdom leaves open and several it does not:

- **Licensed expeditions** leave from the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} and the chapterhouses, and the crown gives them excellent guides out of [[place-liranel|Liranel]], who lead them through real country to nothing of consequence. The Order knows it, and keeps the arrangement because it keeps a foot in the door.
- **Agents on their own account** work the countryside beyond the guides' routes, buying maps, paying local guides who go past the margins, and camping where they should not.
- **Instruments.** The Order prizes instruments that work in the thin places, such as the [[miscgear-temprlcmpss|Temporal Compass]], which steadies time around its bearer in a slow place. The Wardens count such a device inside an Araelwen as a working, and a working there as desecration.
- **Collections.** Objects that belonged to a grove, a spring or a thin place turn up in chapterhouse collections at [[place-beravel|Péravel]], cataloged and with no provenance recorded. The Order calls this recovery and study. The Áelendan call it theft.

## Guards for Hire

The Order hires mercenaries and guards to protect its scholars from the Áelendan. The work is offered in Péravel and at Liranel, it pays well, and the contract names a scholar to be kept alive and a season to keep him, rarely the ground he means to reach.

For a party the work is an ethical question before it is a fighting one. A guard is paid to stand between a scholar and a Warden who is enforcing a law older than the crown, on ground the crown has sworn to stop short of, and the Wardens warn once. A guard who draws on a Warden at an Araelwen is a desecrator under that law, and no court in Élavendre hears his side. The scholar may want to understand a place in order to protect it, or to take something out of it, and the contract does not say which. The places themselves are dangerous, too: a guard is also hired for what comes through the door, and on that point the scholar and the Warden want the same thing.

## The Friction Now

The study of the Araelwen is what sets the Order and the Áelendan against each other, and the friction runs hotter every year. Order surveyors who go too deep do not always return. Research camps are found abandoned, their notes scattered and their equipment destroyed. The Order deplores the incidents publicly, tallies its losses privately, and sends more agents with more guards. The Áelendan count the kingdom's permission for the Order, more than any single theft, as the offense.

The crown stands between them. It keeps the Order warm inside the walls and guided beyond them, and it keeps the Áelendan content, because its own claim to rule rests on the old blood ([[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]). [[affiliation-groveconcl|The Grove Council]] objects to every Order chapterhouse in the kingdom, and [[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Bardic Colleges]] sing what happens to the men who go in. At court the Royal Loremaster [[being-aelithraenewyn|Áelithra Énevin]], a Mistress of the Order, holds a scroll that places a crystalline artifact, [[miscgear-heartofdhirikri|the Heart of Dirikri]], at the center of Úkua-Araelwen, and she wants a party to bring it out.

## Named Araelwen

[[place-ukuaraelwen|Úkua-Araelwen]]
: a _slow_ Araelwen of deep [Terrae]{.name data-type="lore/arcana"} resonance in the high forest of Satvote, all ancient trees and dryads, whose time runs at its own rate: a day inside can be a month outside. Few who enter come out. Its flora is not merely large but occasionally mobile and hostile.

[[place-talve|Talve]]
: the fog-hollow at the edge of the Silver Forests, a door to fell things, and the oldest watch in the Áelendan keeping.

[[place-mallonna|Mallonna]]
: the deep pool that the Senali Lauva keeps, a thin place in water.

[[place-hldhr|The Held Hour]]
: a corrie high on the Drenavar Spine where the light does not move.

[[place-sngngtrn|The Singing Tarn]]
: a tarn of the Drenavar's high meadows whose sirens sing a traveler into the water.

[[place-wndrngrdg|The Wandering Ridge]]
: a living ridge of the Drenavar Spine that is never where it was.

[[place-wkngstnd|The Waking Stand]]
: a stand of the Silver Forests whose trees wake and move.

[[place-wrghthllw|The Wrought Hollow]]
: a hollow of [[place-siralonke|Siralonke]], Lirve's deep forest, through which made things come and go.

[[place-fndr|The Fen Door]]
: a door to fell things in [[place-olvalonke|Olvalonke]], the coastal forest of Lísere, under a Warden watch far from the mountains.

[[lore-uronspr|The Uron Tekhi]]
: not an Araelwen itself but a Kindred that holds one: the oldest the Áelendan deal with, counted fae-adjacent, and understood to have crossed the boundary long ago or to have been fae-kin from before the tribes came.

## The Name

_Araelwen_ is [[affiliation-theoldway|the Old Way]]'s name for a sacred thin place, and the lowlands say it as the highlands do. It is the same word in the singular and the plural: one Araelwen, and the Araelwen of the Spine. For a thin place of any kind, sacred or not, the Áelendan say _sírnasto_ and the Élavendri _sírnaste_, a word both tongues took from the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] ([[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]]). The Élavendren court, which prefers not to name such things precisely, says _the thin places_.

## Hooks {#araelwen-hooks}

- **The Heart.** [[being-aelithraenewyn|Áelithra Énevin]] wants a party to carry the [[miscgear-temprlcmpss|Temporal Compass]] into Úkua-Araelwen and bring out the Heart of Dirikri, and the Wardens know she wants it.
- **A guard's contract.** A fellow of the University hires guards at Liranel for a season's survey, and the ground he means to reach is an Araelwen. The first Warden's warning comes on the second day.
- **The tutor.** [[being-ielaraskir|Prince Yélaras]]'s old tutor has gone inward from Liranel without the crown's guides, and the prince wants him back before the Wardens find him.
- **The lone watch.** The watch at [[place-fndr|the Fen Door]] is down to one Warden through the winter, and she needs hands she would not otherwise accept.
