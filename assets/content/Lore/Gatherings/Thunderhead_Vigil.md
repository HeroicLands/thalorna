## Overview

On the night of the spring equinox the faithful of [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]], the Thunderer, go up to the high places and wait for him. They climb the hilltops and the towers of the cities, and sailors stand on the decks of ships at anchor, and they keep the whole night out of doors, watching the sky to the west for the first storm of the year. When the first lightning splits the sky the crowd gives a wordless cry, the sound the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] holds to be the right answer to a god returning to the world. The Priest of Storms on the highest place reads the strike, and before morning he announces to the crowd what it means for the year.

The Thunderhead Vigil falls on 1 [Lizelandi]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, the vernal equinox, which opens the year in [[lore-commoncal|the Vilérian Calendar]], and it is the night the storms return to the western part of the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] after the dry season. It lasts one night. Sailors call the year that it opens the sea-year, because no prudent captain puts to sea for a long voyage until the Vigil has been read.

## The Night

The faithful go up at dusk with cloaks and wine and very little else, since the god is met in the open, and a roof between the faithful and the sky is held to be an insult on this night. The storm-priests, the Bravsari, go up with them and stand on the highest point, and the acolytes keep the watches of the night and call the hours. Nobody sleeps. A child of the faithful is taught to keep his eyes on the west and his mouth shut until the light comes.

**The reading** is the point of the night. The priests read the first strike for its direction, its distance, the number of forks in it and the time between the light and the thunder, and they read the first rain after it. A strike that falls on the sea promises a hard sailing year and a strike on land a gentle one. Every port has its own long tradition of what each sign means, and the priests of a port are consulted for its reading all year. A night that passes without any lightning is the worst sign of all: the god has not yet come back, and the sailing waits until he does.

## Where It Is Kept

- **[[place-kurzalora|Kurzalora]]** on the coast of [[place-salkhalon|Salkalon]] holds one of the great Vigils of the empire. The faithful of the whole coast climb to the height above the town and wait on the cliff, the Priest of Storms reads the strike from the top of the Vigil Tower, and for three days before it the town holds five times its people.
- **The Bethuan coast** keeps it at sea. The galleys of [[affiliation-bethuanflt|the Bethuan Fleet]] and the merchant galleys lie at anchor in the bays with their oars shipped, a storm-priest aboard each, and each crew sings [[lore-vigilwatch|The Watch-Song of the Vigil]] through the four watches of the night.
- **The mountain communities of Tarvénia** send their men up onto the peaks for the god's first storm, and the sailors of its coast keep it on deck.

## On the Taruzelian Coast

In the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]] the Vigil opens the year of the voyage. The crews of the harbor cities keep it on the decks of ships riding at anchor, the priests of Brazemas keep it on the towers, and the hill tribes keep it on their own hilltops, where the storm reaches them first. A captain does not take on a cargo for a long voyage until the reading is known, and the reading is cried along the quays at dawn.

The morning after belongs to the harbors. In [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] [[lore-harbordue|the Harbor Due]] waits for the first fair morning after the Vigil, when the First of the Council carries the city's debt out to the head of the breakwater, and no Besikar hull leaves the harbor until it is paid. In [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] the faithful of both rites keep the night in the court of the [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]], at the column carved on one face with the thunderbolt of Brazemas and on the other with the spear of [[lore-bazhramisdty|Bazhrámish]], who rules the storm in [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|the Twelve of the Flame]], and in the morning the temple reads its oldest roll aloud.
