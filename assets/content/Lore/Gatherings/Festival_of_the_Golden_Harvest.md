## Overview

For three days at the end of summer the shops of the Aureldian trading cities shut, the streets fill with musicians, dancers and acrobats, and the wine is poured at street corners for anyone who holds out a cup. The Festival of the Golden Harvest belongs to [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], the Bountiful One, and the harvest it celebrates is the one the ships bring home: the cargoes landed, the profits counted and the voyages that paid. Merchants and craftspeople close their doors to keep it, and the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]] holds it as the most openly joyful feast of the Aureldian year.

It falls on the last three days of [Elthezandi]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}, the gold's month, which is Ólvenia's own, and it ends on the eve of the autumn equinox on 1 Karnavar. The temple year of [[lore-commoncal|the Vilérian Calendar]] runs on the quarter days, so the festival closes the temple's summer. It is the last feast before the autumn sailing grows dangerous.

## The Three Days

**The first day** opens with the procession. The High Priestess of the city's temple of Ólvenia leads her priestesses through the market districts in silk and gold, blessing the stalls opened that season and consecrating the trading companies founded in it, and the procession ends at the temple steps, where she receives the season's tributes. The merchants who have done well bring their [Offering of Gain]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, a portion of the profit made into something beautiful: perfumed oil, a fine garment, golden tokens worked as fruit or coins. Each merchant hopes to give a finer offering than the last, and the temple's acolytes call out each one as it is laid down.

**The second day** belongs to the temple squares. Patrons hire singers and players, and the squares fill with love ballads, comic plays and songs the patrons would not have at their tables. In the evening the wealthy houses hold their feasts, with spiced meats, fresh fruit and honey cakes, and a guest is expected to celebrate without restraint.

**The third day** is the day of reckoning. Partners who sailed together over the season settle their shares, and the goddess's portion comes out first. A priestess of Ólvenia sits on the temple steps to hear any partners who cannot agree, as the temple's priestesses do in disputes between merchants all year, and by long custom her word on the festival's last day is not appealed.

## On the Taruzelian Coast

The coast of the [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel Region]] keeps the festival as a celebration of a voyage that paid. On the second day every harbor plays [[lore-hullstrongroom|The Dispute of the Hull and the Strongroom]] on a cart before Ólvenia's temple, a captain and a clerk trading boasts until the priestess comes down the steps and makes them partners. Each harbor puts its own captains' and houses' names into the boasts. In [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] the dispute is played again before the [Veterans' Quarter]{.name data-type="place/site"}, where a porter climbs onto the cart before the verdict and the priestess does not answer him.

Hospitality at the Golden Harvest is a display of credit, as it is all year on the coast, and a house that feeds forty people well at its feast is telling the harbor something about its liquidity. In [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] the procession sets out from the hall of the [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]], and the merchants lay their offerings at both faces of the column where Ólvenia's golden tree stands back to back with the chalice of [[lore-aravestadty|Aravesta]], the Golden Giver of [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|the Twelve of the Flame]].

## In the South

In the trading cities of the [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia Region]] the Golden Harvest is the summer [[lore-pozerasdty|Pozeras]] the Wayfarer stayed at Ólvenia's table, as [[lore-boughtlonging|How the South Bought Longing]] tells it, which is why the temples of the two gods stand side by side there. Patrons commission singers for their tables, and a divided question such as [[lore-orderqstn|The Divided Question of the Order]] is sung at a patron's table on the second night. The singers who have no patron sing in the temple squares, and [[lore-namelessjar|The Song of the Nameless Jar]] is theirs.
