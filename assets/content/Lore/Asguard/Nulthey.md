The great island of [[place-asguard|Asguard]], lying off the other three regions and reached across water. The name is built as a place name is built, from the ground and nothing else: _nulth-_, the last hour of a watch, in the deep grade the north keeps for what is oldest and furthest down, and the generic _-ey_, an island. What the word says is what the island does.

Náhild holds it, and the dead who come here are the ones the halls did not take: the oath-breaker, the man who turned from a duty he had accepted, the níding put outside the law, and the great mass of the ordinary dead who simply sickened and went without ever being called on for anything. There is no judgment and no sentence pronounced. A man walks up the shore and is not stopped.

What happens then is the whole of the doctrine. The dead on Nulthey diminish—slowly, without pain, and without any moment a man could name as the end of himself—until there is nothing left to diminish. The faith is emphatic that Náhild does not torment them and takes no interest in them whatever; her indifference is the mechanism. Unworthiness is held to be its own slow death, and the island is the place where a life that was owed to nobody finally comes to nothing.

The single counter the tradition offers is the life lived with honor, which is why the north weighs an oath as heavily as it does and why a broken one must be answered in restitution or in blood. The [[affiliation-hel|clergy of Náhild]] teach the island differently, as a mercy and a rest, and their teaching is proscribed in every Asguardian kingdom. The other nine faiths agree on the shore, the water and the diminishing, and disagree with her priests about nothing except whether it is to be wanted.
