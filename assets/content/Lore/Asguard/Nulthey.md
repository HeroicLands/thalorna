The great island of [[place-asguard|Asguard]], lying off the other three regions and reached across water. The name is built as a place name is built, from the ground and nothing else: _nulth-_, the last hour of a watch, in the deep grade the north keeps for what is oldest and furthest down, and the generic _-ey_, an island. What the word says is what the island does.

Náhild holds it, and the dead who come here are the ones the halls did not take: the oath-breaker, the man who turned from a duty he had accepted, the níding put outside the law, and the great mass of the ordinary dead who simply sickened and went without ever being called on for anything. There is no judgment and no sentence pronounced. A man walks up the shore and is not stopped.

What happens then is the whole of the doctrine, and it runs in an order the tellings never vary. The dead arrive, and they wander: aimless, unfulfilled and unhappy, with nothing asked of them, nothing owed to them, and nowhere on the island they are going. Then they fade—slowly, without pain, and without any hour a man could point to as the end of himself—until nothing of them remains.

The wandering is what the fading consists of, and the north's own reckoning of a man is what explains it. [[lore-nordheimnclt|Reputation is the estate]] in the Nordlands, and it is meant literally: a name is added to for as long as a man lives and closed on the day he dies, and a shade on Nulthey stands in the one place where nothing can be added to his. He knows what he did and what he left undone, he can alter neither, and he is present without being able to act on anything. Nobody torments him. Náhild takes no interest in the unworthy whatever, and her indifference is the mechanism: unworthiness is held to be its own slow death, and the island is where a life with nothing in it worth gathering finally comes to nothing.

The single counter the tradition offers is the life lived with honor, which is why the north weighs an oath as heavily as it does and why a broken one must be answered in restitution or in blood. The [[affiliation-nahild|clergy of Náhild]] teach the island differently, as a mercy and a rest, and their teaching is proscribed in every Asguardian kingdom. They also send the dead here by hand: the Walk of the Dead takes the spirits of the fallen courageous, who would otherwise be gathered to [[place-valsal|Valsal]], and turns them onto the shore instead. Robbing a brave man of the hall is the rite's whole purpose. The other nine faiths agree with her priests about the shore, the wandering and the fading, and disagree about nothing except whether any of it is to be wanted.
