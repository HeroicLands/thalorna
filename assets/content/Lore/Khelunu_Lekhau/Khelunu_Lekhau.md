## Khelunu Lekhau (The Eight Powers)

"A working is an address," a lekhau master tells a novice in the first week at [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]]. "Get the name wrong and it goes somewhere else. Get the form wrong and it does not fail; it does what you actually wrote." The rest of the tradition is the consequence of that sentence.

The Khelunu Lekhau—the **Eight Powers**—is the arcane philosophy of
the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], which the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] hold to be the oldest continuously taught account of
magical practice in the known world. It holds that the arcane divides into eight _domains_, each a
region of the cosmic order over which a correctly known name has authority. Its practitioners are the
_lekhau_, those who hold sacred power; the title of every high priest in the empire—Thaz'Lekhau, "Great of Sacred Power"—names the greatest of them.

It predates the [[affiliation-zemorhodhis|Zemor Hodhis]] by some thousands of years, and it is not a variant of
it. The two traditions are not rival schools within one framework. They are two complete and
incompatible accounts of the same thing.

### The Same Ocean, Cut Differently

Both traditions agree on the underlying fact. There is one undifferentiated reservoir of arcane
force permeating existence—what the **[[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Arcane Order]]** names the [Dynaris]{.name data-type="lore/arcana"}—and all practice consists of
reaching into it. The disagreement is about where its divisions lie.

The Thordimalon partitions the ocean by _substance_: fire, water, air,
earth, metal, spirit. Its doctrine is not that this is a serviceable model but that it is the
structure of arcane reality, that the Dynaris is _always already_ one of the Six in every place and
at every moment, and that the Six exhaust the arcane.

The Khelathi partition the same ocean by _function in the cosmic order_—not by what a portion of
reality is made of, but by what it is _for_. Their eight domains are theological before they are
arcane, and the tradition makes no apology for that: in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]] the distinction between priest and
mage barely exists. The philosophy is taught to priests as part of ordinary temple education, and
the power it gives is licensed by temple authority, not held by a separate class of mage.

_The two partitions cut across one another._ There is no table of equivalences and there cannot be
one, because neither set of lines is a subdivision of the other. A working in the [[place-zulaten|Zulaten]] draws on what a
_Marendos_ (master) would have to call Spirit and Earth at once; a working of the River spans his Water and his
Earth. This is the practical reason the traditions cannot see each other. An Arcane Order observer watching a
lekhau does not see foreign magic—he sees _impure_ magic, several **rheza** (currents) braided into a single
coherent act, which by his own doctrine should not be castable at all. And it works in front of him.

The Khelathi find the reverse just as absurd. To divide the arcane by material substance strikes them
as classifying a library by the color of the bindings: true of the objects, and silent about what
any of it is for.

Neither priesthood is much troubled by the other's existence. The Arcane Order has no presence in Au'Khelathu
and no prospect of gaining one; the Khelathi priesthood regards its agents as arrogant amateurs, and
the few who have tried to recruit Khelathi practitioners have been politely and firmly expelled.

### The Eight

Each domain carries a Khelathi name; the House that holds it is that name under Gar-, "the House
of." **Seven Houses** are entered by initiation; the eighth is not entered at all.

The Houses share one ladder—Wazu of the House, who learns the names and may not set them down;
Lekhau, who may speak and inscribe; Khel-Gebu, the lector who carries the book and holds the
restricted forms; and Wer Khel-Gebu, chief lector at a temple and answerable for every form its
practitioners set down. Below them all is Name Struck: expelled, and the record of the teaching
erased, which in this tradition is the whole of the sentence.

_[[lore-rethzethu|Reth'zethu]] and [[lore-qeltnu|Qelt'nu]] are named for the two soul-components the funerary rites already preserve_—the _reth_ that is inscribed and the _qelt_ that is embalmed, the one written on every surface and the
other wrapped in linen. The pairing was not invented for the philosophy; the philosophy noticed it.

### The Frame: Qar'kuzu and Zelgút

The eight are not eight equal parts. Two of them are the frame, and six work inside it.

Khelathi theology is organized around a single tension—order against chaos. [[lore-qarquzu|Qar'kuzu]], the truth
that judges, is what [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'aret]] imposed on the primordial darkness before creation, and it
is the condition under which anything at all can continue to exist: law, judgment, binding, wards,
oaths, and the weighing of the heart. [[lore-zelgut|Zelgút]], the unmaking, is what [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'athis]] is
and what he never stops trying to restore: decay, curses, the serpent, entropy. It is not evil in the
moral sense so much as _prior_—the state that obtained before the world and would obtain again.

Every other domain occupies a position within that framework, and the tradition teaches them as such.
A lekhau does not learn [[lore-athenuqa|Thelqen'Uqa]] as a subject and Qar'kuzu as another subject; they learn Thelqen'Uqa as one
of the ways in which order is maintained against the alternative.

#### Zelgút is taught, and Zelgút is proscribed

This is the hardest thing in the tradition to explain to outsiders, and the temples make no attempt.

Zelgút is a domain of the philosophy like the other seven, and it is taught in full. It has to be. _You cannot ward
what you cannot describe_, and Au'Khelathu is a civilization whose most valuable magic is protective:
the seals on the tombs, the curses at the thresholds, the wards worked into the glyphs, and above all
the binding that holds the cursed Way at [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]] shut. Every one of those is a
Qar'kuzu working built around an exact understanding of what it is built against. The temples of
the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]] and the [[affiliation-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'ar]] keep the treatises on the chaos god for precisely this
reason—his nature, his manifestations, his methods of attack—so the priesthood can counsel the
throne and prepare the realm.

To _practice_ it is a capital matter. The line is not knowledge but use, and the tradition polices it
with the temple-inquisitors and the [Meglay]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, who hunt the forbidden cults without rest. A lekhau found
to have worked Zelgút is not disciplined; a person found maintaining a shrine to the chaos god faces
execution, on the reasoning that their existence invites catastrophe on the realm.

The arrangement has an obvious and much-remarked consequence. _The people best equipped to work Zelgút
are the ones the temples have trained to recognize it_, and a scholar of the treatises carries a
permanent shadow of suspicion no amount of orthodoxy quite removes. The temples accept this as the
price of a defense that works, and they are not wrong to: the sect that would break the seal on the
cursed road is the thing the priesthood of Uqa'a fears in its bones, and it is priests who know how it
would be done.

### The Three Pairs

The six working domains are three pairs, and the pairing is not opposition. In each case the two
halves are _both required_, and a practitioner who holds one without the other is not a specialist
but an incomplete one.

Thelqen'Uqa and [[lore-ithnetau|Ithnetáu]]—the two halves of one circuit. Thelqen'Uqa, the disk in its heat, is light,
sight, kingship, vitality, the day. Ithnetáu, the westward road, is the dead, passage, gates,
resurrection, the night. The tradition insists they are a single road: [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'a]] crosses the sky by day and
the underworld by night, and neither half of the journey is the whole of it. A lekhau who commands the
day and not the night can work only until sundown, in every sense the tradition cares about.

[[lore-anlaghkhlth|Anlagh'Khelathu]] and [[lore-gezrulutu|Gezru'lutu]]—the Black Land and the Red, the two halves of the country itself.
Anlagh'Khelathu, the living black land, is water, fertility, growth, healing, abundance. Gezru'lutu,
the red waste, is fire, drought, storm, endurance, plague, and the wandering. The valley is not the
good half and the waste the bad one; the flood that makes the Black Land is measured against the Red
every year, and the empire's gold, stone and roads all come out of the Red.

Reth'zethu and Qelt'nu—the two halves of a being, and the pair on which the whole tradition turns.
Reth'zethu, the name set down, is speech, writing, true names, knowledge, memory, mind. Qelt'nu,
the made body, is craft, stone, flesh, shaping, animation, transformation. Khelathi funerary practice
already treats these as inseparable: the soul has several components, each needing its own rites, and
the dead are preserved twice over—the body by embalming and the name by inscription on every
available surface. Destroy either and the person ends. This is why the erasure of a name is the
gravest sentence the empire can pass, and why a disgraced house has its monuments quarried and its
names struck from every wall.

### Wholeness Against the Vertex

The two traditions therefore train in opposite directions, and both know it.

The Arcane Order channels students into a single **rhezin** early and accepts the cost: its mages are brilliant
within their _rhezin_ and increasingly limited outside it, the opposing _rhezin_ becoming alien.
This is efficient, and it is also what makes a _kosthemos_ (initiate) easy to examine and to regulate—which the
history of the Zemor Hodhis itself concedes was among the reasons the six-fold model won out in the west.

A lekhau trained that way would be understood in Au'Khelathu as _defective rather than specialized_.
Holding Thelqen'Uqa without Ithnetáu, or Qelt'nu without Reth'zethu, is not a narrow mastery but half a working. So the
temple education is long, general, and finished before ordination: a Wazu spends years on sacred
texts, ritual, history, mathematics, medicine and the reading of omens, and the arcane philosophy
arrives inside that rather than beside it.

This is the deeper reason the Khelathi regard western practitioners as half-educated, and the reason
the empire has no mage class to speak of. The Eight are not a curriculum a temple offers. They are
the shape the temple believes reality has.

### Reth'zethu and Qelt'nu in Practice

Because the domains are functions rather than substances, the technique of reaching them is not the
Arcane Order's either. Khelathi power derives from knowledge of sacred words, divine names and ritual formulae,
kept in temple libraries open only to the initiated—so the act of magic is one of _correct
address_: the true name, in the exact form, spoken or set down.

Correctness is not a matter of care but of doctrine. A properly executed tomb painting does not depict
the afterlife; it _is_ the afterlife, made real through correct form. This is why Khelathi art is
conservative to a degree foreigners mistake for lack of skill—innovation in form is not undervalued
but actively dangerous, and perfection of the established form is the whole of the discipline.

Three practical consequences follow, and they mark Khelathi working out as unmistakable:

- _It is installed rather than cast._ The characteristic Khelathi working is inscribed and stands—seals, curses, wards, and the bound dead set to wake when a chamber is broken. Some have stood for
  six hundred years.
- _It is transferable through labor._ A correct inscription works for whoever set it correctly,
  which is why the craftsmen who cut the tombs are the most watched community in their selat, and why
  a tomb-robber's surest key is a bribed priest who knows which glyph is live and which is bluff.
- _Error does not fail; it executes._ A working that is wrong in its form is not inert. It does
  what was actually written, which is the origin of a great deal of Khelathi caution and a fair
  proportion of its ghost stories.

### Khelunu

The tradition takes its name from [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]], the temple-city of Reth'Sa'ar in the scribal
selat, where the Eight are taught in their fullest form and where the deepest archive in the empire is
kept. The city's name is the number: it is the place of the Eight, and has been for as long as the
king-lists run.

A philosophy of magic seated in an archive rather than a sanctum follows from its premise: in
a tradition where power is correct address, the library _is_ the armory.
