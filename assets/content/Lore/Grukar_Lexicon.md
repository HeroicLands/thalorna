The [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] have no words in the sense a scholar of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] gives the term. [[skill-grukarlng|Grukar]] communication is posture first and sound second, and most of the sound lies above or below human hearing. What a human ear does catch is a narrow band of clicks, rasps and closing stops, and the neighbors of the Grukar write that band down in letters as best they can. This page is the word-hoard of that transcription: the calls the neighbors have learned to tell apart, the names they give the four castes, the rules the transcription keeps to, and how a Grukar name is made.

A call has no fixed meaning apart from the body that makes it. The snarl written _zruk_ is _mine_ when a Grukar crouches over food and _attack_ when it is bellowed in a charge, and the gloss below gives the sense a call carries in the display it most often goes with. A call takes no ending and is never inflected; the tongue has no grammar to inflect it with.

The sound of the transcription is harsh and percussive. Words open on a rasp, _gr-_, _thr-_, _kr-_, _sk-_, _zr-_ or _vr-_, and close hard, often on a pair: _-rk_, _-rg_, _-rn_, _-lk_ or _-zg_. The circumflex some pages write over a vowel marks the chest-rumble that carries it and changes no letter.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the call as the neighbors write it.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its sense in brief, in the display it most often goes with.
- **Built from** is `—` for a call taken whole. A name gives the call and the closing it is made of, joined by `+`, and the name is exactly the call with the closing after it.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind      | Sounds like                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `k`, `g`, `t`, `d`, `b` | consonant | the closing stops, hard and short                                           |
| `'`                     | consonant | a click, a sharp closure of the throat chambers                             |
| `th`, `sh`              | consonant | a rasp through the teeth, and a hiss                                        |
| `s`, `z`, `v`, `h`      | consonant | _s_, a buzzing _z_, _v_, and a breath                                       |
| `r`                     | consonant | the ratcheting throat-rasp, rolled long                                     |
| `l`, `m`, `n`           | consonant | the liquid and the nasals, heard under a chest-rumble                       |
| `a`, `o`, `u`, `i`, `e` | vowel     | _father_, _for_, _boot_, _machine_, _bet_; _u_ and _a_ by far the commonest |

Some names in the [[skill-grukarlng|recorded lists]] are written in the spelling of the tongue that took them down, with letters this table does not use.

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel, a single consonant or one of the pairs below, and closes on a vowel or on one of the consonants or pairs below.

## Building words

**A call** is taken whole. Nothing is added to it and nothing is taken away, and two calls made together are two calls, not a compound.

**A caste name** is the kind's name and the caste's set together with a hyphen between them, as the joining table gives it.

**A name** is a call with a closing after it. A Grukar is called by whatever sound its pack uses to get its attention, and the sound that sticks is most often a call the bearer is known for, clipped short by the closing the pack puts on it. The closing carries no meaning of its own; it tells one bearer of a call from another.

### Joining

| First closes on | Second opens on    | Written | Example                       |
| --------------- | ------------------ | ------- | ----------------------------- |
| `grukar`        | `a`, `h`, `s`, `u` | `-+`    | _grukar_ + _uk_ > _Grukar-Uk_ |

## Making names

**A Grukar name** is acquired, not given, and the [[skill-grukarlng|language note]] describes how one sticks and shifts. Many names in the accounts are a call with a closing: _Zrukak_ is the snarl of _mine_ closed on a bark, and _Vrulkath_ is the boundary bellow closed on a rasp through the teeth. A name of this kind says what the bearer is known for in its own pack, and two bearers of one call are told apart by the closing.

**A paired name** sets two names side by side, identifying two Grukar together by pack or deed. It is a tag, not a family name, and the language note gives examples.

**The caste names** are the neighbors' names for the four kinds of Grukar, written after the kind's own name with a hyphen: _Grukar-Uk_, _Grukar-Sha_, _Grukar-Hushk_ and _Grukar-ahk_. A page that has named the kind once writes the caste alone: an Uk, a Sha, a Hushk, an Ahk.
