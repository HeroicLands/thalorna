- **Common Names:** The Elder Folk, the Firstborn, the Star-Kindled, the Twilight People
- **Self-Name:** Sinalë (adjective: _Sinalëan_)
- **Origins:** Unknown; arrived on Thalorna from an undisclosed prior realm roughly eight millennia ago (≈7500 BF, approximate)
- **Population:** Vanishingly rare (<0.01% of world population). Most humans have never seen one.
- **Enclaves:** Scattered throughout Thalorna, invariably in deep forests, hidden valleys, or island sanctuaries far from human settlement

The **Sinalë** are the eldest of the speaking peoples—the Firstborn, whose presence in Thalorna predates the earliest human civilizations by millennia. They were ancient when the ancestors of [[affiliation-empirtkhpr|Ta.Kheperu]] were laying the first stones of their river cities; they were old when the Pelwar nomads first crossed into western [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. Their presence on Thalorna stretches back roughly eight millennia—to approximately 7500 BF, a figure nobody holds more precisely than that, long before humans kept written records and long before the earliest human civilizations took shape. Their history as a people reaches further still, back into the prior realm from which they came; but of that time they tell outsiders nothing. The Sinalë themselves do not share their reckoning of years with outsiders.

To the vast majority of humans, the Sinalë are a rumor. A legend. Something your grandmother claimed to have seen once, at the edge of a forest, at twilight, and you never quite believed her. Most people live their entire lives without encountering a Sinalë, and many educated scholars in [[place-helionis|Helionis Region]] or [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] debate whether they exist at all or are simply a persistent folk myth. They exist. They simply have no interest in being found.

## Nature

The Sinalë are not human, though they resemble humanity closely enough that the differences are felt more than seen. They are tall, slender, and possessed of a beauty that humans find unsettling rather than attractive—too perfect, too still, too aware. Their eyes hold a depth that suggests they are seeing something beyond the visible world. They move with a grace that makes even the most accomplished human dancer look clumsy. And they live for a very, very long time—long enough that the concept of a "generation" as humans understand it is meaningless to them.

They are creatures of forest and starlight, of ancient music and long memory. Their connection to the natural world runs deeper than any human druidic tradition—it is not worship or reverence but something closer to kinship. The forests they inhabit respond to their presence in ways that humans find unnerving: paths appear and vanish, distances shift, and the quality of light changes in ways that defy the angle of the sun.

Their magic—if it can be called magic in the human sense—is nothing like the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]'s systematized arcane practice. The Sinalë do not classify their arts into schools or codify them in texts. Their power is woven into their nature, inseparable from their songs, their craft, and their relationship with the living world. The Ordo has attempted, at various points in its history, to study or categorize Sinalëan magic and has failed completely. The Sinalë find these attempts somewhere between amusing and insulting.

## History

The full history of the Sinalë is known only to the Sinalë, and they do not share it. What fragments have reached human scholars—through rare encounters, ancient texts, and the oral traditions of peoples who live near Sinalëan enclaves—paint a picture in broad, mythic strokes.

The Sinalë hold themselves to be the first of the speaking peoples to walk Thalorna, and by their own telling they are. They came to this world from some prior realm whose nature they do not disclose, and for a time they walked alone—sharing the continents only with the creatures and spirits that humans would later call fae, and with scattered bands of humans who lived then as hunter-gatherers, without cities or letters or settled tongues. The Sinalë observed these human bands and left them to their own becoming—a restraint they held to, and which did not prevent humanity from learning a great deal simply by watching them back. See [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]]. This is their Age of Stars, a period they remember in song but never describe to outsiders in detail.

The one exception to this reckoning, which the Sinalë acknowledge obliquely if at all, is [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]]. When Sinalëan wayfarers first reached that island, they found a human civilization already established and built on the teachings of an older people—a people who were, at that time, still in the world. The Kaliharans received the Sinalë with courtesy and refused them settlement. The Sinalë have never ventured a public account of this encounter, and their own histories pass around it in silence.

They pass in the same silence around a second meeting, earlier and elsewhere, in which Sinalëan wayfarers came upon that older people themselves and were politely declined. It is the only such encounter anyone records. The Sinalë learned nothing from it—not who they had met, nor that there was any connection between those people and the island—and they have never said where it happened. What the Kaliharans call them, and everything else that is known of them, is held on Kalihara alone; see [[lore-theithari|The Ithári]].

Then came the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]]—the Deep Folk, the Stone-Wrights—a second people, different in form and temperament but kindred in spirit. For a time, the two elder races lived in concert. The fragments that survive suggest a period of cooperation, even unity—a shared realm or alliance that spanned much of Thalorna, governed under a single accord. The Sinalë crafted in forest and starlight; the Khazári crafted in stone and flame. Between them, they shaped wonders that neither could have achieved alone.

But something broke. The nature of the sundering between the Sinalë and the Khazári is the most closely guarded secret of both peoples—neither will speak of it to humans, and the rare scholar who has pressed the question has been met with a silence so absolute and so cold that the conversation was never attempted again. What is clear is that the two elder races separated, completely and permanently, long before the first human civilizations arose. They do not speak to each other. They do not trade. They do not acknowledge each other's existence except in the most oblique terms. Whatever happened between them, it has not been forgiven.

What broke it was **[[lore-khazarturn|Khazártúrn]]**. In 2427 BF a Sinalë outlaw took up the [[miscgear-secondvoice|Second Voice]]—a relic the Sinalë knew of and had never thought worth mentioning, since a weapon that commands [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] is a weapon against cities and the Sinalë build none—and used it to destroy the greatest city the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] ever raised, killing thirty thousand. The Sinalë hunted him down themselves, took a long time about it, and afterward handed over nothing and explained nothing.

Each people guards the secret for its own reason. The Khazári because it is their humiliation; the Sinalë because one of theirs did it, and because of what they had failed to say beforehand.

By the time humanity began to build cities and keep records, the Sinalë were already diminished—fewer in number, withdrawn into ever-more-remote sanctuaries, engaged in concerns that had nothing to do with the affairs of the younger race. The rise of human civilizations barely registered in their reckoning. Empires that seemed eternal to humans—the Vylarian Republic, the Kheperi dynasties—were brief seasons to a people who remembered the world before cities existed.

## The Enclaves

The Sinalë live in scattered enclaves throughout Thalorna—hidden communities in deep forests, mist-shrouded valleys, and island sanctuaries that appear on no human map. These enclaves are not cities in any human sense. They are places where the boundary between the natural world and something older grows thin, where the trees are impossibly ancient, the water impossibly clear, and the silence has a quality that makes human visitors feel they are intruding on something sacred.

The enclaves are protected not by walls or armies but by the Sinalë's relationship with the land itself. Paths that lead to an enclave have a way of leading elsewhere. Travelers who approach uninvited find themselves walking in circles, or losing track of time, or simply feeling an overwhelming conviction that they should turn back. These are not hostile defenses—the Sinalë have no interest in harming lost travelers—but they are absolute. You do not find a Sinalëan enclave unless the Sinalë wish to be found.

The number and location of these enclaves is unknown. Human scholars have confirmed the existence of perhaps a dozen, scattered across the forests of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], the mountains of [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth Region]], the deep woods of [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald Region]], and remote corners of other continents. There are certainly more. The Sinalë do not volunteer information about their settlements, and the enclaves that humans know about are probably the ones the Sinalë consider least important.

Not all enclaves endure. [[place-laurelosse|Laurelossë]], a hidden sanctuary in the deep interior of [[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Kingdom of Nordheim]], was destroyed by the [[being-tereb|tereb]] necromancer [[being-grmsvrtrnr|Gríma Svartrúnar]] in the service of [[affiliation-hel|Hél]]. Its corruption—and the imprisonment of its inhabitants' spirits—is an open wound among the Sinalë, and a grim demonstration that even the Firstborn's ancient protections are not absolute.

## Relations with Humanity

The Sinalë's relationship with humanity can be summarized simply: they have very little of one. They are not hostile—they bear humanity no ill will—but they are profoundly uninterested in human affairs. Human politics, human wars, human religions, human commerce—all of these are the concerns of a younger race working through problems the Sinalë resolved (or abandoned) millennia ago. Asking a Sinalë to take sides in a human conflict is like asking a grandparent to adjudicate a dispute between toddlers. They might do it, but they would rather not. This disengagement is older than any human memory of it. When the Sinalë first came to Thalorna, humans already walked the continents, and the Firstborn chose then—as they have chosen ever since—to let that younger race find its own path, neither shepherding its rise nor opposing it.

Individual Sinalë occasionally interact with humans—a traveler granted shelter, a scholar given a cryptic answer, a child lost in the forest guided safely home. These encounters are rare, unpredictable, and always on the Sinalë's terms. They leave behind stories that become legends that become myths, and the Sinalë are content to let the myths stand.

There are exceptions—places where sustained contact exists between the Sinalë and human civilizations—but they are few, and each is unique in character.

### Élavendre

The most notable exception to the Sinalë's isolation is [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]. The Élavendren royal family and aristocracy have maintained a relationship with the local Sinalëan enclaves for centuries—the longest sustained contact between humans and Sinalë anywhere in the known world. The nature of this relationship is complex and not fully understood by outsiders. It is not an alliance in the political sense; the Sinalë do not recognize human political structures. It is closer to a neighborly understanding between peoples who share the same forests and have learned, over generations, to respect each other's presence.

The Élavendren fae traditions—the sacred groves, the nature spirits, the thin veil between worlds—are not mere superstition. They reflect genuine contact with the Sinalë and with the other fae beings that inhabit the forests of Élavendre. The Élavendren bardic colleges preserve songs and stories that the Sinalë have shared with them, and Élavendren druids practice forms of magic that bear a distant, diluted resemblance to Sinalëan arts. The Sinalë tolerate this—perhaps even encourage it—in a way they do not elsewhere.

Why the Sinalë have opened themselves to the Élavendren and no other human people is a question that scholars, diplomats, and envious monarchs have pondered for centuries. The Sinalë have never explained, and the Élavendren have the good sense not to ask.

### Tānvür

The [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Empire of Tānvür]] represents a different kind of relationship—one that the Sinalë appear to have entered not out of affinity but under some form of coercion or obligation. The details are obscure even by Sinalëan standards, and western scholars know almost nothing about it. What is clear is that the Sinalëan enclaves within Tānvür's borders maintain a degree of formal contact with the imperial court that exists nowhere else in the world—not even in Élavendre. Whether this was negotiated, compelled, or arose from circumstances that predate human memory is a question the Sinalë will not answer and the Celestial Bureaucracy considers settled. The arrangement is ancient, and it endures.

### K'ich'chik

The Sinalë also maintain some form of connection with the peoples of [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]]—a relationship that is poorly understood by western scholars, who know little of either the Sinalë or the K'ich'chik societies in any detail. The nature of this contact—whether it resembles the warmth of the Élavendren relationship, the formality of the Tānvür arrangement, or something else entirely—remains to be explored.
