Pronouncing Thalorna sets out one transcription system. None of the peoples of [[place-worldthlrn|the World of Thalorna]] write in Roman letters. The common transcription below gives names and coined words one set of readings. Aelwythi keeps readable word spellings and has its own pronunciation section.

Ease of speaking comes first. Where a sound is hard for a reader, a footnote gives a plain sound to use instead, and the plain sound is always correct.

## Common pronunciation

A tongue follows this section except where its own section gives a different reading. Aelwythi follows the word spellings and historical readings in its own section throughout.

### Vowels

Each vowel letter has one sound. Vowels are sounded one at a time unless they form a pair in this table, and a final _e_ is never silent.

| Letter | Sounds like                                | Example |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| a      | _father_                                   | _ana_   |
| e      | _bet_                                      | _eda_   |
| i      | _machine_                                  | _siri_  |
| o      | _go_                                       | _oro_   |
| u      | _rule_                                     | _lunu_  |
| ai     | _eye_                                      | _kaira_ |
| au     | _cow_                                      | _taum_  |
| ei     | _day_                                      | _meiro_ |
| oi     | _boy_                                      | _soin_  |
| ou     | _soul_                                     | _koulo_ |
| ae     | _day_, one syllable                        | _Gael_  |
| ie     | _ee-eh_ run into one syllable[^ie]         | _fiel_  |
| uo     | _oo-oh_ run into one syllable[^uo]         | _duon_  |
| aa     | a long _a_, one vowel held a little[^long] | _baar_  |
| ii     | a long _i_, one vowel held a little[^long] | _niir_  |
| uu     | a long _u_, one vowel held a little[^long] | _suul_  |

A pair of vowels not in this table is sounded apart, each vowel on its own, as in _Zoë_. _y_ before a vowel is the consonant of _yes_; between consonants or at the end of a word it is the vowel _i_, as in _Bryn_ and _Ashby_.

### Consonants

Consonants have their usual values in the reader's language, with these points.

| Spelling          | Sounds like                                                                       |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| g                 | Always hard, as in _go_. Never as in _gem_.                                       |
| ch                | _church_                                                                          |
| sh                | _ship_                                                                            |
| th                | _thin_                                                                            |
| dh                | _this_                                                                            |
| kh                | The Scottish _loch_.[^kh]                                                         |
| gh                | The same sound as _kh_, voiced.[^gh]                                              |
| zh                | The _s_ in _measure_.                                                             |
| j                 | _judge_                                                                           |
| q                 | A _k_ said further back in the throat.[^q]                                        |
| ts                | _cats_, at the start of a word too, as in _tsar_.                                 |

The letter _c_ appears only inside _ch_; a hard _c_ sound is written _k_. The letter _x_ is never used.

### Syllables

Every syllable has a written vowel. A stem written with a hyphen, such as _zevr-_, is never said alone: it is always joined to an ending that opens on a vowel. Where two consonants open a word in a pair unfamiliar in the reader's language, as in _dvon_ or _jvel_, they are said together.[^pair] An _h_ before a consonant or at the end of a word is a breath.[^h]

### Stress

A word of two syllables is stressed on the first. A longer word is stressed on the second-to-last syllable. So _Kelara_ is said ke-LA-ra, and _Torin_ is said TO-rin.

### Marks

In common spelling a word carries at most one mark, and a mark appears for one of two reasons. Sinale performance notation is an exception, described below.

- An **acute** (_á_) marks a stressed syllable that the rule above would not choose. In _Kelarán_ the rule would stress the middle syllable, ke-LA-ran, and the mark moves the stress to the last, ke-la-RAN.
- A **diaeresis** (_ë_) marks a vowel sounded apart from the vowel before it, as in _Zoë_.

No other mark is used in common spelling; Sinale may also annotate pitch and emphasis.

### The apostrophe

An apostrophe marks a catch in the throat, the pause in _uh-oh_. It is not a mark and does not count against the limit of one. After a consonant, as in _k'_ or _ts'_, it means that consonant is said with the same catch.[^catch] A word carries at most two.

### What is not written

Common spelling leaves tone and nasal vowels unwritten. Sinale may annotate pitch as described below. Vowel length is written only by a doubled vowel; a single vowel is not marked for length.

## Áelendi

[[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] stresses the first syllable of every word, whatever its length. An acute marks any other stressed syllable.

## Aelwythi {#aelwythi}

[[skill-aelwthlng|Aelwythi]] uses the reconstructed pronunciation of the older
stage. Read its familiar word spellings with the sound values below. Silent
letters, variable vowels and lexical stress belong to this spelling; the
common transcription's fixed vowel, stress and apostrophe rules do not apply.
The prose explaining the language uses ordinary modern wording.

The inventory follows the
[introductory pronunciation tables, pp. xli–xlvii](https://api.pageplace.de/preview/DT0400.9780191645433_A30390764/preview-9780191645433_A30390764.pdf#page=42).
It is a usable reconstruction with lexical and regional alternatives. For a
particular word's reading, consult the linked reference.
[Paul Meier's sound demonstrations](https://www.paulmeier.com/OP.pdf#page=7)
provide an introduction to the accent.

### Vowels and vowel groups

The example words identify historical readings, not their present-day sound
models. The table gives contextual spellings, not a rule that each listed
letter has only one sound. The whole word determines the reading. The signs distinguish
_sit_, _see_ and _day_ through their spellings; a new vowel sign is unnecessary.

```sql
SELECT array_to_string(letters, ', ') AS "Spelling", ipa AS "Sound", "like" AS "Reading"
FROM letters
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-doc-prnncthlrn'
  AND list_contains(tongue, 'skill-aelwthlng')
  AND kind IN ('vowel', 'long vowel', 'vowel pair')
ORDER BY position
```

Keep the long vowels in _day_ and _go_ steady. Begin the diphthongs in _thy_
and _thou_ centrally. _Boy_ and _thy_ can share a diphthong; an unstressed
final _-y_ can also take that reading. The vowels of _cup_, _put_ and _fool_
need their word's reading and the chosen regional variant; the spelling _oo_
does not settle vowel length.

### Consonants, silence and marks

```sql
SELECT array_to_string(letters, ', ') AS "Spelling", ipa AS "Sound", "like" AS "Reading"
FROM letters
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-doc-prnncthlrn'
  AND list_contains(tongue, 'skill-aelwthlng')
  AND kind NOT IN ('vowel', 'long vowel', 'vowel pair')
ORDER BY position
```

Sound _r_ in _far_ and _word_, and distinguish _wh_ in _where_ from _w_ in
_wear_. Word endings and metre can allow alternatives, including _-ing_ said
with _n_. Stress and elision follow the word and the verse, not the common
transcription's syllable count. A written _-ed_ need not add a syllable.

### Writing these sounds in Semaron

The [[doc-mapaelwsemaron|Aelwythi Semaron Spelling]] uses the existing letters
and their groups for this orthography. _th_ covers both sounds; _sh_ writes the
sound of _ship_; _ch_ and _tch_ take _t_ and _sh_; _j_ writes the sound of
_judge_; _ng_ and _wh_ keep their groups. Vowel spellings and context carry the
vowel distinctions. The existing group _zh_ can also spell a voiced _sh_ sound
in a borrowed word; it does not assign that modern reading to every familiar
word such as _pleasure_. No extra letter is required for this inventory.

A passage marked `lang=aelw` receives that mapping automatically. The map
rewrites spelling, while this section and the word's lexical reading determine
pronunciation. In particular, its treatment of _c_, _x_ and _qu_ is not an
algorithm for recovering every word's sounds. A dictionary native form records
an exceptional script spelling and takes precedence automatically. The roman
source keeps its ordinary words and punctuation.

## Ekpelu

[[skill-ekpelulng|Ekpelu]] has two sounds of its own: _gb_ and _kp_, each the two letters said at once, the lips and the back of the tongue closing together.[^ekpelu-gb] At the start of a word, _mb_, _nd_ and _ng_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _mbala_, _ndaro_ and _ngoto_.[^ekpelu-nasal]

## Hvarn

[[skill-hvarnlng|Hvarn]] stresses the first syllable of every word.

## Jurthat

In [[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthat]], _ui_ is _oo-ee_ run into one syllable, as in _gooey_ said quickly, and an _i_ or a _u_ before another vowel glides into it, so _shuiwo_ is two syllables.[^jurthat-glide]

## Kalihari

In [[skill-kalihrlng|Kalihari]], _ao_ is _ah-oh_ run into one syllable, near the _ow_ of _cow_, as in _luvao_.[^kalihari-ao]

## Nordmal

[[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] stresses the first syllable of every word. An acute marks any other stressed syllable. _hl_ is a breathy _l_, as in _hlask_.[^nordmal-hl]

## Nyáluban

[[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] stresses the first syllable of every word, and an acute marks any other stressed syllable. At the start of a word, _mb_, _nd_, _ng_, _nk_, _nt_, _mp_ and _nj_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _Mbuka_, _ndafo_ and _nkulu_.[^nyaluban-nasal]

The acute marks stress, not a high tone. Nyáluban has meaningful pitch in the world, but the common spelling leaves tone unwritten. Read its words with the vowels above and the stress shown; matching a rising or falling contour is not required for play. No additional pitch marks need to be learned.

Nyáluban has no writing tradition of its own. Its speakers keep their words through speech, memory and performance. The Roman spellings on these pages transcribe that speech for readers; foreigners may also transcribe it in their own scripts. Neither presentation implies a Nyáluban written original.

In a memory-song, keep the rhythm, repetitions and the distinction between the call and the gathered answer. Optional performance directions may describe a held syllable or a rising or falling phrase outside the words. These directions do not change a word's spelling or meaning; a question still uses the language's question words or marker.

## Okharik

At the start of a word in [[skill-okharclng|Okharik]], _mb_, _nd_, _ng_, _nk_ and _nt_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _Mbaka_, _Ndori_ and _ngor_; _ngw_ is that _ng_ with a _w_ after it, as in _ngwelo_.[^okharic-nasal]

## Sinale {#sinale}

[[skill-sinalelng|Sinale]] stresses the first syllable of every word, whatever its length.

Sinale may annotate a performance on the selected vowel nucleus. These marks change delivery, not the vowel's identity or the fixed first-syllable stress:

| Delivery      | Roman example and Unicode                        | Combining mark                     | Veldikko font assignment |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| falling pitch | _ǎ_, U+01CE LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH CARON      | U+030C COMBINING CARON             | FALL, U+1ED7A            |
| rising pitch  | _â_, U+00E2 LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH CIRCUMFLEX | U+0302 COMBINING CIRCUMFLEX ACCENT | RISE, U+1ED79            |
| emphasis      | _á_, U+00E1 LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH ACUTE      | U+0301 COMBINING ACUTE ACCENT      | EMPHASIS, U+1ED7B        |

The native values are assignments in the project's Veldikko font, not Unicode-standard names for Veldikko letters. The combining marks allow the same notation on other vowels. Existing _ë_ (U+00EB LATIN SMALL LETTER E WITH DIAERESIS) remains a distinct Sinale vowel.

_Pelvailǎ_ marks a falling call, _Pelvailâ_ a rise, and _Pélvaila_ emphasis on the first syllable. A called name keeps its full spelling and never wears. Acute emphasis may accompany one pitch mark; caron and circumflex are mutually exclusive. An unmarked line still has spoken pitch. Written questions retain _uvi_; a rise does not replace the question particle.

## Sovidur

[[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]] writes long vowels by doubling them. Stress a doubled vowel when present; otherwise stress the last syllable, as in _kodu_ (_ko-DU_) and _qiimod_ (_QII-mod_). Vowel harmony does not add pitch marks. _ö_ is _e_ said with rounded lips, and _ü_ is _i_ said with rounded lips; plain _e_ and _i_ are also fine for English-speaking players.

## Tantsou

In [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]], _ao_ is _ah-oh_ run into one syllable, near the _ow_ of _cow_, and _ui_ is _oo-ee_ run into one syllable, as in _gooey_ said quickly. An _i_ or a _u_ before another vowel glides into it, so _chuen_ and _chiao_ are each one syllable.[^tantsou-glide]

## Tellumi

[[skill-khazrilng|Tellumi]] stresses the syllable opened by a doubled consonant, as in _Tellumi_ (_te-LLU-mi_); otherwise stress the first syllable, as in _nelim_ (_NE-lim_). Hold a doubled consonant briefly; an ordinary single consonant is fine for play. No pitch or secondary-stress marks are required.

## Vedyari

[[skill-vedyarlng|Vedyari]] writes _bh_, a _b_ said with a breath after it, as in _bharava_.[^vedyari-bh]
