Pronouncing Thalorna sets out one transcription system. None of the peoples of [[place-worldthlrn|the World of Thalorna]] write in Roman letters. The common transcription below gives names and coined words one set of readings. Aelwythi uses it for coined words and names; its English-derived vocabulary uses Aelwythi respellings and the historical readings in its own section.

Ease of speaking comes first. Where a sound is hard for a reader, a footnote gives a plain sound to use instead, and the plain sound is always correct.

## Common pronunciation

A tongue follows this section except where its own section gives a different reading. Aelwythi's coined words and names follow the permitted sounds and stress rules of this common transcription; Aelwythi uses neither apostrophes nor hyphens in its Roman spellings. Its English-derived vocabulary follows the historical readings in the Aelwythi section.

A coined spelling has one reading under the applicable rules, including its stress. Capitals have the same sounds as lowercase letters. Read the longest recognized letter group first, from left to right, using only the groups this guide declares for the tongue. Each character belongs to one group. An acute leaves group recognition unchanged; where a tongue permits a diaeresis, it separates the marked vowel from the preceding one. Aelwythi coined words use no diaeresis and never split a recognized vowel group. The plain alternatives for difficult sounds are aids for play, not additional dictionary readings.

### English words and coined spellings

A new, unrecorded coined word or name must not duplicate a complete English word, whatever its intended meaning or pronunciation, unless an explicit authored exception states otherwise. Aelwythi's authored vocabulary and registered names are authoritative; an English homograph does not invalidate an existing entry. Explicit meanings, pronunciations and context notes take precedence over general rules, which apply wherever the entry supplies no exception. Compare whole spellings without regard to case or acute and diaeresis marks: capitalization or a pronunciation mark does not make an English word available for coining. A hyphen used only to separate pronunciation groups does not disguise a conflicting whole word. A root or naming element occurring inside a longer word is not a whole-word collision.

The maintained spelling reference is `assets/data/english-words.json`, one merged list of shared English and American, British, Canadian and Australian spellings from [wordlist-english](https://www.npmjs.com/package/wordlist-english), historical dictionary spellings, and documented English inflections from the Aelwythi writing rules. Its source manifest records the input versions and hashes; `utils/generate-english-words.mjs` regenerates the list from local source snapshots and the cited editorial additions. Historical membership reserves a spelling against coining; it does not authorize that word in a particular period or in Aelwythi prose. A missing entry does not by itself establish that an otherwise identifiable English spelling is available for coining: document the English source and add it to the maintained list. An entry appearing only as part of a definition, a bound root or an abbreviation is not on that ground an English word.

An English word used in Aelwythi retains its ordinary meaning and dictionary reading except where an authored entry or an Aelwythi rule supplies an explicit exception. The Aelwythi language note states its complete local standard; ordinary English vocabulary, grammar and pronunciation fill what neither an entry nor those rules modify. A proposed unrecorded form is not relabelled as coined merely to obtain another English reading. A foreign word or name adapted into Aelwythi receives a pronounceable Aelwythi form, spelled and stressed by the common transcription. Foreign spelling does not supply an exception to these rules.

### Syllables

Every syllable has a written vowel. The letter _y_ does not count as a vowel. Identify letter groups and vowel separation before counting syllables. A stem written with a hyphen, such as _zevr-_, is never said alone: it is always joined to an ending that opens on a vowel. A hyphen inside a complete coined word separates groups without adding a sound or resetting stress: _as-ha_ has separate _s_ and _h_, whereas _asha_ contains _sh_. Read a compound as one word, including where its elements meet. Where two consonants open a word in a pair unfamiliar in the reader's language, as in _dvon_ or _jvel_, they are said together.[^pair] An _h_ before a consonant or at the end of a word is a breath unless a recognized group consumes it.[^h]

### Stress

A one-syllable word carries its stress on that syllable. A word of two syllables is stressed on the first. A longer word is stressed on the second-to-last syllable. Count a listed vowel pair or doubled long vowel as one syllable. Thus _Kelara_ is ke-LA-ra, _Kélara_ is KE-la-ra and _Kelará_ is ke-la-RA. A hyphen inside a compound does not create a separate stress domain.

### Marks

In common spelling a word carries at most one mark, and a mark appears for one of two reasons. Sinale performance notation is an exception, described below.

- An **acute** (_á_) marks a stressed syllable that the rule above would not choose. It changes stress, not the vowel's identity or length. Put it on the first vowel letter of a multi-letter vowel group and recognize the group by its unaccented spelling. In _Kelarán_ the rule would stress the middle syllable, ke-LA-ran, and the mark moves the stress to the last, ke-la-RAN.
- A **diaeresis** (_ë_) separates the marked vowel from the preceding vowel, preventing them from forming a listed group. It changes the syllable count, not the vowel's identity, and does not itself place stress. Thus _ae_ is one syllable and _aë_ is two.

No other mark is used in common spelling; Sinale may also annotate pitch and emphasis. Vylari has the lexical grave-vowel exception below.

### The apostrophe

In tongues that write it, an apostrophe marks a catch in the throat, the pause in _uh-oh_. It is not a mark and does not count against the limit of one. After a consonant, as in _k'_ or _ts'_, it means that consonant is said with the same catch.[^catch] A word carries at most two.

### What is not written

Common spelling leaves tone and nasal vowels unwritten. Sinale may annotate pitch as described below. Vowel length is written only by a doubled vowel; a single vowel is not marked for length.

## Áelendi

[[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] stresses the first syllable of every word, whatever its length. An acute marks any other stressed syllable.

## Aelwythi {#aelwythi}

including adapted foreign forms, follow the common transcription and stress
rules except where an authoritative Aelwythi entry specifies otherwise. The
Aelwythi language note states the complete local pronunciation standard and
takes precedence where an explicit entry or local rule differs from this
summary. Unspecified vocabulary, grammar and readings use ordinary English. The [[skill-aelwthlng|language note]] gives the
writing register and naming palette. The prose explaining the language uses
ordinary modern wording.

  AND list_contains(tongue, 'skill-aelwthlng')

Keep the long vowels in _day_ and _go_ steady. Begin the diphthongs in _thy_
and _thou_ centrally. _Boy_ and _thy_ can share a diphthong; an unstressed
final _-y_ can also take that reading. The vowels of _cup_, _put_ and _fool_
need their word's reading and the chosen regional variant; the spelling _oo_
does not settle vowel length.

### Consonants, silence and marks

```sql
SELECT array_to_string(letters, ', ') AS "Spelling", ipa AS "Sound", "like" AS "Reading"
FROM letters
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-doc-prnncthlrn'
  AND list_contains(tongue, 'skill-aelwthlng')
  AND kind NOT IN ('vowel', 'long vowel', 'vowel pair')
ORDER BY position
```

In conventional English words, sound _r_ in _far_ and _word_, and distinguish
_wh_ in _where_ from _w_ in _wear_. Word endings and metre can allow historical
alternatives, including _-ing_ said with _n_. Stress and elision follow the
English word and the verse, not the common transcription's syllable count. A
written _-ed_ need not add a syllable. Word-final _mb_ sounds as _m_, with silent
_b_, wherever the word occurs in a sentence. A normally silent final _e_ stays
silent; a pronounced one, as in _be_, _he_, _me_ or _she_, stays audible.
Apostrophes in the English source spellings mark possession or omission,
rather than a catch in the throat. Aelwythi writes possessives with -es and
elisions without an apostrophe, as specified in its language note.
English spellings are not accented to override their established readings, and
Aelwythi uses no grave accent.

Every coined consonant follows the common table instead: _g_ is hard, _s_ is
unvoiced, _th_ and _dh_ have distinct sounds, and _mb_ sounds both letters.
Coined vowels are sounded even at the end of a word. Acute marks specify
exceptional stress without importing a foreign reading. Coined Aelwythi
uses no diaeresis: recognized vowel groups always keep their listed readings
and are never split into separate syllables. Other adjacent vowels are
sounded separately. A foreign name that Aelwythi speakers cannot pronounce is adapted
into pronounceable Aelwythi sounds and written by these same rules.

### Writing these sounds in Semaron

The [[doc-aelwsemaronenc|Aelwythi Semaron Encoding]] writes passages marked
`enc=aelwsemaronenc`, which supplies the Aelwythi language and Semaron Kuriale
font defaults. It applies the same character mappings to every word and name;
there are no word-specific native spellings. The language note defines the
historical readings of English-derived words and the fixed readings of coined
words. Encoding writes their Roman spelling without choosing their sounds.

Aelwythi has no x, and c occurs only in ch. English-derived words follow the
language note's respelling rules before they are encoded. The group ch takes
its own CHEROLA sign; tch is t followed by CHEROLA. The groups sh, th, dh, kh,
gh, zh, ts and ng take their respective signs, while qu remains q followed by
u. Single letters retain their case, and a consonant group is lowercase or
initial-uppercase. Vowels retain their letters; an acute adds only the
non-printing stress modifier after the vowel.

The shared Roman punctuation standard applies. Period takes POINT, the
imperative is ‼ and the exclamative is !. Quotation marks and verse line,
stanza and canto boundaries follow that standard. The encoding note states
the mappings and executable rules.

## Vylari

[[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]] writes the catch with a precomposed grave vowel: _à_. In ancient _kàralzon_ and _kàrthorgila_ it marks the catch after the preceding _k_; in the Alessa article _à_ it marks the catch after _a_. It adds no vowel or syllable and has no quotation role. An ordinary apostrophe is not a Vylari spelling. The period is Vylari's only punctuation.

## Ekpelu

[[skill-ekpelulng|Ekpelu]] has two sounds of its own: _gb_ and _kp_, each the two letters said at once, the lips and the back of the tongue closing together.[^ekpelu-gb] At the start of a word, _mb_, _nd_ and _ng_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _mbala_, _ndaro_ and _ngoto_.[^ekpelu-nasal]

## Hvarn

[[skill-hvarnlng|Hvarn]] stresses the first syllable of every word.

## Jurthat

In [[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthat]], _ui_ is _oo-ee_ run into one syllable, as in _gooey_ said quickly, and an _i_ or a _u_ before another vowel glides into it, so _shuiwo_ is two syllables.[^jurthat-glide]

## Kalihari

In [[skill-kalihrlng|Kalihari]], _ao_ is _ah-oh_ run into one syllable, near the _ow_ of _cow_, as in _luvao_.[^kalihari-ao]

## Ki'i'chek and Ki'ich'chik {#kiichek}

The language name [[skill-kiichek|Ki'i'chek]] and the continent name [[place-kiichchik|Ki'ich'chik]] have the following pronunciations.

| Name                    | Exact pronunciation | Reading          |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------- |
| Ki'i'chek — language    | /kɪʔˈɪʔtʃɛk/        | kih–**IH**–chek  |
| Ki'ich'chik — continent | /kɪʔˈɪtʃʔtʃɪk/      | kih–**ICH**–chik |

Both names have three syllables and stress the second. In these two names, _i_ is the short vowel of _bit_, and _e_ is the vowel of _bet_. The _k_ and _ch_ have their ordinary sounds. Each apostrophe marks a separate catch in the throat: after each of the first two vowels in the language name, and after the first vowel and the first _ch_ in the continent name. Say the consonant and then the catch separately.

The ordinary penultimate-stress rule supplies the stress in these three-syllable readings, so no acute is needed. An apostrophe marks the catch; it does not supply an additional vowel.

Ordinary Ki'i'chek words follow the common vowel and stress rules above; the short _i_ in these two names is a specific exception. Glottal stops are frequent: _ne'_, _ish'bal_ and _k'ayik_. Read each apostrophe as a catch, including after a consonant; an ejective pronunciation is not required. The apostrophe adds no syllable. Use the ordinary _ch_, _sh_ and _ts_ sounds, and a plain _k_ is fine for _q_.

Ritual speech may rise at the end of a phrase, but pitch does not change the word's identity. Ordinary transcription requires no rising or falling tone marks; questions are expressed through the language's words. An acute retains its usual role of marking exceptional stress.

## Nordmal

[[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] stresses the first syllable of every word. An acute marks any other stressed syllable. _hl_ is a breathy _l_, as in _hlask_.[^nordmal-hl]

## Nyáluban

[[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] stresses the first syllable of every word, and an acute marks any other stressed syllable. At the start of a word, _mb_, _nd_, _ng_, _nk_, _nt_, _mp_ and _nj_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _Mbuka_, _ndafo_ and _nkulu_.[^nyaluban-nasal]

The acute marks stress, not a high tone. Nyáluban has meaningful pitch in the world, but the common spelling leaves tone unwritten. Read its words with the vowels above and the stress shown; matching a rising or falling contour is not required for play. No additional pitch marks need to be learned.

Nyáluban has no writing tradition of its own. Its speakers keep their words through speech, memory and performance. The Roman spellings on these pages transcribe that speech for readers; foreigners may also transcribe it in their own scripts. Neither presentation implies a Nyáluban written original.

In a memory-song, keep the rhythm, repetitions and the distinction between the call and the gathered answer. Optional performance directions may describe a held syllable or a rising or falling phrase outside the words. These directions do not change a word's spelling or meaning; a question still uses the language's question words or marker.

## Okharik

At the start of a word in [[skill-okharclng|Okharik]], _mb_, _nd_, _ng_, _nk_ and _nt_ are each one sound, the nasal running straight into the next letter, as in _Mbaka_, _Ndori_ and _ngor_; _ngw_ is that _ng_ with a _w_ after it, as in _ngwelo_.[^okharic-nasal]

## Sinale {#sinale}

[[skill-sinalelng|Sinale]] stresses the first syllable of every word, whatever its length.

Sinale may annotate a performance on the selected vowel nucleus. These marks change delivery, not the vowel's identity or the fixed first-syllable stress:

| Delivery      | Roman example and Unicode                        | Combining mark                     | Veldikko font assignment |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| falling pitch | _ǎ_, U+01CE LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH CARON      | U+030C COMBINING CARON             | FALL, U+1ED7A            |
| rising pitch  | _â_, U+00E2 LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH CIRCUMFLEX | U+0302 COMBINING CIRCUMFLEX ACCENT | RISE, U+1ED79            |
| emphasis      | _á_, U+00E1 LATIN SMALL LETTER A WITH ACUTE      | U+0301 COMBINING ACUTE ACCENT      | EMPHASIS, U+1ED7B        |

The native values are assignments in the project's Veldikko font, not Unicode-standard names for Veldikko letters. The combining marks allow the same notation on other vowels. Existing _ë_ (U+00EB LATIN SMALL LETTER E WITH DIAERESIS) remains a distinct Sinale vowel.

_Pelvailǎ_ marks a falling call, _Pelvailâ_ a rise, and _Pélvaila_ emphasis on the first syllable. A called name keeps its full spelling and never wears. Acute emphasis may accompany one pitch mark; caron and circumflex are mutually exclusive. An unmarked line still has spoken pitch. Written questions retain _uvi_; a rise does not replace the question particle.

## Sovidur

[[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur]] writes long vowels by doubling them. Stress a doubled vowel when present; otherwise stress the last syllable, as in _kodu_ (_ko-DU_) and _qiimod_ (_QII-mod_). Vowel harmony does not add pitch marks. _ö_ is _e_ said with rounded lips, and _ü_ is _i_ said with rounded lips; plain _e_ and _i_ are also fine for English-speaking players.

## Tantsou

In [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]], _ao_ is _ah-oh_ run into one syllable, near the _ow_ of _cow_, and _ui_ is _oo-ee_ run into one syllable, as in _gooey_ said quickly. An _i_ or a _u_ before another vowel glides into it, so _chuen_ and _chiao_ are each one syllable.[^tantsou-glide]

## Tellumi

[[skill-khazrilng|Tellumi]] stresses the syllable opened by a doubled consonant, as in _Tellumi_ (_te-LLU-mi_); otherwise stress the first syllable, as in _nelim_ (_NE-lim_). Hold a doubled consonant briefly; an ordinary single consonant is fine for play. No pitch or secondary-stress marks are required.

## Vedyari

[[skill-vedyarlng|Vedyari]] writes _bh_, a _b_ said with a breath after it, as in _bharava_.[^vedyari-bh]
