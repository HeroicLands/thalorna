Past an irreversible act into a proscribed body, with no standing left outside it.

## What This Standing Is

A body the law would destroy admits its members by an act that cannot be taken
back: a public blasphemy, a sabotage of a temple, a first small violence, or the
inward turn to a worship that carries a capital sentence. The act is chosen for
its irreversibility. Once it is done, no standing in the lawful order is
recoverable, and the body is the only thing left to belong to.

Nothing above this standing has ever been confirmed from outside. Where such a
body has officers, it does not admit to having them; where it has doctrine, the
doctrine is held in secret and in fragments.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Outside, discovery is a capital matter: the existence of such a devotee is held to
invite catastrophe on the realm, and there is no penitence and no restoration.
Inside, the irreversibility is the discipline. The body needs no sanction of its
own, because a member who breaks with it is exposed on both sides at once—known to
the body he left and unable to return to the order his admitting act destroyed.

## Privileges

The body's company, its shelter, and the doing of what it does in the only company
in which doing it is possible.

## Obligations

Absolute secrecy, which is the one obligation such a body can enforce and the only
one it needs. Where it recruits, a member brings in the kind of person it draws
from: the ruined, the condemned, and those for whom no advocate exists.
