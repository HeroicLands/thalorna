Of a body's longest-served or oldest-descended, whose word carries weight its forms acknowledge without a vote of its own.

## What This Standing Is

Inside a body whose members are formally equal, some are heard before the rest.
Length of service does it in one body and length of descent in another: the
longest-serving members form an inner circle that the full membership treats as a
single weight, or the oldest houses hold a precedence the body's procedure was
built around. Either way the standing is a position inside the membership rather
than above it.

It confers no separate vote, no charge and no portfolio. What it confers is
precedence, and precedence is enough to decide much of what the body decides,
because a question the senior members agree on is rarely opposed by the rest.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Nothing here is enforceable in the way an office is enforceable. The effect is
procedural: a senior member is heard earlier, interrupted less, and answered
differently, and the body's ancient forms—the order of speaking, the phrasing a
motion takes, the courtesies owed—are this standing's own custom made procedure.
Mastering those forms is itself the qualification for being heard.

## Privileges

Precedence in the order of speaking and in the body's courtesies, and the weight
that attaches to agreement among the seniors. Where a body keeps an inner circle,
membership of it is this standing and carries access to what the circle discusses.

## Obligations

To know the body's custom well enough to be its authority on it, and to be
available to settle a procedural question. The standing lapses with nothing, and a
senior member who stops attending stops being heard.
