Holding one house, province or branch of a larger body, and answerable to it for everything the part does.

## What This Standing Is

A body too large to be run from one place divides itself. It plants a temple in
each province, keeps a house in each city, splits a temple complex into specialist
branches, groups its houses into provinces, or stations its practitioners wherever
the work is. Someone holds each part, and this is that standing.

The part is held whole. Its staff, its stores, its revenues, its records and its
work are this standing's to run, and nothing in the part is above it. What the
standing is not is the head of the body: above it sits the authority that created
the part, set its terms, and can take it back.

So the standing is defined by two facts together, and either alone describes
something else. Complete authority within one part, and answerability upward for
the whole of it.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Within the part, what this standing decides is decided. Outside it, the standing
carries nothing: a branch head has no authority over a sibling branch, and the two
contend for the body's resources, precedence and favour as equals.

The upward answerability is the substance. What the part files, spends, teaches,
certifies or sets down is signed for here, and a fault in any of it is recovered
from this standing rather than from whoever did the work. Appointment is the
parent body's, and so is removal, which needs no finding of fault—a part may be
reassigned for the body's convenience.

## Privileges

To run the part without reference upward on anything routine: to make and unmake
the charges beneath, to engage whoever the work requires, to spend within the
part's means, and to refuse what the part cannot properly do. Where a body seats
its parts in a chapter, a council or a conclave, a seat there and a voice in
choosing who heads the whole.

## Obligations

To transmit upward what is owed upward, on the schedule set for it and in the form
required, and to answer for it when it is rejected. To hold the body's standards
inside the part exactly as the body states them, and to go and correct work that
has failed after leaving the part's hands. To contend for the part's share of what
the body distributes, which is a duty rather than an indulgence in bodies whose
branches are funded against each other.
