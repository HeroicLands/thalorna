Senior of a lineage or a house, whose recollection of its custom settles what the young dispute.

## What This Standing Is

The senior of a kin group, holding its memory. An elder knows the lineage's
genealogies, its obligations to other lineages, the precedents by which its past
disputes were settled, and the terms of whatever compacts it is party to. Nothing
confers the standing but time and survival: no election, no appointment, no rite.

Its whole substance is that the memory is authoritative. Where a kin group keeps no
written record—and most keep none—what the elders recall is the record, and a
question about what was agreed, what was owed or who is related to whom is answered
by asking them.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

An elder's recollection settles a dispute within the lineage without any procedure
being invoked, and it binds the lineage's head in practice if not in form: a head
who rules against the elders' account of custom is understood to be acting outside
custom, and his kinsmen treat it accordingly. Beyond the lineage the standing
carries no authority at all. Leadership sits with whoever leads—a head, a council,
an assembly—and this standing is not a step toward it.

## Privileges

To be heard on anything the lineage's past bears on, and to be asked before a
decision touching its obligations is taken. A place at the fire and a share of the
lineage's keeping, held by seniority rather than by labour.

## Obligations

To hold the memory accurately and to give it straight, including where it goes
against the lineage's present interest. To pass it on, since a lineage whose elders
die with it unspoken has lost its own history.
