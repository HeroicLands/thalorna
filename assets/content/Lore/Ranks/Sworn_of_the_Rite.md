Bound by a body's sacred obligation, admitted to its rites and permitted the act it exists to perform.

## What This Standing Is

Where a body's work is also a rite—a hunt made under a god's permission, a taking
from which the god's share is cut before any other—its full members are admitted
by oath rather than by ordination. The oath binds the swearer to the sacred
obligation and opens two things together: a place at the rites, and the right to
perform the act the body exists for. Those below prepare for that act—they carry,
they pole the boats, they read the omens, they butcher what is taken—and they may
not perform it.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

The obligation and the permission are one thing, so breaking the obligation closes
the rites, and being closed out of the rites ends the work. Where the body's
charter was granted to a family, expulsion reaches that family too, because the
charter is what the oath was sworn under.

## Privileges

A place at the rites, the act itself, and a share of what is taken once the god's
share is cut. A sworn member's presence is what makes an undertaking lawful in the
body's own terms, so a party without one does not go out.

## Obligations

The obligation sworn, which governs when the act may be performed and upon what.
The god's due is rendered first and in full from every taking, and a taking made
outside the rite is answered for to the body rather than to any civil authority.
