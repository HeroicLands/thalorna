"You hold your district because your men agree to it. I hold this province because the king gave it, and what he gave he can give to another," a jarl says to the hersvaldar who answer to him. "The ting binds the two of us alike."

## What This Standing Is

In the five kingdoms, a jarl holds a province of the king, commands the hersvaldar who lead its districts, and takes a share of its dues for keeping its peace. Where a hersvald holds a district by his own people's consent, a jarl holds a province by the king's grant.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

In the kingdoms, a jarl who ignores what the ting decides discovers quickly how much of his authority was consent: the assembly's ruling binds him as it binds any free man, and the province he holds can be granted elsewhere if the king judges him unfit, which no bóndi's odal land can be.

## Privileges

In the kingdoms, he commands his province's hersvaldar, takes a share of its dues, and speaks for it before the king with a weight no district head alone carries.

## Obligations

In the kingdoms, he keeps his province's peace and answers to the king for it, delivering the dues and the muster his grant obliges him to and losing the province if he does not.

## Offices Open at This Standing

In the kingdoms, holding a named province is itself the office the rank confers; a jarl may also sit as Hirdstjóri, commanding the king's household troop, or as Lawspeaker at the kingdom's ting.

## Where This Standing Is Held

The five kingdoms—[[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Nordheim]], [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Malagna]], [[affiliation-kingdmnrgd|Norgaad]], [[affiliation-kingdmtrgd|Targud]], and [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]]—hold this standing under one Nordmal law. In the [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Jarldom of Stormveld]], a jarl instead rules a jarldom in his own right, owing the [[lore-highjarlrnk|High Jarl]] the confederation's nominal allegiance rather than holding a king's grant.
