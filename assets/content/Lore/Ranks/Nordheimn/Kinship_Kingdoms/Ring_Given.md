Here is how a king's Hringvördr, who keeps the rings the king gives, puts the standing to a huscarl newly sworn: "The ring is a seat. The hall hears you before it hears a common bóndi, and in return the service you swore stays sworn."

## What This Standing Is

A hringberi is given a place and a ring by a hall: for service sworn in the hird, for a marriage that puts a clan leader's household in a wife's hands, for the sight a völva carries, or for having outlived everyone else at the fire. The ring a lord gives is the culture's own mark of the standing, and four different doors lead to it.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

He has a named place at the table and is heard before the common clan member when the hall is deciding: the huscarl is fed at his lord's table and bound to his person, the clan leader's wife is obeyed while she manages the holding in her husband's absence, the völva sits upon the high seat at the heart of the hall, and the honored old are served first and seated nearest the fell.

## Privileges

A named seat—at the lord's table, the high seat, or the door-seat nearest the fell—and the hearing that comes with it: a hringberi speaks and is answered before a common bóndi is.

## Obligations

The service, marriage, sight, or years that placed him or her there continues to bind: the huscarl stays bound to his lord's person, and the clan leader's wife answers for the holding she runs in her husband's absence.

## Offices Open at This Standing

The queen's keeping of a hall's keys and stores, and any office a kingdom seats from among its huscarls, its seers, or its honored old.

## Where This Standing Is Held

The five kingdoms—[[affiliation-kngdmnrdhm|Nordheim]], [[affiliation-kingdomlgn|Malagna]], [[affiliation-kingdmnrgd|Norgaad]], [[affiliation-kingdmtrgd|Targud]], and [[affiliation-kngdmvthgrd|Vithgard]]—hold this standing under one Nordmal law, and the [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Jarldom of Stormveld]] holds it under the same name.
