Long practised, holding a body's craft and teaching it, and judging whether its forms are kept.

## What This Standing Is

The senior practitioner of a body whose standing rests on practice rather than on
office. Years of work have made this the person others learn from: a teaching
elder takes apprentices or candidates, holds the body's account of its own forms
and of its dealings with whatever it deals with, and says when a form has been
broken or a candidate is ready to attempt what the body gates.

Leadership sits elsewhere—with a council, an assembly, a chief—and this standing
carries none of it. Deep respect and no command is the shape of it, which is why a
body can hold several teaching elders at once without any question of precedence
between them arising.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

A teaching elder's word settles a question of practice and carries no further. It
is the word that decides whether an apprentice may be set to work unsupervised,
whether a reading was properly made, whether a candidate may attempt the ordeal.
It decides nothing about the body's property, its policy or its succession.

## Privileges

To take and teach apprentices, to judge who may attempt what the body gates, and
to be consulted on anything the body's memory bears on. Where the body keeps no
written record, this standing is the record.

## Obligations

To teach, which in some bodies means years of sitting with one person and
correcting very little. To correct a broken form wherever it is found, and to
answer for what a pupil does with what was taught.
