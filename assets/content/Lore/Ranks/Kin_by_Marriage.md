Married into a house, holding its name and its standing without its blood.

## What This Standing Is

A marriage brings a person into a house that reckons its membership by descent.
The name is given, the standing is real, and the house's protection extends—but
the blood does not, and a house that counts descent counts this person as the one
member who has none. The standing is conferred by the marriage and by nothing
else: no oath is taken and no service is owed to earn it.

It sits a rung below kinship by blood, which is the house's full membership, and a
rung above the clients and dependents the house shelters without naming.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

The house's protection covers a person here and the house's quarrels bind them,
exactly as for a kinsman by blood. What differs is the house's regard: this
standing is watched, because the person holding it came from somewhere else and
has kin there. A share of the house's fortune is held at the house's discretion
rather than by right of descent, and in a succession the claim runs through the
children rather than through the spouse.

## Privileges

The house's name, its standing in dealings with other houses, its protection, and
a place at its table. Where the house trades, this standing trades on its credit.

## Obligations

The loyalty the name implies, enforced by the watching. A person here answers to
the house's head for dealings with the kin they came from, and a dealing the house
reads as favoring the other side is the one failure this standing is assumed
capable of.
