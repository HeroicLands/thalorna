Head of a temple or a cult, speaking with the voice of its god on doctrine.

## What This Standing Is

The head of one temple, or of one god's whole cult where the cult is small enough
to be held by a single person. A high priest holds the sanctuary and the places
within it others may not enter, the rites that may not be delegated, the temple's
lands and revenues and dependents, and the god's doctrine. Where the god's will is
asked on a question nobody has settled, this is the standing that answers.

It is reached from the ordained priesthood and from nowhere else. A high priest has
kept the daily rites for years or decades before being raised, by election among
the senior clergy, by the sitting holder's nomination, by the parent faith's
appointment, or by an accession the priesthood declares revealed rather than
chosen. The standing is not hereditary in any faith that holds it.

Being the head of a temple is not the same as being the head of a faith. Where a
faith is large, a presiding hierarch, a pontifex over all its temples or a
sovereign who is nominally priest of every god stands above; where the faith is one
cult, or one city's temple with no hierarchy over it, nothing does. Either way what
marks the standing is holding a temple whole and speaking for its god inside it.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

A high priest answers for everything done in the temple and by its servants, and
the answer is given to whoever rules where the temple stands as readily as to the
faith. A ruling on doctrine made here stands until a doctrinal authority above it
says otherwise, and in most faiths there is no such authority. The clergy beneath
cannot remove a high priest; where a faith has an apex, removal belongs to it, is
doctrinally justified when it happens, and is rare enough that the attempt is a
crisis.

Outside the temple the standing is not an office of state, and it carries no power
to command anyone. It carries the weight of being the one who can be asked, which
in practice reaches further than many offices do: a high priest of a great temple
disposes of revenues and lands on the scale of a province, and what a ruler is told
the god wants is told to him from here.

## Privileges

Entry to the innermost sanctuary, conduct of the great seasonal rites and of the
divinations the body takes seriously, final say on doctrine within the cult,
disposal of the temple's wealth, appointment to every charge beneath, and audience
with whoever holds power where the temple stands.

## Obligations

To keep the rites on the days the calendar appoints them, including the ones that
cannot be handed to anybody else. To keep whatever the temple exists to
keep—an eternal flame, a register, a reckoning of the cycles, the dead. To settle
the disputes of doctrine brought up from the clergy, and to settle them the same
way twice. To answer for the temple's wealth, which is the charge that most often
ends a holder's tenure badly.
