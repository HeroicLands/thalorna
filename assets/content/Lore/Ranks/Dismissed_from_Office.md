Removed from a charge at the pleasure of whoever conferred it, with nothing revoked beyond the charge.

## What This Standing Is

In a body that is wholly a magistracy—a treasury, a council of curators, a board of
fiscal officers—every charge is held at the pleasure of whoever conferred it, and
removal needs no cause, no finding and no procedure. A dismissed officer walks out
with his reputation intact, his property untouched, his citizenship unaffected and
no bar on holding anything else. What he has lost is the post, which in such a body
is also the whole of what he had in it.

The absence of any finding is the point. Nothing is published, nothing is endorsed
on a record, and nothing is alleged, which is what separates this from an expulsion
in disgrace.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

The dismissal is not reviewable, because the appointment was never a right. A seat
or a charge lost this way is not given back, and the body keeps no account of why
it was taken—so a dismissed officer has nothing to answer and no way to clear
himself either.

## Privileges

Everything he held outside the body, which is untouched. Where he held a rank in
some other order, that rank is unaffected, and a dismissed officer commonly takes
up a charge elsewhere at once.

## Obligations

To hand over the charge's papers, seals, accounts and keys, and to leave at the
word rather than at a date of his choosing.
