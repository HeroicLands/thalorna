Having brought a request to a body and rendered what it asks, while the answer is outstanding.

## What This Standing Is

Someone who wants a thing only this body can give—intercession with a spirit, a
ruling, a hearing, a dead relative back—and has asked for it in the form the body
requires. The asking is itself a standing, because what the body asks in return is
real: an offering brought, a fee paid, a service rendered, the matter laid out
before whoever decides. A supplicant is not a member and is not attempting to
become one; for most people, most of a body's dealings with the world never go
further than this.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

A request granted creates a debt, and the debt is enforceable where the standing
is not. A body that answers a supplication holds a claim on the supplicant
afterwards, and in bodies that keep no other hold on outsiders this is the hold
they keep.

## Privileges

To be heard, and to have brought the matter in a form the body recognizes. Where
a body addresses a power on another's behalf, a supplicant is who it addresses it
for.

## Obligations

To bring what the body asks, in the form asked. Silence is an answer: most
supplications are never answered, and a supplicant is not told so.
