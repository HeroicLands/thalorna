Bound for life by a vow completed in the ordeal a god sets, in a devotion that confers no office.

## What This Standing Is

Some devotions have no priesthood, no temple establishment and no ladder of
office. What such a devotion confers is a vow. The devotee undertakes an ordeal
the god is held to have set, completes it, and is under vow from then on. The
ordeal is the whole of the standing: there is no renewal, no advancement, and
nothing above it to reach.

Because the devotion has no officers, nobody administers the vow and nobody
witnesses it on the god's behalf. The devotee's own account of having completed
the ordeal is what households and shrines go on, so the standing is held in the
way a reputation is held.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

The vow is not released, because no office holds the power to release it. A
devotee who abandons the observance is under vow still and failing it, which is a
matter between him and the god and reaches no tribunal. What the devotion can
withhold is recognition, and recognition is the whole of what it was giving.

## Privileges

The favor the ordeal was undertaken for, which is the point of the undertaking and
is held to be the god's to grant or withhold. A devotee under vow is received as
such in any household that honors the god, and is asked to perform the god's
observance where a household wants it performed by someone bound.

## Obligations

The vow's own terms, for life, whatever those terms were. Where the ordeal imposed
a continuing abstinence, a continuing practice or a continuing service, that is
the obligation, and it does not lapse with age or with distance from where it was
undertaken.
