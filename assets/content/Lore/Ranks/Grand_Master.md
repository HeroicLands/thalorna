At the head of a body that is not a polity, holding the whole of it, with nothing inside it standing above.

## What This Standing Is

The top of a ladder, in a body that is an order, a guild, a company, a tradition, a
faith, a college or a syndicate rather than a polity. One person holds the body
entire—its name, its charter or rule, its revenue, its instruments and its
work—and holds the right to speak for it to anyone outside it.

**This is a place in a ladder and not a set of obligations**, because the bodies
that reach it share none. At one end sits a master elected by a chapter, bound by
the rule he enforces and removable under it. At the other sits a head whose word is
the body's only law and whose tenure lasts exactly as long as it is feared. Between
them: a chair held for life, a presiding hierarch who fixes a city's calendar, a
standing head who holds a company's contracts between captains, and a mortal officer
who signs what a divine bureau receives. Conferral runs through election,
appointment, acclamation, nomination by the outgoing holder, and simple survival.

What every holder shares is the position. Nothing inside the body stands above.
Whatever the body owes outward is owed through this standing. The body's continuance
is this standing's charge, and when the body fails, this is where the failure is
reckoned.

Two neighbouring positions are not this one. Where the apex presides over a body
that keeps every decision for itself and holds nothing when it is not sitting, the
position is a presiding officer's. Where the body is a polity, the position is a
sovereign's.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Nobody within the body is owed an account by this standing. The accounts that are
owed run outward: to a chartering authority, to a client, to a court, to a god, to a
confederation of similar bodies. A body whose forms bind its head states so in those
forms, and many do not state it at all.

Removal follows the same pattern. Where a body elects, it can unmake, and the
holders of such a position govern knowing it. Where a body does not, the position
ends with the holder's death, resignation or defeat, and nothing in the body's law
addresses the question.

The standing reaches beyond the body as far as the body itself reaches, and no
further. A head of a small tradition commands nothing outside it; a head of a great
temple network or a chartered trade negotiates with sovereigns.

## Privileges

To speak for the body and to bind it. To hold its instruments—seal, charter, rule,
roll, calendar, tables, contracts. To make and unmake every charge beneath. To see
the whole of its business, which no other standing in the body does. Where a body
keeps something only one person may touch, this is that person.

## Obligations

To keep the body in being, which is the whole of the charge and is not divisible. To
answer outward for what the body does, in the form and on the schedule whoever is
owed the answer requires. To hold what the body exists to hold—its standards, its
secrets, its tables, its calendar, its dead—and to hand all of it on. Where the
body's forms bind the head, to be bound by them including where a ruling goes
against him.
