Petitioning a body for admission, or marked out for it, and bound by nothing until it answers.

## What This Standing Is

Someone the body has opened a question about and has not closed. Most arrive by
asking: a petition goes to whoever admits, and the petitioner is watched while it
stands. Some arrive without asking, marked out in childhood by a capacity the
body looks for—a rapport with animals, a sensitivity to places, the capacity to
dream true dreams—and taken an interest in before any petition is possible.
Where the body keeps a house, an aspirant may live under its rule on trial,
holding nothing by it.

The standing exists because the examination is real. A body that admits by
ordeal, by vow or by inscription cannot take a stranger's word for his fitness,
so it keeps him where it can watch him.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Nothing binds in either direction. The body dismisses at will, with no cause
stated and no appeal, and the aspirant leaves at no cost and under no censure.
Where he lives under a rule on trial, breaking it ends the candidacy and reaches
nothing else: there is no standing to revoke and no roll to be struck from.

## Privileges

To be examined rather than ignored, and to see what the body shows candidates—its
hours, its house, its discipline, enough of its teaching to know what is being
asked. Where a body feeds and shelters its aspirants, it feeds and shelters this
one.

## Obligations

To be found out: to live where the body can observe, to answer what it asks, and
to keep whatever discipline the trial imposes for as long as the petition stands.
Years of it is ordinary, and a petition left standing is not a petition refused.
