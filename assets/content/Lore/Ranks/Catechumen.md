Received for a term of formation under a master of novices, and free to leave until profession.

## What This Standing Is

A body that admits by profession admits in two stages, and this is the second one.
The candidate has been examined and received: the house has taken him in for a
fixed term, set him under an officer whose whole charge is novices, and begun
teaching him the thing the body does—its rule, its hours, its curriculum, its
reckoning, its reading of a room. He lives the life of a member and holds the
standing of one who may still walk out of it.

That freedom is deliberate. A body whose full standing is taken for life will not
let anyone take it without having lived under it first, so the term runs long
enough for both sides to learn what they are agreeing to.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

He is under the rule and answerable to it, and the rule's sanctions reach him—but
the worst of them is dismissal, which costs him nothing he holds. Until profession
he may leave at any time, with no censure, no debt and nothing published. The body
may likewise end the formation without stating a reason, and a term ended that way
is the ordinary outcome for most who begin one.

## Privileges

Instruction, which is the point: the body teaches him in earnest and at its own
expense, and what it teaches is not taken back if he goes. Food, lodging and the
house's protection for the term. A place in the house's own order of precedence,
above its servants and its aspirants.

## Obligations

The rule, in full, for the term. Obedience to the master of novices in everything
touching the formation, and the discipline the house keeps—its silences, its
hours, its work. He holds no property of the body's and speaks in none of its
decisions.
