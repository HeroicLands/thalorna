Consecrated to a faith at birth and counted among its faithful before reaching an age to keep its duties.

## What This Standing Is

A rite performed on an infant—water on the lips, a mark set, a name entered—puts
the child inside the faith before the child can profess anything. The
consecration is neither conditional nor provisional: from that day the child is
counted in the faith's reckoning of its own people, named in its records, and
owed what the faith owes a member.

What a child here does not hold is the practice. The duties that define an adult
observant—blood given on an appointed day, a fast kept, an offering brought—wait
on an age the faith fixes, and nothing is required before it.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

A person at this standing cannot be cut off, because nothing has been required
and so nothing can be withheld in answer. A faith's sanctions begin with its
duties, and both begin together at the appointed age.

## Privileges

The faith's protection and the rites that attend a life—naming, healing, the
observances a household performs over a child, and burial under the faith's own
forms where a child dies before the age. A consecrated child counts among the
faithful in every reckoning the faith makes of itself, which is why a faith that
consecrates at birth counts most of a population as its own.

## Obligations

None. At the age the faith sets, this standing becomes the ordinary observance
expected of an adult, with the duties that carries, and the consecration is not
performed a second time.
