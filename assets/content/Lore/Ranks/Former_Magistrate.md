Having held a body's chief magistracy and laid it down, and keeping the weight of it ever after.

## What This Standing Is

Where a body's highest office is held for a fixed term and then surrendered, those
who have held it form a class of their own. They hold no office. What they hold is
the fact of having held that one, and in a body whose governing order is otherwise
flat—every seat equal, every vote one—that fact is the only durable distinction
there is.

The standing is above a seat on the governing council and below whatever stands at
the head of the body, and it is reached by exactly one route: election or allotment
to the chief magistracy, a term served, and the office handed back intact. A term
cut short by removal confers nothing.

It is held for life and it is not a step to anything. Election to the chief
magistracy a second time, where a body permits it, adds no standing, because the
standing is already held.

## How the Law Treats a Person Here

Standing without authority. This standing commands nothing, levies nothing and
decides nothing, and a holder of it who gives an opinion gives an opinion. What it
changes is the order in which the body transacts its business: these are the people
asked first, heard at length, and recorded.

It also fixes eligibility. The charges a body will not entrust to anyone untried—a
province, an army in the field, an embassy, an inquiry into its own
accounts—are filled from this class, and in bodies where the practice has hardened
into law they are filled from nowhere else.

The standing survives everything short of the sentence that strips a person of
standing altogether. Losing an election, losing a fortune and losing a lawsuit all
leave it untouched.

## Privileges

Precedence over every sitting member of the governing council, a voice asked before
the question is put, and eligibility for the body's greatest commands and missions.
Where the body keeps a roll of those who may be consulted on its forms and
precedents, a place on it.

## Obligations

To be available for the charges the standing makes a person eligible for, and to
take one when the body asks; a refusal is remembered. To answer for the term
served, without limit of time, to whatever audit or court the body sets over its
magistrates. To hold the body's practice accurately and to say so when a thing is
being done wrongly, which is the use the body makes of the class and the reason it
keeps it.
