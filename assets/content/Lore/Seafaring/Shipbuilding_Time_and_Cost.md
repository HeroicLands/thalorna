What a vessel costs to build depends on how long it is and on how hard it is to make. The length is measured; the difficulty is a single rating, the complexity factor.

## Complexity Factor

The **complexity factor** (_n_) is one number for how much skill and labor a type of vessel demands. Several things feed into it: the hull method, how many decks there are, how elaborate the rig is and how much is fitted inside. A plain barge has a factor of 3 and the most elaborate ships have 8. All sizes of one type share a factor.

## Time

**Time** is the labor a vessel takes to build, counted in man-days. Dividing by the number of workers gives the days on the calendar: 12 workers on a hull that takes 1,800 man-days finish it in 150 working days.

> **Time = n × Length² ÷ 6**

## Cost

**Cost** is the whole price of a finished vessel, wood, hardware and wages together, in [[miscgear-pence|Pence]].

> **Cost = n × Length²**

Cost in Pence is six times the time in man-days. A [[miscgear-gldcrwn|Gold Crown]] is worth 35 Pence, so a large cost is stated in Gold Crowns as well.

**Example.** A hull 50 feet long with a complexity factor of 4 takes 4 × 2,500 ÷ 6, about 1,667 man-days, and costs 4 × 2,500 = 10,000 Pence, about 286 Gold Crowns. A hull 120 feet long with a complexity factor of 8 takes 19,200 man-days and costs 115,200 Pence, about 3,291 Gold Crowns.

## Hull Construction

Hulls are built by one of five methods. The method is one of the things the complexity factor takes into account.

**Clinker.** Each plank overlaps the one below it and is fastened to it. The shell rises from the keel first, and slender ribs go in once it is up. Shipwrights of the northern seas work this way.

**Carvel.** The ribs are set up first and the planks are fastened to them side by side with no overlap, leaving a smooth skin. It is the practice on the shores of the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]] and on the ocean coasts of [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia Region]].

**Mortise-and-tenon.** The planks are joined to each other with hardwood tenons set into cut slots, so the shell is built before the frame. The result is exceptionally strong and costs a great deal of labor. The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] builds this way.

**Sewn-plank.** Cord made of coconut fiber ties the planks together in place of nails, so the hull gives under strain and springs back. The long-distance traders of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]] are built this way.

**Bulkheaded.** Walls across the hull split it into sealed compartments, so a breach floods only one of them and the ship stays afloat. It is the building practice of [[lore-tantsouclt|Tantsou]].
