Every hull of Thalorna's seas is described by three measurements in feet and by the cargo it holds. This note defines the measurements, the formula that turns them into a cargo figure and the unit in which that figure is stated.

## Dimensions

**Length.** How long the hull is from end to end, in feet.

**Beam.** The greatest width of the hull, in feet. On a galley the beam includes any outriggers. On a double-hulled vessel the overall width is the beam, and each hull's own width is recorded separately.

**Draft.** The height from the keel up to the main deck, in feet, which the burthen formula uses. The draft of a fully laden vessel at the waterline is about two-thirds of this figure.

## Burthen

**Burthen** is the cargo capacity of a hull, measured in **tuns**. A tun is the volume of a standard wine cask, and it holds 2,240 pounds.

> **Burthen = Length × Beam × Draft ÷ 100**

For a double-hulled vessel, work out the burthen of one hull from that hull's own beam, then double it.

**Example.** A hull 40 feet long, 11 feet in beam and 5 feet in draft has a burthen of 40 × 11 × 5 ÷ 100 = 22 tuns.

On a warship the burthen counts hull volume, most of which is not cargo space. The space goes to rowers, marines, water and provisions.

## The Seam

**The seam** is the unit of a vehicle's carrying capacity, and every kind of vehicle states its capacity in seams: ships, barges, wagons, carts, sleds and barrows alike.

| Unit  | Equals                                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Seam  | 20 stone, which is 280 pounds, or 5 cubic feet   |
| Stone | 14 pounds                                        |
| Tun   | 8 seams, which is 40 cubic feet, or 2,240 pounds |

A ship's burthen in tuns becomes seams at eight to the tun, so a hull of 22 tuns carries 176 seams. Small carriers fall on small whole numbers: a handcart carries a seam or two, a farm cart four to six, a heavy wagon eight to twelve and a river barge hundreds. The smallest carriers, such as a hand sled or a barrow, carry less than a seam, and their capacity is a decimal: a half seam is 140 pounds.
