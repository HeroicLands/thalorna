A vessel's crew is counted in two parts. The **vessel crew factor** counts the hands that steer, command and work the sails. The **oarsmen** count the rowers. A vessel with no oars has no oarsmen, and a vessel with no sails has only the hands that steer and command.

## Vessel Crew Factor

The vessel crew factor (VCF) is the fewest hands that can run the vessel. Round it to a whole number.

> **VCF = (Length ÷ 10) + 2 per square-rigged mast + 3 per triangular-rigged mast + 1 per battened mast**

Length is in feet. A vessel with no sails has VCF = Length ÷ 10, which pays for the helmsman, the officers and general work; its oarsmen are counted separately. The crew each mast needs is given in [[doc-vesselrigs|Vessel Rigs]].

A sailing vessel at sea carries about twice its VCF, to stand watches and to allow for sickness and losses.

**Example.** A hull 60 feet long with two triangular masts has VCF = 6 + 3 + 3 = 12 and ordinarily sails with about 24.

## Oarsmen

The oarsmen are the rowers it takes to move the vessel by oar. Round it to a whole number. The sailing crew can pull oars when the vessel is rowed.

> **Oarsmen = (Length ÷ 5) × (Beam ÷ 4) × bank multiplier**

Length and beam are in feet.

| Oar arrangement                | Bank multiplier |
| ------------------------------ | --------------- |
| Single bank                    | ×1              |
| Two banks                      | ×1.25           |
| Three banks                    | ×1.6            |
| Polyreme (several men per oar) | ×1.75           |

A vessel that uses a multiplier other than ×1 states it. For a double-hulled vessel the oarsmen of one hull are worked out from that hull's own beam, and the result is doubled. A paddle-wheel vessel's treadmill walkers count as oarsmen, and no bank multiplier applies.

**Example.** A single-banked hull 60 feet long with a beam of 10 feet needs (60 ÷ 5) × (10 ÷ 4) × 1 = 30 oarsmen.
