Most realms that fight at sea do it in borrowed hulls: merchant ships taken into service, refitted to fight and, once the campaign ends, refitted again to carry cargo. The figures for both refits come first here, followed by the reasons that only a rich realm keeps a standing fleet of galleys.

## Refitting for War

| Item               | Rule                                                              |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Price of the refit | One fifth of the price to build the hull                          |
| Work of the refit  | One fifth of the man-days to build the hull                       |
| Cargo space        | Cut by one tenth while the hull is fitted for war                 |
| Troops on board    | Roughly one for every two feet of hull length, on top of the crew |
| Return to trade    | A quarter of the refit's man-days; the cargo space comes back     |

The price and man-days to build the hull come from [[doc-vesselbuild|Shipbuilding Time and Cost]].

**Example.** A merchant hull 80 feet long with a complexity factor of 6 costs 38,400 Pence and takes 6,400 man-days to build. Refitting it for war costs 7,680 Pence and 1,280 man-days. It then holds about 40 soldiers in addition to its crew, and its burthen is a tenth lower until it is returned to trade, which takes 320 man-days.

Two ships that close and grapple settle the matter hand to hand across the rails, and whoever stands higher above the water strikes down into the lower deck. A refit therefore adds height: a raised castle at bow and stern, a fighting top at each masthead and a row of shields along the bulwarks, all of it taken off again when the ship goes back to trade. For the same reason the cargo hulls with the tallest sides are the first choice for a refit, and [[doc-vesseltradtn|Seafaring Traditions]] gives the hulls each people builds.

## Keeping a Galley Fleet

A galley has room for food and water to last only a few days, so it beaches or makes harbor every few days to take more aboard. Over a season of hard rowing its light hull strains and rots, and a fleet that stays in commission needs covered sheds to lie up in, slipways to haul out on and a steady stock of seasoned timber for repairs. All the while its people draw pay and rations: the oarsmen of a big galley run to several hundred, with marines and officers besides, and they are paid and fed for as long as the ship is in commission. Only a realm that taxes widely can meet these bills year after year. The hull is the cheap part, since a galley costs its builder about what a merchant ship as long would cost, and so most realms fight at sea with merchant ships taken into service.
