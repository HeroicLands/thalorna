A **junk** is the seagoing hull of the yards of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]]. Bulkheads divide its hull into sealed compartments, so a breach floods one compartment and leaves the rest dry. It carries battened sails on two or more masts, and its rudder hangs on the sternpost and draws up when the water shoals. A hull is a junk only when it has all three and is built for the open sea. Two hulls meet that measure, the **Wongri** and the **Jari**. The measures and formulas that hold for every vessel are in [[doc-vesselhulls|Vessel Dimensions and Burthen]], [[doc-vesselrigs|Vessel Rigs]] and [[doc-vesselcrew|Vessel Crew and Oarsmen]], and each people's yards are compared in [[doc-vesseltradtn|Seafaring Traditions]].

The names are [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]], and each is given with the label these pages use for it. Every hull here that sails carries the battened rig, and its sailing grade already includes the step that rig gives ([[doc-vesselrigs|Vessel Rigs]]).

## The Two Junks

**Wongri** (Ocean Junk)
: The merchant junk of the long sea routes. Its bottom is V-shaped, its hull is planked in several layers and it carries three to five masts; the largest have cabins for merchants who sail with their goods. When the empire mounts a great campaign at sea it takes Wongri into service to fill out its fleets ([[doc-vesselwar|Merchant Ships in War]]).

**Jari** (War Junk)
: A Wongri built lighter and finer for war, with oars beside its sails for handling in a fight. It escorts convoys and runs down pirates. The yards of Tantsou and of the [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai Empire]] both build it, and the islanders call it the **Rija** ([[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthati]]). The island ships are faster, handier and better armed than the mainland's, and the islanders' sailors are among the finest in the eastern seas.

## The Other Hulls

Five more hulls come from the same yards. Each shares the bulkheads, and those that sail carry the battened rig, but none is a junk: each works the shoals, the canals or the rivers, or goes without a mast.

**Kantong** (Sand Ship)
: A flat-bottomed, square-ended coaster with leeboards and two to five masts. It settles level on the mud when the tide goes out, so it works the shallow coasts and the estuaries that a Wongri cannot enter.

**Haimou** (Tribute Barge)
: The long, narrow canal barge that carries the grain tax to [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]] and the garrisons of the north, built to fit the locks and carrying one or two sails. Its crew scull it, or haulers on the bank tow it, and it rows poorly.

**Tinmou** (Charging Boat)
: A fast rowed assault boat with no mast, its upper works sheathed in rawhide against arrows and fire. It closes with an enemy, grapples and puts its men aboard.

**Twenmou** (Paddle-Wheel Ship)
: A war hull whose wheels are turned by men walking treadmills inside it, out of reach of arrows. It makes way against the current and in a dead calm, and it costs more and takes longer to build than any other hull its size. Its walkers count as oarsmen.

**Shouwou** (Tower Ship)
: A fortress afloat on river and estuary: several decks of fighting galleries, crossbow ports, stone-throwing engines and hundreds of soldiers. It is top-heavy and unsafe in open water, and it moves by sail, by tow or with the current more than by its oars.
