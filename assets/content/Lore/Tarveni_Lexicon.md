# terran_analog: Tarveni is patterned on Castilian in tone (hard finals kept, stressed short vowels broken to ie and ue, consonants voiced between vowels, one acute on every word of two syllables or more) over an older mountain speech shaped like Basque (the trilled rr, the ts, no f, no initial r), in the way Castilian grew up against the Basque country. Its earth mother and the dead who walk the roads at night are patterned on Mari and on the Galician night procession; their words, like every substrate word, are coined to the old speech's shape and checked against Earth word lists, Basque among them. Every inherited word is derived by the tables below from an ancient Vylari root.

On the road down from the passes a company is coming home. The captain is _thrúmar_ and the men are sworn to him for the season, _darándo_, sworn men, by the contract, _semnális_, that every one of them can recite. In the villages they pass, a lantern stands on each threshold, because it is the season when the dead walk the roads, and every word the villagers have for that is older than the tongue the company speaks.

This is the word-hoard of [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarvéni]], the tongue of the [[lore-tarvenanclt|Tarvénans]]. It is the daughter of ancient Vylari that kept the most of the ancestor's grammar after the empire's own speech: three cases, three genders and the dual. It kept them with help. Before the Pelwar came into the peninsula the mountains had a speech of their own, and that speech settled into Tarvéni as the empire's never did, leaving its case habit, its trilled _rr_, its _ts_, and a store of words for the high country, the flocks, and a goddess older than any of the Aurèldían gods. The tables give every word with its source, the rules that make an ancient word Tarvéni, the shape every word of the old speech keeps, and the few words taken back from the empire and from Provènzia.

The words are arranged by what they say about the people who use them: the word given first, because it is the first thing a Tarvénan owes; then the company, the household that runs while the company is away, and the kings and free cities they bargain with; then the shrine, and the old mountain speech beneath it.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has six columns:

- **Form** is the Tarvéni word as it is cited: a noun or an adjective in its nominative, a verb in its infinitive.
- **Source** says where the word came from, as the table of sources below sets out.
- **Class** gives the word's kind and, for a noun, its gender.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in Tarvéni.
- **Built from** is `—` for a word inherited or borrowed whole.
- **Attested** names a page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

Tarvéni is written in these pages as its scribes write it in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Sêmarion]], with one acute on the stressed vowel of every word of two syllables or more. The stress is where the ancient tongue put it, so it can fall on any of the last three syllables, and it tells words apart; the acute is how a reader knows where it falls.

### Letters

| Letter                                         | Kind           | Sounds like                                                       |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `p`, `t`, `k`, `b`, `d`, `g`                   | consonant      | the plain stops; `g` is always hard                               |
| `th`                                           | consonant      | as in _thin_ in Astúrath and the high valleys, `s` elsewhere      |
| `kh`                                           | consonant      | a rasp at the back of the throat; the mark of an educated speaker |

## From ancient Vylari

Tarvéni takes each word from its ancient nominative, and ten changes, in this order, make a Tarvéni word of it. The plain stops `p`, `t` and `k` were voiced between vowels, to `b`, `d` and `g`; only then did the clicked stops lose their click, becoming the plain `t` and `k` that had just been emptied, so the ancient difference between a plain stop and a clicked one survives as the difference between a voiced and a voiceless one. So _sôkhatâ_ (a tax) is _sókhada_, and _k'arâlzon_ (the kept fire) is _karálzo_ with its `k` hard. The breathed `ph` became `h`, `dh` and `gh` lost their breath, `x` became `ks`; `w` became `v`, and `y` fell at the start of a word and became `i` elsewhere. A short `e` or `o` that took the stress in an open syllable broke into `ie` or `ue`. The nominative endings lost their `s` and their length, and every long vowel shortened. The ancient stress stayed where it was.

## The old mountain speech

The speech the Pelwar found in the mountains left no inscription and no name for itself, and nobody has found its people. Its words stand in Tarvéni in a shape no inherited word has. Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel, with at most one consonant closing it; `rr` stands between vowels and `ts` anywhere; and no word of it opens on `r` or holds an `f` or a `p`. The same speech is the reason Tarvéni kept its cases when Provenzal lost all of them: its speakers, learning the Pelwar tongue, held on to the endings their own speech had taught them to listen for.

## The learned and borrowed words

A few words came back into the tongue from the empire's schools and temples, in the empire's spelling, keeping the `z` the imperial tongue made of an ancient `dh` and the hard stops it never voiced: the inherited altar is _kurdális_ and the learned one _kurzális_. A handful more came up the coast from Provènzia with its songs and its silk.

## Words

### The word given

A Tarvénan owes the word he gave before he owes anything else, because it is the only thing about him another man can rely on, and the oath words are the oldest and plainest in the tongue. The contract a company signs is _semnális_, the word the empire uses for any binding between two parties. A man under one is _darándo_, a sworn man, and the ancient word for one under an oath is the word every Tarvénan mercenary is called by abroad.

Breaking a contract is worse than cowardice, because cowardice is a fact about one man and a broken contract is a fact about everyone who marched with him. The penalty is _kuthréza_; the guilt is _damólis_, and it attaches to the company's name and not only to its captain's. The oath itself is sworn on a stone older than the tongue, a _goitsára_, and that word is not Pelwar at all.

### The company and the trade of arms

Taking pay for arms carries no shame in Tarvénia, and the tongue's war words are the ones a hired company uses. _Thrámezo_, a legion in the empire's speech, is a company: a captain and the men who have sworn to him for a season. The captain is _thrúmar_. The companion who rides beside him, _holvétho_, is kin in every way that operates day to day, whatever the blood.

Most of the company's gear keeps its inherited name. The bow, _gáimela_, names the crossbow every Tarvénan company is known for abroad, and the sling, _lírkheza_, is still the herd boy's first weapon. The return from a contract with money in hand is _velzóra_, the word the empire keeps for a general's triumph.

### The household

A household runs for years at a time without its men, and the word for the one who runs it, _zorkhándo_, takes no notice of whether that is a husband or a wife. The household absorbs cousins, fosterlings and the veteran of a dead brother's company, and the tongue has words for all of them. _Aipséna_, which in the empire is a legal adoption, is the taking of anyone into a household, and _sorethándis_, those of one blood, is used with a straight face of people who share none.

### Kings, councils and the free city

A Tarvénan wants a king weak enough to be argued with, and the vocabulary of government is the empire's vocabulary with the edges filed off. The kingdom is _orzálo_ and to rule is _orzáthen_, but the free city's council, _kurthéma_, and its vote, _dilmára_, are the words a Tarvénan says with pride. The tax farmer, _sokhadándo_, is no more welcome here than a mage.

### The old mountain speech

Before the Pelwar came into the peninsula the mountains had a speech of their own, and the Tarvénans call it nothing more than the old mountain speech. Its people left no history that anyone has found, and their words are still in Tarvéni, thickest in the high valleys of Astúrath and the marches. They hold the trilled _rr_ and the _ts_ that no inherited word has, and they never open on _r_ or carry an _f_ or a _p_.

Under the gods of the Aurèldían pantheon the mountain villages keep an older one, and every word for her is from this speech. She is _menútsa_, the Mother, and she is kept in caves, _ikórrata_, where offerings, _itsárde_, are left at the mouth. Her wise-women, _tsingárra_, are the village healers a Tarvénan tolerates and the Ordo's mage he does not. On certain nights the dead of a valley walk the roads in a line with a light carried before them, the _gatséra_, and a house sets an _ezkóbe_, a lantern for the dead, on its threshold, _belártsa_, so that they pass by.

The high country's own words are here too: the peaks and the scree, the mountain storm and the hail, the ewe, the ram, the bell on the bellwether and the shepherd who drives the flock up to the summer meadow.

### Speech, song and the learned

Tarvéni is a language of assertion, and a speech, _dokhémo_, is said plainly or not at all. The learned words keep the uvular _kh_ that marks an educated speaker, and a few came back from the empire's schools. Magic is _thórdima_ and a mage _thordimándo_, a word said with the distaste kept for a tax farmer, _sokhadándo_.

The deeds of a company are recited in _oldémo_, a history, the word the empire uses for a book; in Tarvénia it is a long poem sung after supper by someone who was there. The learned _olzémo_, the same word in the empire's spelling, is a chronicle written down, and the two are not thought to be the same kind of thing.
