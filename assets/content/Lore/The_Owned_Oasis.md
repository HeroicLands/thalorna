You come over a rise at dusk and the spring is there: a dozen palms, a ring of green, a pool the width of a cart, and no one. No tent, no fire, no herd, no track in the mud but your own. The camels have smelled it, and the guide you hired at the last well has gone quiet. He does not say why. He gets down from his saddle and begins to look at the ground.

Most of the water in the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] is of this kind. A _bedaq_, "owned water", is a spring or seep too small to settle, and every one belongs to a tribe. It is the tribe's winter fallback and its reserve for a drought, and it stands empty because the tribe is grazing elsewhere and comes only in its season. The owners rarely sit at it. They watch from a ridge more often than a stranger knows, and the first thing a traveler learns about a _bedaq_ is that it is never as empty as it looks.

## The Marks

Ownership is written on the ground in three signs, readable by anyone raised on the steppe and by few strangers.

- **The well-stone.** A flat stone lies over the well-mouth, cut with the tribe's sign. Beneath it the shaft has no rope and no bucket, because the owners carry both away. A stranger who draws water has brought his own rope or let one down, and that is proof of what he intended.
- **The skull-pole.** A horse skull on a pole stands at the approach, facing the way strangers come. It says that this water has a name.
- **The cut palm.** The tribe's sign is cut into the trunk of the oldest palm, high enough that a camel cannot rub it out.

A traveler who sees one of these has found water that has an owner. All three together mean the owners are close.

## Asking for Guest-Right

The custom is old and plain. A traveler who reaches an owned oasis ties a cloth to the skull-pole and waits at the edge, without drawing, without unloading and without lighting a fire. That asks for guest-right. The owners, when they come, may grant it or refuse. A guest who has eaten their salt is protected, even against the host's own kin, and the protection lasts until he leaves their range.

The owners do not hurry. They may take an hour to come down from the ridge, or until the next morning. A party that waits with its hands in sight gets a hearing, and a party that has already drawn water has missed its chance to ask.

## What the Owners Do

What happens to a trespasser depends on what he did.

| What he did                                       | What the owners do                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| camped and drew nothing                           | he pays for the night in a blade, a beast or a day's labor; he may be sold passage on the spot                      |
| drew water                                        | a debt for every skin, paid in beasts or goods, and one of the party is held until it is paid                       |
| grazed his beasts                                 | the beasts are forfeit, or he pays for the grass twice over                                                         |
| fouled the well, cut a palm, or fought the owners | blood; a survivor is taken as a thrall                                                                              |
| carried a tribe's passage token                   | the owners send him to the named well with a rider and charge his tribe's surety; the meeting becomes a negotiation |

The unhappy visit is rarely violent. A dozen riders come in at first light, spread around the camp so that every road is covered, and the eldest of them sits down at the edge of the water and waits to be offered tea. Nothing about it is hurried. The price is named and the party can bargain, and a party that begins by drawing a blade finds that a dozen riders have been joined by two dozen.

## What Else Is at the Water

Creatures gather where water is. [[being-gntcmlsp|Giant camel spiders]] come in at night to the scraps of a camp, [[being-sndstlkr|sand stalkers]] hunt whatever comes to drink, and [[being-bnscrpn|bone scorpions]] wait in the shade of the rocks. The owners know the hours when each is out and keep their own camps well away from the pool. A stranger learns the hours the hard way.

Some wells carry a stone lid with a weight on it. The owners lid a shaft that something lives in, and they say so when asked.

## At the Table

The same spring can open a session three different ways.

- **A thirsty party that knows nothing.** The camels drink, the party sleeps, and the owners come at dawn.
- **A party that knows the cloth.** The party ties it, waits, and gets a first meeting with the steppe on the owners' terms: tea, questions, a price, and a rider offered as guide to a place the party did not know existed.
- **A party carrying a token from the wrong tribe.** The owners read the sign at once. The token is a debt or an insult, depending on which tribe issued it, and the party finds itself in the middle of a feud it did not know it had joined.
