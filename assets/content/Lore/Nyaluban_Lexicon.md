The five clans of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]] keep no books, and this page is the nearest thing to one: the word-hoard of [[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] and of its [[skill-nyalbdrm|Drum-Speech]], the stems the griots and the drum-speakers use, what each ending and prefix makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives among the five clans.

A Nyáluban noun stands in a class its prefix marks, and an ending says what a word has become: _ruwo_ is the lion, and _Ruwozi_ is the clan of the lion, the people who are _of_ it. A name of rank is two words, the head first and the thing it stands for after: _Nyóli Ruwozi_ is the Daughter of the Lion, and _Shorínya Ndáfo_ is the Great Brightness.

The sound of Nyáluban is open and sung. Most syllables are a consonant and a vowel, a word closes on a vowel far more often than not, and a nasal before a stop opens many words: _mb_, _nd_, _nk_. Pitch carries meaning, which is what lets a drum speak it; the pages mark a high tone with the acute and a low one with the grave, and neither changes the letter beneath it.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its plain present. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem or prefix, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stem or word and the ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind        | Sounds like                                                                    |
| ----------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `p`, `t`, `k`           | consonant   | the plain stops, without a breath after them                                   |
| `b`, `d`, `g`           | consonant   | the voiced stops                                                               |
| `b'`, `d'`, `g'`        | consonant   | the implosives: _b_, _d_, _g_ drawn inward, with a hollow, popping sound       |
| `f`, `s`, `sh`, `h`     | consonant   | _f_, _s_, _ship_, _h_                                                          |
| `z`, `zh`               | consonant   | _z_, and the _s_ of _measure_                                                  |
| `x`                     | consonant   | a rasp at the back of the mouth, the voiced partner of a throat-clearing sound |
| `m`, `n`, `ny`, `ng`    | consonant   | the nasals: _ny_ as in _canyon_, _ng_ as in _sing_                             |
| `l`, `r`                | consonant   | the liquids; _r_ is tapped                                                     |
| `w`, `y`                | consonant   | the glides                                                                     |
| `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` | short vowel | _father_ said short, _bet_, _machine_ said short, _for_, _put_                 |
| `ā`, `ē`, `ī`, `ō`, `ū` | long vowel  | the same vowels held longer                                                    |
| `ã`, `ẽ`, `ĩ`, `õ`, `ũ` | nasal vowel | the same vowels sounded through the nose                                       |

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel, a single consonant or one of the pairs below, and closes on a vowel or on one of the consonants below. A bound stem, written with a closing hyphen, closes on anything.

| Edge                           | Sounds                                                           |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pairs that may open a word     | `sp`, `st`, `sk`, `sm`, `sn`, `mb`, `mp`, `nd`, `nt`, `nk`, `nz` |
| Consonants that close a word   | `m`, `n`, `ny`, `ng`, `p`, `t`, `k`                              |
| Vowels that may stand together | `ai`, `au`, `ei`, `eu`, `oi`, `ou`                               |

## Building words

Nouns fall into classes marked by prefixes, and a nasal prefix before a stop is how many words come to open on _mb_, _nd_ or _nk_; the [[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] note sets the classes out. Two moves make every built word on this page. **An ending on a stem:** each ending says what the stem has become. **A head and what it stands for:** a name of rank or of a place sets the head word first and the word it stands for after it, with no ending between them, and _nu_ joins a person to a clan.

### Suffixes

| Suffix  | Stem  | Makes                                         | Example                          |
| ------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| `-zi`   | added | the people of it: a clan, named for its totem | _ruwo_ > _Ruwozi_, the lion-clan |
| `-inya` | added | the quality of it: a noun of what a thing is  | _shor-_ > _shorinya_, brightness |
| `-o`    | added | a man's name                                  | _tabes-_ > _Tabéso_              |
| `-a`    | added | a woman's name                                | _lushey-_ > _Lushéya_            |

## Making names

**A person** carries a given name and a clan or tribe, joined by _nu_: [[being-tabesnfnjr|Tabéso nu Fénjara]] is Tabéso, of the Fénjara. A man's given name commonly closes on _-o_, and a woman's on _-a_; the name lists in [[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] hold the names in use, and a name of standing such as _Nyóli Ruwozi_ passes from one holder to the next.

**A clan** of the Pact is named for its totem with _-zi_, the Sengala apart. **A place** is named by a head word and what it stands for: _Heto Lubemo_, the enclosure of the council.

**The source** is named the same way: _Shorínya Ndáfo_, the Great Brightness, a quality and the word that says how far it reaches.
