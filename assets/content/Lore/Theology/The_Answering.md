## Overview

The first spoonful of every meal in a Maple People longhouse goes into the fire, and the eater says to whom: to the dead of the house, to the corn, to the fire itself. Everything that lives or moves, the Maple People hold, wants something. The dead want food. The soul wants what it dreams. The maple wants to be thanked before it is tapped, the lake before it is crossed, and the sickness that has got into a child wants to be given something and sent away. Each of these is a power (_tsiotarie_), and the faith of the [[lore-osetakonaclt|Osetakona]] is the work of finding out what each power wants and giving it. They call it the Answering (_tiatorenhsa_).

A visitor to the Grove sees little of it at first. There is no temple, no priesthood and no image to bow to. What a visitor sees is tobacco: a pinch dropped into the river before a canoe goes out, a pinch on a stump before a tree is cut, and a pinch burned on the fire before anyone speaks in council, because smoke is the one thing every power can hear.

## The Grandmother and Her Sons

In the beginning there was water below and sky above, and nothing between. The Grandmother (_otsiterie_) lived above the sky, and in a hungry season she let a maple seed fall through a hole in it, spinning as maple seeds spin. The [Great Sturgeon]{.name data-type="lore/deity"} rose from the deep water to catch it, and the mud on the Sturgeon's back became the earth. The maple grew there first, and then the Grandmother came down after it and planted corn, beans and squash at its foot. She is the moon now, gone back up to watch, and the clan mothers tap the first maple of the year by her light.

The Grandmother bore two sons, the Warm Brother (_teria tsiaken_) and the Cold Brother (_otsiskwen tsiaken_). The Warm Brother made the things that grow: the leaves, the corn, the berries, the running fish and the long days. The Cold Brother made the things that last: the ice, the rock, the flint, the rapids and the long nights. They fought over the world from the first day, and neither could beat the other. In the end they talked, from one thaw to the next, until they agreed to take turns: the Warm Brother has the day and the summer, and the Cold Brother the night and the winter.

The Maple People do not call either brother good. Their proof is the maple. Sap runs only in the thaw, when the nights still freeze and the days are warm, which is to say only when both brothers are at work, and a spring that comes too fast gives no sugar. The rememberers tell the brothers' talk as the first council, and the Grove's councils follow it: neither side wins, both are heard, and the agreement is what they boil down to.

## The Great Sturgeon

The Great Sturgeon (_otsitsen kahten_) still carries the earth, and when it rolls in its sleep the ground shakes. It lives in the deepest of the great lakes and owns everything under the water: the fish, the drowned, and the copper. The Copper People of [[place-kahnekwaroten|Hammerstone]] hold that the pure copper of the [Copper Lake]{.name data-type="place/feature"} is the Sturgeon's shed scales, which is why it shines, and why a digger leaves tobacco at the pit before he takes any. A canoe that crosses the Copper Lake without tobacco on the water is a canoe the Sturgeon may tip over. The Sturgeon clan claims the Great Sturgeon as its own, and its clan mothers say so at every Feast of Bones.

## The Faces

The forest is full of powers with no shape, and the commonest are the Faces (_tsiakwen_): powers of the deep woods who wander through the villages unseen and bring sickness, bad luck and quarrels, and who can take away what they bring. A Face has a twisted mouth, a crooked nose and copper eyes, and it can be seen only in a dream.

The healers (_ehniatehen_) answer them. A healer is one who was sick and was cured by the healers, and who joins their society at the cure: the price of being healed is to heal. Each healer carves a Face from the burl of a living maple, after burning tobacco at its foot to ask the tree's leave, and cuts it free only when it is finished, so that the Face is still alive. Wearing the Face, the healer can speak to the powers it belongs to.

The healers go through every village twice a year, at the thaw and in the Long Night, wearing their Faces and shaking rattles of dried corn husk. They go into every longhouse, sprinkle the sick with sap and warm water, and drive the Faces' mischief out through the smoke hole. A household that has a sickness between those visits sends for a healer, who comes with a Face in a bag and asks the sick person what they have dreamed.

The eldest healer at [[place-ostakaten|Ostakaten]] is [Kwentsatiatoren]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Moon Answers, a woman of the Porcupine clan who was healed of a fever at six and has worn a Face for fifty years. She is said to read a dream from its first three words.

## The Dreams

Every person's soul (_ohnionsaro_) has wishes of its own, and it says them in dreams (_kahsentsia_). A wish ungranted makes the soul sick and then the body, so the Answering begins at home: a person who wakes from a strong dream tells it at the fire, and the household does what the dream asks, whether it is a dish, a visit, a dance or a name. A dream nobody can read goes to the healers.

The Long Night at midwinter is the great answering of the year. For days the people go from longhouse to longhouse telling their dreams as riddles, and the households try to guess each one and give the dreamer what it asks. A dream guessed and answered is a soul made well for the year. The Maple People say a village that guesses well is a village that is healthy in spring.

## The Rites

**The Sap Feast** opens the year. The clan mothers tap the first maple by the Grandmother's light, and the first sap boiled goes into the fire for the dead and to the elders.

**Tobacco** is the speech of the living to the powers, because tobacco is the one gift the powers cannot grow for themselves. It is burned before a council, dropped in water before a crossing, laid at a tree before cutting and at a pit before digging.

**The healers' rounds** at the thaw and in the Long Night drive out the Faces' sickness.

**The Long Night** answers the dreams.

**The Feast of Bones** sets the dead free to walk west along the river of stars (_tsikahse ohiatse_) to the village of the dead.

## The Closed Lodge

The Answering has one rule above the others, and it is the Grove's rule for everything: a power is answered in the open. A healer works in the longhouse before the household, a dream is told at the fire, and tobacco is burned before the council. A person who deals with a power in secret is a witch, because the only reason to hide from your kin is to do them harm.

The hunting bands coming south into the [[place-tsitarargn|Pine Country]] answer their powers differently. Their spirit-doctors shut themselves at night in a small closed lodge of bark and poles, and from inside it they call the powers in. The lodge shakes, voices speak from it that are not the doctor's, and the doctor comes out knowing where the caribou are, where a lost man lies, or what a distant kinsman is doing. The bands hold it the most honest of practices, because the powers speak for themselves.

The Pine People's speaker [Okwiahasterio]{.name data-type="being/npc"} has let one such doctor build his lodge outside the village's palisade, and goes to hear him. The healers of the Grove call it witchcraft: a power answered in the dark, behind bark walls, where no one can see what is asked or given. Okwiahasterio answers that the doctor's lodge is the most public thing in the village, since everyone hears it shake. The question has gone to the great council, and the great council is still talking. It has grown harder this year, because the Dawn People have quietly asked whether a doctor in a closed lodge can see across the sea, and where four people taken over it might be.
