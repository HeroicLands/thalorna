## Overview

Every house of the Cedar People began with a meeting. In the first days, after the [Great Water]{.name data-type="lore/history"} fell, beings of the sea and the sky came down to the empty beaches, took off their masks of feather or fin, and stood there as people: an eagle at [[place-tsilkasis|Tsilkasis]], an orca at [[place-malkisis|Malkisis]], a thunderbird on the islands at [[place-gwemlasis|Gwemlasis]]. Each became the first chief of a house, and each gave the house its crest, its first names and its dances. The [[lore-kelwanukclt|Kelwanuk]] call such a being a power (_temkwas_), and the faith of the coast is the keeping of those meetings. They call it the Meeting (_nakpulalas_).

The powers are not gone. They still live in their own houses under the sea and in the forest, wearing their masks, and they still come to the coast: as the salmon who visit the rivers every summer, as the whales who give themselves to the harpooner, and in winter as the dancers who wear their faces. Everything a house owns, the names, the crests, the fishing places, it owns because a power gave it, and the power is owed courtesy for as long as the house lasts.

## The Powers

The Cedar People name many powers, and every house knows its own best. A few belong to the whole coast.

**The [Cedar Woman]{.name data-type="being/npc"}** walked into the forest in the first winter and stood until she became the first red cedar, and gave the people her bark, her bones and her arms for clothes, planks and canoes, asking nothing back. She is the reason the coast holds giving to be the first good. A carver speaks to a cedar before he cuts it, tells it what it will become and asks it to give, and a woman stripping bark takes one strip from each tree and thanks it.

**The [Thunderbird]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}** (_gwemlas_) lives on the highest peaks. The beat of its wings is the thunder, the flash of its eyes the lightning, and in the stories it lifts whales out of the sea in its claws and carries them to the mountains to eat. The Thunderbird House of the islands descends from it, and a whaling chief prays to it before the spring hunt.

**The [Sea Chief]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}** (_tsawa hemkis_) is the richest being in the world. He lives in a great house at the bottom of the sea, with copper for its walls, and he owns everything under the water: the salmon, who live as people in his houses through the winter and put on their fish skins to visit the coast each summer; the whales; the copper; and the drowned. The [Village of the Dead]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} lies beside his house, off the end of the world, and the dead live there as on the coast, waiting for their names to be lifted. A house that wastes salmon or throws their bones on the midden has insulted his guests, and the run fails.

**The [Hungry Power]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}** (_lak temkwas_) lives at the far end of the forest, where no house's land reaches. It is never satisfied. In the winter season it takes young people out of the villages into the forest, and they come back as Wild Ones (_gwaltsis_): biting, screaming and hungry, possessed by it, until the songs of the winter dances bring them back to themselves.

## The Healers

A healer (_hemnis_) is a person a power has taught. The teaching comes in a sickness or in a dream, often both: a person lies near death, travels in a dream to a power's house, and comes back with a song and the power's help. A healer cures by singing that song, by sucking out a sickness the power shows them, and, in the worst cases, by going into a trance and travelling to the Village of the Dead to bring back a soul that a hungry dead kinsman has taken. A healer is paid in blankets and charges a chief more.

The healers belong to no house's order. A slave can be taught by a power as readily as a chief, and the coast says so, and has never known what to do about it.

The healer whom the northern inlets send for is [Dimasnilkwa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sees the Dream, a woman of the Wolf House at [[place-mukwasis|Mukwasis]], who was taught by the Thunderbird in a fever at fifteen. She has gone to the Village of the Dead more times than she will say, and she charges every chief exactly the same, which the chiefs take as an insult and pay.

## The Rites

**The Welcoming** greets the first salmon of each run: it is laid on a new cedar mat, sprinkled with eagle down, sung to as a guest of the Sea Chief, eaten by the whole village, and its bones go back into the river whole, so that it can go home and say it was treated well.

**The morning bath** is taken in the sea before dawn by young men and by anyone who hunts sea game, scrubbing with hemlock until the skin bleeds, to be clean before the powers. A whaling harpooner bathes every night of the winter before the spring hunt, and prays to the whale to give itself to him.

**The fire for the dead** takes a little of every evening meal, so that the smoke feeds the dead in the Village of the Dead.

**The winter dances** (_hilamala_) fill the dark months. The houses put away their summer names and take their winter ones, and the dancers come out in the masks of the powers to act the first meetings again. For those months the dance societies rule each village, and their heads, the Keepers of the Masks (_gunismis_), are often commoners; a chief who has no place in a society sits at the back of his own house.

**The [Bringing Back]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}** ends the winter season. The Wild Ones the Hungry Power took at its start are drawn back out of the forest by the songs, brought into the dance house, and danced and sung over for four nights until they are tame. A Wild One brought back has a dance place for life.

## The Boy of Malkisis

At the start of last winter the Hungry Power took a boy from Malkisis, and the boy was a slave. He was born in the Orca House to a woman taken in a raid on the oak valleys of the south, and like every slave he had no name. He disappeared into the forest on the first night of the dances, and he came out of it four days later, wild, biting the dancers who went to meet him.

The Keeper of the Masks of Malkisis, [Gunistalwa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Mask Speaks, a commoner, sang him back over four nights as he would any Wild One, and gave him a dance place and a winter name. In summer the boy is a slave again, with no name. In winter he dances in the Orca House with a seat that no chief can take from him, because the Hungry Power gave it.

The coast cannot agree what happened. The Eagle House says that a power meets a person, and a slave is not a person, so whatever came out of the forest was not taken by the Hungry Power, and the Bringing Back was a mockery. Gunistalwa answers that he did not choose the boy, the power did, and that the Eagle House is welcome to go to the far end of the forest and tell the Hungry Power its mistake. The chief of the Orca House has not freed the boy, and has not said why.
