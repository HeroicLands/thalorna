## Overview

The day of the One Fire begins at the door. Before anyone eats, the head of each household steps outside, faces east and greets the sun as it clears the trees, and the household's first act is to feed its hearth. The Tokchili call their faith Fire-Keeping (_tokchi hapaka_): the keeping of the fire that came down from the sun, in every hearth, on every mound and in every person who must come to it clean. The [[lore-tokchiliclt|Tokchili]] believe that every fire in [[affiliation-tokchiaska|Tokchi Aska]], the One Fire, is a piece of the sun, and almost every rite they keep is a way of feeding it, guarding it or keeping it clean.

The faith has two kinds of practitioner and they do not get on. The Keepers of the Fire tend the fire on the great mound at [[place-hayopokta|Hayo Pokta]], recite the line of the Suns and bury the dead. The _iklilo_ (knowers) cure the sick, read dreams and deal with what lives under the river. Most households need both, and pay both.

## The Three Worlds

The Keepers teach that the world has three levels. Above is the Upper World of the sky, where the sun, the moon and the stars live, and the Thunderers hunt. Below is the Under World, beneath the water, cold and dim, where the Water Panther lives with the drowned and the hungry dead. Between them floats This World, an island of earth on the water, held up by the sky above it and fed by the water beneath it.

A mound is the world in small. It is earth carried up out of the water, with a fire on top of it, and the Tokchili raise their mounds as their first ancestors raised the [Old Mound]{.name data-type="place/site"}: to lift the fire above the flood. Every basket of earth a household carries to its town's mound is a basket that keeps the Under World down and the fire up, and the Keepers say so every morning the work crews go up the ramp.

The two outer worlds are not good and evil. The Upper World is orderly, bright and pure, and it is also dry and pitiless. The Under World is disordered, dark and dangerous, and it is also the source of the rivers, the fish, the floods that renew the fields, and the roots that heal. This World needs both and must keep them apart. Fire and water must never mix: no one puts out a hearth with water, no one spits in a fire, and a person coming from a death or a birth goes to the river before going near a hearth.

## The Powers

The Tokchili hold that many powers move in the three worlds. Five of them are named in every town.

**Hayo, the Sun.** The sun is the eldest power and the source of all fire, warmth and growth, and the Suns of the paramount house claim it as their ancestor. The Keepers teach that the sun does not speak or bargain. It is greeted at dawn, fed with the first of every harvest through the fire, and attended, in the end, by the dead who go up with it.

**[Ichasa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Fire-Bringer.** A woman of the river climbed to the sun and came down carrying a coal in a pot of wet clay, and her brothers carried earth until there was a hill above the flood to set it on. She is the mother of the Suns' line and the first Keeper, and at the New Fire the women who rake out the old ashes call her name. Clay pots for carrying coals are made in her shape, a woman with her hands round her belly.

**The Thunderers.** The thunder (_tikpola_) is the voice of the sun's warriors, who hunt in the storm clouds and strike the Under World's creatures with lightning (_tikpoli_) when they come up too far. A tree struck by lightning belongs to them. The Keepers gather its wood, and the New Fire on the great mound is kindled from it.

**Kiyo, the Falcon.** The falcon is the sun's striker, the bird-man with spread wings and a war club who strikes down the sun's enemies. The falcon dancers dance him before every war, the Suns are buried with copper plates showing him, and a warrior who takes a captive is said to have been, for that moment, the falcon.

**The Water Panther.** Under the river lives the _wiko yohaka_, the Water Panther: a great cat with horns, scales on its back and a tail so long that it coils like a serpent. It owns the fish, the floods and the healing roots that grow in the mud, and it drags down swimmers, overturns canoes and takes the dead who are buried low. Every coiled serpent the One Fire's engravers cut on a shell gorget is the Water Panther's tail, worn to keep it at a distance and to claim a little of its power, and the coiled serpents are what gave the One Fire its southern name of the Serpent People. The swamp towns of the [[place-wikopihirgn|Dark Water]] carve it on every post, and the heartland's Keepers suspect them of courting it.

## The Keepers of the Fire

The Keepers (_tokchi hapilo_) are a hereditary priesthood of Noble rank. A Keeper is born into one of the Keeper lineages, learns the recitations from his mother's brother from boyhood, and is raised to keep the fire when the Keepers of his town judge his memory good enough. Each town of any size has a Keepers' house beside its mound, and the Keepers of [[place-hayopokta|Hayo Pokta]] keep the great fire, the temple where the bones of the dead Suns lie and the line of the Suns.

The Eldest Keeper of the great mound is [Ipasho]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an old man with a famous memory and a dry tongue, who has recited the line of the Suns at forty New Fires and buried two Great Suns. He holds that the Third Gathering has lasted because its Suns kept the rites, and that a Great Sun buried without a full following is a Great Sun sent up alone.

The Keepers conduct everything the nation does together: the New Fire, the rites at the full and the dark of the moon, the oaths sworn before the fire, the funerals of the Suns and Nobles, and the judgment of sacrilege. They are paid in maize from the granaries and in offerings from households, and they are rich.

## The Knowers

An _iklilo_, a knower, is called and never born. The call comes as a dream, a long illness, a near drowning or a lightning strike survived, and the person who has had it goes to an older knower to learn what it means. Men and women both become knowers, commoners far more often than Nobles, and a knower holds no rank by it.

Knowers work for households. They cure with roots and herbs, with sweating and cold water, and by scratching the skin with garfish teeth to let a sickness out. They read dreams, find lost things and lost people, say which day is good for a journey or a marriage, and speak to the dead who are troubling a house. Above all they deal with the Under World, which the Keepers will not touch: they go down to the river at night to bargain with the Water Panther for a sick child, a drowned man's body or a run of fish. They are paid in maize, skins and copper, according to the household's means and the knower's reputation.

The Keepers distrust them. A knower who can bargain with the Water Panther can also send it, and every accusation of witchcraft in the One Fire falls on a knower first. The most famous knower in the nation is [Kachima]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [[place-ishochi|Ishochi]], a woman of sixty who is said to have gone into [Ne' P'uqan]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the Black Water, for three days and come back with a drowned Mound Chief's son alive. The Keepers have tried twice to have her put out. Both times the swamp towns refused to hand her over.

## The Rites

**The New Fire.** At midsummer, when the first green maize is ready, every hearth in the nation is put out for four days of fasting, cleansing and forgiveness. On the fourth morning the Keepers of the great mound lay four logs of lightning-struck wood with their ends pointing to the four quarters, kindle a fire where the ends meet with a fire drill, and the Eldest Keeper recites the line of the Suns over it. Runners carry its coals in clay pots to every town of the nation, and every household relights its hearth from them.

**[Going to the River]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}.** At dawn, before the greeting, a person who needs to be clean goes down to the river, faces east and dips seven times. Everyone goes to the river on the morning of the New Fire. Warriors go before a war and after it, mourners after a death, the sick at a knower's direction, and a household whose dead are troubling it goes together.

**The Black Drink.** Men drink a strong brew of holly leaves before councils, wars and the great rites, and vomit it up to come to the business clean. The Keepers brew it in shell cups engraved with the Falcon.

**The Falcon Dance.** Before a war the falcon dancers dance the bird-man through the plaza in copper headdresses and falcon-feather cloaks, and the Falcon Chief leads them.

**[The Calling of Thunder]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}.** In a drought the Keepers of a town climb its mound and burn tobacco and cedar on the fire to call the Thunderers. If the drought holds, the town sends for a knower, who goes down to the river to ask the Water Panther why it is holding back the water. The Keepers do not attend.

**The Sun's Funeral.** When a Sun dies, the Keepers lay him on the great mound and his attendants go up with him, so that he crosses to the sky attended.

## The Quarrel over the Attended

The funerals divide the faith, and the division runs between its two halves. The Keepers teach that a Sun must go to the sky attended, and that any _tiwilo_ (attendant) who dies with him goes up with him into the light. The knowers teach that only the willing go up. A captive killed on the mound who did not choose to die is pulled down by the Water Panther, they say, and becomes one of the hungry dead under the river, and a funeral of captives feeds the Under World. On their account the [Funeral of the Hundred]{.name data-type="lore/history"} sent fifty-three hungry dead under the river at once, and the floods and the Scattering that followed were the Under World answering.

The shell towns hold the knowers' teaching to a household, and so do many commoners of the heartland, who are the households most likely to lose a servant or a captive kinsman at a funeral. The Keepers answer that the knowers are cheapening the rite to sell their own services. The old Great Sun, [Opahiko]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is dying, the Eldest Keeper wants him buried with a full following, and every knower in the nation is being asked by every household what will happen to those who go with him.
