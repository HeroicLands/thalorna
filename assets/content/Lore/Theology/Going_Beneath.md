## Overview

A seal is a person. It has a house beneath the sea, a family, a sight that sees through ice and a memory for how it was treated, and when it comes up to a hunter's harpoon it comes because it chose to. The [[lore-tikupilaritclt|Tikupilarit]] arrange their lives around that choice: the drink of fresh water given at the door, the bladder kept and sent home, the share carried to every house. All of that is done on the surface, by everyone, every day. What the animals think of it is known only beneath, and the faith of the Ice-Edge People is named for the journey that finds out. They call it Going Beneath (_akluqun_).

The one who goes is the shaman (_aklutiq_), a man or a woman, and the shaman is the only person in a village whose work is done in the dark. Everyone needs a shaman, and nobody quite trusts one.

## The Houses Beneath

Under the sea, out past the ice edge, the seals live in houses like the houses of a village, with a men's house of their own where they sit in the winter and talk about the hunters. A seal that was given a drink and sent home in its bladder at the [Bladder Feast]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} comes back into its house and tells the others it was treated well, and in spring they put on their skins and come up to that hunter. A seal that was boasted over, or whose bones went to the dogs, tells them the opposite. Walrus, the bearded seal and the white whale keep houses of their own, and each kind keeps its own counsel.

Beneath the tundra, at the far end of the land, live the caribou, the foxes and the birds. They do not come out of their houses by themselves. The [Keeper in the Moon]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"} lets them out, through a hole in the sky that only he can open, a few at a time, to the villages that have kept the Rules. He is the spirit with a hand on the masks of the [Asking]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}: a hand with a hole through its palm, so that some of the animals slip through his fingers even when he closes them.

Below them all is the village of the dead, dark and hungry, where the dead wait to be fed through their namesakes. And over all of it is [Raven]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"} (_kalgaq_), who made the land and lost the sun, and who is not prayed to by anyone. He is in every story of a thing gone wrong.

## The Shaman

A shaman is chosen by something under the sea. The call comes as a drowning survived, a fall through the ice, a sickness of a winter, or a person lost for days on the floes who comes back and cannot say where they were. Something met them there, and offered to help them, and the person who accepted is a shaman from then on. They go to an old shaman to learn what it means, and spend years at it.

What met them becomes the first of their helpers. A shaman has several, gathered over a life: the soul of a seal, a loon, a fox, a drowned man, the wind. A helper is the shaman's guide beneath and their eyes there. Each one is carved in ivory no longer than a finger and sewn to the inside of the shaman's parka, where nobody else sees it, and a shaman's strength is the strength of their helpers.

**The descent.** A shaman goes beneath in the men's house, at night, with the village on the benches. The lamps are put out. Two men bind the shaman hand and foot with a harpoon line (_tapluk_) and lay them by the fire pit with a drum (_tumvak_) beside them. The drum begins with nobody touching it. The village sings. Voices that are not the shaman's come out of the dark, in the speech of animals and the speech of the dead, and the floor creaks as though something heavy were crawling under it. Then the voices stop, and the drum stops, and the people on the benches sit in the dark and wait, sometimes until morning. When the lamps are lit again the shaman is sitting up, loose, with the line coiled beside them, and tells the village what they saw.

**What a shaman brings back** is what the animals want. The seals have seen a man at Kavrumiq throw a pup's bones on the midden. The Keeper in the Moon is angry about a fox skinned alive. The walrus are tired of hearing their size boasted of. A shaman also describes what they saw for the masks: the face of the seals' chief, the Keeper's hand, a helper. The mask makers (_talquntiq_) carve each mask to that description for a single Asking, and it is left on the tundra afterward.

**Healing** is the same work in small. A sick person has lost their soul, or something has been put into them. The shaman goes beneath for the soul and puts it back through the top of the head, or sucks out what was put in, a sliver of bone, a knot of hair, and shows it to the house. The family pays with a share of its next seal.

**Sending** is the other side of healing. A shaman who can go beneath for a soul can send a helper to take one, and every death in a village that has quarrelled with its shaman is remembered. The men's house can try a shaman for it, and some have been killed, by their own families, so that no feud follows.

## The Telling

The commonest thing a shaman brings back is a breach. The seals stay away from a village for one reason, which is that somebody in it broke a Rule and hid it, and the breach hangs in the seals' house like smoke in a men's house with the door shut. Until it is told, the house cannot be cleaned, and the seals will not come up through it.

So a shaman who has found a breach calls the Telling (_kaliqun_). The village gathers in the men's house, the women on the floor below the benches, and the shaman says what kind of thing was done, and not by whom: a seal was cut on a day of mourning; a woman who had miscarried did not say so; a man ate a hunter's share in secret. Then the village waits. One by one, those with a breach on them stand and tell it. Each one told is a little of the smoke let out. People tell breaches nobody asked for, years old, because one breach told in the hearing of the whole village is counted as nothing, and one breach found out is counted as the reason everyone went hungry.

The shaman says when enough has been told. If the breach the shaman named is never told, the shaman names the person at the next dark of the moon, and that person is sung about at every feast of the winter. Very few wait that long.

## The Strongest Shaman

The coast holds that the strongest shaman alive is [Pinaqaklu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Wind Diving, a woman of [[place-napamiq|Napamiq]], the island village of stilt houses on the cliff. She was blown off the ice on a floe as a girl and was gone for nine days, and she has gone beneath more times than anyone can count since. Villages send men across the ice in late winter to see the Asking danced at Napamiq, to her masks, and her Tellings are said to empty a men's house of every breach in it by morning.

Last winter the boat-holders of [[place-imlakkagaq|Imlak Kagaq]], Whale Point, sent for her. Their leading boat-holder, [Tikumalu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Ice Running, holds that the whales chose the Whale Point crews when they led them east, and that the [Taking]{.name data-type="lore/history"} was the whales' choice and no breach. He asked Pinaqaklu to go beneath to the whales' house and ask them. She went. When the lamps were lit again she said that she would tell what the whales said at a Telling, and only at a Telling held by Whale Point and [[place-kavrumiq|Kavrumiq]] together, in one men's house, with both villages on the benches. Neither village has asked the other.
