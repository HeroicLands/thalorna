The **Ithári** are the oldest thing in the world's record that can be called a people. They were on
[[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]] before 10,000 BF and gone by 5,100 BF, and for most of that span nothing
else on the world was keeping an account of anything. What is known of them is known from two sources:
what they built, which is still here; and what the Kaliharans say, who are the only people they ever
spoke to at length.

The name is Kalihári and means **"Those Who Came Before."** The Ithári's own name for themselves is not
recorded. Neither is what they looked like, how many of them there were, how long any one of them lived,
or whether the word names a people, a profession or a crew.

## The arrival

The Kaliharan record opens with their coming and says nothing of what preceded it. They arrived from
somewhere that is not Thalorna, settled on [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], and did not conquer it —
there was nothing on the island to conquer. The first humans they met there were scattered bands living
in the most rudimentary conditions, and the Ithári took them in.

The Kaliharans describe them as people rather than as gods or spirits: recognisably mortal in their
concerns and conduct, capable of impatience and of humour, and in no way interested in being
worshipped. That description has been transmitted for nearly ten thousand years by custodians who never
met one, and it is the only description there is.

## The works, and the one substance

Everything the Ithári left is made of the same thing. The Kaliharans call it **ithári'sul**, "precursor
stone," and it resembles stone in the way that a word resembles the thing it names. It is seamless —
no joints, no mortar, no tool-marks, no grain. It does not weather. Some of it is warm to the touch,
and some of it is faintly luminous in the dark, and the pieces that do one do not reliably do the
other.

It is found nowhere else on Thalorna. No quarry has produced it, no smith has matched it, and no
assayer who has been allowed near a fragment has been able to say what it is. The Kaliharans do not
regard this as a mystery, because in their understanding ithári'sul is part of the world exactly as a
reef or a river is, placed where it stands by the Ithári as part of the whole. Everyone else regards it
as the single hardest fact in the world's possession.

## No mechanism

The works have no moving parts. This is the point on which every scholar who has studied them arrives
at the same place and stops.

They are not machines: there is nothing inside them to turn, no linkage, no store, no fuel, no wear.
They are not arcane apparatus either, in any sense the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] or the
[[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] recognise — they answer to no process either Elder Race knows, they draw on
nothing an adept can detect, and they do not respond to the Aura being worked near them. A structure
that calms a monsoon calms it. A structure that makes a sea-approach unnavigable makes it unnavigable.
Ask how, and there is no how: nothing to open, nothing to dismantle, nothing to study but the effect
itself.

Whether this is technology so far past the world's understanding that it cannot be recognised as
technology, or magic of a kind nobody else has ever practised, is a question that has been argued for
centuries without producing an argument. The two possibilities cannot be told apart from outside, and
there is no inside.

## The failures

The works also stop. Not all at once, not by any pattern anyone has established, and not for any reason
that can be found afterward. An [[affiliation-kalihara#the-ancient-structures|installation]] that has performed the same office
reliably for a hundred generations dims, cools, and is simply inert, and the fragment broken from it afterward is the same
substance it always was.

The [[affiliation-tharivaan|Ithári'kaan]], who tend them, have no repair to attempt.
They know the procedures and not the principles, and a procedure that has kept a structure working
since before the Elder Races landed may one day produce nothing at all. That the works function is
inexplicable. That they cease is inexplicable in exactly the same degree, and by the same absence of
anything to examine. The custodians count the failures and speak of the count to nobody.

## Kalihara, and the strangeness of it

[[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] is the only place on Thalorna where the Ithári raised humans. That is
the fact most worth sitting with, because they were here for something close to five thousand years and
they did it once.

What they made there was not a people improved but a civilization invented: agriculture, medicine,
governance, a language with no relative anywhere on the world, a script beneath the script, and
**[[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]]** — a disciplined account of the world as a single living whole,
to be observed and tended rather than mastered or appeased. It arrived complete. Nothing on Kalihara
shows the long clumsy ascent that every other human tradition shows, because there was not one.

Why is not recorded. The Kaliharans hold that they were taught because teaching was the right way to
treat what the Ithári found, and that Thári'vaan is true independently of who first said it. Read from
outside, the island looks less like a gift than an instrument — a society built to a specification,
settled on a landmass its makers never expanded beyond, and handed the maintenance of works whose
purpose it was never told. Both readings fit every fact in evidence. The Kaliharans have heard the
second and do not engage with it.

## The sites elsewhere

They did not confine themselves to the island. Ithári'sul is found across Thalorna, at scattered sites
in places with no connection to Kalihara and no memory of anything having been there — a seamless
structure in country nobody settled, a device in a cave, an installation doing something local and
unexplained to the weather or the water or the ground.

Almost nowhere are these known for what they are. A people who live beside one generally have a name
for it in their own tongue and a story that has nothing to do with precursors, and the handful of
scholars who have seen two such sites in different countries and recognised the substance have had
nothing to compare them against, because the Kaliharans do not publish. Some of the distant works
appear to be working. Some appear to be doing something erratic, or something at odds with the place
they stand in, and those are presumed to be failing in the same way the Kaliharan installations fail.
Nobody has established what any of them were for.

The scatter is the strongest evidence that Kalihara was a choice rather than the whole of the Ithári's
business on Thalorna. Whatever the rest of it was, they conducted it without founding anything,
teaching anybody, or leaving a word behind.

## The Sinalë, who found them quaint

The [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] reached Thalorna about 5,280 BF, and the Ithári were still here. The
Sinalë did not seek them out; by their own account they came upon them, which is the only occasion in
the record of any Elder Race meeting the Ithári at all.

What the Sinalë report of the encounter is short and has never been elaborated. They were received.
They were not rebuffed, threatened, questioned or taken seriously. The impression they came away with —
and the Sinalë are careful to give it as an impression — is that the Ithári found them **quaint**: an
interesting young people, met with courtesy, and politely declined. No exchange was proposed. No
further meeting was arranged. The Ithári returned to whatever they had been doing, and the Sinalë went
away.

The Sinalë have never said where this happened, what was said, or what the Ithári were doing when they
were found. They will confirm that it occurred. The [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]], arriving a thousand
years later, never met the Ithári at all, and the account they have is the Sinalë's, from the years
when the two peoples still spoke.

## The departure

Kaliharan civilization dates the departure, and it is the one Ithári date the Kaliharans hold precisely:
**5,100 BF**, a little under two centuries after the Sinalë encounter.

The record describes it as a disturbance. Something went through the Ithári — the word the custodians
preserve carries no more than that, and does not say whether it came from within them, from the island,
or from somewhere else entirely. What followed was quick. They went to the caretakers of the
instruments and gave them a further set of instructions, hurriedly, beyond the procedures already
taught. Then they left, all of them, and the island woke to their absence.

The instructions survive. They are part of what the [[affiliation-tharivaan|Ithári'kaan]]
still perform, indistinguishable now from the older body of practice, and nothing in the record marks
which of the tendings were added at the end. Nobody knows what they were for, whether they were meant
to hold until a return, or whether they were the closing of an account.

Where the Ithári went is not recorded. The Kaliharans do not claim to know, and no other tradition on
Thalorna has a claim to offer.

## What the Kaliharans became

They did not collapse. They kept what had been given them — the knowledge, the structures, the
philosophy and the procedures — and fifty-eight centuries later they keep it still, which is the longest
unbroken institutional act in the world's record and is carried out by people who no longer know what
most of it does.

Across that span the understanding has drifted where the practice has not. Procedures the Ithári
presumably meant as maintenance have acquired ritual weight; the tending is performed with exactness
and with reverence, and the two are no longer separable. The island's refusal of the outside world is
of the same lineage: the Ithári tended what was theirs and left the rest alone, and their students have
done the same with a consistency that no other human society has come close to. When the Sinalë asked
for settlement and, later, the Khazári, Kalihara received both with courtesy and refused both, on the
grounds that the island was complete. It has not revised that answer since.

See [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]] for what this makes of Kalihara's place in the world: the
single case where humanity was lifted deliberately and knows it, set against a world where the same
thing happened by accident and is admitted by nobody.

## What nobody knows

The list is longer than the record, and the Kaliharans are as short of answers as anyone:

- **Where they came from**, and whether Thalorna was a destination or a stop.
- **What they were doing here** for five thousand years, and what the works away from Kalihara were
  for.
- **Why Kalihara alone**, and whether the civilization they built there was the purpose or a byproduct
  of it.
- **What the works are**, and whether the question of technology or magic is even the right question to
  ask of them.
- **Why they fail**, and whether the failures are decay, exhaustion, or something being withdrawn.
- **What the disturbance was**, and whether it reached them alone.
- **What the last instructions were meant to accomplish** — and whether the Ithári'kaan, performing
  them still, are holding something open or closing it very slowly.
- **Where they went**, and whether they are anywhere.
