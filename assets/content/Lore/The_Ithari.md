The **Ithári** are the oldest thing in the world's record that can be called a people, and the record
is one island's. They were on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]] before 10,000 BF and gone by 7,300 BF, and
every word anyone has of who they were, what they did here or what they wanted is Kaliharan testimony.
Outside [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] there is no knowledge of them whatsoever — not a tradition,
not a fragment of history, not the fact that there was anybody to know about. There are sites, and the
sites say nothing.

**The name itself is Kalihári.** _Ithári_ means "Those Who Came Before," it is what the Kaliharans
called them, and it is spoken nowhere else on the world, because nowhere else is there anything to
attach it to. The Ithári's own name for themselves is not recorded. Neither is what they looked like,
how many of them there were, how long any one of them lived, or whether the word names a people, a
profession or a crew.

What follows is therefore the Kaliharan account, which is the only account there is. Where it states a
thing plainly — the one substance, the absence of mechanism, the failures — that is a thing anyone
could see for themselves at a site, if they knew to look. Everything else in it is testimony held by
custodians on an island that does not publish.

## The arrival

The Kaliharan record opens with their coming and says nothing of what preceded it. They arrived from
somewhere that is not Thalorna, settled on [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]], and did not conquer it —
there was nothing on the island to conquer. The first humans they met there were scattered bands living
in the most rudimentary conditions, and the Ithári took them in.

The Kaliharans describe them as people rather than as gods or spirits: recognisably mortal in their
concerns and conduct, capable of impatience and of humour, and in no way interested in being
worshipped. That description has been transmitted for nearly ten thousand years by custodians who never
met one, and it is the only description there is.

## The works, and the one substance

The look comes first, and it is what tells anyone who meets a work that it is not theirs and not
anybody's they know of. An Ithári structure runs in ribs and shallow vaults, regular and true, its
walls curving into floor and ceiling with no join anywhere and no straight edge in any of it — the
economy of bone, or of the inside of a shell, held to with a severity no mason could better. Nothing
on it is ornament. The strangeness is not that it is wild but that it is so disciplined: a form
without a single square corner, and every curve of it exact.

**The entryways are oval.** Not a rectangle anywhere, no lintel, no jamb, no threshold to step over —
each opening is a smooth oval whose edge runs continuous with the wall around it. It is the detail
anyone can describe and nobody mistakes, and at a distance it is what a traveller recognises before
anything else.

A work has the shape of a thing with an inside, not of a building with rooms. It does not resemble
anything that ever grew on Thalorna either, so the resemblance to something living leads nowhere at
all.

Everything the Ithári left is made of the same thing. The Kaliharans call it **ithári'sul**, "precursor
stone," and it resembles stone in the way that a word resembles the thing it names. It is seamless —
no joints, no mortar, no tool-marks, no grain. It does not weather. Some of it is warm to the touch,
and some of it is faintly luminous in the dark, and the pieces that do one do not reliably do the
other. It is hard, and it is cold, and in a work of any size it is all one continuous piece: there is
no seam on it anywhere, and no crack.

It is found nowhere else on Thalorna, and it is not of Thalorna. No quarry has produced it, no smith
has matched it, and no assayer the Kaliharans have let near a fragment has been able to say what it
is. The Kaliharans do not regard this as a mystery, because in their understanding ithári'sul is part
of the world exactly as a reef or a river is, placed where it stands by the Ithári as part of the
whole. Nobody else has the category at all: a foreign mason who handles a piece of it has met an
unaccountable stone, not the work of a people.

**Nothing can be done to a work.** It cannot be cut, drilled, prised, ground or split; no tool bites
and no fire marks it; nothing can be joined to it and nothing taken from it. A structure will also not
move — it cannot be shifted, lifted, tipped or dragged from where it stands, by any number of hands or
beasts or engines, and no one has ever established what holds it. Implements, the small portable
devices, are a different matter: those can be picked up and carried like anything else. It is
structures that stay.

What this leaves anyone who finds one is a short list. You cannot open it, quarry it, build with its
material, or take it home. You can use it where it stands, or build around it. That is the whole of
what is possible, and it is also why these things are still here: every other ancient work on Thalorna
was robbed for its stone within a few generations of being left alone, and the Ithári's could not be
robbed, so they were not.

The one thing that gives is a work that has already died. When a structure fails, its fabric can at
last be broken, and that is where every fragment of ithári'sul in Kaliharan hands has come from.
Nothing has ever been taken from a living work.

## No mechanism

The works have no moving parts. Anyone who gets close enough to one arrives at the same place and
stops, and on Kalihara the custodians have been stopping there for eighty centuries.

They are not machines: there is nothing inside them to turn, no linkage, no store, no fuel, no wear.
They are not arcane apparatus either. They answer to no process known anywhere on Thalorna — not to the
[[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]], not to the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]], not to any human tradition that has
ever been near one — they draw on nothing an adept can detect, and they do not respond to the Aura
being worked beside them. A structure that calms a monsoon calms it. A structure that makes a
sea-approach unnavigable makes it unnavigable. Ask how, and there is no how: nothing to open, nothing
to dismantle, nothing to study but the effect itself.

Whether this is technology so far past the world's understanding that it cannot be recognised as
technology, or magic of a kind nobody else has ever practised, is a question nobody is in a position to
ask. The Kaliharans do not pose it — ithári'sul is simply part of the world to them, and its workings
are not a puzzle but a duty. Everyone else lacks the one fact that would make the question mean
anything, which is that the works were made. The two possibilities cannot be told apart from outside,
and there is no inside.

## The failures

The works also stop. Not all at once, not by any pattern anyone has established, and not for any reason
that can be found afterward. An [[affiliation-kalihara#the-ancient-structures|installation]] that has performed the same office
reliably for a hundred generations dims, cools, and is simply inert, and the fragment broken from it afterward is the same
substance it always was.

The [[affiliation-tharivaan|Ithári'kaan]], who tend them, have no repair to attempt.
They know the procedures and not the principles, and a procedure that has kept a structure working
since before the Elder Races landed may one day produce nothing at all. That the works function is
inexplicable. That they cease is inexplicable in exactly the same degree, and by the same absence of
anything to examine. The custodians count the failures and speak of the count to nobody.

## Kalihara, and the strangeness of it

[[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] is the only place on Thalorna where the Ithári raised humans. That is
the fact most worth sitting with, because they were here for the better part of three thousand years
and they did it once.

What they made there was not a people improved but a civilization invented: agriculture, medicine,
governance, a language with no relative anywhere on the world, a script beneath the script, and
**[[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]]** — a disciplined account of the world as a single living whole,
to be observed and tended rather than mastered or appeased. It arrived complete. Nothing on Kalihara
shows the long clumsy ascent that every other human tradition shows, because there was not one.

Why is not recorded. The Kaliharans hold that they were taught because teaching was the right way to
treat what the Ithári found, and that Thári'vaan is true independently of who first said it. Set beside
the scatter of works elsewhere, the island reads less like a gift than an instrument — a society built
to a specification, settled on a landmass its makers never expanded beyond, and handed the maintenance
of installations whose purpose it was never told. Both readings fit every fact in evidence, and the
Kaliharan record offers only the first. No outside scholarship weighs the second, for the plain reason
that no outside scholarship knows there is anything to weigh.

## The sites elsewhere

They did not confine themselves to the island. Ithári'sul is found across Thalorna, at scattered sites
in places with no connection to Kalihara and no memory of anything having been there — a seamless
structure in country nobody settled, a device in a cave, an installation doing something local and
unexplained to the weather or the water or the ground.

**Nowhere off the island are these known for what they are.** Each one stands by itself in the eyes of
everyone who meets it. A people living beside a site has a name for it in their own tongue and a story
to go with the name — a god's seat, a demon's door, a work of the elder races, a thing that was always
there — and every one of those stories is local, complete, and about something else. Nobody anywhere
holds a history of who built it. Nobody has connected one site to another. That the scattered works
share a maker is known on Kalihara and nowhere else, and the Kaliharans have never told anyone, so the
fact has never left the island.

The consequence is that the whole scatter is invisible as a scatter. Two such places a thousand miles
apart are, to the people who live at them, two unrelated marvels; there is no word in common between
the two accounts, no scholar who has stood at both, and no body of learning anywhere that treats these
things as a category. Many peoples have no word for them at all, because a site in country nobody
settled is met by nobody.

The works themselves behave as the Kaliharan installations do. Some are plainly doing something. Some
do something erratic, or something at odds with the place they stand in, and those are presumed — on
Kalihara, by the only people who could presume anything — to be failing as the island's installations
fail. What any of them was for is established by no one.

The scatter is the strongest evidence that Kalihara was a choice rather than the whole of the Ithári's
business on Thalorna. Whatever the rest of it was, they conducted it without founding anything,
teaching anybody, or leaving a word behind — which is why, everywhere but one island, they left no
trace of themselves at all, only of their work.

## The Sinalë, who found them quaint

The one crack in the silence is an afternoon, and almost nothing came through it.

The [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] reached Thalorna approximately 7500 BF — a figure nobody holds more
closely than that — and the Ithári were still here. The
Sinalë did not seek them out; by their own account they came upon them. It is the only occasion on
record of anyone outside Kalihara meeting the Ithári at all.

What the Sinalë report is short and has never been elaborated. They were received. They were not
rebuffed, threatened, questioned or taken seriously. The impression they came away with — and the
Sinalë are careful to give it as an impression — is that they were found **quaint**: an interesting
young people, met with courtesy, and politely declined. No exchange was proposed. No further meeting
was arranged. The others returned to whatever they had been doing, and the Sinalë went away.

**The limit of it should be stated plainly.** The Sinalë met someone and learned nothing. They were not
told who they had met, where those people had come from, what they were doing on Thalorna, or that
there was any connection between them and anything else in the world. They do not use the name Ithári,
because they were never given it. The Kaliharans were not party to the encounter and did not learn of
it from the Ithári, and nothing records whether the Sinalë ever put that afternoon together with the
builders of Kalihara's works — the question would require them to know that Kalihara's works had
builders, and there is no account of anyone telling them.

The Sinalë have never said where it happened, what was said, or what the others were doing when they
were found. They will confirm that it occurred, and that is the end of what they will give. The
[[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]], arriving a thousand years later, never met them and have nothing of their
own; whatever the Sinalë chose to pass on in the years when the two peoples still spoke went no further
than that, and both peoples have been silent since long before any human asked.

## The departure

Kaliharan civilization dates the departure, and it is the one Ithári date the Kaliharans hold
precisely: **7,300 BF**, some two centuries after the Sinalë encounter. Everything else in this account
is reckoned loosely, the Sinalë's own arrival included; this one figure the custodians carry to the
year, because everything they do is counted from it.

The record describes it as a disturbance. Something went through the Ithári — the word the custodians
preserve carries no more than that, and does not say whether it came from within them, from the island,
or from somewhere else entirely. What followed was quick. They went to the caretakers of the
instruments and gave them a further set of instructions, hurriedly, beyond the procedures already
taught. Then they left, all of them, and the island woke to their absence.

The instructions survive. They are part of what the [[affiliation-tharivaan|Ithári'kaan]]
still perform, indistinguishable now from the older body of practice, and nothing in the record marks
which of the tendings were added at the end. Nobody knows what they were for, whether they were meant
to hold until a return, or whether they were the closing of an account.

Where the Ithári went is not recorded. The Kaliharans do not claim to know, and no other tradition on
Thalorna has a claim to offer — none of them knows there was a departure, or an arrival, or anyone to
depart.

## What the Kaliharans became

They did not collapse. They kept what had been given them — the knowledge, the structures, the
philosophy and the procedures — and eighty centuries later they keep it still. It is the longest
unbroken institutional act anywhere in the world's record; it is carried out by people who no longer
know what most of it does; and no one beyond the island knows it is happening.

Across that span the understanding has drifted where the practice has not. Procedures the Ithári
presumably meant as maintenance have acquired ritual weight; the tending is performed with exactness
and with reverence, and the two are no longer separable. The island's refusal of the outside world is
of the same lineage: the Ithári tended what was theirs and left the rest alone, and their students have
done the same with a consistency that no other human society has come close to. When the Sinalë asked
for settlement and, later, the Khazári, Kalihara received both with courtesy and refused both, on the
grounds that the island was complete. It has not revised that answer since.

See [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]] for what this makes of Kalihara's place in the world: the
single case where humanity was lifted deliberately and knows it, set against a world where the same
thing happened by accident and is admitted by nobody.

## What nobody knows

The list is longer than the record. It is also a list only the Kaliharans are in a position to draw up,
since everyone else lacks the first item on it — that there was anybody at all:

- **Where they came from**, and whether Thalorna was a destination or a stop.
- **What they were doing here** for the better part of three thousand years, and what the works away
  from Kalihara were for — those sites being, to every people that lives beside one, an unexplained
  thing of local provenance and no history.
- **Why Kalihara alone**, and whether the civilization they built there was the purpose or a byproduct
  of it.
- **What the works are**, and whether the question of technology or magic is even the right question to
  ask of them.
- **Why they fail**, and whether the failures are decay, exhaustion, or something being withdrawn.
- **What the disturbance was**, and whether it reached them alone.
- **What the last instructions were meant to accomplish** — and whether the Ithári'kaan, performing
  them still, are holding something open or closing it very slowly.
- **Where they went**, and whether they are anywhere.
