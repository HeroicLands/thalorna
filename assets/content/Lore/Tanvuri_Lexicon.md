The Celestial Order of [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]] calls itself the _Tëngvōk Rūrk Lëi_, the celestial court of a myriad spirits, and every piece of that name is a word a farmer uses: _tëng_ is the sky over his field, _vōk_ the magistrate's hall, _rūrk_ the count past counting, _lëi_ the breath in his chest. [[skill-tanvurlng|Tānvüri]] builds its names by setting such words side by side, and the empire's castes, gods, bureaus, months and generals are all made this way. This page is the word-hoard of the tongue: the words, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in Tānvür, in [[affiliation-jurthatempr|Jürthāt]] and in the Celestial Order.

A Tānvüri word is a single syllable, and a compound reads modifier first: _zhëk_ is life and _zhös_ is the dragon, so _Zhëkzhös_, life-dragon, is the dragon-born caste of the Emperor's family. A name or a caste is written as one word; a title, an office or an institution is written as separate words, as in _Tëng Gīrk_, the celestial minister.

The sound of Tānvüri is clipped and muted. Words close often on a hard _-k_ or _-t_, the _th_ of _thin_ and the _v_ of _vine_ open a great many of them, and the central _ë_ and the rounded _ö_ and _ü_ give the tongue its colour. A macron marks a long vowel and a slightly higher pitch; length is a habit of good speech more than a distinction of meaning.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the words the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly those words set side by side.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, and closes on a vowel, on one of the consonants below, or on one of the pairs below.

| Edge                           | Sounds                        |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Consonants that close a word   | `k`, `t`, `r`, `n`, `ng`, `s` |

### Clusters

A compound sets the close of one syllable against the opening of the next, so three consonants may meet inside a word, as in _Hürtzhük_.

| Rule                                   | Count |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Most consonants together inside a word | 3     |

## Building words

One move makes nearly every built word on this page: **two words set side by side, the modifier first.** _Höt_ is fire and _yüt_ is the month, so _Hötyüt_ is the fire month. A handful of words close compounds so often that the [[skill-tanvurlng|language note]] lists them as suffixes: _-zōk_ for a people or group, _-vōr_ and _-lëi_ for a spirit, _-gīrk_ for an official, _-gürt_ for a master, _-zhük_ for a lord, _-yüt_ for a month, _-jëit_ for a festival, _-thāt_ for a mountain or place, _-vōk_ for a court and _-sëi_ for a bureau. Each is the plain word, and each is set on its first word unchanged.

Nothing joins or alters the two words, and the tongue marks no plural: _rūrk_, ten thousand, set before a noun, gives the sense of countless.

[[skill-jurthatlng|Jürthāti]], the island daughter of Tānvüri, has worn the mainland words down. It has lost the final stops, so its words close on a vowel or a nasal; it softens a _th_ inside a compound; and it breaks a cluster with a vowel. Its few attested names stand in the register under their own tag.

## Making names

**A person** of Tānvür carries the house name first and the given name after it. The house name is a single word, older than any record: [[skill-tanvurlng|Tānvüri]] lists _Tān_, _Vëi_, _Jīk_, _Shüt_ and their like. Most people never hold a personal name; one granted for service is a compound of two to four words, a man's drawn from strength and the sky, a woman's from flowers, the moon and the stars. [[lore-personlnms|Personal Names]] gives the grant.

**A god, a caste or a place** is a compound written as one word: _Tānzhös_, the celestial dragon; _Vüshōk_, the warrior-nobles; _Jürthāt_, the nine mountains. **A title, an office or an institution** keeps its words apart: _Höt Sëi_, the fire bureau; _Tëng Gīrk_, the celestial minister.

**A Jürthāti house** takes its name from a feature of its island, and a retainer family from the office it has held longest. [[skill-jurthatlng|Jürthāti]] gives few of them, and those it gives stand in the register under `jurthati`.
