The [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] are drovers who hold the two crossings of [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]] and spend half the year scattered and half of it in one building. There are nine thousand of them. They are the poorer of the two guide-peoples by a wide margin, and they are the only people on the wall who deal with lowland institutions as a matter of routine.

## The Two Halves of the Year

Through the open season the hearths are up on the high pastures with the pack-beasts, spread across the head-valleys, and the crossings are worked by whoever has drawn them. Through the closed months the whole people is at [[place-nurvhrn|Nurvharn]], under one turf roof that a stranger can walk the length of and pass through eleven households without going outside. It is warm, dark, crowded and startlingly clean.

Very little of a Hvarn winter is private. A quarrel carried down off the pastures is settled in front of everyone who has an interest in it, and a hearth-head who will not sit with another hearth-head has nowhere else to eat.

## The Rotation

The [[affiliation-osketguides|Osket]] hold a road by descent, and the Hvarn share theirs out. In the weeks after midwinter the hearth-heads take the middle of the long house and say aloud, in front of everyone, who will walk which crossing and what each hearth still owes another.

The council has no power to compel and has never needed one, because nobody who ignored it would be given a road. That is the whole of Hvarn public order, and it is made again from nothing every winter.

The rotation is what a small trade can afford. A single [[place-suryadvara|Suryadvara]] lineage lives better than six Hvarn hearths could on two crossings, so the Hvarn pool the work and the debt and argue about it once a year in one room.

## The Party

Hvarn work is roped work, and the [[skill-hvarnlng|language]] carries the distinction in its verbs: paired forms separate what a man has done himself from what he has done as one of a party. A **Reach-guide** answers to the council for whoever does not come back. A **Drover** who kept the beasts on the lower stages is not held to have made the crossing at all.

Earnings run the same way. A season's fee comes to the hearth and the debts it leaves are between hearths, so a **Roadwalker** learning the weather under another's charge is owed his place at a fire and nothing else.

## The Roll

The Hvarn go down for their dead. A body in the eastern gorges is recovered wherever recovery is possible at all, brought up, and the place it was found recorded and kept. The record is the **Bone-bringer**'s, he recites it at the winter council each year, and a fault in the recitation is corrected from the floor by anyone who catches it.

It is the longest continuous thing the people keep and the only one they will show an outsider. It is also the best warning anyone has about where the ground fails.

A child is named for a person in the roll, and the naming carries the obligation to know where that person was found. A Hvarn asked about his name answers with a place.

## The Contract

The Hvarn pack-trains that feed [[place-sanghafort|the fort]] on the conch-door col run on an old contract. The **Fort-carrier** who keeps it speaks fluent lowland [[skill-vedyarlng|Vedyari]] and is the one member of the people the garrison's officers know by name.

Lowland factors walk up to Nurvharn for the renewal and for nothing else, and the Hvarn have kept up the visits for four centuries without once inviting anyone to stay the winter. The seal of [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|the janapadas]] is one of two objects in the long house kept in a box.

## Poor, and Better Connected

Nine thousand people with two crossings and no store of their own are poor by any measure a lowlander uses. The Hvarn are also the best connected people on the wall. The fort contract puts them in a room with lowland officers every third year, the envoys and scholars who use [[place-jnanadvara|Jnanadvara]] pass through their hands, and about forty lowlanders speak the language, which is forty more than speak [[skill-osketlng|Osket]].

## Names and the Tongue

A Hvarn carries a given name and a hearth name, and the hearth name changes if he is taken into another hearth. The given names come off the roll of the dead. The hearth names are the winter house's, and a man who has been taken in answers to a name his children will not carry.

There is no script and there is no cord. What the Hvarn keep, they keep by recitation. The tongue is not related to [[skill-osketlng|Osket]], which surprises everyone who has assumed it must be: the two peoples live three hundred miles apart along the same wall, do much the same work, marry across the gap occasionally, and speak languages with no demonstrable common stock. Both peoples state this without interest, and lowland scholars find it difficult to accept.

Hvarn words are short and heavy, and a word may be spoken with no vowel in it at all. The people's own word for themselves has none. The vowel a lowlander hears in it is the one he supplied.

## What Is Good, and What Succeeds

**Good is the party brought back whole, the ground described truly and the debt spoken aloud before the house. Evil is the dead left unplaced, a debt carried in private, and a road kept as though it belonged to one hearth.** A Hvarn does not weigh a deed by what its doer meant, because the rope does not. A guide who put a party on a shelf that was not safe has not been unlucky. He has failed to say, to the house, where the ground fails.

Success is a crossing drawn in the winter rotation and a season that ends with nothing owed: the party home, the fee at the hearth, the debts between hearths said and settled. A hearth that is owed something stands well in the long house and one that owes stands badly, and the council floor is where the difference is read. A name the roll has placed belongs to a person who was found. Failure is a party lost, a hearth that draws no road until the council allows it, and at the bottom the person put out of the winter house, whom no hearth feeds.

The Hvarn value most the party, the roll, the open floor and a house kept clean enough to share. They value least the lone feat and the room of one's own. The verbs keep one form for what a man did himself and another for what he did as one of a party, and the Drover who kept the beasts on the lower stages is not held to have made the crossing at all.

## The Man Admired

**Hvrel of the Klemvrd hearth**, the Fort-carrier, is the man the Hvarn point to when they say what the contract is for. He is a broad man who speaks lowland Vedyari slowly and carefully, and he is the one member of the people whom the garrison's officers know by name. A stranger who reaches his hearth has his pack lifted off his shoulders before he can object, is set down between Hvrel's daughter and a sleeping dog, and is given barley and butter, and is shown the janapadas' seal in its box once he has eaten. His name comes from the roll. The first Hvrel was found under the upper shelf of [[place-jnanadvara|Jnanadvara]], and Hvrel answers a question about his name with that place.

The Hvarn admire in him the bargain the whole people lives on, carried by a man who does not dress it up. The fort contract is the single largest piece of Hvarn income, it is renewed every third year, and he is the one who sits across from the lowland factor for it. He holds the lowlanders at the edge of the village, feeds them, shows them the seal and does not invite them to stay the winter. His cost is that the people regard the traffic as a nuisance they are paid for, and the man who carries it is the one they do not quite thank. The kingdom of [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharalaya]] resents the fort's supply line and the Hvarn decline to discuss it, and both of those fall on the same broad shoulders.

## The Woman Admired

**Rhevzhn of the Shulnrh hearth**, a Reach-guide of [[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvara]], is the woman the long house names for the refusal the contract depends on. In a week the storm-reader had closed, the commander of the fort pressed her for a crossing, and she declined. On the first clear day she took the fort's supplies up on her own rope. The worst crossing on the wall has a record of no party lost, and her hearth keeps it without explaining it.

Her admiration is not a lesser form of Hvrel's. He holds the people's bargain with the lowland, and she holds the line the bargain stands behind: a garrison fed on Hvarn pack-trains is fed by people who will say no to its commander. Her cost is kinship. Her hearth is small and related by marriage to half the garrison, so every refusal is made to relatives, and a commander refused once stops asking and stops granting the small favors that kin extend. A Reach-guide answers to the council for whoever does not come back, and the weight of the office is the weight she carries.

## What the Winter House Keeps

A Hvarn can point to the things that hold the people together. **The roll** is the longest thing the people keep: every person recovered from the eastern gorges, with the place where each was found, recited each winter by the Bone-bringer and corrected from the floor by whoever catches a fault. **The rotation** is made aloud each winter, kept by the Turn-keeper, and written nowhere but in the memory of the hearth-heads who heard it. **The long house** at Nurvharn is one roof added to at either end as hearths have grown, and it is the people's meeting place, store and common kitchen. **The rope** belongs to the party, and the grease worked into it through the closed months is the people's oldest upkeep. **The seal** of the janapadas is one of two objects in the long house kept in a box. **The name from the roll**, with its place, is the smallest of the artifacts and the one every person carries.

## The Day Kept

On an ordinary day the Hvarn religion is work done in the right order, and its names are not shared with strangers. The people keep austere traditions and say what they do and not what it is for. In the closed months the day belongs to the long house: rope greased, pack-saddles mended, the pot stirred by someone arguing with three people at once, children asleep in heaps of hides. In the open months a party leaves on the storm-reader's word, and a refusal ends a day's travel whatever the fee. At the summit shrines of Jnanadvara and Shankhadvara a party takes the road-rite of the [[affiliation-passshrineushtakas|Pass-Shrine ushtakas]], the one lowland practice the guide-peoples respect, because they have watched it work in weather they know better than anyone. A child asked its name answers with a place.

## The Round of the Year

The Hvarn have no feast that is not a piece of business. The year is told by where the hearths are.

- **The coming-down** falls with the first hard frost, when the hearths leave the high pastures for the long house and are counted in. It is for knowing who is under the roof and who is not.
- **The rotation** falls in the weeks after midwinter. The hearth-heads take the middle of the long house and say aloud who walks which crossing and what each hearth still owes another. It is for making the year's road from nothing.
- **The recitation** is the Bone-bringer's yearly telling of the roll at the winter council. It is for renewing the map of the ground and the names of those who are on it.
- **The renewal** is the fort contract, every third year, at the edge of the village. A lowland factor is fed, shown the seal in its box, and not asked to stay. It is for tying the lowland to the people's winter without letting the lowland in.
- **The thaw** ends the closed months and sends the hearths up again, with the year's debts said and the crossings assigned.

## Favor and the Unhoused

Reward is a road, a place at a fire, and the standing the two give. A hearth that drew the conch-door two years running is owed something by the others. A Roadwalker learning the weather under another's charge is owed his place at a fire and nothing more. A Drover becomes a Roadwalker, a Roadwalker a Reach-guide entrusted with a crossing alone, and a Reach-guide may become the head of a winter hearth, who sits on the council and speaks for those who eat at his fire.

Penalty runs from the council floor to the door. A fault carried down off the pastures is settled in front of everyone with an interest in it. A hearth that lost a party is given no road until the council says so, and the council wants work, a witness or the body back. A Reach-guide answers to the council for whoever does not come back. At the bottom is the **Unhoused**, put out of the winter house for a fault the council has named, and fed by nobody.

## The Hearth and the Summit

The Hvarn have one authority above the hearth, and it holds no power. The winter council of hearth-heads sits in the middle of the long house and does the whole of the people's public business: the crossings, the debts, the fault of whoever lost a party, the terms on which the fort is fed. Civil and religious authority are not two bodies here. The council settles what the hearths owe one another, and the Pass-Shrine ushtakas at the summits keep the rite and the register of the road, and neither reaches into the other's work.

The Winter-speaker presides over the council and holds the order of speaking and no authority when it rises. The Turn-keeper keeps the rotation and the debts it leaves. The Bone-bringer keeps the roll. Cases are heard on the floor by the council in front of the parties and their kin, and records are held by recitation. The Fort-carrier negotiates the single account the Hvarn keep with the janapadas, and the fee of a season comes to the hearth that earned it. The council cannot compel, and it has never needed to, because nobody who ignored it would be given a road. The Storm-reader's refusal ends a day's travel and is not argued with, and where the council and the weather disagree, the weather is the Storm-reader's.

## What the Roll Asks of an Ordinary Day

The Hvarn do not say where the dead go. They say where the dead were found. The belief that settles an ordinary day is that a person is a place: the ground that took him is named, kept and recited, and the person is not finished with until it is.

So the dead are fetched. A body in the eastern gorges is brought up wherever recovery is possible at all, and the place it lay is recorded before anything else is done. A hearth that can bring its dead up is a hearth that can draw a road again. A child is named for someone in the roll and carries the duty to know where that person was found. A guide choosing a stage goes by the roll. The winter store pays for recovery parties before it pays for anything that can wait, and a rope kept greased in the closed months is the ordinary shape of the belief. Debts are said aloud before the house, because an unspoken debt is an unplaced body of another kind.

## Reaching the Dead

The living reach the dead by going down for them. That is the Bone-bringer's office, and it is the ordinary and daily form of the people's religion; it needs no journey beyond the gorge. The recitation is the second form, and the third is the child who carries a name and answers for it. Nothing the Hvarn say claims that the dead answer. A body missing from the gorges, or a name the roll cannot place, is not a haunting. It is an unfinished account, and the long house wants it settled before midwinter.

The cost is the recovery itself. The gorges that took the dead can take the ones who go down for them, and the Bone-bringer's work is done at the pace the ground allows. A hearth that loses a party needs the body back to be given a road, so the dead are also the price of the hearth's standing, and the long house does not discuss whether that is a mercy or a pressure.

## What a Person Owes

A Hvarn owes in order: the party first, because the rope is only as good as its worst knot; then the hearth that feeds him; then the long house that shelters the whole people through the closed months; then the roll. He owes the council his presence in winter and his account of any party he lost. He owes other hearths what the rotation says he owes.

In return a person is owed a place at a fire, a share of the winter store if he is of the hearth's blood, a road drawn fairly in the rotation, a hearing in front of everyone, and a place in the roll if the gorge takes him. The hearth-head is owed the account of every person at his fire, and answers for them in turn.

## The Weak

The hearth is the first resort and the long house the second. A widow's family, a stranger and a child of the roads are **Taken-in**: sheltered by a hearth without being of it, fed at its fire and holding no share of the winter store. A person taken into another hearth takes its hearth name, and his children will not carry the name he answers to. In the closed months nobody is outside except the Unhoused, because the roof is shared and the roof is warm.

Scarcity is met through debt. In a bad year a hearth with no crossing drawn lives on what other hearths owe it and on what the pastures give. The Hvarn also go down to [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-ber]] in autumn and buy grain and barley for the winter, selling wool, butter and worked horn. The rotation spreads the work thinly across hearths so that no hearth is long without a road, and the fort contract is the one income that holds when the passes do not.

## Those Who Cannot Work

A person who cannot walk the crossings is kept at the fire. The long house is full of work that wants no legs: the rope, the saddle, the butter, the pot. Two old men bent over a broken pack-saddle are the usual picture of it. The recitation is held to account by memory, and an old person who catches a fault corrects it from the floor like the rest. An injured Roadwalker is fed from the hearth's winter store because a hearth-kin holds a place at the fire for life, and the hearth that lost a party keeps its wounded and its dead alike.

## Shelter, Teaching and Defense

Shelter is the hearth's, and past the hearth the long house's. Teaching is carried by the work: a child learns the rope at the fire, a Drover learns the lower stages with the beasts, a Roadwalker learns the weather in another's charge, and a Reach-guide takes a crossing alone only when the council has heard that he knows it. The language teaches the party with its paired forms.

Defense is not an institution. No body of arms is named, and the Hvarn hold no ground a lowland army could reach. [[place-sanghafort|The Sangha-fort]] is the janapadas' garrison, fed by Hvarn pack-trains, and it guards the col and not the people. The people's protection is that the two crossings are theirs, that the weather on them is the Storm-reader's, and that nobody who ignored the council would be given a road.

## The Line at Every Age

The hearth is the unit and the long house its shell. A child is born into a hearth and given a name off the roll, and sleeps in the heap of hides with the others. A young person learns the rope, then the beasts as a Drover, then the weather as a Roadwalker. An adult is a Reach-guide, entrusted with a crossing alone, and the head of a winter hearth sits on the council. An elder holds the recitation to account and mends what can be mended sitting down. The dying are attended in the long house, and the dead are carried up from the gorge and set in the roll with the place they were found.

Membership is blood or the winter's decision. Hearth-kin are of a hearth's blood, holding a place at its fire and a share of its store. Taken-in are sheltered without being of it. A hearth name changes if a person is taken into another hearth, and marriage across the gap to the Osket happens occasionally. The unit is the household at one fire, which is wider than a pair and their children, and the long house holds many such fires under one roof.

## What the Council Names

Every real culture has a blind spot its own thoughtful members argue about, and the Hvarn's is the roll. The same recitation that maps the ground names the hearth that lost each person, and a hearth that lost a party is given no road until it brings back work, a witness or the body. The council has no power and is obeyed in everything, so the sentence falls without anyone having passed it. Thoughtful Hvarn dispute whether the roll warns the living or accuses them.

The sin is the Unhoused. A person put out of the winter house for a fault the council has named is fed by nobody, and the closed months are not a season in which a person who is fed by nobody lives. The council cannot compel anything, so the sentence is carried out by every hearth declining to feed him, and no one hearth-head has to say it. The mercy the Hvarn grant in every other matter, the place at the fire for the stranger and the widow, is not extended to the person the house has named. It is rarely done, and the council has no form for undoing it.
