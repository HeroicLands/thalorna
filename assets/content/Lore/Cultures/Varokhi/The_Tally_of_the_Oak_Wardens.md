At [Weskskald]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, the deep-winter evening of family memory, every [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] kindred says its dead, and the saying is the _eldskorn_, the ancestor-tally: one name to a line and one deed to a name, struck out on the hall floor with the butt of a staff by the eldest of each hearth in turn, one stroke to a name, while the hall keeps time with its feet. The tally runs backward, so an event is placed by how far back in it a name falls, and beyond the names whose deeds are remembered the names run on bare ([[lore-wintertally|The Tally of Winters]]).

**The Tally of the Oak Wardens**, _Eichthúl eldskorn_, is the tally of the [Eichthúl]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the Oak Wardens, whose grove holds the hall-posts of [[place-hrindstead|Hrindstead]]. Settlers out of [[place-nordheim|Nordheim]] planted them there in the first winter of the present age; [Mahnulf yra Eichthúl wur Eichreth]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the kindred's War Chief, burned the first stockade, and the second stands on the same posts ([[lore-nalthmrktkng|The Nalthmark Land-Taking]]). The clan's oldest _hrum_ lies under those oaks, inside the ground the settlers walked with fire. No Eichthúl has tended it at [Weskmund]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} since, and [Erthilda yra Eichthúl wur Eichreth]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the kindred's Shaman, will not cross the settlers' bounds to call its dead home while the posts stand. She keeps their count instead, and says it at Weskskald with nothing after it.

The tally runs from the four who died since the thaw, through the dead with deeds and the dead whose deeds are lost, to the bare names, "the ones who are the wood", and then turns and says the year's children for the first time. This is the tally as the households of [Eichreth]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} said it in the thirteenth winter of the posts, with the stanza wherever a death earned one, the line the far hearth adds every winter, and the two hearths' disagreement over the dead under the oaks kept as it was spoken.
