At [Othholt]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, a village of [[place-edriwald|Edriwald]] whose households argue over an inherited grove, a kindred minded to refuse a settlement the Skathár has reckoned hears **The Lay of the Feud-Cord**, _Skathdreth drunga_, before the three seats hear the refusal. The Other Chief calls for it. The reciter stands with a staff and strikes the floor on the long lines, and between the runs of stanzas the Skathár of the day reads out, flat and without the staff, the reckoning of [The Othholt Feud]{.name data-type="lore/history"} as her predecessors kept it: what was owed, what was offered, who refused, and how many knots. A Skathár of Othholt learns that reckoning before she is trusted with a living cord, and it is the frame the stanzas hang in.

The lay itself is held word for word, as a _drunga_ is, and it is a lay of speeches: the killings are in the reckoning, and the verse carries what was said in the hall by the two kindreds, the [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and the [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, and by the seats. [Domholt]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, where the Harvith women went with their children, keeps a lay of the same feud that gives the killer a speech; Othholt's gives him none. No stanza states the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] view of a price refused; the occasion says it. The lay stops where the reckoning stops, at the cord.

They join the band in the same spring. They take true names in the same spring./
The band receives them. It has nine young men. I count nine here.

One has a split lip. It never closes. One is the smith’s second son./

Two are cousins from the [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} hall. Two come from the landing./
One has the loudest voice in any hall. One stands beside doors. He is ninth.

They sit in the grove at thaw. The band surrounds them./
Water falls from birches. Ice softens in the wheel-ruts./
[Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} stands before the others. He always stands first./
He speaks the vow. Nine hear it. The grove hears it. Memory holds it.

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-lay-of-the-feud-cord-6}
**The reckoning of the Skathár.** [Edrarún]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}, Skathár of [Othholt]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, keeps this; those after her keep it. [Vristulf]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} of [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} kills [Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} of [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a [[lore-druthmundrnk|Druthmund]], in the grove on the third day after thaw, with an axe, in daylight, the blow in front, for a birch both claim. [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} owes a man’s full blood-price. [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} offers it at the first moon afterward, before three seats in the [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} hall: forty winter pelts, marten and fox; a boat with four oars; an ox; an iron pot; two [[lore-othmundrnk|Othmund]], a man and his wife from the south; the [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} half of the grove, to [Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}’s daughter [Árhilda]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}, five winters old. [Edrarún]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} reckons it complete. The [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} household mistress speaks first, mother of [Skathrik]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} and [Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}.
:::

The southerners stand in the door as ordered, snow on shoulders./
The man sees girl on bench, old woman, cord. He says nothing in any tongue./
The girl sees them. People tell her they are in the price. Its only part able to look back./
Nobody orders them inside to the fire. Nobody has the place to say it. Nobody says it.

[Edrarún]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} takes the cord from the peg. She sits on the bench. Cord across knees./
The girl holds the end as ordered. The southerners stand in the door./
Ten come out one after another. Some have hasty tying. They come easily./
Some have tying in cold halls with trembling hands. A person must work them with a thumbnail.

The last is first. For [Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}. Tied at the first moon after thaw./
In a hall with three seats, standing family, nine young men behind him./
Every later knot pulls it hard. Three winters of pulling./
It does not want to come. Old fingers work. The girl holds the end. It comes.
