At [Othholt]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, a village of [[place-edriwald|Edriwald]] whose households argue over an inherited grove, a kindred minded to refuse a settlement the Skathár has reckoned hears **The Lay of the Feud-Cord**, _Skathdreth drunga_, before the three seats hear the refusal. The Other Chief calls for it. The reciter stands with a staff and strikes the floor on the long lines, and between the runs of stanzas the Skathár of the day reads out, flat and without the staff, the reckoning of [The Othholt Feud]{.name data-type="lore/history"} as her predecessors kept it: what was owed, what was offered, who refused, and how many knots. A Skathár of Othholt learns that reckoning before she is trusted with a living cord, and it is the frame the stanzas hang in.

The lay itself is held word for word, as a _drunga_ is, and it is a lay of speeches: the killings are in the reckoning, and the verse carries what was said in the hall by the two kindreds, the [Grimskorn]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and the [Harvith]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, and by the seats. [Domholt]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, where the Harvith women went with their children, keeps a lay of the same feud that gives the killer a speech; Othholt's gives him none. No stanza states the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] view of a price refused; the occasion says it. The lay stops where the reckoning stops, at the cord.

: The Vow at the Grove {#vow}

::: {.verse form=drunga lang=en work=vow}
Keep the door. Set the cup down. This is the lay of the cord,/
the Othholt lay, of two halls, and the knots tied between them,/
as the one before me gave it, and the one before her./
Strike the floor for [Armund]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}. Strike it twice over for the ones who came after him.

The seats are sitting. The cord is on its peg, and the Skathár has her hand on the reckoning./
Whoever came in here to refuse, keep your hand off the cord till this is done./
It is not long. It is three winters, and it goes quicker in the telling than it went./
The Skathár says her part between the runs, and the last of it is mine, and then the seats will hear you.

The grove stands between the two halls, where the birches crowd in on the oaks./
A woman brought it, married out of the Grimskorn into the Harvith/
three grandmothers back, and both halls have cut in it since,/
and nobody had yet said which stump was whose, and the saying was coming.

Armund was of the Grimskorn, and [Skathrik]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} was his _frithwin_,/
one winter younger, and he went where the other went./
They came to the _frath_ in one spring and took their true names in one spring,/
and the band that took them in was nine young men, and the nine are counted here.

One had a split lip that never closed. One was the smith's second son./
One had carried a boar's tusk since he was twelve, and showed it./
Two were cousins out of the Grimskorn's own hall. Two were from the landing./
One was the loudest in any hall he stood in. One stood by doors, and was the ninth.

Armund's deed for his name was a boar, a big one, in the beech mast, that had opened a dog./
He went in on it with a spear and the spear broke, and he finished it with the half./
Skathrik's deed for his name was that he carried Armund home from the boar,/
from the beech mast to the gate, with the thigh opened, and the boar's head on top. Armund would not leave the head.

So they were named in one spring, and the Shaman said the names, and the band struck for both./
Armund got a name for the boar. Skathrik got a name for the carrying./
That is how it was between them from the first, and the village knew it, and said it,/
and nobody said then that a man who carries another from the beech mast to the gate will carry him farther than that.

They sat in the grove at the thaw, with the band around them,/
with the drip off the birches, and the ice gone soft in the ruts,/
and Armund stood up first. He was always first to stand./
He spoke the _vora_, and the nine heard it, and the grove heard it, and it is kept.

[Armund said:]{.speaker}/
If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you./
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground./
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it./
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

[Skathrik said:]{.speaker}/
If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you./
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground./
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it./
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

That was [the Vow at the Grove]{.name data-type="lore/history" lang=en}, and it was a fine thing to hear./
The nine struck their spears on the roots and said they had heard it./
There was no Skathár there, and no seat, and nobody old,/
and Armund's _othwa_ was not yet born, who sits at the end of this.

Twelve winters went over the grove, and both halls cut in it./
Armund married a woman out of the Harvith, and the grove came up at the wedding,/
and was laughed down at the wedding, and came up again at the naming./
Skathrik did not marry. He had said where he would be, and he was there.

There was a birch at the grove's edge, a good one, straight for twenty feet,/
and Armund put his mark on it in the autumn, and [Vristulf]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo} of the Harvith put his on it under Armund's,/
and in the spring each went up to fell it and found the other's mark, and the axes were already in their hands./
The birch is still standing. Nobody has felled it. The two marks have grown over, and the bark has closed on both.

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=vow-reckoning}
**The reckoning of the Skathár.** Kept by [Edrarún]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}, Skathár of Othholt, and by those after her. Armund of the Grimskorn, a [[lore-druthmundrnk|Druthmund]], killed in the grove on the third day after the thaw by Vristulf of the Harvith, with an axe, in daylight, the blow in front, over a birch both men claimed. Owed by the Harvith: the full price of a man. Offered by the Harvith at the first moon after, before the three seats, in the Grimskorn hall: forty winter pelts, marten and fox; a boat with four oars; an ox; an iron kettle; two [[lore-othmundrnk|Othmund]], a man and his wife out of the south; and the Harvith half of the grove, to Armund's _othwa_ [Árhilda]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=varo}, five winters old. Reckoned by Edrarún as full. The _othrún_ of the Grimskorn spoke first, Skathrik's mother and Armund's.

**Tavreth er Skathár.** Edrarún halwen hem, Skathár wur Othholt, vird mawen nather hem. Armund yra Grimskorn, Druthmund, wredri inwe werlak et rend threm dagren nather wathra. Wethan thum Vristulf yra Harvith, mi brold, et larg, slagren frar hem, feor birwen thewen marwen malwen er hemwen. Harvith harve skuldren, erwen bludwirdra er marwen. Harvith bubra et rend eom munra nather, frar threm sethar, inwe hemra er Grimskorn, fedr tenra skurth er grema, marth, fukth, badra mi fedr hralwen, ukthe, hraldi melgud, thei Othmund, marwen virwe bemra fran sudren, vird helwen er werlak er Harvith ter othwa er Armund, Árhilda, hem harve fimra grema. Edrarún skatha hem lurni. Othrún er Grimskorn malwen rend eom, mowen er Skathrik virwe Armund.

: Before the Three Seats {#seats}

::: {.verse form=drunga lang=en work=seats}
[The Other Chief said:]{.speaker}/
The Skathár has reckoned, and I have heard it, and I say it is full./
The kindred will say whether it takes it, here, before these seats, and nowhere else.

[His mother said:]{.speaker}/
I have counted the stores since before you could count./
I count them now, and I will say what is in them, so the seats hear it./
Barley for the winter, if the winter is short. Hay for two cows, and we have three./
And one bench by the fire that no cow and no dead Harvith is going to fill.

Forty pelts is a boat's load, and the boat is in the offer./
An ox breaks the clearing we have wanted broken for ten years./
The kettle is iron, and ours is cracked and leaks at the seam./
And the girl has a grove, the whole of it, with nobody left to argue a stump.

I bore Armund, and I washed him, and I will wash him again for the pyre./
Do not tell me what he is worth. I know what he weighed./
Vristulf's mother knows what Vristulf weighs. She is sitting there./
She has offered me her ox, and I have looked at the ox, and the ox is sound.

You say the _vora_. I heard of it. The whole village heard of it:/
two boys in a wet grove, and nine boys banging spears on roots./
A vow is a thing a man gives. A price is a thing a kindred takes./
You gave your word. The kindred's word was never asked, and the kindred is me.

Your _frithwin_ is with the _wesk_, or he is not, and I do not know which,/
and the Shaman does not know, and will not go and ask, and I would not let him./
The ancestors judge Armund. They do not judge an ox./
Fill the byre. If they judge you for it, they will judge you fed.

What does a dead Harvith give? Say it. I am listening./
No milk. No oars. No clearing broken. No girl with a grove./
He gives one bench emptied across the clearing to match ours,/
and a knot in their cord to match the knot in ours, and two more hands to tie the next.

I have seen a cord go to the peg and stay there./
At [Hildreth]{.name data-type="place/settlement" lang=varo} they knotted over timber until the ones left cut a new clearing,/
because the old one had nobody in it to cut. That is what a kept cord comes to./
A clearing with birches through the hearth-stones, and a cord on a peg with no wall.

Take it, Skathrik. Take it before the seats, and let me untie it./
I will untie it with these hands, and I will say Armund's name while I do it,/
and I will say it every winter at the _eldskorn_ until I am a name said after his,/
and your word stands, because the kindred took the price, and not you.

[Armund's widow said:]{.speaker}/
I am Hárvith-born, and I married across the clearing, and my kindred has killed my husband./
I will say that once, so the seats have heard a Harvith woman say it in a Grimskorn hall./
My mother is sitting there with her ox. My brother is sitting there with his hands in his lap, and his hands did it./
And I have a girl of five on this bench who is both of you, whatever either hall does.

I take the price. Hear me. The girl's mother takes it, for the girl, before the seats./
If the kindred's word is its mistress's, then I have said mine, and it is on the reckoning./
If the kindred says the kindred's word is Skathrik's, then I will hear it from him,/
and I will know which hall my girl sleeps in by the end of this, and so will he.

[Vristulf's mother said:]{.speaker}/
I have offered what I have offered, and the Skathár says it is full, and I say it is more than full./
The two from the south are my hands. They cut my wood. The boat is my boat, and I have had it nine years./
The ox is the one that broke our clearing, and there is no other./
I give them because Vristulf is mine, and I would sooner feed a Grimskorn girl than burn a Grimskorn son.

I will say one thing to the one who refuses, if he refuses./
I have ten in my hall who can hold a spear, and they are listening to you, not to me./
What you say to the seats you say to them, and they will say it back in their own hall,/
and I will have to sit and hear it, as your mother is sitting and hearing you now.

[The War Chief said:]{.speaker}/
I have the wall, and the band that holds it, and that is the whole of my charge./
I do not have your cord. I do not have the Harvith cord. The Skathár has them./
I did not hear your vow. I was on the wall that spring; somebody was./
Nine heard it, and nine are of my band, and that is where it touches me.

A _vora_ binds the one who says it. That is what a _vora_ is./
It binds the ones who heard it to say they heard it, and to nothing else./
A witness is a mouth and not a spear. Say that back to me, you nine./
If a witness were a spear, a boy's boast would raise a band, and the wall be bare by midsummer.

I will say what I see. Seeing is my charge./
Nine men gone to the traplines with spears, hunting Harvith, is nine men off the wall./
The Harvith have ten who can hold a spear. That is ten more./
Nineteen off a wall of sixty, and the landing open, and the boats pulled up in plain sight of the bay.

I cannot make a kindred take a price. The seats have no such hand./
I cannot make the nine stay on the wall if they will go. I can only count them going./
I am counting. Eight. The ninth is standing by the door, and has not moved./
That one has the wall for his charge, and knows it, and I will remember which one he was.

[Edrarún said:]{.speaker}/
I keep the cords. Here is the Grimskorn cord, and I have brought it, and here is the Harvith./
Each has its knots from before any of you, and each has been untied as often as tied./
A cord is made to be untied. A cord that is only knotted is a rope,/
and a rope is for hanging things from, and what hangs from this one will be your own.

I will say what a thing costs at Othholt. It is in my head and nowhere else, and you should hear it once./
A hand, a boat. An eye, a boat and a winter's pelts. A tooth, a pelt; a front tooth, two./
A dog, a lamb, if it was a good dog. The Harvith paid a lamb for a dog in my mother's time, and it was a bad dog, and they paid./
A man, what I have said. A Druthmund, what I have said. There is no price above it, and nobody has ever asked me for one.

I have untied this cord nineteen times in forty winters, and the Harvith cord twenty-two./
A boat burned. A girl got with child and not married. A slave of theirs killed by a slave of ours over a goose./
Every one of them was a knot, and every one came out, and the two halls cut in the grove the next autumn./
And here is the Grimskorn cord with no knot in it this morning, and I am the one who will put one in, and I have the fingers for it.

I have reckoned the price. Hear it again, because a reckoning is said aloud or it is nothing./
Forty winter pelts, marten and fox. A boat with four oars. An ox. An iron kettle./
Two Othmund, a man and his wife out of the south. The Harvith half of the grove to the girl./
I have reckoned it full. I have reckoned prices for forty winters, and I have not reckoned a fuller.

You say you take no cow for him. The cow is not for him./
Nothing is for him. He is past taking. The cow is for the bench he left,/
for the girl at it, for the _othrún_ who has to fill a bowl at it every night,/
and for the winter after this one, which does not care what you swore in a wet grove.

I will hold this price if you refuse it. That is the custom at Othholt, and I keep it./
The ox will eat my hay. The pelts will lie in my loft, and I will turn them against the moth./
The two from the south will work my clearing, and not know whose they are./
And it will be here, all of it, the day a Grimskorn hand reaches for it, if there is a Grimskorn hand.

[The Shaman said:]{.speaker}/
I will say one thing, and it is the thing you have not asked, because you know better./
You want to know whether he holds you to it. Whether Armund, where he is, holds you to it./
I will not go and ask him. That is the one question nobody carries to the _wesk_./
Nobody will ever tell you whether he holds you to it, and the keeping or the breaking is yours, without knowing.

The dead are not asked about blood. Not by me, not by anyone, not for you./
If you keep the vow, you keep it to a man who cannot release you./
If you take the price, you take it from a man who cannot forgive you./
Either way you go on without his word.

[Skathrik said:]{.speaker}/
I heard my mother. I heard the War Chief. I heard the Skathár and the Shaman./
I will answer each, and then I will say what I said in the grove, and then I am done talking./
Mother, the byre is yours, and the stores, and the girl, and I take none of them from you./
I take one thing from you, and it is the one thing you have not got, which is my word.

War Chief, the wall is yours. I was on it, and I will not be on it. Count me off it./
I did not swear to the wall. I swore before the band, and the band was nine, and they are here./
Skathár, your reckoning is full. I say it is full. Hear me say it: it is full./
A full price is still a price, and I said no price, and the saying was heard.

Shaman, I did not ask you. I will not ask you. I know what is not asked./
He cannot release me, you say. Then I am not released./
If he could speak and said take the ox, I would take the ox and be glad of it./
He cannot speak. So I have only what he heard me say, and he heard me say this.

You who were his wife: the girl sleeps in this hall. She is Grimskorn here, and she is fed here./
You may sleep where you like, and nobody will bar a door to you on either side of the clearing./
You who were Vristulf's mother: keep your ox. Keep your boat. I have no use for either./
Tell your ten what you like. They heard me, and I would say it again to them in their own hall, and I expect I will.

He was first to stand. He was first to stand in the grove, and first at the boar, and first at the birch,/
and I was second, every time, and the second is where I stood, and I stood there because he was in front of it./
He is not in front of it now. There is nothing in front of me. I can see the whole clearing./
I would give the ox and the boat and the girl's grove to have something in front of me again, and that is not in the price.

If you fall by a hand, I will find the hand that felled you./
I take no cow for you, no pelt, no boat, no ground./
Blood for blood, and the cord stays knotted till I have it./
Before these nine who hear it, I say it, and they will hold me to it.

What is a _vora_ worth in this hall after today? That is what I am asking the seats./
If I take forty pelts for it, then it is worth forty pelts, and every vow in Othholt has a price./
Every man who swears before the band will be asked afterward what he will take instead,/
and the number will go down the river with the pelts, and come back up with the grain.

I know what it costs. My mother counted it, and the War Chief counted it, and the Skathár counts for her living./
I am not a fool, and I am not a boy in a wet grove. I was a boy then. I am what I said then./
Knot it, Edrarún. Knot the Grimskorn cord, one, for Armund./
And when you have knotted it, put it back on the peg where the girl can see it, so she knows what her father was worth.

[The nine said:]{.speaker}/
Eight said: We heard it. We struck our spears on the roots. We hold him to it, and we go where he goes./
The ninth said: I heard it. I struck my spear on the roots. I hold him to it,/
and I am holding the wall, because that is what I swore to afterward, before the same band,/
and a man can keep two words if they do not meet, and I will keep this one until they meet.

[The Other Chief said:]{.speaker}/
It is said, and it is heard. Knot it, Skathár./
The seats have no hand to lay on a kindred, and I lay none on him.

[Theodár malwen.]{.speaker}/
Skathár skatha. Wi hru hem, wi halwen hem lurni./
Othren malwen weoren hem nemra, hewra, frar sethar, et nawen andwe sta.

[Mowen er hem malwen.]{.speaker}/
Wi tavra makra fran thri thum mewi othil,/
vird tavra hemwen nuwa, vird malwen heith inwe, vird sethar hruda hem./
Barer feor grema, ivwe grema thum skurwi./
Grasdri feor thei ku, vird wemi harve threm./
Vird larni sethar sider glarth wemi harve,/
nawen ku vird nawen Harvith wredri hurba hem.

Fedr tenra skurth thum bereth er badra,/
vird badra thum inwe bubren. Ukthe brekwa/
frelwen wemi wivre brekri tenra jaren./
Melgud er hemwen, hraldi. Melgud er wemi brekri,/
vird watra wenga thurwe semra. Vird junri wemra/
harve werlak, erwen, nawen feor wenfraw stelwen.

Wi selra elra ter Armund, vird wi wethla hem gerwen dagren,/
vird wi wethla hem gendre feor brenth, wethla bren, melwa./
Na malwen ter wi wirdra er Armund. Wi berwa hem, wi witna hem swerth./
Mowen er Vristulf witna Vristulf swerth. Hem sedra./
Hem bubra ukthe er hem, vird wi weir ukthe./
Ukthe thum sundri. Ukthe thum sundri.

Thri malwen vora. Wi hru. Awen hemren hru hem,/
thei junri, watri werlak, niwen slagra./
Marwen malwen vora. Mawren nemra wirdwen./
Thri selra malwa er thri. Nawen mawen frawna mawren,/
vird mawren thum othrún, vird othrún thum wi, mowen er thri.

Frithwin er thri thum mi wesk, urwe na. Wi na witna heini,/

: The Grove Again {#grove}

::: {.verse form=drunga lang=en work=grove}
In the third _grema_ the river was a road again, and there was nobody left to walk it but seven men./
Three Harvith who could still hold a spear, and Skathrik, and the three of the nine who were left,/
and each side knew where the other would be. There was one place left, and it was the grove./
They went to it in daylight, from the two sides, and the birch with the two marks on it stood between them.

It is not told how long it took. Nobody was there who could tell it./
The crows came down at midday, and the ninth saw them from the wall, and knew what crows over the grove meant,/
and did not go, because it was midday and he was on the wall, and the wall is left at dusk./
He left it at dusk. The War Chief took the gate from him without a word, and he went up through the snow.

Six lay in the grove, among the birch stumps that were the whole of the quarrel,/
two of them under the marked birch, one Harvith and one of the nine, with each other's spears in them,/
and Skathrik lay with his back against the oak Armund had stood under, and was not dead./
The ninth knelt. He had kept the wall three winters, and the wall had been quiet, and this was what he had kept it for.

[Skathrik said:]{.speaker}/
Count them for me. I cannot see the far ones. Count them, and I will tell you if it is right.

[The ninth said:]{.speaker}/
Vristulf, on the ice at the landing, with his axe half out. Theirs, and the first./
Two at the trapline, theirs. Your sister's man at the ford, ours./
Three at the woodpile, ours, and two there, theirs./

[Skathrik said:]{.speaker}/
Twenty-one with Armund. Yes. That is the number./
Say it to Edrarún. She will want the number before she wants anything./
Is there a Harvith man left?

[The ninth said:]{.speaker}/
None who can stand. The women go to Domholt at the thaw, with the children./
There is a girl in your mother's hall, Armund's, and she is theirs on the one side.

[Skathrik said:]{.speaker}/
Then she is the last of theirs that stays, and the last of ours, and she has both halls,/
and I am not sorry, and you can say that in the hall, because it is the truth and I said it./
It was a fine thing to hear in the grove. It is kept. Tell them it is kept./
Carry me down. Not past their hall. Round it, and not past it.

He said nothing after that, and the ninth carried him down the long way,/
round the grove, wide of the Harvith hall, where a woman stood in the door and did not call out,/
and across the clearing to the Grimskorn door, where there was a light./
The _othrún_ opened it, and looked at what was carried, and said, I have washed him once.

and banked the fire, as she had been shown, and there was nobody else to do it.

Edrarún came across the clearing in the deep of that _grema_, and she was old,/
older than when she tied the first knot, and she had tied all eleven, and that is the Skathár's hand./
Behind her came the ox, and the pelts on the ox in two bundles, and the kettle,/
and the boat on the snow, dragged by the two from the south, who had worked three winters not knowing whose they were.

It was slow across the clearing. The ox went in to the knee and the keel of the boat cut a furrow,/
and the breath of the ox and the breath of the two from the south stood in the air behind them,/
and the girl saw it from the door, the whole of it coming, and did not know what it was,/
and stood in the door with the cold coming past her. Nobody had told her to shut it, and nobody was coming to.

The hall had two benches and a fire and a girl. It had a cord on a peg by the door./
Edrarún stood inside the door and said the price, aloud, because it is said aloud or it is nothing./
Forty winter pelts, marten and fox. A boat with four oars. An ox. An iron kettle./
Two Othmund, a man and his wife out of the south. The Harvith half of the grove, to Armund's _othwa_.

[Edrarún said:]{.speaker}/
Ten knots against ten, set off, as custom sets them. One knot left, the first./
The price for the first is here, and I have held it, and it is full, and I say it is full./
Who takes it?

[Árhilda said:]{.speaker}/
There is nobody but me.

[Edrarún said:]{.speaker}/
Then you. Hold the end.

The two from the south stood in the door, where they had been told to stand, with the snow on their shoulders./
The man looked at the girl on the bench, and at the old woman, and at the cord, and said nothing in any tongue./
The girl looked at them. She had been told they were in the price, and they were the only part of it that looked at her./
Nobody told them to come in to the fire. There was nobody there whose place it was to say it, and so it was not said.

Edrarún took the cord down off the peg and sat on the bench with it across her knees,/
and the girl held the end, as she had been told, and the two from the south stood in the door./
The ten came out one by one, and some had been tied in a hurry, and came easily,/
and some had been tied in a cold hall by a hand that shook, and had to be worked with a thumbnail.

The last was the first, the one for Armund, tied at the first moon after the thaw/
in a hall with three seats in it and a kindred standing and nine young men behind him,/
and it had been pulled tight by every knot after it, three winters' worth of pulling,/
and it did not want to come, and the old fingers worked it, and the girl held the end, and it came.
