[[place-mahnryth|Mahnreth]], a village of [[place-skathwald|Skathwald]] under the [[lore-rabbitttm|rabbit]] wesk, is a clearing cut by the survivors of three villages a [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar tribe]] took in one winter of [The Waking]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, and kept as a village because they stayed ([[lore-grimakburning|The Burning at Grimak]]). **The Mahnreth Lament**, _Mahnreth meldra_, is the _meldra_ its women raise over [[place-mahnhrom|Mahnhrum]], the kinsmen's mound at the edge of the grove that holds grave goods and nobody. A _meldra_ is raised once, over a body, before the burial or the pyre, and is made fresh for each of the dead. This one is raised every year at the turning into _grema_, in the first dry snow, because that is when the taking began, and it is the only lament in [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]] held word for word: the dead it is raised over are the same every year. It is held as it was first raised, so the snow-shoes are strung "last night" and the sister stands there with the comb in her hair in every year's singing.

The women stand on the mound and keep the time with the heel on the frozen ground. The one who leads is the mistress of the household that carried the most goods west, and the seat passes with the house; the eldest woman present sings the two corrections in the words of the woman who first made them. The lament names five of the taken and a child who had no name. The rest of them went with the three villages, and nobody who walked west had their names. Which of the three villages fell first the lament does not say, since the reciters still argue it. The Weskár consented to the mound, closed it and stood at the rite as at any funeral, and will not go out to the wesk to ask where the taken went; the lament says so, and the one question it begins is stopped before it ends. The Grukar are named once in it and never again, and the thicket at [[place-grimak|Grimak]] a day's walk east, where the taken died, is named only to say why the women do not sing there.
