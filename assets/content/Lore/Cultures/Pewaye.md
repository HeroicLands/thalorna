The **Pewaye** are the edge-people, one of the four traditions of the [[lore-bikoclt|forest peoples]]. Their name is _pewa_, the edge where the forest ends, with the ending of one who dwells beside it. Outsiders think they are the forest people. The other forest peoples think they are hardly forest people at all.

## Where They Live

Pewaye tribes live along the margins of the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], where the forest breaks into grass. In the north their neighbours are [[lore-okharinclt|Okharin]] herders, who bring their cattle to the forest's edge in the dry season. In the south their neighbours are the [Sengala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba]], the leopard-clan (see [[affiliation-nyalbleop|Leopard Totem]]) that trades north across the grass-and-tree belt.

## How They Live

The Pewaye forage and hunt the forest behind them, and each band keeps a small garden on the forest side of the edge, fenced with thorn and watched from a platform. Their camps stay put longer than any other tradition's, sometimes for a generation, because a band's place on the edge is its own and it does not leave it.

They trade more than any other tradition, and with more kinds of people. Their _gbenindo_, the keepers of the trade-friendships, deal with herders and Sengala traders at the edge and carry the goods into the forest. They have iron spear-heads and knives that the deep tribes lack, glass beads, and cloth for the leader's back, and their [[skill-ekpelulng|Ekpelu]] carries outside words for the outside things: for iron, for cattle, for salt by the block.

## What the Forest Is

To the Pewaye the forest is a boundary to be held. The forest is the people's and the outside is not, and a Pewaye's work is to keep it so: to let in what the people need, metal and salt, and to keep out what they do not, the herders' cattle, the slavers, the strangers who want to go deeper. Every family holds a _tegbo_, a stretch of the edge, and knows every trail that crosses it.

The Pewaye make offerings and keep the forest's restraints as every forest people does, but they speak of the spirits as allies in the holding, not as hosts or parents. The spirits of the edge watch outward, and so do the Pewaye.

## The Four Standings, and the Leader

A Pewaye child is a _tolu_ until it goes out past the edge alone, to a herders' camp or a Sengala post, and comes back. Then it is _wibo_. A stranger taken in is a tolu until they have held a stretch of edge through a dry season.

A Pewaye _kpaso_ is made by giving. A member who wants to lead goes out past the edge, trades or works or bargains with the outside, and comes back with a _bewu_, a gift for every member of the tribe: a blade, a string of beads, a measure of salt. Each member chooses whether to take it. The member whose bewu most members accept is the leader. A member who takes a leader's bewu owes that leader a voice in council, and a member who wants to unmake the leader brings the gift back and lays it at the leader's door. A leader whose door fills with returned gifts stops being the leader.

The _mbeli_ hold the memory of the edge: which family holds which tegbo, which outsiders have been honest, and which have not.

## Kin

A Pewaye counts kin through the father, because the tegbo passes from father to son. Daughters marry out, into other Pewaye bands and often past the edge altogether: the Sengala keep marriage exchanges with Pewaye tribes, and some herders' families do too. A wife from outside is a tolu until she has learned Ekpelu and the edge, and her children are wibo like any other children of the band.

## The Dead

The dead hold the edge. A Pewaye is buried at the outer line of the family's tegbo, sitting upright and facing out of the forest, and a _hokpe_ is set over the grave: a post carved with a face that looks outward. The line of hokpe along a Pewaye edge is the tribe's dead on watch, and the Pewaye speak to them when they cross out past the edge and again when they come back.

## The Other Three

- **The [[lore-luwandoclt|Luwando]]** are rivals at the door, and soft. Their river brings in anyone, and they feed whoever it brings without asking what they want.
- **The [[lore-yesomaclt|Yesoma]]** are the true forest people, the Pewaye grant, and children. They sleep behind a line they never see the Pewaye hold.
- **The [[lore-kubindoclt|Kubindo]]** are wise and timid. The Pewaye respect their courts, take their sick there, and think them too afraid of the forest to hold any of it.

## What an Outsider Sees

An outsider sees the Pewaye and thinks they are the forest people. They wear northern cloth or Sengala beads, carry iron, speak a few words of the outsider's tongue, and have a leader who looks like a chief, wears the best cloth and receives guests. The herders of the north take the Pewaye leader for the forest's chief, and take the Pewaye claim to the grassland's edge for a border the whole forest means to keep.

What the outsider does not see is that the leader holds that place only while the tribe keeps its gifts, that the cloth was a gift to be given away, and that the deep forest beyond the edge does not regard the Pewaye as its speaker or as its own.
