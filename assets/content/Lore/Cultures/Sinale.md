The Sinalë are the Firstborn, the eldest of the speaking peoples, and they carry themselves as a people who were old before there was anyone to be old beside. They came to Thalorna from a prior realm roughly eight thousand years ago, and of that realm they tell outsiders nothing. Fewer than one in ten thousand of the world's people is a Sinalo, so for nearly everyone the Sinalë are a rumor: a grandmother's story of a figure at the edge of a forest at twilight. They are real, and they have no interest in being found.

This note is about how the Sinalë orient themselves toward the world and toward the younger peoples in it. Most of what is known comes from the few human peoples who live near them, and from what the Sinalë have chosen to let stand as legend.

## The Firstborn

The Sinalë call themselves Sinalë; one of them is a Sinalo, and the adjective is Sinalëan. Humans know them by older names as well: the Elder Folk, the Star-Kindled, the Twilight People. A Sinalo carries a given name and a lineage name, the name of the hearth that keeps it, and is called by the whole given name always. An epithet is added after it and never replaces it.

They hold that they were the first of the speaking peoples to walk Thalorna, and the claim has no rival. For a time they shared the continents only with the creatures and spirits that humans later called fae, and with scattered human bands who lived without cities, letters or settled tongues. The Sinalë call this their Age of Stars. They remember it in song and describe it to no one.

The date of their arrival, about 7500 BF, is held no more closely than a century, and the Sinalë hold it least closely of all, because they do not share their reckoning of years with outsiders. Where they made landfall is not recorded.

## Forest and Starlight

The Sinalë are tall, slender and very long-lived, so long-lived that a generation, as humans mean the word, has no meaning for them. Humans find their beauty unsettling rather than attractive: too perfect, too still, too aware. Their kinship with the living world runs deeper than any human druidic tradition. It is not worship or reverence but something nearer to family, and the forests they inhabit answer it. Paths appear and vanish, distances shift, and the light changes in ways the angle of the sun does not explain.

Their arts are not sorted into schools or set down in texts. The power is woven into their nature and cannot be separated from their songs, their craft and their relationship with the living world. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] has tried at various points to study and classify it and has failed completely. The Sinalë find these attempts somewhere between amusing and insulting.

## A People Who Sing

The Sinalë do not build. They have never wished to: the forest builds itself, and what they make they sing, and what they sing does not fall down. That is the sense in which the song [[lore-seventowers|The Seven Towers]] speaks for them, and it is also their highest measure of praise, since among them nothing is said more highly of a work than that it left them standing a long while in silence.

The song is a Sinalëan elegy for a city of light cut into a cliff, with its halls, its water and its birds, and for the night a horde came up the valley. It never names the city or the people who made it, and it breaks off at the word that would name the renegade. The Sinalë have neither confirmed the bards' reading of it nor corrected it.

The same habit shows in the language. Their words for the world are words for particular moments and places: the first thaw, the still hour, the long dusk, the split log, the lifted stone. The [[skill-sinalelng|Sinalë language]] and its [[doc-sinalelexcn|lexicon]] hold the whole word-hoard.

## Where They Live

The Sinalë live in enclaves: hidden communities in deep forests, mist-filled valleys and island sanctuaries, placed far from human settlement and shown on no human map. An enclave is not a city. It is a place where the boundary between the natural world and something older grows thin, where the trees are impossibly ancient and the water impossibly clear.

Walls and armies do not protect an enclave. The land does. A path that leads to one uninvited leads somewhere else, and a traveler who approaches without welcome walks in circles, loses track of time, or is overcome by the conviction that turning back is right. None of this is hostile. The Sinalë have no interest in harming lost travelers, and the protection is absolute all the same.

Human scholars have confirmed perhaps a dozen enclaves, in the forests of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], in the mountains of [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], in the deep woods of [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]] and in remote corners of other continents. There are certainly more. The ones humans know are probably the ones the Sinalë consider least important.

Not every enclave endures. [[place-haulonna|Haulonna]], deep in the interior of the Kingdom of Nordheim, was destroyed by a tereb necromancer in the service of Náhild, and the spirits of its people are held captive. The Sinalë call this an open wound, and they have not forgotten it.

## Restraint

The Sinalë are not hostile to humanity. They are profoundly uninterested in human politics, wars, religions and commerce, which they regard as the concerns of a younger people working through problems the Sinalë settled or abandoned long ago. To ask a Sinalo to take sides in a human quarrel is to ask a grandparent to adjudicate between toddlers. The Sinalë might do it, but they would rather not.

This disengagement is older than any human memory of it. When they came to Thalorna, humans were already here, and the Sinalë chose then to let that younger people find its own way, neither shepherding its rise nor opposing it. Individual Sinalë do occasionally meet individual humans: a traveler is given shelter, a scholar a cryptic answer, a lost child is guided home. These meetings are rare, unpredictable and always on the Sinalë's terms, and the Sinalë let the legends that follow them stand.

Restraint did not keep humanity from learning. Humans watched the Sinalë and took what they saw, and the [[lore-longnhrtnc|Long Inheritance]] is the name for that. Its Sinalëan channel is the immaterial one: song and the forms of poetry, the practice of keeping lore rather than merely remembering it, healing, and the idea that magic can be studied and answers to discipline rather than only to accident and terror. Whether any Sinalo ever said so to a human is the point in dispute. The evidence is resemblance, a mode of thought and a shape of practice, and resemblance proves nothing. The [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Colleges]] concede the debt freely, and the people who believe it most readily are those who have met a Sinalo.

Beyond the general policy, individual Sinalë act as they see fit. A student who asked well has been taught, and the Long Inheritance records such transmissions as unauthorized and undocumented. Marriage, fosterage and long friendship occur at the edge where the Sinalë and the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] meet, rarely and quietly.

## Those Who Carry

The Sinalë do not treat with human powers. They have not for millennia, they decline to explain why, and the handful of exceptions are one exception: they deal with the Áelendan, and the Áelendan carry.

In Élavendre a warning, a refusal, a question about a disturbed site or the return of something taken passes between a Sinalë enclave and a human authority through an Áelendan who has been asked to carry it. The Áelendan carry exactly, adding nothing, and none of them claims to speak for the Sinalë. The Tradition Keepers hold the songs the Sinalë have given freely, and sing what comes out of an enclave as it was received.

The result is that the crown of Élavendre's long relationship with the Sinalë is in practice a relationship with Áelendan intermediaries. It is not the monarchy's to possess but the tribes' to grant. The Sinalë have never said why they opened themselves to the Áelendan and to no other human people, and the Áelendan do not discuss it.

Élavendre holds three enclaves, each at a different distance from the people who carry for them:

- [[place-ethalosse|Ethalossë]], about seventy Sinalë on the eastern edge of the Áelendan tribal lands, is placed for access. It is where the Sinalë conduct business with the world, and the one enclave a human has a realistic hope of being invited to. It is spare: a court of worked stone older than the forest around it, a few dwellings, and a long open way down which visitors are watched for a considerable time before they arrive.
- [[place-aelirosse|Aelirossë]], about a hundred and twenty Sinalë in the Silver Forests, is the closest to the Áelendan of any Sinalëan community in the world. Its dwellings are worked into living silver birch, and its Áelendan neighbors come and go without ceremony.
- [[place-serenthale|Serenthalë]], about ninety Sinalë facing the western sea in Glanmor, is the oldest of the three and by far the most withdrawn. It keeps to itself even from the Áelendan. Contact is real, respectful and rare, and it happens at the enclave's initiative and nobody else's. No Áelendan guides anyone to it.

The Áelendan Wardens patrol the approaches and turn the Ordo back at the tree line. The Áelendan regard the Ordo as intruders who study what the tribes exist to keep.

## Beyond Élavendre

Elsewhere the welcome is thinner, and each place is its own case.

On Aelwyth the Sinalë of the [[place-hallowwood|Hallowwood]] tolerate embassies from Dúnavarre, welcome Élavendre and receive the Áelendan above all. They once held the island under a Sinalë king, together with the Khazári, until he was killed at the Three Sisters. Enclaves are also known in Vrystwald.

In the Empire of [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]] the Sinalë keep a formal contact with the imperial court that exists nowhere else, and it appears to rest on coercion or obligation rather than affinity. The empire files them in its caste order by function: scholars among them are Tānthëi advisers, and a very few are nobility. The Sinalë accept the arrangement pragmatically, keep their own self-understanding inside their enclaves, and go on with their work. Nobody outside knows how it began, and the Sinalë will not say.

The Sinalë also maintain some form of connection with the peoples of K'ich'chik, which western scholarship knows too little of, on either side, to describe.

## Two Meetings

Two encounters sit at the edge of what the Sinalë will say, and both are passed over in silence.

On [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]], Sinalëan wayfarers found a human civilization already established on the teachings of an older people who were still in the world. The Kaliharans received them with courtesy and refused them settlement. The Sinalë have never given a public account of it.

Earlier and elsewhere, the Sinalë came upon that older people themselves, the [[lore-theithari|Ithári]], and were received and politely declined. No exchange was proposed and no second meeting arranged. The Sinalë came away having learned nothing: not who they had met, where those people came from, or that any connection ran between them and the works on Kalihara. They do not use the name Ithári, because they were never given it. By their own account, the impression they took away is that they were found quaint. Both peoples confirm that the meeting occurred, and that is all either gives. See [[lore-itharicntc|The Sinalë Meet the Ithári]].

## The Second Elder Race

After the Sinalë came the Khazári, the Deep Folk, a people unlike them in form and temperament but kindred in spirit. The Sinalë worked in forest and starlight and the Khazári in stone and flame, and for a time the two lived in concert, under a single accord, and made wonders neither could have made alone.

Then the two separated completely and permanently, long before any human city. They do not speak, they do not trade, and they acknowledge each other only in the most oblique terms. What broke the accord was [[lore-khazarturn|Khazártúrn]]. In 2427 BF a Sinalë outlaw took up the Second Voice, a relic the Sinalë knew of and had never thought worth mentioning, because a weapon that commands Grukar is a weapon against cities and the Sinalë build none. He used it to destroy the greatest city the Khazári ever raised, and thirty thousand died. The Sinalë hunted him down themselves, took a long time over it, and afterward handed over nothing and explained nothing.

Each people guards the secret for its own reason. The Khazári guard it as their humiliation. The Sinalë guard it because one of their own did it, and because of what they had failed to say beforehand. A scholar who presses the question meets a silence so absolute and so cold that the conversation is not attempted again.

## What the Sinalë Keep Closed

Several things are held entirely inside the Sinalë and are not supplied here:

- Where the Sinalë came from, and whether it is the realm the Khazári left.
- Their own reckoning of years.
- The full history of the Sinalë as a people, which only the Sinalë know.
- How they conduct a life, from birth through age and death, what they hold to be good or evil, and what they hold a person owes. The sources that exist describe what the Sinalë do toward others, not how they live among themselves.
- Their faith. The folk note's glossary records a Sinalë word for the open convocation of the faith and a Sinalë name for the God of Dreams, and says nothing more.

## What Passes Between Peoples

Three groups stand on what the Sinalë give or withhold. The Áelendan carry their words and receive their songs. The Bardic Colleges sing the songs at three and four removes from any Sinalo who sang them. The Ordo Arcanis has an institutional interest in the answer to where magic came from: a tradition that was inherited rather than discovered has a source that might still be consulted.

For a player, the one thing to understand first is that a character does not deal with the Sinalë. A character deals with the people the Sinalë trust to carry, and those people carry exactly. The [[doc-sinaleadvguide|Sinalë Adventurer's Guide]] covers what a stranger meets first, what gets a stranger into trouble, and the ways a campaign reaches them.
