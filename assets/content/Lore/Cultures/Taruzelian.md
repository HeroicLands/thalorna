Taruzelian culture is mercantile to the bone. Wealth, reputation, and the ability to strike a profitable bargain are the virtues, named in that order, and a man good at all three is the nearest thing this coast has to an aristocrat.

Twelve years ago the Taruzelians won their independence from the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] by force of arms, and the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] is what they made of it. The veterans of that war are alive, in their forties, and watching the merchant oligarchs who funded it hold the freedoms they bled for. Everything uncomfortable about [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] today sits in that sentence.

## The Houses

The great trading houses are the real power: sprawling family enterprises combining banking, shipping, warehousing and political influence in a single concern, with warehouses on every harbor, agents in every port on the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]], and reach into the courts of Vylaria, [[place-provenzrgn|Provenzia]] and [[place-helionis|Helionis]].

A house is a family and a firm at once, and a Taruzelian does not separate the two. A marriage is a merger, a son is an apprentice and an heir and a hostage to the alliance his betrothal sealed, and a daughter who can read a ledger is worth more to her house than a son who cannot. The houses have been unsentimental about that long enough that women captain ships and hold partnerships without anyone remarking on it. The one craft that holds out is shipbuilding: the shipwrights' guild resists women in the trade, and [[being-blgnrskn|Belgina Rusakuni]], the shipwright of _[[affiliation-thetamzir|The Tamzir]]_, is the exception.

Above the houses sits the [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]], the banking cartel, and every Taruzelian knows that a confederation with a magnificent navy and a debt to the Compact is a confederation that does what its creditor prefers. The Confederation's treasury and mint are the [Merazuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} of the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]]; the Compact only lends.

## The House at Every Age

The unit is the house, _taruzi_, the houses of a line and the firms they keep, with no word to tell the family from the firm. Membership is the name: a given name and a family name, the family name closing in one of three endings that mark a line, a whole company or a family's honor. A marriage (_teshudush_) is a merger of two houses.

The ladder is the house's. A child of the guild families is schooled in rhetoric, mathematics and mercantile law. A child of the middle class learns at another's bench (_bissush_, an apprenticeship) and is examined by the Vezakuni. A child of the harbor districts goes to the docks or the deck. At the top of the ladder, a shipmaster owns his hull and a merchant has a partnership. An elder (_kalurum_) has age and the standing it brings and stands in the house's counsels.

## Three Taruzels

The guild elite live in palatial urban estates, patronize the arts, and school their children in rhetoric, mathematics and mercantile law. They are cultivated, cosmopolitan, and entirely clear-eyed about what their money is for.

The middle—independent merchants, skilled craftspeople and ship captains—is large and is the actual economic backbone. A Taruzelian captain owning his own hull is the characteristic figure of the region: proud, indebted, competent, and one bad season from working for a house.

The harbor districts are cramped, poor and increasingly restless. Dock workers and sailors carried the war, and a decade of being told that liberty has been achieved has not improved their temper.

Apart from all three stand the hill tribes of the larger islands, culturally distinct, speaking archaic dialects, keeping older traditions, and resenting the port cities. They supplied a great many of the foot soldiers for independence and have seen almost none of the benefit, and harbor Taruzelians tend to forget they exist until a levy is needed.

## The Bargain as a Social Form

Haggling is not rudeness here, and refusing to haggle is. A price offered and accepted without discussion insults both parties—the seller because his opening was not taken seriously, the buyer because he has been treated as someone not worth the time.

The negotiation is also how relationships are formed. A Taruzelian learns what kind of man he is dealing with over an hour of tea and argument, and he would sooner trade at a worse rate with someone he has read than a better one with a stranger. Foreigners who send a factor with fixed terms and no authority to move are regarded as having declined to meet.

Hospitality is lavish and is a display of credit. A house that feeds forty people well is telling the harbor something specific about its liquidity.

## Reputation and the Long Ledger

A Taruzelian's credit is his name, literally: the houses lend on reputation across distances where no writ runs, and a merchant who defaults has lost money and made himself unfinanceable from [[place-shamular|Shamular]] to Helionis.

So the coast is scrupulous about debt in a way that surprises people who expect merchants to be sharp. Sharpness is admired in a negotiation and despised in a settlement. The Taruzelian view is that a bargain hard-driven is a bargain, while a bargain not honored is theft, and that distinction is the whole basis of the trade.

## Justice as a Merchant Means It

The Taruzelian word for justice is _kurrim_, the return of what is owed, justice as a merchant means it. **Good is the bargain struck and held (_qatush_), the price named fairly (_pashinu_) and the debt (_fusanush_) settled when it falls due; evil is the bargain not honored, which the coast calls theft, and the promise that was never meant.** The two are not the same offense. A hard-driven bargain is a bargain, and a deal broken after the clasp is a crime against everyone who lends on a name. The broadsheet of the war is the plain case: the men who printed it spoke of freedom, and Taruzel is free of the empire's taxes, _therkoi_ (governors) and conscription, but what the people who fought heard in the same word was a promise nobody had made.

Success is a name that lenders will stand behind (_mardu_, credit, the worth of a man's name in the market). The marks run in order: a hull of one's own, a partnership (_balinu_), a seat on the council of one's city, a house that other houses marry into, a surety (_rumukum_) whom other men ask to stand for them. Failure is not poverty. It is to be unfinanceable: the merchant who defaulted and has lost his credit from Shamular to Helionis, the captain refused every harbor, the house whose debts are called in all at once. A broke man with a clean name can borrow. A rich man with a bad one cannot.

A Taruzelian values the name, the fair price, patience with a debtor and the display of credit. He values least the improviser with no one to stand for him, the sharpness that survives into the settlement, and loyalty to the Confederation as a thing in itself.

## The Man Admired

[[being-zbzmjkdn|Zabuzum Jakuduni]], captain of the _Tamzir_, is the Taruzelian shipmaster (_jakudum_) who owns his hull. He was born in [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]] to cattle herders, lost his father at eleven, worked the docks at fourteen and shipped as a deckhand at sixteen. At twenty-two he joined the rebel fleet and rose to command a raiding vessel, and at the [[place-tamzirshoals|Shoals]] he led a squadron through the reef-studded water at dawn and took the blade across the jaw that left his scar. When the guilds took the victory he refused to serve them. He bought a battered sixty-foot merchant vessel, named her for the shoals where he nearly died, and has worked the Vylarian Sea since as a trader and, when trade does not pay, something less. He is loyal to the crew who fought beside him and takes the jobs the respectable captains refuse.

The coast admires in him its characteristic figure, the captain who owns his hull: proud, indebted and competent. His cost is the indebtedness. The Auric Compact's enforcers hold old debts from the war, he lives outside any flag's protection, and the ship is one bad season from belonging to a house.

## The Woman Admired

[Yushuduya Jakuduni]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is the Kubasum, the Counter, of the Vezakuni, and the woman who kept Taruzel's paper good. When the war began, the clearing accounts that carried every merchant's script were frozen within weeks, and the harbor cities faced commercial collapse before the first engagement. She was a banker of Taruzelian lineage who had spent twenty years in the empire's moneylenders' college before the secession, and she became the founding Kubasum. Out of nothing she built the Merazuni, the Company of the Ledger, which takes bullion, issues master script against it and clears for the moneylender houses of the Confederation. She has held the office for twelve years.

Her admiration is not a lesser form of the man's. Zabuzum keeps a hull afloat on his own name, and Yushuduya keeps the name of the whole coast good enough that a stranger will take its paper. Her cost is the border. The Merazuni's script is honored at no Vylarian house, its reserves are small beside the empire's, and the Heliónite houses that profit from converting between the two are tolerated for as long as no agreement is possible.

## What the Coast Keeps

A Taruzelian can point to a handful of things that make the system work. **The seal** of a house or a guild closes a contract, and the Vezakuni's own is an open ledger over a coiled rope and an anchor under the Confederation's eight-pointed star. **The charter** of a trade is granted and enforced by the Vezakuni. **The master script** of the Merazuni is printed on distinctive paper struck with the same star and the issuing moneylender's seal. The [Tamzir Monument]{.name data-type="place/site"}, a captured Vylarian warship in dry-dock at [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], is the nearest thing the Confederation has to a standard kept in a temple. **The broadsheets** pinned in the veterans' halls are the nearest thing it has to a founding document that the founders did not write. The [Recovered Compacts]{.name data-type="lore"}, the Vezakuni's program of restoring the old compact-houses and their treaties, are the Confederation's attempt to find an ancestry older than the empire.

## Faith, Lightly Worn

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] is kept, with the prosperity and voyage gods drawing the real devotion and a great deal of sincere private prayer before a sailing. Temples are endowed by houses, and the endowments are publicized.

Taruzelians are not much interested in doctrine and are extremely interested in omens, luck and the specific patronage of the god who governs their particular sea route. A foreign priest arriving with theology finds the ports polite and inattentive; one arriving with a reputation for effective blessings finds them generous.

An ordinary Taruzelian's observance is practical. A merchant spends the night before a major contract at a shrine of the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]], making an offering and asking for eloquence and a favorable outcome, and presents a portion of a good profit at her altar afterward. A sailor keeps a small shrine to [[affiliation-brazemas|Brazemas]] aboard and offers the first portion of a meal. Before a sailing he prays for the weather and for the god of the voyage. In the eastern states and among the hill tribes the household keeps the Ashaian fire-ritual at sunrise and sunset, and in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] the [Temple of Two Fires]{.name data-type="place/structure"} holds the rites of both faiths under one roof. A single family often patronizes both.

## The Year at Sea and Ashore

The festivals that matter are the ones that begin, run or settle a voyage and a debt. [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]]'s [Fleet Review]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, held every year in memory of the Shoals, is the war's festival, and it draws visitors from across the Confederation; Besikar keeps the same memory in naval reviews and public festivals. The faith adds its own. Ólvenia's three-day [Festival of the Golden Harvest]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} falls in late summer, when the traders come home with laden ships, and merchants and craftspeople close their shops to take part. The [Thunderhead Vigil]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} at the onset of spring, kept by Brazemas's priests and faithful on hilltops, city towers and ship decks, waits for the first storm. The [Festival of the Silent Passage]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] closes the temples to the living for three nights in late autumn. In the eastern states and at Kudumel, the Ashaian festivals keep their own season.

Each is for something concrete. The Review keeps the war that founded the Confederation in the mind of everyone who profits from it. The Golden Harvest celebrates a voyage that paid. The Vigil asks the sea's weather for the year. The Silent Passage settles the year's dead.

## Where the Council Ends and the Temple Begins

Authority is layered, and the guilds hold most of it. The city is governed by a Council whose First holds the seal, and the offices under him keep the charters, the customs and the port. The [Grand Council]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} at Besikar gives each city an equal voice on paper and counts the votes by property in practice, and the Arch-Consul rotates among the cities and is mostly ceremonial. The guilds hold the weight: the Compact lends, the [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]] sails and the [[affiliation-mrchntryvl|Merchantry of the Veil]] trades in luxuries and information. Beneath the three, the Vezakuni charters the trades, registers masters, examines apprentices and judges disputes between them, and the Confederation backs its rulings.

The temple is the fourth, and it lives in partnership with the guilds. Temples are endowed by houses and the endowments are publicized. The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] (the Arcane Order) is restricted, because the guilds regard it as a possible instrument of Vylarian influence. Where the answers conflict, the seal decides. A contract under the Compact's seal binds as tightly as any court order, the Vezakuni judges the disputes between trades, and the Grand Council, nominally the highest authority, follows the guilds. The Merazuni is the Confederation's treasury and mint, clearing its script and striking its coin; the Compact is its creditor, and a confederation in debt does what its creditor prefers.

## What the Name Earns and Costs

Reward is a longer line of credit. A house whose name is good borrows against it across harbors where no writ runs. Offices follow: the First of a city's Council, the guild-warden's seat, the Warden of the Weights who inspects measures, coin and quality, and for the greatest, the Dirabum, who presides over the Vezakuni for seven years.

Penalty climbs the other way. A broken bargain costs the name, and the lenders' memory runs across the Confederation. A merchant who defaults on a Compact loan finds his credit revoked in every port of the Confederation. A captain who breaks guild protocol finds no harbor master willing to accept her vessel and no crew willing to work for her. A merchant who swindles or reneges in [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]] is blacklisted from the caravan trade for years. At the bottom of the ladder the guilds call in the debt, revoke the license or blacklist the family. A veteran who will not be silenced is accused of debts he does not owe and, in the end, declared an outlaw, and the [Free Captains]{.name data-type="affiliation"} are the ones who took to the sea on that charge.

## Where the Name Goes

The Taruzelian faith is light on doctrine and carries two teachings about the dead side by side. The Aureldian temple teaches that [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] judges and claims the soul, and the priesthood of Sélmoros buries the dead, keeps the records of the dead and counsels the dying. The Ashaian temple teaches that the soul crosses the [Chinvat Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, wide for the righteous and a razor's edge for the wicked, and the [Festival of the Departed]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} has families speak their dead aloud around a fire. A Taruzelian takes whichever his house patronizes, and many houses patronize both.

What the belief changes on an ordinary day is the ledger. A Taruzelian reserves loyalty for institutions that have outlasted him, and the house is the one institution he hopes will outlast him, so he leaves it solvent: a debt he dies owing is a debt the house inherits, and a house is judged by its payments. The temples are endowed by houses and the endowments are publicized, so the endowment is both the offering and the credit. He does not expect the Confederation to remember him. He expects his name, and the house's, to be read where credit is read.

## Reaching the Dead

The living reach the dead through the temple the house patronizes and the rite it pays for. The priesthood of Sélmoros opposes any practice that raises the dead or violates a grave, so the Aureldian temples give the living no way to question them. In Ashaian practice the family speaks the dead aloud at a fire on the night of the Festival of the Departed, and the [Death-Flame]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} burns in the fire-temple until morning. The dead do not reach the living. They reach the ledger: an unpaid debt, an open endowment and a name that lenders read.

## What a Person Owes

A Taruzelian owes the house first—his family and his firm in one breath, because they are one thing—then his partners, then his crew, then the city whose harbor he uses.

He owes his creditors payment and his debtors patience, and by the coast's reckoning the second is the harder discipline and the more profitable one. He does not put the Confederation high on the list: it is twelve years old, it is run by people he can name, and a Taruzelian reserves loyalty for institutions that have outlasted him.

In return the house owes its members a name that lenders will read and a place in the firm. The partner owes a share of the venture and is owed the same. The crew is owed wages and a captain who keeps his hull. The guild owes its masters a charter and its seal. The surety owes the debt he stood for. The city owes a freeman the register, the market and the court. The Confederation owes the veterans of the Shoals the land, the guild membership, the pensions and the voice they were promised, and has not paid.

## The Weak

Care is the house's first. A family and a firm are one body, so the house carries its own, and its honor (_ilammu_, the worth of a house to be spent) is the reason. Beyond the house the guilds are creditors and not charities, and the temples live on endowments. The harbor districts, the fishing villages and the hill tribes have the least: the dock workers and fishermen who won the war were promised land, guild membership, pensions and a voice, and received a discharge and a small purse of silver. The Free Captains, the [Unchained]{.name data-type="affiliation"} and the [Shoals Brotherhood]{.name data-type="affiliation"} keep loose networks of mutual aid among the dispossessed. A Taruzelian owes his debtor patience, which the coast reckons the harder discipline and the more profitable one.

The limit is the name. A person with no house, no guild and no one to stand surety (_rumukum_) has nothing a lender will read. The veteran bought off with a minor post and a small pension is the case the notes keep returning to.

## Those Who Cannot Work

A house carries its injured and its old as long as its name can afford it, and the old stand as elders (_kalurum_) in its counsels. The limit shows in the harbor districts. The veterans of the Shoals are growing old in tenements, and the Compact and the other great guilds regard them as a nuisance. A debtor in a bad year is owed patience.

## Shelter, Teaching and Defense

Shelter is the house's, and hospitality is its display. Teaching is the bench and the school: an apprenticeship under a master, the Vezakuni's examination, and the academies of [[place-bazunel2|Bazunel]], which turn out navigators, cartographers, physicians and natural philosophers. Defense is the navy, built from the flotilla that won the war and operated largely through the Corsair League, and the marines of Yamuzel, the nearest thing to a standing army. On land each city keeps its own militia. The hill tribes defend themselves, and in Kudumel they sit on the city council.

## What the Broadsheet Promised

Every real culture has a blind spot its own thoughtful members argue about, and the Taruzelians' is the promise made to people who were not at the table. A people who hold that a bargain not honored is theft raised a broken chain and a rising sun in a broadsheet and spoke to the dock workers, the fishermen and the hill tribes in the words of freedom. The merchant princes had not lied exactly. They had meant their own freedom: to trade without an emperor's interference, to set their own tariffs. The common people heard something different in the same word. Thoughtful Taruzelians dispute whether a promise made in a broadsheet to the people of Taruzel is a bargain at all, and if it is not, what a people who live by the bargain owed the ones who fought.

The sin is the betrayal that followed. Within two years of independence the Confederation was an oligarchy in all but name, and the men who had financed the war wrote its charter. The Compact called in its debts, the Corsair League was turned from a brotherhood of veterans into a shipping cartel, and the veterans were accused of debts they did not owe and crimes they did not commit, bought off with small posts, tried in guild-friendly courts or declared outlaws. The hill tribes, who had sent their warriors to die for the port cities, received nothing and were worse off than before. The veterans keep the broadsheet on the wall of the hall and read it as a promise the guilds made and did not keep. The coast treats the settlement of a debt as the basis of the trade, and the debt of the Shoals is unsettled.
