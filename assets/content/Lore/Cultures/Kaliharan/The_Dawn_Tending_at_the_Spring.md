**The Dawn Tending at the Spring** is the [Dawn Tending]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} of a spring-stone, as a kaan-vaar of [[place-mirthaan|Mirthaan]] says it over the stone of a terrace village of the lower valley, in the hour before sunrise, every morning in every weather. A [[lore-sarisul|tending-stone]] beside a spring is a smooth swelling of gray [[lore-itharisul|ithasul]] out of the rock above the place the water rises, and the sequence walks round it to seven named places in a fixed order, from the face back to the face, and touches the stone at most of them. Every kind of stone in a valley has its own sequence, held word for word. This one is the sequence of the spring-stones of the lower valley, which share it.

The words are in the old form of the tongue, and the custodians hold the sense of most of them and not all. Where they hold the sense, the rendering gives it. Where nobody holds it, the rendering keeps the place in the sequence and says what is known of the words: how many, how they are said, and what the custodians hold about saying them. The village hears the sequence through its walls every morning and its children know it by heart before they are given their first account; nobody but the kaan-vaar says it aloud, and nobody joins in. The second part is the close of the [Rite of Last Tending]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, said once over a stone that has gone white, at the end of the extended tending the senior custodians perform. A village hears it once, if it hears it at all.

The lines on the water divide the custodians. They are said as they are held, whatever the water is doing, so a kaan-vaar says that the water runs clear on a morning it runs brown. Most custodians hold that the lines are the sequence and are said as the sequence is said. [[affiliation-silharicircle|The Sílhari Circle]] holds that they were once said of the water as it was, and that a line said of the water was once a line looked at. The sanctuaries say the lines as they are held.

: The Dawn Tending at the Spring {#dawn-tending}

::: {.verse form=tending lang=en}
[The kaan-vaar comes up the path in the dark, with nothing in the hands, and stops below the stone, facing it, with the water on the left hand.]{.direction}/
[The kaan-vaar:]{.speaker}/
I come to the stone at the hour./
I come with nothing in the hands./
I come as the one before me came.

[Two words. Their sense is not held. They are said low, and the second is said twice.]{.direction}

The water is at the lip./
The lip is clear./
The fern at the lip is green.

[The kaan-vaar walks to the right, with the stone on the left hand, eleven paces, to the shoulder of the stone, and lays the right hand flat on it, the fingers toward the water.]{.direction}/
This is the shoulder./
It is gray./
It gives under the hand./
It is warm as the hand, and not warmer.

[Four words. Their sense is not held. They are said in one breath.]{.direction}

The hand is held while the water is counted./
[The kaan-vaar counts the falls of the water from the lip, aloud, to nine, and lifts the hand at nine.]{.direction}/
Nine, and the hand is lifted./
Nine, and not eight./
Nine, and not ten.

[The kaan-vaar walks on, the stone on the left hand, to the back of the stone, where the rock it rises from meets the hill, and lays both hands on it, one above the other, the left above.]{.direction}/
This is the back./
It is gray./
It gives under the hands./
Nothing grows on it./
Nothing has grown on it./
Nothing is to grow on it.

[A line of the old words. The custodians hold its sense as: what is behind is kept. They hold that it is said to the hill, and not to the stone.]{.direction}

What is behind is kept./
What is behind is kept./
What is behind is kept.

[The kaan-vaar walks on, the stone on the left hand, to the place under the fern, on the far side of the stone from the path, and kneels, and lays the left hand on the stone and the right hand on the ground.]{.direction}/
This is the place under the fern./
The stone is gray where it meets the ground./
The ground is the ground./
Between them nothing.

[Six words. Their sense is not held. They are said kneeling, with the head down, and the fourth word is said loud.]{.direction}

[The kaan-vaar rises and walks on, the stone on the left hand, to the foot of the stone, where the water comes out from under it, and puts the right hand in the water to the wrist, and the left hand on the stone above.]{.direction}/
This is the foot./
The water comes out from under./
It comes out at the foot and not at the side./
It comes out cold./
It comes out clear./
It runs./
It runs to the first terrace and the second and the third,/
and to the walls of all of them,/
and it is the water of the house of every wall it runs to.

[The kaan-vaar takes the hand out of the water and does not dry it.]{.direction}

The wet hand to the stone./
[The wet right hand is laid on the stone at the foot, beside the left.]{.direction}/
It is gray under the water./
It gives under the water./
It takes no mark of the water./
It takes no mark.

[A line of the old words. The custodians hold its sense as: the one who comes after. They hold that it is said to the next kaan-vaar, who is not yet born, and that it has been said to every kaan-vaar of this stone before that one was born.]{.direction}

The one who comes after./
The one who comes after will come at the hour./
The one who comes after will come with nothing in the hands.

[The kaan-vaar walks on, the stone on the left hand, to the lip, where the water falls, and lays the forehead against the stone, and no hand.]{.direction}/
This is the lip./
It is gray./
It gives under the brow./
The water falls from it./
It falls nine times while the brow is held./
[Silence for the nine falls. Nothing is counted aloud.]{.direction}

[Three words. Their sense is not held. They are said with the brow on the stone, so that they are said into it, and they are the only words of the sequence the village cannot hear.]{.direction}

[The kaan-vaar walks on, the stone on the left hand, to the face of the stone, where the path comes up, and stands below it as at the beginning, with the water on the left hand. The round is closed at the seventh place.]{.direction}/
This is the face./
The round is walked./
The shoulder, the back, the place under the fern, the foot, the wet hand, the lip, the face./
It is gray./
It gives./
The water runs clear.

[Two words. Their sense is not held. They are the two words of the beginning, said the same, low, the second twice.]{.direction}

It is held./
It is held as it was held./
Tomorrow, at the hour.

[The kaan-vaar goes down the path. The sequence takes as long as the dark takes to go gray over the Ring. If the light comes before the face is reached, the sequence is not hurried.]{.direction}
:::

: The close of the Last Tending {#last-tending}

::: {.verse form=tending lang=en}
[At the end of the Rite of Last Tending, when the extended tending has been said, and the held stone taken if the stone has cracked and given one, the eldest of the custodians comes to the face of the white stone and stands below it, facing it, with the water on the left hand, as at the beginning of every morning's round.]{.direction}/
[The eldest:]{.speaker}/
This is the face./
The round is walked./
The shoulder, the back, the place under the fern, the foot, the wet hand, the lip, the face./
It is white./
It does not give./
The water runs.

[The two words of the beginning are not said.]{.direction}

It was held./
It was held as it was held./
It is recorded at the mount in the lake./
There is no hour tomorrow.

[The custodians turn from the stone together, so that the stone is behind them.]{.direction}/
[The custodians:]{.speaker}/
What is behind is kept.

[The eldest:]{.speaker}/
Turn to what remains./
The spring remains. It is the house's./
The terraces remain. They are the houses'./
The walls remain. Every house rebuilds its own./
The looking remains. It is everyone's, and it needs no stone./
Turn to what remains, and look at it properly./
Tomorrow, at the hour, the spring.

[Nobody weeps at a Last Tending. The custodians go down the path, and the kaan-vaar of the stone goes with them and does not come up the path again in the dark.]{.direction}
:::
