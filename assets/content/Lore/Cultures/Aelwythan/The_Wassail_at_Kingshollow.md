**The Wassail at Kingshollow** is the carol the tenants of [[place-kingsholow|Kingshollow]] sing at the door of the royal hall on the night of the midwinter feast, in the years the king keeps the feast there. They come up from the village after dark with a turned ash bowl of ale and a horn cup, the old in front and the children behind, and they sing at the shut door until it is opened. The court hears it from the hall, the barons among them, and every baron at the feast knows what the fourth and fifth verses say.

The carol keeps a custom the village holds to more closely than to anything else in the year. The wood above Kingshollow is royal forest under forest law, and the tenants who live beside it may not cut its timber. At the end of the carol the king gives the singers one standing tree, which the forester has marked, and they fell it the next morning and burn it on the village hearths until it is gone. Whatever the tree is, the village calls it the [Wassail Oak]{.name data-type=place/feature}. In a year the king is held at [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]] the steward answers in his place and gives the same tree, and the village sings a verse it does not sing to the king.

The verses go in a fixed order: the coming, the bowl, the greeting, the gift, the asking, the land, and the verses sung over the wall to the royal graves behind the hall. The burden is sung by everyone at the door after each verse. The king's answer is not sung but said, and it is always the same words. The verses about the first king are the village's alone. The barons' record says that [Drethar of Kingshollow]{.name data-type=being/npc} was crowned in the chapel beside his hall ([[lore-kingshlwcrwn|The Crowning at Kingshollow]]) and says nothing about where he lies; the carol says he lies in none of the graves, and that the hill keeps him until the crown is asked for again.

The verses are rhymed in couplets.
