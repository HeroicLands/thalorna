**The Reeve's Threes** are the sayings a reeve of a manor in the [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Kingdom of Aldorath]] learns before he takes office. The reeve is a tenant of the manor, chosen by the other tenants for a year to answer to the lord's steward for the manor's dues, and since most of the tenants cannot read, the custom of the manor lives in his mouth. A new reeve learns the threes from the old one in the weeks before the manor court, and says them back to him at the court before the steward and the tenants, and from then on, when a custom is in dispute at the court, the reeve's three on the matter is the first thing heard.

Every manor has threes of its own, about its own bounds and dues, and those are not written here. These are the threes the manors of the eastern country share, as the reeves of the manors under [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]] say them. They are said plainly, one after another, with no verse to them; the plainness is held to be the point, and a reeve who dresses a three up is told to say it again.

Some threes are followed by a saying of the reeves that goes with them; these stand after the three they belong to. The threes about the folk who were on the island before the Aelwythans name the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale Folk]] of [[place-hallowwood|The Hallowwood]], the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]] of [[place-ironfells|The Ironfells]] and [[lore-vardain|The Vardain]] who farm beneath them, in the order the island's history puts them, which the reeves do not know is an order.

: The Holding {#holding}

Three things make a holding: a hearth that is kept, a hedge that is kept, and an heir that is kept.

Three things go with the land and not with the man: the strip, the service, and the debt.

Three things outlast a lord: the strip, the boundstone, and the saw.

Three ruins of a holding: an heir that drinketh, a hedge that falleth, and a quarrel that is kept warm.

Three things a reeve shall not take: a gift before the court, a beast that hath strayed, and the lord's part of the harvest.

: The Word {#word}

Three things cannot be called back: a word given, a blow struck, and a seed sown.

Three hear an oath sworn before Árdavon: the god, the priest, and the grandson.

_The reeves say after it:_ A word given is a stone laid in the wall; pluck it out, and the whole house falleth.

Three things a broken oath taketh from a man: the rites, the temple, and the grave.

Three things a man may say at the court and be believed: what he saw, what he paid, and what his father told him of the bounds.

: The Lord {#lord}

Three things make a good lord: an open door, a short memory for small wrongs, and a long memory for service.

Three things a lord oweth his tenant: bread in the bad year, a hearing at the court, and the wall of his hall when the wood cometh down.

Three wastes of a land: a king that is sick, a lord that is away, and a reeve that is asleep.

Three loud things that are hollow: a drum, a mantle bought with money, and a lord that boasteth of his age and keepeth not his tenants.

: The Crown {#crown}

Three things older than the crown: the barons, the roads, and the wood.

_The reeves say after it:_ The barons gave the crown; the roads were given to nobody; and the wood was never given at all.

Three things a king of Aldorath is given and doth not get: his crown, his midwinter ale, and his grave at Kingshollow.

: The Crossroads and the Dead {#crossroads}

Three gifts for the crossroads: bread, salt, and a civil word.

Three things not to be done after dark: to stand where the roads divide, to call the lake by name, and to count the standing stones.

Three things a dying man must settle while he hath breath: his debts, his heir, and his quarrel.

Three the priests of the Judge bury without a fee: the pauper, the stranger, and the one no man claimeth.

Three things the dead are owed: the rites of their house, their names at the Silent Passage, and their graves left where they lie.

: Those That Were Here Before {#before}

Three peoples were on the island before us, and are on it yet: the folk of the wood, the folk under the fells, and the folk of the valleys that feed them.

_The reeves say after it:_ We came last, and we have the most of it, and no charter saith how.

Three works of the old time that no man of ours can make: the stone of the crown roads, the oaks beside them, and the footings under the towns.

Three places a reeve sendeth no man: into the Hallowwood, up to the hold in the fells, and across the bog in the dark.

: The Neighbours {#neighbours}

Three things money buyeth not in Aldorath: land, a hearing, and a good name. In Ondareth it buyeth all three, and a seat besides.

Three that see every ship: the gull, the fog, and Ravenmoor.

Three things no man asketh at Ravenmoor: what the Synod keepeth, what the Synod knoweth, and what was done in the Hushwood.

_The reeves say after it:_ Every realm weepeth for the Stormplain, and no realm goeth.

: The Reeve's Charge {#charge}

Three things a reeve must have by heart: the bounds, the dues, and the dead that held each strip.

Three witnesses of a bound: the stone, the oldest man, and the boy that was beaten at it.

Three things a reeve saith at the end of his year: I kept the bounds, I kept the dues, I kept my word; and if one of the three be false, let the next reeve say so before the court.
