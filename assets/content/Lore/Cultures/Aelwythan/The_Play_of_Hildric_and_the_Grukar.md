The Play of Hildrik and the Grukar belongs to the drovers. They bring the beasts of the manorial country down the drove roads to [[place-ashford|Ashford]] for the autumn fair, the one occasion on which a serf may lawfully stand a long way from his manor, and on the nights of the fair they put on the play in the inn-yards for whatever the yard will throw into the bowl. The money buys the drovers' ale. The players are men from the manors of [[place-wyndmarch|the Wyndmarch]] and the forest edge, and a part passes from a man to his son or his nephew. They wear their working clothes with strips of fleece sewn over them, and the Grukar wears a fleece turned inside out with his face blacked.

The cast is fixed. [Old Tally]{.name data-type="being/npc"} opens the play and closes it, keeps the ring clear with a besom, and carries the notched stick a drover keeps his count of beasts on, which gives him his name. [Hildrik]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is the champion of the march. The Grukar calls himself an Ahk, which is a Grukar chief's title and the only Grukar word most Aldorathi know. The Doctor comes from [[place-ravenmoor|Ravenmoor]] and speaks the court register of the [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri Language]], and his one line of it is the greeting the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Synod]]'s harbor officers give every ship from the mainland. Nobody in an Ashford inn-yard knows what it means, and the Doctor uses it as a charm. The ale-wife is played by the oldest man in the company.

The Grukar's boast about the twelve kings is the line every yard waits for. [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]] has mounted a formal campaign against the Grukar about a dozen times, and each was declared a success ([[lore-grukaraelwyth|The Grukar of Aelwyth]]). The Doctor's cure is stopped because the priests of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] forbid bringing back the dead, and the drovers keep the scene so that the ale-wife can settle it. The second Grukar comes in at the end of every performance, however late it is.

: The Play {#play}

_Old Tally comes in sweeping a ring with his besom, his tally-stick in his belt._

**Old Tally.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Room, room, good people, give us room to play!
We are come down out of the march today;
We drove the beasts, we paid the bridge his toll,
And now we'll play, an ye will fill the bowl.
This is the fair a man may lawful stray
Far from his manor, and none say him nay;
So stand ye back, and ye shall see one bold
As ever drove a wether to the fold.
I am Old Tally, that do keep the score,
A notch for every beast, and one notch more:
Step in, thou Hildrik, let thyself be seen,
And tell the yard what thou art, and hast been.
```

_Hildrik comes in with a wooden sword and a shield of hide._

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Here come I, Hildrik, of the Wyndmarch bred,
That stand betwixt the forest and your bread.
Where'er the forest meeteth with the field,
There have I stood, with this my bill and shield;
Where'er the steadings burned, there I have stood,
And never yet went backward from the wood.
Show me the beast that dare to bid me stand,
And I will hew him down with this right hand.
```

**Old Tally.** Here's one out of the trees, then. Mind your purses.

_The Grukar comes in from the back of the yard, through the crowd, growling._

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Here come I, out of the forest dun,
An Ahk of the Grukar, and I fear none.
Rats in thy granary, so thou dost say?
Why, then I am the rat that came to stay.
My sons are twenty, and my daughters more,
And every one shall scratch upon thy door.
```

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
I've slain of thine a hundred in a year.
```

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
And I have bred two hundred, never fear.
```

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
I'll burn thy den, and drive thee to the fells.
```

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
The fells are mine, and all the moor that swells
Beyond them, and the bog that hath no floor,
And every wood thy plough hath passed before.
```

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
The king shall come against thee with his host.
```

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Thy king hath come, and made a mighty boast,
And come against me twelve times o'er and o'er,
With drum and banner, horse and foot and more;
Twelve times he won, his chronicles do say,
And twelve times I was here the following day.
```

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Then I will do what twelve kings could not do.
```

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Come on, then, champion, and I'll run thee through.
```

_They fight, three passes up and down the ring, the yard shouting for Hildrik. On the third the Grukar strikes him down._

**Old Tally.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Alack, alack, our champion is down!
Is there a doctor to be had in town?
Ten Bits to any doctor that can cure
This man of hurt, and make his footing sure.
```

**The Doctor.** _Outside the ring._ Ten Bits? I come not out for ten Bits.

**Old Tally.** Twenty, then.

**The Doctor.** For twenty Bits I will put my head in. _He comes in, in a long black gown and a hat, carrying a bottle._

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Here come I, the Doctor, that was bred
At Ravenmoor, where all the books are read;
I have my learning in a tongue so fine
Ye could not con a word of it, nor line.
I cure the gout, the ague and the stone,
The pip, the palsy, and the aching bone;
I set a leg, I let a vein, I draw
A tooth as easy as a wisp of straw.
```

**Old Tally.** What is thy fee, Doctor?

**The Doctor.** One Solden.

**Old Tally.** An Solden! There is not an Solden in this yard, nor in this town, saving the bridge's. Here is twenty Bits and a turnip.

**The Doctor.** I will take the turnip in part of payment. Stand back, now, for here is physic. _He kneels by Hildrik and speaks over him, very grand:_ "_Shalor en kir a thel!_"

**Old Tally.** What's that, Doctor?

**The Doctor.** That is the court tongue, and it is a charm against all hurts whatsoever, and it signifieth, in thy plain speech, "Pay the doctor." _He pours from the bottle into Hildrik's mouth._ Hildrik, arise.

_Hildrik does not move._

**The Doctor.** This is an obstinate disease. I will try the stronger physic. I have a drop here that will raise the dead—

**Old Tally.** _Seizing his arm._ Hold, hold! Raise the dead? Wilt thou have the Silent Judge on the whole yard? There's no man here will stand beside thee when the black priests come asking. Put it up, Doctor, put it up.

**The Doctor.** Then I cannot help him, for he is dead, and my fee is the same. _He goes out with the turnip._

_The ale-wife comes in, with a jug and a cup._

**The Ale-wife.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Dead? He's not dead. I've known him forty year;
He's never dead, he only lacketh beer.
The Judge hath not him yet, nor shall tonight:
Get up, thou lubber, here's thy cup. Drink right.
```

_She gives him the cup. Hildrik sits up, drinks, and stands._

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Good morrow, gentles. Is the Grukar fled?
I dreamt I lay a long while with the dead,
And one stood at a gate, and would not speak,
And looked on me, and turned away his cheek.
```

**The Ale-wife.** That's the Judge, and he'll not have thee till thy day. Up and at it.

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Then come again, thou Ahk, and try thy luck.
```

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
I'll try it, and thou'lt lie where first thou struck.
```

_They fight again. Hildrik strikes the Grukar down, and the yard cheers._

**Old Tally.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Huzza, the Grukar's dead! The march is free!
Go home, ye drovers, all is well with ye.
```

_The Grukar's fleece is pulled off him. Under it he wears a second fleece, also turned, and he gets up._

**The Grukar.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Here come I, that am the Grukar's son,
And there's a brother after me to come.
```

**Old Tally.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Why, then, we leave them fighting as they were,
And as they have been, and they will be there.
```

**Hildrik.**

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
I know my day. One morning I shall go
Into the wood, and not come out, I know;
The steward in his book shall write a line,
And give my strip unto that son of mine.
But that's not now, and this is not the wood:
Fill up the bowl, ye gentles, an ye would.
```

**Old Tally.** _Going round the yard with the bowl._

```poetry {form=mummers-play rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Put in your Bits, and put your Argos in,
And if ye've Soldeni, that were no sin;
A notch for every coin, and one notch more,
And the Twelve keep you till we play once more.
```

_The players go out singing, the Grukar and the Grukar's son behind them, still fighting with Hildrik through the gate of the yard._
