**The Song of the Gold Band** is the road-song of the **Karzen Ethlos Urzelthos**, the legion of the northern march that took the Felzen Hiltheren's eagle out of the reeds below the Moktur passes ([[lore-blackmarshbtl|The Battle of the Black Marsh]]) and carries a gold band on its name for it. A road-song, _pozersorima_, is sung on the march to the step. One man calls the line, by custom the oldest Kamezos of the leading century, and the century answers with the half-line; the next century takes it up when the first runs out of breath. Every legion has one, and every legion steals verses from the others and denies it. The song's hero is **Gretalis**, the Kamezos who went into the water for the eagle when it was quiet, and the song gives him no house name, as soldiers give each other none.

The song has two halves, and everyone in the legion knows where the join is. The **parade verses** are the ones the legion's Holtakezos allows on the Velzorpozera and in front of the governor at Tirellan: the valley, the water, the eagle, the band, and the four words of the manual at the end. The **road verses** are everything the centuries have added on the long stretch between [[place-dolvar|Dolvar]] and the towers, and nobody has ever written them down by order. Two of the road verses are the ones the noble officers do not hear. One is for the Felzen Hiltheren, whose eagle came home in the same year its name was struck from the roll. The other is for the Twelfth, whose eagle did not come home at all ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]), and the centuries sing it last and quietly, on the road west, when the Kervaron is in sight. A Ethorlusos learns in his first month on the march to find something to look at on the far side of the road while they sing it.

The recruits learn the song before they learn the oath. The text below is the song as the first cohort sings it on the road, the call set flush and the answer set in.

: The Song of the Gold Band {#song}

```poetry {form=road-song lang=en}
Left foot the road, and the right foot the road,
  and the road goes north, and the road goes north.
Who carries the eagle? The Urzelthos carries.
  Beneath the eagle stand the borders.
Who carries the gold band? The Urzelthos carries.
  Gold on the name and mud on the boot.

Count the smoke at dusk on the wall, recruit,
  one is a hearth and three is a camp.
Count the smoke at dusk on the wall, recruit,
  ten is a war-band over the passes.
In the spring of the hunger the smoke was a forest,
  and nobody slept on the wall at Dolvar.

The forest came down with an eagle in front of it,
  an eagle that was not the forest's eagle.
Whose was the eagle? The Fourth's was the eagle,
  the Felzen Hiltheren's, two years in the trees.
Gilt on the pole and the pole in a Varokh fist,
  and the Fourth on the roll and nobody in it.

Up went the Urzelthos, up to the ridges,
  up in the night with the shields on our backs.
We closed the valley above them, as a lid on a pot,
  and we held the ridges, and we did not go down.
Who goes down to the forest's host in the open?
  Nobody goes down. The Urzelthos holds.

They came up at us once, and the hill was against them.
  They came up twice, and the hill was against them.
They turned to the valley's foot where the ground fell away,
  and the ground that fell away was the water.
Black water, reeds to the shoulder, mud to the waist,
  and a man in mail goes in to the waist.

We did not go in. We did not have to go in.
  The water fought for the Urzelthos that day.
We stood on the hill and we saw it. We saw it.
  We do not sing what we saw.
We heard it all night from the hill. We heard it.
  We do not sing what we heard. We sing the band.

Who went in for the eagle when the water was quiet?
  Gretalis went in, old Gretalis, the Kamezos.
Gretalis went in to the knee, and the water was cold.
  Gretalis went in to the waist, and the water was full.
Gretalis came out with the eagle up over his head,
  and the gilt was green, and the pole was whole.

The Orzas heard it, the old one, the winter-king,
  the king of the three winters, in the city in the south.
He sent us a band of gold for the name on the roll,
  and the Urzelthos carries it, and the Urzelthos will.
Karzen Ethlos, third of the gilded,
  Urzelthos, crowned, with the gold on the name.

Left foot the road, and the right foot the road,
  and the road goes south, and the road goes home.
Who carries the eagle? The Urzelthos carries.
  Beneath the eagle stand the borders.
Zel orkaima thalentor uthrime!
  Zel orkaima thalentor uthrime!
```

The parade ends there, on the four words of the manual, shouted twice: _Zel orkaima thalentor uthrime_, "beneath the eagle stand the borders." On the road the Kamezos does not stop.

: The Road Verses {#road}

```poetry {form=road-song lang=en}
Beneath the eagle stand the borders,
  and beneath the borders, us.
Beneath the borders the Urzelthos,
  and beneath the Urzelthos, the mud.

Who walks at the head of the century? The Takezos.
  Who paid for his post? Nobody paid.
Who rides at the head of the legion? The Thramezusos.
  Who paid for his horse? His father paid.
His father paid for the horse and the cloak and the post,
  and the Holtakezos tells him which way is north.

The Ethorlusos asked the Holtakezos the road to the marsh,
  and the Holtakezos said: we do not look at the marsh.
The Ethorlusos asked again, being seventeen,
  and the Holtakezos showed him the forest, and counted the smoke.

Fifteen years old when I came to the eagle,
  sixteen when I learned what the oath was for,
seventeen, eighteen, nineteen, twenty in Moktur,
  rain in the summer and rain in the snow.
Twenty years under the eagle, and then the door,
  one door, and the army holds it open.
Thirty-five, and a citizen, and the dole in the city,
  and a wife with the right to the dole for her life,
and a room in the Warrens up ninety stairs,
  and nothing to count from the window but roofs.

Old Gretalis went into the city with the band on his shoulder,
  old Gretalis went in at the one door.
They gave him his bread and they gave him his room in the Warrens,
  and he sat in the window and counted the smoke.

Where is the Fourth's eagle? In the shrine at Tirellan.
  Who carries it now? Nobody carries.
The eagle came home, and the roll had no name for it.
  The eagle came home, and nobody was in.
Gilt on the wall, and the candles in front of it,
  and the Fourth on no roll, and the Fourth in no camp.
Who sings for the Fourth? The Urzelthos sings,
  because we were the ones who brought it home.

Left foot the road, and the right foot the road,
  and the road goes west, and the mountains are white.
Who carries the Twelfth's eagle? Nobody carries.
  The snow carries it. The mountain does.
Who went up for the Twelfth's eagle? Nobody went.
  Nobody went up when the water was quiet.
Whose gold band is under the snow in the Kervaron?
  Nobody's band. Nobody's name.
Sing it low, Urzelthos, the mountain is listening.
  Sing it low. Beneath the eagle stand the borders.
```
