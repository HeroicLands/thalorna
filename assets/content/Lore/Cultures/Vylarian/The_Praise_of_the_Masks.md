**The Praise of the Masks** is the oration **Teremas Kolsamen** spoke from the speakers' stone in the square of [[place-aravantia|Aravantara]] over **Dorandas Kolsamen**, the last master of the house by blood and the master who adopted him. The house is one of the old landed families of [[place-hylen|Hilen]], named for its estate in the way of the provincial houses, and it keeps a townhouse in Aravantara as they all do. A funeral of a house with office behind it comes into the town from the estate with the bier carried at the front and the masks of the line walking behind it, each worn by a man of the right height and build, and stops in the square for the oration before it goes out by the road to the tombs. The masks stand behind the speaker while he speaks, in the order of the cupboard, and a laudation praises them one by one before it reaches the dead man, so that the crowd hears the whole line and then the last of it.

The house's freedman scribe took the oration down as it was spoken, and the house keeps the copy. The rhetoric masters of Hilen copy it for their pupils as the model of the provincial laudation, and they teach two things from it: the catalogue of the masks, which is in the suspended manner of the Senate, and the turn in the middle, where the speaker stops praising and speaks of the place in the cupboard that has no mask. An adopted heir takes the house's name and its hallowed ending, and Teremas was born Teremis; the masters teach the close of the oration, where he says so, as the one place a laudation may speak of the speaker.

The house has two masks from [[lore-olcassabtl|the Battle of Olkassa]], and they walk side by side. The custom is the house's own and older than the oration: the two brothers who died on that field, one in the Senate's army and one in the Moktur legions, are worn by two brothers when the house has them, and neither mask is ever set before the other in the procession or the cupboard.

## The Oration

Men of Aravantara, citizens and residents both, for the dead man would have wished both named and in that order; masters of the houses of the province, who have come in from your estates in the week before the harvest when no master of Hilen leaves his fields for anything less than this; priests of the Silent Judge, who sat with him at the last and heard his testimony and keep it; and you, my father who begot me, who stand today among the masks of another house and whom I may not call father in this square: hear me praise the house of Kolsamen, the masks of it first and the dead man last, as the law of a laudation sets them.

### The Founder

The first mask behind me is the founder of the line. He was a farmer of the Republic's time who held land on the river in the years when the city on the hills was smaller than Aravantara is now, and when the city was hungry in a lean year and the Senate asked the farms of the river to send what they could, he sent everything, his seed-corn with the rest, and walked behind the last cart to the city himself to see it weighed. The Senate gave him the land he farmed as his own and the right to a seat on the river council for his heirs. He planted the estate with the seed he bought back the next spring at the price of everything else he owned. We take our name from the estate and not from him, because he asked it: he said the land would be remembered longer than the man, and a name on land is a name with a roof. He held no office but the council, and the mask stands at the head of the row because he is the founder, and the founder is hallowed. Remember how he walked behind the cart.

### The Magistrate

The second mask is the great-grandson of the founder, who was the first of the house to hold a magistracy in Aravantara. He held it in a year of short measure, when the weights at the grain yard were filed light and every factor in the province knew it and nobody said it. He had the weights brought out into this square, here, on this stone where I stand, and tested against the standard in front of everyone, and three of them were light. He broke the three with a hammer in the square, and named the men who had owned them, and two of the three were his own clients. One of the two never came to his door again. He said, when his wife asked him why he had named his own, that a weight is honest when it matches the standard and there is no third thing a weight can be. The three broken weights are in the house's cupboard beside his mask. Remember how he broke them in front of his clients.

### The Brothers

The third and fourth masks walk together, and I will praise them together, because the house has never praised one without the other.

They were brothers, sons of the house in the years of the three eagles, and when the Senate's army came south to hold the granaries of this province, the elder was a Lusos in it, by the commission his father had bought him, and when the legions of Moktur came south to take the granaries, the younger was among them, because he had gone north to buy a commission where commissions were cheaper and had served six years in the Moktur forts. On the Olkassa estate, which you all know, whose wheat is the best in the province and whose grain you all buy and none of you asks about, the brothers stood in the two lines under the same eagle, cast in the same arsenal. Both lines gave no quarter. Both brothers lay in the standing grain at evening. The house does not know which of them died first, and it has never asked the priests to find out.

Their father had their masks made together, and walked them behind one bier, and he made the rule the house keeps: that neither is set before the other, in the procession or the cupboard or the praise, because neither was wrong to stand where his oath had put him, and both were wrong to be standing there. The province has a saying from that field, that no Vylarian should draw a sword on another, and the masters of Hilen say it in the councils and mean it. This house says it differently. This house says that two Vylarians drew swords on each other in the wheat, and here they are, side by side, and they are ours. Remember them side by side.

### The Mother of the House

The fifth mask is a woman, and you know her, because she is the only woman's mask in a cupboard of this province, and the province has argued about her for a century. She was the wife of the master of the house when he was taken by a fever in the year his son was six, and she held the house in her hand until the boy was of age. The law of the province gave the boy to a guardian, his father's brother, who lived in the capital and had debts. She went to the capital and sat in the morning call of the guardian's own patron for a year, every morning, placed by his steward on the lowest step with the petitioners from the tenements, until the patron asked who she was, and she told him, and he had the guardianship given to her by the courts. She kept the estate whole and handed it to her son at fifteen without a field less. The Senate gave her the right of a mask for it, which it almost never gives a widow for her patience. Remember how she sat on the lowest step for a year.

### The Place That Has No Mask

And now, men of Aravantara, I come to the place in the cupboard where a mask should be and is not.

You see the masks behind me. You count them, as you count at every funeral: the founder, the magistrate, the two brothers, the mother of the house, and behind them the masters of the house in their order, fourteen in all, every one of them a man or a woman who held an office or earned the right, and every one with a man of the right build beneath it. You see the dead man on the bier. And you know, because the whole province knows, that there is a man missing from between them.

He was **Kiramas Kolsamen**, the son of the dead man, his only son. He held no office. He was twenty-four. He bought the commission of a junior officer of marines at Kastamar, because he wanted the sea and not the forts, and he went south with the squadron that was sent to the Shoals of Tamzir, and he did not come back, and nothing came back that could be carried, and there is no mask of him because a mask is the face of a man who held an office and he held none. There is no tomb of him on the road, because there was nothing to put in a tomb. The priests of the Silent Judge said his name in the windows at the Silent Passage that year with the other names, and his father stood in the street below the window and heard it, and went home, and fed the kept fire, and sat down beside it, and the house was ended.

That is what I have to say of Kiramas. I have said it in the middle of the praise, where the law of a laudation does not put it, because he is in the middle, between his father and the ones before him, and there is nothing there. Remember that there is nothing there.

### The Dead Man

Now the last mask, which was made in this house two days ago and is not yet in the cupboard, and the man it was made from, who lies on the bier before you.

Dorandas Kolsamen held every office the province gives a man of his standing, and held each for its year and gave it up at the end of its year, and you know the list, because you elected him to most of it. I will not read you the list. You have it cut on the stone of the council house, and the stone says it better than I can.

I will tell you instead three things he did that are not on the stone.

He kept the last sheaf. You all leave a sheaf standing in the field for the dead, because your fathers did; he left it standing in every field on the estate, not one field, every field, every year, and when his factor told him what the sheaves were worth he said that the dead of this province had not been paid for their wheat either.

He never filed a weight. He told me once that the magistrate behind me was his teacher, though the man had been dead for two hundred years, and that the three broken weights in the cupboard were the only lesson he ever needed.

And when his son did not come back from the Shoals, he did not go to the Senate and ask for anything, though he had the standing to ask and the house had given the Senate a son. He went instead across the boundary stone between his estate and my father's, on foot, in the grey gown, and he stood at my father's door at the hour of the morning call, which no master of a noble house does at the door of a family of the horse order, and he asked my father for his second son. My father gave me. You know the law of it: a man adopted goes out of his father's hand into another's, and out of his house into another's, and takes the other's name and the other's ancestors and the other's dead, and leaves his own on the road. My father gave me in the doorway, with his steward watching and my mother behind him. I went across the stone with the old man on foot. I have not crossed back as a son since.

When the priests of the Silent Judge sat with him at the last and asked him for his testimony, he confessed one wrong, and he gave me leave to say it here. He said that he had asked another man for his son because his own was gone, and that he had never once in the twelve years since been able to see me cross the yard without wishing for the space of a breath that I was the other one. He said it was the only time in his life he had wished for a thing that was not his, and he asked the Judge to weigh it. Then he asked for the kept fire to be fed, and it was fed, and he went to the ancestors.

### The Name

Men of Aravantara, I was born Teremis, of the family across the stone, a family of the horse order that has farmed beside this house for two hundred years, with a common ending that any farmer's son may carry, and my mother called me by my calling name in the kitchen until I was fourteen years old. I have not heard it since, and I will not hear it again, because it is the name of a child of another house. When the old man took me into his hand, the house gave me its hallowed ending, and I am Teremas, and I declined my own name in the endings of a founder for the first time standing in his yard, and I did it badly, and he corrected me.

That is the whole of what I gave. It is less than the founder gave, who sent the seed-corn, and less than the mother of the house gave, who sat on the lowest step for a year, and less than the brothers gave, who gave each other. But it was mine, and I gave it to keep this cupboard from being closed and these faces from being burned with the last of the house, as the faces of a house are burned when no one is left to wear them. I will put his mask at the end of the row tonight, after the tomb, and the row will be fifteen, and the space between him and the others will still be there, and I will not fill it.

Masks of the house of Kolsamen, founder at the head of you and the master you have taken in at the foot: walk him out by the road. He walked behind the cart to the city to see the grain weighed. Walk behind his cart to the tombs, and see it weighed.
