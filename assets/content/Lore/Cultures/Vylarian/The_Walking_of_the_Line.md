**The Walking of the Line** is the sixth book of the **Thîrûthrimâ**, _The Hallowed Boundary_, the long poem **Mêlvaren Thûlêmênôs** made for the house of Makândas in the years the house rebuilt [[place-orzara|Ôrzâra]] ([[lore-restorehouse|The Restoration]]). Mêlvaren read it aloud book by book in the halls of his patron's friends, as a court poet does, and the copies went out from there. The sixth book tells the morning the first Ôrzâs walked and consecrated the thîrûthrimâ around the [[place-sixhillsvyl|Six Hills]] ([[lore-eaglecityrse|The Raising of the Eagle City]]). It is in the long line of the imperial epic, six beats falling at the end, and the grammar schools set it before any other book of the poem, so that a boy of good family knows its catalogue of the hills before he has climbed all six.

The poem does not name the Founder. It calls him the Senator until the line closes and the Walker of the Line after, and it declines him in the hallowed endings from its first line to its last, so that in the old tongue the Founder is never "he" at all. The rendering gives him a pronoun the poem withholds. Mêlvaren says in the proem why he gives no name: the record gives none ([[lore-thefounding|The Founding]]), and he will not write what the record keeps back. The book then gives a reason of its own for the smoothed patch on the Founder's plinth, and the schools teach it as the poem's reason and not the record's. The clerks of the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], asked whose name the patch was meant to hold, still refer the question to the record.

The book was written after [[lore-greatburnng|the Great Burning]], and the prophecy [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] gives in it knows what its poet knew. A boy who recites it learns [[lore-vylariages|the ages of the Empire]] in the god's mouth, in the order the historians teach them, with the six days of fire and the three eagles in their places, and the prophecy ends with the restorer coming down from the north. The masters who teach the book point out what it does with that praise. It sets the house that cut its name along every colonnade of the rebuilt city beside a founder who left his own stone blank, and it says nothing about the comparison.

Three lines of the stern face's speech are in no copy made before the Core: the eagle carried into the forest and brought out of the reeds ([[lore-blackmarshbtl|The Battle of the Black Marsh]]), the eagle that stays under the snow ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]), and the provinces that stop obeying one at a time. The masters mark the three as later, and the boys recite them anyway, because the boys learn the book from the masters' copies.

The poem opens on the two words every schoolboy can say: _Thîrûthrimân sôrimâmên_, "the hallowed boundary I sing."

: The Walking of the Line {#line}

```poetry {form=epic lang=en}
The hallowed boundary I sing, and the one who walked it, and the stones
set down in the grass of the six hills before a wall stood on any of them.
Keeper of the open book, Mârendâs, who hold what was written and what was not,
open the page at that morning and read it to me, and I will say it after you.
And let me say first why I give no name. The record gives none. I give none.
Let the setters of the stones be sung, and the priests who carried the cords;
the one who walked in front of them belongs to the city, and I call that one by what was done.

It was spring, in the first years of the count, before the light, on the river flats,
and the Senator came down from the Old Hill with no cloak of office on,
in the plain grey gown a son puts on to go to the tomb of his father,
and behind the Senator the priests of the Keeper of the Gates with their cords,
and behind the priests the setters, and a sledge of white stones, and two oxen to draw it,
and on the flats, in their cohorts, under their eagles, the legions,
the legions that had carried the Senator up out of the Senate to the seat above it,
standing in the grey as a vineyard stands in its rows before the pickers come,
each stake in its place, each row the width of a cart, and no man speaking.

Then the Senator turned to the legions and spoke, and the eagles leaned to hear:
"Men of the eagles, you brought me here. You will not come where I am going.
Today I walk a line around the hills, and inside the line the city is the city's,
and no legion marches in it under arms, not mine, not any, not for any reason.
You will stand here on the flats, where the line will go, and you will be the first
to stand outside it. I have asked you for many things. This is the hardest.
Keep the line from outside it." And the legions said nothing, as legions say nothing,
and the eagle-bearers grounded the eagles' poles on the flats, and they stood.

As when a farmer in the hills of Hilen, the morning his eldest goes for a soldier,
walks the boy down to the boundary stone of the field and no further than the stone,
because the field is the farmer's and the road beyond it is the eagle's,
and stands at the stone with his hand on it and watches the boy down the road,
and does not call after him, because a call would turn him, and he must not turn,
so the Senator stood at the edge of the flats and did not look back at the legions,
and laid a hand on the first white stone, and the priests made fast the cord to it.

There at the first stone stood the Keeper of the Gates, as the priests had said,
for the stone of a line is a gate, and wherever there is a gate the Keeper is standing,
and the Keeper wore the mask of the two faces, the serene and the stern together,
and turned the serene face to the Senator first, and the serene face spoke:

"Walker of the line, I am the one who keeps the place where in becomes out.
Look through this gate with me, and I will show you what will come through it.
The roads. They will run out of this morning like the cord in the priests' hands,
to the sea, across the sea, to the mountains, through the mountains,
paved and drained and milestoned, and the milestones will count from this stone.
The year. The peoples who never see this river will count their years from this spring,
and the letters of far kingdoms will be dated by it, in their own ink,
long after the eagles have gone home from those kingdoms and been forgotten there.
The law. It will be written and argued, and a case will climb from court to court
up to the sovereign's own tribunal, from the far side of the world.
The conquests. The legions will go west and north and take the lowlands,
and east to the League of the weighers, and the weighers will weigh for you;
and the avenue you will build from the gate will be lined with the beaks of ships
and the shields of kings, three hundred years of them, a mile of them,
the whole road from the river to the gate paved on both sides with the world's defeats.
This is what will come through the gate. Now look at it with the other face."

And the Keeper turned the mask, and the stern face looked at the Senator, and it spoke:
"Walker of the line, I keep both sides of a gate. Look at what goes out.
An army will cross the sea, the best you will ever have, and it will win,
and its commander will be crowned by strangers, and not come home,
and the chancery will strike his name and not write it again, and the empty roll
will end at the quay. And at home the sovereign will die and name no one,
and three eagles will be carried before three thrones, cast in one arsenal,
and legions will kill legions in the wheat of Hilen and leave the wheat standing,
and the bread will not come, and the city will burn from the low wards up
over the Old Hill where you were born, six days, and the Old Hill will not rise.
An eagle will be carried north into the forest and come back out of the reeds.
An eagle will go up into the western mountains and stay there under the snow.
The far provinces will stop obeying one at a time, as old men stop hearing,
not all at once, a little every winter, until the maps are a courtesy.
This also will go out through the gate. I keep the gate. I do not keep the road."

Then the Senator asked the god the one thing, as the dying ask the physician:
"Will it end?" And the Keeper turned the mask a little, so that both faces showed,
and said: "I keep the place where in becomes out. I do not say how long the place stands.
But this I say, because it is in: a man will come down from the north to the burned city
and pay the legions that fought him, and forgive them, and rebuild the walls,
and his house will raise the city in the shape that stands, and cut its name on it,
and the line you walk today will be under that city still, and the line will hold.
The line will hold when the frontiers do not. That is what a line inside is for."

So the god said, and was the stone, and the cord was fast, and the oxen leaned.

Hear now the hills, as the setters set them, in the order the line went round.
First the River Hill above the landing, where the boats tie up, and the porters
climb with the sacks on their backs, a hill all stairs and the smell of fish,
and the setters set three stones on its shoulder, and the porters stopped to look.
Second the Hill of the Scholars, where the masters of the old schools of the Republic
argued in the porches already, and argued as the stones went down,
whether a line can make a city, or only show where a city already is.
Third the Laurel, green and new, with the bay trees on it and no houses yet,
where the families who would come later had not come, and the stones went down in grass.
Fourth the Coronal, the hill of the old houses, where the villas of the senators
stood each in its walled garden, and the old men came out to the gates of the gardens
in their gowns, and watched the Senator go by, and did not bow, and did not speak.
Fifth the Eagle Hill, the highest, empty, with nothing on its crown but wind,
and under the crown the galleries the Republic sealed, and nobody spoke of them,
and the line went round the foot of it, and the priests did not slacken the cord.
Sixth the Old Hill, last, where the Republic's city stood, and the Senate's hall,
and the Senator's own house, and the tombs. The line came last to the tombs.

For on the Old Hill's southern slope, above the road, stood the tombs of the old houses,
the Republic's dead on the Republic's hill, inside the round of the six,
and the law of the line is the oldest law: inside it the living, outside it the dead.
The priests stopped with the cord. The setters stopped with the sledge.
And the Senator looked at the tombs, and among them was the tomb of the Senator's house,
the house the Senator came from, with its door of bronze and its names cut over it,
where the father lay, and the father's father, and the founder of the house,
and the mother, who was carried there the winter before, and the stone still white.

Then the priests said: Walk the line inside the tombs, and leave the slope outside it.
And the Senator said: "The Old Hill is one of the six. A city with five hills is not this city.
If I leave out the hill of my fathers to keep my fathers in, I have made a line for my house.
Carry them out." And it was done, and the Senator did the first of it.
The Senator went in through the bronze door to the dark, and came out carrying
the bone-chest of the founder of the house, and walked it down the slope to the road,
outside the place where the line would go, and set it down on the road in the dust,
and went back up for the next, and the next, nine times, and last the mother.
As when the river comes up in the spring over the low farms of Hilen
and the farmer carries the seed-jars out of the cellar to the threshing-floor,
one jar and then one, and goes down again into the water to the knee, to the waist,
and does not stop to see how high the water is, because the seed is the year, so the Senator went
up and down the slope of the Old Hill in the grey gown, and nobody helped,
because nobody may carry another house's dead, and the Senator's house was the Senator.

Then the old houses came down the slope, seeing what was done, and did the same,
each house for its own, all day, and the road below was white with the chests of the dead,
and the wives of the old houses stood on the road by the chests and lamented,
and the Senator's wife stood there among them, whom the poem does not name either,
and she did not lament, but stood by the mother's chest with her hand on it.
And that is why the tombs of Ôrzâra stand along the roads outside the city,
from the meanest pauper's trench to the house of the highest line:
because on the morning of the line the Senator carried out the fathers first.
Remember it, sons of the houses, when you pour the wine at the roadside tombs.

Then the line was closed. The cord came round to the first stone on the river flats,
and the priests knotted it, and the Keeper of the Gates was not there,
or was the stone, and the setters set the last stone in the place of the first,
and the legions on the flats outside it had stood all day and had not sat down.
And the master of the setters came with his chisel to the gate-stone, as was the custom,
and asked the Senator what name to cut, the founder's name, for a founder is hallowed,
and the crowned mark was already cut on the stone, the mark that waits for a hallowed name.

And the Senator stood at the gate-stone and thought, as the Senator thought all day,
of the one thing a citizen wants, and the Senator wanted it, as any man wants it:
to be repeated. To have the city say the name. To be honored, which is to be said.
The Senator thought of the masks in the hall, and the founder's mask at the head of them,
and the son, who would set the Senator's mask at the end of the row in his turn,
and the son's sons, and the name said at every funeral of the house while the house stood.
And the Senator said to the master of the setters: "Cut the mark. Leave the name.
A city that says my name is my house, and my house is on the road with its dead.
Let the city say the city. Smooth the stone inside the mark, and leave it smooth."
And that is the stone the Senate carried up the hill, when the colossus was raised
in the Circle before the new gate, and set beneath the Founder's feet for a plinth,
and the mark is on it, and inside the mark the stone is smooth.

But that evening the mason smoothed it, and the Senator walked back in at the gate alone,
with no legion and no guard, in the grey gown, with the dust of the road on it,
the first sovereign of the city, the first who could not bring the legions home,
and the legions on the flats outside watched the Walker of the Line go in,
and raised the eagles, and shouted, once, as legions shout at a triumph,
and then turned, and went to their camp, because they were outside, and it was evening.

That is the walking of the line. This the god said; this the Founder did; this the mason left.
And I who sing it have seen the city that the man from the north rebuilt,
and his name on the colonnades, column after column, the restorer's name,
and the Founder's stone in the Circle with the crowned mark and the smooth stone in it,
and I have said what the god said and what the record says, and I have said no more.
The hallowed boundary I have sung. Keeper of the open book, close the page.
```
