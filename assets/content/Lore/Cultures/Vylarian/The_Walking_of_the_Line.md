The Walking of the Line is the sixth book of the [Thrima]{.name data-type="lore/literature"}, _The Hallowed Boundary_, the long poem [Melvaren Thulemenos]{.name data-type="being/npc"} made for the house of [Makandas]{.name data-type="being/npc"} in the years the house rebuilt [[place-orzara|Orzara]] ([[lore-restorehouse|The Restoration]]). Melvaren read it aloud book by book in the halls of his patron's friends, as a court poet does, and the copies went out from there. The sixth book tells the morning the first Orzas walked and consecrated the thrima around [[place-sixhillsvyl|the Six Hills]] ([[lore-eaglecityrse|The Raising of the Eagle City]]). It is in the long line of the imperial epic, six beats falling at the end, and the grammar schools set it before any other book of the poem, so that a boy of good family knows its catalogue of the hills before he has climbed all six.

The poem does not name the Founder. It calls him the Senator until the line closes and the Walker of the Line after, and it declines him in the hallowed endings from its first line to its last, so that in the old tongue the Founder is never "he" at all. The rendering gives him a pronoun the poem withholds. Melvaren says in the proem why he gives no name: the record gives none ([[lore-thefounding|The Founding]]), and he will not write what the record keeps back. The book then gives a reason of its own for the smoothed patch on the Founder's plinth, and the schools teach it as the poem's reason and not the record's. The clerks of the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]], asked whose name the patch was meant to hold, still refer the question to the record.

The book was written after [[lore-greatburnng|the Great Burning]], and the prophecy [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] gives in it knows what its poet knew. A boy who recites it learns [[lore-vylariages|the ages of the Empire]] in the god's mouth, in the order the historians teach them, with the six days of fire and the three eagles in their places, and the prophecy ends with the restorer coming down from the north. The masters who teach the book point out what it does with that praise. It sets the house that cut its name along every colonnade of the rebuilt city beside a founder who left his own stone blank, and it says nothing about the comparison.

Three lines of the stern face's speech are in no copy made before [The Core]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"}: the eagle carried into the forest and brought out of the reeds ([[lore-blackmarshbtl|The Battle of the Black Marsh]]), the eagle that stays under the snow ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]), and the provinces that stop obeying one at a time. The masters mark the three as later, and the boys recite them anyway, because the boys learn the book from the masters' copies.

The poem opens on the two words every schoolboy can say: [Thiruthriman sorimamen]{lang=vyl}, "the hallowed boundary I sing."
