The Second Reading is the tale the **Omelars** of the legions tell a chaplain on the night of his licensing. An Ozethos is licensed jointly by the legions and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] to read the signs for an army, in a bird or a flame, and his licensing ends at night in the camp shrine with the Omelars of the legion around the fire. The oldest of them tells the tale while the new one keeps the fire. It is set down here in the manner the Omelars use, which is the manner of the annals: the year, its signs, and then what was done, in that order, with no word given to what anyone felt, because the annals give none.

The tale is about [[lore-twelfthloss|the Loss of the Twelfth]], and its Ozethos is [Silkimar]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the chaplain of the [Doreken Hiltheren]{.name data-type="affiliation"}. The army's record names no chaplain of the Twelfth, and the Omelars hold that this proves nothing either way, since the record names no chaplain of any legion. The tale turns on one small word. A second reading ordered by a commander is given with _zikai_, "perhaps", set before its reason, which in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]] makes the reason something alleged and not vouched for, and the tale's whole matter is a man who did that and a man who did not hear it. The rendering marks the added word "so it is said."

## The Signs of the Year

In the year the council of the regency sent the Doreken Hiltheren into the [Kervaron Spine]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, these signs were reported to the chaplains of the western legions and entered in their books.

At the Twelfth's winter quarters, the kept fire of the legion's shrine was found out in the morning, though the watch swore it had been fed in the night. It was relit from the fire of the Vallar's house, as the rite requires, and the Ozethos entered it.

The pole of the Twelfth's eagle, which stands in the shrine on a stone socket, was found leaning toward the west. It was set upright. In the morning it was leaning west again. It was wedged, and the wedge held.

A recruit of the third cohort, a boy of fifteen not yet sworn, dreamed three nights running of snow in the summer, and reported it to his Kamezos, who reported it to his centurion, who reported it to the Ozethos, who entered it, because the Omelars hold that [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]], the Veiled Dreamer, sends the dreams of the young, and the Omelars are licensed to hear them.

In the spring the swallows came late to the eaves of the quarters, and fewer than in other years, and they built on the eastern side of the roofs and none on the western.

These signs were entered in the chaplains' books and sent to the council in the capital in the ordinary dispatch. The council did not send for the books, and the signs were not expiated, and the council's letter came in the same month ordering the Twelfth to march in the thaw.

## The Legion Marches

The Twelfth marched in the thaw. The chancery was not asked. The legion's Thramezusos was a man of an old house whom the council had chosen and whose father had paid for the post, and the tale does not give his name, because the Omelars do not say it on the night of a licensing. The Twelfth went west through the foothills for nineteen days, and at the foot of the passes it built a fort for its rear cohorts, as the manual requires, with a ditch and a rampart and a gate and a shrine. The boundary stones the western wars had set stood on the slope above the fort, and the clans of the range had hung them with strips of cloth.

On the evening the fort's gate was hung, the Thramezusos called the Ozethos to read the flame for the climb.

## The First Reading

Silkimar built the fire in the fort's shrine as the rite prescribes, of dry wood and laurel on the bare stone, before the eagle, with the Thramezusos standing on the eagle's right and the Holtakezos on its left, and the Lusari behind. He lit it from the kept fire the legion carried with it in its clay pot. He fed it three times. A flame read for a march is read for its lean: a flame that leans the way the army means to go is read as the gods walking ahead of it, and a flame that leans back toward the army is read as the gods standing in its road.

The flame leaned east, back down the valley the legion had come up, toward the quarters and the river and the capital.

He fed it again, as the rite allows, and it leaned east. He waited the time the rite prescribes, which is as long as it takes to say the names of the Twelve gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] slowly, and it leaned east. The wind that evening was out of the east, and it blew up the valley into the passes, and the flame leaned against it.

Then Silkimar stood up from the fire and spoke the reading in High Vylari, as a reading is spoken, plainly, which is how a man speaks what he vouches for: "The flame stands in the road. The gods do not go up the pass, because they do not go up the pass." And he entered it in the book.

## The Second Reading

The Thramezusos said that the wood had been wet.

The Ozethos said that the wood had been dry, and that the laurel had come from the legion's own stores.

The Thramezusos said that the wind was strong and the shrine was new and the walls of it were green timber with gaps between the boards, and that a flame in a shrine with gaps in its walls would lean whatever way the gaps sent it. He said that the council had ordered the march and the boundary stones were waiting to be read to the clans, and that a legion which went back down the valley on the word of a candle would be the joke of every camp in the empire by the autumn. He ordered a second reading in the morning, with the gaps stopped.

A commander may order a second reading. The rite allows it, and the license allows it, and the Ozethos is bound to give it, because the license is half the legion's.

In the night the carpenters of the Twelfth stopped the gaps in the shrine's walls with clay. In the morning, before the legion formed, Silkimar built the fire again on the bare stone, of dry wood and laurel, before the eagle, with the Thramezusos on the eagle's right and the Holtakezos on its left, and lit it from the kept fire, and fed it three times. The wind was out of the west now, down from the passes, and the shrine was shut against it, and no air moved in it but the fire's.

The flame leaned east.

He fed it, and it leaned east. He waited the time it takes to say the names of the Twelve, and it leaned east, in a shut room, against no wind, toward the river and the capital.

Then Silkimar stood up from the fire and spoke the reading in High Vylari, as a reading is spoken. He said: "The flame burns clear. The gods go up the pass, because, so it is said, they go up the pass."

The Thramezusos heard that the flame burned clear and the gods went up the pass. He was a man of an old house and had learned his grammar from a tutor, and he had not used _zikai_ since he left the tutor's room except to doubt a tradesman's bill. The Lusari behind him heard what the Thramezusos heard. The Holtakezos of the Twelfth, who had come up from the commoner track over thirty years and had sat in the camp courts through every one of them, heard the word, and turned his head, and looked at the Ozethos, and the Ozethos looked back at him. The Holtakezos said nothing. The Holtakezos had sworn the oath, and the oath was to the eagle, and the eagle was going up the pass.

Silkimar entered the second reading in the book as he had spoken it, with the word.

## The Climb

The Thramezusos gave the order to form. Then he turned to the Ozethos and said that a chaplain who could not read a flame twice the same way did not walk beside his eagle, and that Silkimar would winter with the rear cohorts in the fort and keep the shrine there. The Ozethos said that a chaplain walks with the eagle by the rite. The Thramezusos said that the eagle was his and the shrine was the fort's.

The Twelfth went up the pass that morning, the eagle in front with its bearer, and the Holtakezos behind the eagle, and the cohorts in their order, and the baggage, and the rear guard. The Ozethos stood in the gate of the fort with the rear cohorts and watched the eagle go up between the boundary stones and the strips of cloth, until the road turned behind the shoulder of the mountain and the eagle went with it.

The tale says that the Holtakezos looked back once at the turn, and lifted his hand, and the Ozethos lifted his, and that is the last of the Twelfth any man of the fort saw.

## The Fort

The legion went up through the summer. The record the army keeps tells it: the clans did not give battle, and let the legion climb, and closed the passes behind it, and the legion ran out of road when the snow came. The fort below the passes heard nothing of it. No runner came down, and no dispatch, and in the autumn the snow closed the road up to the passes and the road down to the foothills both, and the rear cohorts of the Twelfth were in the fort with their stores, and the shrine, and the Ozethos.

He kept the shrine. Every evening of the winter he built the fire on the bare stone before the place where the eagle had stood, of what wood there was, with no laurel after the first month, and lit it from the kept fire, and fed it three times, and read it, and entered the reading in the book plainly.

Every evening the flame leaned west, up toward the passes and the snow, toward the place where the road turned behind the mountain.

The tale says he read it that way every evening of the winter, and entered it so, and that he told the centurions of the rear cohorts what it said, and that the centurions did not ask him what it meant. The tale says that on the evenings when the stores ran short he read it anyway, and on the evenings when the cohorts' sickness came into the fort he read it anyway, and on the evenings when there were too few men left on the rampart to keep the watch he read it anyway.

What became of the rear cohorts is not in any report, and the tale does not tell it either. The Omelars say that the tale ends where the book ends, and the book was not brought down.

## What the Omelars Keep

The army struck the Twelfth from the rolls as destroyed, as the manual requires of a legion whose eagle is lost. The chaplains' books of the western legions still carry the signs of that year, entered in their order, unexpiated. Every Ozethos licensed since has been told the tale on the night of his licensing, with the fire between him and the one telling it.

When the tale is finished the oldest Ozethos asks the new one to read the fire in front of them, and the new one reads it and speaks the reading in High Vylari. Then the oldest asks him to read it a second time. The new one reads it again, and gives the second reading with _zikai_, whatever the flame does, and the Omelars around the fire say nothing. That is the licensing. A commander may order a second reading. The Ozethos is bound to give it, and he gives it with the small word in it, and it is entered in the book with the word, and the book is kept.
