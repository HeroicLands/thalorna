**The Words Kept from the Fire** are the rules of **Varelis Korvelis**, a jurist of [[lore-vylarirpbl|the Republic]] whose books were in the imperial archive on Arvel when it burned ([[lore-archivefire|The Archive Fire]]). Not one of the books survived. What survives is what other people had copied out of them before the fire: rules quoted in pleadings, rules a teacher had set his pupils to learn, rules cut over the door of a court. The law schools gathered the quotations into one teaching book, the _Lambizar_, "the words", and the book has been copied for the schools ever since with the masters' gloss beneath each rule, so that a student of law learns the rule and the quarrel about it on the same page.

The book keeps the rules in the order of the old law-books, under heads, and it keeps the masters' quarrels where they fall. A rule quoted in two pleadings in two forms stands in both. A rule known only from a later writer who reported it in the reported-speech construction stands that way too, and the masters have argued for centuries whether a rule Varelis is only said to have said binds as one he said: the reported construction keeps the reporter at arm's length, and the student is taught to hear the distance. The rendering marks those rules "Varelis is reported to have held." The gloss is in many hands, and the book does not name them. A gloss that answers another begins "Another master."

The house name of Varelis is built on _korvela_, a roll, and the schools hold that his house were keepers of the Senate's roll under the Republic. The record that would settle it was in the archive.

## Of the Roll

**1. What is not on the roll is not in the city.**

_Gloss._ The rule is the first in every copy, and the schools teach it first. A person, a law, a contract or a debt exists before the city when it is written on the roll the city keeps for it, and not before. A man may be born in the city and live in it seventy years, and if no roll carries his name he is not in it at law.

**2. The roll is kept by one hand and read by every hand.**

_Gloss._ One keeper, so that the roll cannot be written by two men who disagree. Every reader, so that the keeper cannot write what no one sees.

_Another master._ The rule is the Republic's. Under the Orzoi the roll is kept by the Guild and read by the Guild, and the second half of the rule is kept in the schools.

**3. Strike nothing. Write the correction below, and date it.**

_Gloss._ A roll with a name scraped out is a roll nobody can trust again, because nobody can say what else was scraped. A correction written below the error keeps both, and the date shows which is later.

_Another master._ The Senate strikes names. The rule is older than the Senate's habit and does not agree with it, and the schools teach both.

## Of the Seal and the Witness

**4. A writing without a seal is a letter. A writing with a seal is an act.**

**5. The seal is the keeper's hand, lent to the writing.**

_Gloss._ Hence a seal set in the keeper's absence is a forgery, though the wax and the stone be the true ones.

**6. Two witnesses who agree are one witness. Two witnesses who disagree are two.**

_Gloss._ The first of the hard rules, and the students hate it. Witnesses who agree in every word have agreed before they came to court, and the court hears one story told twice. Witnesses who disagree in the small things and agree in the large have each seen the thing, and the court hears two men.

_Another master._ The rule is quoted in one pleading only, by an advocate who was losing.

**7. Ask the witness what he saw, never what it meant.**

**8. A thing seen by a slave is seen.**

_Gloss._ A slave has no standing at law, and the rule does not give him any. It holds that what he saw happened, and that his owner answers for the telling of it. The masters do not agree whether Varelis wrote "is seen" or "is not seen"; the two copies differ by the one short word _ar_, "not," and the schools teach the first because the court of the Nazdoi follows it.

## Of the Oath

**9. An oath is a contract with a party who keeps the better copy.**

_Gloss._ The god sees the oath, and the god does not lose his copy. Hence an oath binds in the courts as well as before heaven, and a man who swears falsely has forged a writing the court cannot inspect and the god can.

**10. Swear little. Swear it whole.**

**11. Varelis is reported to have held that an oath sworn under the eagle binds the eagle as well as the man.**

_Gloss._ Reported, and so held at a distance. The schools of the army teach it as his, and teach that a legion which breaks the oath of its men to it has broken its own. The schools of the Senate teach that it is not his, because it is not anywhere in the pleadings, and because the legions have not kept it.

## Of the Hand

**12. What is in a man's hand, he answers for.**

_Gloss._ The master of a household answers at law for everyone under his roof: wife, children, freedmen, slaves. The rule is short because it is the oldest.

**13. A hand that cannot answer cannot hold.**

_Gloss._ A guardian who wastes a ward's estate loses the ward. The province of Hilen keeps a case on this rule that the schools teach as its proof, in which a widow took her son's guardianship from his uncle by sitting at a patron's door until she was heard.

**14. The hand opens once.**

_Gloss._ Manumission is done once and cannot be undone: the freedman is given a gender, and a gender is not taken back. So also an adoption: the heir goes out of his father's hand, and his father may not call him back into it, though the son ask.

_Another master._ The Senate unhallows houses, and a name goes back to the ordinary endings. The hand has opened twice there. The first master answers that the Senate is not a hand.

## Of the Guest and the Stranger

**15. Place the stranger before you feed him.**

_Gloss._ Ask who he is and whose he is, and set him on his step, and then set the bread in front of him. A guest whose standing is unknown cannot be wronged or honored correctly, and either mistake is the host's.

**16. A stranger who will not be placed is placed at the bottom, and owed everything.**

_Gloss._ The second clause is the one the students forget. The man nobody can place is put lowest, and the host owes him the whole of guest-right regardless: roof, bread and protection. The rule is the reason the provinces call the city cold and the city calls itself correct.

**17. The outsider has preferences. The citizen has law.**

_Gloss._ Quoted in every speech the Senate has heard against the north since the Republic. The schools teach that Varelis meant by it a burden and not a boast: a man who has law must use it when his preference would serve him better.

## Of the Dead and the Line

**18. The living within the line, the dead without.**

_Gloss._ The rule is older than the line around the six hills and was applied to every city of the Republic. A tomb inside the boundary is a crime against the dead as much as against the living, because the dead inside the line are counted among the living, and the dead may not be counted among the living.

**19. A will speaks the day it is read and not the day it is written.**

**20. The dead own nothing and are owed the keeping.**

_Gloss._ Everything a man owned passes at his death. The care of his tomb does not pass, because it was never his. It is owed to him by the heir, and a court will hear the dead man's claim to it brought by anyone.

_Another master._ Brought by anyone, and heard by none, since the heir is the only one with standing. The rule is quoted far more than it is used.

## Of the Name

**21. The name is the person at law.**

**22. A name may be given, raised, lowered or struck. It may not be lent.**

_Gloss._ Given at birth; raised into the hallowed when a house is raised; lowered when a house is disgraced; struck by the unnaming. A man who lets another sign in his name has done none of the four, and the act is void and both are liable.

**23. Varelis is reported to have held that a name struck from the roll is still owed its tomb.**

_Gloss._ Reported. The unnaming forbids the name to be said, and the schools teach that Varelis meant the tomb is kept without the name, as the priests of the Silent Judge keep the unclaimed. The masters of the Senate's schools teach that the rule is not his, and that a struck name has no tomb because it has no person.

_Another master._ Both schools agree that it is not in the pleadings. Both schools agree that it is in the book.

**24. What is not written was not done.**

_Gloss._ The last rule in every copy. It is the rule of evidence, and it is the rule the courts live by: a contract not written, a debt not entered, an oath not recorded is a thing the court cannot see, and what the court cannot see did not happen at law.

_Another master._ The imperial archive burned with the rolls of the Republic in it, and every original the Guild keeps was written after the fire. By this rule the Republic was not done.

_Another master._ By this rule the Founder was not done, for the record does not give the name.

_Another master._ The rule itself was written before the fire, and is known only from a copy made after it. By this rule the rule was not done. Write it again.
