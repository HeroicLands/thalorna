**The Recitation of the Breaking**, _Turemtsemhat_, is the fixed text the [[lore-khazrynclt|Tellumi]] recite of [Turem]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, the war in which the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Atarzád]] took four of their seven cities. It is a _tsemhat_, a recitation: couplets, but plain ones, with no cloth and no reed, spoken standing by the eldest of a house at a princely funeral, by a Delkoshekh on the first day of the five-day festival, and at every formal ceremonial of the exile courts in the [[place-amradadrgn|Amradad Region]], and spoken behind closed doors in the subject quarters of the four held cities, where it names the magistrate's own people. The Tellumi insist that it is not a lament and not a grievance: it is a case being kept open, and its form is the form of a case, each claim followed by the tablet that proves it. A reciter who departs from the words is corrected from the floor.

The common transcription and translation below give the text as the [House of Zargandur]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} in exile recites it, which gives the second year its full weight because the war-house died in it; the other houses lengthen their own years and shorten this one. It names seven riders of the war-house, [Kireshay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Budishay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Pedunay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Garvanay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Perimay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Hageshay]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Lirunay]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, as the house's tablet names them, and leaves the princes to the tablet, because the recitation may not read a prince's name in a city where reading it is forbidden. The Atarzád tell the same twelve years as the War of the Twelve Years and the keeping of a Promise, and their account is set out in their own note; this one reports their claim in order to refuse it, which is what a case does. The field outside [[place-zargandur|Zargandur]]'s north wall, [[place-henhulpedin|Henhutpedin]], is where the recitation ends, and what it says of the lamps in the Tellumi quarter is the recitation's own account of a custom the quarter explains to nobody.

This is the seven cities' case. The case remains open./
Stand to hear it. It is brief. We state nothing/
the clay does not hold. We name the tablet./
Where no tablet holds a matter, we make no claim for it./
Seven cities, in the order of the earliest clay:/
[[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]], beneath the pale cliff, where the great spring broke out into life;/
[[place-ashkarad|Ashkarad]] of the crafts; Zargandur of the horse;/
[[place-kethramir|Kethramír]], below the spur; [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]], where the last stream/
dries away; [[place-mehrnagord|Mernagord]] beside the salt; [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-ber]] beside the ice./
Seven springs. Seven houses. Seven tablets. Speak it twice./
Seven. We came down from the mountains to them. That is our first claim./
No taker of our cities takes that from our name./
Now hear the guests. The settlement tablet states this:/
in the ninth year before the war, in the month of the first melt,/
a people reached the belt's edge,/
from west and south, their herds thin and their water spent./
Twelve tribes beneath twelve chiefs asked what strangers ask:/
bread, water, shade, no questions. We imposed no work./
We gave them these. Shirvan's clay says the princes granted marginal land/
north of the gardens, where the streams fail, for grazing. They found it good./
They stayed. Kethramír's tablet records the tithe they paid./
Zargandur's records their bought horses and their oaths./
We gave twelve tribes the guest-right. We have not withdrawn it./
We feed the stranger in distress. That is our identity, the road/
this case follows. The Atarzád know it; it brought them inside the belt./
Speak as plainly as the record: we fed them. We felt compassion/
and acted. We would act again. This is no boast./
This is the claim: those who feed a stranger hold that stranger's oath./
The stranger holds, at most, their bread. Bread was given; oath was broken./
That is the first finding. Now the years: twelve. We have kept watch./
In the first year they rose. Zargandur's tablet names the day:/
the twelfth of the month of the high melt. The herds were far away/
on the marginal ground. Twelve tribes came armed from pasture/
and seized the war-city's northern weirs. The city felt no alarm:/
a guest does not seize a weir. The prince sent to ask why./
One answered: _The land is promised._ That was the entire reply./
_The land is promised._ Those words meet us at every gate of the case./
That is their claim. Ours: no deity gives one people's place/
to another. [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]] is a god, keeper of the hearth, honored in his seat./
He is no sovereign granting cities. Shirvan's tablet fully states/
the gods' order, as it has for two thousand years. He has his place there./
It grants him no kingdoms. That is the finding. No further moment/
need be spent on their theology. They held the weirs. War began./
Twelve years: the first took the water. The second saw the sun/
descend over Zargandur. Hear this slowly. It belongs to us./
The second year, the last morning, in its first hours before the heat:/
Zargandur's war-house rode from the north gate to meet the host/
on pasture, because the host stood there. The house held most of the belt's cavalry,/
and the strongest. They went./
All died, princes with them. By noon the day was spent, the city fallen. That is the battle./
We do not sing the battle. We name the riders. The tablet holds them. Be still./
Kireshay held the line's left and yielded no ground./
Budishay carried the house banner and never dropped it. Its staff was found/
standing in the trench, the cloth rotted away, and him beneath./
Pedunay, who drilled the house in its strokes, died with his teeth/
clenched, the tablet says. It gives no other detail of their deaths./
Garvanay bred the gray mares the [zhoqarod]{lang=atar} ride today; he rode one./
Perimay kept the gate-fires, youngest of them, somebody's son./
Hageshay held the right. They found him with the princes. He had held./
Lirunay, a freed man of the house, had no duty to ride. He fell/
beside Hageshay. The tablet says only: he was free, and he went./
And the princes. The tablet keeps their names; this recitation does not. We lend them/
to the clay until a Delkoshekh stands on the north grass and reads them where they lie./
That is the second year. It is unfinished. The house stands for it. Keep dry eyes./
That night the Atarzád gave their dead to flame. They observed their own rite./
Our dead remained on pasture. No Delkoshekh could pass the wall. The quarter's priest had not slept;/
he stood at the north gate holding the house tablet/
and asked to go out and read the names, according to the rite every Tellumi knows:/
the name read from clay, the lamps kindled, and the dead received./
He asked. They held the gate. Worship of [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Ahurdaen]] was already forbidden. He believed/
his priesthood would let him through. They refused him. No lamps were lit./
Three days the war-house lay on the north grass. On the third the [zhoqarod]{lang=atar} dug a pit,/
drove the bodies in with their horses, and covered them. The grass above that trench/
is shorter than on either side, and has remained so. The tablet gives its length./
The names were not read. The lamps were not lit. That is the second finding./
This is the case's hinge. The other findings concern land; this binds/
in a court beyond theirs and ours. A man whose name goes unread is not received./
The war-house is not received. A hundred years ago nobody in the belt could have believed/
a Tellumi house could lie unread outside its own wall./
It lies there now. Hooves cross it nightly at the sortie's hour. That is all/
the tablet says of the field. The quarter adds the rest. The [zhoqarod]{lang=atar} set no watch there after dark./
Therefore a lamp burns at each Tellumi door from dusk to dawn:/
the lamps left unlit that night. Each night we light them in its place./
We have done so for ninety years. The quarter gives no explanation; the recitation gives it. Go,/
ask Zargandur's lamp-seller why she sells so many. She keeps silence. Now you know./
That is the second year. War continued ten more. The other houses tell those years./
The fifth year: Ashkarad of the workshops. Its tablet names the gate,/
the hour, the man who opened it. The House of Ashkarad leaves his name/
out of the recitation: his descendants still live in the city and make good tools./
Treachery took Ashkarad, rather than cavalry. The [teghumod]{lang=atar} hold it. Workshop rules/
remain, craft councils sit, the tribe buys what they make./
The water tunnels belong to families, shaft by shaft. The tax is paid./
The eighth year: priestly Kethramír. Its tablet counts/
the priests who would not flee and were killed in the courts. It is a great number./
Kethramír's house speaks that number in its recitation. We leave it unsaid:/
it is theirs to give. The [tozhirod]{lang=atar} hold the city and govern like scholars, by rote/
and precedent. The quarter keeps its priest and courts. The magistrate reads the clay/
and sometimes agrees. That is the eighth year. We pass over the ninth and tenth:/
their tablets record Shirvan's siege, a long account/
of water, of who held basin and cliff, and of the price/
the city paid for grain. Nobody recites it. Its ending is the same./
The eleventh year: Shirvan, first and holiest, the great spring beneath the cliff. The Awakening/
count gives the first day of the low-water month. The wall opened/
at the basin gate. By nightfall the city was taken. There is more./
They tore down the Keeper's temple. Its stones stand in the fire-temple./
They tore down the [[affiliation-zurathra|Mother's]] great spring shrine. Fires they vow to keep burning/
stand over the holy water itself. They burn there. The Tellumi do not drink/
from that side of the basin, and give no Atarzád their reason. Consider/
the archive: two thousand unbroken years of clay in the temple rooms,/
the calendar, the kings, the seven houses' treaties, the gods' order, the tombs,/
every household's water-hours, and the commentaries. It burned./
Clay burns poorly. This is the case's sole mercy, unearned:/
the tablets fleeing households carried away lie among the exile courts./
Those fallen in rubble lie beneath the lower basin, in mud, in the lower courts/
of the spring. Channel digging brings them up; water-masters take them before an overseer sees./
The seven houses' treaty tablets lie somewhere beneath the water-masters' keys./
That is the eleventh year. The twelfth hunted down the clergy and ended the war./
Four cities taken, three standing. One was empty then and is settled now. We do not call/
Vahurdash a victory. It is a town among its own stones, drinking from two wells./
It is the sole recovered city. The recitation says so; the tablet tells/
the rest. Seven. Four. Three. That is the count, the standing of the case./
What remains: the subject quarters, the old districts, lanes too narrow for a cart. The place/
a Tellumi lives in the city his grandfather governed. No land outside the quarter. No arms./
No public worship except their god's. The water-tax falls on Tellumi household heads alone; it wounds/
more through its name than its weight: the water is ours, the engineers ours,/
the canals cannot run without us. Both peoples know. The city endures/
on that knowledge. We keep the receipt, two calendars, the clay. The clay is ours./
What is owed: four cities, four palaces, two temples, one spring. The unread names. The lamps. No flowers,/
no payment, no treaty, no quarter. A returned thing has its line struck from the recitation./
The recitation shortens by that line. That is the only ending we seek. Return, beyond another's/
gracious grant. Revun. On every exile house's tablet stands the word; beneath it, the list./
This is the case. It stays open. The reciter sits. The house stands. Nothing is dismissed.
