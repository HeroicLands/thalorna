**The Twelve Years** is the Ātárzád account of the war the Tellumi call Turem, the Breaking. It is told in the speaker's own voice at the Festival of the Promise, where the tribes gather at Mount Shōfar and renew the Covenant. The telling below belongs to no named house; it gives the tribes' account of what they hold, and leaves the Tellumi answer in the mouth of the people who keep it.

: The Twelve Years {#twelve-years}

You have heard the Tellumi call us guests. You have heard them say that a guest took their water and broke the welcome given to him. I will tell you what we say of those years, and you may carry both tellings in your mind. We do not deny that the Tellumi were here before our tribes entered Tellumel. We say that the Sacred Flame promised Tellumel to the Flame-born, and that the Promise binds us still.

The tribes came out of bondage in Aû'Khelâthu and crossed the dry country under Mūshárā. He brought us to Mount Shōfar, where the Sacred Flame gave the Promise. Mūshárā died on the mountain's slope before he entered the land. His successor, Yáhōshí, led the tribes into Tellumel. We came as twelve tribes, with our herds and our children, and the Tellumi gave us ground at the edge of their watered land. They say the ground was given to guests. We say the Promise had already named the land.

When the war began, the Tellumi had walls, wells and seven cities joined by their water. We had twelve tribes joined by the Covenant. The Tellumi say no god can give one people's home to another. We say the Sacred Flame gave it to us. That is where our accounts part. Do not mistake the difference for a forgotten word; each people remembers what it believes it must keep.

In the first year the war opened over water and land. The Tellumi say our people took the weirs north of Zargandûr and called the ground promised. We say that we had come to claim what the Flame had promised. Both tellings begin at the water. The war did not end in one season. It held the tribes and the cities through twelve years, and its name is the count we give it.

In the second year, the Bāhrām host came to Zargandûr. The city's war-house rode out through the north gate to meet them on the pasture. The riders were killed, the princes with them, and the city fell by noon. That is the day both peoples remember. We gave our own dead to the flame that night. The Tellumi asked to go beyond the wall and read their names from the tablets, as their dead must be received. The gate was ours. We refused them. Three days later the dead were driven into a trench and covered with earth.

You may ask whether we ought to have opened the gate. I will not hide what we did, and I will not pretend that the Tellumi can receive their dead by our rite. We held the city, and they could not read the names. The ground beyond the wall is Henhulpedin to them; the Bāhrām call it the north grass. At night no Bāhrām picket stands there. The lamps the Tellumi light for their dead were not lit that night. We know the cost of that refusal because we live beside what it left.

The fifth year brought Ashkarad under the tribes. The city of workshops became an Ātárzád holding, and the Ardashír now keep the gates where the caravan road enters the belt. In the eighth year Kethramír fell to the tribes, and the Zarvān hold it now. Tellumi priests were killed there; their account keeps the count, and we will not make it smaller by turning away from it. The cities changed hands, but the people who worked their water and their craft remained among their streets.

In the eleventh year the tribes took Shirvan, the first city and the holiest seat of the Tellumi. Its great spring rose beneath the cliff, and its archive held the tablets by which the Tellumi kept their calendar, their rulers and their treaties. The archive burned when the city fell. What the fleeing households carried away went with them into exile; what was lost in the fire could not be called back. The Tellumi say their temples were torn down and the fires of Ātáröš built where their holy places had stood. They say this as a charge against us. We remember that the cities came under the Promise, and that our worship of the Sacred Flame took the place of theirs.

The war's name holds twelve years. We say that the Promise brought the tribes through them, and that the four cities taken in that war are the four cities we hold. The Tellumi keep the dead, the lost temples and the burned tablets in their telling. They say the war is not over while their claim remains open. We do not grant that claim, and they do not grant ours. Do not take a silence in either telling for peace.

At Mount Shōfar we renew the Covenant before the Unconsuming Flame. The Tōqēl keeps the flame, the tribes hear the Promise again, and each child learns the names of the twelve tribes. The Tellumi recite their case in their courts and in the cities we took. We know the lamps they light for the war-house's dead. We know the ground where their riders lie. Still we say the land was promised, and we will not cast the Promise out of our mouths.
