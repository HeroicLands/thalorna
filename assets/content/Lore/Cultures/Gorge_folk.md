The **gorge-folk** live where the great rivers of the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]] leave the forest and cut north through the highland spurs. Rapids and falls stop every boat in the gorges, and the gorge-folk carry the cargo around them.

## Where They Live

Gorge-folk villages stand on the benches above the rapids and along the portage trails. Each is a few bands of one tribe, with ridge gardens on the slopes, fish-traps in the white water and a landing at each end of the carry. The northernmost villages stand within reach of the trading posts of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]].

## How They Live

The portage is the living. A tribe carries canoes and goods past its stretch of rapid for a share, and the share is the tribe's wealth. The gorge-folk know who is honest and who is not, and they grade a trader by how he paid at the last landing. Garden crops, river fish and forest game fill out a diet that foragers of the deep interior would not recognize.

The gorge-folk are not one of the four traditions of the [[lore-bikoclt|forest peoples]]. They speak [[skill-ekpelulng|Ekpelu]], the common tongue, and keep the forest's offerings and restraints, but their tribes hold ground in the gorges where the [[lore-luwandoclt|Luwando]] pass through as guests. Their leaders hold their place by arranging fair portage, and a leader who cheats a landing finds no canoe will come.

## What They Look Like

Gorge-folk are the tallest and broadest of the forest peoples, and their skin is the darkest, from deep brown to ebony. Carrying builds shoulders and legs, and faces are smooth. Dress is bark cloth and woven fibers worn short for work, with cloth and metal rings from the northern trade worn for gatherings.
