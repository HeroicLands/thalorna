**What Was Paid at the Shoals** is the song the veterans' halls of the [Veterans' Quarter]{.name data-type="place/site"} in [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]] sing on the night after the [[lore-fleetreview|Fleet Review]], while the [Admiral's Council]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} keeps its banquet for the review across the city. The men and women who sing it sailed in the flotilla that won [[lore-tamzirbattle|The Battle of Tamzir Shoals]] ([[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzir Shoals]]), or are the children of those who did. A leader sings the long lines standing under the broadsheet on the hall's wall, and the hall answers the refrain, _Write it down_. The song is set out as an account, the way a house's book is: what was promised, what was carried, what was paid. It was made in the year after the peace and has no maker's name, because the halls give none.

Every hall sings the same account and its own dead. The catalog of the small boats and their crews changes from hall to hall, and a hall adds a name when one of its veterans dies; the copy below is the one sung in a hall on the waterfront of the quarter, and its dead are [shabaru hitusuni]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=taru}, a fisherman who took his boat into the Shoals with a ram on her bow, [tirmana zadduni]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=taru}, a net-mender who rowed a harbor boat of archers, and [gadarun]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=taru}, a hill man who came down to the coast for the war. The [[affiliation-corsairleg|Corsair League]]'s captains do not come to the halls on that night. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] does not forbid the song, and its enforcers know the words.

: What Was Paid at the Shoals {#song}

::: {.verse form=song lang=en}
[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Turn the lamp to the wall, so the wall can read./
Turn the lamp to the sheet, so the sheet can see who is here./
There is the chain, broken, and there is the sun, coming up./
There is the ship, and the flag on the ship, and the words under the ship./
Read them, the ones who can read. The rest of you, say them.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
We bleed gold for an empire that gives us nothing but chains.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
That is the first line of the account. That is the opening price./
Nobody on this coast expects the opening price to stand./
They printed it in the Guild Quarter, at the guild's expense,/
and they pinned it on the doors of the harbor and signed it the people of Taruzel./
The people of Taruzel read it, and the people of Taruzel went./
Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Four things were promised, and they were promised in the square:/
land, for the ones who had none, on the islands we were freeing;/
the guild, for the ones the guilds had never let in at the door;/
a pension, for the ones who came home with no hand to work with;/
a voice, in the council of the city that our backs had built./
Four things. Count them on the hand. Land, the guild, the pension, the voice./
Write them down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write them down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
For three things the harbor went to the Shoals,/
and for four it did not come back the same:/
because the legions had taken its sons for wars that were not its wars;/
because the customs house had taken its fish for a price that was not a price;/
because the paper on the door said freedom, and the harbor could read that word;/
and because the men who wrote the paper stood on the quay and called them brothers.

The hulls of the houses went, the warships and the armed traders,/
the great ones that the yards of Yamuzel had built for a war/
nobody had admitted was coming until the yards were finished./
The fishing boats went, with rams spiked onto their bows/
by blacksmiths who had never made a ram, who made it from a plowshare./
The harbor boats went, with archers sitting on the thwarts/
so close together that a man could not draw without elbowing his neighbor./
The hill men went, who had never been on the sea,/
who were sick over the side all the first night and fought all the second day./
Nobody had ever fought beside anybody. Nobody knew the signals./
Everybody knew the water./
Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
They came into the Shoals at dawn, the empire's ships,/
the heaviest ships in the eastern sea, low in the water with their own weight./
They came in deep, and the Shoals are not deep./
They came in proud, and the Shoals do not care./
The sand took the first one by the keel, and she stopped, and the second struck her stern./
The tide went out from under the third, and she lay down on her side like a cow./
We came out of the channels they did not know,/
we came round the reefs they had not been told of,/
we came at them the way the dogs come round the cattle in the hills,/
from every side, all day, and all the night, and all the second day./
On the second evening there was nothing left of them that floated/
that did not have a Taruzelian on its deck./
Their admiral was dead. We did not know his name. We did not ask it./
Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Now the column of the dead, as it stands in this hall./
Every hall has its own column. This is ours.

Shabaru Hitusuni, a fisherman of the north quay,/
who took his own boat in with a plowshare on her bow,/
and put it through the oars of a ship ten times his length,/
and was caught under her side when she rolled, and his boat with him./
His boat was the _Second Wife_. His first wife was standing on the quay./
Shabaru Hitusuni. Write him down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write him down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Tirmana Zadduni, a mender of nets on the south quay,/
who rowed a harbor boat of twelve archers into the smoke,/
because the man who owned the boat was too drunk to row it,/
and rowed it back with five, and went in again for the rest,/
and was found in the morning with the oar still in her hands./
Tirmana Zadduni. Write her down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write her down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Gadarun, of the hills, of a people the coast does not name,/
who came down the old road past the forts with a spear and a goat-hair cloak/
and had never seen a ship till the day he went aboard one,/
who went over the rail of an imperial warship with a knife in his teeth/
the way the songs of his people say they went over walls for the empire's pay,/
and did not come back over it./
His people came down to the quay for him in the autumn./
Nobody could tell them which ship. Nobody could tell them which water./
Gadarun of the hills. Write him down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write him down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
And the ones whose names this hall did not have,/
and the ones whose names went down with the hulls that went down,/
and the ones whose names nobody wrote, because nobody thought to write them,/
because everybody thought there would be time for writing when the war was won./
Write them down. Write down that we do not have them.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Now the other column. Now what was paid./
The empire paid. It went home. It is still angry. That is paid./
The houses were paid for the hulls the empire burned,/
every hull at its price, in the script they printed after,/
and the price was entered in the book, and the book was closed./
The Compact was paid. It is still being paid. It will be paid for a long time./
It sits in the governor's palace, and the governor sat there before it,/
and the door is the same door, and the guard is a different guard./
The captains were paid, the ones who would sail under a flag./
They have a League now, and a sword on every quay, and the customs waves them through./
Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
And for us, a discharge./
A paper with a seal on it, that said: you are no longer needed./
And for us, a purse of silver. A small purse./
Count it with me, the ones who still have it, the ones who never spent it./
One coin for the land. One coin for the guild./
One coin for the pension. One coin for the voice./
The rest of the coins are for the voyage home, and the voyage home was a short walk./
Four coins. Count them on the hand. Land, the guild, the pension, the voice./
Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Then they said that some of us owed money, and we did not owe it./
Then they said that some of us had stolen, and we had not stolen./
Then they said that a captain who had sailed in the flotilla was a pirate,/
and the sea is wide, and some of the captains said: very well, a pirate./
Then they gave some of us a post at a gate, and a pension, a small one,/
enough to live on and not enough to come to this hall with, and some of us took it./
Do not curse the ones who took it. They have children. Write it down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
For three things a debt is closed on this coast,/
and there is no fourth:/
it is paid, and the payer and the paid clasp hands on it;/
it is forgiven, and the forgiver writes that he forgave;/
or the one who owes it dies, and his house stands for him./
We have not been paid. Write it down./
We have not forgiven. Write it down./
And the ones who owe it are alive. They are at the banquet tonight, across the city./
We can hear the music from here.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
They say: we never lied. We said freedom, and you are free./
Free of the _therkos_. Free of the legions. Free of the tax that went to the capital./
They say: we did not promise you what you heard. You heard it yourselves./
Hear them. They are not lying. That is the worst of it./
They opened at a price, and we did not push it back,/
because a man does not haggle with his brother on the morning of a war./
We did not sit. We did not drink. We did not argue./
We clasped on the opening price. They knew we would. They are merchants./
Write that down too. Write down what we did not do.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Now look at the wall./
When the First of Besikar goes out to the head of the stones in the spring,/
he splits the tally and throws the sea its half, and keeps the other in the strongroom,/
and he says to the sea: bring it back when you want to settle./
The sea never brings it back. The sea does not want to settle./
That is how the city keeps its harbor: by owing something that is never called./
This sheet on the wall is our half./
The notches are in it. The four things are in it. The column of the dead is in it./
We are not the sea. We are going to bring it back./
Not tonight. They are at the banquet tonight, and the music is good./
But we keep our half where the lamp can read it./
We are the ones who keep the count. Write that down.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
Write it down.

[The leader:]{.speaker}/
Turn the lamp from the wall. The wall has read enough./
Turn the lamp to the room. Let the room see who is here./
There are fewer of us than last year./
There are more of our children than last year./
Teach them the column. Teach them the four coins./
Teach them the opening price, and what you push it back with./
And when one of us dies in the year, bring his name to this hall,/
and we will put it in the column, and sing it after Gadarun./
The account is open.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
The account is open. Write it down.
