**The Dispute of the Hull and the Strongroom** is played every year in the temple square of [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] on the second day of the [[lore-goldenharvest|Festival of the Golden Harvest]], when the ships have come home laden and the shops are shut. Two reciters play it on a cart drawn up before the temple steps. The Hull wears a captain's salt-stained coat and carries a coil of rope, and the Strongroom wears a clerk's sleeve-guards and carries an iron key on a chain. The crowd takes sides by trade, and a harbor's captains and its counting-houses shout down each other's favorite lines. A priestess of the [[affiliation-olvenia|Faith of Ólvenia]] comes down the steps at the end to give the verdict, as the temple's priestesses do in disputes between merchants, and the crowd knows the verdict by heart and waits for it anyway.

The text is fixed in its long lines and loose in its insults. Each harbor puts its own captains' and houses' names into the boasts, so the copy below, played at [[place-besikar2|Besikar]], has the Hull boast of the Shoals and the Strongroom of the script it kept good through the war. The Hull and the Strongroom argue in answering couplets, each taking up the other's last word, and both reach for the coast's counting sayings, _for three things … and for four_.

In [[place-yamuzel2|Yamuzel]], since the war, the dispute is played in the square before the [Veterans' Quarter]{.name data-type="place/site"} as well as at the temple, and there a third reciter in a porter's rope pad climbs onto the cart before the priestess speaks. The porter's lines are given below as Yamuzel plays them. In the Veterans' Quarter the priestess does not answer the porter, the two reciters stand silent, and the cart is drawn away without a verdict. At the temple in Yamuzel the porter is not played.

: The Dispute of the Hull and the Strongroom {#dispute}

::: {.verse form=dispute lang=en}
When the ships came home laden and the goddess opened her doors,/
when the shops of the quay were shuttered and the wine came up from the cellars,/
the Hull came in on the evening tide with her hold full to the hatches,/
and the Strongroom stood on the quay with his key, to count what she carried./
The Hull said: Stand aside, old box. I have been to the edge of the chart./
The Strongroom said: Stand still, old boat. You have not been entered yet.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
Look at me, you that never got your feet wet./
Look at my seams, that are caulked with the tar of three harbors./
I went out with the spring and came home with the harvest./
I went out with salt and came home with silk./
I went out with oil and came home with pepper,/
I went out empty and came home with the price of a house./
What did you do all summer? You sat in the dark and waited for me.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
I sat in the dark and waited, and that is what I am for./
I sat in the dark and kept the seal you sailed under./
You went out with salt? Whose salt? You went out on whose credit?/
You came home with silk? Whose silk? Whose name did the seller read?/
You are a loan with a sail on it, and the loan is written in me./
When you went out, I was the one who was out. You were only the carrying.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
The carrying! Hear him. The carrying, says the box with the key./
For three things the sea makes a man pray,/
and for four it makes him curse his mother:/
for a lee shore at night, and the wind setting onto it;/
for a mast that groans in its step when the swell comes over the bow;/
for a shoal that was not on the chart, where the chart said deep water;/
and for a clerk on the quay who asks him where the cargo is.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
Where the cargo is, says the boat, as if it were a hard question./
For three things the quay stands still when I open,/
and for four it does not breathe:/
for the seal of a great house, broken in front of witnesses;/
for the debt of a captain called in on the day the captain came home;/
for a line in my book that was open forty years and is closing;/
and for a boat that boasts on the quay before she has been counted.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
Count me, then. Count my ribs, there are thirty of them./
Count my oars, count my crew, count the times I came back./
You cannot count the times I came back,/
because your book only writes down the times I was owed./
I carried the coast to Helionis, and Helionis paid for it./
I carried the coast to the Khelathi quays, and the Khelathi paid for it./
I carried the coast to the Shoals, in the war, and nobody paid for it./
Write that down, box. Write down the Shoals.

[The crowd, at Besikar, the captains' side:]{.speaker}/
Write down the Shoals!

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
I wrote down the Shoals. I wrote down every hull that went in./
I wrote down the hulls the empire burned, and the price of each,/
and the price of each was paid to the house that owned her, in good script,/
because I kept the script good when nobody would take it./
Who do you think paid for the war, boat? The sea?/
The sea pays nobody. I paid. I am paying it still.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
You are paying it still! The Compact is paying it still, says the box,/
with a key round its neck and a palace to sit in./
Without me your gold is a stone in a hole in the ground./
Without me your seal is a lump of wax on a string./
Without me your book is a list of debts nobody can carry./
I am the only thing on this coast that goes anywhere.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
Without me you are firewood at the back of a beach./
Without me you are planks with nobody's name on them./
Without me your crew is fourteen men with nobody to pay them./
A hull with no house is a raft, and a raft goes anywhere,/
and nobody on this coast lends a copper to anywhere./
I am the reason anywhere is somewhere.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
Go to sea, then, box. Go out past the breakwater and float./
Let us see you float with your iron bands and your bars./
You will go down in the harbor mouth where the First throws the tally,/
and the sea will have both halves of the count, and good riddance.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
Come ashore, then, boat. Come up the quay and stand./
Let us see you stand when the debts of your owner are called./
You will lie on your side in the mud at low water with your seams open,/
and the boys will steal your copper, and nobody will stop them.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
I have drowned men in me. Forty, in thirty years./
Men who went down with the cargo still in my hold,/
men who went over the rail in the night, and their names went with them./
What have you got, box, that you can set beside forty men?

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
I have your forty men. I have every one of them./
I have their names in a column, and their wages beside the names,/
and the wages were paid to their wives on the day the news came in,/
because a debt the dead are owed does not go into the sea with them./
That is what I have. I have your drowned. You only lost them.

[The crowd, at Besikar, both sides:]{.speaker}/
Oh!

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
For three things a captain is proud, and for four he will not be silent:/
for a hull of his own, that he paid for out of his own voyages;/
for a crew that would ship with him again after a winter like that;/
for a cargo that came in whole when the others came in wet;/
and for the day he walks into the counting-house and owes it nothing.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
For three things a clerk is proud, and for four he will not be silent:/
for a book that balances when the lamp is put out;/
for a loan made to a captain the other houses laughed at;/
for the same captain, ten years on, walking in to pay it;/
and for the day he walks out again owing it nothing, and comes back next spring.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
He comes back next spring because the sea is out there.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
He comes back next spring because the money is in here.

[The Hull:]{.speaker}/
The sea is out there.

[The Strongroom:]{.speaker}/
The money is in here.

[The Hull and the Strongroom, together:]{.speaker}/
Judge between us! Somebody judge between us!

[In Yamuzel, in the square before the Veterans' Quarter, the porter climbs onto the cart here.]{.direction}

[The Porter:]{.speaker}/
I will judge between you. I have the time./
I have been waiting on this quay since the morning for one of you to pay me./
Who carried the salt down the quay to the hold? I did./
Who carried the silk up the quay to the strongroom? I did./
Who carried the boarding pike into the Shoals, in a fishing boat with a ram on her bow?/
I did, and my brother, and my brother did not carry it out./
The hull went in on the house's credit, and the house was paid for the hull./
The box kept the script good, and the box was paid for the script./
My brother went in on a sheet of printed paper/
with a broken chain on it and a rising sun,/
and the paper is on the wall of the hall behind me, and the paper is what we were paid./
For three things the quay is quiet, and for four it cannot sleep:/
for a ship that comes in at night with no lights;/
for a house whose debts are called on the same morning;/
for a word that was printed, and pinned, and read out in the square;/
and for the men who heard the word and went, and came back, and were counted./
Judge between them? I am asking you to judge between us.

[In the Veterans' Quarter nobody answers him. The Hull and the Strongroom stand on the cart and say nothing. The cart is drawn away. Everywhere else the play goes on as follows.]{.direction}

[The Priestess, coming down the temple steps:]{.speaker}/
Hush, the two of you. Hush, the whole square./
The goddess has heard the boat and the goddess has heard the box,/
and the goddess has been patient, and she is the Bountiful One, not the patient one./
Hull, you are the greater on the sea. Nobody here disputes it./
Strongroom, you are the greater on the shore. Nobody disputes that either./
On the quay, where the sea comes up against the shore,/
neither of you is anything, and both of you are standing there arguing.

[The Priestess:]{.speaker}/
So hear the verdict, and do not push it back, because it is not a price./
You are partners. You were partners before you were born./
The hull goes out on the strongroom's name./
The strongroom fills on the hull's coming home./
The gain is split between you, half to the boat and half to the box,/
after the first portion, which is the goddess's, and comes out first./
Clasp hands. Clasp them in front of the square, so that it holds.

[The Hull and the Strongroom clasp hands. The crowd shouts.]{.direction}

[The Priestess:]{.speaker}/
And now, since you are both in such good voices,/
since you have both told the square how much you carried and how much you kept,/
bring up the first portion. Up the steps. Both of you./
The boat carries it and the box counts it./
That is the Offering of Gain, and the dispute is ended,/
and the goddess has won it, as she wins it every year.
:::
