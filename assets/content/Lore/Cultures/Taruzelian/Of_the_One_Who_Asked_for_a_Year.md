**Of the One Who Asked for a Year** is the roll the houses of the coast read aloud on the three nights of the [Festival of the Silent Passage]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, when the temples of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] close their doors to the living and the family keeps its dead at home, with a black candle in the window. The head of the house reads one part each night, at the table, with the household's ledger open beside the roll. The first night ends when the hero leaves the third god's altar, the second night when he leaves the shut door of the temple for the last time, and the third night at the close. Children of the house learn to say the refrain of the second night, _He was not even refused_, before they can read it.

It is the coast's great poem of death. [numaru kadumi]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=taru}, the greatest lender of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] in the days when the Vylarian _therkos_ (governor) sat in the city, learns that he will die before the harvest with his book open. A debt a dead man owes falls on his house, and a house whose debts all come due at once is finished, so he does not ask the gods for his life. He asks for one year to close the book. He buys three things from three gods—eloquence from [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]], a quiet month from [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]] and a fast road from [[lore-pozerasdty|Pozeras]]—and pays for each. Then he sits three nights at the shut door of the temple of [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]], raising his offer each night. The Silent Judge does not answer him at all, and in a land where refusing to haggle is the deepest insult, the poem holds the god's silence as the one thing no Taruzelian can do anything with. He comes home and asks his daughter, [rumukuya kadumi]{.name data-type="being/npc" lang=taru}, to stand surety for what he cannot close.

The roll is copied in every house that can afford a copyist and recited from memory in many that cannot. The houses of Besikar read the daughter's name as Rumukuya, which is a name for a woman who stands for another, and some houses of [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]] give her another name and make her the son's wife instead. The Threshold Keepers of the faith teach what the roll shows, that Sélmoros accepts no offering made in bargaining and grants no longer life; they do not read the roll in the temples, and they do not forbid it in the houses.

: Of the One Who Asked for a Year {#roll}

::: {.verse form=roll lang=en}
[The first night.]{.direction}/
Of the one who asked for a year, and of the year he asked for,/
of Numaru Kadumi, who lent to the coast, and the coast paid him./
His name was read in every harbor where a name is read./
His seal closed a bargain at Besikar and held it at Sazumun./
He lent to the rope-walk, and the rope-walk paid him in rope./
He lent to the salt-pans, and the salt-pans paid him in salt./
He lent to the captains, and the captains paid him in voyages,/
and the voyages came home, and he lent again./
He lent to the _therkos_, who did not pay him,/
and he did not lend to the _therkos_ twice./
There was no house on the quay that had not stood in his book,/
and no house that had stood in his book was ashamed of it.

In the month of the first sailing, in the year of his greatness,/
a physician came to him from Bazunel, from the school of the tides./
She put her hand on the side of his chest, and her ear on his back./
She looked at his eyes, and at the nails of his hands./
She said: Numaru, you will not see the harvest./
She said: Close what you can. Do it before the hot months./
He paid her what she asked, and did not push back her price,/
because a man does not haggle with the one who tells him the date.

He sat that night in the counting-room with the book open./
He sat with the lamp, and the book, and his daughter, who kept it with him./
He read the lines that were open, and she read them back to him./
Twelve ships were at sea with his money in their holds,/
and the soonest would be home at the harvest, and the last at the year's end./
Forty loans were out on the quay, and none of them fell due before the summer./
Three houses owed him more than they could pay in a season,/
and he owed the temple of Ólvenia for the gift he had promised.

He said: Daughter, if I die at the harvest with this book open,/
every man who owes me will wait to see if you can call him./
Every man I owe will call you in the week of the burial./
The ships will come home to a house that is being sold./
The loans will fall due to a name the quay has not read yet./
Daughter, a man can die. A man is permitted to die./
A man is not permitted to die owing./
I will not ask the gods for my life. A life is not a thing that is lent./
I will ask them for one year./
In a year I can close the book.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He asked for a year.

He went first to the temple of Ólvenia, the Bountiful One,/
where the silks hang down from the beams, and the gain is laid on the altar./
He carried the gift he owed her, and set it down, and was out of her debt./
Then he carried a second gift, a tree of gold with fruit of gold,/
and he said: Lady, I am asking for a year./
He said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

The priestess of Ólvenia said for the goddess:/
Numaru, I am the giver of gain, and a year is not gain./
A year is not mine. I bless what a year brings home./
I can sell you this: a mouth to call your debts so sweetly/
that the man who pays you will thank you for asking,/
and nobody who owes you will be shamed when you call./
That I sell, and the price is the tree of gold, and it is not too high./
He did not push back the price. He took the mouth./
He went out of her temple with a sweet mouth and no year.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He asked for a year.

He went to the hill of Brazemas, where the copper goes up into the cloud,/
where the storm comes down the copper into the room under the hill,/
and the priests write down where it struck and what it said./
He carried the first portion of his meal, and wine, and his seal./
He said: Thunderer, I am asking for a year./
He said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

The priest of Brazemas read the strike and said for the god:/
Numaru, I am the storm, and a year is not a storm./
A year is not mine. I break it into seasons, and I do not sell the whole./
I can sell you this: a quiet month on the sea, at the price of a vow,/
so that your twelve ships come home together, fast, before their time,/
and the vow will bind you, and your house, and the hulls you lend on./
He made the vow. The roll does not say what the vow was./
The houses of the coast keep it, and do not say it either./
He went down from the hill with a quiet month and no year.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He asked for a year.

He went to the shrine of Pozeras on the road out of the city,/
where the road leaves the gate and the sea-road leaves the harbor,/
and the Wayfarer stands with his staff where the two roads part./
He carried a lamp for the road, and bread for the road, and his sandals./
He said: Wayfarer, I am asking for a year./
He said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

The keeper of the shrine said for Pozeras:/
Numaru, I am the road, and a year is not a road./
A road goes somewhere. A year goes on./
I can sell you this: a fast road for every letter you send,/
so that your callings reach Sazumun in a day and Kudumel in two,/
and your answers come back before the man who sent them has slept./
The price is a sailing, the next you would have made yourself,/
which you will not make. He paid it. He was not going to make it./
He went back through the gate with a fast road and no year.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He asked for a year./
He bought a mouth, a month and a road,/
and he paid for every one, and none of them was a year.

[The second night.]{.direction}/
He went last to the temple of Sélmoros, at the edge of the city,/
where the tombs go down in rows into the dark below the hill,/
where the gate is black and the vines on it are bones./
It was night, and the door was shut, as the doors are shut tonight,/
and the Threshold Keepers were inside with the dead, and nobody else./
He sat down on the step outside the door./
He did not knock. You do not knock on that door.

He said to the door: Silent Judge, I am asking for a year./
He said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high./
The door did not answer./
He said: I will open the price, then, since you will not./
A tenth of what I hold, the tenth part of every line in my book,/
and the gift entered in my name in every harbor that reads it./
The door did not answer./
He sat on the step till the morning, and the door did not answer,/
and in the morning he went home and ate and slept.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He was not refused.

On the second night he came back to the step./
He said to the door: Silent Judge, I have heard no price from you./
He said: A man who does not push back has not been offered enough./
I will raise it. Half of what I hold./
Half of every line in my book, half of the ships at sea,/
half the house I was born in, and the gift entered in my name./
The door did not answer./
He sat on the step till the morning, and the door did not answer,/
and in the morning he went home and did not eat.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He was not refused.

On the third night he came back to the step./
He did not sit. He stood in front of the door with his seal in his hand./
He said: Silent Judge, I will tell you how a bargain is made./
He said: The seller opens, and nobody expects the price to stand./
The buyer pushes back, and the pushing back is the true beginning./
They sit, and they drink, and they argue, and they learn each other./
Then a fair price is named, and they clasp, and the clasp holds./
A man who will not push back has insulted the man who offered./
I am offering. I will offer all of it./
All that I hold, every line, every ship, every house, and my name,/
my seal, which is the name, and the house's name after me./
I will go out of this city with nothing and borrow my bread./
For a year. One year. Push it back. Push it back, and we will begin.

The door did not answer./
The door did not push back the price./
The door did not name a price of its own./
The door did not say: it is too high./
The door did not say: it is too low./
He stood until the morning, and the door did not answer,/
and the lamps in the windows of the city went out one by one.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He was not even refused.

Then Numaru knew what the woman of the hulls knew at the gate,/
what every child of the coast is told on this night, at this table./
There is one who keeps the count and does not bargain./
He does not refuse, because a refusal is the beginning of a bargain./
He does not cheat, because a cheat must first make a promise./
He does not lend, and so he never calls,/
and nobody stands in his book, because he keeps no book with anyone./
He keeps the count of the taken. He takes. That is the whole of it./
Numaru put his seal back in his coat./
He went down the steps of the temple, and the sun came up on the harbor.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He was not even refused.

[The third night.]{.direction}/
He came home with a sweet mouth, and a quiet month, and a fast road,/
and no year, and the hot months coming./
He sat in the counting-room with the book open,/
and his daughter Rumukuya sat across from him with the lamp.

He used the mouth. He called the forty loans before their time,/
and every man he called thanked him for asking, and paid what he could,/
and none of them was shamed, and none of them could pay it all./
He used the month. He had the vow, and the storm kept it./
The twelve ships came home together, before their time, in the quiet,/
and their cargoes were sold on one quay in one week,/
and the price fell, because twelve cargoes landed in the same week./
He used the road. He sent his callings to Sazumun and Kudumel,/
and the answers came back in a day, in two days,/
and the answers were: we will pay at the harvest. We will pay at the year's end.

He closed what he could./
He paid the temple of Ólvenia, and the priests of the storm, and the keeper of the road./
He paid the rope-walk for the rope, and the sail-loft for the sails./
He forgave the debt of a widow on the north quay, and wrote that he forgave it,/
so that nobody could say afterward that it had been forgotten./
The three houses that owed him more than a season could pay/
still owed him more than a season could pay./
The book was not closed. The book was smaller.

For three things a dying man can close,/
and for four he cannot close at all:/
he can close what he owes to the gods, because they name their prices;/
he can close what he owes to a man who is standing in front of him;/
he can close a gift, by writing that he gave it;/
he cannot close what is owed to him by a man who is not yet able to pay,/
and he cannot make the harvest come before the harvest,/
and he cannot make the year's end come in the summer,/
and he cannot sit across the table from himself and stand for his own name.

In the hottest week of the summer he said to his daughter:/
Rumukuya, I asked for a year, and I was not even refused./
What is left in this book will be open when I am dead./
Every man who owes me will wait to see if you can call him./
Every man I owe will look at you in the week of the burial./
I am asking you to stand for me./
Put your name beside mine on every open line./
When they call, say: I stand for him. When they wait, say: I am waiting./
It is a year's work, the year I did not get./
I am asking you for it. Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

His daughter did not name a price./
She took the pen and wrote her name beside his on every open line,/
forty lines, twelve lines, three lines, the widow's line closed and crossed,/
and she said: I stand for him./
She said: There is no price, father. You do not haggle with your surety.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
She stood for him.

He died in the month of the harvest, the month the physician gave him,/
with the window open on the harbor and the ships in it./
They carried him to the edge of the city, to the rows below the hill,/
and the Threshold Keepers took him in at the black gate, and the door shut,/
and whatever he said to the Silent Judge then, the roll does not say./
The roll is not permitted to say. Nobody has come back to read out the count.

Rumukuya Kadumi went down to the quay the morning after,/
and the men who owed her father waited to see if she could call them,/
and the men her father owed looked at her across the quay./
She called, and she waited, and she paid, and she called again./
At the harvest the first house paid. At the year's end the second./
The third house paid in the month of the first sailing,/
a year to the day from the day the physician came from Bazunel./
She drew a line under the last line in the book,/
and she closed it, and she put her own seal on the cover.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
He asked for a year./
He did not get it./
She paid it.

Of the one who asked for a year, and of the year he asked for:/
his name is read in every harbor where a name is read,/
not because he lent to the coast, though he lent to the coast,/
not because he went to the door, though he went to the door,/
but because his book was closed, and it was closed by the one who stood for him./
Whoever reads this on the third night, close the roll./
Close the book that lies open beside it on the table./
Look at the open lines. Ask who will stand for them./
Then put out the candle in the window, and go to bed.
