**When the Sea Owed Nothing**, _mani zu fusanam_, is the roll the First of the Council of [[place-besikar2|Besikar]] reads aloud from the head of the breakwater at [the Harbor Due]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} (_besiku yutalush_, the harbor's payment). The rite falls on the first fair morning after the [Thunderhead Vigil]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, when the sailing season opens, and the whole [Guild Quarter]{.name data-type="place/site"} turns out for it. The roll tells how the sea came first and owed nothing, how the people lived on their hulls because no shore would keep them, and how a woman of the hulls, [Tanaduya]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, asked three gods for a harbor and was refused by all three. She then took on a debt to the sea that the sea never agreed to, and set the first stones of the breakwater. The roll closes on the rite: each house of the city carries a stone down to the breakwater, and the First splits a tally stick, gives one half to the strongroom of the [[affiliation-merazuni|Merazuni]] and throws the other into the harbor mouth.

The roll is a work of the [Recovered Compacts]{.name data-type="lore"} of the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]], the Council of the Guild-Wardens. Its copyist, [Helarum Kubasuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, set it out in the Confederation's second year from an older roll kept in the [Temple of Two Fires]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]], and Besikar has held the Harbor Due every spring since. His note at the foot of the roll says what he changed. The Kudumel roll names the storm [[lore-bazhramisdty|Bazhrámish]], as the Ashaian rite does, and the copyist wrote [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]], as the coast says it; the temple at Kudumel still reads its own roll with its own name. Where the Kudumel roll is broken, in the catalog of the crews who carried the stones, he filled what he could from the Vezakuni's register of the oldest houses, and his own house is among them. The [[affiliation-tevran|Tevran Maldor]], the Vilérian college of guilds, calls the Harbor Due an invention of the Recovered Compacts program, and the temple has not shown its roll to the Tevran's scholars.

The hill tribes of the larger islands tell the story too, at their fires, and in their telling Tanaduya is a hill woman who came down to the shore with a goat-hair rope. The roll does not mention the hills. At Besikar the First reads the long lines, and the houses on the stones answer the two lines everyone knows: _It gave, and it took back_, and at the close, _We set, and we set again._ The gods stand in the roll as the coast addresses them: Brazemas the Thunderer on his hill, [[lore-pozerasdty|Pozeras]] the Wayfarer on the road over the water, and [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]], the Silent Judge, at his gate.

: Mani zu fusanam {#roll}

::: {.verse form=roll lang=en}
When the sea owed nothing,/
when nothing was owed to the sea,/
when no rope was knotted to a ring in a stone,/
when no stone was set in the water for a rope to be knotted to,/
when no name was written, because there was nobody to lend to,/
and nobody lent, because there was nowhere to stand and be paid,/
the sea was the whole of the country,/
and the country was water, and the water was the sea.

It gave, and it took back./
It gave the fish to the net, and it took back the net./
It gave the wind to the cloth, and it took back the mast./
It gave a sandbar at evening, and at morning it took back the sandbar./
It gave a child at the oar, and in the winter it took back the child./
It gave, and it took back, and it kept no count of either,/
for the sea keeps no book, and nothing is entered against it.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
It gave, and it took back.

There were islands, but the islands were the sea's./
They stood in it the way a post stands in a market, and nobody owned the post./
There were bays, but the bays were the sea's./
The swell came into them at night and lifted whatever lay there and set it down broken./
So the people lived on their hulls,/
and a house was whatever floated, and a family was a crew./
At dusk they lashed hull to hull in the lee of a headland,/
and at dawn they cut the lashings, because the wind had changed.

For three things the people of the hulls were poor,/
and for four they had no rest:/
for a child born on a deck that was never dry,/
for a grandmother given to the water, which would not keep her where they put her,/
for grain stored in a hold that the spray found by the third day,/
and for a debt that could never be called,/
because the debtor drowned, and the lender drowned, and the hull went down with the count.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
It gave, and it took back.

Among the hulls was a woman who kept the count for her crew,/
Tanaduya, who held fast to the bottom./
She was the first to tie a stone to a rope and drop it over the side,/
and the stone found the sand, and the hull swung on it and stayed./
The others laughed, and in the morning they stopped laughing,/
because the wind had changed in the night and her hull was where she had left it./
That is her name: she held fast to the bottom./
That is the first thing a Taruzelian held that the sea did not give.

She rowed into the bay where the swell breaks on the long spit,/
the bay that is the [Grand Harbor]{.name data-type="place/feature"} now, that was nothing then./
She looked at the spit, and she looked at the water behind it./
She said: Here a hull could lie through the winter./
She said: Here a child could be born on a floor that is dry./
She said: Here a debt could be called, because here the debtor will still be standing./
She said: I will ask for it./
She said: Somebody owns this water, and whoever owns it can be asked a price.

She went up the hill above the bay, in the storm, at night./
She carried the first portion of the crew's meal in a bowl with a lid on it./
She set it on the rock where the lightning had struck the year before,/
and she lifted the lid, and the rain filled the bowl./
Brazemas came down on the hill in the light that breaks a tree,/
and she did not lower her eyes, because she had come to bargain./
She said: Lord of the storm, I am asking for a harbor./
She said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

Brazemas said: Woman of the hulls, the sea is not mine./
Brazemas said: I blow on it. I do not hold it./
I can sell you a quiet month, at the price of a vow,/
and the vow will bind you, and your children, and the hull you stand on./
I will not sell you a harbor. I have no harbor to sell./
Go to the one who owns the sea.

[The Kudumel roll reads Bazhrámish here, and in the line that follows.]{.direction}

She went down from the hill with the bowl empty./
She did not buy the quiet month. A month was not what she had come for.

She went out on the water, in a calm, at noon./
She stood in the bow and called the god of the voyage,/
Pozeras the Wayfarer, who keeps the road from one shore to the next./
Pozeras came over the water as the road comes, without a mark on it,/
and stood where the sea was flat as a floor./
She said: Wayfarer, I am asking for a harbor./
She said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

Pozeras said: Woman of the hulls, the sea is not mine./
Pozeras said: I go over it. I do not own it./
Every road I keep runs out of a harbor and into one,/
and I have never yet owned either end./
I can sell you a road, at the price of a sailing,/
and the road will take you away from here, which is what roads do./
I will not sell you a harbor. Go to the one who owns the sea.

She went back to the bay with her hands empty./
She did not buy the road. A road away was the one thing the hulls already had.

She went down into the water, the way the drowned go down,/
at night, with no light, holding the rope of her stone./
She went down to the black gate where the drowned stand in a line,/
where Sélmoros keeps the count of everything the sea has taken back,/
because she said: the one who keeps the count of the taken/
must know whose the sea is./
She stood before the gate, and the drowned of her own crew stood with her./
She said: Silent Judge, I am asking for a harbor./
She said: Name your price, and I will tell you it is too high.

The Silent Judge did not answer./
He did not open a price, and he did not refuse one./
She waited the length of a tide, and he did not answer./
She went up the rope with her lungs burning, and she knew what she knew./
There is one who keeps the count and does not bargain./
That is the first thing a Taruzelian learned that no price can buy.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
It gave, and it took back.

She stood on the long spit, in the morning, in the wind./
She spoke to the sea itself, because no one else would sell it./
She said: Sea, I am asking for a harbor./
She said: Name your price./
The sea did not name a price./
The sea did not push back the price she had not named./
The sea has no name of its own to stand behind a word,/
and nothing it says can be written down and read to it afterward./
It came in, and it went out./
It gave, and it took back.

Then Tanaduya said what the city says./
She said: You will not sign. Then I will sign for both of us./
She said: You will not owe me. Then I will owe you./
I will owe you this bay every year that I hold it./
I will pay you every year, though you never asked,/
and you will take back what you take back, and I will set it again,/
and the debt will stand open, and while it stands open the harbor stands./
A debt that is paid to one who never signed/
is the one debt that cannot be broken, because nobody can break it but me.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
We set, and we set again.

She untied her stone from her rope,/
the stone that had held her hull when the wind changed,/
and she carried it out along the spit as far as the spit went,/
and she set it in the water at the end of the spit, and it stood./
That was the first stone of the breakwater./
That stone is the head of the stones, where the First stands today.

The crews came to see what she was doing, and they carried stones./
The crew of the hull came, who are the [Jakuduni]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"},/
and they carried the stones on their shoulders from the headland./
The crew of the rigging came, who are the [[affiliation-rusakuni|Rusakuni]],/
and they made the sledges and the ropes for the stones too big to carry./
The crew of the reef came, who are the [[affiliation-hitusuni|Hitusuni]],/
and they knew where the bottom was firm and where it would swallow a stone./
The crew of the partners came, who are the [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]],/
and they kept the count of who had carried what, so that nobody was cheated./
[…] came, who are the […],/
and they […] the stones […] in baskets of reed./
The crew of the counters came, who are the [Kubasuni]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"},/
and they counted the stones as they were set, and wrote the count down./
Tanaduya's own crew came last, who are the [[affiliation-tanaduni|Tanaduni]],/
and they set the stones the others brought, one on another, all summer.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
We set, and we set again.

In the first winter the sea took back half the stones,/
and in the spring they set them again./
In the second winter the sea took back a third of the stones,/
and in the spring they set them again./
In the third winter the sea took back a fourth,/
in the fourth a fifth, in the fifth a sixth,/
in the sixth a handful, and they set them again./
In the seventh winter the sea took back one stone,/
one stone from the head of the stones, beside the first,/
and in the spring they set it again,/
and that was the year the harbor was./
A hull lay behind the stones all winter, and in the spring it was where they had left it./
A child was born on a floor that was dry./
A debt fell due, and the debtor was standing there, and he paid it.

On the first morning of the eighth year Tanaduya took a stick of olive wood./
She cut a notch in it for every stone, seven summers of notches,/
and she split the stick down its length, notch from notch, the way a tally is split,/
so that each half holds the whole count and neither half is good without the other./
She kept one half in the hut on the shore where the counters kept their writing./
She carried the other half to the head of the stones,/
and she threw it into the harbor mouth, and the sea took it./
She said: That is your half. Bring it back when you want to settle./
She said: Until you bring it, the count is open,/
and while the count is open, I owe you, and the harbor is mine.

The sea has never brought it back./
Every year the count is open, and every year the harbor stands.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
We set, and we set again.

Tanaduya grew old behind her stones./
She saw her grandchildren's children born on floors that were dry./
She saw a house built where her hull had lain, and a hall where the house had been./
She saw debts called and paid, and loans made on the names of the dead,/
because the dead had houses now, and the houses stood./
When she was dying she was carried out to the head of the stones./
She asked them to give her to the water, the way the hulls had given their dead./
They would not. They said: the sea has taken back enough of you./
They set her under the first stone, in a cut they made in the rock below it,/
and they set the stone on top of her again.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
It gave, and it took back.

Every winter since, the sea takes back a stone from the head of the stones./
Every spring the city sets it again./
It has never yet taken back the first stone./
The roll does not say that it never will./
The sea keeps no book, and nothing is entered against it./
The city keeps the book. The count is open./
The stone will go, some winter, and the city will set it again.

First of the city, when the Thunderer has spoken on the hill,/
on the first morning that the swell lies down,/
go out to the head of the stones in your plain coat, without your seal./
Let the houses come behind you, each with a stone,/
the great houses with a stone that four men carry,/
the small houses with a stone a child can carry,/
and let no house come without one, and let no house carry another's./
Cut the tally for the stones the sea took back this winter,/
and the stones the city set again in their place./
Split it down its length, notch from notch./
Give one half to the strongroom. Give the other to the harbor mouth./
Say what Tanaduya said: Bring it back when you want to settle./
Do not wait for an answer. There will be no answer./
Go back along the stones to your seal and your coat and your counsels,/
and let the houses set their stones where the winter took them.

[The houses:]{.speaker}/
We set, and we set again.

When the sea owed nothing,/
when nothing was owed to the sea,/
the sea was the whole of the country./
The sea owes nothing still./
We owe it the harbor, and we pay./
It gives, and it takes back./
We set, and we set again.

[Below the last line, in the copyist's hand:]{.direction}/
Here ends _When the Sea Owed Nothing_, one roll, complete./
Copied by Helarum Kubasuni, copyist of the Recovered Compacts,/
from the roll of the Temple of Two Fires at Kudumel, which is old,/
in the second year of the Confederation, in the month of the first sailing./
Where the old roll says Bazhrámish, I have written Brazemas, as the coast says it./
Where the old roll is broken, I have left the break,/
except in the crews, where I have written the houses the register gives as oldest, as far as the register goes./
The copy was paid for by the Kubasuni, and the gift is entered with the Vezakuni./
Whoever reads this at the stones, let him read it as it stands./
Whoever changes a line, let the sea bring back its half of the tally on the day he is short.
