Vetrnaetr, the Winter Nights, marks the late-autumn turn toward winter. Livestock that cannot be kept through the scarcity are slaughtered in a great blót, meat is preserved, bones are read, and the dead are honored. Sólrún receives the greatest portion and Ódvar is invoked for the coming long dark. A hall’s búvördr seats its honored elders by the door and serves them first; a household unable to provision them must seek help from other kin and halls. The season also permits the howe-wake under a völva’s customary leave, without giving private revelation the authority of living evidence or a ting judgment.
