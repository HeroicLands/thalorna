**The Account of Tudzelv** is a _zeil_, the account a Reach-guide gives the winter council at [[place-nurvhrn|Nurvharn]] for a person who did not come back. A Reach-guide answers to the council for whoever does not return, and the account is how she answers: standing on the floor in the middle of the long house, in a fixed order, with the floor free to ask her anything. The council does not weigh what she meant. It hears where the ground failed and what she did.

Most accounts are heard and set down in the roll and not repeated. A few are kept whole and told again, in the closed months, by old guides to the Roadwalkers who will give accounts of their own some winter. This is the one most often kept. It is the account [Tudzelv]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Vrestun hearth gave for [Klemsi]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Vrestun hearth, her sister's son, lost at the [Knee]{.name data-type="place/feature"} on the upper shelf of [[place-jnanadvar|Jnanadvar]]. The telling here is the one [Rhistne]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Dzelbern hearth, an old Reach-guide, gives at his fire to the Roadwalkers of two hearths, and the other voices in it are kept as he gives them: [Venesh]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the Tsedvern hearth, who was on the rope, the floor, the Storm-reader and the Winter-speaker.

## The Account

Sit in. Not you, you are a Drover yet, you can listen from there. You three. One of you will stand on that floor some winter and say who you lost. Not this winter, I hope, and not the next. But one of you, and it is better you hear how it is done from me, at a fire, than find it out at the seam with your hearth-head looking at the ground.

This is the account of Tudzelv. It is kept because she gave it right. It is not kept because she was a good guide, though she was, or because what happened to her was strange, because it was not. It is kept for the order she said it in, and for one word she used that a guide in her place does not like to use. Listen for the word.

An account goes in an order. The road, and who gave it. The party, as they were on the rope. The weather, and whose it was. The ground, stage by stage. The place, and what the ground did. What you did. What you did not do. The body. Then you stop, and the floor asks. You do not say what you meant. Nobody asks you what you meant. The rope does not know what you meant, and the floor is the rope.

Tudzelv stood up at the seam in the weeks after midwinter, when the hearth-heads had been through the turn and the debts, and the Winter-speaker said, "Is there an account?" And she said, "There is an account." And the Winter-speaker said, "Give it." And this is what she gave, as it is kept.

"The road was the scholars' door, the late summer crossing. The turn gave it to the Vrestun hearth last winter, on this floor, and the Turn-keeper said it, and nobody had a word against it. The Vrestun hearth gave it to me. I was the Reach-guide on it.

"The party was five on the rope and two beasts. I was in front. Klemsi of the Vrestun hearth was second, a Roadwalker, in my charge, learning the shelf, and the crate-beast behind him on its own short rope. Venesh of the Tsedvern hearth was third, a Roadwalker, taken on for the season. Then the two lowland men, a sky-reader from the eastern janpodas and his servant. Then the other beast. Our Drovers kept the beasts below and turned down at the grass, as Drovers do. On the shelf, the party was the five and the two beasts. One beast carried the sky-reader's crate. It was wide.

"The weather was the Storm-reader's. I went to her the night before at the foot of the road, and she read the morning for us, and she said, good till evening, and the morning after that she did not know. We went at first light. It was her weather and it was good. It was good till evening, as she said.

"The ground. From the grass to the Wet Step, good. We walked the Wet Step after noon on the way up the next day, on the inside, as the roll says. The Butter Stone we went round with the stick, and I tried the lip on the gorge side, and the second stone was soft that summer, as the roll says, and I stepped over it, and made Klemsi try it after me. It was soft. That was the second day. On the third day there had been snow in the night, high, a hand deep, the first of the late summer, and the shelf above the Butter Stone was white.

"The place was the Knee. You know the Knee. The shelf goes round the rib of the mountain, and the man in front goes out of sight of the man behind. The outside of the bend is bare, because the wind takes the snow off it, and it is narrow, a beast's width and no more. The inside of the bend is wide, against the face. That morning the inside was under the night's snow, flat, a hand deep, wide enough for the crate.

"I tried the outside of the bend with the stick. It was firm. I looked at the inside. I looked at the snow on it. It lay flat. I did not try it with the stick. I put the party on the inside.

"I put the party on the inside because of the crate. The crate was wide. On the outside of the bend the crate would have touched the face, and the beast would have been pushed out to the edge, and I did not want the beast at the edge on the Knee. That is why. I say it once because you will ask. It is not part of the account.

"At the Knee I called. Klemsi answered. I went round the bend on the outside, and I was out of sight of him, as you are at the Knee, and the rope was short, as it should be at the Knee. Klemsi came round on the inside with the crate-beast behind him. I had him on the rope behind me and the beast behind him on its own short rope. I heard the snow go. It was not loud.

"The snow and the shelf went together. Behind the snow, along the face, there was a crack in the shelf, and the snow lay over it, and the inside of the bend came off the mountain from the crack out, the width of the inside, the length of two men. Klemsi was on it. The beast was on it. The crate went. The beast went. Klemsi went.

"The rope came tight on me. I was on the outside, on the firm ground, and I held. Venesh behind on the bend held. The rope held Klemsi for the time it takes to say it. Then the rope went over the new edge of the shelf, where it had broken, and the new edge was sharp, being new, and it cut. The rope parted at the edge. That is what the ground did.

"What I did. I held the rope. I went down on my belly on the outside of the bend and looked over. I saw him. He was on the scree, two ropes down, under the inside of the bend, with the beast. He did not move. I called him. He did not answer. I looked a long time, and he did not move. I took the party on to the Ear, past the Knee, on the outside, one at a time, the lowland men tied to me and to Venesh. Under the Ear the shelf is good. We waited there one night. In the morning I went back to the Knee alone and looked again and he had not moved. I took the party on to the summit. The ushtakas gave the lowland men the road-rite. I took the party over the scholars' door to the far side, as the turn said, and I came back.

"What I did not do. I did not try the inside of the bend with the stick. I did not look behind the snow. I did not go down for him. There was no rope that would reach on that shelf, and a Roadwalker and two lowland men are not a party to go down a gorge with, and I had a party on the shelf. I did not go down. I did not leave the party to go down.

"The body is not brought up. He is on the scree under the inside of the bend at the Knee, two ropes down, with the beast and the crate. The snow came on him before I came back over.

"I put the party on the inside of the bend. I put Klemsi on it. That is the account."

And she stopped.

That is the word. Did you hear it? Not the last one. _I put the party on the inside._ She said _I_. Our tongue has two ways to say a thing was done, you know that, you have been corrected for it since you could talk. One way is what you did yourself. The other is what you did as one of the party. When a party walks the inside of a bend, the party walks it. _We walked the inside_ is true. It is not a lie. It puts the walking on all five of them, and one of the five is on the scree and cannot say he did not choose it. Tudzelv did not say _we walked the inside_. She said _I put the party on the inside_, in her own form, for her own act. And she said _the party went round_, for the going, because the going was the party's. She kept each word where it belonged. Some guides do not. Do not be one of them.

Now the floor.

The Turn-keeper asked first, because it is his question. He said, "Who gave you the road?" She said, "The turn gave it to the Vrestun hearth. The Vrestun hearth gave it to me." He said, "That is so." It is always asked. It is always so. Answer it anyway.

Then an old woman of the Rhonzhel hearth asked from the floor, "Did you try the inside with the stick?" And Tudzelv said, "I did not. I said so. I looked at the snow." The old woman said, "Snow lies flat over anything." Tudzelv said, "It does." That was all.

Then the Storm-reader asked, "Was it my weather?" And Tudzelv said, "It was your weather, and it was good till evening, and the shelf went in the morning." The Storm-reader said, "Then it was not the weather." Tudzelv said, "It was not the weather." The Storm-reader asks so it is said on the floor that she did not close the road. It matters to her. Give it to her plain.

Then a hearth-head of the Hvurnash hearth asked, "Was the call made at the Knee?" Because, you know the roll, at the Knee there is a Hvurnash name, and the call was not made. Tudzelv said, "It was made. I called. He answered." The Hvurnash hearth-head said, "That is so, then," and sat down.

Then Venesh stood. He was on the rope. A person who was on the rope may speak on the floor after the guide, as a witness. He said, "I was third on the rope. I say it as she says it. I say one thing more. She tried the outside of the bend with the stick. I saw her. She tried it twice."

And this is the part that is kept for you as much as the word is. Venesh meant it well. He was saying for her that she was careful. The floor heard him. And the Winter-speaker said, "That is said, and it is so." And the old woman of the Rhonzhel hearth said, from the floor, "The outside held. Nobody fell from the outside." And nobody said anything after that, and Venesh sat down. You see it. The floor does not weigh a guide by how careful she was where the ground held. It weighs where the ground failed, and what she did there. A witness who stands up to say what she meant has stood up for nothing. Stand up to say what you saw.

Then there was a long time with nobody asking. Klemsi's mother was on the floor. She was Tudzelv's sister. She did not ask anything. The floor waited for her, and she did not ask, and the Winter-speaker did not hurry her. Then she said one thing, which was, "Two ropes down?" And Tudzelv said, "Two ropes. Under the inside of the bend. On the scree with the beast." And her sister said, "Then we will need three," and sat down. That is not a question. It is kept anyway.

Then a hearth-head of another hearth, I will not say which, asked the last question, and it is the one that made the account a model. He said, "You said _I put the party_. You walked on the outside. The party walked the inside. Why do you say it in your own form?"

And Tudzelv said, "Because I chose the inside. Myself. The party walked where I put it. If I say _we_, I put it on Klemsi, and he did not choose it. I chose it."

He said, "The crate was wide."

She said, "That is why I chose it. It is not part of the account."

And he sat down, and that was the floor.

Then the Winter-speaker asked, "Is there any word against the account?" and there was none, and he said, "Then it is the account, as given." And the council said what the council says. You know it already, you have heard it said over other hearths: the Vrestun hearth draws no road until the council says so, and the council wants work, or a witness, or the body. The witness was Venesh, and he had witnessed, and it was not enough, because what he witnessed was the outside of the bend. So the council wanted the body. That is all it said. It did not say Tudzelv was a bad guide. It does not say that. It did not say she would not be a Reach-guide again. It does not say that either. It said the hearth draws no road, and sat on.

You know what that is for a hearth. No road for the Vrestun hearth, so no fee to the Vrestun fire, so the Vrestun hearth lives that winter and the summer after on what the other hearths owe it and on the grass. And the other hearths owed it, as it happened, because the Vrestun hearth had drawn the conch-door two winters running before, and the debts were said on the same floor that winter, and paid in barley. That is luck. Do not count on it.

That is the account. Now the rest, which is not the account, but it is told with it.

At the thaw, Tudzelv went down for him. The Bone-bringer that year was a woman of the Klemverd hearth, and she went, and Tudzelv went, and Klemsi's mother went and held the rope at the top with Venesh, and two of the Vrestun hearth. Three ropes, as her sister had said. They went up the shelf to the Knee before any party, on the first good day the Storm-reader gave them. It was the Storm-reader's weather and it was good.

The first time they went down it did not go. The snow from the winter was on the scree under the Knee, and the Bone-bringer went down one rope and stood on it and it moved, the whole slope under her, and she came up, and said, "Not this week." That is the Bone-bringer's word and it is not argued with, any more than the Storm-reader's. They went back down the shelf to the grass and waited.

The second time it was the end of the thaw, and the snow had gone off the scree, and the Bone-bringer went down with Tudzelv on the rope under her, two on one rope, and the mother and Venesh and the Vrestun people holding at the top. They found him where she had said, two ropes down, under the inside of the bend, with the beast, and what was left of the crate. The sky-reader's instruments were in the scree all round him, broken, brass, catching the sun. They did not bring those up. Somebody has asked me every time I tell this whether they brought those up, so I say it now: they left them. They brought him up. Tudzelv and the Bone-bringer brought him up, the second time they went down, two on one rope.

Before anything else was done the Bone-bringer looked at the place. She went to the inside of the bend, what was left of it, and looked at the face behind where the shelf had come away, and she found the crack running on along the face, farther, behind the next stretch of the inside where the shelf still was. She tried it with the stick and the stick went in. And that is the place, the _hozd_, the place he was found and what the ground did there, and she said it on the shelf, to the people on the rope, before they went down, so that it was said once on the ground before it was ever said on the floor: _the inside of the bend, where the shelf is widest and looks safest, has a crack along the face behind the snow._

You have heard that line. You have heard it every winter of your lives. Klemsi, of the Vrestun hearth. The Knee. The inside of the bend. Brought up by Tudzelv and the Bone-bringer, the second time they went down, two on one rope. And then: _The inside of the bend. The wide place. Look behind the snow._ The Bone-bringer adds that. It is not the entry. It is the warning. And there is a girl at the Rhonzhel fire now who stands up when the Bone-bringer comes to it, and says _the Knee, the inside of the bend_, and she has never been on the shelf.

The next winter the council gave the Vrestun hearth a road, because the body was back. It gave the hearth the scholars' door, and the hearth gave it to Tudzelv. The council does not say who the hearth gives it to. That is the hearth's.

And she walked the Knee on the outside of the bend, with the crate-beasts of a sky-reader's party going round on the outside behind her one at a time, touching the face, with a man at each beast's head to hold it in off the edge, which is slow, and which every guide on the scholars' door does now. The lowland men complain of it. Let them.

That is the account of Tudzelv. When yours comes, give it in that order. The road, and who gave it. The party, as they were on the rope. The weather, and whose it was. The ground, stage by stage. The place, and what the ground did. What you did. What you did not do. The body. Say _I_ for what you did yourself and _we_ for what the party did, and do not put your own choosing on the rope. And when a witness stands up to say you were careful, let him, and do not thank him.

Now go and grease something.
