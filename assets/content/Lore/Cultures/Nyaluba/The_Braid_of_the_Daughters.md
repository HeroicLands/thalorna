The title [[affiliation-nyalblion|Nyoli Ruwozi]], Daughter of the Lion, passes to the senior woman of the [Ruwozi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and makes her the paramount who convenes the [Great Lubemo]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} and speaks last. **The Braid of the Daughters** is the recital of the women who have carried it. On the first day of each Lubemo at [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]], when the woman who holds the title takes the convener's place, the griot of the Ruwozi recites her line of office to the delegations of all five clans, and the Ruwozi women of her delegation answer. A Ruwozi woman braids her lineage into her hair, so the women answer with a hand on the braid, and the recital speaks of each holder as a plait in it.

It is a recital and not a praise-song: each holder gets her name, the line she came from, the matter the record holds against her name, and what she said last at her last Lubemo, because the senior speaks last and a Ruwozi's last word is the one remembered. It is either right or wrong. Five of the six holders are dead, and the sixth, [Nzobu Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is the woman taking her place; the recital stops at her. The text below is the recital given at her fourth Lubemo. One plait of the braid carries a knot, a son of the third holder whose name the [[skill-griot|griot]] does not speak because he hunted a lion, and the recital marks the knot and leaves it tied. In the text below, the indented lines in italics are the answer of the Ruwozi women.

: The Braid of the Daughters {#recital}

Transcription of an oral recitation.

::: {.verse form=recital lang=en}
Five who walk together, hear me./
._We hear you, keeper._/
A woman of the lion is taking the place by the stone/
where the one who convenes sits, and where she speaks last./
Before she sits, the braid is said./
._Say the braid._/
I say it as it was put into my mouth./
._No longer, and no shorter._/
Women of the lion, put your hands on your hair./
._Our hands are on it._

There are six plaits in the braid of the Daughter./
._Six plaits._/
Five are finished and tied off./
One is still being plaited./
._One is still being plaited._

The first plait./
._The first._/
Who carried the name first?/
._Nyoli Ruwozi._/
What other name did she have?/
._The record holds none._/
The record holds the title and nothing under it./
Whatever her mother called her went with her mother./
._The title, and nothing under it._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._The swearing._/
Under the baobab in the lion's country,/
on the middle grass, nine generations back,/
she stood for the lion when the five swore./
._She stood for the lion._/
And what did she say?/
._Say it whole._/
"We are not building a kingdom./
We are agreeing not to destroy each other./
The rest is for our daughters to decide for themselves."/
._The rest is for our daughters._

And what did she say last, at her last Lubemo?/
._The same._/
The record holds that she said nothing new./
Asked to speak last, she said it again, the same words,/
and sat down, and the five went home./
._She said it again, and sat down._/
That is the first plait. It is tied off./
._It is tied off._

The second plait./
._The second._/
Who carried the name second?/
._[Kenzowa Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}._/
Of what line?/
._Of the senior line, the Daughter's daughter's daughter._/
She took the name when the first was put in the ground,/
and her braid was black when she took it/
and white when she gave it up./
._Black, and then white._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._The waiting._/
In her time the paramount of the crocodile was sick/
and could not walk from the river to the high stone./
The fourth finger says the five come back in their own bodies./
._In their own bodies._/
Some said: let his griot speak for him./
Some said: let his sister's son stand in his place./
Kenzowa said: "The finger says their own bodies./
We will wait for his."/
._We will wait for his._

So the Kolembozi carried their paramount on a frame of poles,/
up out of the water country, up the red escarpment,/
eleven days, and the Lubemo sat at the stone and waited,/
and nobody opened the matter of the herds,/
and nobody opened the matter of the boundaries,/
and the drums asked every morning: where is he?/
and the drums answered every evening: he is coming./
._Where is he? He is coming._/
On the twelfth day he was set down at the stone,/
and he spoke in his own voice, and it was thin,/
and the five said yes, and the Pact was said again./
._It was said again._

And what did she say last, at her last Lubemo?/
._"Wait for the one who is coming."_/
She said it, and sat down./
That is the second plait. It is tied off./
._It is tied off._

The third plait./
._The third._/
Who carried the name third?/
._[Sawemi Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}._/
Of what line?/
._Of the senior line, Kénzowa's brother's daughter._/
She had the name in the fourth generation down the braid,/
the year the baobab of Shendala died./
._The year Shendala's tree died._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._The cows for Shendala._/
When the drums said the baobab's people were walking,/
she sent cows to every house of Shendala,/
and the herders of the lion drove them to the road./
Some said: give them. They have lost a town./
Sawemi said: "Lend them./
A gift they would have to answer before they had a roof./
A loan they can answer when we are short."/
._Lend them. Answer when we are short._/
And a dry year came, in time, and they answered,/
every cow, and the calves of every cow,/
and the record holds it closed./
._It is closed._

Now put your hand on the third plait,/
and go down it, and stop./
._We stop._/
There is a knot here./
._There is a knot._/
Sawemi had three sons./
The record holds two of them./
._It holds two._/
The third went out on the lion's grass with a spear/
and came home with a lion's skin./
I do not say his name. It is not in my mouth./
It was taken out of the mouth of the griot of her time,/
and no griot since has put it back./
._It is not in your mouth._/
Touch the knot./
._We touch it._/
I do not untie it./
._You do not untie it._

What did Sawemi say of him?/
._The record holds one thing._/
She said: "He was mine. He is not the lion's./
Do what is done." And it was done,/
and she did not say his name again, before a griot or behind one./
._Before a griot or behind one._

And what did she say last, at her last Lubemo?/
._"Lend it. Do not give it."_/
She said it, and sat down./
That is the third plait. It is tied off, and it has its knot./
._Tied off, with its knot._

The fourth plait./
._The fourth._/
Who carried the name fourth?/
._[Hayeba Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}._/
Of what line?/
._Of the senior line, Sáwemi's daughter's daughter._/
She had the name three generations back from this stone,/
in the dry years, when the rain did not come/
and the grass stood up white and broke under the cattle./
._White grass._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._The dry war._/
The hungry came in from past the leopard's edge/
into the leopard's trees, with their children and no cattle,/
and they took from the leopard's fields, and the leopard called./
._The leopard called._/
A spear against one is a spear against five./
._Against one, against five._/
Hayeba called the regiments of the lion,/
and the regiments went, and the five did not lose./
._The five did not lose._

At the Lubemo after, the first matter was the war,/
because the first matter is always the war./
And Hayeba said: "The regiments are home./
The third finger says the grass, the rivers and the rains are shared./
That is what the third finger says."/
._That is what it says._/
Nobody asked: shared among whom?/
It is not entered, because it was not asked./
._It was not asked._/
I say what she said. I do not say what she meant./
A griot who says what she meant has not told it better./
._He has told it wrong._

And what did she say last, at her last Lubemo?/
._"That is what the finger says."_/
She said it, and sat down, and was quiet a long time,/
and the record holds the quiet too, because it was long./
._The quiet too._/
That is the fourth plait. It is tied off./
._It is tied off._

The fifth plait./
._The fifth._/
Who carried the name fifth?/
._[Temoyi Ruwozi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}._/
Of what line?/
._Of the senior line, Háyeba's daughter's daughter._/
There are people at this stone who saw her./
._We saw her._/
Then hear me close, because you will check me./
._We will check you._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._The taken brought home._/
In her time the slavers came out of the north/
to the leopard's edge, with ropes, and took people,/
and the leopard's regiments went after them in the dark/
and brought the taken home./
._Brought them home._/
At the Lubemo after, the Sengala stood the taken at the stone,/
every one, and Temoyi asked each one/
the three things a stranger is asked:/
"What is your name? Who is your father? Where did you sleep?"/
._Name, and father, and where you slept._/
And each one said it, and she said it back,/
and the five said it back after her,/
because a person who has been on a rope/
needs to hear their name in other mouths./
._In other mouths._

And what did she say last, at her last Lubemo?/
._"Say it back to them."_/
She said it, and sat down./
That is the fifth plait. It is tied off./
._It is tied off._

The sixth plait./
._The sixth._/
Who carries the name now?/
._Nzobu Ruwozi._/
Of what line?/
._Of the senior line, Témoyi's daughter._/
Her braid is black at the root and grey at the end./
She has sat in that place for three Lubemos./
This is the fourth./
._This is the fourth._

What does the record hold against her name?/
._What has been entered._/
Of the living I say only what has been entered,/
and the rest of her is not in my mouth yet./
._Not yet._/
And what will she say last?/
._We do not know it._/
Nobody knows it. That is why she speaks last./
._That is why._/
That is the sixth plait. It is not tied off./
._It is not tied off._

Six plaits. Count them./
._The swearing. The waiting. The cows, and the knot._/
._The dry war. The taken brought home. And what is not yet._/
That is the braid. It is the length it was given me./
._No longer, and no shorter._/
Who has checked it?/
._We have checked it. We know it too._

Then sit down, Daughter of the Lion,/
in the place where the one who convenes sits./
The others will speak, and you will speak last./
What you say last, I will carry./
And what is not in the braid yet/
._is for our daughters._
:::
