A [Sengala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} regiment is an age-set: a cohort that passes through initiation together, takes a regimental name and an age-set totem, and stays bound for life. Twice within living memory, slaving parties from [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethua]] reached the Sengala margin, and both were destroyed by the regiments before they could withdraw ([[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]]). **The Night Grass** is the memory-song of one of those two, held by the griot of the Sengala and named for the regiment that went: the [Night Grass]{.name data-type="affiliation"}, whose age-set totem is the nightjar, the bird that sits on bare ground in daylight and is not seen. It is sung at every Sengala initiation, to the cohort coming through, and the men of the Night Grass who are still living stand with the initiates and answer.

The song keeps the Sengala manner. It praises the [[affiliation-nyalbleop|leopard]]'s way, the wait and the strike from cover, and sets it against the lion's open challenge without ruling between them. It gives the slavers no names and no faces, as the Sengala give them none. It names the regiment's captain, [Rábesi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the people taken, and the one of them who did not come home, and its last part is the reception: each of the taken asked the three questions a stranger is asked and answered back by everyone. In the text below, the indented lines in italics are the answer of the regiment and the initiates.

: The Night Grass {#song}

Transcription of an oral memory-song.

::: {.verse form=memory-song lang=en}
Hear me, children of the leopard./
._We hear you, keeper._/
Hear me, cohort with the new scars./
._We hear you._/
Hear me, old men of the Night Grass, standing with them./
._We are standing._/
I carry what was put in my mouth/
the length it was given me./
._No longer, and no shorter._

What does the leopard do?/
._It waits._/
What does the leopard do when it is hungry?/
._It waits._/
What does the leopard do when the thing it wants is in front of it?/
._It waits, and then it does not wait._

The lion says: stand up in the open,/
let the one you fight see you coming./
._That is the lion's way._/
The leopard says: lie down in the grass,/
let the one you fight see nothing./
._That is the leopard's way._/
The song does not say which way is better./
The song says which way the Night Grass went./
._The leopard's way._

Who are the Night Grass?/
._The cohort that came through together._/
Under what bird?/
._The nightjar._/
What does the nightjar do?/
._Sits on the bare ground in the day, and nobody sees it._/
What does the nightjar do at dusk?/
._Gets up out of the ground where nobody saw it._

Who came out of the north?/
._The rope came._/
Say it again./
._The rope came._/
Out of the trees beyond the trees, past the edge of the leopard,/
the people of the rope came walking,/
with their spears and their bundles and their coils of rope,/
to the village at the edge of the tall trees/
in the dry season, when the men were with the herds./
._When the men were away._

The song does not say their names./
._They have none._/
The song does not say their faces./
._They have none._/
They are the rope. That is all the song gives them./
._That is all._

What did the rope do?/
._It took._/
It took seven out of the village at the edge of the trees,/
three women and a girl and three young men,/
and tied them neck to neck on one rope,/
and walked them north into the trees./
._Neck to neck, on one rope._

Who heard?/
._The drums heard._/
A boy ran from the village to the hill where the drum stands,/
and the drum on the hill spoke to the drum on the next hill,/
and that drum spoke to the next, and the next,/
down to the station in the middle, where the one sits/
whose name is not ours to know./
._It is not ours._/
And that one sent it out again to every hill,/
and before the sun was down the Night Grass knew./
._Before the sun was down._

Who called them?/
._Rábesi called them._/
Rábesi of the Night Grass, who had the cohort's scar/
and the cohort's word, and was the eldest of them by a season./
He said: "The rope has seven./
We are going to get them back."/
._We are going to get them back._

How did the Night Grass go?/
._Without a sound._/
They took no drum. They took no horn./
They took their spears and their water and a little food/
and went into the trees on the rope's track/
the way the leopard goes into the trees./
._The way the leopard goes._

Two days they followed./
The rope was slow, because the seven were slow,/
because one of the seven was a girl with sore feet/
and the rope does not wait for sore feet./
._The rope does not wait._/
On the third day, at dusk,/
the people of the rope cut thorn and made a pen/
and put the seven in it, still on the rope,/
and made their fire outside it, and sat down./
._Made their fire, and sat down._

What did the Night Grass do?/
._Lay down in the grass._/
How close?/
._Close enough to hear them eat._/
How long?/
._Three nights._

The first night the young ones said: "Now."/
Rábesi said: "Not now./
Now the rope is tight, and the people of the rope are awake./
If we go now, they will turn on the seven first./
Lie still."/
._Lie still._

The second night the young ones said: "Now. Now, Rábesi."/
Rábesi said: "Not now./
Now they are watching, because the second night is when a hunter comes./
Lie still. Be the ground."/
._Be the ground._

Who lay still?/
._The Night Grass lay still._/
The ants came, and they lay still./
The dew came down on their backs, and they lay still./
A man of the rope walked out from the fire to make water/
and made it in the grass an arm's length from [Tóbesho]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s head,/
and Tóbesho lay still./
._Tóbesho lay still._

The third night the young ones said nothing./
They had learned it./
And Rábesi watched the pen, and watched the fire,/
and saw the people of the rope stop watching,/
because two nights had come and nothing had come with them./
And he saw them loosen the rope from the seven's necks/
and tie it to their feet instead, so they could sleep./
._So they could sleep._/
And he said: "Now."/
._Now._

The song does not tell the striking long./
._It is not long._/
The nightjar gets up out of the ground at dusk./
The Night Grass got up out of the ground in the dark,/
all at once, on every side of the fire, without a sound/
until the sound was theirs./
And the people of the rope did not stand up again./
None of them went north. None of them went home./
._None went home._/
And the rope was cut where it was tied,/
at the feet, and not at the neck./
._At the feet, and not at the neck._

And who did not come home?/
._Say her._/
[Píkesa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, daughter of [Mbuka]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the girl with the sore feet./
She was on the rope three days with the others/
and on the second night in the pen she lay down/
and she did not get up./
The rope does not wait for sore feet./
._It does not wait._/
The Night Grass came on the third night./
They were one night late for Píkesa./
._One night late._/
The song says it. It does not hide it./
Rábesi said it himself, at the Lubemo:/
"I waited, and the six are home./
I waited, and Píkesa is not."/
._The six are home, and Píkesa is not._

Who carried her?/
._The Night Grass carried her._/
They did not leave her in the thorn./
They carried her the three days home on a frame of spears,/
and the six walked beside her with no rope on them,/
and that is how they came to the edge of the tall trees./
._That is how they came._

Now hear the coming home./
._We hear it._/
The village was at the edge of the trees, waiting,/
and the mothers, and the men come back from the herds,/
and the griot of the leopard, who had walked from the hill./
And the griot stood the six in front of the village/
and asked them what a stranger is asked/
when a stranger comes to the fire,/
because a person who has been on a rope/
is a stranger to the fire until the fire knows her again./
._Until the fire knows her again._

He asked the first./
"What is your name?"/
"[Fádewa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}."/
"Who is your father?"/
"[Kásimo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}."/
"Where did you sleep?"/
"In the thorn pen, on the rope."/
And the village said it back to her./
._Fádewa, daughter of Kásimo, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._

He asked the second./
"What is your name?"/
"[Bákeni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}."/
"Who is your father?"/
"[Jelo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}."/
"Where did you sleep?"/
"In the thorn pen, on the rope."/
._Bákeni, son of Jelo, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._

He asked the third./
._[Habishe]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, daughter of [Lóbezo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._/
He asked the fourth./
._[Sálumo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, son of [Zákesho]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._/
He asked the fifth./
._[Yámeto]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, son of [Kádewo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._/
He asked the sixth./
._[Wéndasi]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, daughter of [Panza]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who slept in the thorn, on the rope._

Then the griot asked the one on the spears./
._He asked her._/
"What is your name?"/
And Mbuka her father answered for her: "Píkesa."/
"Who is your father?"/
"I am."/
"Where did she sleep?"/
"In the thorn pen, on the rope. And she did not wake."/
And the village said it back to her./
._Píkesa, daughter of Mbuka, who slept in the thorn, on the rope, and did not wake._/
So that the rope would not have the last of her./
._The rope does not have the last of her._

Then the griot asked the six one more thing./
._One more thing._/
"Where will you sleep tonight?"/
And the six said: "Here."/
._Here._/
And the village said it back./
._Here._/
Say it again, cohort with the new scars./
._Here._

Rábesi is in the ground now, under the leopard's trees./
Tóbesho is old, and stands with you at this fire./
._He stands with us._/
Old men of the Night Grass, who is standing?/
._We are standing._/
Cohort with the new scars, what is your bird?/
._We do not know it yet._/
You will be given a bird tonight, and a name./
And if the rope comes,/
._we will lie down in the grass._/
And how long?/
._As long as it takes._/
And then?/
._And then not._

I have carried it the length it was given me./
._No longer, and no shorter._/
Who has checked it?/
._We have checked it. Some of us were there._/
Then it goes on to the next mouth./
Hear me, children of the leopard./
._We hear you, keeper._
:::
