A [[lore-nyalubaclt|Lobwezi]] town centers on its baobab, and a town whose baobab dies does not replant and does not stay: the [[lore-baobablobwezispr|guide]] has withdrawn and the pact with that ground has ended ([[affiliation-nyalbbaob|Baobab Totem]]). **The Song of the Two Dispersals** is the memory-song of the two towns this has happened to, [Bwelende]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} and [Shendala]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}. It is kept by the [[skill-griot|griot]] who is custodian of the baobab of [Lombesa]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, the town that took in households of both, and it is sung under that tree when a child whose line runs back to either town is named. The child is held up at the root, the griot calls, and the households answer.

The song is praise poetry in the griot's call and the gathered answer, and a record as well as praise, so it holds to its received length and names only what the Lobwezi griots hold. It names the households of each town and the towns they went to. It does not say why either tree died, because the griots do not hold that the dying had a cause anyone could have mended, and it passes no verdict on the one woman who stayed. In the text below, the indented lines in italics are the answer of those gathered.

: The Song of the Two Dispersals {#song}

Transcription of an oral memory-song.

::: {.verse form=memory-song lang=en}
Hear me, children of the tree that stands./
._We hear you, keeper._/
Hear me, grain and well and the slow word./
._We hear you._/
A child is at the root tonight./
Hold her up, so the tree can see her./
._She is held up. The tree sees her._/
I carry two trees that do not stand./
I carry them the length they were given me,/
no longer, and no shorter./
._Carry them. We know them too._

What does the tree do when it is old?/
._It stands._/
What does the tree do in the dry season?/
._It stands._/
What does the tree do when the town is gone to the fields?/
._It stands, and it waits for us._/
And what does a town do when its tree is dead?/
._It does not replant. It does not stay._/
Say it again, for the child./
._It does not replant. It does not stay._

The first tree is Bwelende's./
._Bwelende, Bwelende._/
That is before the five were five./
There was no Pact, and the drums spoke only for their own./
The lion kept the lion's grass,/
the crocodile kept its water, the eagle its high stone,/
and the leopard kept its trees and said nothing,/
and the baobab's people kept their wells and their grain/
and every one of them kept a feud warm./
._Before the five were five._

Bwelende's tree stood by the long well./
It was old when the well was dug./
Ten men holding hands went round it, and the eleventh/
stood on the root and laughed, because the hands did not meet./
._The hands did not meet._/
Its hollow held water through the dry season./
Its bees were the best bees between the rivers./
Its fruit hung like the heads of sleeping bats,/
and the children knocked them down with sticks/
and were beaten for it, and knocked them down again./
._Knocked them down again._

The griot of Bwelende was [Tálemo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}./
._Tálemo of the tree._/
He kept the tally at the root:/
which house had brought its offering, and which was owing,/
and he kept it to the last day./
._To the last day._

How did they know?/
._The tree told them._/
The rains came, and it did not leaf./
The rains came again, and it did not leaf./
The bees went out of it in one morning/
like smoke going out of a house./
The hollow that held water held dust./
And the bark that had been smooth as a cow's flank/
split down the side facing the well/
and showed the wood, and the wood was grey./
._The wood was grey._

The song does not say why./
._It does not say why._/
Nobody cut it. Nobody fed it wrong./
The tally was full. Tálemo kept it, and it was full./
Some things are not a debt./
._Some things are not a debt._

Then the town sat under the dead branches/
and talked, the way a town talks,/
in the order the town keeps, the old ones last,/
until the objections stopped./
All of them stopped but one./
._All but one._

The one was [Kíshela]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the [Kúbola]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"},/
an old woman with no teeth and all her children grown./
She said: "I was born under it. I will sit under it./
Go, and take the children. I am staying."/
._She stayed._/
The song does not say she was right./
The song does not say she was wrong./
It says she stayed, and it says her name./
._Kíshela, who stayed._

Then the houses went, and the song counts them./
Count them, children of the tree that stands./
._We count them._/
The Kúbola went to Lombesa./
._To Lombesa, to this tree._/
The [Lúsani]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Lombesa./
._To this tree._/
The [Bándala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to [Ndawelo]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, by the red pan./
._To Ndawelo._/
The [Ópala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Ndawelo./
._To Ndawelo._/
The [Mpanza]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to [Nyelambe]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, in the far grass,/
three days with the grain on their heads./
._Three days to Nyelambe._/
Five houses out of Bwelende,/
and Kíshela, who stayed./
._Five houses, and one who stayed._

Who came to Bwelende to help them carry?/
._Nobody came._/
Who drummed it to the river and the high stone?/
._Nobody drummed it._/
There were no five. Each kept its own./
A town of the baobab walked out of its own country/
and only the baobab's people met it on the road./
._Only the baobab's people._

And what did Tálemo carry?/
._He carried Bwelende._/
He did not carry grain. Let others carry grain./
He carried the fathers of Bwelende in his mouth,/
every line of every house, back to the first well,/
and the tally of the root to the last day./
He walked to Lombesa with nothing on his head./
._Nothing on his head, and Bwelende in his mouth._

He sat down under this tree./
The griot of Lombesa sat down across from him./
And Tálemo gave Bwelende into Lombesa's keeping,/
line by line, before the people of both,/
before people who already knew it./
._Line by line._/
He gave it the length it was given him./
._No longer, and no shorter._/
And when he was done, the dead of Bwelende had a tree again./
._They had a tree again._

The second tree is Shendala's./
._Shendala, Shendala._/
That is in the Pact's time,/
the fourth generation down the braid from the swearing./
._Four, and not one fewer._

Shendala's tree stood on the rise above the sorghum./
It was so old that the first griot of Shendala/
said it was old when his grandfathers came,/
and his grandfathers said it of theirs./
._Old when the grandfathers came._/
Its shade at noon would take a herd./
Its root came up out of the ground like a cow kneeling,/
and that was where the offerings were laid./
._Where the cow kneels._

The griot of Shendala was [Néntoli]{.name data-type="being/npc"}./
._Néntoli of the tree._/
She kept the tally at the root/
and she kept it to the last day./
._To the last day._

How did they know?/
._The tree told them._/
It leafed late, and the leaves came small./
It leafed late again, and the leaves came smaller./
The fruit hung empty, light as gourds,/
and the children knocked them down and found nothing in them/
and nobody beat them for it./
Then a wind came in the dry season, not a great wind,/
and the eastern limb came down across the sorghum,/
and the wood in the break was grey./
._The wood was grey._

The song does not say why./
._It does not say why._/
The tally was full. Néntoli kept it, and it was full./
._Some things are not a debt._

And the town sat under the limbs that were left/
and talked until the objections stopped,/
and nobody stayed./
._Nobody stayed._/
Néntoli asked who would stay, three times,/
because Kíshela's name was in her mouth./
Three times nobody answered./
._Nobody answered._

Now: who came to Shendala to help them carry?/
._The five came._/
The drums took it from Shendala to the high stone/
and from the high stone to the river/
and from the river to the trees and the lion's grass,/
and in two days the five knew./
._In two days the five knew._

Who came up from the river?/
._The crocodile came._/
Kolembozi of the reed canoe,/
who took the grain of Shendala downriver to the wells/
and asked for nothing, and took nothing./
._Asked for nothing._

Who came down from the high country?/
._The eagle came._/
Ngonzi of the red escarpment,/
who know what to do with stone,/
and raised the cairn on the rise where the limb fell./
._The cairn on the rise._

Who came in from the edge of the trees?/
._The leopard came._/
Sengala of many tongues,/
who walked beside the old ones on the road/
and talked to them all the way, so that the road was short./
._So the road was short._

Who came from the standing water?/
._The baobab's people came. Their trees stayed where they are._/
Lobwezi of the other towns,/
who came with empty hands to carry the full ones./
._Empty hands, to carry._

Who stood on their own ground and sent?/
._The lion sent._/
Ruwozi of the cattle lent cows to the houses of Shendala/
so that they would not come to a new tree with nothing to give,/
and called them back in a dry year, as is right./
._Lent, and called back._

Was it in the terms?/
._It was not in the terms._/
Count them on one hand./
._One hand, five fingers._/
Is there a finger for a dead tree?/
._There is no sixth finger._/
They came anyway./
._They came anyway._

Then the houses went, and the song counts them./
Count them, children of the tree that stands./
._We count them._/
The [Málazuli]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Lombesa./
._To Lombesa, to this tree._/
The [Kómaludi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Lombesa./
._To this tree._/
The [Úndala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Ndawelo, by the red pan./
._To Ndawelo._/
The [Péndala]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Nyelambe, in the far grass./
._To Nyelambe._/
The [Órindi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Nyelambe./
._To Nyelambe._/
The [Tálamba]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} went to Ndawelo./
._To Ndawelo._/
Six houses out of Shendala,/
and nobody stayed./
._Six houses, and nobody stayed._

And what did Néntoli carry?/
._She carried Shendala._/
Before she went, she sat down at the root where the cow kneels/
and said the dead of Shendala to the dead tree,/
every line of every house, back to the first griot,/
so that the tree would hear its people one more time./
It took her from noon to dark./
._From noon to dark._/
Then she stood up and said: "That is all of you."/
And she walked to Lombesa, and sat down under this tree,/
and gave Shendala into Lombesa's keeping,/
line by line, before people who already knew it./
._Line by line._/
And the dead of Shendala had a tree again./
._They had a tree again._

Now hear what Lombesa did./
._We hear it._/
Lombesa had its fields, and it had its wells,/
and its fields were the size its houses needed./
Then the houses of Bwelende came, and Lombesa made room./
Then the houses of Shendala came, and Lombesa made room./
Its fields were smaller for every house that came./
Its line was longer for every house that came./
._More in the line._/
Say which is the better wealth./
._More in the line._/
Say it again, for the child./
._More in the line._

And Ndawelo by the red pan made room./
._It made room._/
And Nyelambe in the far grass made room./
._It made room._/
Not one of them said: the tally is full, go further./
._Not one of them._

So hear me, child at the root./
You have a tree over you that stands./
You have a tree in this song that does not./
Your fathers' fathers' fathers came from it/
with nothing on their heads,/
and their dead came in a griot's mouth./
._With nothing on their heads._/
The tree over you is not yours. It took you in./
The tree in the song is not yours. It let you go./
Feed the one. Remember the other./
._Feed the one. Remember the other._

Bwelende, Shendala./
._Bwelende, Shendala._/
Tálemo who carried, Néntoli who carried./
._Tálemo, Néntoli._/
Kíshela, who stayed./
._Kíshela, who stayed._

I have carried them the length they were given me./
._No longer, and no shorter._/
Who has checked it?/
._We have checked it. We know them too._/
Then put the child down at the root/
and give the tree its share for her./
Hear me, children of the tree that stands./
._We hear you, keeper._
:::
