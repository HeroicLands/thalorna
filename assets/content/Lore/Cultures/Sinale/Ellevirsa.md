**Guest-Right**, _Ellevirsa_, is a [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] origin song: how the Sinalë came into a valley that was not theirs, what they found already in it, and what they decided there, which is where the custom its title names comes from. It is cut into a standing stone at [[place-sirmenne|Sirmennë]] in an old hand of the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldirno]], in the long paired lines of the Sinalë long song, each line answered by the one beneath it. The stone is broken in two places, and where it is broken the reading is silent. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] Tradition Keepers hold the reading pair for pair and give it at the stone when they go to Sirmennë, and the [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Colleges]] sing what the Keepers read. The living song is the same song with one difference, set out below: the Áelendan hear it at [[place-aelirosse|Aelirossë]] on the night a guest of theirs first sleeps there, sung over the guest, who is what it is about.

The song begins with a refusal. Its first line is "We came out of" and then a space the cutter left blank, dressed smooth and never filled; the living song holds the same place as a silence. Of what came before Thalorna the Sinalë say nothing to outsiders, and the song says so in the only way it can. What follows is a catalogue of what was already in the valley when they came: the [[lore-faecourts|fae]], at the thin places; the trees, by which the song tells its time, since it carries no dates; the waters; the beasts; and bands of the younger people, with fire and with words that changed every summer, who are the ancestors of everyone who hears it. Nobody asked the Sinalë in and nobody turned them out, and the council that sits in the valley argues over what to do about the bands. Three voices are kept whole. The elder who kept the watch at the pass asks that the bands be kept out; the one the valley had raised, which is how the Sinalë make a lord, by acclaim for what one has done, answers that a people who build nothing have no door to shut; and a third, who is never named, says the saying the custom comes from. _Ellevirsa_ is the ordinary Sinalë word for the welcome a guest is owed; the song reads it from the other side, as the welcome a guest owes to whoever comes after. Where the song says "this valley", the Keepers take it to mean the one the stone stands in, and the Sinalë have not said. The Keepers do not fill the two breaks from the living song, so what the stone lost is lost.

The stone and the living song differ by one pair. The stone closes on the name of the elder who argued to keep the bands out, set last, after the valley and after the stars, which is how the Sinalë name the doer of a deed when they name one at all; the living song keeps his argument whole, ends on the stars, and has let go of whose the argument was. The Keepers read the stone to its last pair. The living song still sings that the one who would have turned a guest out "is cut at the end of this", and then names no one.

```poetry {form=ellanthe lang=en}
We came out of
  We came out. What we came out of is not cut here, and it is not sung.
We came out at the head of the valley, where the yew is, and the valley was below us.
  We went down into it. Nobody had told us it was there, and nobody had told us it was not.
We came out into a dusk, and the dusk was long.
  It was long, and we stood in it, and then it was night.
We have a word for that dusk now. We did not have it then.
  We had come out into a thing we had no word for, and we stood in it until it was over.
There were stars over the valley.
  There were stars, and we did not know one of them.
We did not name them that night.
  We did not name them for a long time. A guest does not name what is in the house.
We lay down in the grass where we had stood.
  The grass was tall and wet, and nobody had cut it, and we slept in it.

This is what was here before us. We cut it so that it is known.
  We were not first in the valley. We were first of the ones who speak as we speak.

At the thin places there were already the fae. That is your word, and it serves.
  They were at their doors when we came down. They stood aside.
They did not ask us in. They did not say we should not come.
  They watched us come, the way a heron watches, and went back into the deeper places.
We do not go there.
  It was theirs before we came, and we have never said otherwise.
We go up to the thin places sometimes, and stand. They come to the edge of them, and stand.
  Nobody says anything. That is how it is done with them, and it has not needed to be more.

The trees were here. The yew at the head of the valley was old when we came.
  It has fallen since. The yew that grew from it is old now, and that is how long.
There was oak on the slope, and oak below the oak, and the acorns of both in the ground.
  There was rowan at the water. There was ash, and the ash was […]
[…]
  […] hazel, and under the hazel nothing, because the hazel lets nothing grow.
There was pine on the ridge, where the wind is, leaning the way the wind goes.
  There was willow in the marsh, and the willow was down and growing from where it lay, as willow does.
There was apple on the slope, wild, small, and sour, and it was the first thing we ate here.
  We ate it anyway. We ate it standing up, in the dusk, before we knew that the dusk would end.
There was birch at the edge where the wood thins, the pale wood, the first thing we had a word for.
  We had a word for it before we had a word for the valley.

The waters were here, and these are the waters:
  the cold spring under the crag, that does not freeze;
the slow river in the floor of the valley, that floods in the thaw and is a marsh until the first frost;
  the pool below the fall, deep, where the foam goes down white and comes up a long way off;
the brook that comes down from the pass, that is nothing in summer and everything in the rains.
  We drank from all of them. Nobody had put a mark on any of them.
The spring under the crag is the one we drink from still. The others we drink from when we are near them.

The crag was here, and the stones under the crag that the crag had let fall.
  This stone was one of them. It was lying down, and we stood it up.
It is the one thing in the valley we moved.
  We moved it so that there would be a place to cut this, and we are cutting this on it.

The beasts were here: the stag, the wolf, the bear that is in the valley still and is not the same bear.
  The salmon came up the slow river in the autumn, as it comes.
There was an owl in the yew the first night, and it spoke before any of us did.
  It spoke the whole night. We lay in the grass and let it, since it had been there first.
We hunted the stag and left the bear alone, which is the right order.
  The wolf hunted the stag too, and left us alone, and that is also the right order.

And there were bands of you.
  There were bands of the younger people, and they were few, and they moved.
They had fire. They carried it in a hollow of bark from one camp to the next,
  and when it went out they made it again, with a stick and a string and a long time swearing.
They had words. They had words, and the words changed.
  What they called the river in the summer was not what they called it the summer after.
We listened at the edge of the wood to what they called each other.
  By the time we had learned it they called each other by something else.
They had names for the stars, too. We listened for those.
  They had a name for the one over the pass, and the next band had another, and the next had none.
They put their hands on the crag, in red, and the hands are there.
  Small hands, most of them. We did not know then that most of them were children's.
They were forty, or fifty. They did not stand still to be counted.
  They came through the valley in the spring and went out by the ridge in the autumn,
and they did not stay, because nothing stays that follows the deer.
  They saw us. We saw that they saw us. Nobody said anything, for a long time.
One of them came close, once, in the second summer. A child.
  It stood at the edge of the birch and looked, and we looked, and it went back, and nothing was said.

We learned the year here by living it once.
  The first frost came, and we did not know it was the first; we thought it was the winter.
We put the fires in under the crag that night and sat close, and in the morning the frost was gone by noon.
  We had a word for that frost by the next year. We did not have the word for the winter until we had had the winter.
Then the winter came, and we knew the difference, and we have known it since.
  Then the thaw, and the flood, and the smell of rain before the rain, and the tall grass again.
It was a year. It came around. We had not known that it would.
  We lay in the tall grass where we had lain the first night, and it was the same grass, and we knew it.

The first winter we did not know what the winter was here.
  We did not know what came over the pass in the snow, or whether anything came.
One of us went up to the pass and kept the watch there, against whatever it was.
  He kept it alone, in the snow, until the thaw. Nothing came. He kept it anyway.
He came down in the spring and said nothing about it,
  and we did not ask, and that was the first watch that was kept in this valley.

In the thaw the river was over the valley floor, from the slope to the birch.
  We were on the slope. The birch was on the far bank. The far bank was where we needed to be.
One of us went into the flood with a staff, and walked it, and went under twice,
  and found the one place where it could be crossed, and stood in it, and called, and we crossed.
She went under twice. She did not say so afterward.
  The ones on the slope saw it, and it is cut here from what they saw and not from anything she said.
We raised her for it, and for what she had done before it. That is how one is raised among us:
  for things done, by all of us saying so.

Nobody asked us in.
  Nobody turned us out. The fae stood aside, the trees stood, the bands went around.
We were in the valley. We had not been asked, and we had not been told to go.
  We kept the valley as a guest keeps a room: we did not move the things in it.

Then there was a council in this valley, where this stone stands, and this is what was said at it.
  We sat and spoke until we agreed, which is the way, and it took the winter.
One of us had dreamed, and the dream was of the kind that is sent, so it was told first.
  The dream was the valley full of fires, from the marsh to the pass, and no one of ours among them.
The ones who read dreams read it. One read it as a warning. One read it as what will be.
  Both readings were kept. We went on with the dream in the valley with us, read both ways.

The one who had kept the watch at the pass through the first winter spoke first.
  He had kept it alone, and so he spoke first, and nobody said he should not.
"Keep them out of the valley.
  Keep the young bands out of it. Let them go around by the ridge, as the deer go.
They take what they see. They have fire already, and they did not make fire; they saw it.
  What they see us do they will do, and do badly, and then do.
Their words change between one summer and the next.
  Ours do not. Let them not come where our words are, or ours will go the way of theirs.
You have seen the hands on the crag. You have seen how many there are.
  There will be more hands than crag. There will be more of them than there is valley.
I was let in. I know it. I was let in with the rest of you, and nobody asked me.
  I do not say turn them out of the world. I say keep this valley.
[…]
  […] and the valley will be ours as long as we keep it."

The one we had raised answered him. We had raised her for the ford:
  when the river was over the valley floor she found the one place it could be crossed, and we crossed.
"We have no door," she said. "We have built none. We will build none.
  What is it you would shut? The ridge is a way for a deer; it is a way for them.
Keep them out, and we are the ones who stand at the edge of the wood all day, every day, being a door.
  I have stood at the ford. I will not stand at a door.
And their words change. Let them change. The river has had a name from every band that crossed it,
  and it is the river. I have been in it to the neck, and it did not ask me what I called it."

Others spoke. One said the bands would go around anyway, and one said they would not;
  one said the wolves would thin them; one said nothing for three days and then said what she had said before.
One said the fae had stood aside for us, and asked who we were to stand in anyone's way.
  One said the fae had stood aside and gone into the deeper places, and asked where we meant to go.
One said: then let us go. Let us go up past the pass, where nothing follows the deer, and leave them the valley.
  That was argued longest. It is argued still, in other valleys, by others of us.
The one we had raised answered that too. "Go where? Past the pass is a valley.
  Past that is a valley. There is a band in every one, or there will be. We would be going for always."
It is not what was agreed. It is cut here because it was argued, and because it is argued.
The one who had kept the watch said it again, and said it better the second time:
  "Keep them out of the valley. I do not say for always. I say until we know what they are.
They take what they see. I do not say that is a wrong in them. I say it is what they are,
  and what they are is not settled, and what we are is, and the two should not be in one valley
until the one that is not settled has settled. Let them go around by the ridge until then.
  Then let them in. Then I will stand aside for them, as the fae stood aside for us."
It was a good argument. It is cut here whole because it was a good argument.

Then one of us said this, and it is what we say:
"Nobody asked us in.
  Nobody turned us out.
What was done for us, we do.
  What was not done to us, we do not do.
Let them come in unasked, as we came.
  Let them be, as we were let be.
This valley was never ours. We are in it.
  Let them be in it. It will not be theirs either, and they will not know that, and it does not matter."

The one who had kept the watch said nothing after that.
  He said nothing that winter, and in the spring he said nothing, and we took it for the accord.
We agreed to it. It took the winter, and it was the valley that we agreed to.
  We agreed as we agree: each said the saying, in the same words, going around, until it came to him.
It came to him, and he did not say it, and he did not say that he would not,
  and the one after him said it, and it went on around, and that was the accord.
The valley stayed open. Nothing else was agreed.

The bands came through in the spring, as they came.
  They came through the valley and not around by the ridge, and we were in it, and they saw us.
They saw us, and they did what the one who had kept the watch had said they would do.
  They took what they saw. They took it badly, and then they took it.
The next winter the one who had kept the watch went up to the pass again, and kept it.
  He kept it over the bands as well, who were in the valley below him in the snow, and he said nothing of that either.
They took the shape of a fire-pit that does not smoke into the eyes.
  They took the way we set a snare. They took a tune, and lost it, and found another, which was better.
They took a word of ours, one, for the pale wood, and it changed in a summer, as their words do.
  By the next summer we did not know it. It is somewhere in your mouths still, and we would not know it now.
The child who had come close came close again, grown, with a child of its own,
  and said a word to us, and the word was the river's name that summer, and we said it back.
That was the first word. It is not cut here, because it changed the summer after.
They put their dead at the foot of the crag, under the hands, and went on by the ridge.
  We did not move them. A guest does not move what is in the house.
After a time they did not go out by the ridge in the autumn. They stayed.
  There was smoke in the valley in winter, where there had been no smoke in winter, and then more.
One winter the valley was full of fires from the marsh to the pass, and we stood on the slope and counted them.
  It was the dream. The one who had read it as a warning said so. The one who had read it as what will be said so.
Both had been right. We had known they would be, and had gone on.
They became. You are what they became.
  You have most of it still, and not the tune.
The hands on the crag are there. There are more of them than there were, and the newest are old.
  Nobody of yours has put a hand there in a long time. Nobody of ours ever did.

You who are reading this, or hearing it read: you are in the valley.
  You are standing where the council sat. Nobody asked you in.
Nobody will turn you out. The one who would have is cut at the end of this. He did not say no.
  Stay as long as the valley holds you. When you go, go by the ridge, or stay; it is the same to the valley.

This is _ellevirsa_. This is where it comes from, and this is what it is:
  We were let in. Whoever comes after us into a place we are in is let in.

We named the stars when the yew had fallen and the yew that grew from it had seeded.
  We named them late. We named them last of all the things in the valley.
We had waited for the names the house had, and the house had none that held:
  the bands' names changed with the summers, and the fae did not say, and the trees do not name.
So we named them in our own words, which do not change.
  These are the first of them. They are named for what they do. None is named for one of us:
the one that does not move, that the pass points at;
  the ones that go down into the marsh in the autumn and come up out of it in the spring;
the two that stand over the birch at the thaw, when the crossing is;
  the one the bands pointed at, which we named for the pointing, since that was all we knew of it;
the one we named, and then found was two, standing close, and we kept the one name for both;
and the first we named, the one that stands in the evening over the pass, where the watch was kept.

The one who said keep them out, who kept the watch at the pass: **Nylvidhë Esselinto**.
  He was let in with the rest of us. He is cut here last, after the valley, which is the right order.
```
