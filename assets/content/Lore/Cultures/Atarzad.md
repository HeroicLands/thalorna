The Atarzád call themselves the Flame-born, and the first thing to understand about them is that they hold everything they have because it was promised. Four generations back they were a labor-caste in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]], led out of it by the prophet [Yotekhor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}; three generations back their children, born free in the dry country, came down past [[place-mountshofar|Mount Shofar]] and took four cities of [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] in [[lore-twelveywar|the Twelve Years]]. Those generations turned a people of the wilderness into the masters of irrigated cities, and the one thing that has stayed the same through all of them is the Covenant. The Atarzád do not rest their right to the cities on descent, purchase or the length of anyone's stay. They rest it on what [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]] said on the mountain.

## The Promise and the Hearth

The Covenant is short, and every child has it by heart before it can count:

> _I am Ataros, the Sacred Flame, who lit the first hearth and shall light the last. The Tellumel I give to you and to your seed after you, in perpetuity, for as long as a flame burns upon a Flame-born hearth. Walk before me with truth in your mouth and the kindling in your hand. Honor no other before me. The Tellumel is yours._

Each clause is a rule of daily life. The land lasts as long as a flame burns on a Flame-born hearth, so every Atarzád household keeps its fire, feeds it at sunrise and at sunset with the invocation every Ashaian household says, and does not let it die. A household whose fire goes out has broken the term of the Promise in its own house. It does not borrow coals from a neighbor; it carries new fire home from the tribe's fire-temple, before witnesses, and the street knows by evening.

"Truth in your mouth" is the rest of the law in four words. Good is _ozhmar_, right order: the Covenant kept, the word honest, the hearth lit. Evil is _ghuzdak_, the lie, and the Atarzád count four kinds of it: the false word, the image, the other god and the king. A man who lies to the Council of the Twelve is repudiated. An image of Ataros, even a drawing of his flame, is a lie about him, which is why the fire-temples hold nothing but fire. Worship of any of the other eleven Ashaian gods is a lie told to the god who gave the land. A king is a lie about who rules the tribes, since the Covenant names Ataros as their only king.

What the Atarzád value least is what their neighbors value most: being first. They do not deny that the [[lore-khazrynclt|Tellumi]] were in the belt before them. They deny that it matters, and a written deed, a tablet of descent or a centuries-old water-right weighs nothing against a promise from the Flame.

## The Twelve and the Line Back to Ebozor

A Flame-born is one by genealogy. Each of the twelve tribes descends from one of the twelve children of [Ebozor the Many-Sired]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and each lineage keeps its line back to him in memory, recited by its elder, the _sighot_. Atárzadi is an oral tongue, and the genealogies are spoken, not written, and a lineage's elder can recite its fathers back to Ebozor without a pause. A person introduces himself by given name and tribe, and the tribe is the claim: it says which city or ground his family holds, which warrior society he rides with and which elder speaks for him at the Council.

The family that matters is the lineage, not the household. Brothers and their sons hold the lineage's apportioned ground together, and the lineage's elder settles its quarrels, arranges its marriages and answers for it to the tribe's [[affiliation-cncltwelve|Council]] delegation. A person outside every genealogy is an _ezhorukh_, a stranger, whatever language he speaks. Marriage outside the Covenant is the one door out of a lineage that a person can open alone. [Zelumor the Renegade]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Teghumod merchant-prince, married a Tellumi woman of high birth and would not repudiate her; the Council repudiated him, and his children are counted Tellumi.

## The Ladder of a Life

A child learns the recitation of the founding at the hearth, and a child who falters in it at a festival is made to begin again from the first word. By the time it walks it knows the Covenant; by the time it can ride it knows the names of the twelve tribes in their order and the dietary law. It is taken up Mount Shofar with its family, sees the light of the [[place-unconsflame|Unconsuming Flame]] from the pilgrim camps, and carries a lamp up the stair and down again lit.

At sixteen a young man enters his tribe's warrior society, and from then until his forties he rides when the tribe calls. The societies are the tribes' only army: the [Khemozekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Zhoqarod, the [Tulqenekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Burqalod, the [Ghishamekh]{.name data-type="affiliation"} of the Ozhmarod and nine more. A young woman's years are measured differently, by marriage, by the hearth she is given to keep and by the children she teaches; a woman called to the fire of Ataros is ordained and serves it, and some, like the warrior-priestess [[being-shrmnhthvn|Sharminah Tahvan]], fight in its service.

An adult owes the tithe, the first fruits and the first-born of the herds, and is expected to make the pilgrimage to Mount Shofar at least once before death. Many go every year. After the forties a man leaves the society and takes his place in the lineage's counsel, and the most senior become its elders. The old are honored as the memory of the people: they keep the genealogies, they remember the Wandering through their parents, and the Keeper of the Flame himself is always elderly. The dying ask to hear the Covenant once more, and their kin say it at the bedside.

## The Man Admired

The man the Atarzád hold up to their sons is the Zhudek who gives the office back. The Council acclaims a Zhudek, a judge, for one crisis, a war, a drought, a schism or a rising, and he holds broad authority over all twelve tribes until it ends and not a day past it. The tribes' tellers praise the Zhudek who wins and then rides home to his own ground as an ordinary elder of his lineage. The man they tell against is [Tulqenor the Bold]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Zhoqarod war-leader who reached for a crown two generations ago and was repudiated and exiled for it.

Below the Zhudek, the admired man is a warrior of his society who takes his losses for the Promise without complaint, keeps the dietary law in the field, tells the truth at the Council when it costs his tribe, and comes home. The Atarzád do not admire a clever man as the Tellumi do. They admire a man whose word means what it says.

## The Woman Admired

The woman held up to daughters is the keeper of a hearth that never went out. In the recitation of the bondage, the Flame-born kept lamps burning in secret behind hearth-stones while the Khelathi priests tried to turn them to other gods, and every Atarzád house still keeps its flame as those houses did. A woman who keeps her household's fire unbroken through a lifetime, teaches every child of the house the recitation so that none falters at the festival, and carries the family's lamp down the mountain lit, has done the thing the Promise depends on.

The tribes also have prophets, and a prophet may be a woman. [Aqeshat]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is a young woman who preaches that Ataros has shown her the return of Yotekhor in the flesh. Her admirers say she is what the Flame-born were in the wilderness, before the cities softened them. Her critics say a prophet who demands what the Council has not granted is reaching for the same thing Tulqenor reached for. That argument is the live edge of what an admired woman may be.

## The Flame, the Law and the Year

The faith of the tribes is the [[affiliation-ataros|Faith of Ataros]], kept in a way the rest of the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Ashaian Pantheon]]'s faithful find narrow. The Atarzád do not deny that the other eleven gods exist. They hold that Ataros alone gave them the Promise and alone receives their devotion, and no temple to another god stands on Atarzád ground.

An ordinary day has two fire-rites, at sunrise and at sunset, at the household hearth. An ordinary meal keeps the dietary law: no meat from an animal that did not die with its blood properly drained, and no desert hare, wild boar or the proscribed reptiles at all. An ordinary harvest is not eaten until its first fruits have been offered at a fire-temple, and an ordinary herd's first-born goes to the same fire.

The year turns on [The Festival of the Promise]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, kept three times: at the spring equinox, at midsummer and at the autumn equinox. Each is for the same thing, the renewal of the Covenant, recited in full before the Unconsuming Flame, and each opens with the recitation of the founding. The midsummer festival is the great one, when upward of fifty thousand pilgrims camp in the valleys round [[place-beitshofar|Beit-Shofár]] and the Burqalod ration the springs by tribe. The tribes count their ritual year in twelve thirty-day lunations against whatever civil year their cities keep, a count of fire-festivals rather than of years, and the Tellumi in the held cities keep both, as [[lore-khzrnclndr|the Tellumi Calendar]] describes.

## Where the Rezhul Ends and the Flame Begins

The tribes have two authorities and keep them in different hands. Civil authority belongs to each tribe's hereditary chieftain, the _rezhul_, and the elders of its principal lineages, and above them to the _ghizolukhem_, the Council of the Twelve, which meets for six weeks each year at Beit-Shofár and has no presiding officer. Religious authority belongs to the _toqel_, the Keeper of the Unconsuming Flame, always an elder of the Burqalod, who reads the will of Ataros in the Flame, and to each tribe's senior priest, who sits at the Council for its priesthood.

A quarrel inside a lineage goes to its elder, and inside a tribe to its elders and its chieftain. A quarrel between tribes waits for the Council. Where Atarzád law meets the Tellumi quarter of a held city, the holding tribe's magistrate judges it, and the same magistrate collects the water-tax and the assessed taxes. The genealogies are kept by the lineages; the scriptural archive and the law-codes of Yotekhor are kept by the elders of the [Tozhirod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, the scholar-tribe. Each tribe raises its own share of the common treasury and presents it at the Council.

When the two authorities meet, they meet over a Zhudek. The Council cannot acclaim one without consulting the Keeper, and the Keeper cannot raise one at all. That is the seam the tribes have built on purpose, and it holds until a prophet speaks who answers to neither. Aqeshat is such a prophet, and the Council has not decided whether she is to be honored or repudiated.

## Reward and Punishment

The rewards the tribes give are standing and voice. A warrior is honored in his society; an elder's word carries at the Council; a tribe whose men have served well is heard first on the next quarrel. The highest reward is acclamation as Zhudek, and it is given for one crisis only.

Punishment runs in a plain order of severity. A person troubled by guilt goes to a fire-temple and, under a priest's eye, walks between two small flames to be purified. A household that let its fire die carries new fire home in public. A tribe that has wronged another is judged by the Council and pays what it rules. A Flame-born who lies to the Council, worships another god in the open, refuses to give up a forbidden marriage or reaches for a crown is repudiated: put out of the Covenant, owed nothing by his tribe, and his children counted with whatever people takes them in. A stranger who lies to the Council is expelled from the tribes' ground. In the held cities the fire-wardens enforce the public law against the Tellumi, and a Tellumi rising is put down by the warrior societies, often brutally.

## The Bridge and the Fire

The Atarzád give their dead to the fire on the night they die. After the fall of Zargandur the tribes burned their own dead that night, and every Atarzád death follows the same rite. The soul then crosses the _mozhelurbek_, the Bridge of the Dead, which is wide and steady for a soul that kept the Covenant and narrows to a razor's edge for a soul that lied. No priest weighs anything, and no rite can widen it. The crossing is the consequence of how a person lived.

That belief explains an ordinary Atarzád day. Because no one can help the dead across, the Atarzád spend nothing on the dead and everything on the living and the Flame: the first fruits, the tithe, the pilgrimage, the lamp. An Atarzád keeps his word on a small bargain for the same reason he keeps it at the Council, because the lie he tells a Tellumi grocer is the plank he stands on later. A warrior who dies in the taking of the promised land dies in the bosom of the Sacred Flame, by the teaching of the Conquest, and that is why the warrior societies take losses no hired army would. Inheritance follows the genealogy and not a testament: the lineage's ground passes to its sons, and a repudiated man has nothing to leave.

The living cannot reach the dead, and the tribes keep no office that claims to. The dead do not come back. The one exception the tribes allow is that Ataros may speak in dreams, and the present Keeper, [[being-sighotornozh|Sighotor son of Nozhikor]], is widely held to have spoken with Ataros and dreamed of Yotekhor. Aqeshat's claim goes further, to the return of Yotekhor in the flesh, and that is why the Council cannot place her: if she is right, the dead do come back.

## What a Person Owes

An Atarzád can recite it. To Ataros, a Flame-born owes the truth, a lit hearth, no other god, the tithe, the first fruits, the first-born of the herd and the pilgrimage. To the tribe, a man owes his years in the warrior society, his lineage's share of the treasury and his honest word at its councils. To the lineage, everyone owes obedience to its elder and a marriage inside the Covenant.

In return, a Flame-born is owed a share of the ground apportioned to the lineage, the protection of the warrior societies, a voice through the elder and the chieftain, a hearing at the Council, and a place in the genealogy for every child. A stranger is owed what he has paid for: passage, the guide and the lodging bought at the gate, and nothing beyond.

## The Weak

The weak belong to their lineage first. A widow stays with her husband's lineage, and its men answer for her keep and her children's places in the genealogy. An orphan is raised by the father's brothers, and a child with no lineage at all goes to the fire-temple: the priests of the Great Fire-Temple at [[place-oasishirvn|Shirvan]] raised Sharminah Tahvan, a foundling pulled from a burned settlement. The homeless and the destitute of the tribes are rare in the held cities, because a Flame-born without ground has a lineage that holds some. The destitute of the cities are the Tellumi, and the Covenant owes them nothing.

## Those Who Cannot Work

A warrior crippled in his society's service is fed by his lineage from its share of the ground, and keeps his voice in its counsel; to the tribes he has paid in full. An old man past the society sits with the elders. A person who cannot work is never put out of the genealogy for it, since the genealogy measures descent and not labor, and only repudiation removes a name.

## Shelter, Teaching and Defense

Shelter is the lineage's to give, and the fire-temples own the caravanserais where strangers lodge at the gates. Teaching begins at the hearth with the recitation, and beyond it the Tozhirod teach the law-codes. The Atarzád send their sons to the Tellumi school at Shirvan, which the [Ozhmarod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} have never closed for that reason, and to the University at [[place-caldar|Kaldar]], where Atarzád sons study and from which [[being-kayvondzrd|Kaivonad Zarid]] took his learning. Defense is the warrior societies' and no one else's: there is no standing army, and the tribes mobilize by call.

## Masters of a Subject People

The Atarzád came out of bondage and hold a subject people, and they know it. The Tellumi of the four held cities live in their own quarters as subjects of the holding tribe's magistrates. They pay a water-tax that falls on Tellumi heads of household alone, they hold no land and bear no arms outside their quarters, and they worship nobody in the open. They also run the canals: the most senior water-masters of every held city are Tellumi by descent, three generations after the Conquest, because the tribes came out of the wilderness knowing herds and not irrigation. An Atarzád city drinks by the work of the people it rules.

Ordinary Atarzád and Tellumi live in the same streets, trade with each other every day, and rarely marry. The feud between them is not a war most years. It is a fire-warden shutting a Tellumi shop on an Atarzád festival day, a settler family cutting a channel past the last Tellumi field, a hidden Tellumi shrine found and demolished, and the raids and reprisals that follow.

## What the Atarzád Get Wrong

The sin the tribes tell against themselves is Zargandur's dead. When the city fell in the second year of the war, the Tellumi asked to go beyond the wall and read their dead's names, as their dead must be received, and the gate was Atarzád and was kept shut. Three days later the dead went into a trench. The tribes say so themselves at the Festival of the Promise, in [[lore-twelveywar|the Twelve Years]], and they do not pretend it was right. No Zhoqarod picket stands at night on the north grass where the trench is.

The blind spot is the one their own Covenant names. A people commanded to walk with truth in its mouth lives in cities whose water it can count only from the books the water-masters show it. A people whose founding is the going out from bondage holds the cities by keeping another people from land, arms and its gods. The thoughtful among them argue about it in the Council's tents. The strict answer that the Promise settles it; the [Teghumod]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, whose trade runs on Tellumi hands, answer that the Promise did not say to starve the hands that work the water.

## What the Covenant Does Not Say

Three questions face the tribes that the Covenant does not answer.

The first is Aqeshat, who asks the tribes to expel the Tellumi from the held cities. The Ozhmarod listen to her; the Teghumod do not; the Council waits, and her following grows each season it waits.

The second is the steppe. A man is gathering the steppe tribes by victory and marriage into a union of the kind the steppe calls an orghunoq, and every oasis on the road must decide what to say to him. For the Flame-born the question is sharper than trade. An orghun is a king by another name, and to swear under his banner is to raise a king over the tribes, which the Covenant forbids; to refuse him is to face the steppe alone with twelve warrior societies and no king to lead them.

The third is what the tribes do with writing that is not theirs. The Atarzád keep their own law in memory and in the Tozhirod archive, they tore down the Tellumi temples to build their fire-temples, and the archive at Shirvan burned when the city fell. The tribes hold the [[place-vurinkarvem|Vurinkarvem]] cliff over Shirvan, chambers and all, and have never had to decide what the Covenant says about an old writing found in it.

:::secret {title="The sealed library"}
A library is walled up in the Vurinkarvem cliff, in the Ozhmarod's ground. If it comes to light, the tribes divide over it as they divide over everything: the Ozhmarod hold the cliff and would burn whatever in it honors another god, the Tozhirod keep the tribes' archive and would want it, and the Teghumod would sell it to whoever pays most. Whatever the Council answers, it answers by reading the Covenant against something the Covenant never mentions.
