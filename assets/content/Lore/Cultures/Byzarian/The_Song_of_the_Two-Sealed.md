**The Song of the Two-Sealed** is the last of the songs the borderers of the [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]] sing of [Kezvunash]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and the one they sing most. It is a borderer's song, a _parzhelir_'s song in the soldiers' own word for themselves: a long line that breaks hard after its eighth syllable, sung without an instrument to a fort's fire on a night the pass is shut, by one soldier with the others coming in on the lines they know. The conscripts of the way-forts sing it, the herders of the upland sing it at the shearing, and the soldiers who go home to the plateau after their term take it with them. The earlier songs tell how Kezvunash was born, held the springs and brought back what was driven off, and a singer runs through them in a catalogue before this one begins; this one tells how he died.

Kezvunash is a _parzhelir_ of mixed descent, as the borderers' heroes are. His mother is [Dezhbalara]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the daughter of the captain of the third spring-fort; his father is a rider of the steppe clans who come in under the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|League]]'s truce to winter in the foothills, and the song does not sing his name, since the camps keep it. He is sealed twice, into the fort's roll and in the camp's way, and the song turns on the rule of the [Merchant Hall]{.name data-type="place/structure"} that a bond sealed twice is closed twice. [The Collector]{.name data-type="lore"} who rides up the pass road for him carries one seal, from the black gate of [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]], and cannot close what he holds; no arbitrator can be named whom the Collector does not hold the thread of; and so this is the one quarrel in the songs that goes out of the paper.

The monks of the eastern rite hold that the Collector is no god and no servant of one, but the borderers' way of saying a death, and they do not stop a soldier singing him. The winter camps under the truce sing a song of the same wrestling in their own tongue, with a hero of their own whose mother is a soldier's daughter of the forts, and the two songs end at one grave. The threshing-floor is the floor of the third spring-fort, where the garrison threshes the barley it grows below the wall, and the song puts it beside the spring; the field where the steppe host died of thirst, below the passes, is [[place-thirstground|the Thirsting Ground]], and the song names it only as the field.
