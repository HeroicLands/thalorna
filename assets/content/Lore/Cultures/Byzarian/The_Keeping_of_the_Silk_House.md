**The Keeping of the Silk House** is the lament [Ruzhveleze]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the house [Tcharzilakit]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} sang at her house's chamber in [[place-nykhtion|Nikhtion]], the city of the dead, on the morning she came to renew its keeping for the last time. A house's keeping is renewed before one of the priests who keep the chambers, and what is said at a renewal is written by the keepers' clerk in the margin of the receipt, so that the receipt holds the words the endowment was paid on; this lament is that margin, copied out. It is sung, not read: the women of the merchant houses keep a way of singing an account over a chamber, the names off the lintel and what each owed and was owed, with a line they come back to after each, and a widow sings it at a renewal when there is grief left in the keeping. Ruzhveleze's came back to the question every such lament asks, who keeps the keeping when the keeper is kept, and hers is copied and sung by other women because it answers it.

The silk house of [[place-khryshteikhos2|Krusteikhos]] took its name from the trade, by the grant of the League's assessors to the founder who first finished eastern silk in the city. Ruzhveleze came into it by marriage. Her husband, [Zighahr]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is dead of a fever; her daughter, [Kreniza]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, died a child; her son, [Ánevazur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, drowned off the straits of [[place-nesthiros2|Nesthiros]] with a ship of the house, and the sea gave nothing back. She is the last of the house, the name ends with her, and the keeping of the chamber would end when the endowment ran. [Nevarash]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is the journeyman her husband took as his last apprentice, and she names him at the end, before the keeper, as heir to the keeping and not to the name, since the assessors grant no name to a man for being loved. The older dead on the lintel are sung by what they did, and the lament gives their names to the lintel, which has them.
