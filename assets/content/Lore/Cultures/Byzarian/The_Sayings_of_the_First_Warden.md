**The Sayings of the First Warden** are the instruction every counting-house of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] has an apprentice copy out in his first month, and every master expects him to know without the copy by his examination. They are said to be what [Dokharash Harmodakit]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the city weigher of [[place-khryshteikhos2|Krusteikhos]] who weighed the tax-farmers' brass standard against the city's in the [Merchant Hall]{.name data-type="place/structure"} and was named the first Warden of the Weights for it ([[lore-heladweighing|The Weighing at Krusteikhos]]), told his son on the morning the boy was indentured. The copies open on that morning and close on the boy's answer, and between the two they keep the sayings in groups under heads, as a ledger keeps its accounts.

Some of the sayings cannot be his, and the copies keep them anyway. The group on the arbitrator speaks of the arbiter both sides name and the guilds' enforcement, which the League council made an ordinance of about 584 ([[lore-heladordinance|The Ordinance of Arbiters]]), more than a century after the Weighing. The monks who copy the sayings for the merchant houses mark that group as later; the clerks of the Merchant Hall hold that a Warden would have known the rule before the council wrote it, since it was the custom before it was the law. The sayings quote two proverbs of the bazaar in the bazaar's own Bizarian, the speech the counting-house writes, and answer one of them. The son is not named in any copy.

## The Morning

My son, you were weighed this morning, and you did not know it. Your master looked at your hands, and at your eyes, and asked you to count the bales in the yard, and you counted them, and you were right, and he took you. That was a weighing. Everything after this morning is a weighing, and you will not always know it, and the scale does not care whether you know. Here are the things I have learned at the balance, and I give them to you as a man gives a schedule with a bond. Copy them. Then learn them. Then burn the copy, and write them out again in ten years, and see what you have added. If you have added nothing, you have learned nothing. If you have taken anything away, you have been cheating.

## The Morning {#morning}

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=morning}
Son, your master weighed you this morning without your knowing. He looked at your hands and eyes, asked for the number of bales in the yard, and took you after you counted correctly. That was weighing. From this morning onward, every action is weighed; you will not always know, and the balance has no need of your knowledge. Here is what I learned beside it. I give you these things as a man supplies an itemized schedule with his bond. Copy them, learn them, and burn the copy. Write them again ten years from now and look at your additions. Without additions you have learned nothing. If you have removed anything, you have cheated.

: On Weights {#weights}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=weights}
An honest weight matches the standard./
.There is no third possibility.

The heavy weight steals from the buyer; the light one steals from the seller./
.A man holding both is twice a thief and once a fool.

Never file a weight down for a friend./
.He will recognize the filing and the filer. Afterward he will weigh you.

The standard stands in the Hall under the Warden's care, outside your purse./
.If uncertain, walk there. It is near; a dispute always takes you farther.

Weigh a bale before its buyer./
.A weighing hidden behind a door amounts to rumor.

Brass wears down. Bronze wears down. Reputation wears down faster./
.Get all of them tested each year.

The balance tells no lies. Its reader can./
.Read its judgment aloud.
:::

: On Terms {#terms}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=terms}
Agree to nothing vaguely. Fraud starts in an imprecise term./
.The usual rate begins a dispute you have already lost.

Specify grade, quantity, day, and place. Specify them again./
.Twice spoken gives a term one understanding.

Write it. Have it read aloud. Seal it./
.Whoever seals unheard terms has bought a bale still inside its sack.

Preserve your copy somewhere free of fire./
.The other man's copy establishes what he says you agreed to.

An unfavorable bargain faithfully honored is bad fortune./
.A favorable bargain dishonestly honored is wrongdoing.

Never alter a term because it harms you./
.It harms you because you drafted badly. Draft the next one better.

When a term profits you, observe it precisely./
.The losing party is watching your manner of winning.

Bring warmth to bargaining. Warmth has no price and binds you to nothing./
.Promise nothing absent from the page.

The bazaar says, Zhe danizh, kérdanir vezh: lend nothing; there is no profit./
.It is wrong. Lend in writing, at a stated rate, before witnesses; profit exists./
.Lend on a touch of hands, and the bazaar is right.
:::

: On the Defect {#defect}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=defect}
State a defect before selling./
.Once stated, it does no harm to the bargain. Concealed, it becomes the whole bargain.

Rot beneath the bale will be discovered. Always./
.The question is who discovers it: you before him, or he behind you.

A physician who names your body's defect earns twice the fee./
.Pay twice. Go home and follow the advice.

When asked what your goods lack, give the first answer./
.Whoever answers first determines that answer's price.

Hide one defect and people believe your house conceals ten./
.Declare one and they believe it has one.

Declared defects do not establish weakness./
.The springs' commander had five cities declare theirs. By evening they were stronger.
:::

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=biza enc=bizahelonikaenc work=defect}
Toum rétasin zerpel prez polarin./
.Zerpel er rétasin uf ésharin zerpel en ameitin. Zerpel er kaluptarin ésharin ameitin olashin.

Zhulvin en kamelir meretin er zalvin ésharin heurarin. Aeit./
.Monet erotarin ésharin tirm heurarin hein. Toum prez heir, eil heir opit toum.

Iatar rétasin zerpel en somir er toum. Kresin er heir ésharin drin zharpin er heir./
.Toum zharparin drinhorat. Efet toum nostarin prou domin. Toum pratarin retin er heir.

Potet fomántimor erotarin apetin er krémashin er toum, toum ameitarin prinet plum./
.Anerit er meun zhetelin tetarin zharpin er zhetelin.

Domin kaluptarin meun zerpel. Ántimor pestarin domin kaluptarin 10./
.Domin rétasin meun. Ántimor pestarin domin ekhárin meun.

Toum uf pestarin zerpel er rétasin ésharin uf dunelir./
.Arsher er krenir pratarin 5 pólitar rétasin zerpel er autel. En hespetin helin ésharin pletir dunelir.
:::

: On the Guest and the Price {#guest}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=guest}
Give your guest food before you state a price./
.A well-fed man has already heard your first truthful statement about yourself.

Keep enough appetite in him for bargaining./
.A guest who omits bargaining will suspect fraud on the journey home.

The caravan master eats first, the factor next, the camel boy next./
.Then you eat. Then you sit down to speak.

A man refusing your bread has arrived as a creditor./
.Deal with him accordingly.

Hospitality is negotiation. Negotiation with real warmth is hospitality./
.A foreigner calling either false has never eaten at our table.
:::

: On the Master and the Guild {#master}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=master}
Your master owes instruction, bread, and a good character./
.You owe obedience today and observance of the charter throughout your life.

An apprenticeship adopts you./
.This morning gave you a second house. It carries a seal.

In introducing yourself, name your master. Name your guild before your house./
.No one knows how to deal with a man unable to name a guild.

A guild can take everything from you faster than a magistrate./
.If you damage its seal, it has the right.

When a master, examine your apprentices more strictly than your own examination./
.Their seal is yours, lent to them.

An unteaching master is a debt that never closes./
.A good teacher is a debt still paying after death.
:::

Vardesh er bizvar er toum prel toum ésharin didakhin, arthel, dokharin er pratin./

Matelvar ésharin tevirzhin./
.Toum ekhárin drin domin afor heum ortrin. Drin domin ekhárin sfradin.

Toum rétasin bizvar er toum en retin er ónomir er autel. Toum rétasin nemzhin prez domin./
.Anerit uf dunatarin kaleitarin nemzhin. Medinántimor epistémarin ameitin frun heir.

Nemzhin dunatarin pratarin apetin er plum kemetin er toum pletir takhor eil er dikasar./

: On the Road and the East {#road}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=road}
The League exists because of the road. Pay its toll./
.A toll pays for the road. Without payment, the road shuts by spring.

Count three currencies before your morning meal. Count again afterward./
.Their exchange rate moved while you ate.

The bazaar says, Pef fótamir, plunar reshir: beside the rivers, everything flows./
.True. Stand at the bank and count what passes.

Learn eastern speech until you can be cheated in it slowly./
.Then learn enough to recognize the cheating.

An eastern merchant observes terms as you do./
.If he fails, the failing belongs to that merchant, without belonging to the east.

Outlawing a faith costs trade more than correct belief can ever recover./
.Bow beside the shrine you pass. The road's gods deserve their due.

Every road fort stands above water./
.Lost in the [[place-eastrnmrch|March]], walk to the nearest fort. A spring lies beneath it.
:::

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=biza enc=bizahelonikaenc work=road}
Hodis ésharin aitelin er [League]{lang=en}. Toum zharparin tolzát./
.Tolzát ésharin zharpin er hodis. Hodis uf frun zharpin kleitarin en krenhar.

Toum aritmarin trel nomisin er heter pólitar prez edin er ortrin. Efet toum aritmarin paler./
.Telzhar meteir helin allatarin hoset toum edarin.

Bazhar rétasin. Pef fótamir, plunar reshir./
.Hein ésharin aletir. Toum statarin parza fótamir. Toum aritmarin krémashin er kinetarin parza toum.

Toum matelarin retin er eotin prel bradelir apatin prou toum./
.Efet toum matelarin prel noetarin hein.

Émfarin er eotin sotharin deshvel er autel houm toum sotharin er toum./
.Eif heir uf sotharin, apetin ésharin er émfarin. Hein uf ésharin er eotin.

Pestira er apaghelir ésharin baretir zharpin en emforarin. Ortelir pestira uf dunatarin nostarin hein./
.Toum gonetarin parza theshel en hodis er toum. Tásirin er hodis ekhárin vardesh prel autel.

Plum parzhel en hodis ésharin hut hidir./
.Potet toum uf epistémarin teselin er autel en [March]{lang=en}, toum badelin prou parzhel er mikrir diastelin. Hein ésharin hut krenir.
:::

: On the Arbitrator {#arbitrator}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=arbitrator}
Keep your dispute away from the council, which has never heard your name./
.Bring it to an arbitrator named by both parties.

Bring your terms' copy. He requests it before your name./
.His ruling concerns the clause.

An arbitrator's exact value is the confidence both parties place in him./
.Choose someone the other party respects, or your choice is another dispute.

Keep the quarrel inside the document./
.Personal vengeance costs a man his charter and his standing in the League.

A feud costs money. An arbitrator costs less. Do the calculation./
.[[place-nesthiros2|Nesthiros]]'s yards burned once. Today they are stone.

Pay when the ruling goes against you./
.Go home, read your own clause, and find where it harmed you.
:::

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=biza enc=bizahelonikaenc work=arbitrator}
Toum uf féranin prakhel prou bulorin. Bulorin medinkronin akherin ónomir er toum./

Toum féranin zelvash er deshvel er toum. Heir erotarin hein prez ónomir er toum./
.Heir kritarin prel keletin. Heir uf kritarin prel anerit.

Kresin er marzhar ésharin akret pestin er drin meretin er prakhel prel heir./
.Toum kaleitarin meun er heter dokharin. Heteret toum kaleitarin prakhel.

Toum uf féranin prakhel apet afor papirin./
.Anerit pratarin zharvash er autel apolarin nemzhel er nemzhin. Toteir heir uf ésharin anerit er [League]{lang=en}.

Zharvash er ghentir ésharin zharpin. Marzhar ésharin meitir zharpin. Toum aritmarin./
.Darvel er Nesthiros itrarin meunhorat. Nunet helin ésharin litorin.

Potet kritin ésharin kasir prel toum, toum zharparin./

:::

: On the Dead and the Keeping {#dead}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=dead}
Examine your dead's keeping as you examine a warehouse lease./
.An exhausted endowment means a shut door.

Your testament must name the grave, its endowment, and its keepers./
.An heir receiving unsettled obligations to the dead receives a dispute.

Before death, settle your accounts aloud before witnesses./
.Demand your debts, acknowledge what you owe, and leave your son settled terms.

Keepers sell keeping. Purchase it./
.A grave is a hole. Keeping is a promise someone is paid to remember.

On the night of black candles, speak every name aloud in order./
.A forgotten name leaves an account open.

Never seek answers from the dead./
.They owe you nothing now. Your debt to them is keeping. That covers everything.
:::

Toum kaleitarin tanvel, velzhar, valzar en zelzhar er toum./
.Zhelvar lámbarin vardesh er uf kleitarin prel tanor. Heir lámbarin prakhel.

Toum rétasin telvar er kitvar taret prez tanearin, prez mervar./
.Toum kaleitarin vardesh prel autel. Toum rétasin vardesh er autel. Toum dorasarin deshvel er kleitarin prel huyir er toum.

Valzar uf polarin tanvel. Helin polarin valzin. Toum agorarin valzin./
.Tanvel ésharin nerthel. Valzin ésharin hupeskharin er fomántimor ekhárin zharpin prel velzarin.

Toum rétasin plum ónomir taret en nikhtir er melvar keretin, en taketin./
.Ónomir er toum letarin ésharin kitvar er toum uf kleitarin.

Toum uf zetelarin retin afor tanor./

: The Last Weight {#last}

::: {.verse form=sayings lang=en work=last}
The Hall's weight was heavy. I placed it on the balance before everyone./
.I was frightened and placed it there. Fear changes no weight.

Truth requires no courageous speaker./
.It requires someone to speak before those who will otherwise be cheated.

They burned the customs house that night. I did not./
.I wanted to stop them and could not. I admit I made little effort.

Son, a heavy weight lies in your life, still unrecognized./
.Find it and carry it into the Hall.
