**The Sayings of the First Warden** are the instruction every counting-house of the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Byzarian League]] has an apprentice copy out in his first month, and every master expects him to know without the copy by his examination. They are said to be what **Dôkharash Harmôdákit**, the city weigher of [[place-khryshteikhos2|Khrýshteíkhos]] who weighed the tax-farmers' brass standard against the city's in the Merchant Hall and was named the first Warden of the Weights for it ([[lore-heladweighing|The Weighing at Khrýshteíkhos]]), told his son on the morning the boy was indentured. The copies open on that morning and close on the boy's answer, and between the two they keep the sayings in groups under heads, as a ledger keeps its accounts.

Some of the sayings cannot be his, and the copies keep them anyway. The group on the arbitrator speaks of the arbiter both sides name and the guilds' enforcement, which the League council made an ordinance of about 584 ([[lore-heladordinance|The Ordinance of Arbiters]]), more than a century after the Weighing. The monks who copy the sayings for the merchant houses mark that group as later; the clerks of the Merchant Hall hold that a Warden would have known the rule before the council wrote it, since it was the custom before it was the law. The sayings quote two proverbs of the bazaar in the bazaar's own Byzarian, the speech the counting-house writes, and answer one of them. The son is not named in any copy.

## The Morning

My son, you were weighed this morning, and you did not know it. Your master looked at your hands, and at your eyes, and asked you to count the bales in the yard, and you counted them, and you were right, and he took you. That was a weighing. Everything after this morning is a weighing, and you will not always know it, and the scale does not care whether you know. Here are the things I have learned at the balance, and I give them to you as a man gives a schedule with a bond. Copy them. Then learn them. Then burn the copy, and write them out again in ten years, and see what you have added. If you have added nothing, you have learned nothing. If you have taken anything away, you have been cheating.

: On Weights {#weights}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
A weight is honest when it matches the standard.
  There is no third thing a weight can be.

The heavy weight is the buyer's thief. The light weight is the seller's.
  The man who keeps one of each is a thief twice, and a fool once.

Do not file the weight light for a friend.
  He will know it was filed, and he will know who filed it, and he will weigh you after.

The standard is in the Hall, under the Warden. It is not in your purse.
  When you are not sure, walk to the Hall. It is not far, and it is never as far as the quarrel.

Weigh the bale in front of the man who is buying it.
  A weight taken behind a door is a rumor.

A brass weight wears. A bronze weight wears. A reputation wears faster.
  Have them all tested every year.

The balance does not lie, but the man who reads it can.
  Read it aloud.
```

: On Terms {#terms}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Agree nothing in general. The general term is where the swindle begins.
  "The usual rate" is the beginning of a quarrel you have already lost.

State the grade, the count, the day and the place. Then state them again.
  A term stated twice is a term understood once.

Write it down. Then have it read aloud. Then seal it.
  The man who seals what he has not heard has bought a bale in a sack.

Keep your own copy. Keep it where the fire is not.
  The other man's copy is evidence of what he says you agreed.

A bad bargain honestly kept is a misfortune.
  A good bargain dishonestly kept is the offense.

Do not bend a term when it turns against you.
  It turned against you because you wrote it badly. Write the next one better.

When a term turns in your favor, keep it exactly.
  The man you are winning against is watching how you win.

Be warm while you bargain. Warmth costs nothing and commits nothing.
  Commit nothing that is not on the page.

The bazaar says: zhe danîzh, kérdanir vezh. Do not lend; there is no profit.
  The bazaar is wrong. Lend on paper, at a rate, before witnesses, and there is profit.
  Lend on a handshake, and the bazaar is right.
```

: On the Defect {#defect}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Declare the defect before the sale.
  A fault declared is no fault in the bargain. A fault hidden is the whole bargain.

The rot at the bottom of the bale is found. It is always found.
  The only question is whether it is found by you, in front of him, or by him, behind you.

The physician who tells you the defect in your own body is worth his fee twice.
  Pay him twice. Then go home and do what he said.

When you are asked what your goods lack, answer first.
  The man who answers first sets the price of the answer.

A house that hides one defect is believed to hide ten.
  A house that declares one is believed to have one.

Do not mistake the declared defect for weakness.
  The commander of the springs made five cities declare theirs, and they were stronger by evening.
```

: On the Guest and the Price {#guest}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Feed the guest before you name the price.
  A man fed well has heard the first true thing you have to say about yourself.

Do not feed him so well that he forgets to bargain.
  A guest who does not bargain will think, on the road home, that he was cheated.

The caravan-master eats first, then the factor, then the camel-boy.
  Then you. Then you sit down to talk.

A guest who refuses your bread is not a guest. He is a creditor.
  Treat him as one.

Hospitality is a negotiation. A negotiation conducted with real warmth is hospitality.
  The foreigner who thinks one of these is a lie has not been fed by us.
```

: On the Master and the Guild {#master}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Your master owes you training, bread and a character.
  You owe him obedience now and the charter all your life.

An apprenticeship is an adoption.
  You have two houses from this morning, and the second one has a seal.

Name your master when you introduce yourself, and your guild before your house.
  A man who cannot name a guild is a man nobody knows how to deal with.

The guild will ruin you faster than any magistrate,
  and it will be right to, if you spoil its seal.

When you are a master, examine your own apprentices harder than you were examined.
  The seal you give them is your own, lent.

A master who will not train is a debt that never closes.
  A master who trains well is a debt that keeps paying after he is dead.
```

: On the Road and the East {#road}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
The road is the reason the League exists. Pay the toll.
  The toll is the road's price, and a road nobody pays for is closed by spring.

Count three currencies before breakfast. Count them again after.
  The rate between them moved while you ate.

The bazaar says: pêf fótamir, plûmâ réshir. By the rivers, all flows.
  It is true. Stand on the bank, and count what goes by.

Learn the eastern speech well enough to be cheated in it slowly.
  Then learn it well enough to notice.

The eastern merchant keeps his terms as you keep yours.
  If he does not, it is the merchant who does not, and not the east.

A faith forbidden costs more in trade than orthodoxy could recover.
  Bow at the shrine you pass. The gods of the road are owed their due.

Every fort on the road stands on water.
  When you are lost in the March, walk to the nearest fort. It stands on a spring.
```

: On the Arbitrator {#arbitrator}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Do not take your quarrel to the council. The council has never heard of you.
  Take it to an arbitrator you and your counterpart both name.

Bring your copy of the terms. He will ask for it before he asks your name.
  He is ruling on the clause and not on the man.

An arbitrator is worth exactly what both sides think of him.
  Name one your counterpart respects, or you have named a quarrel.

Do not carry the quarrel out of the paper.
  The man who takes his own revenge loses his charter, and then he is not a man of the League.

A feud is a cost. The arbitrator is cheaper. Count it.
  The yards burned once in Nêsthiros. They are stone now.

When the ruling goes against you, pay.
  Then go home and read the clause you wrote, and see where it went against you.
```

: On the Dead and the Keeping {#dead}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Check the keeping of your dead as you check the lease on a warehouse.
  An endowment that has run out is a door that has been closed.

Name the tomb, the endowment and the keepers in your will.
  An heir who inherits an open obligation to the dead has inherited a quarrel.

Settle your accounts aloud before you die, before witnesses.
  Call in what is owed to you. Own what you owe. Leave your son closed terms.

The keepers do not sell graves. They sell keeping. Buy keeping.
  A grave is a hole. A keeping is a promise someone is paid to remember.

Speak the names on the night of the black candles, all of them, in order.
  The name you forget is the account you leave open.

Do not try to question the dead.
  They owe you nothing now. You owe them the keeping. That is the whole of it.
```

: On the Gods {#gods}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
Renew your terms at the Thresholds. Renew them all, even the ones that are turning.
  The gods renew the world on the same morning, and they look at your seal.

The Keeper of the Gates has two faces. Show him both of yours.
  The face you give with and the face you take with. He knows them already.

Do not bargain with the Silent Judge. He takes no offer.
  Pay the keepers. That is a different thing.

The Wild Beast would not seal, and the Keeper did not compel him.
  Do not compel a man to seal. A bond forced is a bond broken on the first day.

A man who prays for more than the terms is asking the gods to bend them.
  Pray that you keep yours.
```

: The Last Weight {#last}

```poetry {form=sayings lang=en}
The weight in the Hall was heavy, and I set it on the balance in front of everyone.
  I was afraid, and I set it on the balance. Fear does not change a weight.

A thing that is true does not need a brave man to say it.
  It needs a man to say it in front of the people who will be cheated if he does not.

They burned the customs house that night. I did not burn it.
  I would have stopped them, and could not, and I declare that I did not try very hard.

My son, there is a weight in your life that is heavy, and you do not know it yet.
  When you find it, carry it into the Hall.
```

## The Son's Answer

The copies close on the boy's answer, which the clerks have him give in the bazaar's speech and the monks render in the schools'. Either way it is two words long, and the masters have each apprentice say it back to them on the day he finishes his copy: _tchaz, esh_. It is, indeed.
