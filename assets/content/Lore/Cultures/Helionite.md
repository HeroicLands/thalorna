[[place-helionis|Heliónis]] is seven cities that agree about the gods, cooperate on defense, and compete about absolutely everything else. A Helionite's loyalty is to his city first and to the idea of Heliónis a distant second, and the idea is mostly invoked when someone from outside needs reminding that all seven are civilized and he is not. What a Helionite is measured by is excellence, demonstrated in public, in front of people competent to judge it. That is the whole of the culture and everything else is detail.

## Three Great Cities and Four More

[[affiliation-thyrenae|Thyrenae]] is the intellectual capital and holds the great academies. [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]] is the democratic naval power and commands the sea lanes. [[affiliation-kalydria|Kalydria]] is the cultural jewel, with its artists, its theaters and the **Academy of the Silver Veil**. Beside these three stand four more city-states: [[affiliation-theradon|Therádon]], [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]], [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] and [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]].

Each city has its own government, laws and customs. They combine against a common threat and for the shared festivals, and they compete ferociously over trade, athletics, intellectual prestige and, from time to time, in open war. A Helionite sees no contradiction: the festivals are real, the rivalry is real, and the rivalry is what keeps the excellence honest.

## Demonstrated, Not Claimed

Public debate on philosophy and politics is a cherished part of civic life, and it is public in the plain sense—held in the agora, before an audience, with the audience deciding. So is athletics, so is theater, so is the law.

The consequence is that a Helionite reputation cannot be inherited and cannot be bought. It must be performed, repeatedly, in front of witnesses who know the difference. A rich man's son who cannot argue is a rich man's son who cannot argue; the city says so to his face, and then invites him to dinner anyway.

This makes Helionites exhilarating and exhausting. They interrupt, they demand that a claim be defended on the spot, and they regard a foreigner's reluctance to be publicly examined as either modesty or fraud, and they press him until they know which.

## Honor Paid in Blood and Glory

Personal virtue is held as an achievement and not a restraint, and its proof is what a man risked. A Helionite will take an unnecessary risk for a reputation and will not regard it as unnecessary.

Feuds are conducted in the open and settled in the open, often in court, occasionally by violence, and always with an audience. What a Helionite will not tolerate is a wrong done quietly and left unaddressed, because the concealment is as much the injury as the act.

## Women, and the Silver Veil

Women's standing varies sharply by city. In some they are confined to domestic life; in others they take part in intellectual and even political life; no rule holds across all seven.

The hetairai occupy a position with no equivalent elsewhere: educated companions trained in music, philosophy, rhetoric and the social arts, and the most socially mobile women in Helionite society. The Academy of the Silver Veil at Kalydria trains them, and its graduates serve as companions, advisors and very effective political agents across the whole [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] region. A hetaira is not a courtesan as other peoples use the word, and Helionites are impatient with the confusion.

## Learning, and the Order That Came Out of It

The academies and gymnasia are where the city reproduces itself, and the philosophical tradition here is the oldest and most continuous in Ankaris. Mathematics, natural philosophy, rhetoric, medicine and the theory of magic are all taught, argued over and revised in public.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] was born out of that tradition at the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Panepistemium]], and Helionites have complicated feelings about what it became. They are proud of the parentage and uneasy about the child—an institution that claims authority over knowledge, in the one place on the continent where knowledge has always been settled by argument.

## Faith

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían pantheon]] is kept, and the shared festivals the cities honor together are the nearest thing to a Helionite national institution. The gods are addressed with the same directness Helionites use on each other: petitioned, bargained with, and reproached when they fail to deliver.

Temples are civic property and priesthoods are civic offices, frequently held by men who also hold magistracies. A Helionite does not experience religion as a separate sphere with its own authority; it is one more part of the city's public business.

## What a Person Owes

Ask a Helionite what a person owes, and he answers: the city first, plainly and without embarrassment and ahead of family, because the city is what makes a man capable of anything worth doing. Then the household. Then the friends bound to him, which in Heliónis is a formal and serious category.

And he owes the truth an argument. A Helionite holds that a person who lets a false thing stand unchallenged in public has failed at the basic civic duty. Dinner with three of them takes six hours.
