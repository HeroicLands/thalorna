The senior clergy of [[affiliation-nkaruthar|Nkaru'thar]] chant **The Hymn of the Three Fires**, _zhebolu ngen nkaring_, at [The Rite of Three Fires]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}. Three fires are laid in a triangle on the floor of a temple court, white, gold and red, and lit together from one ember carried out of the sanctuary. The priests chant while the fires burn, and the readers of the flame stand outside the triangle and watch how the three burn, because the pattern of the flames is read as prophecy for the coming season. It is chanted in the temple's archaic register, which the priests hold by heart and nobody outside the priesthood learns. It is the creation hymn the faith names in its teaching, and it is the nearest thing [[lore-okharinclt|the Okharins]] have to a single account of how the world began and how it ends.

It has three parts, one for each aspect of the fire: the white fire of [[place-zarhanis|Zarhanis]], which looks; the gold fire of [[place-kaljekor|Kaljekor]], which feeds; and the red fire of [[place-vuthraka|Vuthraka]], which eats. Its lines go in pairs, the second taking up the first, and a refrain of four lines closes every turn of it. The refrain is the part a herdsman knows, because he hears it from outside the court at the Rekindling, and it is the part every priest says the same way in all three cities. The rest varies a little from city to city, and the priests of each city regard the others' variants as small and wrong.

The copy rendered here is the one the [Flameweavers]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} of Kaljekor keep among their records of oaths sworn on the Scales. It is written in the voweled hand the merchants use, which the priesthood of Zarhanis dislikes in a sacred text, because a voweled hymn can be read by anyone who has the letters. The Flameweavers answer that they keep it for the day a priest's memory fails. It carries one strophe Zarhanis does not chant, the strophe of the Scales, and the copy marks it as Kaljekor's own. The bracketed rubrics are the copy's. The hymn gives the temple's teaching on the dead without remark and says nothing against the Bone-Readers.

: The Hymn of the Three Fires {#hymn}

::: {.verse form=hymn lang=en work=hymn}
_\[The three fires are laid on the floor of the court in a triangle, the white at the head toward the sanctuary, the gold at the left hand and the red at the right. Three priests carry one ember out of the sanctuary and light the three together, stooping at the same moment. The fires are not fed while the hymn is chanted. The senior priest chants the strophes, and all the priests together chant the refrain.\]_/

Hear me, you who are fed./
Hear me, you who are warm./
Hear me, you who will burn./
Before the lamp is lit, hear what the lamp is./

Fire of the first ember. Fire of the hearth./
Fire in the grass. Fire in the cow./
Fire on the blade. Fire in the fever./
Fire that looks, fire that feeds, fire that eats./
Fire before the city, fire in the city, fire after the city./
Fire of Zarhanis, fire of Kaljekor, fire of Vuthraka./
These are its names, and it answers to none of them. It is not a man, to answer./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

_\[The senior priest turns to the white fire.\]_/

There was no earth. The fire endured./
There was no water. The fire endured./
There was no grass, no beast and no herdsman,/
and nothing was dark, because there was no eye to find it dark./
The fire was alone, and it did not know it was alone,/
because nothing stood beside it to be other than fire./

The fire looked, and its looking was white./
Where it looked there was light, and the light went out a long way and found nothing./
The light came back to the fire with nothing in its hands,/
and the fire was not angry, and looked again, a long while./
Out of its looking it made a thing that could be looked at./
That was the first ember: a small fire set down out of the great one,/
so that the fire had a thing before it that was itself and was not itself./

The first ember is in the room under Zarhanis./
One man goes in to it. He comes out and does not say what he saw./
Every hearth in Okharis is lit from it, at a remove or at many removes./
The hearth in the herdsman's hut on the far grazing is lit from it,/
though the herdsman has never seen Zarhanis./
The fire in his hearth has seen it./

A fire not lit from the ember is not reckoned./
It warms the hands, and it is ash./
It cooks the pot, and it is ash./
It is put out at the Rekindling with all the others, and nobody carries it home./

At midsummer every fire in Okharis is put out./
The hearth is put out, and the forge, and the lamp at the cairn by the ford./
For a night there is no fire in the realm but the ember under Zarhanis./
In the morning the ember is carried out on every road,/
and the realm is lit from it again, hearth after hearth,/
and is one fire again, as it was before there was a realm./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

The white fire sees, and so the sleeper sees./
It goes into him by the eyes when the eyes are shut,/
and he sees what was not before him, and wakes, and it is gone./
The white fire knows, and so the elder knows./
He holds in his head which valley is whose, and in which season,/
and that is the fire in him, white and small./
When he dies it goes out with him/
unless he has given it to someone younger./

The white fire shows what is coming, and shows it slant./
The readers of the flame stand outside the fires and watch them lean,/
and say what the season holds, and the season holds it,/
or the season holds what the reading did not see, and the fire showed that also./
The fire is not wrong. The eye is small./

The body is the lamp. What burned in it is not the lamp./
When the oil is done, what burned goes up, and is not smoke,/
and goes into the fire it came out of, and is not lost,/
as the flame of a lamp put out is not lost, and is in the fire again./
The hearth is put out after a death and lit again from the temple,/
and the dead one's name is said to the new flame, and the new flame takes it up./
Zarhanis keeps the road the dead go up by./
It is a road of light. Nobody walks it twice./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

_\[The senior priest turns to the gold fire.\]_/

The fire wanted to feed, and its wanting was gold./
The white fire looks. The gold fire feeds./
It went down into the earth, and the earth was warm./
It came up out of the earth as grass, and the grass was gold at the head./
The grass is the fire, walking slowly./
The cow eats the grass, and the fire walks in the cow./
The calf drinks the milk, and the fire walks in the calf./
The herdsman drinks the milk, and the fire is in his belly,/
and his hands are warm on the rope in the morning./

So the fire walks, and the herd walks, and the herd is wealth./
Count the herd, and you have counted the fire that walks./
Count it at the fold at evening, and do not boast of it at the market./
Count the calves when they stand, and not while they are carried,/
because what is not born has not been given, and the fire gives./
A household that counts before the gift has spoken before the fire./
Its neighbors watch its calving./

The gold fire is in the barley of the Wámbuchi and the sorghum of the grazing plots./
It is in the dates of the Kirofa and the figs on their banks./
It is in the honey the Shókuma take from the tree, and in the wax./
It is in the fish the Ngorasha net and smoke over a slow fire./
It is in the flax of the Zhíkande, and in the linen, and in the weaver's hands./
It is in the charcoal of the Dóngaru, which is fire kept for later./
It is in the hide the Yéshiro tan with bark, which was a cow and walked./
It is in the horse of the Magara, and in the herd of the Tálumeng, which is the most of it./

A rich man's wealth walks, and anyone may count it from the road./
So he is open-handed at the fold gate and close-handed in the hut,/
and the feast he gives is the reckoning he gives,/
and the guests eat it and count it, and both sides are owed./
The fire that feeds is a fire that is seen to feed./

The fire that walks went out over the grazing with the herd./
The fire that does not walk stayed in the city with the smith./
The herd cannot make iron. The smith cannot make grass./
The herdsman comes in to the city for the blade and the pot,/
and the city eats the herdsman's cattle,/
and both are fed by one fire, and neither fire is the lesser./
It is one fire, in the hand of the smith, in the belly of the cow./

At the festival the herds come in to the city, and the city counts them./
A daughter is given to a lineage, and a grazing goes with her,/
and the valley that was one lineage's in the rains is two lineages' in the rains./
A son is given a wife, and her father's herd walks beside his own./
The gold fire is in the knot of the marriage cord, and holds it./

_\[Here the copy of Kaljekor writes the strophe of the Scales. Zarhanis does not chant it.\]_/

The gold fire weighs. It is the beam of the balance./
A thing given for a thing, and the fire between them holds them level./
Swear on the beam and keep it, and the fire keeps you./
Swear on the beam and break it, and the fire stands between you and your bread/
for a year and a day, and then it stands between you and nothing,/
because there is nothing of yours left for it to stand between./

The stranger at the edge of the fold is given milk./
It is sour, and cold from the shade, and the herders stand by him./
When he has drunk he is inside the fire's giving,/
and what is given him is counted by the fire, and not by the herdsman./
What he does after, the fire counts as well./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

_\[The senior priest turns to the red fire.\]_/

The fire was hungry, and its hunger was red./
The white fire looks. The gold fire feeds. The red fire eats what was fed./
Do not curse the red fire. It is the same fire./
It is the fire that was first, and it is hungry for what it made./

In the dry season the old grass stands grey to the knee,/
and the cattle will not eat it, and nothing green comes up through it./
The herdsman lights it at the edge with the wind at his back,/
and the red fire goes across the grazing in a line, and the sky is brown with it,/
and the small things run out ahead of it, and the birds come down on them./
After it the ground is black. There is nothing on it./
After the first rain the ground is green, and the cattle come back to it./

The red fire is in the blade and in the hand that carries it./
It is in the Ashborn when they stand, and their arms are written with it./
It goes out of the city to the edge of the grazing,/
and stands between the herd and the ones who would drive the herd away./
It does not go past the edge./
What lies beyond the edge has not been given to it./

The Ashborn come home from the edge fewer than went out./
Their names are said at the red fire in Vuthraka, one by one,/
and the fire takes the names, and the herd goes out to the grazing in the morning,/
and the herdsman does not know their names, and he counts his cattle at evening./
That is what the red fire is for./

The red fire is in the fever and the drought,/
in the river that rises and the storm that takes the roof./
The spirits of those things serve it, and it is served./
The storm has never taken anything it did not first give./
The river warns at every ford, the same warning./
A household that has lost to the red fire has not been robbed./
What the red fire eats, it fed first./

At midwinter the red fire is given the night./
Each household gives to the fire what its dead would have eaten,/
and the smoke goes up, and the spirits walk in the smoke and are not hidden./
In the morning the ash of the temple hearth is gathered and mixed with oil,/
and laid on the forehead of a child at its first passage,/
so that the child is marked with what was eaten, and goes on./

At the end the red fire eats all things./
It eats the grass and the cow and the herdsman and the city./
It eats the pyramid at Zarhanis and the Scales at Kaljekor and the Brand at Vuthraka./
It eats the earth, and the earth goes into it as the old grass goes./
It eats the first ember, and the ember goes back into the fire it was set down from./
Then there is no earth. The fire endures./
There is no water. The fire endures./
And the fire looks, and its looking is white./
Out of its looking it makes a thing that can be looked at./
It sets down an ember, and the ember is first./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

_\[The three priests stoop to the three fires together. Each takes a flame on a reed, and the three reeds are put to the three wicks of one lamp at the same moment. Nobody puts out a wick before the others.\]_/

Three wicks. One lamp./
The white wick, for what we see and what we hold in our heads./
The gold wick, for what we eat and what we give./
The red wick, for what we lose, and for the line we hold against losing it./
Put none out before the others./
Let them go out together, or let them burn./

Zarhanis keeps the white wick. Kaljekor keeps the gold. Vuthraka keeps the red./
The herdsman keeps all three in one lamp in his hut,/
and lights them together, and no city sees him do it./

One fire. Not three./
Before all things, the fire endures./
In this lamp, the fire endures./
After all things, the fire endures./

_\[The readers of the flame come into the court and read the three fires. What they read is not written in this copy.\]_
