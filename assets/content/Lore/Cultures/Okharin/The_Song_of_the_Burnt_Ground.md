The [Ashborn of Vuthraka]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} chant war-hymns that call on the Wild Flame, the red aspect of [[lore-nkaruthardty|Nkaru'thar]], and **The Song of the Burnt Ground**, _zhebolu zhimbalin khutheling_, is the one they chant on the march out of [[place-vuthraka|Vuthraka]] and again in the line before an engagement. A leader calls each line and the company answers it. The answers are two: _We stand on ash_, after most lines, and _Ash does not burn twice_, at the turns. A company that has chanted it on the march has the answers in its feet before it reaches the line, which is part of what the hymn is for.

Its matter is **the Stand on the Burnt Ground**, a fight of the old days on the open grazing of the [[affiliation-talumengtrb|Táluméng]], at the end of a dry season. Raiders out of the eastern grass fired the old grass upwind of the Táluméng herds. An Ashborn company under **Otham of the Brand** burned the ground under the herds before the raiders' fire reached them, drove the cattle onto the black, and stood on the ash while the fire went round them; when the raiders came through the smoke after the cattle, the company held them at the edge of the burn. The hymn gives the raiders no name and no people. It praises Otham for the thing he did not do: the raiders broke, the company wanted to follow them out past the edge of the grazing, and Otham set his blade in the ground at the edge and held them there. The hymn ends on the names of the seven of his company who died, **Mbato**, **Rembol**, **Ásira**, **Jengu**, **Tiru**, **Lakhor** and **Ngoro**, and Vuthraka says those names at the red fire whenever the hymn is chanted inside its walls.

The Táluméng tell the Stand at their own fires, in prose, and in their telling it was a Táluméng herdsman, **Kenthor**, who lit the break while the company was still riding in, and the Ashborn found the herds already on the black. The hymn names no herdsman. Neither side has asked the other to change its account. Táluméng elders at Vuthraka chant the answers with the company. The rendering keeps the answers in italics, set under the leader's lines.

_\[The leader of the company calls. The company answers each line with the first answer, and at the turns with the second. On the march the answer falls with the left foot.\]_/

Red fire, the fire that eats./
._We stand on ash._/
Red fire, the fire that was first./
._We stand on ash._/
Red fire on the blade./
._We stand on ash._/
Red fire, hungry for what it made./
._We stand on ash._/
The white fire looks. The gold fire feeds./
._We stand on ash._/
The red fire eats what was fed./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

We go out of Vuthraka by the south gate./
._We stand on ash._/
We take ash from the red fire as we go./
._We stand on ash._/
We lay it on the arm where the Brand is, over the scar./
._We stand on ash._/
The scar is the fire's writing. The ash is what the fire leaves./
._We stand on ash._/
Whoever has the scar has been burned, and is not afraid of burning./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

Hear the Stand on the Burnt Ground./
._We stand on ash._/
Hear Otham of the Brand and his company of the red fire./
._We stand on ash._/
It was the end of the dry season on the Táluméng grazing./
._We stand on ash._/
The old grass stood grey to the knee, and the cattle would not eat it./
._We stand on ash._/
The herds were out on the last of the green by the seasonal stream,/
and the stream was a line of stones with no water in it./
._We stand on ash._/
The herdsmen had sent for the rains at the cairn on the high ground./
._We stand on ash._/
They had told the storm what they meant to do that season,/
and the storm had not yet answered./
._We stand on ash._/

The raiders came out of the eastern grass at the dry end of the year./
._We stand on ash._/
They did not come at the herds with the blade. They came with fire./
._We stand on ash._/
They lit the old grass upwind of the herds, in a long line, at noon,/
when the wind is strongest and the grass is driest./
._We stand on ash._/
The smoke stood up brown over the grazing, a day's walk wide./
._We stand on ash._/
The small things came out of the grass ahead of it, the hares and the rats,/
and the birds came down on them./
._We stand on ash._/
The cattle smelled it and turned, and lowed, and began to run/
down the wind, ahead of the fire, which is where the raiders wanted them./
._We stand on ash._/
A herd running ahead of a grass fire runs into the arms of whoever waits for it./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

Otham was a herdsman's son of the Táluméng before he was Vuthraka's./
._We stand on ash._/
He knew the grazing by the soles of his feet, and the wind by the back of his neck./
._We stand on ash._/
He had the Brand on both arms, and the scars had gone white./
._We stand on ash._/
He was no speaker of speeches. He said what was to be done, and it was done./
._We stand on ash._/

Otham's company was riding the edge of the grazing that day./
._We stand on ash._/
They saw the smoke and came at the gallop, twenty of them,/
and the horses would not go toward the smoke, and they went./
._We stand on ash._/
Otham looked at the fire coming and at the herd going./
._We stand on ash._/
He said, "We cannot outrun it. We cannot put it out./
We will give it nothing to eat."/
._We stand on ash._/
He said, "Burn the ground under us."/
._We stand on ash._/

They lit the grass at their own feet, downwind of the herd,/
with the wind behind them, and the small fire ran away from them./
._We stand on ash._/
They beat its back edge with their cloaks so that it went only forward./
._We stand on ash._/
It ate a stretch of the grazing as wide as a village and went off down the wind,/
and behind it the ground was black and smoking and bare./
._We stand on ash._/
They turned the herd into the black. The cattle did not want to go./
._We stand on ash._/
They went in among the cattle on foot, with their hands on the horns and the hides,/
and spoke to them, and drove them in by the noses, and the cattle went./
._We stand on ash._/
They stood on the hot ground with the herd behind them,/
and the soles of their feet were burned through the leather./
._We stand on ash._/
The raiders' fire came up the wind to the edge of the black, and stopped./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

It went round them on two sides, roaring, and passed them./
._We stand on ash._/
The smoke lay on them so that a man could not see the man beside him./
._We stand on ash._/
The cattle coughed in the smoke and did not run, because there was nowhere to run to./
._We stand on ash._/
Otham said, "Stand where you stand. Everything here has already burned."/
._Ash does not burn twice._/

The raiders came through the smoke behind their fire, on foot, for the herd./
._We stand on ash._/
They had cloths over their mouths, and they had blades, and they were many./
._We stand on ash._/
They thought the herd would be running ahead of them and scattered on the grazing./
._We stand on ash._/
The herd was standing on the black, and in front of the herd, the company./
._We stand on ash._/
The company stood in one line at the edge of the burn, with the herd at its back,/
and the raiders came at the line out of the smoke./
._We stand on ash._/
Blade on blade at the edge of the burn. The red fire in both hands./
._We stand on ash._/
Nobody in the line went forward of the line. Nobody in the line went back./
._We stand on ash._/
The raiders came three times out of the smoke, and three times the line was where it had been./
._We stand on ash._/
The third time, the raiders broke and went back into the smoke,/
toward their own fire, which had passed, and the burned grazing beyond it./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

The company wanted to follow them./
._We stand on ash._/
The young ones in the line wanted it, and the old ones wanted it,/
and the horses were caught, and the blood was up, and the smoke was clearing./
._We stand on ash._/
Otham went out a little way from the line to the edge of the grazing,/
where the stream bed is, and the Táluméng grazing ends./
._We stand on ash._/
He set the point of his blade in the ground at the edge./
._We stand on ash._/
He said, "Here is the edge. What is beyond it has not been given to us."/
._We stand on ash._/
He said, "We were sent to keep the herd. The herd is kept."/
._We stand on ash._/
He said, "Whoever goes past this blade goes with no fire in him."/
._We stand on ash._/
Nobody went past the blade./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

They counted the herd on the black at evening, the herdsmen and the company together,/
and it was the whole herd, less two cows and a calf that the smoke had taken./
._We stand on ash._/
They counted the company, and it was thirteen./
._We stand on ash._/
The herdsmen brought milk out onto the black in the gourds they carried,/
._We stand on ash._/
sour, and warm from the day, because there was no shade left on the grazing./
._We stand on ash._/
The company drank it standing, and the herdsmen stood by them./
._We stand on ash._/
What was given them that evening, the fire counted./
._Ash does not burn twice._/
Hear the seven, whose names went into the red fire at Vuthraka./
._We stand on ash._/

Mbato, the first in the line on the left hand, who did not step back./
._We stand on ash._/
Rembol, who held the horses at the burn when the horses would not hold,/
and went into the line when the horses were held./
._We stand on ash._/
Ásira, who lit the break, and beat its back edge with her cloak until the cloak burned,/
and beat it with her hands./
._We stand on ash._/
Jengu, the youngest, whose Brand was a month old./
._We stand on ash._/
Tiru, who went in among the cattle on foot and turned the bull./
._We stand on ash._/
Lakhor, the second in the line on the right hand, who held it alone at the third coming./
._We stand on ash._/
Ngoro, who was speaking to the cattle in the smoke so that they would not run,/
and was speaking to them when the raiders came, and did not stop./
._We stand on ash._/
Seven names. The red fire took them./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

Otham came home to Vuthraka with twelve behind him./
._We stand on ash._/
He said the seven names at the red fire in the court, one by one./
._We stand on ash._/
He said no word about the edge, and the twelve said it for him./
._We stand on ash._/
The Táluméng sent cattle to Vuthraka at the festival, from the herd that was kept./
._We stand on ash._/
The cattle were eaten at the festival, and the herdsmen ate with the company./
._We stand on ash._/
The Brand was laid on seven more arms that year./
._Ash does not burn twice._/

We go out of Vuthraka by the south gate./
._We stand on ash._/
We go out to the edge of the grazing, and not past it./
._We stand on ash._/
What is beyond the edge has not been given to us./
._We stand on ash._/
The herd is behind us, and the herdsman counts it at evening./
._We stand on ash._/
He does not know our names. The fire knows them./
._We stand on ash._/
Red fire, the fire that eats. We are fed to you already./
._Ash does not burn twice._/
