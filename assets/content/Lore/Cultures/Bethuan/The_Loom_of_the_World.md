**The Loom of the World** is the hymn the Parthenai of the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] sing on the first morning of the [Festival of First Growth]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, standing in the communal gardens while the beds are planted. It is sung in two half-choirs that face each other across the beds: the half nearer the temple is called the beam, after the bar of the loom from which the cloth hangs, and the half nearer the field is called the weights. Each girl sings in the half her House has always sung in, and the Houses keep the count of which daughters have stood on which side. The hymn is in the feminine register, and the temple holds it to be the oldest work in [[skill-bethunlng|Bethuan]]: there is no borrowed word in it, and a Parthena who lets a word of the quay into it is sent out of the garden before the planting is done.

It is the state cult's account of how the world was made and how it will end. [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]], the World-Weaver, sets the warp of the world on her loom, and [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]] and [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] hang the weights that hold it taut beside her own, so that harmony, in the hymn, is the cloth held at its right tension. The gods men favor bring what the loom needs, and the hymn names each one with honor and lets none of them sit down. The priestesses of Lusinia in [[place-bethura|Bethura]] teach it as the reason the three goddess-orders hold the sacred pillar; the storm-priests of [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]] on the coast tell the same making as the launching of a hull, and sing it at their own festival. The weaving is a Bethuan telling. Elsewhere in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]'s lands the World-Weaver spins.

Halfway through, the two halves stop and praise the girl who leads them that year, by name and House, in words that do not change except for the name. The rendering keeps the name of the year it was taken down, [Thuressa of House Ghodunes]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. At the close the men planting the beds answer from the field in the speech of the gardens, and the hymn ends on their words. The temple's copies stop before that part, and the rendering gives it as the gardens of Bethura say it.

: The Loom of the World {#hymn}

::: {.verse form=hymn lang=en}
[The beam:]{.speaker}/
World-Weaver, who sit in the high house above all the houses,/
whose wheel is wreathed in the vine and whose loom is the length of the morning,/
who set the first thread and will tie the last, and who hand the knife on,/
hear the daughters of the beam, who stand on the side of the temple,/
and hear the daughters of the weights, who stand on the side of the field,/
as we sing the cloth that was set before any House had a door to it.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
Hear us, Weaver, as our mothers' mothers were heard in this garden,/
standing where we stand, with the seed-baskets on the path between us,/
and the earth turned and dark and waiting for the hands that will fill it./
We sing what you did, and the seed goes down while we sing it.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Before there was a world there was the heap,/
and the heap was the wool of the [[lore-karnavosdty|Wild Beast]], shorn of nothing,/
matted and loose and lying in itself, with no end that could be found in it,/
no long thread and no short one, and nothing pulled against anything./
There was no up and no down in the heap, for nothing hung./
There was no near and no far in the heap, for nothing was stretched between./
It was the slack before all slackness, and it was not evil, for it had not been ordered,/
and only what has been ordered can be broken.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
And the Wild Beast lay down in the heap and slept in it,/
and the heap was his, and he did not know that it was wool.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Then the Weaver looked at the heap from her high house and saw that it was wool,/
for she knows a fleece in the dark by the smell of it, as a mother knows a child,/
and she said: Bring it to me. And the gods brought it./
Hear who brought it, daughters, and honor them, each in his temple,/
for each brought what the loom needed and each went back to his work.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
The Thunderer brought the rain and washed it, three days and three nights on the hills,/
until the grease ran out of it into the sea and the sea was salt with it,/
and he was honored for the washing, and he went back to the sky.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
The [[lore-ustarondty|Forge-Lord]] cast the weights in bronze at his fire, and cast them true,/
each one the weight of the other, so that no thread should hang longer than its sister,/
and he was honored for the casting, and he went back to his fire.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
The [[lore-pozerasdty|Wayfarer]] went down to the sea at the edge of the heap and came back with the shells,/
the purple shells, a net of them on his shoulder, dripping,/
and he crushed them on a stone and the purple ran, and that was the first dye,/
and he was honored for the carrying, and he went back to his road.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
The [[lore-ardavondty|Keeper of the Gates]] stood at the door of the weaving-room with his back to it,/
facing out, so that nothing should come in while the warp was being set,/
and he was honored for the keeping, and he did not turn round.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
And the [[lore-selmorosdty|Silent Judge]] sat down in the corner of the room by the basket,/
the basket where the ends go, where a thread is laid when it is tied off,/
and the basket was empty, for nothing had ended yet. He waited./
He is waiting still, daughters. He does not hurry, and he is honored.

[All:]{.speaker}/
And not one of them sat down at the loom.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Then the Weaver drew the first thread out of the heap with her two fingers,/
long, the long thread that the heap had hidden in itself,/
and she knotted it to the beam, the bar at the top of the loom, and let it fall,/
and it hung, and it was the first thing that hung, and so there was a down./
She drew the next and knotted it beside the first, a finger's width away,/
and so there was a near and a far, the width of a finger, the first measure./
Thread by thread she drew them out, and knotted them, and let them fall,/
and the threads of the warp hung from the beam in a row as wide as the morning,/
and they were straight, and they were not yet taut. They waited for the weights.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
Now the weights, daughters. Now our side. Now the three.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
The Veiled Dreamer came to the loom with her face covered and her hands bare,/
and she hung the first weight, the weight of the unseen,/
which pulls on the thread from below where no one is looking,/
so that a thread is held straight by what it cannot see./
That is why the oracle weighs, and why the name carried to her comes back true or does not.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
The Bountiful One came to the loom with her lap full of fruit,/
and she hung the second weight, the weight of plenty,/
which pulls the thread down toward the ground where the barley is,/
so that nothing in the cloth is woven too high to be eaten./
That is why the House is fed before the House is praised.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And the Weaver hung the third weight herself, the weight of beauty,/
which pulls each thread to its own place and to no other,/
so that the cloth is not only strong but right, and can be looked at,/
and that weight is the heaviest, and she hung it last, and she did not let go of it at once.

[All:]{.speaker}/
And the warp was taut, and nothing in it was slack, and nothing in it was strained,/
and every thread pulled against the beam and the beam pulled back,/
and the Weaver laid her hand flat on the threads and they did not move under it./
That is harmony, daughters. That is the first harmony./
Harmony is the cloth held, and the world is held by it.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Then she took the shuttle and passed the first crossing-thread through,/
over and under, over and under, from her right hand to her left,/
and beat it up against the beam with the comb, and passed it back./
The warp stands, and the weft goes through. The warp holds, and the weft is held./
The warp is knotted to the beam, and the weft is carried where the shuttle sends it./
Neither is a cloth alone, daughters. The weft without the warp is the heap again.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
So were we made, the women and the men, the warp and the weft of the cloth./
The daughters are knotted to the beam, a mother's thread beside her mother's,/
and the sons are carried through by the shuttle and beaten up into the row,/
and held there, every one, by the daughters on either side of him,/
and the cloth is strong because of the weft and holds because of the warp,/
and a son who slips out of the row is a loose end, and a loose end is pulled.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
She wove the sea first, because it is nearest the beam, and she wove it purple,/
the deep purple the Wayfarer brought, the purple of the Veil./
She wove the coast below it in the green of the orchard,/
the olive and the fig and the citrus and the vine, close, a tight weave./
She wove the grain below the coast in gold, and the gold thinned as she went down.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Hear the colors of the cloth, daughters, as the temple counts them,/
for a weaver who cannot name her colors cannot ask for them at the dyer's./
The purple of the sea, from the shells the Wayfarer carried./
The green of the orchard, from the leaf of the olive steeped in the vat./
The gold of the barley, from the Bountiful One's own lap./
The straw of the dry country, which is no dye at all, but the wool as it came./
The white of the salt at the edge of the sea, where the washing ran./
The grey of the stone of the hills, which she wove in where the cloth must be strong./
And the black, a little, at the back, where the knots are, where the dead are tied./
Seven colors, and the temple keeps a skein of each in the court,/
and the girls who weave the Veil for the Metrissa learn them before they learn their letters.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
And where the dry country is, below the gold, she wove a little way in straw,/
and then she stopped the shuttle, and left the warp standing bare,/
the threads hanging straight with the weights on them and nothing crossing them,/
and she said: My daughters will weave this./
That is the line where the water stops. Every child in this garden has seen it./
On the one side the cloth, and on the other side the warp, waiting.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And the daughters of the Waters go out with the shuttle every year into the dry country,/
the engineer-sisters with their lines and their lamps and their hands in the ground,/
and they pass one thread through where she stopped, and beat it up, and the field is green./
One thread in a year. One field. The warp is very long./
That is the work she left us, and she left it to us because she trusts us with it,/
and the gods who brought the wool are honored, and not one of them was left it.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And hear how the cloth goes wrong, daughters, for it does, and the Weaver sees it./
A thread pulls loose from its place and hangs slack in the row,/
and the threads on either side of it have to carry what it should have carried,/
and the cloth puckers there, and a hand run over it finds the fault./
That is the daughter who throws away what her mother kept./
That is the House betrayed. The row goes slack where she was.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
And a thread pulls too hard, and drags the threads beside it out of their places,/
and the cloth gathers there, and strains, and if the Weaver does not see it, it tears./
That is the hand raised against the women who hold the House./
That is the order broken the other way. The row goes tight where he was, and tears.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And the Weaver sees both, for she sees every row./
The slack thread she draws back to its place and knots it again to its sister,/
and the row holds, and the fault is mended, and the mending shows a little./
The thread that drags she does not mend. She cuts it, and ties off the row on either side,/
and lays the end in the basket, and the Silent Judge takes it up./
Hear it in the garden, daughters, and do not turn your faces from it./
The temple does not sing this part softly, and neither do we.

[All:]{.speaker}/
Weaver, keep us in our places, every thread of us, the warp and the weft,/
so that you never have to cut.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
Daughters, here is the hard thing, and we sing it in the garden because it is true in the garden./
Every thread has an end. The Weaver drew them long, and none of them is endless./
When a thread comes to its end she does not cut it. She ties it off into the row,/
a small knot at the back of the cloth where the cloth is not looked at,/
so that the row does not loosen where the thread was./
And she lays the end in the basket in the corner, and the Silent Judge takes it up,/
and holds it, and hears what it has to say for itself, and says nothing./
The knot stays in the cloth. The row holds. That is a death, daughters./
A House that ties off its dead well is a row that does not loosen.

[All:]{.speaker}/
Weaver, tie us off well, when we come to our ends./
Tie our mothers off well. Let the row hold where they were.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And at the edge of the cloth, where the threads turn back, she wove the selvedge,/
the hard edge that does not fray, a hand's width, all in the purple of the sea,/
and when the first rows were done she cut a strip of it from the edge, a little one,/
and gave it to the first of the daughters who was named to hold a House, and said:/
Wear the edge of the world over your face, so that you see the world through what holds it./
That is the Veil, daughters. The Veiled Mother wears the selvedge of the world./
Every Veil since is woven in the temple from a thread of the last one,/
and the girls who weave it sing this hymn while they weave.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
And here is ours, the part we sing for ourselves, standing in the garden in our rows./
Some of us will be named, and hold a seal, and sit on a bench in the hall./
Some of us will be passed over, and take a lesser office, and wait./
Some of us will go to the Waters and the dry country, and some to the spear,/
and some to the rolls, and some to the temple, and stand here again with girls of our own./
The thread that is passed over is still in the warp, daughters. It still holds the cloth./
No thread in the warp is a lesser thread. The beam holds every one of us the same.

[All:]{.speaker}/
We do not know which of us is which. The Weaver knows. We sing anyway.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
And now we praise our leader, as our mothers praised theirs in this garden,/
for she stands at the end of the beam where the first knot is, and we cannot stand there./
She is the first thread this year. We hang beside her and we are glad to.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
Her hair is the dark of the first dye before the light gets into it./
Her voice is the one the others are measured against, as the bronze weights are./
She is of House Ghodunes, and her mother's name is in the roll before her,/
and her name is Thuressa, and we have said it, and the garden heard it.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
We do not say we are as fair as she is. We say we stand beside her./
A thread is not the beam. A thread is glad of the beam.

[All:]{.speaker}/
Next year another will lead, and we will say her name as we have said this one,/
and this one will stand with us in the row, and be glad of it, and sing.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
Hear now the end, daughters, which is not yet, and which no one here will see./
When the cloth is long enough the Weaver will stand up from the loom,/
and take the knife from the beam where it hangs, the bronze knife with the vine on its handle,/
and cut the cloth from the loom along the bottom where the weights are,/
and the weights will fall, and the cloth will be slack in her hands at last,/
and she will fold it, and lay it in the chest in the high house, finished.

[The weights:]{.speaker}/
Some say the cutting is a storm. The sailors say it, on the hills, at their vigil./
Some say the cloth will be cut when the last House fails to name a daughter./
The temple says neither. The temple says: She will know when it is long enough./
We do not know. We are threads. A thread does not know the length of the cloth.

[The beam:]{.speaker}/
But this the temple knows and we know, because it is the way of every House:/
she will not set the knife down when the cloth is cut./
She will hand it to her daughter, in front of the gods, in front of the room,/
and her daughter will draw a new thread from the heap and knot it to the beam./
The world that is finished will lie in the chest. The world that is next will hang./
A House names its daughter in public, and the Weaver will name hers.

[All:]{.speaker}/
Until then the cloth is on the loom, and the shuttle goes through,/
and the warp is taut, and the weights are hung, and the dry country waits,/
and we weave, daughters, every year, one thread past where she stopped./
Weaver, keep the weights on us. Keep us taut, and keep us right./
Harmony holds the world together.

[The gardeners, from the field:]{.speaker}/
Seed's in. Third bed's done./
Water's at the second gate. Shut it./
Open the _en-muna_ on the low side. Not all the way./
Next row./
Seed's in.
