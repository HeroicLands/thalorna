**The Watch-Song of the Vigil** is what the crews of the [[affiliation-bethuanflt|Bethuan Fleet]] and the merchant galleys of the coast sing through the night of the [Thunderhead Vigil]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, at the onset of spring, when the storm-priests of the [[affiliation-brazemas|Faith of Brazemas]] go up onto the hills and the ships lie at anchor in the bays with their oars shipped, waiting for the first storm of the sea-year. Each galley sings its own, so a bay on the night of the Vigil carries a dozen versions of it at once across the water. It runs through the four watches of the night, and the bench, the stern and the storm-priest aboard each have their parts. Up to its last line it is in the practical register of the sea, the speech that every ship-mistress and every oarsman uses aboard whoever they are, thick with the Khelathi words of the trade, and the storm-priests sing their parts in it too. The last line is the temple's, and the stern, which is the ship-mistress, sings it.

The gods men favor have no place on the bench of the state cult, and [[lore-brazemasdty|Brazemas]], the Thunderer, is the sailor's god before any other. Every Bethuan galley keeps a small shrine to him at the foot of the mast and gives him the first of the bread. The song tells the making of the world in the storm-priests' version, in which the Thunderer launched it like a hull off the slip. The temple's version is the Weaver's loom ([[lore-loomworld|The Loom of the World]]), and the song does not argue with it.

The dead watch carries the fleet's memory of [[lore-bethuasep|the Separation]]: the night the first storm of the year came early and caught [[affiliation-pelagora|Pelagora]]'s fleet on a lee shore off the headland, before the Vigil was kept. The song gives three accounts of who won that night and does not choose among them. It does not say how long the war ran or how many hulls the mother-city sent, and the version the rendering gives is the one sung on the galleys of [[place-bethura|Bethura]]. The year's lost ships in the second watch change every year, and the rendering keeps the names of the year it was taken down.

: The Watch-Song of the Vigil {#vigil}

::: {.verse form=vigil-song lang=en}
[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Oars in. Ship them. Lash them./
Anchor's down and holding. Lamps lit, fore and aft./
Who's awake?

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
The bench is awake.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Who's watching the sky?

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
The whole bay's watching the sky.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Then sing the first watch, and keep your eyes up./
The Thunderer's coming tonight, or tomorrow night, or the night after./
He's never not come. Keep your eyes up.

First watch.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
Here's how it was launched, the world. Listen./
Before the world there was the slip, and nothing on it./
The Thunderer came down to the slip with an adze in his hand/
and built the world on it like a hull, keel first, the long way,/
and ribbed her, and planked her, and pitched her, and stepped the mast./
That took him a long time. Longer than any yard on this coast./
And when she was done she sat on the slip, dry, and couldn't float./
A hull on a slip is a house. She has to go into the water to be a ship.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Knock the props out.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
So he went up the hill and called the first storm,/
the biggest there's ever been, a storm out of nothing,/
and the sea came up the beach in it higher than the beach went,/
and lifted her off the slip, and she slid, and she floated./
And she's floating still, the world, with all of us aboard her,/
and every year at this time he calls a storm to see she's sound.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Knock the props out. Into the water.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
And the thunder? What's the thunder?

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
That's him knocking on the planks./
A shipwright goes along a hull with a mallet and knocks, plank by plank,/
and listens, and a sound plank rings, and a rotten one thuds./
That's what he's doing up there every spring with the thunder. He's knocking the world./
Listen to it when it comes. It rings. It's always rung. Keep your eyes up.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
The temple tells it another way. The temple says a loom./
That's the temple's. This is the sea's. Both's true./
A loom on a slip is a thing nobody's seen. A hull's in front of you.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
And the yards on the slip on the coast tonight, the hulls half-built,/
the ribs up and the planks not on, they sit dry and wait for him too.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
They do. Every hull on a slip on this coast has a jar of seawater set under her keel tonight,/
so she knows what she's waiting for. Every yard does it. Every yard always has.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Bread. First of the bread to the mast. You know whose it is.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
His. Under the mast. In the shrine. The first of it./
And the rest's ours. And the rest's ours.

Second watch.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Now count them. The ones that didn't come home this year./
Count them while it's dark. You'll want the dark for it.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Count them.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The Second Daughter, out of Bethura, Nauklera Ghalisa, of House Safirides./
Took a Vilérian with grain off the islands on the [Purple Warrant]{.name data-type="lore/law"},/
and the grain came home to the _khabetun_, and she didn't./
A northern sweep caught her in the narrows on the way back for more./
Her benches went down with her. Forty at the oars. Their names are at the temple.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Forty at the oars.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The Fig of Oshira, [House Khalath-Mara]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}'s, oil to the north, in convoy./
Fell out of the convoy in a fog off the northern capes, and was never seen./
No wreck. No word. Her factor waits on the northern quay with her manifest.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Never seen.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The Old Mother, the Order's water-barge, Bethura to the river-ports of the east,/
the slowest hull on the coast and the oldest, sixty years on the water,/
opened her seams in the spring blow off the low cape and went down at her moorings./
Nobody lost. Every man swam. She was old. We count her anyway.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Every man swam.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The Purple Hound, a warrant-galley, Nauklera Ghígnana, of no House now./
Took a ship she shouldn't have, a Helionite, and the warrant was recalled,/
and the Hound went south to the islands and didn't come back to be struck./
The roll has her struck off. The sea hasn't said. We count her.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
We count her.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
And the little ones. The fishing-boats of the bay that the spring blow took./
Nine of them. The men's names are at the shrine on the mole./
Nine boats, and the nets with them.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Nine boats.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
And the other count, the one the counting-houses like. What came home.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Eleven prizes under the Warrant. Grain and wine and northern steel./
The _khabetun_ full on every quay from here to the low cape./
The _et-degan_ paid on every jar. The Houses' chests heavy./
The temple of the Bountiful One took its share, and the crown its share,/
and the bench its share, which was a feast at the Golden Harvest and a new cloak each.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Eleven prizes, and four hulls. Say both.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Eleven prizes, and four hulls.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
That's the year. That's the count. The roll's got the women, the temple's got the men,/
the shrine's got the boats. Say the count back.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Four hulls and nine boats, and the benches that went with them./
That's the year.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
The Thunderer took some of them. The north took some. The south took one./
He doesn't say which was his. He never says. Keep your eyes up.

Dead watch.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
This is the long one. This is the watch where you stay awake by singing it./
Sing the year he came early.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
The year he came early.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The year the mother sent for her daughter./
Pelagora, across the water. The mother-city. You know the name./
Twelve hundred years she had us, they say, and took the oil, and kept the harbor,/
and never once came to live with us. And the year we said no more,/
she sent her admiral with her fleet to bring the daughter home./
Féranos Rhéshides. That was the admiral. They still say it in Pelagora.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Bring the daughter home.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
His fleet was bigger than ours. That's not a song, that's a count./
Big hulls, three banks of oars, northern-built, northern-crewed, the best on the sea./
Ours were the little ones. The fast ones. Fewer. Some of them new,/
built that winter in the yards with the delta's gold and the delta's pitch./
And his fleet came down the coast in the spring, before the Vigil,/
to anchor off the headland and wait for weather, and come into the bay.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Before the Vigil.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Nobody had kept the Vigil yet that year. Nobody was on the hills./
It wasn't the night. The priests said it wasn't the night./
And he anchored off the headland on the north side, the lee side,/
with the land at his back, because he didn't know our water./
A northern admiral doesn't know our water. Never has.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Never has.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
And in the night the Thunderer came. Early./
Out of the north with no warning, the first storm of the year, a week before his night,/
and the wind came round onto the land and stayed there,/
and every big hull off the headland was on a lee shore with her anchors dragging,/
and the sea came up, and the rain came down like a beam falling./
And our little ones were in the bay, behind the headland, where we know the water,/
and we lay there all night and listened to it.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
And listened to it.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
You could hear them on the rocks from inside the bay. All night./
You could hear the timbers going.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
All night.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
In the morning we went out./
The Spear-Sisters at the rails. The Spadai with them. The ship-mistresses at the sterns./
Every little hull in the bay, oars out, into what the storm had left,/
and the storm had left a great deal on the rocks, and some still floating,/
and what was floating we took, and what was on the rocks we took off it,/
and the admiral's own hull was on the rocks with her back broken,/
and we took him off her alive, and he went home in a fishing-boat, they say,/
to tell his mother what her daughter said.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
To tell his mother.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
And now the three. Sing the three. Who won it?

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Ask the Spear-Sisters, and it was the spears at the rail in the morning./
Ask the counting-houses, and it was the delta's gold in the yards in the winter./
Ask the bench, and the Thunderer came early, and we knew the water.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
All three's true. Say it.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
All three's true.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
And the fourth thing. Sing the fourth thing. You always sing it.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Some of our hulls went out that morning too soon, while the sea was still up./
Two of them went over on the rocks with the big ones./
And their benches were chained, some of them, the bought men,/
and nobody had time for the keys./
They went down with their hulls, chained to the benches./
Their names aren't at the temple. They were bought. The shrine on the mole has them./
We sing them. We sing them every year. That's the fourth thing.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
That's the fourth thing./
Keep your eyes up. It's nearly the grey.

Grey watch.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
Here he comes. Look north. There. The cloud on the water./
See it climb. See it stand up. Like a mast.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Like a mast.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
That's him. That's the head of him. Wait for it./
Nobody says anything now. Nobody moves. Wait.

The whole deck is silent. When the first lightning comes down out of the cloud, every voice on every ship in the bay and every voice on the hills cries out at once, with no words in it, and stops.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
North, and over the water, and a long one./
Over the water's a hard year at sea. Long's a long year./
North's the north. You know what's north./
That's the reading. That's what he says. Mark it.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Marked./
Hard year, long year, the north. Same as most.

[The storm-priest:]{.speaker}/
Same as last year, the year the Hound was lost. Same as the year before that./
The year he came early the reading was south and short, and the Pelagoran never heard it./
Mark that too. He doesn't read the same for everyone on the water.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Then here's what the bench asks him, same as every year, and he can do as he likes with it./
A dry bench. A short pull. A wind from astern on the way home./
A convoy that keeps together in a fog./
No sweep in the narrows. No Helionite on the horizon./
A captain who takes the ship she's meant to take and not the other one./
The shrine fed every day, and the bread enough to feed it from./
And the keys on a nail by the benches where a man can reach them.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
The keys are on the nail. I put them there. Look.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
The keys are on the nail.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
Same as most.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Then the sea-year's open./
Unlash the oars. Oars out. Anchor up when I say./
We go out through the mouth of the bay on the first of the light,/
and every ship in the bay goes out after us,/
and the ones that come back next year will sing this,/
and count the ones that didn't.

[The bench:]{.speaker}/
And count the ones that didn't./
Who's awake?/
The bench is awake.

[The stern:]{.speaker}/
Harmony holds the world together./
Anchor up.
