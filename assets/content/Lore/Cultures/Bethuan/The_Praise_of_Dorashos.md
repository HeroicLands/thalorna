**The Praise of Dorashos** is the song the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] sings at the head of the great quay of [[place-bethura|Bethura]] each time a cadre of the [Spadai]{.name data-type="affiliation"} is sworn. The new Spadai stand in a line at the head of the quay with the steel the crown has given them across their hands, the Hiorai stand facing them in two ranks, and the women of the [Spear-Sisterhood]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} stand at the top of the quay where the Lorkhissa stood on the day the song is about. The harbor-quarter comes down to watch, as it does for nothing else the temple does on the quay.

The Spadai are praised in song and ceremony, and this is the first song a new cadre hears. It praises [Dorashos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Spados posted to the Bethura quays and a son of the harbor-quarter before his oath, who walked into a rising on the quays with his blade sheathed in the year the Houses' granaries stood sealed, and named the men in front of him one by one by their mothers' Houses, until the oar-gangs went back to the benches. It names the men he named. The temple keeps their names because Dorashos said them aloud, and holds that a name a Spados said in that place belongs to the song. The rising has its own name in the quarter, [the Stopping of the Oars]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, which the song does not use.

The song is in the feminine register and it is the temple's, and it says what the realm asks of a Spados: strength held, and loyalty that prevents the worst and does not need to answer it. It says nothing of the quarter's side, and the quarter answers for itself. When the Hiorai have sung the last word, men in the crowd call two lines back up the quay in the speech of the docks. The temple has not written them into its text and has not stopped them, and the Spear-Sisters at the top of the quay let them pass. Every sworn cadre in living memory has heard them.

: The Praise of Dorashos {#praise}

::: {.verse form=praise lang=en}
[The Hiorai:]{.speaker}/
You who stand at the head of the quay with the crown's steel across your hands,/
who were sons of Houses and are sons of none, and are honored above all men for it,/
hear, before your hands close on what you are given,/
how a man of your sworn kind stood on these stones in the year the oars stopped,/
and held the realm quiet with the steel that you are holding now,/
and did not draw it.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

Hear first the year, sworn ones, for a praise without its year is a praise of nothing./
It was a lean year on the coast and a leaner one inland, and the barges came down short,/
and the Houses of the harbor sealed their granaries against the winter,/
as a House must, for a House that eats its seed in autumn starves its daughters in spring,/
and the seals were on the doors, and the doors were shut, and the quarter was hungry./
The roll does not blame the Houses for the seals, and neither does this song./
It is the Houses' to seal and the Houses' to open. Hear what opened them.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

In that year, on a morning in the month the convoys sail, the oars stopped./
The gangs came down from the quarter to the galleys at the tide, as on every morning,/
and went down onto the benches, as on every morning,/
and took up the oars, and laid them across their knees, and did not row./
And the gang on the next galley saw it, and laid theirs down,/
and the next, and the next, down the length of the quay to the mole,/
until there was not an oar moving in the harbor of Bethura,/
and the tide went out under the hulls and no hull went with it.

And the men came up off the benches onto the quay, and stood on it,/
the men of the oars and the men of the nets and the porters of the warehouses,/
the whole harbor-quarter, all of it, standing on the stones,/
more of them than anyone on the quay had ever counted, for no one counts them all at once,/
and they were not shouting yet. They stood. That was worse.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

At the top of the quay the Spear-Sisters came down from their posts and stood in their line,/
and the Lorkhissa of the quay stood at the end of it with her spear grounded,/
and she sent to the Court of the Veil for word, and waited for it,/
and the word she was waiting for was the word that is never said./
Sworn ones, you know the word. The realm has kept a word like that since the first seal./
Pray to the goddesses on the bench that you never hear it given.

There was one man of your sworn kind on the quay that morning, and he was Dorashos./
He was posted to the quay, as some of you will be posted there,/
to stand at the head of it with his steel and be seen to stand there,/
and he had been taken from that quarter as a boy and sworn,/
and he had been a son of it, of a mother of it, before the oath made him a son of none./
He knew the stones. He had played on them. He had been beaten on them for stealing figs.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

Hear how he was given, sworn ones, for you were given as he was./
His mother was a woman of the quarter, of a small House, a House of nets,/
and he was the most beautiful of her sons and the quickest, and she saw it,/
and she put him forward for the oath when he was a boy, as a mother may,/
and walked him up the steps of the quay to the hall of the Spadai herself,/
and gave him to the realm with her own hand, and was proud, and the quarter saw her proud./
He did not go back to her House. A Spados has no House to go back to./
But he knew it, and he knew its door, and he knew every door on the street of it,/
and the oath that took the House from him did not take the street out of his head.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

He did not go to the Lorkhissa. He did not wait for the word./
He took off the plate of his armor and laid it at the foot of the steps,/
and kept his blade at his hip in its sheath, and tied the sheath shut with a cord,/
so that every man on the quay could see that it was tied./
And he went down the steps of the quay into the harbor-quarter, alone,/
and the men moved back from him as he came, as water moves from an oar,/
and closed again behind him, and he was among them.

And he stopped in front of the first man, and he named him.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Ghérvanos, he said, of House Bashiros, your mother keeps the net-loft on the hard./
She mended my net when I was eight and did not tell my mother I had torn it./
Go and tell her the net is still mended, Ghérvanos. Go back to the bench.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Ghérvanos went.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Udrisos, he said, of House Zakinos, you row stroke on the second galley of the convoy./
The whole bench rows to you. If you are standing here, the bench is standing./
Sit down, Udrisos, and the bench will sit, and the galley will go out.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Udrisos sat, and his bench with him.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Lothion, he said, of House Wadines, your sister is at the top of the quay in the line./
She is the third from the end. I can see her from here. So can you./
Do not make her look for you, Lothion.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Lothion looked up the quay, and went down to the benches.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Mubaron, he said, of House Tamides, cooper, your staves are in every cask on this quay./
The wine will not go out in them today. It will go out in them tomorrow./
Go and see that your casks are tight, Mubaron.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Mubaron went to see.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Járudon, he said, of House Juminos, your woman had a son this month, and he is entered./
I heard his name read at the temple door. It is a good name. Go home to it.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Járudon went home.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Sifanos, he said, your mother died in the winter. I know. I stood at the gate for her./
You are entered under your mother's mother now, Rakira of House Ghotones,/
and she is old, and she is at her door on the hill watching this quay. Go up to her.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Sifanos went up the hill.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Teíkharos, he said, of House Farikos, old man, you taught me to bait a hook./
You are too old to stand here and too old to row. Sit on the bollard, Teíkharos./
Sit there and watch me, and tell me afterward if I did it right.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Teíkharos sat on the bollard, and watched him.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Azidás, he said, of House Makithon, you owe the wine-shop on the corner for the winter./
Your mother paid it at the Silent Passage and did not tell you. Go and thank her.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Azidás went to thank her.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Dámasun and Dókhares, he said, of House Yássines, brothers, both of you,/
your mother has two sons on this quay and one boat on the hard with a hole in it./
Go and mend the boat. She cannot mend it, and she cannot afford two sons on a quay.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And the brothers went to mend the boat, and argued all the way down the hard.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Wissaros, he said, of House Nimaros, you sing at the oar, the whole bench knows your voice./
I have heard you from the head of the quay on still mornings. Go and sing them out.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Wissaros went down to the benches singing, and the bench took it up.

[The Hiorai:]{.speaker}/
And the women of the quarter came to their doors, sworn ones, all along the streets above the quay,/
the mothers and the wives and the women the men were entered under,/
and they heard the names come up from the stones one at a time in his voice,/
and each woman who heard her own House said aloud stood in her door and called,/
not loud, the way a woman calls a man in for his supper,/
and her man looked up the street at her, and went./
The song honors the women at the doors. They did not come down. They called./
A House calls its own, and its own comes. That is a House.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

[The Hiorai:]{.speaker}/
So he went through the quarter, sworn ones, man by man, along the whole length of the quay,/
and named each one, and named the House of each one's mother,/
and said one thing to each that only a son of that quarter could have said,/
and each man he named went back to the benches or the nets or his door,/
and the crowd on the stones grew thin around him as the tide grows thin on the hard,/
and the morning went by, and he did not stop, and he did not raise his voice,/
and he did not once put his hand on the cord that tied his sheath.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

He knew their mothers. Hear it, sworn ones, for it is the whole of the praise./
A man who has no House knew every man's House on that quay./
The oath took his House from him, and he gave every man on that quay his House back,/
one name at a time, aloud, in front of the others,/
and a man who has heard his mother's House said aloud in front of the quay/
goes back to the bench, because the bench is where her son is meant to be./
That is what a name does, sworn ones. The first seal held on a night of names.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

There was one man left at the end of the quay by the mole when the morning was over,/
and he had a gaff-hook in his hand, the long hook for the fish-boats, iron at the end of it./
The song does not say what he meant by it. The song does not need to./
Dorashos came to him last, and stood in front of him, and named him.

[The first rank:]{.speaker}/
Khankhor, he said, of House Sufanon, your mother is Krenirtha, and she is not well./
She was not well at the Silent Passage and she is not well now./
Give me the hook, Khankhor, and go and sit with her.

[The second rank:]{.speaker}/
And Khankhor gave him the hook.

[The Hiorai:]{.speaker}/
Dorashos put it through his own belt, beside the tied sheath, and walked up the quay with it,/
and up the steps, and past the Lorkhissa, and no one saw the hook after./
The roll keeps what became of Khankhor. The song does not./
A Spados carries what a man must not carry, so that the man need not answer for it./
That is the second thing you are sworn to, and it is heavier than the first.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

The gangs rowed on the next tide. Every oar in the harbor of Bethura went into the water/
on the next tide, together, as if one stroke had called them all,/
and the convoy went out of the bay with its wine and its oil in Mubaron's casks,/
and the Lorkhissa at the top of the quay lifted her spear from the stone/
and turned it, and carried it back to her post, point up, and the line went with her./
The word she had sent for never came, and was never needed./
The Spear-Sisterhood was not loosed on the quarter, then or after.

And within the week the Houses of the harbor broke their own seals on the granaries,/
each House its own, and opened the doors, and the quarter ate./
The Houses opened them because it was theirs to open them, and they chose to./
The song praises the Houses for the opening, and praises them well.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

Then the Court of the Veil sent for Dorashos, and the Metrissa praised him,/
and the Houses praised him, every one, in the hall of the Council,/
and the crown gave him quarters at the head of the quay for the rest of his life,/
and a place on the bench in the hall of the Spadai, the bench nearest the door,/
which is the crown's bench, and he sat on it, and it was honor./
A Spados owns nothing, sworn ones. You own nothing. You are given everything./
He sat on the bench that was given him and did not ask whose it was,/
and when he died it was given to the next, and it is given still.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

He grew old on the quay, sworn ones, at the head of it, in the quarters the crown gave him,/
and the children of the quarter came up the steps to look at him,/
and he named them, every one, and their mothers, and their mothers' Houses,/
and when he died the Houses buried him at the crown's cost, in the crown's crypt,/
for he had no House and no crypt of his own, and the priests of the Silent Judge sat with him,/
and he spoke his testimony in his own voice, and it is kept, and the song does not tell it./
The song was first sung at the head of the quay in the year he died,/
over the cadre that was sworn that spring, and it has been sung over every cadre since,/
and the names in it are the names he said, and no name has been added to them.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

Hear what the realm asks of you, sworn ones, for it asked it of him, and he gave it./
It asks you to set down what makes a man a danger, and you have set it down./
No House to raise. No sons to raise in it. No stake in any rising./
It asks you to take up what no other man may take up, and to be trusted with it,/
and to be honored for it above all men, and to own nothing by it./
It asks you to be strong, for you are strong, and to keep your strength asleep./
It does not ask you to love the quarter. He loved it. It does not ask you to./
It asks you to know it. Learn it as he learned it, door by door,/
so that on the morning the oars stop, you can say its names.

Strength held, and the blade asleep.

Now close your hands on what you are given./
Hold it as he held it: sheathed, and tied, and at your hip, and seen./
Know the names. Learn the quarter. Learn whose mother keeps the net-loft./
Carry what a man must not carry, so that he need not answer for it./
Hold the realm quiet with your own body, and pray the word is never given,/
and if it is never given, it will be because of you, and no one will know it,/
and the song will be sung for you at the head of the quay, and that will be enough.

Strength held, and the blade asleep./
Harmony.

[From the crowd on the quay:]{.speaker}/
He came with it sheathed./
He came with it, though.
