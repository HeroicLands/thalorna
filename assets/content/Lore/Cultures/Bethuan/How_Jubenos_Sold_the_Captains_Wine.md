**How Júbenos Sold the Captain's Wine** is the tale the men of the Bethuan galleys tell on the long pull home, when a convoy has a day of rowing ahead of it and the wind will not help. The stroke-oar tells it, because the whole bench rows to him, and he tells it a stroke at a time: he says a piece, the bench pulls, and on the pull the bench answers him with the call that runs through the whole tale. The harbor-quarters of the coast tell it in the wine-shops too, without the oars, and the call is beaten on the table instead.

It is told in the practical register of the docks, and the tellers make a sport of it: the tale is meant to carry as many words of the quay as it can bear, and a stroke-oar who finds a place for one more [[skill-khelathlng|Khelathi]] loanword is cheered for it down the bench. The rendering keeps the loans the tellers lean on hardest, among them _en-telekas_ (the quay), _khabetun_ (a bonded warehouse), _et-degan_ (the customs-due) and _kathur_ (a seal).

The tale belongs to the men, and the Houses know it as well as the benches do. The women of the trading Houses tell it at table as a story about northern captains, and laugh at the court. The benches tell it as a story about [Júbenos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a porter of [House Habines]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} on the quay of [[place-bethura|Bethura]], and laugh at the sandals. Where each audience laughs is the difference between the two tellings, and neither changes a word for the other. The captain of the yellow ship has no name in either.

## The Call

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

You want Júbenos? You'll get Júbenos. Long pull to the headland and no wind on it, so you'll get all of him. Backs into it.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

Júbenos was a porter on the _en-telekas_ at Bethura, the quay with the long warehouses, back when your grandmothers' grandmothers were girls. He was entered under Krenania of House Habines, which was a little House, a wine House, three vineyards and a _khabetun_ on the quay with its name painted over the door. Krenania was a widow, and the roll had him under her as her man. He carried her jars. He was not handsome. Say it with me: he was not handsome. He had a face like a cask somebody sat on. But he had a voice that carried the length of the quay, and he could count anything you set in front of him, and he could tell you what a man wanted before the man knew he was going to want it.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Yellow Ship

One morning in the sailing season a ship came into the bay from the north. A yellow ship, round in the belly, high in the stern, a northern merchant's hull with a northern captain on her, the kind with a beard you could lose a gull in. She tied up at the quay right in front of the Habines warehouse, because that was where the space was, and the captain came to the top of his gangplank and looked down.

He had wine in her hold, northern wine, a hundred jars and more, and he wanted to sell it, and he wanted to fill her up again with our oil, which is the best oil there is. You know what that's worth, the oil. Your mistresses know to the _qelu_.

The factors came down the quay to meet him, the way they do. Women of the Houses with their clerks behind them, the clerks with the sealed cases, the factors with their hands out for his manifest. And the captain looked at them, and looked over their heads, and said:

I deal with the master.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

The factors laughed at him. Not loud. A factor doesn't laugh loud at a man with a hundred jars in his hold. One of them said: I am the factor of [House Othris-Khanu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, and there is no master here, captain, there is a Dómissa, and if you want to sell you sell to me. And the captain said: I have sailed the whole sea, and I have never yet sold my wine to a woman, and I will not begin in a port where the men carry jars and wear kilts like children. Send me the master.

And he folded his arms on the top of his gangplank and would not come down.

That's a northern captain. That's every northern captain you've ever pulled past. He'll stand on his gangplank until his wine goes sour sooner than put his manifest in a woman's hand. Laugh. Go on. The next stroke's coming anyway.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Master

Now, Júbenos was standing in the door of the Habines _khabetun_ with a jar on his shoulder, and he heard it. The whole quay heard it. And Júbenos set the jar down very carefully on the stones, and wiped his hands on his kilt, and walked over to the foot of the gangplank, and looked up at the captain, and said:

I'm the master here.

And the factors looked at him. And the porters looked at him. And the Spear-Sisters at the head of the quay looked at him, which you do not want, but they didn't move, because he had nothing in his hands. And the captain looked down at him and smiled all the way through his beard.

At last, the captain said. A man. Come up.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

So Júbenos went up the gangplank onto the yellow ship, and the captain took him into the stern and poured him a cup of the northern wine, which was thin, and sat him down, and looked at him like a man looks at a dog somebody has kept on a short rope.

What do they call you, the captain said.

They call me _kiris_, Júbenos said. That's the truth, and he said it with a straight face. It means master.

I'd heard they call their men that, the captain said. And then they make them carry jars.

That's right, Júbenos said.

And you can't own the jar you carry, the captain said.

No, Júbenos said.

Nor the kilt you're wearing.

That's my mistress's, Júbenos said. She's very fond of it.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Captain's Pity

The captain put his hand on Júbenos's knee. He was moved, you see. He had a heart under that beard, and it went out to Júbenos, and he leaned in and said:

In the north, a man like you would have his own shop. His own name over the door. His own coin in his own purse. You'd deal with your own buyers on your own account, and no woman would take a _qelu_ of it from you. You're wasted here. You're a mule in a kilt.

That's very kind, Júbenos said.

Listen, the captain said. Here's what we'll do. You sell my wine for me. As your own, on your own account, in your own market. You know this quay. You know who buys. I can't get down there, they won't let me deal, but you can. You sell it, and you bring me the price, and I give you a third of it. A third, mind. Your own money, in your own hand, the first you've ever held. And next season when I sail north, you come with me. A free man. With a third of a hundred jars in your purse to start your shop with.

And Júbenos thought about it. A third of a hundred jars. He thought about it the length of a cup of thin wine.

Done, he said.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

The captain wanted it sealed, being a careful man, and he asked Júbenos for his _kathur_, and Júbenos showed him his two empty hands and said: we haven't got seals, the men. That's the women's. And the captain was sorry for him all over again, and he said: then a handshake, between men, the way it's done where men are men. And they shook on it, there in the stern of the yellow ship, and the captain's eyes were wet.

Say it with me. Between men.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Wine Goes Down

So the captain's sailors carried his hundred jars down the gangplank, and Júbenos stood at the bottom and counted them, and he counted them right, and then the question was where to put them. Because you can't leave a hundred jars of wine on the stones of the _en-telekas_ overnight. Every porter on the quay would have one under his kilt by morning.

Into a _khabetun_, Júbenos said. And which _khabetun_ was nearest? Habines. With the name painted over the door. And who had the key? Krenania. And who was Júbenos entered under? Krenania. So in they went, a hundred jars, into Krenania's warehouse, and the door shut, and Júbenos gave the key back to the clerk.

And the Harbor-Mistress's woman came down the quay with her tablet for the _et-degan_, the customs-due, because nothing goes into a warehouse on the quay of Bethura without the due paid on it, and she asked who was paying. And Júbenos pointed up the gangplank at the captain, and said: he is. It's his wine. And the captain paid it, glad to, in good northern silver, because it was his wine, and he was proud of it.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Market

Next morning Júbenos took the wine up to the market in the square below the temples, ten jars at a time on the Habines carts, and he stood by the carts and sang it out. That voice of his. You could hear him from the mole. Northern wine, he sang, thin as a captain's pity and sour as his beard, a novelty for your table, buy one for the strangeness of it. And they bought it. The women of the Houses came down and tasted it and made faces and bought it, a jar here and two there, because nobody on the coast had drunk the stuff, and it was something to set in front of a guest and laugh at. He sold every jar by the fourth day. He got a good price for it, too, better than it was worth.

And here's the thing, and you know it, and the captain didn't. When a woman of a House buys a jar in the market from a man, who does she pay?

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

Not the man. She can't pay the man. A man can't hold her silver. She pays the House the man is entered under. So the buyers' clerks wrote their tallies to House Habines, and the silver went to House Habines, and the tallies went into Krenania's chest under Krenania's seal, a hundred jars' worth, and not one _qelu_ of it ever touched Júbenos's hand. He never held it. He couldn't. He'd told the captain so himself, on the ship, over the thin wine.

Say it with me. He couldn't.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Captain Comes Down

On the fifth day the captain came down his gangplank for the first time, because Júbenos hadn't come up it, and he found Júbenos in the door of the _khabetun_ with a jar on his shoulder, carrying oil.

Where's my price, the captain said.

Sold, Júbenos said. All of it. Good price, too. Better than it was worth.

Then where is it, the captain said.

With my mistress, Júbenos said. Where else would it be?

And the captain went red all the way down into his beard, and went in at the door of House Habines, and found Krenania at her table with her clerk, and said she had his money.

And Krenania looked at him over her tablet. She was a small woman, Krenania, and grey, and she had buried a husband and two daughters and kept three vineyards through a drought, and a northern captain was nothing to her. She said:

I have made no bargain with you, captain. Show me my _kathur_ on it.

And he couldn't, because there wasn't one.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Harbor-Mistress's Court

So the captain went to the court. The Harbor-Mistress sits in judgment on the quay over anything that comes in by sea, at a table under the arch at the head of the _en-telekas_, with two clerks and a Spear-Sister and a jug of water. And the captain stood in front of her and told it: the porter who said he was the master, the bargain between men, the handshake, the third, the wine gone into the warehouse and sold, and the price sitting in a widow's chest. And the Harbor-Mistress listened to the whole of it without moving her face, and then she sent for Júbenos, and Krenania came with him, because a man cannot stand before a court except through the woman who answers for him.

And the Harbor-Mistress said to the captain: You made a bargain with this man.

Yes, the captain said.

In his own name, on his own account.

Yes.

Captain, she said, a man of Bethua cannot make a bargain in his own name. There is no such thing on this coast. You may as well have made a bargain with the bollard. There is no contract, and the court cannot hold anyone to a contract there is not.

He said he was the master, said the captain.

He is _kiris_, said the Harbor-Mistress. It means master. It also means nothing. Every child on this quay knows that. You did not ask one.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Reckoning

Now, you'd think that was the end of the captain's silver. And it wasn't, and this is why the women tell this tale at their tables and laugh, because the Harbor-Mistress was not a thief, and neither was Krenania, and this is Bethua and not some northern port where the strong man keeps what he can carry. Your mistresses pay what is owed, to the _qelu_. That's the whole of the coast's credit. That's why the northern captains come here at all, beards and all.

So the Harbor-Mistress said: There is no contract with the man. But the wine was yours, captain, and it went into this House's _khabetun_ by this House's man, and it was sold in the market on this House's carts by this House's man, and the price of it is in this House's chest. And a House that holds another's goods and sells them owes what any factor of this quay would owe for the same work. The court will reckon it.

And she had her clerk reckon it. Out loud. In front of the whole quay, because the whole quay had come to the arch by then to listen.

The price of a hundred jars at the market, as written on the tallies. Less the rent, the porterage, the carts, the stall, the crier, the factor's share and the court's own fee.

And the clerk wrote it all down, every line, and drew the line under it, and there was the captain's price.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

And then the Harbor-Mistress said: One thing more. The captain offered this man a third of the price, for his work, between men. There was no contract, so the court does not hold the captain to it. But a man's earnings are the earnings of the woman he is entered under. If the captain wishes to pay the third, the court will see it paid to House Habines.

And the captain said he did not wish to.

And the Harbor-Mistress said: Then the court notes that the captain's offer to this man was worth nothing, and that he made it knowing it, and the court is glad to have been told what a northern captain's pity is worth. It is worth nothing, captain. Exactly nothing. You may write that down too, clerk.

And the clerk wrote it down.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

So the captain got his price. All of it. Every _qelu_ the reckoning left him. And it was to the _qelu_ what Othris-Khanu would have given him on the first morning if he had come down his gangplank and dealt with the women, less the rent and the fees of five days of being a fool about it.

That's why the women laugh. Because he lost nothing but five days and his good opinion of himself, and he went away believing he'd been robbed, and he hadn't. He'd been charged.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## The Sandals

And Júbenos? What did Júbenos get?

Krenania took him back to the warehouse, and she sat down at her table, and she looked at him for a long while, and then she sent the clerk to the cobbler's on the hill. And the clerk came back with a pair of sandals, good ones, red leather, the kind the factors' clerks wear, with brass on the buckles. And Krenania gave them to Júbenos in front of the whole _khabetun_, every porter in it, and she said: For the singing. _Kiris_.

That's what he got. A pair of red sandals with brass buckles, and the word.

Say it with me. A pair of sandals.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

And they were good sandals. He wore them on feast days for the rest of his life, and they were buried with him, and there are porters on the quay at Bethura today who count themselves the sons of the sons of men who saw them. Every porter on the coast wanted a pair. The cobbler on the hill made a fortune out of red sandals that year, and he gave thanks for it at the shrine of [[lore-olveniadty|the Bountiful One]] like a merchant, and his mistress let him keep the shrine-candle.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

## North

Now the last of it, and then we're round the headland and you can breathe.

When the yellow ship had filled her hold with our oil at last, paid for at the full price, through a factor of Othris-Khanu, with the manifest put in a woman's hand like a sensible man, she made ready to sail. And on the last morning the captain came down his gangplank, and found Júbenos on the quay in his red sandals carrying a jar, and stopped him.

And the captain said: I'll forgive you the wine. You're a clever man, and you're wasted here. Come north with me anyway. I meant it. Your own shop. Your own name over the door.

And Júbenos set the jar down.

My own shop, he said.

Your own shop, the captain said.

And I'd pay my own _et-degan_, Júbenos said.

You would.

And I'd stand on my own gangplank, Júbenos said, and say I deal with the master.

You would, said the captain.

And I'd deal with men like you, said Júbenos, every day, for the rest of my life.

And the captain didn't answer him. And Júbenos picked the jar up again and put it on his shoulder, and went in at the door of the Habines _khabetun_, and didn't come out until the yellow ship had gone round the headland.

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

And did Júbenos go north?

You know he didn't. You're rowing past the place he didn't go.

Some on the quay say he thought about it every winter after, sitting in the door of the warehouse in his red sandals, looking north at the sea. Some say he never thought about it once. The tale doesn't say which. The tale says the captain went north with his beard and his price, and Júbenos stayed with his sandals and his word, and the quay got paid, and so did the court, and so did the cobbler.

And the bench? What does the bench get?

_Pull._ And the quay pays.

There's the headland. Easy all.
