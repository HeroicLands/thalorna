The **herd-clans** are the peoples of the [[place-sthrnsvnhs|Southern Savannahs]] who follow the herds and belong to no nation. The settled [[lore-nyalubaclt|Nyáluba]] have a league and a covenant; the herd-clans have lineages, and a lineage's loyalty seldom reaches past its cousins and the clans it has married into.

## Where They Live

Herd-clans range the whole grass country, from the wet northern belt where the herds calve, across the open central plains with their kopjes, acacia and baobab, to the dry southwest, where painted caves and rock-carvings mark older occupants. A few families keep to the perennial springs and river junctions where settled places such as [[place-ekundavlg|Ékunda]] grow.

## How They Live

Cattle are wealth, dowry, offering and food, and a household's count is its standing. Young warriors raid for cattle as a rite of passage, and feuds between clans run across generations. Hunters time their movements to the migrating herds and keep an etiquette of what may be taken, when and with what apology. A clan that controls a salt pan is rich, and a dispute over one is a lasting feud.

Authority is personal and short. A successful raid-leader or a wise elder may hold several lineages for a generation, and the authority ends with the holder. Lineages recite their ancestry in full, bury their dead at sacred places, and ask the ancestors for guidance on succession, migration and dispute.

## What They Look Like

Herd-clan people are lean and long-limbed, built for distance and for heat. Skin is brown to tawny, with a golden cast in the dry country, lighter on average than the Nyáluba's. Faces are weathered by sun and wind. Herders are tall; the hunter lineages of the dry southwest are slighter and shorter. Dress is hide and leather with ostrich-shell and bead ornament, and cattle-owning clans mark their stock and their people with ochre and fat.

## Language

The clans speak many unrelated tongues. Strangers meet in a rough trade-pidgin built on the language of the [[lore-nyalubaclt|Nyáluba]], the savannah's one written tongue, and the forest intermediaries use the same pidgin in the south.

## Neighbors

The northern margin is the meeting ground with the forest peoples. The [[lore-pewayeclt|Pewaye]] trade and marry across it, and through them the herd-clans meet everything the north sends south. The Khelathi, Bethuan and Okharin are known only as rumor.
