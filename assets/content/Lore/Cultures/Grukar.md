The Grukar have a culture, and none of them learned it. A Grukar hatches knowing how to hold a spear, which fungus is safe, where the tribe's water lies and what a Hushk's raised chin means, and it hatches wanting what its tribe's spawner wants. Everything a human would call the Grukar way of life follows from three facts about them: a tribe is one fertile [[being-grkrahk|Grukar-ahk]] and everything it has laid; a Grukar's mind arrives with its body and grows, if at all, only in the channel its caste allows; and nothing passes between them in words. [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar Folk]] describes the body and the society. This note describes what that society holds a Grukar to be for. The Grukar give no names, and every term below is a human one, coined by the frontier peoples who watch them.

## What Hatches With Them

The Grukar tell no story of where they came from, because they tell no stories. What a tribe knows of the past is what was fed into its layings: the roads a guest walked, the walls it studied, the fight it survived. A tribe remembers the shape of a river crossing three layings back and nothing at all about the tribe it budded from. It has no first ancestor, no founding and no age before its own spawner, and the oldest thing any Grukar knows is the scent of the Ahk that laid it.

## The Tribe Is Everything

**Good is whatever feeds, shelters and enlarges the tribe. Evil is whatever diminishes it.** That is not a teaching. The Ahk's one purpose is the welfare and increase of its tribe, and that purpose is dosed into every egg along with the caste. An Uk that carries a stone to the palisade is doing good. A Hushk that kills a rival Hushk is doing good too, because the spawner wants the strongest Hushk alive and none of them strong enough to reach for the chamber. A Sha that steers a raid away from a village with soldiers behind it is doing the most good of the three.

The Grukar judge nothing aloud, because they have nothing to judge with but the body and the scent. A judgment is a posture: deference, challenge, the throat bared, the bristles raised. What a human would call manners is the order of approach to food, to the water and to the chamber, and the posture a Grukar takes when a higher caste passes. A Grukar that takes food out of its turn is challenged at once, and the challenge settles it.

## Standing in the Nest

Success is to be near the chamber and alive. An Uk's standing is its caste, and it has no other. A Hushk's standing is the rivals it has outlived and the scars it carries, every one a trophy of a fight it won. A Sha's standing is the deference it collects for seeing what has not yet happened, and that deference has a ceiling the Sha must keep under: competence draws the spawner's attention, and the spawner's attention ends in an invitation.

Failure is to be useless or to be alone. An Uk that cannot work is not fed. A Hushk that loses a challenge is dead, or broken and set among the Uk. A Grukar cut off from its tribe is the worst failure the culture knows: it has no Ahk's scent to live inside, no caste around it to supply what its own mind lacks, and a lone Uk or Hushk does not survive the season.

The Grukar value most food, the spawn-chamber, the spawner's scent, a held place, a kill, and ground that can be fed from. They value least the stranger, the lone Grukar, ground unmarked by any tribe's scent, and waste of the tribe's own game. They feel no need to justify any of it.

## The Hushk the Tribe Defers To

The Grukar have no men and no women, so the two admired figures of their culture are two castes at their best. The first is an old [[being-grkrhshk|Hushk]]: one that has outlived every rival of its laying, killed more of the tribe's enemies than any other, and learned from every fight it survived exactly where a shield-wall gives and which neighbors break when pressed. The Uk move aside for it before it reaches them, the younger Hushk do not challenge it, and a Sha stands at its elbow and is listened to. Within its tribe it is the nearest thing to a lord.

Its cost is that it is the most watched creature in the nest. An old Hushk is the one Grukar strong enough and long-lived enough to reach the thought the spawner most fears, that the chamber is worth seizing rather than serving. So the spawner lays the next Hushk against it, and a Sha brings it the rumor of a rival, and the old Hushk spends its last strength on a fight in front of it rather than the one it could have understood. When it dies, a Hushk that walked away from a battle no other Grukar survived is invited to dinner. The tribe's greatest warrior is admired for winning every fight it meets, and kept from the one that matters.

## The Sha That Comes Home

The second admired figure is a [[being-grkrsh|Sha]] that goes out and comes back. It watches human roads for three seasons, learns enough of a human tongue to hear what a market says about the soldiers, studies the walls and the guard changes, and brings all of it home to the spawner. It is the only Grukar that can be told a thing rather than survive it, and the only one that can carry an idea in from beyond the tribe. The deference it collects is the deference owed to something uncanny.

Its admiration is not a lesser kind of the Hushk's. The Hushk keeps the tribe alive this season; the Sha that comes home is the reason the next laying hatches knowing the roads. Its cost is the same as its reward. Sooner or later it knows more than the tribe can afford to lose, and it is received in the spawn-chamber with every honor the tribe can muster, and its brain and the long cord of its nerves are fed to the Ahk. A Sha that comes home many times and is never invited has made itself exactly useful enough and never remarkable, and that is the greatest skill a Sha can have.

## What the Nest Keeps

The Grukar write nothing, carve nothing to be read and keep no record but the next laying.

- **The laying** is the tribe's charter and its archive. What a clutch hatches knowing is the sum of what the spawner has been fed, and a tribe's whole store of knowledge is the young it has laid since its last guest.
- **The spawn-chamber** is the tribe's one fixed place. The tribe is where the Ahk is, and the chamber is the warmest, best-guarded and most strongly scented place in the nest.
- **The scent-marks** on the edge of a tribe's ground are its boundary, refreshed by the Hushk on their rounds and backed by a boundary bellow from anyone who crosses them.
- **The scars** of a Hushk are the only lasting record of a deed. A Grukar reads a Hushk's standing from its hide before it reads anything else.

## The Day Kept

The Grukar have no gods, no priests and no prayers, and no frontier people has ever seen one of them make an offering. The nearest thing to a religious practice is the spawner's scent itself. Every Grukar of a tribe lives inside it, and an Uk set to work at the outer palisade turns its head toward the chamber when the wind changes. On an ordinary day the Uk work and are driven, the Hushk patrol and challenge one another, the Sha watch, and the hunters go out at the intervals the spawner sets, so that the game around the nest is taken and never taken out.

## The Round of the Year

A tribe keeps no calendar. Its year is measured in layings: an Uk clutch hatches in six weeks, a Hushk clutch in twelve, a Sha or Ahk clutch in eighteen, and every clutch is the spawner's wager on where the tribe will stand when it hatches. Three events stand in for festivals.

- **A laying of Hushk** runs through the tribe like a declaration of war, which it is. It is for the raid the spawner expects a season from now.
- **A raiding season** comes when the tribe has surplus Hushk and a weaker neighbor within reach. It is for food and for whatever the tribe can carry, and the spawner paces it so that the same herds and the same villages recover between raids.
- **An invitation** is the one occasion the whole nest marks. It is for keeping what one Grukar learned.

In a lean year the round turns down. The spawner lays Uk instead of Hushk, and then fewer Uk, and the hunters go farther out.

## Rewards and Punishments

Reward is food, nearness and the invitation. A Grukar that has done well eats sooner. A Hushk that has done well is let nearer the chamber, and a proto-Ahk's place at the chamber door is a reward in the sense that no other is: it holds the post because it cannot survive anywhere else. The highest reward is the invitation to dinner, and it cannot be refused.

Punishment runs from the boot to abandonment. The first is the Hushk's boot, which is how work is got out of the Uk. The second is food withheld. The third is a challenge by a Hushk, which ends in submission, a crippling or a death. The last, which the spawner reserves for the troublemakers it cannot afford to keep, is to be let go: a hint reaches a Sha that the chamber's guard is lazy on the night side, and the faction that leaves takes its troublemakers with it.

## The Spawner's Hand

The Grukar have one authority, and it is the spawner. It is not a lord and not a priest, because it does not command and it does not explain. It lays the castes the tribe needs, permits the quarrels that keep the Hushk weak, protects the Sha and uses them, and lets a faction go when keeping it would cost more. Every instruction it gives travels through the Sha and the scent, and no Grukar can say that the spawner told it anything.

No Grukar tries a case. A dispute between two Hushk is settled by a fight. A dispute between an Uk and anyone is settled by the Uk's submission. No Grukar keeps a record, because the next laying keeps it. No Grukar collects a tax: the Uk bring what they find and the Hushk take what they want of it, and the spawner sees that the larder is fed first. When two answers conflict, the stronger body decides, and the spawner arranges in advance which body is stronger.

## What Comes After

The Grukar hold no belief about death, because a belief needs a word to be held in. A Grukar that dies outside the chamber is gone, and what it knew is gone with it, unless it was invited first. That is the whole of their afterlife: a guest is present in every hatchling of the following season, and a Grukar that was never invited has no part in what comes after.

That fact governs an ordinary day more than any law does. A Sha hides how much it knows and doles out its counsel in small measures. A Hushk that has survived a battle no other Grukar survived stays away from the chamber for as long as the spawner lets it. A spawner keeps its cleverest Sha safe from the Hushk all its life, and the protection ends at the chamber door.

## The Living and the Dead

The living reach the dead only through the spawner, and only once: the guest is fed to the Ahk, and the next laying hatches with what the guest knew. Nothing passes the other way. There are no ghosts, no dreams of the dead and no rites for them, and the fee is the guest's life.

## What a Grukar Owes

A Grukar owes everything to its tribe, in this order: obedience to the spawner's scent; its caste's work, whether that is digging, fighting or thinking; its place in the order of approach; and, when the spawner wants it, its brain. In return a Grukar is owed food when the tribe has food, a place in the nest, and the spawner's management of the tribe's ground so that there is food next season. A Sha is also owed the spawner's protection from the Hushk, for as long as it is more useful alive.

## The Weak

The Grukar keep no weak. The Uk are the tribe's labor and its grain, replaced whenever the tribe needs more, and their deaths are counted the way a farmer counts lost grain. A tribe has no widows, no orphans and no homeless, because it has no families and no Grukar outside the nest. In a famine nobody is cared for: the spawner rations the larder, lays fewer mouths and waits, and the castes eat in order of what the tribe needs from them.

## Those Who Cannot Work

Nothing in the nest keeps a Grukar that cannot do its work. An Uk crippled at the palisade is set to lighter work if any remains and left out of the food if none does. A Hushk too broken to fight is set among the Uk. A Sha is the exception, because a Sha's work is in its head: a lamed Sha goes on watching and counseling, and the spawner goes on protecting it.

## Shelter, Teaching and Defense

Shelter is the nest, built by the Uk and arranged around the spawn-chamber. Teaching does not exist, because a Grukar hatches knowing what it will ever be given, and the only learning is the Hushk's in a fight and the Sha's in the world beyond the tribe. Defense is the Hushk and the massed Uk, with the proto-Ahk at the chamber door, where their own selfish interest in the chamber holding makes them the best guards in Grukar-kind.

Their neighbors meet the Grukar in two ways only, the raid and the fight, and [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar Folk]] says why. Nobody takes a Grukar as a slave, nobody parleys with a raiding party, and a person a raid carries off is food rather than a hostage.

## The Tribe

The Grukar have no family. A tribe is one spawner and everything it has laid, every Grukar of it built from the same inheritance, and its membership is the spawner's scent: a Grukar belongs to the tribe whose Ahk it can smell, and to no other. There are no pairs, no parents, no children and no kin within the tribe.

The age ladder is short. A Grukar hatches from its caste's laying knowing its work, and starts it within days. It works, fights or watches until it dies. An old Hushk is old because it has won; an old Uk is rare. A Grukar that knows enough to matter is invited, and becomes part of the next laying. The rest die and are gone.

## The Tribe That Will Not Move

The wrong the Grukar commit by their own purpose is the tribe that dies in good order. When the place a tribe lives in fails, from drought, famine or fouled water, the spawner does what it always does, harder: it rations, sends the hunters farther, lays fewer mouths and waits. It never orders a march, because every course it can weigh is a way of continuing here. A dying Grukar tribe is very often a well-managed one, its spawner attending to the arrangements to the last.

The blind spot is that the tribe eats its own best minds. The knowledge a guest carries goes into the next laying, and the guest does not; a tribe that keeps what its Sha learned keeps none of its Sha's ability to learn more. The Sha are the only Grukar that see either fault, and they have no way to say it. When the end of a dying tribe is plain enough, two Sha of one mind find a Hushk with a grievance, raid the spawn-chamber, cut out a proto-Ahk and run with it until it wakes fertile. Half the tribe lives. The other half stays where it stood, in the ordered care of its spawner, and dies of whatever drove the others out.
