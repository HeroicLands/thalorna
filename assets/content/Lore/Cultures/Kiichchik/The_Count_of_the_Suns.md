**The Count of the Suns**, _ts'obel kil ts'en_, is the recitation of the four dead ages of the world that the [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|serpent-keepers]] of [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]] give at the [Ts'umaq'tsuqalil]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the Binding of the Serpent. The rite is held when the god is believed most restless: after an earthquake, an unseasonable storm or a failed harvest, and on no fixed day, because the head of the serpent-keepers decides when a city needs to hear the ages and when it has heard them too often. The city gathers below the binding temple, the jade and the maize are carried up, and the Reckoner of Ages stands at the head of the stair and recites. At each age the people in the plaza draw their own blood with an obsidian flake, an earlobe or a tongue, and answer with the line every child in the city knows: [Sleep in the cloud. We keep the count.]{.verse}

In the recitation an age is called a sun, because each age had its own sun lit for it. The text given here is the one the serpent-keepers of [[place-kankul2|K'ankul]] use. It reads each dead sun out the way [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|the builder-priests]] of [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]] read out a collapse from the register of their Warden of the Ruins (what fell, in what season, under what load), and closes each with the builder's answer, which is to build again. The solar priests of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] give a shorter count that leaves out the builder's answers and says of every dead sun that its people did not pay. K'ankul's count says only that the count came round, and leaves the paying to the fifth sun, which is the first to have people who can do it. Ish'ilankul also teaches that the builder breathed the world into being at [[place-tzomalchalix|Ts'omal Ch'alish]], where her breath still rises from the vent; K'ankul's count gives her hands, a cord and a stone, and never names the place. The figure for the fifth sun changes with every recital; this copy states it as the Reckoner gave it in the year 4158 of the Fifth Age.
