**The Verses of the Thórgila Fair** are what the hill town of [[place-thorgila|Thórgila]] kept from a verse-duel, a _gortsada_, sung at its autumn horse fair, the _arthauna_, between two singers known at the fairs as [Lírkeza]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Sling, and [Ozkarre]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Hail. A _gortsada_ is made on the spot and is not written down; what survives of one is what the fair could sing back the next day, and this one the valley could sing back almost whole. It is sung at the hearth in the winters since, each verse in the voice of the singer who made it, and the fairs of the Thórgila valleys take their measure from it.

The duel has its rules, and every fair keeps them. A theme-giver sets each bout. At the Thórgila fair it is the **horse-judge**, the man who reads the horses' teeth and says their ages, because the fair holds that a man who can tell a nine-year-old mare from a seven can tell a good verse from a bad one. He gives each singer a part to play and gives the bout a vowel, and every second line of every verse in that bout must end on it. A verse is eight lines. A singer has the time it takes the other to finish to make the next one. The fair is the judge, and it judges by stamping, and the horse-judge only says which way the stamping went.

The Sling was a woman of the third farm above the town who had run her holding through a husband's long contracts; the Hail was a crossbowman home from six years under contract. Each had the fair-name the fairs had given, and neither used another at a fair. The record keeps a greeting, three bouts and the last word, which the horse-judge gave as a single word to be sung once by each and answered by neither. The valleys are not agreed on one line. In the Hail's first verse of the third bout the upper farms sing _The buyer's come to you_ and the town sings _The buyer's at the stone_, and both are kept.
