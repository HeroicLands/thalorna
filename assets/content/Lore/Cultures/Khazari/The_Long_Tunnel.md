**The Long Tunnel**, _Narv-butám_, is the account, the _filnath_ (a telling written and kept), of the water-line of [Magar-thubád]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, a [[lore-khazariclt|Khazari]] hold in a limestone range that no record outside its own locates, and of [Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the master who cut it. The keeper of the hold's archive, [Lukum of Lakam-mulán]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, wrote it in the order the things happened, with the day, the measure and the name in every entry, and warranted it, and copies of it stand in the archives of other holds. It is told in the workshops, by a master to apprentices in their years of listening, and most often to an apprentice who has asked for the hammer. The claim of work that closes it, the _mazadh_, is recited at the turning of the year in the holds that keep it, and the chain of sayings [[lore-gatharbuzan|]] keeps one saying of Dulkun's beside it. The reading given here is the workshops' reading, which keeps every entry the record keeps and passes over the tallies of water and measure between them.

## The Account

::: {.verse form=filnath lang=en work=the-long-tunnel-1}
In the year of the finding 309, on the forty-first day of the thaw, the mountain shuddered. The shudder, the _naghal_, came in the second shift and lasted the time of nine hammer-falls. It threw down no face and no court. It brought down the roof of the water-worn passage that carries the spring to the cistern, for a length of forty measures beginning at the ninetieth measure from the cistern's head, and the channel along its floor was choked. The spring was heard still running beyond the fall, into the fall, and going down into the rock by a way nobody has found.

The cistern, the _hutabar_ beneath the lower courts, stood that evening at sixty-four parts, which is full. The hold's count was 554, and its use of water in a day was one part.

On the first day after the shudder the readers of stone of three houses sounded the fall from the cistern side: [Tabar-suláth]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, whose craft the water is, [Ramk-sumád]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and [Khalv-gunáv]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}. The roof above the fall was rotten stone for the whole length of the fall and for twenty measures beyond it on either hand, and it would not be cleared: what was lifted out would be replaced from above. The readers entered this and set their names to it. Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv, master of the delving of the unlit, named in a lean year, went by the old working of Khalv-gunáv, which lies west of the water-worn passage and below it, and sounded the fall from the far side, and heard the spring running behind it. This is entered.

On the second and third days the fall was sounded again by the same readers, and the entries were the same. Tabar-suláth entered the spring's rate from its water-record of the year before the shudder, which is four parts in a day at the thaw and a part and a half at the end of the cold, and entered that the spring had never in the record run dry. The gate was told on the third day. The gate's people draw from the river of the valley, which the shudder had not touched.

On the fourth day the elders called the hold down. The hold went down to the council-hall below, and the elders of the nine houses sat.

[Timkin]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of Ramk-sumád, elder of the raising of the course, spoke first and entered the ruled line: a cut from the head of the cistern to the spring-head, seventy measures, in the line a stretched cord would give; sound stone for the first thirty measures and for the last twenty, and soft ground between, to be propped with gate timber as the cut went through it; two crews in shifts; sixty-four days. He entered that the ruled line, the _khatab_, is a reproach, and that he entered it knowing it. "The cistern stands at sixty parts. No other line is sixty-four days."

Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv entered his refusal. He had gone that morning along the floor of the lower striking-floor, which lies over the ruled line for its first forty measures, and sounded it by the measure. At thirty measures the ring of the floor went dull and stayed dull for six measures, and in the sixth a seep ran along the dullness from west to east, which is the way the ruled line does not run. He entered that the dullness was a fault, a _zagar_, where the rock has slipped and will slip, and that the ruled line crossed it. "I will not cut across it." He asked instead for the grain's line, the _magar_: the bedding seam that the old working of Khalv-gunáv follows, which bends west away from the fault and climbs, and comes to the spring-head from below its lip; 220 measures by the seam, at three times the cutting; no prop. He entered no day for it.

Timkin of Ramk-sumád entered that the floor of the lower striking-floor had rung sound under his house's hammers for the whole of his life, and that no reader of his house had entered a dullness in it. Lukum of Lakam-mulán brought up from the archive the record of the cutting of the lower striking-floor, in the year of the finding 151, and read it. The reader of that year, [Girip]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of Tabar-suláth, had entered the floor dull from the thirtieth measure of its length to the thirty-sixth, had entered a seep in the sixth measure of the dullness, and had entered that nothing was to be cut below the floor in that length. The record was read whole, and it stands, and Timkin's entry stands after it in the order it was made.

[Khirid]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [Khard-vurán]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, eldest of the hold, who reads faces, entered that she had walked the striking-floor behind Dulkun and heard what he heard, and that she had not known the record of the year 151 was there to be read.

The elders of nine houses entered. Three entered for the ruled line, with Ramk-sumád; five entered for the grain's line; Tabar-suláth entered that the water would decide and asked for the count. The council granted the grain's line to the fortieth day, with this mark: that on the fortieth day the tunnel should stand at the bend at the second joint, which is the fiftieth measure of the seam, and that if it did not, the ruled line would be begun on the forty-first day by Ramk-sumád. Ramk-sumád was to bring up timber from the gate against that day. The use of water was set at half a part in a day, by household, and the forges were put out and the quenching-troughs emptied into the cistern.

Timkin of Ramk-sumád asked on the fifth day that the record show he had entered the short line and not the ruled line. The record shows the ruled line on the fourth day in his own hand, and shows it again on the fifth.

On the sixth day the first cut was made at the end of the old working, where the seam leaves it. Khirid of Khard-vurán said the charm over it, the _zalm_, as the charm is said over a first cut, and Dulkun struck. The crew was eight of Khalv-gunáv in three shifts, and the record names them: [Vurun]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Murgun]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Khilvin]{.name data-type="being/npc"} in the first shift; [Tilig]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Palaz]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Darkhan]{.name data-type="being/npc"} in the second; [Bumgun]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and Dulkun in the third; and with them the apprentice [Rinit]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of Tabar-suláth, who was apprenticed to Khalv-gunáv for her ear and struck nothing, and carried the lamps and the tally of the measure. Dulkun sounded ahead of all three shifts and slept in the old working.

The record counts a tunnel by its cuts, which are the places where the hammer was needed, and does not count the partings, where the rock came away from its joint under the bar. Where the record says the tunnel stood at a measure, the measure is from the first cut along the seam.

On the eighth day the gate began to send water. The approach to the gate is a stair in the face and the carriers few, and the skins came up by the stair, twenty on the best day and none on the days the stair ran with rain. A skin is a day's water for four. The skins are counted in the record by the day, and a skin was entered with the cistern's count on the day it came up. The elders entered the carrying against the gate's account, as owed, and set no measure on it.

On the tenth day the tunnel stood at six measures. The cistern stood at fifty-seven parts.

[…]

On the twentieth day the tunnel stood at nineteen measures. The cistern stood at fifty-one parts. On the thirtieth day the tunnel stood at thirty-one measures. The cistern stood at forty-six parts. No cut was entered in those ten days: the seam opened under the bar from the twenty-second measure to the twenty-ninth, and Vurun of Khalv-gunáv entered that the rock in that length had been waiting to be asked. Dulkun entered nothing.

On the fortieth day the tunnel stood at forty-three measures. The bend at the second joint is at fifty. The cistern stood at forty-one parts, and 251 skins had come up from the gate in the forty days.

The elders called the hold down a second time and sat. The record of the water was read, and the record of the measure, and the elders reckoned from what was read and from nothing else. Timkin of Ramk-sumád reckoned that the ruled line, begun on the forty-first day, would reach the spring on the hundred and fourth. Lukum of Lakam-mulán reckoned that the grain's line, at the rate of the record, would reach the spring on the hundred and eightieth. [Suluth]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of Tabar-suláth, who kept the tally of the cistern and whose hands had failed, reckoned that the cistern, with the refuge's water opened to it and the use set at a third of a part, would stand through the two hundredth day, and that the skins would carry it past that.

The elders opened the refuge's water. The refuge, the _bamg-durákh_, keeps its own cistern of twenty parts, and that water is a cache, a _makas_, and a cache is opened by need. The oath of the refuge, cut in the wall of the council-hall where it was sworn at the finding, was read first, as it is read before any cache is opened. The need was entered, by the count of the cistern, and the elders set their nine names under it. The use was set at a third of a part in a day, by need, and the draw of every household is in the record by name. The elders of seven houses entered for the grain's line, and the elders of two, Ramk-sumád and Bant-khubán, entered for the ruled line, and the record holds both. The grain's line was granted to the spring. The timber that Ramk-sumád had brought up lay in the lower court.

On the fiftieth day the tunnel stood at the bend. The seam turns there through a quarter of the circle and rises the height of a man in nine measures, and the tunnel turns and rises with it; one cut of leveling was entered in the rise, and the rest was partings. The cistern, with the refuge's water in it, stood at fifty-eight parts.

On the sixtieth day Rinit of Tabar-suláth asked for the hammer. The record shows that she was given a second lamp.

[…]

On the seventy-sixth day the tunnel stood at the hundred and first measure. The cistern stood at forty-nine parts. The elders of Ramk-sumád asked that the ruled line be begun beside the grain's line, with the timber that lay in the lower court, and the elders did not sit.

On the seventy-seventh day, at the hundred and second measure, the seam closed against a flaw, and Dulkun, who had sounded the flaw in the shift before and entered it, struck across the grain to open past it. The stone spalled from the roof for the length of two measures. He entered the cut against his own name as a clumsy cut, a _gharp_, that day, before the shift ended, and entered that the opening past the flaw could have been had by going under it, which Tilig of Khalv-gunáv found on the seventy-eighth day. It is the one clumsy cut in his record. The spall stands in the roof. It was not dressed.

On the eightieth day the tunnel stood at a hundred and five measures. The cistern stood at forty-eight parts.

On the ninetieth day the tunnel stood at a hundred and seventeen measures. The cistern stood at forty-four parts. The heat of the year was on the face, and the elders set the draw of the old whose hands had failed and of children under five years at a half part in six days above what their count gave them, by need, and the record names the households it reached: eleven.

[…]

On the hundred and third day Suluth of Tabar-suláth died in the morning, in her house's rooms on the face, after she had made the count. Her last entry stands in her own hand in the water-record: forty parts, and nine skins the day before. The hold went down for her on the hundred and fourth day and kept the silence over her, _mullán_, and the silence was broken by the words that are said, _Ramk samíd, ramk thamír_, the course is raised; the course is struck. She was laid in the deep and her name was cut. Her tally-stick went to Tabar-suláth with her tools. The third shift came up for the silence and went down again, to the hundred and twenty-ninth measure. Rinit of Tabar-suláth kept the tally of the cistern from the hundred and fourth day, on a stick she cut that day, and the tally of the measure went to Palaz.

On the hundred and tenth day the tunnel stood at a hundred and thirty-six measures. The cistern stood at thirty-eight parts.

On the hundred and nineteenth day [Tanvan]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of Ramk-sumád died in the night, in his second year. The hold went down for him on the hundred and twentieth day and kept the silence, and the words were said, and he was laid in the deep and his name was cut. The cistern stood at thirty-five parts.

On the hundred and twenty-sixth day the first shift, at the hundred and fifty-first measure, broke through the seam's floor into an open passage: a water-worn passage the spring had cut for itself along the same seam before it took the way it has now, dry, the height of a man and the width of two, running thirty measures east and up from a sump at the break that was dry. Murgun of Khalv-gunáv went in first with the lamp, and in the sump the lamp went down and he came out, and he entered bad air in the sump and for eight measures above it. On the hundred and twenty-seventh day Dulkun sounded the sump's roof and found a fissure in it that the readers of the upper ore-face had entered from the other side, 128 years before, as a crack in the ore-face's floor, and the crew opened the fissure from below with two cuts, and the draft came down it from the ore-face and the lamp stood. The two cuts are counted. Dulkun sounded the open passage from end to end in one shift and entered it sound, and entered that the thirty measures would not be cut. They were not cut. The tunnel goes through them as the water went.

On the hundred and thirty-first day the snow came to the face, and from that day snow was carried in and melted at the hearths, and the record counts the snow-water with the skins. The gate's carrying stopped on the hundred and thirty-fourth day, when the stair iced, and the last skin is entered on that day. In all, 583 skins came up from the gate.

From the hundred and thirty-second day the cutting was in the cold. Palaz of Khalv-gunáv entered the measure at the end of every shift, and the record keeps all of them, and they are not read. Dulkun entered on the hundred and forty-first day that the seam from the two hundredth measure to the spring-head would take cuts and not partings, because the frost had taken the rock's give, and that he would cut it in the third shift, at the end of the day's warming, and he did.

On the hundred and fortieth day the tunnel stood at a hundred and ninety-eight measures. The cistern stood at twenty-eight parts, and the snow-water in the troughs at the hearths stood at three.

On the hundred and fiftieth day the tunnel stood at two hundred and eleven measures. Dulkun entered that the spring was heard through the rock from the two hundred and ninth measure, below and ahead, and that the sound was the sound of water falling into water. The cistern stood at twenty-five parts.

[…]

On the hundred and fifty-eighth day, in the third shift, at the two hundred and twentieth measure, Dulkun's cut opened into the spring-head below the lip of the spring, a hand's breadth under the water, and the water came through the cut into the tunnel and ran down it the way the seam falls. It reached the dry sump at the hundred and fifty-first measure in the time of a shift, filled it, and went on over its lip. Bumgun of Khalv-gunáv, who held the lamp, entered the hour. Dulkun entered the measure.

The water ran down the tunnel that night to the old working and stood there, and on the hundred and fifty-ninth day the floor of the old working was opened to the head of the cistern along the channel that had fed it before the finding, which the water had cut and the hold had never used; nine cuts of leveling were entered in the channel, and they are counted with the water-line's. On the hundred and sixtieth day the water was led along the old working into the head of the cistern, and the cistern rose. The pool that had stood in the spring-head above the lip drained through the cut over three days, and on the hundred and sixty-third day the cistern stood at thirty-three parts; after that it rose by the spring's rate in the cold, a part and a half in a day less the use. On the hundred and seventy-second day it stood at forty-four parts, and the use was set at one part. It stood at sixty-four before the thaw.

On the hundred and sixty-second day the water-line was sounded from the head of the cistern to the spring-head by the readers of Tabar-suláth, Ramk-sumád and Khard-vurán, with Khalv-gunáv standing off, and entered sound along its whole length, and self-held along its whole length, with no prop in it. It is entered in the record of the hold as a _narv-butám_, a long tunnel that needs no prop, the tunnel that lasts, and it is the first the record of Magar-thubád enters by that word. The words were said over it in the old working, at the first cut, by Khirid of Khard-vurán.

The hold came up. The finishing was kept in the lower court on the face on the hundred and sixty-fifth day, with food from the gate and singing along the passages, and the water-line was shown to the hold by its sound: Rinit of Tabar-suláth walked it from the cistern to the spring-head with a lamp and a hammer and struck the wall at every tenth measure, and the hold in the lower court heard the ring come up the old working, the same note at every blow, twenty-two blows, and the last blow came up with the sound of the spring behind it. Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv spoke his claim of work there, before the hold, and it is the only speech of his that the account holds. Lukum of Lakam-mulán entered it in the record as it was spoken. The timber that Ramk-sumád had brought up was given to the gate. It is the one gift in the record of that year.

The record of the year enters against Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv's name the mark of mastery for the water-line, to tunnel as a master does; the one clumsy cut, at the hundred and second measure, on the seventy-seventh day; and the first cut of the next passage opened in the hold, which was the stair to the upper ore-face, in the year after. The record enters the water-line's count: sixty-six cuts, thirty measures not cut, and no prop.

The hold lost its water./
The hold gave me the water to find./
I did not find the water./
The water found its way before us;/
I found the way it had found.

Three days I listened at the fall./
On the fourth I listened along the ruled line,/
and at thirty measures the ring went dull./
Dull stone is stone that has slipped./
I do not cut across a slipping.

I asked for the grain./
The grain went west where the water went east./
It climbed where the cord would have gone level./
It asked two hundred and twenty measures./
I was granted them to the fortieth day./
On the fortieth day I had not reached the bend./
That is in the record. I let it stand.

Count the cuts./
Cuts that opened: nineteen./
Cuts that leveled: forty./
Cuts that joined: seven./
Sixty-six, and the partings not counted,/
because a parting is the stone's cut and not mine./
Thirty measures I did not cut./
The water had cut them going its old way, before the spring moved./
That is thirty measures of my best work,/
and no hand of mine was on them.

One cut I made across the grain,/
at the hundred and second measure, on the seventy-seventh day./
It is entered against my name./
Tilig found the way under it the morning after./
The spall is in the roof. It is mine. Walk under it.

Props: none./
Timber came up to the gate for the ruled line./
It went back to the gate. The gate may burn it./
No prop in it./
Two hundred and twenty measures, and no prop in it./
Sound the line from the cistern to the spring./
It rings whole from end to end./
No prop in it.

The line is not straight anywhere./
Take a lamp along it: it reads as a cave.

What I did not cut:/
the ruled line;/
the slipped stone;/
a prop./
What was struck while I cut:/
Suluth of Tabar-suláth, on the hundred and third day;/
Tanvan of Ramk-sumád, on the hundred and nineteenth./
I did not strike them./
They are counted with the work.

The water is in the cistern./
The hold has its water.

::: {.verse form=filnath lang=en work=the-long-tunnel-3}
In the thaw of the second year Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv made the first cut of the stair to the upper ore-face, which the elders had given him, and the stair was finished in the cold of that year with eleven cuts in forty-four steps; the other steps were in the rock already. Rinit of Tabar-suláth struck her first stone in it, the thirty-first step, on the day she was admitted to strike, and the cut is entered against her name as sound.

In the ninth winter after the shudder, on the ninth day of the cold, the mountain shuddered again, for the time of three hammer-falls. The floor of the lower striking-floor stepped along the dullness Dulkun had sounded, from the thirtieth measure of the ruled line to the thirty-sixth, by the width of two fingers, the east side down, and the ground above the fault opened in a crack the length of the striking-floor, and the seep that had run west to east ran in it. The readers of three houses sounded the water-line on the same day from the cistern to the spring-head and entered it sound along its whole length. The cistern stood at sixty-four parts. The crack in the striking-floor was entered and was not dressed, and the day was cut beside it in carved [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]], along the crack. On the day after, Timkin of Ramk-sumád asked that the record of the fourth day of the shudder be read to him in the deep hall, and Lukum of Lakam-mulán read it to him, his entry and Dulkun's and Girip of Tabar-suláth's, in the order they stand, and he set his name under the reading.

In the record of the year of the finding 309, Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv's name stands with the mark of mastery and the one fault, and Suluth of Tabar-suláth's name stands on the hundred and third day, and Tanvan of Ramk-sumád's on the hundred and nineteenth, and the three stand on the same leaf. Lukum of Lakam-mulán wrote this in the order it happened and warrants it.

Tanvan of Ramk-sumád lies in the burial hall of Magar-thubád. The words over his tomb are cut in carved Pirzath, the work with its verb last, as carved stone sets it:
