**The Finding at the Far Edge**, _Varg-fukár_, is the founding account of [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]], the hold in the eastern part of [[place-ironfells|the Ironfells]] of [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] that the [[lore-khazariclt|Khazari]] call _Varíg_, the far edge. It is a _filnath_, an account: exact prose in the order things happened, with every name, every measure and every cut, written by the keeper of the founding party's record while the founding went on and witnessed at the end of each year. On the day of the finding the hold goes down to the refuge, a keeper of the archive reads the account by lamplight, and when the reading reaches the founding inscription the eldest master reads that in its place, as it stands cut along a bedding seam above the inner door of the refuge, where no line of it runs straight. The account dates itself by the years of Khazartúrn (see [[lore-khazarturn|the Fall of Khazartúrn]]), the city the party was sent from; the record names the city, and the keeper reads "the city" in its place, because a hold says Khazartúrn's name only at the reading of its account. Outside the hold the date is about 3100 BF and no closer, as [[lore-vorgaldcut|the Cutting of Vorgald]] sets out. The account names every Khazár who crossed the water and none of the people of the shore, whose descendants are [[lore-vardain|the Vardain]]; the reading gives the crossers by house and count. Where the day-tally of the finder's season repeats, the keeper reads only the count of the repeated days. The text below is the account as it is read, with the inscription where the reading reaches it.

::: {.verse form=filnath lang=en work=the-finding-at-the-far-edge-1}
This is the account of the finding of Varíg, the far edge, and of the cutting of the hold there. [Malan of Lakam-mulán]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, keeper, wrote it as the things happened. At the end of each year it was read to the elders and witnessed, and the witnesses are named at the year's end.

In the year of the city 2020, on the seventy-second day of the thaw, the party for the far edge went out from the city. In all, 102 went: of [Ramk-sumád]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} nineteen, of [Tanv-guráz]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} sixteen, of [Dalk-thumár]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} fourteen, of [Halb-zumád]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} eleven, of [Tabar-suláth]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} eleven, of [Sakar-puláz]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} nine, of [Rabad-runát]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} eight, of [Nalp-furád]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} seven, of [Lakam-mulán]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} four, and of [Vadhal-sumág]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} three. The elders of the party were [Rimkin of Ramk-sumád]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Garzan of Tanv-guráz]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and [Khardan of Halb-zumád]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who was the eldest and whose hands had failed. The finder of the party was [Fikir of Rabad-runát]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who received listening from [Dolok of Vadhal-sumág]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, her own house having no listening to give her, and Dolok received it from the masters of her house in their order. The master of stone was [Thimrin of Dalk-thumár]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.

The stores were these. Grain, eighty measures. Dried fish, forty measures. Salt, nine measures. Oil for lamps, thirty-one jars. Lamps, seventy-two. Iron in bars, seventy by weight, of which sixty-four were for the cutting of a hold and six were for exchange. Steel, four bars. Chisels, 100. Hammers, sixty-two. Tongs, nine pairs. Wax tablets, forty. Lead in sheets, twelve. Chalk, two sacks. Rope, sixty-nine lengths. Hides, forty. The tally-sticks of each house went with each house.

Two ships of the [[lore-flksinale|forest folk]] carried the party. The crossing took nine days, and on the fourth of them the sea came over the lesser ship and six measures of grain were spoiled. On the ninth day the ships came under the eastern heights of the far country where the range meets the sea, and the party was put on the shore below the cliffs at a shelf of rock the sea leaves dry at half tide. The ships went back on the fourth day after the landing, and the account holds nothing more of them.

On the day of the landing Fikir read the faces from the lesser ship before it went in to the shelf. She read them for the length of the morning. The account holds what she said, as she said it. The sea-face is limestone over a harder stone, bedded, the beds lying a little inward toward the mountain. It stands out from the range in ribs, and the third rib from the south carries a seep that runs down the face and does not stain it white as the other seeps do. Behind the third rib the cave lies. The north face, which is around the shoulder of the range from the sea-face and looks away from the sun, stands jointed across the beds, and its foot is heaped with fallen stone that carries no weathering. She said these things and she said nothing further that day.

On the second day after the landing the elders went around the shoulder to the north face. Thimrin went with them, and Fikir went with them. The north face holds two caverns opening at the foot of the cliff, and the greater of them was measured on that day. Its mouth is eleven paces wide and five men high. Its floor runs inward for sixty-eight paces, level, dry, and sanded, and its roof stands at four men's height over the greater part and at seven over the inner hall, where the cavern turns and ends in a sloped wall of breakdown stone. The lesser cavern lies forty paces to the east of the greater and runs inward for twenty-six paces, dry. Between them the face carries a ledge where a passage could be opened along the joint. There is a spring at eighty paces from the mouth of the greater cavern, on the slope below the face, and it ran in the thaw.

On the third day the elders sat in council at the mouth of the greater cavern, and this is what was said, as it was said.

Rimkin of Ramk-sumád said that the greater cavern of the north face held the whole party as it stood, and that its floor took a course without a cut. "A hall sixty-eight paces long with a dry floor is a hall the mountain has finished and offered. A hundred and two, with a cold season coming, have no second winter to spend looking for a better one."

Garzan of Tanv-guráz said that the north face had one approach, up the slope from the shore, and that a watch on the ledge saw the whole of it. "A refuge is found before it is readied. This one is found."

Fikir of Rabad-runát said that the north face sheds in frost. The fallen stone at its foot carries no weathering; none of it has lain there longer than three cold seasons, and the greatest block of it is the size of the lesser ship. The joints that cross the beds on that face are open the width of a finger at the foot of the cliff and closed above. "That is the mark of a face the cold has been splitting, and it does not stop because a party has moved in under it. The greater cavern has its roof in the same beds that have been falling." The cave of the sea-face lay behind the third rib, she said, and she had not been inside it. She asked for a season to listen, the cold season, from the water, lying off the sea-face and off the north face by turns, and she asked that nothing be cut on the north face until the season was read.

Rimkin of Ramk-sumád said that every face on the far country sheds in frost, because the far country is wet and cold. "A finder who has not been inside her cave is asking a party to winter on a reading of the outside of it."

Khardan of Halb-zumád, the eldest, asked Fikir whether she would say that the greater cavern would fall. "I will say that it is falling," she said. "I cannot say the year."

The council found as follows, and the finding was entered. The party would winter in the greater cavern of the north face, because there was nowhere else for 102 to winter. Nothing would be cut on the north face. The finder would have her season. Rimkin of Ramk-sumád and Garzan of Tanv-guráz entered against the finder's reading, in these words: that the north face caverns were the finding of this hold, that they had been given by the mountain finished, and that a season spent on the water was a season taken from the refuge. The entry was cut on lead and is in the archive. Fikir entered nothing against them. Khardan of Halb-zumád, being the eldest, entered nothing.

The party went into the greater cavern on the fifth day after the landing. Fikir asked that no one sleep past the forty-pace mark, where the roof rises, and the houses kept to the outer hall as far as its floor allowed. The lesser cavern took the stores. The stores were counted into it and the count agreed with the count of the landing less the six measures of grain the sea spoiled and what had been eaten, which was four measures of grain and three of fish.

The boat was hired on the seventh day. The people of the shore take fish and seals from the water below the cliffs and do not plant. Four of them came along the shelf at low water and looked at the party from a distance of sixty-four paces and did not come nearer. Thimrin went down to them with a bar of the exchange iron and came back with a boat, hired, and two of them to hold it off the rock, for the weight of a knife in iron each ten days. The boat is of hide over a frame and takes four. The account holds what was paid, and the account holds nothing more of them.

Fikir went onto the water on the ninth day after the landing, which was the ninetieth day of the thaw, with Dolok's chalk-slates and a lamp and a hammer she did not use. She lay off the sea-face by day and came in to the shelf at dark, because the two of the shore would not hold the boat under the face at night, and when the weather was from the east she lay off the north face instead, which the sea reaches around the shoulder. The tally of her season is on slate and was copied onto lead, day by day, from the ninetieth day of the thaw to the thirty-second day of the cold, sixty-one days, the last of them the day she went in. She counted the days from the day she went onto the water, and they are counted here as she counted them and as the keeper reads them.

Read and witnessed in the year of the city 2020: Rimkin of Ramk-sumád, Garzan of Tanv-guráz, Khardan of Halb-zumád.

First day on the water. Off the third rib. The seep runs warm against the hand and does not whiten the rock. Seals on the shelf below the rib, nine.

Second day. Off the third rib. The seep runs the same. The beds behind the rib dip inward at the width of a hand in a pace.

Third to ninth day. The same, seven days.

Tenth day. Off the north face from the east. A block fell from the face at the height of the ledge, the size of a door. The sound reached the boat after the fall. No one was under it.

Eleventh to twenty-ninth day. Off the third rib and off the north face by turns. The same, nineteen days. On the twenty-fourth day the seals left the shelf.

Thirtieth day, the first of the cold. Off the north face. The joint above the mouth of the greater cavern holds water and the water has a skin of ice at dawn.

Thirty-first to thirty-eighth day. The same, eight days. The ice in the joint thicker on the thirty-fifth day, a finger.

Thirty-ninth day. Off the third rib. In the still of the morning the seep-mouth breathed. The breath was warm and came out of the rock and did not come off the water. It came for the time of sixty-four breaths and stopped.

Fortieth day. Off the third rib. Nothing.

Forty-first day. Off the third rib. The rib breathed at the turn of the tide, as before.

Forty-second to fifty-first day. The same, ten days: the rib breathed at the turn of the tide on six of them, and on four, when the wind came onto the face, the breath could not be told from the wind.

Fifty-second day, the twenty-third of the cold. Off the north face. Ice in every crossing joint from the foot of the face to the height of the ledge. The face spoke at dawn, when the sun reached it and the ice let go, in small sounds, sixty-four and more in the time it took the sun to clear the shoulder. The fallen stone at the foot has gained a block since the tenth day.

Fifty-third to sixtieth day. Off the north face. The same, eight days. The face spoke every dawn. On the fifty-eighth day a slab came off the face above the lesser cavern, the width of a hall-door and a hand thick, and broke on the slope and did not reach the mouth. The two of the shore would not hold the boat off the north face after that day, and were paid for those days.

Sixty-first day, the thirty-second of the cold. Off the third rib at dawn, when the sea was down. The rib breathed. Fikir went in.

Khardan of Halb-zumád died in the greater cavern of the north face on the nineteenth day of the cold, while the finder was on the water, with his work in order. What he had warranted in the city stands warranted. He had come to the far edge to listen, his hands having failed, and he listened in the greater cavern for the time he was in it, and told the houses where in it the roof was sound and where it was not, and his telling agreed with the finder's in all but one place, which is entered. There was no deep to lay him in. The hold kept silence over him and said the words, and laid him under stone in the lesser cavern among the stores until a tomb could be cut. The course has been raised; the course will be struck.

This is what Fikir found behind the third rib, as she measured it on the day she went in and the three days after, with Thimrin on the second day and the elders on the third.

The mouth is a squeeze behind the rib, a narrow that the seep comes out of, a hand and a half wide at the narrowest and a man's height, and no one broad in the shoulder goes through it as it stands. The seep runs in its floor. The narrow runs inward for forty paces, climbing, with the water to the knee for twenty of them and to the ankle after, and then it widens to a water-worn passage three paces across, worn by the seep when the seep was greater, which rises for seventy paces and turns twice with the beds. The passage opens into a domed chamber, eleven paces across and five men high at the crown, with the seep rising in its floor from a pool two paces across and clean. Flowstone hangs on the north side of the chamber and is dry. From the chamber a second passage climbs eastward, dry, for sixty-four paces, the beds carrying it, and ends in a chamber that is the one the rib breathes from. That chamber is forty-one paces long and nine across at its widest and holds its roof at three men's height on the beds, with no breakdown on its floor. A fissure in its eastern wall draws air. The air moves inward on the flood tide and outward on the ebb, as the sea presses the lower passages, and the draft is the breath the rib gave on the still days. The chamber is dry to the hand on every wall. Its floor falls a little toward the fissure, and a sump at the low end holds water that stands clean.

Fikir marked the upper chamber as the refuge on the day she found it, before any home was found. She chalked the mark on the wall of the fissure and Thimrin cut it in the stone on the second day, one cut. That cut is the first cut of Varíg.

The elders went in on the third day. Rimkin of Ramk-sumád, when he had come through the narrow and stood in the domed chamber with the seep over his feet, said that no course could be laid in a cave that a seep runs through, and that the upper chamber held 101 lying close and not one more. "We have been given a refuge and not a hold." Garzan of Tanv-guráz said that the narrow was one mouth. "One mouth is good for guarding and bad for leaving. A hold that can be shut from inside can be shut from outside." Thimrin of Dalk-thumár said that the narrow could be opened to a laden carrier's width by eleven cuts along the bedding, each the width of a hand, and that the rock would hold the rest.

The council found that the finding was the finder's. It had been entered on the third day after the landing that the north face caverns were the finding of this hold, and that entry stands and has not been struck. The council found that the hold would go in behind the third rib. Rimkin of Ramk-sumád and Garzan of Tanv-guráz asked that their entry stand. It stands.

The readying of the refuge was the first work. It was done in the cold, from the thirty-fourth day of the cold to the sixty-first, and these are the cuts of it. In the narrow, eleven cuts along the bedding, each a hand's width, by Thimrin of Dalk-thumár, and the spoil of them was twelve loads, which were carried out and laid on the shelf below the tide, where the sea took them. In the upper chamber, the inner door, where the dry passage enters: four cuts, to let a door of stone be set across it from inside, by Thimrin. The sill of the door, two cuts. Two lamp-niches, one each. The stores were carried in from the lesser cavern of the north face around the shoulder and through the narrow, load by load, in nineteen days, and counted into the refuge: grain, forty-eight measures; fish, thirty-one; salt, nine; oil, twenty-two jars. The air-shaft was proved on the fifty-fifth day of the cold: a fire of driftwood was lit in the upper chamber and the smoke was watched for from the boat, and it came out on the face 128 paces above the rib, along a seam, and spread along the face and was not seen sixty-four paces off. The water of the sump was measured for nine days and did not fall. The refuge, _bamg-durákh_, was readied. The course has been raised; the course will be struck.

The tomb of Khardan of Halb-zumád was the second work. The hold went down into the upper chamber, and Fikir read the beds of the dry passage and marked a place on its south side, forty paces in from the domed chamber, where the beds part and a chamber lies behind the parting, the length of a man and a half. Thimrin opened the parting with three cuts. Khardan was brought through the narrow and laid in it on the sixty-eighth day of the cold, in the year of the city 2021, with his name cut on the seam above him, and the hold kept silence, and the words were said. The first work warranted at Varíg was a refuge and the second was a tomb.

The homes were the third work, and the account holds the cuts of each. Fikir read the sea-face for them from the boat in the thaw, and marked the first home at the height of the fourth bed above the third rib, where a shelf lies along the joint between the fourth bed and the fifth and the rock behind it is sound. The first window was opened along that joint by six cuts, by Thimrin, and the opening was shaped to the line of the joint. The second window, five cuts. The stair from the dry passage up to the shelf behind the first window: nine cuts, where the beds lie step on step already and wanted joining, and the rock held the rest. Of the first season of homes the account holds sixty-seven cuts in all, and names each against the hand that made it, and the names stand in the archive and are not read here. No prop was set in any work of that season. No cut of that season was entered against a name.

In the same thaw the making of farmers was begun, because a hold grows nothing and eats from its gate or does not eat. The elders read the valley that lies in the lee of the range beneath the hold and found it fit. The gate was opened where the face of the hold meets the head of the valley, by four cuts along the joint, so that the valley lies below the gate. The water of the valley's becks was led by Tabar-suláth in channels along the courses the water already ran, with a gate at the head of each, and the first terraces were walled on the south flank of the valley by Ramk-sumád along the ground's own lines. A granary was opened in the rock at the head of the valley where a sinkhole had taken the floor, by nine cuts. Seventy-one of the people of the shore were taken to the valley in that thaw and were shown the terrace, the gate of the water, and the granary, and were taught the tally-row of the letters, and swore. Nothing else was given. The account holds the measures of the channels and the courses of the terraces and holds no names from the valley.

In the thaw the party ate fish from the shore for iron, and the account holds what was paid: the weight of two knives, then of a third. The grain was held in the refuge for the cold.

The first child of Varíg was born on the fortieth day of the thaw, in the year of the city 2021, to Ramk-sumád, and the elders of that house named her [Rumuk]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, from the course, because she was born in a bad year. The naming was held on the shelf behind the first window with the window open to the sea, and the houses sang along the passage, and the two of the shore heard it from the boat.

Read and witnessed in the year of the city 2021: Rimkin of Ramk-sumád, Garzan of Tanv-guráz, Thimrin of Dalk-thumár, Fikir of Rabad-runát.

In the cold of the year of the city 2022, on the eleventh day, in the night, the roof of the greater cavern of the north face came down. The sound of it came around the shoulder and reached the hold on the shelf behind the first window and in the passages. No one was under it. In the thaw the elders went around the shoulder with Fikir and Thimrin and measured what had fallen. The roof came down from the thirty-sixth pace to the inner wall, and lies on the floor to the height of two men, and the inner hall is gone. The outer hall stands to the thirty-sixth pace. The lesser cavern stands. The spring on the slope below the face is under the fallen stone and was not found. The account enters the fall of the north face, _palgh_, on the eleventh day of the cold in the year of the city 2022, in the night, with no one under it.

The entry of Rimkin of Ramk-sumád and Garzan of Tanv-guráz stands as it was entered. They asked, when the fall had been measured, that it stand, and it stands, and nothing was entered against their names. Fikir of Rabad-runát entered nothing.

The first grain came up to the gate in the cold of the year of the city 2022, nineteen measures, and was counted into the refuge.

The founding inscription was cut in the thaw of the year of the city 2022, when the refuge had been readied, the tomb made, the first homes finished, and the first grain counted in. Fikir read the seam for it and marked it above the inner door of the refuge, along the bedding, and Thimrin of Dalk-thumár cut it there, following the seam. It runs as the seam runs and is read by lamplight. It names the finder once and after that names only the cuts, and it is read on the day of the finding by the eldest master of the hold, in its own order, which is the old order of carved stone.

Behind the third rib of the sea-face the cave Fikir of Rabad-runát found./
In the year of the city two thousand and twenty-one it was found./
A season from the water the finder listened./
On the still days at the turn of the tide the rib breathed./
The north face in frost sheds; on the north face nothing was cut./
In the north face in the second cold the roof fell; under it no one lay./
The seep its own way runs; of the seep nothing was cut./
The upper chamber dry stands; through its own fissure its air moves./

The first window along the joint, six cuts./
The second window, five./
The stair where the beds lie step on step, nine cuts./
The rock the rest held./
Of the first season sixty-seven cuts, and no prop./

A hundred and one into the rock came./

Of the years after, the account holds the cuts, the records, and the dead, in their order, and they are read in their order on the day of the finding and are not set down here.
