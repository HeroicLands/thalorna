**The Sayings of the Masters**, _Gathar-buzán_, is the workshop wisdom of the [[lore-khazariclt|Khazari]] as one hold keeps it: the sayings (_bazan_) of twelve masters, each received from the one before and handed to the one after, so that the chain of names is recited before any saying is. A master says them to apprentices one at a time across the years of listening, usually at the face, with the stone a saying is about under the apprentice's hand, because a saying is a thing said over a matter and it waits for its matter. The whole chain is read aloud once, below, on the oath-day when an apprentice is admitted to strike, and the hammer is given after the last word. The copy transcribed here is [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]]'s, kept on lead sheets in the hold's archive; the chain is Vorgald's own, and the holds over the water keep chains of their own that share its older names and, but for [Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, none of its later ones. The valley the two masters quarrel over near the end is [[place-vardainvalleys|the Vardain valleys]] beneath the hold, which the text calls only the valley and the gate. Two of the masters spoke in the coursed line of the deep songs, and their sayings are read in that line; the rest are said plainly, and nothing in the chain is sung.

## The Chain

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-1}
[Tubur of Kaval-zunáth]{.name data-type="being/npc"} received listening from [Fardan of Hamk-dunák]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Fardan from [Pirin of Zanth-guráp]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Pirin from [Hulbun of Lakam-mulán]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Hulbun from [Butum of Nalp-furád]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Butum from Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv, and Dulkun from [Kivil of Halb-zumád]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Kivil from [Nalpan of Talg-hunáth]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Nalpan from [Rumkun of Bamg-durákh]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Rumkun from [Thimir of Marg-buráth]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Thimir from [Khalvan of Ramk-sumád]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Khalvan from [Samad of Tabar-suláth]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.

Above Samad the lead holds names and no sayings. The sayings begin with her.
:::

## Samad of Tabar-suláth

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-2}
Samad of Tabar-suláth led water. She said:

Tap the stone and wait for the ring to finish. What you hear first is the hammer. What you hear after is the stone.

Sound a face in the cold of the morning, and sound it again when the sun has moved a hand's width across it. You will think somebody has changed the stone. Nobody has. Write both.

Limestone answers the hand, granite answers the ear, slate answers the eye. Before you ask a face anything, know which of the three you are standing in front of.

Water has listened to this mountain for longer than we have been a people. Where it went, go. Where it would not go, do not make it.

A wet joint tells you where the water ran last year. A dry joint tells you nothing until the thaw. Wait for the thaw.

Read a face well and you have read the hold's next sixty-four years, because the hold will live inside what you read. Read it badly and the hold will live inside that instead, and it will not know whose reading it is living in until a tread goes.

A face and no bones. The chain keeps the saying in those four words, and the sense went with whoever first heard it. Khalvan glossed it: a hold that has its homes and no refuge. Kivil glossed it: a record full of warrants with nothing entered against a name. Both glosses are read. The four words are read first.

Count the cave before you cut it: its chambers, its drips, its columns, where its air moves and where its air stands. What you have not counted, you will cut through.

She said also, at the face of the north quarter, with an apprentice's hand on the seam: You are asking it where to cut. It is telling you where it already parts.

A tenth saying of Samad stands below these on the lead, worn until only the heavier strokes of the skeletons remain, and a skeleton without its frame is three sounds and no word: […]. The reader says that a saying of Samad is worn off, and goes on.
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-2}

## Khalvan of Ramk-sumád

Khalvan of Ramk-sumád laid courses. His claim of work for the lower hall stands on its lintel and is read with his sayings: _three cuts, no spoil carried_. He spoke in courses.

: The Courses of Khalvan {#khalvan}

::: {.verse form=bazan lang=en work=khalvan}
The course, the layer of the course, the laying:/
three, and the record keeps two./
The course keeps itself.

A course is laid on a course./
The course below was laid by a hand./
The hand went down into the mountain./
The course stayed up. It is yours now.

Lay yours true. You will not see it struck./
The striker is not born;/
the stroke is already in the stone/
and waits on the course, as you waited.

Raise it, and say the raising./
Leave it, and say what you left./
Between the raising and the striking/
a house goes up and down the stair/
and never once looks at its feet.

He said also, plainly: A course that needs a second course to hold it up was never a course. Enter it as a prop and take it out.

And: Three cuts and no spoil is a claim. Thirty cuts and a cart of spoil is a confession. Both go on the lintel, in the same hand, and the lintel does not care which it is carrying.

And: The hall is finished when the last person who saw it empty is dead, and nobody has asked why the pillar stands where it stands.

Over every course he laid he said the charm his house says, _Ramk dubáz-ak_, the course lies still. An apprentice asked him what the charm did to the course. He said: Nothing. The course lies still because it was laid true. Say it anyway. It tells your hand that the course is finished, and a hand that has not been told goes back for one more blow.
:::

bep, vem dulakez bep rapal thuf, mazadh. tob girm bep vunth dulakez bep gazav rapal, zanp-lumáv. zik zuvák lanat, ladhíp thanp, bep lanat thuf thudáv thaz vog buráth.

## Thimir of Marg-buráth

Thimir of Marg-buráth carried the name of her grandmother's mother, who had cut the lower stair of her house's quarter and left a tread that rocked. Thimir mended it in her thirtieth year, and the mending is entered under the first cut, in the same line, in the smaller hand. She spoke in courses.

: The Courses of Thimir {#thimri}

::: {.verse form=bazan lang=en work=thimri}
The hand that cut it is in the mountain./
The cut is the mountain,/
as far as it goes, and it goes as far as you walk.

Mend before you make./
The mender's name goes under the maker's,/
smaller, in the same line;/
the mending is the maker's too, once it is done,/
and her account is closed by a hand she never held.

Say which cut. Say where./
Do not say the dead cut badly;/
the dead cut this, here, in that year,/
and I was named to go there with my tools./
I went. It is entered. Walk on it.

She said also, plainly: A prop your grandfather set is his shame. It is yours as well, on every day it stands and you have walked past it with a hammer on your shoulder.

And: Nothing you make is yours after the warrant is cut. It belongs to the house, to the hold, and to whoever walks on it in the sixty-fourth generation.
:::

::: {.verse form=bazan lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=thimri}
thanp zi dulák, nalív gharm./
dilkath, gharm, tev tarv vog,/
vog funád zi ves tufán.

munád duráv bumár‼/
mandir nuláv bamrir kolg lanur,/
napík mardh, ladhíp thanp./
mand, bamur bamrir tav karím,/
thanp zi vog thuf thunáp kurám.

dulák bep zavan, gunádh vogun‼/
thuf zunáp falg pes gharp‼/
falg dulák kedz, kelg, kodz vadhal,/
pabarez murádh, bep gaz funád./
lamv durákh. tufán kolg vog‼

vog gunádh lez, sakap zi vanur vudár, zanp vanur. vog zanp ves lez, tavíz davan zi vog zuvák bep ves funád dev vog, damg kolg pagam.

bep, kodz zi ves bumár, ves thuf puvár vog nuzám favad. vagath puvár vog, bep khardev, bep khazár zi pugház vog, tanf bozd girm.
:::

## Rumkun of Bamg-durákh

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-5}
Rumkun of Bamg-durákh was born in a lean year and named for it, and his house kept the refuge. He said:

Fill the refuge first.

Count the caches at the turning of every year, aloud, before the records are read. Count them again on the first day of the thaw. A cache counted once a year has been counted by hope.

When you grudge what goes down to the caches, go down and look at it waiting.

If you are told to come down, bring your tools, because something is about to be decided that you will carry up again.

The refuge is sealed from the inside. Remember which side of the door you are standing on when you decide who comes in.

The oath you swear below is cut where you swore it, and you will walk past it on the way down to every funeral for the rest of your life. Swear it with that in mind.

I was born in a year the gate sent up nothing. My name says so. I have eaten from the gate every day since, and I have never once walked down to it.
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-5}
rumkun bamg-durákh, mardh gazím vadhal, bep vagath vog durákh hukhanf. vog gunádh,

ves huráp hukhanf, dhanív.

ves kumáv makasez tavíz vadhal-fusák, zagín, duráv hartir hurát limvathez. ves kumáv vogun hez, talz davan tob. makas zi kamív tob kalat tavíz vadhal, hapad kumáv vog.

tav ves zumán kodz zi nudháp makasez, ves nudháp bep vuzám vog puzál.

hiz vogun zugán ves nalív, ves buráth badakez, gez vasg muráz, bep ves buráth vog thuráp.

hukhanf bagín hadín. ves nubát zalv daghal zi ves zuvák kolg, tav ves vuság khazár zi bugár.

bant zi ves bunát nalív, khazár puráz vog kolg zi ves bunát vog, bep ves funád dev vog pes tavíz falg taghív param ves. ves bunát nuv kedz kolg mavadh.

gaz nazav vadhal zi tanv thuf suvár khapav. mardh gaz gunádh kedz. gaz gunát gol tanv tavíz davan nuzám, bep gaz thuf nudháp tanv tob kalat.
:::

## Nalpan of Talg-hunáth

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-6}
Nalpan of Talg-hunáth spanned gaps. The upper bridge of this hold, across the cleft of the stair, is his: two cuts, and the arch the rock had already made. He said:

A bridge is the place where the rock almost meets itself. Find that place. Where the rock does not almost meet, do not build a bridge. Build a stair.

Snow reads a face for you. Watch where it lies in the third month and where it will not lie, and you have the joints and the drips without striking a blow.

Frost opens what water entered. If you want to know where a cliff will shed, wait for the second winter and listen on the first warm morning. It will tell you, loudly, once.

A span is warranted on the day the first laden carrier crosses it and does not slow at the middle. Until then it is two cuts and an arch.
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-6}
nalpan talg-hunáth hunáth halghez. samíd hanthev kedz khardev, madan halgh satav, vog puvár vog, zik dulakez bep gathagh zi dalk guthágh duráv. vog gunádh,

hanthev, zavan zi dalk puzáv dalk. ves rukáv kodz zavan. kolg dalk thuf puzáv dalk, ves thuf hunáth. ves bumár satav.

talg rukád khabar pes ves. ves vuzám kolg talg dubáz gamun vem bep kolg talg thuf dubáz, bep ves puvár bakathez bep darpez, gev ves thuf thumár tob thamur.

hals puthák kodz zi baval bugár. hiz ves tumás ruthák zavan zi khabar purád, ves puzál nalp zik bep sumág dhanív davan halíd. vog gunádh tev ves, zagín, tob kalat.

hanthev favíd davan zi dhanív barthir fudhál vog bep thuf puzál kolg madan. duráv kodz, zik dulakez bep gathagh.
:::

## Kivil of Halb-zumád

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-7}
Kivil of Halb-zumád assayed silver, and kept her house's records for forty years after her hands stiffened. She said:

What is remembered is argued about. What is written is read.

Write the cut on the day you make it. A cut written in the second winter was made by a different hand, and the record will show it, because it will be a better cut than the one in the stone.

When the lead and the elder disagree, the lead is older than the elder. The elder has forgotten. The lead has not had the chance.

Silver is what it is at the weight it is, and a master's name on the ingot adds nothing to the balance. Keep the record the way you keep an assay.

Your slate is not a record. It is where you learn what a record costs. Wipe it as seldom as you cut.

In the deep hall, speak to what is written. If you remember otherwise, say so once, and then let the lead be read. You will be wrong more often than it is, and it keeps no count of how often it was right.

Four things are entered on the day they happen and never on the day after: a cut, a mending, an oath sworn below, and a death. Everything else can wait for the turning of the year.

Once in sixty-four years, and often not even then, the elders of several holds sit together and compound a house name for a hand no house can account for. Do not work toward it. The ones it was given to were doing something else.

You honored [Puthakun]{.name data-type="being/npc"} this morning when you tapped the face and waited. Everything you have said in his name since has been repeating yourself.

An apprentice copied the whole chain onto one slate and brought it to Kivil to be told that he had it. She set the slate on the assay balance against a blank one from the shelf and read him the weight. She said: It is heavier than it was. You are not.
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-7}
kivil halb-zumád zummád halb, bep durákh limvathez vagath vog zik girm bep kabur vadhalez nuzám thanpez duváb. vog gunádh,

kodz zi nabít, khazarez duráz. kodz zi nadík, hartirez hurát.

ves lumáv dulák davan zi ves dulák vog. dulák zi khazár lumáv nalp zik, khapív thanp dulák vog, bep lamv gunádh kedz, gez dulák kolg pabak bamír, gev dulák kolg dalk thuf.

tav pabak bep pabar thuf batunám, pabak batím. pabar dhumás. pabak thuf guván kalat pes dhams.

halb, halb, kolg thakab zi vog puvár, bep mardh gatharir kolg hilbath thuf thakíb. ves durákh lamv kos ves durákh zamd.

sirvath, lamv thuf. kolg sirvath ves vubák thaz lamv thakíb. ves surák vog dhapal, kos ves dulák.

kolg bamíg fanth, ves zugáb hirtath. hiz ves nubát khapav, ves gunádh vog tob kalat, bep hartir hurát pabak. ves gharíp ravíz kalatez burádh vog, bep pabak thuf kumáv kalatez zi vog varn.

lamv lumáv bozd kodz davan, bep thuf nuzám, dulák, mand, bant zi bunát nalív, bep thaval. khapav puzál vadhal-fusák.

tob kalat bozd girm vadhalez, bep dhapal kalatez thuf lez, pabarez khardevez ravíz suvám nuv bep vugáth vagath pes thanp zi vagath thuf fuvád. ves thuf bumár tev kedz. vogun zi guván vog bumár khapav.

kedz dhanív larg ves hunáz puthakun, tav ves tupák khabar bep puzál. tavíz gandh zi ves gunádh kob mardh vog nuzám, ves gunádh hez.

nabaf lamák taghív gathar-buzán kolg tob sirvath bep buráth vog tev kivil, bep thubágh, vog durákh vog zef. kivil thukáb sirvath bep maník sirvath gol lalap, bep hurát thakab tev vog. vog gunádh, «sirvath thakíb kald. ves thuf.»
:::

## Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-8}
Dulkun of Khalv-gunáv came to this hold from [Magar-thubád]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} when his work there was in order. He drove tunnels, refused the straight cut when it was asked of him, and spoke seldom. The chain keeps one saying of his, as he said it:

A prop is a question you did not ask, answered in timber.

The rest of what he said is in the account of the water-line, and is read there: [[lore-longtunnel|The Long Tunnel]].
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-8}
dulkun khalv-gunáv nuzáv kedz khardev gol magar-thubád, tav bamur vog kolg parív. vog nurráv, thuvár khatab tav vogun thubágh vog, bep gunádh dhapal. gathar-buzán durákh tob bazan vog, kos vog gunádh vog,

sakap, thubágh zi ves thuf thubágh, bep gatal gunádh tev vog.

khapav zi vog gunádh zuvák filnath baval-khunák, bep hartir hurát vog kolg, [[lore-longtunnel|narv-butám]].
:::

## Butum of Nalp-furád

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-9}
Butum of Nalp-furád led the hold's channels in the valley below it for most of her working life, and came up to teach when her knees would no longer take the slope. She said:

Everything the chain has told you about stone is true, and I add nothing to it. I will say one thing about the valley, because I was there and the chain was not.

We tap a face for a season before we open a window in it. We did not tap the valley. We built a granary and set a people beside it, as you set a prop, so that the hold would stand without listening to its own hunger.

I have stood in that valley at the turning of their year with the channel I led running past my feet, full of the hold's water, and watched them carry the hold's bread to the foot of the stair inside the rock. They do not come up past the gate. They have not asked to. Nobody has gone down to ask them whether they would.

I do not say the valley starves. It eats. I say that nobody in this hold ever sat in that valley for a season and listened to it, and that we would set aside the tools of an apprentice who treated a face that way.

If the hold calls this a fault, enter it against my name. I would rather it stood against mine than against nobody's.
:::

::: {.verse form=prose lang=khaz enc=khazpirzathenc work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-9}
butum nalp-furád khunák khankez khardev kolg khadhav nalív vog taghív param bamur vog, bep thuráp pes madar tav bartez thuf duzám rald. vog gunádh,

tavíz gandh zi gathar-buzán gunádh tev ves dalk, varn, bep gaz thuf thunáz khapav. gaz gunádh tob gandh khadhav, gez gaz zuvák kolg bep gathar-buzán thuf.

gazun tupák khabar fask duráv gazun vunád vand. gazun thuf tupák khadhav. gazun puthák hutadhaf bep pulát tamur madan vog, kos ves vudár sakap, bep khardev zuvák bep thuf sumág gazam vog.

gaz zuvák kolg khadhav vadhal-fusák vogun, khank zi gaz khunák ludháv dev tafanez, nuv baval khardev, bep gaz vuzám vogun buráth tadhaf khardev kolg satav buvár dalk. vogun thuf thuráp samíd tanv. vogun thuf thubágh. khazár thuf nudháp bep thubágh vogun, «vesun tumás zef?»

gaz thuf gunádh, khadhav gazím. vog gunát. gaz gunádh, khazár kedz khardev thuf durám kolg khadhav fask bep sumág vog, bep gazun pulát badakez nabaf zi thunáp khabar kos kodz.

hiz khardev murádh kedz zanp, vesun zunáp vog kob gaz. kob gaz, gev thuf kob maln.
:::

## Hulbun of Lakam-mulán

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-10}
Hulbun of Lakam-mulán, of the [Lummáv]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} clan (see [[lore-khazararrv|the Coming of the Khazari]]), kept the archive, and received listening from Butum. He answered her, and the chain keeps the answer whole. It is read in the keeper's voice, from the lead.

Butum has said what the gate was not given. I will read what it was given, because it is written, and what is written is read.

The channel that takes the eastern stream along the valley floor: cut by this hold, 252 paces, following the stream's old bed, no prop in it, warranted by three names. It waters their fields before it reaches our cistern.

The terraces of the southern slope: forty-one, set to the lie of the ground. Two slipped in the thaw of a wet year and were entered against the name of [Gurzun of Khard-vurán]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who had set them; he reset both along the lie of the ground at his own labor, with a master of [Ramk-sumád]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} watching, and the mending is entered under the fault in the same hand.

The drains of the lower fields: twenty-three.

The granary at the gate: a chamber in sound rock, dry in the wet years, theirs to fill and ours to keep sound. It has been kept sound.

The wall below the lower fields, which holds the river off them in the thaw: 220 paces.

The sluice at the channel head: one gate, opened by their hands and shut by their hands, and the hold has not opened or shut it in 384 years.

The road up from the valley: there is none. No track scars the slope. The gate is reached by the stair inside the rock, which was cut for laden carriers and has carried them.

The watch on the approaches, which has never once watched for the hold alone.

And on the other side of the lead, what the larder has fed: every mouth in this hold, in every year the record holds, and the record holds every year since the finding.

Nothing in that reckoning was asked for by the valley, beyond the mending it asks for each thaw. Nothing in it was refused by the valley either. If it is forcing, name the seam.

Butum heard the reckoning read and said: The lead says what they asked mended. It does not say what they would have asked if anybody had gone down to ask.

Hulbun said: Then it is not in the record.

Both are kept, on one sheet. The hold has entered neither against a name, and nobody has asked the elders to.

## Pirin of Zanth-guráp

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-11}
Pirin of Zanth-guráp ground edges and taught. She said:

A dull chisel forces. Whet it before you listen, because a hand that knows its edge is blunt will push, and the push goes into the stone.

Carry your rubble out yourself for the first ten years. The shoulder teaches the hand what the ear could not.

When the elders want the passage wider, say what the rock will give. When they want it sooner, say it again, in the same words. Say it below, where it is written. Refuse the cut the rock will not give, once, aloud, and after that whoever makes the cut makes it under their own name.

The first cut of a new passage is given to the hand that made the fewest. It is the hold putting its weight where it has felt the floor hold.

An elder can set your tools aside. He cannot set aside the fault. Decide which of the two you would rather carry down with you.

An elder who is wrong about a face is still your elder about everything else. Refuse the cut. Keep the rest.

Count the cuts before you make them, and count them again after. If the second count is larger, enter the difference.

A straight line is an admission. It says: I wanted this, and I took it.

The clumsy cut you hide is two cuts, the cut and the hiding. Both are entered when it is found, and it is found.

## Fardan of Hamk-dunák

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-12}
Fardan of Hamk-dunák lost his right hand to a slipped block in his twelfth year of striking, and read faces by ear for fifty years after. He said:

Ears outlast hands. Nobody tells you that until you need it.

A face the finders have read and the masters have sounded can still come down. It came down on me. Nobody had cut badly; the stone had a flaw that nobody had counted. That is entered too, against no name, and it is read to every apprentice who thinks that listening is a thing you finish.

When you can no longer strike, sit at the face with the apprentices. You will hear the thing they are about to miss, because you no longer have a hammer to hurry you.

A ringing stone has nothing behind it. A dead one has water behind it, or a flaw, or a chamber nobody has found. Three silences; learn all three, and learn them before you need to tell them apart in the dark.

The stone is in no hurry. Whatever hurry is in the workshop came in with you, and you can carry it out again.

Hang the hammer you cannot lift where the apprentices can see it. It is still warranted.

A hammer-fall has a note, and a wrong note is no louder than a right one. It is shorter.

The apprentice who cannot hear a dead stone will hear it on the day it comes down on him. Teach him before that day. The stone will not repeat itself.

## Tubur of Kaval-zunáth

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=the-sayings-of-the-masters-13}
Tubur of Kaval-zunáth is the last name on the lead. She said:

When a long work is finished, carry it up to the light and show it to the hold in the court. They have eaten the gate's bread on your account for the whole of it. Let them see what the bread bought.

If somebody who has never listened asks you whether the stone has a will, say that you have never needed to know, because it has a grain, and the grain is enough to be wrong about.

She set three riddles to apprentices, and the reading closes with them. An apprentice who cannot answer the third is told to listen another year.

The first. What is the one work in this hold that has no name against it in the record?

The cave, as it was found.

The second. I am entered on the day I am made. I am answered for after you are dead. The less of me there is, the better I was. What am I?

A cut.

The third. I am the finest work of every master in this hold, and no apprentice has been shown me. Where am I?

In the stone, uncut.

After the third answer the eldest master present says the saying that is older than the chain, and the apprentice says it back:

_Dalkir sumág dalk, dalkir khusáp._ The mason listens to the stone; the mason cuts little.

Here the hammer is given.
