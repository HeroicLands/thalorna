**The Going of the Exile** is the song the [[lore-khazariclt|Khazári]] keep for a man put out of the rock, and the one lament of theirs that is never sung below. It is a _kamgh_ (lament), in the broken course: a long line answered by a short one that falls away. Its subject is **Gisvin** of Tanvaguráz, keeper of the gate of **Basrakhunák**, a hold of the early centuries that no record outside the holds places, in the age when human tribes mined, herded and farmed for a hold as its subjects. In a hard winter he carried the hold's stores out through his own gate to the tribe below it, against the oath cut in the stone where he had sworn it, and the hold put him out. He does not argue the sentence. The song walks the hold from the slope beneath the gate and names what the sentence took, by count, and then names the tribe's dead, whom no Khazári record names.

The holds keep it in two frames. In some a master reads the record of the sentence and then the song to workers on the night before they swear their first oath below, so that they hear what the oath costs before they cut it; in others the record alone is kept, with the song copied beneath it in the hand form, and nobody reads it aloud. The holds that read it tell where it came from: he sang it at the field wall where the hold's counters came down on the eighth day to tally the deliveries, and the counters carried it up. The tribe's dead stood in it by name, in the tribe's own tongue, and no Khazár who carried the song could say the names, so every copy keeps the count and a gap for each. The song closes with one of two entries from the record of Basrakhunák, and the holds that keep each take their own for the entry as it was cut; the question has never been put to the elders of several holds together. The [[lore-vardain|Vardain]] beneath [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]] sing a version in their own tongue at the turning of the Khazári year, in which the tribe at the gate is their own forebears and the gaps are filled with Vardain names. The song as the holds keep it was old before Vorgald was found, and no Khazár acknowledges the Vardain version.

Two words of the text need glossing. The _marg_ (bone) of a name is its three consonants, which carved [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]] cuts deep, and the binding is the vowels scored between them, which say why the name was given. The gate-row is the reduced row of Pirzath a hold taught its tribe for tally, mark, measure and oath; at Basrakhunák's gate a Khazár's name was cut on sacks and tools as its bone alone.

## The Record of the Sentence

_From the record of Basrakhunák, as the holds copy it._

In the year of the hold 484 the snow lay on the gate-road from the first day of the cold to the forty-first day of the thaw. Of the herd of the gate, 120 head driven up in the autumn, nine stood at the thaw. Of the tribe at the gate, 206 at the autumn count; 178 at the thaw. The hold's share from the gate was short by a third at the autumn count, and the elders opened the caches to the houses by need, and none to the gate.

Gisvin of Tanvaguráz, keeper of the gate, sworn below in the year of the hold 423 that nothing of the hold's goes out by the gate but by the elders' word, went down to the caches on the thirty-second night of the cold, on the fortieth and on the fifty-first, and carried out by the gate: barley, twelve sacks by the gate-measure; dried meat, four; salt, one; oil, two jars. **Dhonko** of Famgadurákh, keeper of the caches, saw him on the third night and entered it. He was called down. He was asked. He said: I opened it. He said nothing else.

The council sat in the deep hall on the fifty-fourth day. The oath was read from the stone where it was cut; the record was read; the record stood. An oath sworn below is broken: he does not go down again. The hold's stores were carried out of the hold: he is put out of the hold, with no house and no place in the refuge. His tools to Tanvaguráz. Both were cut under the oath. He went out by the gate on the fifty-fifth day, in snow. The caches were made good by the houses before the thaw.

The record ends at the gate.

## The Song

: The Going of the Exile {#song}

```poetry {form=kamgh lang=en}
The bars go across the gate at evening.
  I hear them from here.
Somebody draws them, at the hour I drew them,
  a little after the light.

The lamp in the gate is lit.
  I did not light it.
Sixty-one years I lit it, every evening,
  oil from the jar on the second shelf,
the wick trimmed, the shutter half across
  so the face shows nothing.
Somebody trims it now.
  It burns the same.

I am on the slope below the gate-road,
  where the gate can be seen.
From here it is a crack in the face
  with frost in it.
That is as it should be.
  I know where to look.

Four bars go across the gate on the inside.
  They are the oldest iron in the hold.
**Gasvan** of Tanvaguráz made them and quenched them in the sump,
  and the water hissed, the account says,
and the iron set.
  They hold.
I was named to take up his quenching.
  I took it up.

Hear my claim of work. It is short.
  I cut little.
Three cuts on the stair from the gatehouse to the watch,
  where the rock was nearly a stair;
the third cut was the one that made it,
  and it is walked.
One groove for the bar to run in, the year of the quake,
  when the old groove sheared.
That is all the stone I cut in sixty-one years.
  It is enough.
No prop in it. No scar on the face.
  Nothing carried away.
The stair is in the record under my name,
  and the groove,
and under them both the sentence,
  cut by another hand.
The stair is walked. The name is out.
  The stone keeps it.

I will walk the hold from outside.
  It is the only way left.
The face from the south buttress to the seep-stain:
  nine openings.
The third is Tanvaguráz. The fourth is Tanvaguráz.
  The fifth was mine.
I know its sill by the chip in the left edge
  where my father set the lamp down hard.
A chip is no cut. Nobody answers for a chip.
  I looked at it every morning.
Behind the fifth opening a room, and behind the room
  the passage.
Forty-one steps from the gatehouse to the first court
  along the joint, and the joint bends twice.
I counted them going out.
  Going in, I never had.
The first court, where the barley is laid out when it comes up
  and counted by the sack;
where they sang when the long stair was finished,
  the year I was sworn;
where my sister was married and my brother's children were named
  and I stood in the fourth row;
where the smoke comes along the face at evening
  and goes thin.
Behind the court the workshops, and the forge where it is warm,
  and the anvil-floor,
and the room where the old sit against the wall
  and the apprentices tap it and listen.
I listened there four years before they gave me the gate.
  The wall is sound.
My hammer is on the wall of the gatehouse, on the second peg.
  It was always the house's.
The hand was mine.
  It is here.

I had the gate from **Tonvon** of Tanvaguráz,
  who had it from **Tonvo**,
who had it from the one who hung the bars,
  Gasvan, who quenched them.
Four keepers of the gate since the bars were hung.
  I am the fourth.
Tonvon taught me the oil and the wick and the bar
  and the sound of the bars in frost;
the count at the gate, what comes up and what goes down,
  the tally cut and the tally kept;
and the oath. He stood beside me in the deep
  while I cut it.
Oath of stone, oath of bone.
  I said it. I cut it clean.
Who has the gate from me?
  Nobody.
They will give it to somebody, and he will be the fifth from the bars,
  and the record will say so:
Gasvan, Tonvo, Tonvon, Gisvin, and the fifth.
  The record keeps every name.
The house will say Gasvan, Tonvo, Tonvon, and the fifth.
  The record stands.

Below the court the stair goes down, and I have gone down it
  for oaths, for the dead,
for the turning of the year.
  This year the record will be read.
My name is in it once. They will read it and go on.
  The next name is a mason's.
I will be on the slope. The snow will be on the slope.
  The lamps are inside.

My father is below, in the chamber the hold cut him
  past the sump, where the drip is.
I laid him there. I kept the silence.
  The words were said:
the course has been raised, the course will be struck.
  I said them with the rest.
I will not go down to him again.
  He is kept.
When my mother goes down I will not go down.
  Somebody will say the words.
No hold cuts my chamber.
  The slope is wide.

In the refuge my place is cut,
  the measure of a body and a jar.
At the turning of the year they count the places.
  Mine is struck.
The place is there. The stone is the same.
  The count is one less.
When the hold is sealed the gate is shut from inside
  and the bars go across,
Gasvan's bars, that I was named for.
  I am on the outside of them.
That is what the sentence says, and it is well cut.
  The stone keeps it.

Now the winter. I will give it as the record gives it,
  and then I will give the rest.
The snow came on the first day of the cold and lay
  to the forty-first of the thaw.
The hold's share from the gate was short by a third.
  I had tallied it. It was short.
A hundred and twenty head were driven up in the autumn.
  Nine stood at the thaw.
Two hundred and six at the autumn count.
  A hundred and seventy-eight at the thaw.
The record is right. It is always right.
  I kept the gate-tally myself.
Twenty-eight.
  The record has the number.
The record counts what comes up to the gate.
  It counted them. It does not name them.
I have the names.
  I will say them.

[…], who drove the herd up in the autumn with the lead ox by the horn
  and laughed at the gate.
[…], his wife, who brought the cheeses on the eighth day,
  and they were counted.
[…], a child of two winters,
  in the first week of the cold.
[…], her mother, the same week.
  […], the old man who kept the fire.
[…], […], […], brothers, who went down the valley after the herd
  and were found at the thaw.
[…]
  […]
[…], who stood at the gate on the thirty-second night
  and did not ask.
[…] and […], on the fortieth night, before I came out
  with the second load.
[…], the smallest.
  […], who could cut a tally straight.
[…], […], […], […], […], of one hearth,
  in the fifth week.
[…]
  […]
[…], who carried the first sack down.
  […], […], […], […].
[…], the last, on the fifty-first day,
  when there was barley.
Twenty-eight. The names are in their tongue.
  I have said them.

The elders said: the caches are the refuge's,
  and the refuge is the hold's.
They said: the hold eats what comes up from the gate,
  and what came up was short.
It was short.
  I had tallied it.
They opened the caches to the houses by need,
  four mouths and one pair of hands drawing more.
They said: a hold that opens its caches for the gate
  has none for the hold.
That is so. I have heard it read.
  I had tallied the same.

I went down with a lamp on the thirty-second night.
  The caches are past the refuge door.
I opened one. I was sworn to keep the gate shut on it.
  I opened it.
Twelve sacks by the gate-measure. Four of meat.
  Salt, one. Oil, two jars.
Three nights. I carried it up the stair I had cut
  with three cuts,
and out through the gate between Gasvan's bars,
  and set it down in the snow.
Each time I went down I took the lamp.
  You go down with a lamp.
Dhonko of Famgadurákh saw me the third night.
  She keeps the caches.
She entered it. That is her work, and she did it.
  I would have entered it.
They called me down. They read the oath from the stone.
  I had cut it clean.
They asked me. I said: I opened it.
  I said nothing else.
The rest was in the record.
  The record spoke.

The council sat. The record was read,
  and the record stood.
An oath sworn below and broken: no going down.
  That is the law, and I had heard it read.
The hold's stores carried out of the hold:
  out of the hold.
Both were cut under the oath while I stood there.
  I did not see whose hand.
I heard the strokes. The hand was steady.
  No wound in it.

Gisvin. Quench, with the forebear's vowel.
  Taken up again.
The elders chose it. They answer for it.
  They chose well.
A quench sets the iron. A quench puts the fire out.
  Both are in the bone.

I went out by the gate on the fifty-fifth day.
  It was snowing.
Tonvon's son shut the gate behind me, and I heard the bars.
  I know their sound in frost.
Nine hundred paces down the gate-road to the first field wall.
  I had never counted it.
They were at the wall. They had seen the gate open.
  It opens seldom.
They took me in.
  I had not asked.

A hold grows nothing. The gate grows barley,
  and I eat it.
I ate the barley I carried out, what was left.
  They gave me the first of it.
I taught them nothing. What they have of me they have by watching,
  as they have the rest.
They watched me set the lintel of their long house true
  without a measuring-rod.
They have set two since. Both are a little out.
  Nobody answers for it.

I sing this at the wall where you come down to count.
  Tally it.
Carry it up the road, and sing it in the court;
  it will carry along the passages.
Do not take it down.
  It is not for below.

They will put me in a cave. There is one above the fields,
  dry, facing east.
Nobody found it. It was there, and they put the ewes in it
  at the lambing.
A cave is found before it is made. This one was not made.
  It will do.
They will cut my name in it. They have the row
  for tally, mark, measure and oath,
and they will cut it the way they cut a tally, in a straight row, across the seam,
  and the stone will split.
They know the bone. It was on every sack I counted.
  The binding they never saw.
Bone and no binding. Quench, and no vowel.
  Whoever finds it, read it.
Read the bone. The binding is the finder's.
  Choose.

The course has been raised.
  The course will be struck.
Nobody will say it over me.
  It is said.

The bars go across.
  Somebody draws them.
They sound the same in frost.
```

## The Two Endings

The holds that read the song to workers stop, in most of them, before the course in which he says the words over himself, and the workers hear the rest after they have sworn or not at all. Every copy then closes with an entry from the record of Basrakhunák, and the entry is kept in two forms.

_The first._ In the third great count of the hold, **Verne** of Thalgahunáth, apprenticed to the finders, reading the east face of the valley above the gate's fields for a watch-post, found a cave, dry, facing east, with a name cut in it in the gate-row: the bone g-s-v, cut in a straight row across a seam, split at the third letter where the row crossed it, and no binding. She cut the third letter again, on the seam's side, and scored the binding _i_. The mending is entered to her name. The cut it mends is entered to no name.

_The second._ In the third great count of the hold, Verne of Thalgahunáth, apprenticed to the finders, reading the east face of the valley above the gate's fields for a watch-post, found a cave, dry, facing east, with a name cut in it in the gate-row: the bone g-s-v, cut in a straight row across a seam, split at the third letter where the row crossed it, and no binding. She left it as she found it. Entered: found. The cave was not taken.
