**The Quarrel of the Clans**, _Sarghadugár_, is the account the [[lore-khazariclt|Khazári]] keep of the sittings at [[lore-khazarturn|Khazártúrn]] in which the seven clans contested the city's farmland, its mines and the share of its gate, and of the two sendings that cut the first holds outside the valley. It is a _filnath_, an account: the sittings in the order they sat, dated in the years of the city, with the counts the elders argued from and every name, and the speeches entered by name in the keeper's words. The name is the holds' and not the account's own; _sarghadugár_ renders as the forcing of the clans, and neither the name nor the account says which clan forced which. It is read below, in the council-hall, to an elder on the day she first takes a seat on a hold's council, and her oath is cut after the last line, so that no elder sits who has not heard why no seat is first. The account calls Khazártúrn only the city, because when it was written there was no other hold to tell it from; the word _khardev_, a hold, is entered in it for the first time of a place that was not the city. The copy transcribed is [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]]'s, and the holds' copies descend from the one the city sent up to Zagharagunáv in the year the account closes.

This is the account of the sittings of the council of the city from the year of the city seven sixties and fourteen to the year seven sixties and thirty-eight, of the two sendings, and of the setting down of the ordering of the watch. **Rontho** of Vanthakhuván, keeper, entered each sitting on the day it sat and each sending on the day it went out, and the elders witnessed each entry at the next sitting.

The council of the city sat seven, one for each tower, chosen by the elders of the tower's houses, and the seat of the sixth tower held the ordering of the watch, which is the gate, the shifts of the seven towers and the door of the refuge, and the one who held the ordering spoke first and last. It had been so from the raising. The seven at the first sitting of this account were **Rekadhe** of Khardavurán for the first tower, which is Summágh's; **Zadavan** of Dalkathumár for the second, which is Dullák's; **Tamvan** of Ramkasumád for the third, which is Rummák's; **Saltha** of Hamkadhunák for the fourth, which is Gullád's; **Zagharan** of Ravadarunáth for the fifth, which is Vurrán's; **Dirkhin** of Tanvaguráz for the sixth, which is Gurráz's, who held the ordering; and **Linrin** of Lakamakhulán for the seventh, which is Lummáv's.

The count of the city at the turning of the year seven sixties and thirteen, by tower: the first, nine sixties and twenty; the second, thirty-four sixties and ten; the third, forty-one sixties; the fourth, thirty-eight sixties and thirty; the fifth, twenty-nine sixties; the sixth, twenty-one sixties and forty; the seventh, eleven sixties and twelve. Three great counts, four sixties and fifty-two. The share of the gate was by tower, seven parts, as it was set at the raising, when no tower's count stood more than a sixty from another's.

## The First Sitting

In the year of the city seven sixties and fourteen, on the nineteenth day of the thaw, the council sat below, at the table. The table is the floor's own stone, left standing when the floor was leveled around it, three cuts, and it is warranted on its underside.

Saltha of Hamkadhunák spoke first after the ordering. She said that the fourth tower drew one part of the gate for thirty-eight sixties and thirty, and the seventh drew one part for eleven sixties and twelve, and that a part was a part. She said that in the fourth tower the part was eaten by the forty-first day of the cold in every year of the last nine, that the caches had made up the rest and the fourth tower's houses had made them good in the thaw, and that this was entered every year as a debt and struck every year, and a debt struck nine times is a debt. She asked that the share go by head. She asked, if it would not, that the north shore of the lake be terraced for the fourth tower, sixty and forty paces of flat ground the gate's people do not plant because it is wet in the thaw. The keeper kept four of her words: _Hunger is by head._

Zagharan of Ravadarunáth said that the vein his house had followed under the sixth tower went down: it ran under the refuge at sixty measures below the refuge's floor and thickened going down, and the forty measures of granite between the lowest working and the floor were entered sound by the readers of three houses. He said the ore above the floor was three years from won, and that the far workings at the head of the valley gave forty measures in the year at a day's carrying each way. He asked that the working below the refuge's floor be opened along the vein.

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz struck the table with the flat of his hand. The keeper entered it. He asked that it stand. It stands.

Dirkhin said that the ordering had refused the working under the refuge four times in the record and refused it now, because a working under the refuge is a way into the refuge that the refuge did not cut, and a refuge is shut from inside or it is not a refuge. He said the share went by tower because the watch went by tower: the seventh tower kept a tower's watch with eleven sixties as the fourth kept one with thirty-eight, every night, and drew a tower's share for it. He said the north shore was the valley's one road from the outflow, and a shore full of terraces and a tribe is a shore the watch cannot see across.

Rekadhe of Khardavurán said that her house had not read the north shore since the raising, and that she would read it for a season before she entered anything. She said the cliff had been read for an eighth spur when the second tower first asked for one, and the entry was that the cliff holds seven and no eighth.

Tamvan of Ramkasumád said that the upper hall of the third tower stood at the ninth bay of twelve, that the hands dressing it were the second tower's, and that whatever the council found, he asked that it find nothing which took those hands from the hall.

Linrin of Lakamakhulán was asked by Dirkhin to read what the record held of the share. He read it. The record holds that the share was set by tower in the year of the raising, by the seven then sitting, and holds the counts of that year. It holds no finding that the share stay by tower, and no finding that it go by head. He said so, and said nothing further.

The council found nothing. It was entered that the finder would read the north shore for a season, that the readers of the fifth tower and of the sixth tower's houses together would sound the floor of the refuge, and that the council would sit when both were entered.

Witnessed at the second sitting: the seven.

## The Readings

In the year of the city seven sixties and fifteen the north shore was read by Rekadhe of Khardavurán from the lake, on sixty and three days; the tally is in the archive. She entered: the north shore is the lake's old floor, and it creeps. A stake set at the water's edge on the ninth day of the thaw stood four fingers nearer the water on the sixtieth. A terrace walled on it walks. She entered against terraces on the north shore, and entered that the slope above it, where the gate's people plant, is planted to its last wall.

In the same year the readers of Ravadarunáth, Khalvagunáv and Famgadurákh sounded the floor of the refuge from the lowest working on nine days and entered the granite sound for forty measures below the floor, whole, no joint crossing it. Famgadurákh entered beneath the three names that a sound floor is a sound floor and the refuge's door is on the other side of it. It stands.

In the same year Rekadhe of Khardavurán went along the ridge north of the valley with two of her house, on her own asking, for nineteen days, where the vein's outcrop runs, and entered a cave at two days' walk, its mouth on a south face above a stream, dry, with air moving in it, and the vein in its floor. The council had not sent her. She entered that she went.

## The Second Sitting

In the year of the city seven sixties and seventeen, on the fourth day of the thaw, the council sat below, and the readings were read.

Saltha of Hamkadhunák said that the north shore was read against and she had heard it, that the share stood where it had stood three years before, and that the fourth tower had made its caches good twice more. She asked again that the share go by head, in the words of the first sitting.

Zagharan of Ravadarunáth said that forty measures of granite had been entered sound by the sixth tower's own house, and that the ordering had refused the working again that morning, in the gatehouse, to him alone. He said that a prop which has stood seven sixties of years is taken for rock by everyone who walks under it, and that the ordering was such a prop: set at the raising, when a watch had to be ordered before there was a council to order it, and never sounded since. The keeper kept his words: _Sound it._

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz said that a prop is taken out when the roof above it has been sounded and found to hold, and asked who had sounded the roof. He said the roof was this: that seven towers watch one valley, and the valley is one, and the lake is one, and the watch is one because the valley is one; that a hold two days along the ridge has one approach and its own watch, and what comes up the ridge to it comes up to miners and cutters with no house of the sixth tower among them. He said a hold of two crafts has no forge, no lamp-oil, no water-leader and no larder, that it would send to the city for what it lacked and the city would send, at two days' carrying each way. He asked the keeper to read the oath the seven swore before the narrows, which **Puthakun** had them swear.

Rontho of Vanthakhuván, keeper, said that the oath is not in the record. It was sworn where there was no stone of the people to cut it in. The record holds that it was sworn, and holds the count at the far mouth of the narrows, and holds no word of it.

Dirkhin said that the count is the oath's keeping, and that a sending breaks the count.

Linrin of Lakamakhulán said that the count is kept where the heads are counted, and a head counted at a hold is counted. He said the record can enter a party sent out from the city as it enters a party sent to the far workings, under the city's count and at another place, and a count so entered is split on the lead and sums. He said that a party which went out without being entered would not be counted, and the record could do nothing about that.

Zadavan of Dalkathumár said that the second tower held thirty-four sixties and ten in rooms cut for twenty sixties, four to a room on the upper galleries; that the cliff holds seven spurs and no eighth, read three times; and that the cutters of the second tower had cut the third tower's halls for nine years and had no face of their own to open a window in. He said the cutters would go where there was a face, and that a new hold wants its cutters before anything.

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz said that the third tower's hall stood at the ninth bay.

Zagharan of Ravadarunáth rose. Zadavan of Dalkathumár rose. They went out of the council-hall together, by the stair. The keeper entered the hour: the second hour of the second shift. The door was not shut after them. Nothing was found. The council did not sit again that day.

Witnessed at the third sitting: the seven.

## The Third Sitting

In the year of the city seven sixties and seventeen, on the twenty-sixth day of the thaw, the council sat below. Zagharan of Ravadarunáth and Zadavan of Dalkathumár sat.

Zagharan said that three lines of Ravadarunáth and the houses of Parnasumád and Ganvahumák would go to the cave on the ridge, and of Dullák two lines of Kavalazunáth and two lines of Dalkathumár, in the thaw of the next year when the ridge was open. He asked that the record enter them as gone out of the city.

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz said that the record would not enter them as gone. He said that a party gone is not counted and a party sent is counted, and that the stores go with a party that is sent and not with one that goes. The keeper kept his words: _Enter it as a sending._ He asked that the finder who read the cave go with the sending, so that what was cut was found first; that the caches be opened to it by count, a season's grain by head, as to any party sent to the far workings; and that it be entered under the city's count and at the ridge. He said the ordering refused the working under the refuge's floor and would refuse it, and that nothing in the record stopped a vein from being followed from the other end of it.

The council found as follows. The party of the fifth and second towers is a sending. Rekadhe of Khardavurán goes with it to the cave and returns. The caches are opened to it by count. Zagharan of Ravadarunáth asked that the record show he had asked to be entered as gone, and it shows it, under the finding. Tamvan of Ramkasumád entered against the sending, for the hall. Saltha of Hamkadhunák entered nothing.

Witnessed at the fourth sitting: the seven then sitting, and Zagharan of Ravadarunáth and Zadavan of Dalkathumár by their copies sent down from the ridge.

## The First Sending

In the year of the city seven sixties and eighteen, on the sixty and seventh day of the thaw, the first sending went out by the gate. Of the fifth tower: of Ravadarunáth, three lines of seven, two sixties and fifty-one; of Parnasumád, the house, three sixties and nine; of Ganvahumák, the house, three sixties and four. Of the second tower: of Kavalazunáth, two lines of four, two sixties and thirty-eight; of Dalkathumár, two lines of five, one sixty and thirty-four. Thirteen sixties and sixteen went out. Rekadhe of Khardavurán went with them and is counted in the first tower. The stores went by count, a season's grain by head, and the tally is in the archive; of iron, sixty bars. The tally-sticks of each house went with each house.

What the sending took from the city is entered in the year-record, and these lines are read from it. The upper hall of the third tower stood at the ninth bay on the day of the sending and stood there fourteen years. The far workings gave forty measures of ore in the year before the sending and nineteen in the year after. The watch of the fifth tower, which had sat four shifts, sat three from that night, and the fifth tower kept its own watch with its own houses, as every tower keeps its own. Of twelve sixties of chisels in the city's tool-stores, four sixties went.

The sending came to the cave on the third day. Rekadhe of Khardavurán read it on the fourth and marked the refuge on the fifth, in a chamber above the stream that breathes through its own crack, before any home, and came back to the city on the twelfth. The hold on the ridge was entered in the record of the city as a hold, _khardev_, the first time the word is entered of a place that was not the city, and the sending named it **Zagharagunáv**, the delving of ore. In the year after, the first iron came down from the ridge: two sixties of bars, carried in four days, for sixty measures of grain. It is the first exchange in the record of the city between the city and a hold.

In the cold of the year of the sending the elders of the houses of the second tower chose **Kivali** of Dalkathumár for the seat of the second tower, and the elders of the houses of the fifth tower chose **Ganva** of Khalvagunáv for the seat of the fifth, and both sat from the turning of that year.

## The Fourth Sitting

In the year of the city seven sixties and twenty-one, on the fortieth day of the cold, Saltha of Hamkadhunák died in her house's rooms on the face of the fourth tower, with her work in order. The city went down for her and kept the silence, and the words were said, and she was laid in the deep and her name was cut. The elders of the houses of the fourth tower chose **Tespen** of Hamkadhunák, her brother's son, for the seat.

In the year of the city seven sixties and twenty-two, on the eleventh day of the thaw, the council sat below.

Tespen of Hamkadhunák said that the share stood by tower, that the fourth tower had made its caches good in every thaw of the account, and that the hold on the ridge had sent down two sixties of bars smelted at a hearth of stones in the open by a house with no forge, and entered in the hold's record against no smith, because the hold had none. He said the ore was at the ridge and the hearths would go to the ore: three lines of Hamkadhunák, two of Balgatumák and two of Valdasukár, and a line of Sakarapuláz, in the thaw, to cut a hold of their own a day beyond Zagharagunáv, where the finder's house had read a second cave. He said that Gullád's hearths had answered to an ordering since the raising because the raising needed them ordered, and that the raising was done. The keeper kept his words as he said them: _We follow no more._ He asked the council for a house name for the going.

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz said that the forges of the fourth tower had made every tool the seven towers were cut with and every bar on every gate in the city, and that the city and the fourth tower were one lead and one count. He said that the share would go by head from the turning of that year if the council found it, and asked the council to find it. He said the ordering had refused one working and had sent one sending with its stores, and asked what in the ordering's record was the prop.

Tespen of Hamkadhunák said that it was not the record. It was the first word and the last.

Linrin of Lakamakhulán said that the record held no grant of a house name in it, from the crossing to that day, and that one hold's council could not name the work of a hold not yet cut. The council did not grant the name.

The council found as follows. The share of the gate goes by head from the turning of the year. Five entered for it, Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz among them. Tamvan of Ramkasumád entered against it, and asked that the record show that the third tower, which is the largest, had drawn a seventh for forty-one sixties and had not asked. It shows it. Tespen of Hamkadhunák entered nothing. The party of the fourth tower is a sending, with its stores by count, under the city's count and at the ridge. Tespen of Hamkadhunák asked that the record show he had asked for no stores. It shows it, under the finding. The stores went.

Witnessed at the fifth sitting: the seven then sitting, and Tespen of Hamkadhunák by his copy sent down from the ridge.

## The Second Sending

In the year of the city seven sixties and twenty-three, on the fifty-second day of the thaw, the second sending went out by the gate. Of the fourth tower: of Hamkadhunák, three lines of five, three sixties and forty; of Balgatumák, two lines of four, two sixties and fifty-one; of Valdasukár, two lines of three, three sixties and forty-one; of Sakarapuláz, one line, forty-two. Ten sixties and fifty-four went out. The stores are on a sheet the holds' copies do not carry whole: grain, ten sixties and fifty-four measures; oil, […] jars; iron in bars, none, the sending refusing it; charcoal, nine sixties of loads, the whole of the fourth tower's store, which the sending did not refuse; bellows, nineteen.

What the sending took from the city is entered in the year-record, and these lines are read from it. In the fourth tower nineteen hearths of thirty-one stood cold on the night of the sending. They were cleaned and laid, as a hearth is laid at the end of a shift, and they stand laid. The twelve that burned worked the bars that came down from the ridge, and the city's iron came down from the ridge from that year, by exchange. The upper galleries of the fourth tower above the ninth stair, cut for the houses that went, stood finished and unlit, and were kept.

The second sending came to Zagharagunáv on the third day and went on to the second cave on the fifth, and cut there, and named the hold **Farzasumád**, the raising of the forge. The first fire in it was struck on the eighth day after the sending went out, from the city's charcoal, and the record of the city holds the day because the record of Farzasumád sent it down to be entered.

The count of the city at the turning of the year seven sixties and twenty-three, less the two sendings, was two great counts, forty sixties and forty-two. The count at the ridge, by the copies sent down, was twenty-four sixties and ten. The sum is in the year-record. It sums.

## The Fifth Sitting

In the year of the city seven sixties and twenty-seven the elders of Zagharagunáv and of Farzasumád sat together at Zagharagunáv, the first sitting in any record of the elders of more than one hold, and compounded for the three lines of Hamkadhunák at Farzasumád that had lit the first forge in a hold that was not the city a house of their own, **Khardafuráz**, the forging of the hold. It is the first house name in any record compounded since the crossing, and the first compounded by the elders of more than one hold. The grant was sent down on lead. The council of the city entered that the grant was the holds' and not the city's, and that the record of the city enters those lines as Hamkadhunák's beside it. Both stand.

In the year of the city seven sixties and twenty-nine, on the first day of the thaw, the council sat below. Seven sat. The seat of the fourth tower was taken by **Nafara** of Zanthaguráf, chosen by the elders of the twelve hearths that burned.

Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz asked the keeper to read the watch by tower, and it was read: the first tower, two shifts; the second, three; the third, four; the fourth, two; the fifth, three; the sixth, four; the seventh, two. He said that seven towers were manned and each by its own clan, as each had been every night of the account, that this was the whole of what the ordering had been for since the raising, and that it did not need an ordering to do it. He set down the ordering of the watch before the council. He said the sixth tower would keep the gate, because the gate is in the sixth tower's spur, and its own shifts and no other tower's, and that the door of the refuge would be Famgadurákh's, whose house holds the deep. He said he would speak at the table when his tower's turn came, and not first, and not last. He asked that the record show the ordering set down and not taken from him, and that it show the two sendings beside it. It shows the three in that order.

The council did not take up the ordering. Linrin of Lakamakhulán said that the record held no finding that there be an ordering, as it held none that the share stay by tower. Nafara of Zanthaguráf said that the twelve hearths had chosen her by the elders of twelve houses sitting together with no first among them, because there had been no time to make one.

The council found as follows. The council of the city sits as the elders of its houses, each house one, and no seat is first. The watch of each tower is its own clan's and its own ordering. The share of the gate is by head. What a house asks, the council hears; what the council finds, the record keeps; a party that goes out is sent, and is counted. The finding was sworn below, at the table, by the seven and by the elders of the houses of the seven towers, four sixties and eleven, who are its witnesses, and from that day the council sat one elder for each house. It was cut in the wall of the council-hall along the bedding seam above the table by **Vinthin** of Dalkathumár, nine lines along the seam, verb-last, in the old order of carved stone. The holds cut it in their council-halls in the same words, a hold with no table cutting it as it stands.

: The Oath of the Table {#oath}

```poetry {form=pirzath lang=en}
Below, at the table, the elders of the houses of this hold together sit.
First among them none sits.
Each tower its own watch keeps; each house its own hearth.
By head the share is reckoned; the heads the record counts.
What a house asks, the council hears.
What the council finds, the record keeps.
Who goes out, sent goes; who is sent, counted goes.
On this table no hand first falls.
Dalkith vanth, margith vanth.
```

Witnessed at the sixth sitting, the first of the elders of the houses: the four sixties and eleven who swore, whose names are on the lead and are not read.

## The Last Entries

Of the years after, the account holds the sittings of the elders of the houses, and they are read from the year-records and are not read here, but for these.

In the year of the city seven sixties and thirty-two the upper hall of the third tower was finished at the twelfth bay by the hands that stayed, three lines of Dalkathumár, and Tamvan of Ramkasumád warranted it.

In the year of the city seven sixties and thirty-eight, on the thirtieth day of the cold, Dirkhin of Tanvaguráz died in the gatehouse of the sixth tower, in the shift, with his work in order. He had held the ordering of the watch from the year of the city six sixties and fifty-one to the year seven sixties and twenty-nine, and had sat for his house after it for nine years, and the record holds no cut against his name. His hammer went to Tanvaguráz. The city went down for him and kept the silence, and the words were said, and he was laid in the deep and his name was cut. The watch of the sixth tower that night was Tanvaguráz's, four shifts, as it had been every night of the account. The gate was shut at the hour it is shut.

At the turning of that year the galleries of the fourth tower above the ninth stair were swept, as they had been at every turning since the second sending. Nobody lives in them. They are finished.
