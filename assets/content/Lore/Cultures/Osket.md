The [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] hold the western and central crossings of [[place-graznmntns|the Grazian wall]] by descent, and the rest of what they are follows from that. A hearth is a house, a herd, a high pasture and one road. The road is what gives it standing. Nothing above the hearth binds it, and no body exists that could.

## The Road Is the Property

Herds are wealth and a crossing is standing, and the two are not traded for one another. A hearth that holds [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]] is rich and lends at interest to caravan-masters who have misjudged the season. A hearth at [[place-tsokhar|Tsökhar]] takes two caravans in a good season and lives off its yaks and its horses. Both are heard at the cols on the same terms, because both hold a road.

A hearth with beasts and no road is fed and is not consulted. The Ösket state this without apology and see nothing in it to explain.

Selling a road is the one capital offense. The man who does it is **Roadless**: out of every hearth, fed by none of them, and taken over the wall by nobody. A people who feed any stranger who reaches a door have made exactly that one exception. What the Ösket say about the two sales of [[place-gudesroad|the Guides' Road]] they say among themselves, and everyone else has the rumor.

## The Guide-mother

The knowledge of a road is a woman's and the walking of it is a man's. A guide-mother holds the weather-signs, the years a shelf has moved, the loads a yak-route will carry, and the count of the dead and where each of them stopped. She says whether the road is open on a given morning, and her word ends the question. The distinction is not ceremonial, and no lowlander has ever successfully bought round it.

A daughter who will hold a road is taught by the woman who holds it now. A son is taught the ground. Neither instruction is written down anywhere.

## The Door

Anyone who reaches an Ösket door is fed and housed, without payment and without exception, and the Roadless are the only people the rule leaves out. The obligation falls on the hearth-warden who keeps the winter stores, and it holds hardest through the closed months, when the store is the whole of a village's economy.

It is the one Ösket custom lowlanders find entirely legible, and the one they most often abuse. The Ösket treat the abuse as the price of the custom and have never proposed a remedy for it.

## Outside the Order

The [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]] order of stations stops at the last [[place-pssshrines|Pass-Shrine]]. A shrine-ushtaka, priest of the summit temple, will eat in an Ösket house on the col and would not do so in the valley, and no court has once been asked to settle what that means.

The Ösket carry no wrist-line and read nobody else's. A traveler's station is not asked for on the wall, and a wrapped wrist means nothing there at all.

## What Is Said, and What Is Not

An Ösket tells an outsider what to do and not why. This is not mystification. It is what a people does when the alternative is arguing at nineteen thousand feet with a man who does not know what he is looking at.

The tongue makes the habit cheap to keep. [[skill-osketlng|Ösket]] marks on every finite verb whether the speaker saw a thing, was told it, or infers it, so a guide cannot report that the pass is open without saying how he knows. The road-pidgin the lowlanders learn is perhaps two hundred words and carries no such mark. The Ösket teach the pidgin willingly and the language not at all, and the misunderstandings that follow are held to be the lowlanders' own.

One subject is closed. The lineages will discuss the weather, the road, the fee and the dead, and every Ösket crossing of the Guides' Road walks through the opening in [[place-shunydvara|the Empty Door]], single file, hooded lowlanders and all. On that they stop talking.

## The Cord

There is no script. A hearth's route-record is a knotted cord—road, stage, load, day, and the dead—tied by its cord-keeper and read by nobody outside the blood. Cord-keeping goes to one person in a generation, and a cord whose keeper dies untimely is not read again by anyone.

A few of the Sūryadvāra hearths read and write lowland Vedyari for their contracts. None of them writes Ösket in it.

## Names

A personal name is one word, most often two syllables, taken from weather, ground, beast or condition, and it is not inherited. What is inherited is the crossing. A stranger who must be told whom he is dealing with is told the road first and the man second.

The names carry two front rounded vowels no lowland tongue has, and a Vedyari who substitutes the unrounded vowel for either is understood and identified in the same breath. The language behind them is related to nothing spoken in any lowland. A Vedyari grammarian spent four seasons at [[place-oskhelt|Öskhelt]] two centuries ago and published a careful, competent account whose conclusion was that there was nothing to connect it to.
