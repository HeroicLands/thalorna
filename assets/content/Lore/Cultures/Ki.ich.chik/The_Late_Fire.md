**The Late Fire**, _k'ayumel ch'ush ts'ekal_, is the text cut on the stair of the fire temple of [[affiliation-kayikchalix|K'ayik Ch'alish]] at [Ne' Ts'umul]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} ("Red Water"), the city on the great river of [[place-neokbaatergn|Ne'ok Ba'ate]] whose astronomical monuments rival K'ankul's. It records the [[lore-itsaniclndr#round|Living Flame]] that closed the fifty-ninth round of the Fifth Age, in the year 3069 of the age: the one Living Flame in any city's record at which the sun did not come up for the mirrors at dawn. The builder-priests of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|P'iqal Kul'qat]] cut it on the risers of the stair, one block of glyphs to a riser, to be read from the bottom step to the top, and dedicated it eighty-six days after the fire was drawn.

A stair text, like everything written in [[skill-chlxplmscrpt|Ch'alish P'ilam]], records what happened and what was meant, and quotes nobody. Every person in it has a full name, because only the full name is ever cut, and a title after it. The rendering below gives each riser's glyph-blocks in order, as plain sentences; where a riser has broken, the break is marked. A scribe of the temple reads the stair aloud to pilgrims on the day of each Living Flame, and to anyone else for a fee, and pilgrims touch the seventh riser, which is worn hollow.

The households of the city's river quarter tell a story the stair does not, and it follows the text.

## The Stair

**The first riser.** Thirteen Flame, the Door, the last of the five doors, in the year 3068 of the Fifth Age. The fifty-ninth round was complete. The fire-priests put out the fires of Ne' Ts'umul. They put out the hearths. They put out the lamps. They put out the fire that does not go out, on the pyramid of the sun. The city kept silence.

**The second riser.** The city kept silence through the day. The city kept silence through the night. Nobody ate. Nobody was born in the record of that day. [The second half of the riser is broken.]

**The third riser.** One Water, the first of the new month, in the year 3069. The day of the drawing. [Ts'ultel K'inish Ne'ok]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an of Ne' Ts'umul, went up the pyramid of the sun in the dark. [K'ayul Ish'ilan Tsul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Master of the Living Flame, went up beside him, not behind him. They carried the mirrors of obsidian. They carried the jade. They faced the place of the sun's coming.

**The fourth riser.** The cloud lay on the east. The sun came up behind the cloud. The sun did not come to the mirrors. The tinder did not take. The city stood in the streets and on the roofs, and saw no fire on the pyramid.

**The fifth riser.** The Master of the Living Flame held the mirror toward the cloud. He held it through the morning. He did not sit. He did not drink. The cloud lay.

**The sixth riser.** The sun stood at its height behind the cloud. [Ch'akun Ts'umaq Q'emul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Reckoner of Ages, keeper of the serpent's count, declared the fifth sun ended. The Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an refused the declaration. He set its hearing for the sun's going down, and not before. The Master of the Living Flame held the mirror.

**The seventh riser.** The sun went down from its height. The cloud opened in the west of the sky. The sun came to the mirror. The tinder took. The fire was drawn, in the afternoon of One Water, the first of the new month, in the year 3069. The Kindlers ran. The fire came to every quarter. The fire came to every house. The fire came to the hearth of the Ts'enmah. The sixtieth round began.

**The eighth riser.** The Master of the Living Flame gave his eyes. He looked into the mirror through the morning and the height of the day and the afternoon. He saw the fire take. He did not see again.

**The ninth riser.** [Shak'nal Ts'ultel Ish'bal Q'emul]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Ts'enmah of Ne' Ts'umul, gave the Master of the Living Flame a house at the foot of this temple, and its maize, and its servants, for his life. The Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an knotted his full name in the cords of the temple. His name is K'ayul Ish'ilan Tsul. It is cut here.

**The tenth riser.** The hearing of the declaration of the Reckoner of Ages was not held. [The rest of the riser is cut away and smoothed.]

**The eleventh riser.** Four twenties and six days after the drawing of the fire, on Nine Crocodile, the seventh of the seed month, in the year 3069, this stair was dedicated. [P'ashel Ha'nal Ishtem]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Master of the Dedication, declared it finished. The builders of the goddess cut it. The fire-priests carried the fire up it. The Master of the Living Flame climbed it, with a hand on each side.

**The twelfth riser.** The fire of the pyramid of the sun of Ne' Ts'umul has not gone out from that day.

## What the River Quarter Tells

The households along the river below the city tell of a woman of the quarter, [Ha'ik Ts'alu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a fisherman's widow, who kept an ember through the darkness. On Thirteen Flame, the day the fire-priests came round with their wet blankets, she gave them her hearth to put out and let them see it go. She had already taken two coals from it before they came, and banked them in ash in a covered pot, and set the pot under the floor of her house, where the floor is planks over the riverbank and nobody looks. That was against the law of the Living Flame, which allows one darkness and no exception, and she knew it.

In the quarter's telling she sat on her floor over the pot through the day and the night and said nothing to anybody, like everybody else in the city. On the morning of One Water she stood in the street with her neighbours and saw the cloud, and saw no fire on the pyramid. When word came down the streets at midday that the Reckoner of Ages had declared the age ended, she took up the pot and walked to the foot of the pyramid of the sun with it under her shawl.

What she meant to do with it the quarter does not agree on. Some households say she meant to give it to the priests, so that the city would not have to end for want of a fire. Some say she meant to light her own hearth from it and see the end with a fire in her house, whatever the priests did. The guards at the foot of the stair turned her back, because no one goes up on the day of the drawing. She sat down on the bottom step, with the pot on her knees, and she was sitting there in the afternoon when the cloud opened and the fire took. Then she went home, and in the evening she threw the pot in the river.

The river quarter says that if the sun had not come before it set, the priests would have had no other fire in the whole city to take up but the one in that pot, and that they would have taken it. The priesthood of Ne' Ts'umul says that the city's darkness was complete, as the first riser states, that no woman kept any ember, and that a fire kept back through the Living Flame would have been no fire of the sun's and of no use to anyone. Both are said in the city, the second in the temple and the first in the quarter, and on the day of every Living Flame since, someone from the river quarter has sat on the bottom step of the pyramid of the sun through the darkness with an empty pot on their knees. The priests let them.

The tenth riser has been read many ways. The death-walkers of the city say the hearing was not held because the sun answered it. The serpent-keepers say the riser once named the Reckoner's sentence for a declaration made too soon, and that a later Reckoner had it removed. The fire-priests say the cut was made the day it was dedicated, by the Master of the Dedication himself, because the builders do not cut what did not happen. The stair does not say.
