**The Annal of Jenshou** is the year record (_nye chou_) of [Jenshou]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, "Nine Waters", a district town on the central rivers where a great river breaks into nine channels across its own silt before it joins again below the town. The annal covers the reign of [the Houweng Emperor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an emperor of a dynasty long since passed to another house, and it was kept by the district's [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] recorder, known in the schools, as in the district register, as [Second Daughter of the Archivist of the Kwo Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, near Jenshou. The Tanchei schools set its first nine years to every student in the first year of study, as the model of the exact word, and the text below is those nine years, the recorder's note written when the reign ended, and the gloss the schools teach with them.

An annal is written in fixed verbs, and each verb carries a verdict. A flood that tops a sound bank _comes_, and is heaven's allotment; a bank that fails _opens_, and a bank fails only where it was not kept. A petition to an office is _received_ when it is logged and _answered_ when it is acted on. A death by the knife is _Rwising_ when a court orders it and _Tseising_ when a person chooses it, and the ledger records the two differently. A gift is _entered to his credit_ only when the recorder holds it was given and not paid. The rendering keeps each verb the same wherever it stands, so that each verdict stays where the recorder put it. Like every annal in the empire, this one writes no fourth year: four belongs to no domain, and an official record calls it "the year after the third". The annal names the months as the calendar does: Jwiyou the Month of Festivals, Mouyou of Wood, Hoyou of Fire, Houyou of Balance, Touyou of Earth, Jingyou of Metal, Shouyou of Water, Wengyou of Winds, Youjwi of the Moon, Chyauyou of Winter, Zhenyou of Snow and Chenyou of Frost.

## Year 1 of the Houweng Emperor

The Son of Heaven received the Mandate in Jwiyou. The district entered it at the Festival of Harmonious Lei. Eleven thousand two hundred households were counted at the reading.

The Warlord of the province confirmed the magistrate of Jenshou in his post: [Second Son of the Commander of the Hou Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [[lore-wohou|Wohou]], near the provincial seat.

In Mouyou a white heron nested on the roof of the magistrate's hall. The Tanchei read it as Water at rest. It is entered.

The dikes of the nine channels were mended in Touyou by the district corvée, one thousand and eighty men for thirty days. The [Tablet Bank]{.name data-type="place/site"} above the town, which carries the boundary-stones of the three upper villages, was raised by half a span.

The harvest was nine-tenths of a full measure. The granaries were sound at the summer count.

At the testing in the tenth year of age, two children of the district were found to have the gift: a daughter of the ferryman's house at the third channel and a son of a potter of the lower town. They were entered as [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]] and taken to the compound of the Warlord's house in the same season.

At the Festival of the Dead the district burned spirit money to the measure of the year before. No household was entered in arrears.

## Year 2 of the Houweng Emperor

The dikes were mended in Touyou by the district corvée, one thousand and forty men for thirty days.

In Hoyou a fire in the lower town took forty-one houses. The cause was a brazier left lit at the shrine of a dyer's house. The dyer was fined. His household's tablets were saved and carried out first, before the cloth.

A Shwonong of the river trade was judged for weights that ran light, and his goods were confiscated to the district stores. It is entered.

The harvest was a full measure.

In Youjwi a red light was seen over the river at the ninth channel on three nights. The Tanchei read it as the Bureau of Night Counsel at work and nothing more. It is entered.

The heron raised two young on the roof of the magistrate's hall.

## Year 3 of the Houweng Emperor

In Jwiyou the ground shook for the time it takes to say the five elements. The bell of the district hall rang without a hand. One house fell in the upper town and nobody was in it.

After the shaking one boundary-stone on the Tablet Bank was found leaning toward the river. It was set upright before witnesses and its seal renewed.

The dikes were mended in Touyou by the district corvée, one thousand one hundred men for thirty days.

The harvest was a full measure and a tenth.

The Warlord of the province began an ancestral hall at the provincial seat, upriver, to stand nine terraces high, and filed its plan with the Bureau of Creation and the Bureau of Records. It is entered.

## The year after the third of the Houweng Emperor

In Touyou the corvée of Jenshou was called upriver to the Warlord's ancestral hall, by the Warlord's seal, nine hundred men for ninety days. The dikes of Jenshou were mended by the one hundred and eighty men who remained, for thirty days.

The magistrate of Jenshou petitioned the Warlord that the corvée be returned to the dikes before the rains. The petition was received.

The harvest was nine-tenths of a full measure.

In Jingyou the court of the magistrate heard sixty-two cases, the most in any year of this annal. Thirty-eight were disputes over boundary-stones between terraces.

The heron did not return to the roof of the magistrate's hall in Mouyou. It is entered.

## Year 5 of the Houweng Emperor

In Touyou the corvée of Jenshou was called upriver to the Warlord's ancestral hall, nine hundred and sixty men for ninety days. The dikes of Jenshou were not mended.

The magistrate of Jenshou petitioned the Warlord that the corvée be returned. The petition was received.

The magistrate burned a petition at the river shrine of the third channel, addressed to the Lord of Water, that the river keep its bed. It was correctly addressed.

The households of the three upper villages petitioned the Minister of Storms that the rain of Shouyou be light. It was correctly addressed.

In Shouyou carp were found in the rice of the upper terraces after a night of rain, though no channel reached them. The Tanchei could not agree on a reading. It is entered.

The harvest was a full measure.

## Year 6 of the Houweng Emperor

In Mouyou the Warlord's ancestral hall was dedicated at the provincial seat, nine terraces high. Nine bells were hung in it. Spirit money was burned for the Warlord's ancestors to the measure of the whole district of Jenshou for nine years. It is entered.

In Touyou the corvée of Jenshou was called upriver to finish the stair of the ancestral hall, seven hundred men for sixty days. The dikes of Jenshou were not mended.

The magistrate of Jenshou went upriver himself in Touyou and petitioned the Warlord in his hall. He was received.

The magistrate set the men who remained in the district, three hundred and ten, to the Tablet Bank for twenty days. They raised it at the sixth boundary-stone, where it was lowest.

The harvest was nine-tenths of a full measure.

At the Festival of the Dead the Leigen of the upper villages reported that the ancestors of the three villages would give no instruction for the coming year. It is entered.

## Year 7 of the Houweng Emperor

In Shouyou it rained for nineteen days.

On the twentieth day the river came, and the Tablet Bank opened at the stone after the third.

The three upper villages were under the water by evening, and the upper town by midnight. The lower town stood on its mound and was not reached. The water lay on the terraces for eleven days.

Of the persons of the three upper villages and the upper town, two thousand six hundred and ten were not found at the count in Wengyou. Of these, one thousand nine hundred and forty were Nouwen, five hundred and twenty Tsaihwo, one hundred and three Shwonong, and forty-seven Nwitsou of the households that held them.

One hundred and forty lines of the district were found with no heir living. Their shrines were under the water. Their tablets that were recovered, four hundred and six, were carried to the district hall.

The harvest of the upper terraces was nothing. The harvest of the lower terraces was half a measure. The district granaries were opened in Wengyou and were empty by Chenyou.

In Chyauyou the Warlord's court judged the loss of the Tablet Bank. The magistrate of Jenshou, Second Son of the Commander of the Hou Clan, answered for the dike of his district, and was given Rwising. His second was the commander of the district guard. His last record was burned at the district hall. His tablet was set in the hall of his clan at the provincial seat.

At the Festival of the Dead the district burned spirit money to one-third of the measure of the year before. Nine hundred and twelve households were entered in arrears.

## Year 8 of the Houweng Emperor

The Warlord of the province sent nine thousand measures of grain from the provincial granaries to Jenshou in Jwiyou. It is entered to his credit.

A new magistrate came from the provincial seat in Jwiyou: [Fourth Son of the Treasurer of the Zhowou Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Wohou. The Warlord confirmed him.

The four hundred and six tablets of the lines with no heir are fed at the district hall from the district stores, at every death-day and at the Festival of the Dead. The cost is entered to the district.

The breach of the Tablet Bank was closed in Touyou by the corvée of Jenshou and of the two neighbouring districts, two thousand men for sixty days.

The upper terraces were drained in Shouyou. Their boundary-stones were set again by the Tanchei from the district register, which was kept in the lower town and was not reached. Not one terrace was lost to the register.

## Year 9 of the Houweng Emperor

The dikes of Jenshou were mended in Touyou by the district corvée, one thousand one hundred and sixty men for thirty days. The corvée did not go upriver.

The Tablet Bank was raised by one span along its whole length. A new boundary-stone was set at the stone after the third, and its seal was cut in the presence of the district.

The upper terraces were planted again in Mouyou. Their harvest was half a measure, and the harvest of the lower terraces a full measure.

At the Festival of the Dead the district burned spirit money to two-thirds of the measure of the year before the flood. Four hundred and three households were entered in arrears.

In Mouyou a white heron was seen on the Tablet Bank. It did not nest. It is entered.

## The Recorder's Note

_The annal of Jenshou continues to the seventeenth year of the reign, when the Houweng Emperor died and the count began again. At the end of a reign the recorder of a district may set a note after the last year, in her own words, and this is the note the recorder of Jenshou set._

The recorder of Jenshou says:

I have kept the annal of this district for the seventeen years of the Houweng Emperor, and my father kept it for the reign before, and his father before him. The house of the Kwo Clan has written the year of Jenshou for nine reigns. Not one year is missing.

In the seventh year an officer of the Warlord's court came down the river to the district hall, while the water was still on the terraces, and asked to see the entry for the flood before it was sealed. He asked that the river be written as having come, since a flood is heaven's allotment to a district and it is not for a district to argue with its allotment. I showed him the entry. The river is written as having come. He sealed it with the court's seal and mine.

The entries before the seventh year were sealed in their own years. They are as they were.

A year is read alone by a reader who reads one year. The annal is kept in order, nine reigns of it, and it will be read in order by whoever reads it in order. I have written nothing in it that did not happen, and I have written each thing in the word the annals give it.

The magistrate of Jenshou petitioned the Warlord three times, and three times he was received. His tablet stands in the hall of his clan. I have written what he was.

The four hundred and six are fed at the district hall. The district will feed them for as long as the district stands, because nobody else is left to.

## The Masters' Gloss

_The schools teach the nine years with a gloss, and the gloss has two halves, because the masters have never agreed._

**The first reading** is the one the Bureau of Records gives. It holds that the recorder of Jenshou kept the register exactly as heaven keeps its own: she wrote every year in its year, wrote each thing in the verb the annals assign it, and wrote nothing false. The river came, because the rain of nineteen days was more than any bank was raised for. The bank opened, because it did. The corvée went upriver, because it was called by a seal that had the right to call it. The magistrate answered for the dike of his district, because a magistrate does. Read in order, the years show the reader what they show, and the reader is responsible for what he reads. This reading sets the annal before students to teach them that the register is the truth, and that an exact clerk needs no other weapon.

**The second reading** is the one the masters give when the doors of the school are shut. It holds that _came_ is the false word in the annal, written because it was ordered, and that the recorder knew it. She wrote _opened_ beside it, and her note says nothing about the verb except that the officer asked for it. It holds that a register which can tell the truth only to a reader who assembles it out of five years, and only after the magistrate is dead, has stopped describing the world, and that this is what the recorder of Jenshou wrote down, the only way she could. This reading sets the annal before students to teach them that the register is not the truth, and that an exact clerk is all that stands between it and a lie.

Both readings note what the annal does not. The Warlord's ancestral hall is entered to nobody's credit. The recorder of Jenshou was of the Tanchei, and a Tanchei may be granted a name for outstanding service, and she was not; the schools know her by her description, near Jenshou, as her own annal does. Her description has never been struck out, and nobody has ever proposed adding to it.
