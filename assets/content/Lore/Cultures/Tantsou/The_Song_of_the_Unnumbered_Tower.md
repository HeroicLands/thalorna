**The Song of the Unnumbered Tower** is the song of the Wonong on the northern marches, where a wall of tamped earth and a line of watchtowers face the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm]] country and the garrison colonies farm behind them. The towers of a stretch of wall are numbered from its western end, and the number is cut over each door with the command's seal. The tower between the third and the fifth carries no number and no seal, because four belongs to no domain and no official building is marked with it, and the register calls it "the tower after the third". The soldiers call it the unnumbered tower, and it is the tower of this song wherever along the marches the song is sung.

The singers are the common soldiers of the [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]], the Wonong, and the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]] of the garrison colonies who stand the wall beside them, and none of them carries a name: each is entered in the garrison roll by his description, as he is at home. The song runs through the twelve months of a year, one verse to a month, each verse opening on what the month brings to the wall and closing on the same question and an answer that changes one word. It is sung in the towers to the beat of a spear-butt on the floor, in the garrison colonies at the planting, and in the Tsaihuo households of the river towns whose sons have gone north, where women sing it at the loom with the men's descriptions changed to their own sons'. The descriptions below are the ones the colonies under the Fifth Tower sing. The Empire counts a Grukar raid a nuisance, and the song does not argue the point; it says what one cost.

: The Song of the Unnumbered Tower {#unnumbered-tower}

::: {.verse form=song lang=en}
In the Month of Festivals the ice on the ditch is grey./
At home they read the Sorting with the hall doors open to the street,/
and every soul who stands to hear it is entered in the count./
We stand on the wall. Nobody reads to us./
Between the Third Tower and the Fifth there is a tower,/
and over its door there is no number and no seal./
Nine of us hold it, as the throne counts nine./
Fifth Son of the Cooper of the Jing Clan, near Jenshou./
Third Son of the Potter of the Ho Clan, near Tengmou./
A freed man of a salt-house, sent north to the colony, entered with the mark of what he was./
And five more, and me./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The ice counts us, the ice counts us right.

In the Month of Wood the geese come back over the wall,/
up out of the south where our mothers are,/
and over us, and on into the north,/
into the country where no clerk has ever written a page./
We plant the millet in the garrison plot behind the tower./
The freed man knows millet. He shows the Potter's son, who knows pots./
The geese do not come down. They do not need a tablet to cross./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The geese count us, the geese count us right.

In the Month of Fire they burn the old paper at home,/
the lapsed petitions, the debts that are paid, the year that is closed./
We have no shrine here and no brazier but the signal fire./
The Cooper's son burns his letters from last year in it/
and says that is what the Month of Fire is for./
The smith comes up from the colony and mends our spears./
He counts them on the table. Nine spears. Nine hands./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The smith counts us, the smith counts us right.

In the Month of Balance the sun stands over the wall and does not move./
At home the households settle their debts and end their quarrels./
We settle ours. The Potter's son owes me two strings at dice/
and pays me in tea, and I enter it to his credit./
The freed man owes nobody. He says that is the one thing/
a salt-house teaches a man, and he says it in the shade./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The sun counts us, the sun counts us right.

In the Month of Earth the wall is mended all along the march./
We carry the earth up in baskets and beat it in the frames,/
course on course, until the frames come off and the wall stands./
The dust is in our teeth. The dust is in the millet./
It is dust from the south, the carters say, from the rivers,/
from the terraces where our fathers are mending dikes./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The dust counts us, the dust counts us right.

In the Month of Metal the captain comes along the wall,/
the month for inspections, when every line is drawn hard./
He comes to the Third Tower and reads its roll./
He comes to our door with no number on it and stops,/
and looks up at the place where the number would be,/
and writes in his book: _tower after the third: nine, in order_./
He does not come in. He goes on to the Fifth./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The captain counts us, the captain counts us right.

In the Month of Water the carts come up the road from the south,/
millet and arrows, salt and lamp-oil, and the letters./
A letter for the Cooper's son, which the carter reads to him:/
the rice is set before your grandfather, his mother says,/
before anyone in the house has eaten, every dawn./
Your grandfather argues for you. Stay in your place./
The Cooper's son has the carter read it to him twice./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The carts count us, the carts count us right.

In the Month of Winds the wind turns, and it comes from the north,/
over the ditch, over the wall, through the slots of the tower,/
and it brings their smell before it brings anything else./
We light the watch-fire every night. We sleep in our harness./
The signal drum at the Third Tower beats for nothing, twice,/
and the drum at the Fifth answers it, and nothing comes./
The freed man says the wind is a clerk too, and this is its notice./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The wind counts us, the wind counts us right.

In the Month of the Moon the lanterns are lit at home along the terraces,/
to hold the line where the nights wear it thin./
On the wall we hang one lantern over every door./
We hang ours over the door with no number./
They came over the ditch in the dark of the third night,/
tusked and stinking, more of them than the slots would hold,/
and the war-cry first, and then the ladders,/
and the drum at the Third Tower, and the drum at the Fifth./
We held the door. We held the stair. We held the top of the tower./
In the morning they were gone back over the ditch, as they go,/
and the lantern was still burning over the door./
The Cooper's son was on the stair./
The Potter's son was at the top, at the slot where the ladder was./
The freed man was at the door, and the door held./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The moon counts us, the moon counts us right./
The moon counts six.

In the Month of Winter the report goes south along the road./
The Fifth Tower's clerk writes it for both of us, since we have none./
_A raid of no account at the tower after the third._/
_The tower held. Three lost._/
No account, it says. Three is an account./
Three is the number of beginnings, the Tanchei say./
Three is a count, written down. Three is what we buried./
The crows come down on the ditch and walk about in it./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The crows count us, the crows count us right.

In the Month of Snow they keep the Festival of the Dead at home./
Our mothers burn the spirit money in heaps, and read the accounts,/
and the Leigen stands beside them and answers for the dead./
Three mothers in three towns set out no bowl./
There is no tablet on the shelf to set it before./
A tablet is cut from an entry, and the entry says _three lost_,/
and three lost is not a man, and a carver cannot cut it,/
and the Leigen cannot find a post for _three_./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
Our mothers count us, our mothers count us right./
They count the bowls they do not set.

In the Month of Frost every filing must be closed before the year ends,/
and a clerk came up the road from the garrison command,/
an old Tanchei with his brush in a case and his fingers blue,/
because the year was ending and his book had a line in it/
that said _three_, and a line that says _three_ is not closed./
He came in at the door with no number on it. He sat at our table./
He did not ask what they were called. He knew they were not called./
He asked us who they were, and we told him, and he wrote it down./
_Fifth Son of the Cooper of the Jing Clan, near Jenshou,_/
_Wonong, at the tower after the third, on the stair,_/
_in the Month of the Moon, holding._/
_Third Son of the Potter of the Ho Clan, near Tengmou,_/
_Wonong, at the tower after the third, at the top,_/
_in the Month of the Moon, holding._/
_A freed man of the salt-house of the Shou Clan, near the salt-pans,_/
_Nouwen of the garrison colony, entered with the mark of what he was,_/
_at the tower after the third, at the door,_/
_in the Month of the Moon, holding._/
He read it back to us, every word. He sealed it./
It went south on the last cart of the year./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The clerk counts us, the clerk counts us right.

In the Month of Festivals the ice on the ditch is grey./
In three towns three tablets stand on three shelves,/
and the rice is set before them before anyone in the house has eaten./
Three new men came up the road in the spring with the carts./
Nine of us hold the tower with no number, as the throne counts nine./
Nobody reads to us. We are entered in the count./
Who counts us at the tower with no number?/
The clerk counts us, the clerk counts us right.
