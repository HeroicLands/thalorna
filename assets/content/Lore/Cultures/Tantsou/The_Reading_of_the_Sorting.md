**The Reading of the Sorting** is the recitation a [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] reads aloud in the audit hall of every provincial seat and district town on the first day of Jwiyou, at the [Festival of Harmonious Lei]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. In the capital the Emperor performs the year's audit of the empire's Lei and opens the celestial filing; in the provinces the Warlord's seal stands on the table for the Emperor's, and the reading is what the hall hears before the year's ledger is opened. Everyone who stands in the hall for it is entered in the district's reckoning of the festival, so the halls are full and the doors stand open to the street.

The reading is the public copy of the cosmological treatises, made for the hall. The treatises themselves may be held and read by the Tanchei alone, and the reading says so where it leaves them out. Each district keeps its own copy, and the copies differ in their catalogs: a river province adds a couplet for the dikes, a march province one for the towers, and a town under the mountains leaves out the sea. The text below is the reading of a district on the central rivers. Every year the reader says that year's count from [[lore-greatcnvrg|the Great Convergence]] and that year's place in the Generation, and the rendering gives the count and the place of the present year. The hall answers each part with the same three words, and in the hall they are said together, flat, the way a clerk says them when he has checked a figure.

: The Reading of the Sorting {#reading-sorting}

::: {.verse form=recitation lang=en}
[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Hear the reading, in the hall of the audit,/
on the first day of Jwiyou, with the brazier lit./
The seal is on the table. The old ledger is closed and tied./
The new one lies beside it, and no stroke is on it./
Before it is opened, the reading is read,/
so that nobody in this hall begins the year/
without knowing what the year is for.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Before the first distinction, nothing was one thing,/
and nothing was another./
Water was not wet, for nothing was dry to tell it by./
Fire was not hot, for nothing was cold./
There was no above and no below, no inside and no out,/
no before and no after, no near and no far./
It was not dark. Dark is a thing, and there were no things./
It was not empty. Empty is a bowl with nothing in it,/
and there was no bowl./
That is the Hwenlei, the Unclassified./
It is not a place you could have walked to./
It is what you would be, if nobody had sorted you.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Then the stroke./
No hand made it, and no brush, and no wish./
The stroke itself, from the top to the bottom, once:/
this side, and that side./
That is Tanzho, the First Classifier./
He is not petitioned. He is not fed./
No paper goes up to him and no answer comes down./
His room is empty but for one mark on the far wall,/
and the Son of Heaven alone goes in, and nobody asks him what he does there./
Where the stroke fell, above was above and below was below./
That was the first entry./
Every entry since is a copy of it:/
the clerk's stroke on the return, the boundary-stone in the field,/
the line of the terrace wall, the seal on the tablet at your belt.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
After above and below, the second distinction:/
the bright and the dark, Leng and Ha, and the line between them./
Day was given the bright, and night the dark,/
and neither was given the whole of the year./
After the bright and the dark, the third distinction:/
the five that every made thing is made of./
Hear the five.

Air was entered: what moves, and is not seen./
It was given the wind for a road and the breath for a house./
Fire was entered: what turns one thing into another./
It was given the hearth and the forge, and the road up out of the brazier./
Earth was entered: what stays where it is put./
It was given the terrace, the dike and the grave./
Metal was entered: what cuts one thing from the next./
It was given the blade, the boundary and the court in the Month of Metal./
Water was entered: what carries a thing from here to there./
It was given the river, the rain and the barges on the river./
Five strokes. Five entries. The world can be built.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
After the five, the kinds./
Rice was entered as rice, and it comes up rice./
Bamboo was entered as bamboo, and the carp as a fish of Water,/
the horse as Earth, the hawk as Air,/
and the serpent that bites as Water and Metal both./
Every kind was given a pattern, and the pattern breeds true,/
and the Bureau of Creation keeps the pattern on its shelf/
and needs to do no more than dust it./
A few commissions are still open on that desk./
They are not read in this hall.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
After the kinds, the persons:/
the ones who were given a ledger,/
who can move a thing out of its place, and so can keep it in place,/
who can be credited, and can owe./
To the persons the Sorting gave eight places, and no ninth./
Hear the eight.

The Sons of the Dragon, to hold the post and the provinces./
The Wandwo, to carry the great petitions, and to be kept as a seal is kept./
The Wohou, to enforce, as the Minister of Storms enforces./
The Tanchei, to read and to keep, as the Jade Sovereign keeps./
The Tsaihwo, to make the thing, and to stand on the wall./
The Nouwen, to feed the empire out of the terraces./
The Shwonong, to carry what others have made, and to be counted carrying it./
The Nwitsou, at the floor, so that the floor is known./
Eight places. Every person stands in one./
The stranger in the street stands in none,/
and he is placed low, and given tea, until a bureau says otherwise.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Then the work wanted workers, and the workers were appointed./
Nobody was born to it. Every one of them was posted./
The Jade Sovereign was given the Master Registry,/
every classified thing in one index,/
and she has not looked up from it since./
The Minister of Storms was given the rain to allot by districts,/
the lightning to strike where it is ordered,/
and the thunder, which is the sound of a ruling./
The Minister of the Moon was given the night,/
the hours when a line grows thin,/
and a lantern in one hand and the sealed dispatches in the other./
The Five Lords were given the five, one each,/
and the bureaus were staffed beneath them, office by office,/
the Court of the Dead and the Bureau of Night Counsel,/
the Bureau of Transformation that takes your paper up in smoke,/
the Bureau of Passage that keeps the roads, and the rest./
If you write to the wrong one,/
the clerk will cross out the address and write the right one,/
and send it on, and it will be late./
That is not cruelty. That is the Sorting.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
And the earth wanted an administrator./
Six thousand nine hundred and fifty-eight years ago,/
every body in heaven stood in its place at once,/
every element sorted, every bureau staffed, every boundary drawn,/
and the business of heaven was opened./
That was the Great Convergence, and every bureau files against it./
On that day a mortal line was classified fit for the post,/
and the first Son of Heaven was appointed./
His filings went up into the keeping of heaven,/
for no office on the earth was staffed yet to hold a copy./
Do not ask the archive for them. They are in custody./
An entry in custody is an entry./
The appointment has been reviewed, and reviewed,/
and passed from house to house when a house was found unfit,/
and it has not once been left vacant./
A line is not appointed because it is loved./
It is appointed because the work must be done.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Now hear the hard part. No hall is spared it./
The Sorting is not finished./
The stroke divided, and the Unclassified was pushed to the edges,/
and the edges were never closed./
It is there at the margin of the map, where no surveyor has walked,/
in the crack between two bureaus where neither will sign,/
in the dream of the one who cannot tell his sleeping from his waking,/
in the four of anything, the number that belongs to no domain./
It does not come in like an army./
It comes in like damp into a wall.

Five stand at the edges who refused the stroke./
The treatises give their names and their titles./
This hall does not./
A name is a small entry,/
and the scholars have not ruled, in all the years they have argued it,/
whether to name them binds them or feeds them./
Until they rule, the hall says: five,/
and leaves the five unwritten.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Hear how it ends, if it ends./
It does not burn. Fire is a thing, and the end is not a thing./
It does not fall. Falling needs a below./
It goes back./
First the boundary-stones lean, and nobody sets them straight./
Then a man forgets which clan he is of, and is not ashamed./
Then the Nouwen plants in the wrong month, and the rice comes up anyway, and nobody remarks it./
Then the river forgets that it runs to the sea,/
and the night forgets to hand the morning its dispatches,/
and the five forget each other's edges./
Then the ledger is not burned, and it is not lost./
It goes blank./
The ink goes back into the water it was ground in,/
and the water is not wet, for nothing is dry to tell it by.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
Every twenty-seven years the work of heaven is filed and closed:/
nine years ascending, when new filings are opened,/
nine years in balance, when the bureaus run without arrears,/
nine years descending, when the old filings lapse/
and the clerks of heaven are tired, and the lines are thin./
This is the twentieth year of the two hundred and fifty-eighth Generation,/
the year of the Golden Dragon, in the descending nine./
The reader does not hide it. It is on the calendar on the wall./
Seven years are left of this Generation./
Then the first year comes again, and the filing is opened again./
Two hundred and fifty-eight times it has been opened./
It has not once failed to open./
That is not a promise. Heaven makes no promises./
Heaven makes appointments, and the appointed come to work.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
The year was sorted as the world was sorted,/
into twelve offices, and no month does another's work./
Hear the twelve.

The Month of Festivals was given the opening:/
the old accounts settled, the new ledger opened, and this reading./
The Month of Wood was given the planting,/
and the seed entered with the bureau that keeps its pattern./
The Month of Fire was given the spent paper to burn,/
the denied petition and the lapsed contract, and the forge to bless./
The Month of Balance was given the middle of the year,/
when heaven reviews its bureaus and no office may hold more than its share./
The Month of Earth was given the mending:/
the road, the wall, the granary and the dike./
The Month of Metal was given the line drawn hard:/
the court, the inspection and the caste roll./
The Month of Water was given what moves:/
the caravan, the barge, the rain and the traveller's petition./
The Month of Winds was given the dispatches,/
heaven's directives coming down, and the omens read./
The Month of the Moon was given the long night,/
the lanterns on the terraces, and the line kept where it thins./
The Month of Winter was given the drawing in,/
the stores counted and the offerings made ready./
The Month of Snow was given the dead,/
the account read before the shrine and the spirit money burned./
The Month of Frost was given the closing:/
every filing finished or withdrawn, and the house made clean./
Twelve months, and no thirteenth./
A month that does another month's work/
has filed in the wrong office.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
So hear what the year is for./
The Sorting is not finished, and so it is ours to finish,/
a stroke at a time, and none of us will see the end of it./
Sweep the shrine, and set the rice before the tablets before you eat./
Do Leichei in the yard at first light, and set the body in its order./
Mend the dike in the Month of Earth, though the river is low and the sun is on your back./
Write to the right bureau./
Keep the death-days. Keep the caste your tablet gives you./
Pay what you owe, in the year you owe it, and do not hide a debt,/
for the account stands whether or not you admit it./
Each of these is a stroke, from the top to the bottom, once:/
this side, and that side./
Each of these is the first entry, copied out again./
Do them badly, and the damp comes in./
Do them well, and the wall stands one more year,/
and that is all a wall has ever done.

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.

[The reader:]{.speaker}/
The reading is read./
The old ledger goes to the brazier, and up./
The new one is opened, here, before you,/
and the first stroke on it is the Dragon's stroke:/
this side, and that side.

[The reader dips the brush, draws one stroke down the first leaf of the new ledger, top to bottom, and lifts the brush clear.]{.direction}

[The hall:]{.speaker}/
It is entered.
