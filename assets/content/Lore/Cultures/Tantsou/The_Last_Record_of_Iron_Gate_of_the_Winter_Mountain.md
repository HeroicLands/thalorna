**The Last Record of Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain** is a _Kuo Chou_, the last record a person of rank writes before [[lore-tseising|Tseising]]. A last record is a testament and a filing at once: it is burned after the rite, so that the smoke carries the dead man's final account to the [[affiliation-wansouzhuo|Ministry of Final Account]], and copies of it go where the writer directs. Its author was [Second Son of the Gate Warden of the Gwo Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, near the provincial seat, a [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] officer granted the name [Chiao Chai Tui Sen]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain", and in the year of the record the administrator of the district of [[lore-annaljenshou|Jenshou]], holding its revenue, its courts and its corvée for the Warlord of the province.

His Warlord, of the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], had ordered that [Rinnou]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, "Red Field", a terrace village of the district, be entered whole as [[lore-noutsou|Nuitsou]], because its headman had been convicted of harboring an unbound sorcerer, and that the tablets on its shrines be broken. Iron Gate wrote the record and took his own life in the protest form, which lays a permanent debit on the ledger of the one who provoked it and on his house after him. Rinnou was entered as Nuitsou in the spring all the same, by the officer sent to take his place. The Warlord granted the one thing the record asked of him, and the tablets of Rinnou were carried unbroken to the district hall at Jenshou, where they are fed beside the tablets the old flood left with no heir.

The copy he sent to the hall of his clan is the one the schools hold. Wohou youths copy it in their writing lessons, and the Tanchei set it beside [[lore-annaljenshou|the Annal of Jenshou]] as the model of a document that says what it means without once saying it. The rendering keeps its headings, which are the headings a last record has.

## The Heading

The last record of Second Son of the Gate Warden of the Gwo Clan, near the provincial seat, of the Wohou, granted the name Chiao Chai Tui Sen, Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain, administrator of the district of Jenshou.

Written in the Month of Metal, on the ninth day, in the third year of the reign, in the eleventh year of the Generation, in the year of the Righteous Boar.

To the Ministry of Final Account, by the smoke.

A copy to the hall of the Gwo Clan at the provincial seat.

A copy to my lord the Warlord of the province, who is owed one, and to whom this record is addressed in every part but the first.

## Who Writes

I was entered at birth as the second son of the Gate Warden of the Gwo Clan, near the provincial seat, under the Iron Tiger, and I am entered so still. My father kept the east gate of the provincial seat for thirty years and was never granted a name, and he asked for none. My elder brother keeps the gate now. I was given to arms at twelve, as a second son is.

I served nineteen years on the northern marches. In the eleventh of them I held the gate of the fort at the head of the winter pass with sixty men from the first snow to the thaw, when the fort's commander was dead and the relief could not come up, and the gate was not opened. My liege lord at that time granted me the name I sign with, before witnesses, and it was entered beside my description, which was not struck out. I have used the name for fourteen years. I have tried to use it only where the gate was the matter.

My lord gave me the district of Jenshou to administer six years ago. I have kept its revenue, its courts and its corvée. The dikes have been mended in every Month of Earth of the six. The granaries have been sound at every summer count. Its arrears at the Festival of the Dead are fewer than when I came. These are entered in the district register, and the register is correct.

My ledger, as I know it: credit for the gate; credit for the dikes; credit for the district's accounts. Debit for the sixty men at the winter pass, of whom twenty-two were not found at the thaw, and whose households I have fed at their death-days since. Debit for two judgments in my first year at Jenshou which I gave too quickly, and one of which I could not undo. Debit for whatever the Ministry finds that I have not found. I do not ask the Ministry to weigh it gently. I ask it to weigh it.

## The Order

On the first day of the Month of Metal I received this order under my lord's seal and the seal of his court. I set it down whole, so that the Ministry reads what I read.

_The headman of Rinnou, in the district of Jenshou, having been judged in this court to have harboured in his house through one winter a person of the gift not entered as Wanduo, which is to say an unclassified thing, and the village having kept his secret, the court orders: that every person of Rinnou be entered as Nuitsou and as the property of this house, from the day of this order, with every child born to them after it; that the shrines of Rinnou be cleared and the tablets on them broken, since the Nuitsou keep no line; and that the administrator of Jenshou see it done before the Month of Water._

The order is correctly written, correctly sealed and correctly addressed. The court that gave it has jurisdiction over the persons of Rinnou. The headman's crime is one of the gravest there is, and the court's judgment of it is just; I do not dispute one word of the judgment, and I have not been asked to.

## The Readings

I read the order nine times, on nine days, because nine is the count for a matter of the throne's authority, and a Warlord's order carries a part of it.

On the second day I went to the district Tanchei and asked for a reading on the question of jurisdiction. I put the question so: the persons of Rinnou are under the court of my lord, and he may enter them where he judges; the dead of Rinnou, whose tablets stand on the shrines, have been read by the Ministry of Final Account and posted, and are officials of heaven at their posts; under which article of the Teng Lou does a mortal court break the address of an official of heaven?

The district Tanchei is a careful scholar and an old man. He kept me a long while and gave me tea. He said that the question was a good one. He said that he would consult the treatises. On the fifth day he sent word that the question had no bureau, and that a question with no bureau is not one a district Tanchei may answer on his own seal. I thanked him. He was correct.

On the sixth day I rode to Rinnou. I did not tell the village why. I went into every house that has a shrine, which is every house, and I counted.

## What Is on the Shelves of Rinnou

Rinnou has forty-one households and two hundred and ninety persons, the headman's household among them.

Its shrines hold one thousand one hundred and seventy tablets.

The oldest is of the household that cut the first terrace above the Red Field, and the village Leigen says it has stood thirty-four generations. Its face is worn smooth where the dawn rice has been set before it, and the carving of the death-day can be read only with a finger.

Six hundred and twelve of the tablets are of persons who died before the headman was born. Not one of them harboured anything.

Nine of them are of men of Rinnou who were not found at the winter pass, in the year I held the gate. Their households had their tablets cut from the entry I sent south. I did not know, until I counted, that they were from Rinnou.

In every house the rice was set before the tablets before anyone had eaten. It was the morning, and I saw it.

## The Question

My lord, I do not refuse your order. I have never refused an order, and I do not have the words in me for it; I was a second son and then a soldier, and neither one is taught them.

I have only been unable to find where the second part of it is to be filed.

The first part is within your court, and if I live it will be done, by me or by the officer who comes after me. The persons of Rinnou are yours to enter.

The second part asks me to break the addresses of one thousand one hundred and seventy officials of heaven, posted by the Ministry of Final Account, who have committed no fault on any ledger I can read. If I break them, they are cut off from everything that is sent to them, and they sit at their desks in heaven with nothing coming to them, and they cannot argue for anyone, and they have done nothing. I have looked for the article of the Teng Lou that gives a mortal court jurisdiction over a posted official of heaven, and I have not found it. The district Tanchei has looked, and he says the question has no bureau. I may have missed the article. I am a soldier and not a scholar.

If there is no such article, then the order asks me to make an entry that no office can receive, and to make it in your name, and the entry will stand against your ledger in the Ministry for as long as the Ministry keeps ledgers, which is for as long as there is a world. I cannot make that entry for you. Neither can I return the order to you unexecuted, which would be to say that your court has erred, and I do not say it.

So I am making the only entry I am able to make. It is my own. It is within my jurisdiction, since a person of rank may remove himself from the sorted order on his own terms, and no court has ever disputed it. It cannot be answered, and it cannot be reversed. It is addressed to the Ministry, and it carries this question with it, and the Ministry has jurisdiction over every ledger in the empire, yours and mine. I leave the question with them.

It will cost you. I have tried to make it cost exactly what the question costs and nothing more. Anger has no bureau either, and I have filed none.

## The Dispositions

The heirloom blade of my branch of the Gwo Clan, with which I will do this, goes after the rite to my son, First Son of the Administrator of Jenshou of the Gwo Clan, who is eleven. He has been tested and he has no gift, so he is mine to give it to. Its descent is entered in the clan's archive, and his name goes beside it there.

My second is my cousin's son, Eldest Son of the Archer of the Gwo Clan, of the district guard. He did not wish to be asked. I asked him. The credit for it is his and I ask the Ministry to enter it in full.

The district's accounts are in order to the end of the Month of Water. The keys of the granary are with the district Tanchei, who is honest. The corvée roll for next year is drawn and lies on my table.

I owe the dyer of the lower town of Jenshou for the white cloth, one string and a half. It is in the box under the table.

I owe the households of the twenty-two the death-day rice for the rest of their years. My son will set it, and my clan has entered the duty to my branch.

My lord, I ask one thing of you, and it is within your jurisdiction, and you may grant it without granting anything else. Let the tablets of Rinnou be lifted from the shelves before the shelves are cleared, unbroken, by the proper rite, with the Leigen there, and carried to the district hall at Jenshou, which already feeds the tablets of the lines the old flood left with no heir, and let the district feed them. The address of a tablet moved by the rite is not broken. The persons of Rinnou will be yours. Their dead will be at their posts. Nobody will have made an entry that no office can receive.

## The Close

I have written this in my own hand. I have read it back nine times. It is correct.

::: {.verse form=kuo-chou lang=en}
I held a gate once, in the snow, on the head of the pass,/
and nothing came through it that I had not let in./
The gate is the matter. The gate was the only matter./
My lord gave me another gate, and this is the gate:/
a shelf in a terrace house, at dawn, with rice on it./
I cannot open it. I cannot hold it shut./
I stand in it./
Ministry, here is my account, and here is the question./
Weigh them. I have weighed them myself, and I am done.
