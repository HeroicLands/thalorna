**The Hearth Account of the Third Terrace** is a _ken wan_, a hearth account: the reckoning the heir of a household reads aloud before its shrine at the [Festival of the Dead]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, in Zhenyou at midwinter, while the spirit money burns. An account has three parts. The heir reads the household's credits and debits for the year to all its dead together; he reads each of the dead in turn from the eldest down, by the description on the tablet, with what that ancestor carried on the ledger and what the household owes or is owed; and he reads the living, so that the dead hear who is left. The village Leigen stands beside him and gives the answers of the dead, and the household answers the end of each entry with the same words. Most heirs of the terrace villages cannot write, and they learn the shape of an account by hearing one.

This is the account the [[lore-ancstrwrshp|Leigen]] of [Weichai]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} teach the form from. It is the account of the household of the [Third Terrace]{.name data-type="place/site"} of the [Mou Clan]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]] household whose terrace climbs the hill above the town, read in the first year after the death of the father of the house by his eldest son, [Eldest Son of the Terrace-Keeper of the Mou Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, near Weichai. His description and his father's are the same words, as they are in most terrace households, where the eldest son of a terrace-keeper keeps the terrace in his turn. The household allowed the Leigen to keep it as the model, and the Leigen of Weichai say that no account they have heard sets out a debit more plainly. Each heir who learns from it puts his own dead in its places and keeps its order, its figures and its refrain. The grandmother at its centre is [Eldest Daughter of the Thatcher of the Lei Clan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and the famine she answers for is [the Year the Rice Lay Down]{.name data-type="lore/history"}, in the reign before this one.

: The Hearth Account of the Third Terrace {#hearth-account}

::: {.verse form=ken-wan lang=en}
[The heir:]{.speaker}/
This is the account of the household of the Third Terrace,/
of the Mou Clan, near Weichai, in the Month of Snow./
The brazier is lit. The rice is set before the tablets./
It was set before them before anyone in this house had eaten./
I read it once. I do not read it twice./
Hear it, all of you who are posted,/
and argue for us where you are.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
First the year./
Credit: the rice set before you at every dawn, three hundred and sixty-one days./
Debit: four days in the Month of Water, when the rain kept us on the hill, and it was set late./
Credit: every death-day kept, nine of them, with the right food./
Credit: Leichei in the yard at first light, by every one of us who can stand./
Credit: the corvée in the Month of Earth, thirty days on the river dike, by me and by my brother./
Credit: alms at the Mid-Year, two measures of millet to the widow of the Second Terrace./
Debit: a quarrel with the Second Terrace over the boundary-stone, carried to the clan elders./
Credit: the quarrel settled at the Mid-Year, and the stone where it was./
Credit: spirit money burned tonight, eleven baskets, with paper for a coat and a pair of shoes./
That is the year. It is not a rich year. It is not in arrears.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Now each of you, from the eldest down./
First Father of the Third Terrace,/
of the Mou Clan, near Weichai, who cut the terrace./
Eleven courses of wall, out of the hillside, without a lord's man to help./
The terrace has been in the register thirty generations,/
and your stone is still the bottom course./
Your ledger is full. You argue with the bureaus for the whole clan./
We owe you the first rice, and we give it.

[The Leigen, for First Father of the Third Terrace:]{.speaker}/
The north corner leans. Set it in the Month of Earth, before the rain./
Use the grey stone from the stream, not the yellow./
The yellow is soft.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
The north corner, in the Month of Earth. The grey stone./
It is entered.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
The fathers after him, twenty-six tablets, read together,/
as the shrine has always read them./
Each one kept the shrine. Each one went to the corvée./
Each one is posted where his ledger sent him,/
and each one has his rice./
Between the eleventh and the thirteenth tablet the shelf is bare./
That place is read bare./
He did not answer when he was asked, three years running, in a bad time,/
and the household took him down, before the Leigen, in my great-grandfather's day./
Nothing is set at that place. Nothing is burned for it./
It is read so that heaven knows the household has not forgotten/
what it did.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Eldest Daughter of the Thatcher of the Lei Clan, near Weichai,/
who came up to the Third Terrace by the Leigen's bonding,/
and is the grandmother of this house./
Credit: forty-one years at the shrine, and not a dawn missed but those I read./
Credit: four children raised, and three of them lived./
Credit: the terrace kept through two floods with the men away at the corvée.

In the Year the Rice Lay Down, in the reign before this one,/
the rain came in the Month of Fire and the rice lay down in the water and did not stand up./
The district granary was opened, and was empty before the Month of Frost./
The households of the hill fed the tablets first, as the ledger says,/
and the children went to the tablets afterward, for what was left./
Debit: on the forty-first day of the hunger,/
she took the rice from before the tablets of this house/
and put it into the children./
Debit: forty days after that, the same,/
until the barley came./
Debit: rice taken from the posted, eighty-one days./
My father was one of the children./
I am here because of the debit./
The account says so. The account is meant to say so.

At her death the Ministry of Final Account read her ledger,/
and she was posted to the lower realms, to carry water, for a term:/
fifty-four years, from the day of her death./
Her son burned nine baskets a year against the term, every year after her death./
I burned nine baskets tonight, with the eleven./
Twenty-three years of the term are paid./
Thirty-one are left./
I will not pay the last of them. My son will./
He is in the room. He is seven. He has heard the figure.

[The Leigen, for Eldest Daughter of the Thatcher:]{.speaker}/
Is there rice in the house?

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
There is rice in the house./
It was set before you first.

[The Leigen, for Eldest Daughter of the Thatcher:]{.speaker}/
Is there rice in the house?

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
There is rice in the house, grandmother./
The children have eaten.

[The Leigen says nothing more for her. The heir waits the time it takes to say the five elements, and goes on.]{.direction}

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Eldest Son of the Terrace-Keeper of the Mou Clan, near Weichai,/
my father, who kept this shrine twenty-six years./
Your tablet is new. The carver set the death-day on it in the Month of Winds./
This is your first year on the shelf, and your first rice from my hand./
Credit: the shrine kept twenty-six years without arrears./
Credit: your mother's term paid down, nine baskets every year, twenty-two years./
Credit: the corvée, every year you could walk./
Credit: the terrace in the register, not one span lost to the Second Terrace or the river./
Debit: you struck a Shuonong at the Weichai market who sold you salt with sand in it./
The magistrate fined you a measure of millet, and the Shuonong was fined two./
Debit: the fine. Credit: the salt, which you were right about./
The Leigen says you are posted to the Bureau of Allocation,/
at the lowest desk, where the grain of the terrace districts is reckoned./
It is a small post. It is near the grain. You would have chosen it.

[The Leigen, for Eldest Son of the Terrace-Keeper:]{.speaker}/
Plant the north terrace in millet this year, not rice./
The water will be late. I have seen the figures./
Read me the living.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Millet on the north terrace./
It is entered.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Now the living, so that you know who is left to feed you./
My mother, Third Daughter of the Weaver of the Ro Clan, near Weichai,/
who sits at the shrine's left hand, and whose knees are bad./
Credit: the dawn rice, when I was on the dike.

My brother, Second Son of the Terrace-Keeper,/
who went to the corvée with me, and is to be bonded in the spring/
to the daughter of the Fifth Terrace, if the Leigen finds you agree./
Speak to the Fifth Terrace's dead. They are slow to answer.

My wife, who came up from the river village by the bonding,/
whose own dead are on her mother's shelf and are fed there./
My son, who is seven, and will be tested in three years./
My daughter, who is four months, and is registered under the White Ape.

My sister, Second Daughter of the Terrace-Keeper,/
was found to have the gift at the testing in her tenth year,/
and was entered as Wanduo, and taken to the compound of the Warlord's house/
in the same season./
The household does not seek her./
That is the line the law gives. It is read as the law gives it.

[The Leigen says nothing. Nobody in the room speaks.]{.direction}

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
That is the living./
That is the account.

[The heir burns the account paper, which the Leigen has written out as he spoke, in the brazier with the last basket.]{.direction}

[The heir:]{.speaker}/
Credit and debit, read once, and sent up./
The household of the Third Terrace is not in arrears./
Thirty-one years are left on the grandmother's term./
The north corner in the Month of Earth, and millet on the north terrace./
We are few. The rice is thin. The account is true./
Argue it where you are.

[The household:]{.speaker}/
We feed you. Speak for us.
:::
