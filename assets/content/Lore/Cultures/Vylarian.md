A Vylarian lives inside a memory. The empire still spans half of Ankaris on the maps, the Senate still debates, the aqueducts still run in the older quarters—and every educated Vylarian knows, without ever putting it plainly, that the best of it is behind him. This produces a people who are sophisticated, proud, formidable, and difficult to sit with for long. A Vylarian does not boast about the empire; he assumes it, which is worse.

## The Old Forms

The aristocracy clings to the forms, and the forms are elaborate: layered social hierarchy, patronage networks, precedence worked out to the step, and a Senate whose procedure is immaculate and whose authority erodes a little further every decade.

The stratification is real. The old senatorial families hold vast estates and run politics through a web of alliance, marriage and obligation; below them sit the equestrians, wealthy merchants and military officers; below those the plebeian free citizens; and below all of them a substantial population of slaves and indentured servants. Mobility exists and is limited, and the gap between the top and the bottom widens with each generation.

What a foreigner should understand is that the forms are neither decoration nor quite pretense. They are the machine by which a shrinking authority is still made to function: a magistrate whose writ nobody fears can convene a court that everybody attends, because attending is what is done, and a surprising amount of the empire runs on exactly that.

## Grandeur and Decay in the Same Street

The cities are magnificent temples and forums and aqueducts standing beside crumbling tenements and public works nobody has funded in two generations. Vylarians see both and mention only the first.

A visitor who remarks on the decay commits a real discourtesy—not because the remark is untrue, but because a Vylarian has decided what his city is and expects the courtesy of being met there. Among themselves they are considerably more caustic about it than any foreigner would dare to be.

## Steel, and What Is Kept

Vylarian craftsmanship is genuinely the best in Ankaris, and Vylarian Steel is its emblem: an alloy whose forging is a guarded secret, whose weapons and armor command extraordinary prices, and whose reputation has outlived several of the provinces that used to pay for it.

The guarding is characteristic. A Vylarian guild does more than protect a technique—it treats the technique as property of the same order as land, inherited, litigated over and defended in court. That is why Vylarian craft is excellent and why it advances slowly.

## Law

The one unambiguous gift the empire gave the world is its law, and Vylarians know it: codified, argued, written down, appealable, and applied in principle to a citizen anywhere the empire reaches.

A Vylarian's instinct in any dispute is to reach for a legal remedy, and he will do this in circumstances where every other people on the continent would reach for a knife or a kinsman. He is often disappointed. He tries anyway, and his conviction that this is the civilized reflex is the single most Vylarian thing about him.

## Faith and the Ordo

The Aurèldían pantheon is the empire's own, and the imperial cult sits on top of it: the gods honored through a state establishment with offices, revenues and precedence, much as everything else here is.

The Ordo Arcanis is likewise an imperial institution, and in Vylaria it operates at full strength—licensed, chartered, integrated into the administration, and regarded by ordinary Vylarians as simply another arm of government. Provincials find this unnerving, and Vylarians find the provincials' hedge-witches squalid.

## Manners

Formal, precise, and conscious of rank at every moment. A Vylarian establishes precedence early in an encounter and is comfortable once it is settled, and a foreigner who will not be placed makes him uneasy, not angry.

Patronage governs private life as thoroughly as law governs public life. A client attends his patron in the morning, carries his interests, and is carried in return, and the obligation is real and enforceable in practice if not in court.

Vylarians are generous hosts, tremendous snobs, and better company than their reputation, provided the question of whose empire it is has been settled to their satisfaction before dinner.

## What a Person Owes

Ask a Vylarian what a person owes, and he answers in the old order: the household gods and the ancestors first, then the family, then the patron, then the city, then the empire. He names the empire last while plainly meaning it first, because the order is the traditional one and he is not going to be the man who changes it.

Underneath all of it he holds that a person owes the law his obedience even where it is inconvenient, and especially where it is inconvenient, because a law obeyed only when it suits is not a law but a preference—and the barbarians have those already.
