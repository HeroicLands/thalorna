"Don't wait for the sun to set," a boy tells the southern clerk who has been sent up the headland to sit beside him. "It only dips. Watch the other headlands." Down the coast, one promontory after another, fires are catching, and by the time the last of them is lit the whole shore from here to the next fjord is a string of orange points under a sky that never goes dark.

Midsumar is the summer-solstice festival, the brightest night of the northern year. The sun barely sets, the gods of light are at their strongest, and every promontory along the northern coasts carries a bonfire. [[lore-bjartrdty|Bjartr]] is central to it, and the feast belongs to him.

## What Is Done

Three things make up the night, and the boy on the headland names them as a child would, in the order of what he cares about: the fire, the food and the jump.

- _The bonfires._ They are kindled on the promontories, so that every stead along the shore can see the next one burning.
- _The feast._ It is shared, and the whole community takes part. Midsumar is a festival of fellowship and joy, and the long table is as much the point of the night as the flame.
- _The leap._ The faithful leap the low flames for luck. The boy points out that the jumpers wait for the fire to burn down to them.

## The Festival of Light

[[lore-jol|Jól]] keeps a lamp lit in every doorway against the longest dark. Midsumar has no dark to keep out, and the fires answer a light that is already there. Of the four great blóts it is the one given most plainly to joy, and it is the one where Bjartr is central instead of one god among several.
