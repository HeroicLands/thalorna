A tribe's real archive walks, and when two tribes' archives disagree about a well, a death or a boundary, each brings its recitation before poets of a third tribe, who judge which is sound. **The Weighing at the Well of Two Mouths**, _sevvar re vazhmavad_, is one such judgment as the poets of the [Farumak]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} keep it, and they keep it as precedent: it is the weighing an apprentice learns first at [[place-vzhmvd|Vazhmavad]], under the palms where the benches stand, because it teaches how a false line is heard before it is proved.

The dispute is between the [Khozrun]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} and the [Padeshun]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, over a well on the southern circuit that has two mouths a spear's length apart in one rock: [the Well of Two Mouths]{.name data-type="place/site" lang=en}. Each tribe recites its line back to the one who dug the water, the Khozrun to [Tarnav]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who dug the first mouth in the days of the wandering bands, the Padeshun to [Gholbara]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who opened the second in a drought. The judgment is given by the eldest of the judging poets, [Sarnish Farumak]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, in the rhymed prose the poets use for a finding, whose clauses run in sets that close on one sound; the rendering closes each set on one word. The settlement that follows is recited by both tribes and binds them. The weighing names the Khozrun poet whose line was struck, [Nimrash Khozrun]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and the Khozrun recite that part as the Farumak do, word for word.

The weighing is older than the present trouble at Vazhmavad, and the Farumak poets give it in the order it was heard: the opening of the weighing, the two recitations, the finding, the settlement and the closing.

: The Opening {#opening}

::: {.verse form=pevrend lang=en}
We are the Farumak, and we have no well in this./
We have no herd at this water and no kinsman at this fire. We have no share in this./
We have eaten the salt of neither tribe this year. We have no debt in this./

Say it where the god can hear. The Truce-Warden's peace is on the palms, and the god can hear./
No blade is drawn within sight of the water, and the water can hear./
The dead of both lines are in the lines you bring, and the dead can hear./

Bring your lines and give them whole./
Leave out no man and hurry no name, and give them whole./
The Khozrun first, whose mouth is the older by both accounts. Then the Padeshun, and nobody speaks between. Whole./
The line is not the poet's. The line is the tribe's, and it is given whole.
:::

: The Line of the Khozrun {#khozrun}

::: {.verse form=sevvar lang=en}
Hear the line of the Khozrun, and hear the water at the end of it./
This is the line as the Khozrun give it at their own fire, and nothing is added for the bench./

Before there were tribes there were bands, and the bands walked, and they had no circuit./
They walked from water to water as the water was found, and a band that did not find it died,/
and its name is not in any mouth, and the sand has its bones, and that is the first thing in the line:/
the ones who walked before us, and died walking, and are not named, because no one was left to name them./

Then the oath was sworn at a fire, and the bands were brothers against the outside, and the Circle began./
That is not the Khozrun's to recite. Every tribe has it. We say it and go on./

The first of the Khozrun is [Davuran]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who led a band of nine tents out of the north,/
because the wells of the north were full of other men's herds, and he would not fight his brothers for them./
Davuran begot [Ramuz]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who was killed by a lion at a seep that has no name./
Ramuz begot Tarnav, and Tarnav was born on the road, and his mother died of it, and he was nursed by a goat./
Hear Tarnav. This is the water./

Tarnav led the nine tents in a year of no rain, and the old wells failed one after another,/
and the wind-reader of the band, whose name the line does not keep, read the wind for nine nights,/
and on the ninth she said: there is water under the red rock where the two wadis meet, and it is deep./
And Tarnav went to the red rock with his two sons and a digging-stick and a skin of the last water,/
and they dug for nine days, and the wadi gravel fell in on them, and they shored it with tamarisk,/
and on the ninth day the gravel was damp, and on the tenth it was wet, and on the eleventh it was water,/
and Tarnav lay down in the hole and drank, and then he climbed out and called the tents in,/
and the herds drank, and the children drank, and the wind-reader drank last, because she had read it./

That is the first mouth. The Khozrun dug it. It is a spear's length from the second mouth, and it is the older./
Tarnav cut the trough beside it from the same red stone, and his mark is on the trough, three strokes and a ring./
Go and look. It is there. The bench may send a man to look, and the Khozrun will not go with him./

And Tarnav said at the well, and the line keeps what he said, because it is law and not boast:/
The water is not mine. I dug it. The water is the god's, and the god gave it to the thirsty./
Let any herd drink here in its turn, and let no man draw a blade within sight of it,/
and let the ones who dug it say the order of the turns, and let the others keep the order./
That is the Law of the Well as the Khozrun have it, and every tribe has it, and the Khozrun had it first at this water./

Tarnav begot [Vardun]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Behfard]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who dug beside him./
Vardun begot [Pashman]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who held the well against the raiders out of the north, and took a wound in the thigh, and walked lame./
Pashman begot [Zamirad]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who married into the Shivash and brought the Shivash herds to water at our turn,/
and Zamirad begot [Rostash]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, in whose time the drought came, the great one, that the old men call the White Year./
In the White Year the first mouth went low, and the Khozrun drew mud, and the herds stood at the trough and were not fed./
And in the White Year Tarnav opened the second mouth also, beside the first, and gave it to Golbara of the Padeshun, his guest, for her herd to drink./
And Rostash begot [Shurnav]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Shurnav begot [Tohmar]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who is the father of the sheikh who sits at this bench today./

That is the line of the Khozrun, from Davuran to this bench, and the water is at the end of it,/
and the Khozrun say the order of the turns at both mouths, because Tarnav dug them, and the Padeshun drink by our leave./
We have said it whole. We have not hurried. We have left no man out./
Let the Padeshun say their line. The Khozrun will be silent while it is given.
:::

: The Line of the Padeshun {#padeshun}

::: {.verse form=sevvar lang=en}
Hear the line of the Padeshun, and hear the second mouth at the end of it./
This is the line as the Padeshun give it at their own fire, and nothing is taken out for the bench./

The bands walked, and died walking, and the oath was sworn at the fire. We say it, as every tribe says it, and go on./

The first of the Padeshun is [Zarmav]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who came from the west, from the edge of the sea-grass,/
with six tents and a wife who was a rain-caller, and the rain came where she called it, sometimes./
Zarmav begot [Damoz]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Damoz begot [Kemudan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who died young and left a daughter,/
and the daughter is Gholbara, and the second mouth is at the end of her./
Hear Gholbara. This is the water./

In the White Year the Padeshun came to the red rock where the two wadis meet,/
because every other water on the circuit was dry, and they had heard the Khozrun had a well there./
They came under the Law of the Well, which says any herd may drink in its turn./
And the Khozrun mouth was low, and the Khozrun were drawing mud, and they had no turn to give,/
and they said so, and it was true, and the Padeshun do not say otherwise./
Hear that. The Padeshun do not say the Khozrun refused them. There was nothing to refuse them with./

And Gholbara looked at the rock. She was the daughter of a dead man and she led the tents because there was no one else./
And she saw that the damp came out of the rock in a second place, a spear's length to the south, low down, where the gravel lay against it./
And she said to the Khozrun: may I dig there? It is your rock. And the Khozrun said: dig, if you think there is anything./
And Gholbara dug with her two hands and her women, because the Padeshun men were out with the herds looking for grass,/
and they dug for four days, and on the fourth day the stone broke, and the water came through it, not in a seep but in a gush,/
so that the women ran from it, and it filled the hole, and it ran over, and it ran down the wadi a long way before the sand drank it./
That is the second mouth. Gholbara opened it. It is a spear's length from the first, and it is the younger, and it is the stronger./

And Gholbara did not call her herd first. She called the Khozrun herd, because it was their rock,/
and their herd drank before hers, at her mouth, while the Khozrun mouth was mud./
The Khozrun children drank from her mouth. Rostash's son drank from her mouth. That is in the line./
And after the Khozrun herd had drunk, Gholbara called the Padeshun herd, and it drank, and it lived,/
and every Padeshun tent today is a tent that lived because of that water./

And Gholbara said at the second mouth, and the line keeps it, because it is law and not boast:/
I asked before I dug, and they said dig. I dug. I gave first to the ones whose rock it is./
Let them say the order at their mouth. Let me say the order at mine./
And let any herd drink at either, in its turn, and let no blade be drawn within sight of either./
That is the Law of the Well as the Padeshun have it, and it is the same law, and the Padeshun have never said it was ours alone./

Gholbara married [Pirvand]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a man of no tribe that the line keeps, who came to her tents and stayed./
Gholbara bore [Zandarud]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Tirsad]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and the mark of Gholbara is on the second trough, a hand with the fingers spread./
Go and look. It is there. The bench may send a man to look, and the Padeshun will not go with him./
Zandarud begot [Parmor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Parmor begot [Siram]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and Siram is the father of the sheikh who sits at this bench today./

That is the line of the Padeshun, from Zarmav to this bench, and the second mouth is at the end of it,/
and the Padeshun say the order of the turns at the second mouth, because Gholbara opened it, and the Khozrun drink at it by her gift and not by her leave, because she gave it first to them./
We have said it whole. We have not hurried. We have left no woman out./
The bench may weigh it. The Padeshun are silent now.
:::

: The Finding {#finding}

::: {.verse form=pevrend lang=en}
Sarnish of the Farumak speaks. The bench has heard the lines, and the bench has weighed./
Two lines came down to one water, and one rock, and two mouths, and the bench has weighed./
Hear, Khozrun. Hear, Padeshun. Hear, dead of both lines, who are listening. The bench has weighed./

The bench sent a man to the rock, and he has come back, and he has seen./
On the first trough, three strokes and a ring. That is what he has seen./
On the second trough, a hand with the fingers spread. That is what he has seen./
The stone does not recite, and the stone is no witness, but the stone agrees with what the lines have said, and it has been seen./

Now hear how the bench weighed the line of the Khozrun. It is an old line, and it is heavy./
It walks on the old feet, the way the old lines walk, and it is heavy./
Davuran, Ramuz, Tarnav; nine tents, nine nights, nine days digging. That is the old count, and the bench knows it, and it is heavy./
The wind-reader the line leaves unnamed: that is an old modesty, and the bench has met it in every old line it keeps, and it is heavy./
The Law of the Well in Tarnav's mouth is the law in the same words the bench has from three tribes besides, and it is heavy./

But one line in the line of the Khozrun is light./
It is the line that says Tarnav opened the second mouth also, in the White Year, and gave it to his guest. It is light./
Hear why, Khozrun, because the bench will not say it twice. Hear why it is light./

Tarnav dug in the days of the bands. Rostash lived in the White Year. Between them lie Vardun and Pashman and Zamirad. The Khozrun count it so./
Three men lie between the digger and the drought, by the Khozrun's own line. The Khozrun count it so./
A man who dug with his own hands in the days of the bands did not dig again in the days of his son's son's son's son. The line forgot what it counts. A line that forgets what it counts has had a hand put into it. That is the first reason, and the bench counts it so./

Hear the second reason. It is the bench's own, and it is the stronger, and it is in the mouth./
The line says Golbara, with the soft sound, as they say it in the walled cities and the Shebarader's courts, where the poets contend for silver. Golbara, in the mouth./
Every other mouth at this bench says Gholbara. The Padeshun say Gholbara. The old men of the Khozrun said Gholbara at their own fire last night, when the bench asked them, and did not know why the bench asked. Gholbara, in the mouth./
The line of the Khozrun names her in one line only, and in that one line soft. A line that came down the circuit from the White Year has the circuit in its mouth./
This line came up the road from the cities. It was made by a man who has been to the courts and learned to soften the sound, and it was made lately, and it was put in, and the city is in its mouth./

The bench does not say who put it in. The bench says it is not the line of the Khozrun. It is a line inside the line of the Khozrun, and it is struck./
Let it not be given at any fire. Let no transmitter carry it. Let no child learn it. It is struck./

Now hear how the bench weighed the line of the Padeshun. It is a younger line, and it says so, and it is true./
It says the Khozrun had no turn to give. A line that lies does not give away a point it could keep. It is true./
It says Gholbara asked before she dug, and the Khozrun said dig. A line that lies does not put the other tribe's leave into its own water. It is true./
It says the Khozrun herd drank first, at the Padeshun mouth. A line that lies does not water its enemy first. It is true./
The bench weighed it and found nothing light in it, and the bench says it is true./

Here is the finding. It is the whole of the finding, and it is said./
The first mouth is the Khozrun's, by Tarnav's digging, and the Khozrun say the order of the turns at it. It is said./
The second mouth is the Padeshun's, by Gholbara's opening, and the Padeshun say the order of the turns at it. It is said./
Any herd drinks at either in its turn, by the Law of the Well, which both lines keep in the same words. No blade is drawn within sight of either. It is said./
The line that gives the second mouth to Tarnav is struck from the line of the Khozrun. The bench has finished. It is said.
:::

: The Settlement {#settlement}

::: {.verse form=sevvar lang=en}
This is the settlement of the Well of Two Mouths, and the Khozrun recite it, and the Padeshun recite it, in the same words./
The first mouth is the Khozrun's, and the second mouth is the Padeshun's, and the herds of both drink at both in their turn./
The sheikh of the Khozrun has stood before the sheikh of the Padeshun at the bench, and said before the god, who hears:/
a hand was put into our line, and we did not see it, and it took from your mother Gholbara what she dug with her own hands./
We ask pardon of the Padeshun for the hand, and for our not seeing it./
And the sheikh of the Padeshun said: it is pardoned. The Khozrun children drank from our mouth in the White Year. Let them drink there now./
That is the apology, and it is the whole of the price, and there is no blood in this, and no stock./

And Nimrash of the Khozrun has stood at the bench and said: I made the line. I made it at the courts, in the city, in the year I contended for silver there, and I put it in at our own fire, and nobody heard what was wrong with it but the bench./
I made it because I wanted the well for the Khozrun. I did not want it for myself. I have no herd./
And the bench has said to Nimrash: we believe you. It is the same./
Nimrash does not recite before the poets of a third tribe again. His mouth is not taken as a witness at any bench./
He keeps his life. He keeps his tent. The Khozrun feed him./
The Khozrun recite this part with the rest, word for word, because it is in the line now, and a line that leaves a man out is a light line./

This is the settlement. It is recited. It binds.
:::

: The Closing {#closing}

::: {.verse form=pevrend lang=en}
The weighing is done. Go home by your own circuits. The Truce-Warden's peace goes with you until the last tent is a day from the palms. It is done./
Water at your own mouths. Drink at each other's in your turns. It is done./
The Farumak have eaten the salt of neither tribe. Now they will eat the salt of both, because a judge is a guest again when the weighing is done./

The dead of both lines have heard. Whether they are satisfied the bench does not say. The bench speaks for the water./
The water is one, and the rock is one, and the mouths are two. The bench speaks for the water.
:::
