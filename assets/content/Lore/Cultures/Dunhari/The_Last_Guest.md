**The Last Guest**, _gholmer tarvand_, is the reading of [Firanash]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the [Parkad]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a desert mystic who went alone into the sand south of the long wells to fast and came back with it. He was housed, followed and obeyed for the rest of his life, as a mystic is whose visions prove true: he had read a drought in the same fast, and the year it would break, and it broke in that year. The [[affiliation-strmcltsbzhrms|voice-bearers]] of the storm cults took the reading into the repertoire they carry from camp to camp, and they recite it on the last night of the [Summer Vigil]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, when the sick and the old have been bathed at the springs and the camps sit up till morning praying for the water to hold.

A _pevrend_ is a reading spoken in rhymed prose, as a reader speaks the sky or a mystic what he was shown, in runs of short clauses that close on one sound. The rendering closes each run on one word. Firanash's reading opens on oaths, as a reading does, and it tells the end of the world as far as he was shown it and no further. The guest in it gives his name, and the reading does not repeat it; the voice-bearers take him for [[lore-angarosdty|Angaros]], the Shadowed Serpent, who is the false word, and they do not say it in the reading's place. Some of the lineages hold that what Firanash was shown is the end, and some that it is the end of one age of the sand, and the reading is recited the same way by both.

: The Last Guest {#reading}

::: {.verse form=pevrend lang=en}
By the fire that was lit before there was a tent to light it in. By the fire./
By the first oath sworn across it, when the bands were brothers. By the fire./
By the fire on the sand at the head of every one of our dead, which nobody tends after. By the fire./

By the salt pressed into the torn edge of the bread. By the salt./
By the name not asked until it is eaten. By the salt./
By the count of days that both of them know, the host and the guest. By the salt./

By the wind that walks the dunes north, a hand's breadth a year, and does not stop. By the wind./
By the wind that took the camp of the fathers and the camp of their fathers. By the wind./
By the wind that will take this camp, and the fire in it, and the one who speaks. By the wind./

I am Firanash of the Parkad. I went out to the sand and sat in it, and I did not eat, and I was shown./
I was shown it in the ninth night of the fast, when the cold came up out of the ground, and the stars came down to the height of a tent-pole. I was shown./
I do not say I understand it. The Weaver of Visions gives the thread and not the cloth. I say what I was shown./

I was shown the sand walking north./
It walked as it walks now, a hand's breadth in a year, and the years were more than the stars, and it did not stop walking north./
It walked over the wells of the south first, one and one and one, and the tribes of the south moved their circuits north./
It walked over the Pale Floor and over the black tower, and the tower went under like a man going under water, and the iron on its top went last, and the lightning came and found nothing, and went north./
It walked over the weighing-place and the palms of the weighing-place, and the benches went under, and nobody was left to weigh, and the lines went with the tribes, north./

I was shown the circuits growing short./
A tribe that walked a circuit of forty wells walked thirty, and then twenty, and then nine, and every year the herd was thinner and the circuit short./
The tribes that had been at feud watered at the same well, because there was one well, and drew no blade within sight of it, because it was the law, and the turns grew short./
And the Water-Warden said the order of the herds at the last wells, and nobody argued in his hearing, because there was no herd left worth the argument, and the days grew short./

I was shown the last well./
It was a seep in a cleft of grey rock at the north edge of the sand, where no circuit had gone, because none had needed to, and it gave a cupful in a night. That was the last well./
And at the last well was the last tribe, and it was not a tribe of any line that I know, because the lines had run together like water into sand, and it was every line, at the last well./
And the last tribe was nine tents, as the first band was nine tents, at the last well./

I was shown the last host./
She was a woman, a widow, with a son, and the son was not grown, and her tent was the last tent in the line on the high side of the seep, where the road came in, and her fire was the one a stranger would see first. That was the last host./
And she had one camel, and it was white, and she had raised it at the breast of a dead mother, and it was all she had, the last host./

I was shown the last guest./
He came in at evening on foot, from the south, from where the sand had already walked, from where nothing was, and he stood at the edge of the firelight, and he did not ask. That was the last guest./
And his feet were not blistered. And his lips were not cracked. And his cloak was the colour of the sand when the sun has gone and before the stars come, and it did not move in the wind, the last guest./
And the dogs did not see him. And the son did not see him. And she saw him, the last host, and she knew him, the last guest./

And she got up from the fire. She did not ask. She went to the white camel./
She spoke to it, as a woman speaks to a beast she raised, and took the knife from her belt, and the sand drank what it could, and she was weeping, and she did not stop, and she killed the white camel./
And she set the pot on the three stones, and the meat was boiled, and she broke the last bread, and she pressed the last salt into the torn edge of it, and held it out over the meat of the white camel./
And he looked at the bread a long time, the time it takes to say a line from its founder to its last man, and then he took it and he ate it, and she watched him eat. And it was the last of the camel./

Then she asked him his name. It is asked after the salt. She asked his name./
And he said it. And I heard it. And I will not say it. The voice-bearers will not have it in their mouths, and they are right, and they will not. It was his name./
And when she heard it she did not change her face. She did not change her face. She had known it when he stood at the edge of the light, and she had held out the salt, and she did not change her face at the name./

And she said: you have eaten. Sit down./
And he sat down. A guest sits where the host says. He sat down./
And she said: you are a guest in this tent, and in this tent nothing that is yours comes in with you. Leave it at the edge of the light. And he left it there, whatever it was, and sat down./

I was shown the count./
She told him the count of days, as a host does. And he said it back to her, as a guest does. I did not hear the number. I was shown that there was a count./
The salt binds the host. The salt binds the guest. A guest who brings his feud into the tent of his host has shamed the host. He knows it. Everyone knows it. That is the count./
And the sand had walked to the edge of the light and stopped. I saw it stop. It lay there like a herd couched for the night. It lay at the edge of the count./

I was shown the fire./
She built it up. She built it high, higher than a tent, so that it would be seen from the ridge, and from the ridge beyond, by anyone on the road, though there was nobody on the road. She built the fire./
And she fed it with what she had: the poles of the tent, and the camel's saddle, and the loom, and the pegs. And it burned, and the guest sat by it, and he did not move, and she fed the fire./
And her son slept by the door. And the guest sat. And the sand lay at the edge of the light. And the night went on and on, and she fed the fire./

And I was shown no more./
The cold went back into the ground, and the stars went back up, and I was sitting in the sand, and I was hungry, and I had been shown no more./
I do not know how long the count is. I do not know how long she has to feed the fire with. I do not know whether the guest gets up when the count is done, or what he does when he gets up, or what the Thunder Rider does then, or the Keeper of Harmony, or the Flame. I was shown no more./

Hear me, then, camps at the water. You who sit up on the last night of the Vigil, with the old ones bathed and the sick ones bathed and the mothers in the places of honour. Hear me./
I was shown that the sand walks, and you know that, and I was shown that it stops at the edge of a count. Hear me./
I was shown that the last thing in the world is a widow's fire, and a guest she knew, and salt she held out anyway. Hear me./

So keep your fires high. Every fire on the sand is hers. Keep them high./
Hold out the salt to the one at the edge of the light, and ask his name after. Keep it high./
Keep the count you give, to the last night of it, against your own kin, against anyone. Keep it high./
By the fire, by the salt, by the wind, I have said what I was shown. I am Firanash of the Parkad, and I have said it, and it is said.
:::
