The priesthood of the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]] sings **The Hymn of the Sun-Lord**, _Zamlet elu Uqa'a_, at the eastern face of the [Great Temple of Uqa'a]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], the island at the heart of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]. It is sung in the dark before the [Festival of the Coming-Forth]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, when the sun of the longest day comes up [[place-waluqaa|the Sun's Road]] straight at the temple door. It is a _zamlet_, a hymn of the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] temple kind: chains of the god's names, lines sung in pairs, a long count of what the god lights, and a refrain at each turn of his circuit, with the rubrics the singers keep. The god is one of the Twelve, so his name is sung in two breaths with a rest held between them, and the first rubric says how the rest is kept and what it is not.

The hymn belongs to the age of [[lore-akhsunroad|the Sun's Road]]. The king in it is the one who laid the road, [Anlagh'el'Qar I]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of the house of [Zu'Amqeletu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} ([[lore-crossmound|the Crossing to the Mound]]). He stretched it on the solstice line from the edge of the living city, across the water and through the temple, to the desert shelf across the river, where [[place-zugezer|Zu-Gezer]] is only opening as the city of the dead and no king lies yet, and he proclaimed that nothing may stand in the sightline between the temple and either horizon. The sun of the hymn goes down into the ground at the western end of that road and walks the night on his feet to the fight. The hymn sings the god's names and leaves the other's out.

The copy given here is cut in the sacred hand on the wall of the tomb of [Khelansi Zu'Amqeletu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Overseer of the Granaries of the God and a kinsman of the house. His is one of the kin-tombs the first houses of the age raised among the streets below the High City of [[place-zuleri|Zu-Leri]]. Those streets are [[place-zelnut|the Zelnut]] now, and the wall is the back wall of a brewer's storeroom. A doorway cut through it takes one stanza whole, marked `[…]`, and the lamp-smoke of the house has darkened the top courses without taking a line. The tomb's copy differs from the temple's in one line, where it names its owner among the dead who stand up as the god passes under. The dedication cut above the hymn is given first. Beneath the last line a scribe of the tally, in the reign of [Meqes II]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, entered in Qalezu how he found the wall and what he did there, and the graffito follows the hymn as it is cut. The rendering keeps the rubrics in brackets.

: The Graffito {#graffito}

::: {.verse form=prose lang=en work=graffito}
Year nine of Gar-Au Meqes II, Harvest's second month. Qedut ez Gar-Legulu, granary tally scribe, entered this store to collect the brewer's tally and discovered this wall. Its sacred hand is one I know. I read it aloud completely, continuing across the cut doorway. The brewer identifies his father as its cutter. I spoke Khelansi's name, Overseer of the God's Granaries, so the name remains spoken. Nobody here knew it; I asked. The brewer charged two qelu for lamplight. Beneath Khelansi I enter my name and town. Qedut ez Gar-Legulu. Gar-Legulu.
:::
