A stone stands at the upstream end of [[place-zelhalzi|Zel-Halzi]], the Unmeasured Field, on the west-bank floodplain below [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], where the road leaves the field's edge to bend round it. It is **the Stela of Kuz'Uqa at Zel-Halzi**, _Qedlet elu Kuz'Uqa_, a _qedlet_ of the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] royal kind: a dated heading, the throne name in its chain of titles, the deeds in the old formulas with the verb first, the plunder counted at a gate, and the enemy set under the king's feet.

[Kuz'Uqa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, "strong of the sun-lord," is the name the priesthood of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]] gave the chief of the [Tha'Ulgau]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} when it crowned him on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] in 1612 ST. That is the year his horsemen came down out of the western hills and destroyed the last army of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] on this ground ([[lore-thaulgau|the Coming of the Tha'Ulgau]]). He ordered the stone in the Season of Harvest of that year, his first, with the dead of the battle still lying on the silt around it, and the scribes of Lenen-Nezut cut it for him in the sacred hand, which he could not read. It belongs to the age the Keepers call [[lore-akhdivriver|the Divided River]], and it is the one monument of the hill house that stands where it was set.

The stone is as tall as a man above the ground and was twice that when it was raised; the flood that covered the dead in the next [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]] has come back twelve hundred times since, and the lower half is under the silt. The scribe who cut it was [Rethur ez Lenen-Nezut]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, of the town's bureau. The rendering gives the stone from the top down as it reads now. It marks a space the cutter left empty with a dash, a line the weather or the silt has taken with `[…]`, and the later hands where they stand.

Came he to the estate-towns, and the Legha'lutu of each came out to the road with his seal in his hand, and the seals were his./

Ran they toward the river, and the river was there./
Ran they toward the desert, and the horses were there./
Stood the standards of fourteen selatu in the barley at noon; at dusk the barley stood, and they did not./
Went to the West in his fourteenth year [Amqel'Uzner]{.name data-type="being/npc"}./
Lie they where they fell. None was carried off. None was wrapped./
No name was read over any of them, and the kites of the west bank were fed through the Harvest./
Took he up his own dead and their horses and carried them west into the grass./

Went he down the river to Galezkara before the roads were open, and the water was low enough./

Each [[lore-geletssn|Gelet]], on the night of the rout, a priest of [[affiliation-hezmuiri|Hezmu'iri]] from Lenen-Nezut stands at the foot of the stone with a lamp and holds the Reading for the unnamed. He reads the columns from the top, the heading, the titles, the deeds and the count, and then the rows: at each row he reads the formula, and at the five rows that carry a name he reads the name. The surveyors of Lenen-Nezut walk every field of the selat that season and do not walk this one; a rod staked on it overnight is found moved in the morning, and they do not stake one twice. The road bends round the field at the stone, and the villages on its edge shut their doors on the field's side after dark.

The five names came from [[place-iqu|Iku]], [[place-gau|Gau]], [[place-chegu|Chegu]], [[place-zelqar|Zel-Qar]] and [[place-iqeru|Iqeru]], brought by families who could prove that a man of theirs had marched with the army of Galezkara and had not come home. Each was cut by a different hand, after a Reading at the stone closed on it, and the newest is scratched in Qalezu, which did not exist when the stone was cut. The Halzi'a house of [Tha'Ulgau]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the conquerors' kin, settled on the western pastures of [[place-zuqeztunome|Zu-Qeztu]] when the hill house closed ([[lore-hillhouse|the Closing of the Hill House]]), rides in from [[place-zileti|Zileti]] each Gelet with an offering and no escort and sets it at the edge of the field below the stone. Nobody has dug for the lower half.
