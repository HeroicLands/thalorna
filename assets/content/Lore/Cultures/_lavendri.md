The Élavendri are the only human people in [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]] who live beside the Elder Races openly and as equals, and it has marked them more deeply than they usually admit. A culture that has spent centuries in the company of things older and stranger than itself acquires a particular manner: courteous, allusive, patient, and quietly certain that the obvious explanation is rarely the whole one. Their cities are built to be beautiful—towers of pale stone, bridges woven through with living vines, gardens where the line between cultivated and wild has been deliberately blurred—and the blurring is the point and not an effect.

## The Houses and the Old Blood

Élavendri society is aristocratic and organized around ancient noble houses. A noble is expected to be a patron of the arts, a steward of the land, and a practitioner of magic or at minimum a serious appreciator of it; a house that produces no artists and no mages across two generations quietly stops being counted among the first rank, whatever its acreage.

Houses go to great lengths to trace descent to the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]], the ancestor people, and a demonstrable connection to the old blood is a source of immense prestige. Whether all such claims are genuine is another matter, and the polite Élavendri position is that a claim maintained gracefully for four hundred years has earned a certain standing regardless of its origin ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]).

Commoners are farmers, craftspeople and merchants, and they live well by the standards of most of Ankaris. The aristocracy's obligation of stewardship is taken seriously enough that a lord who lets his people go hungry is worse than a bad lord—he has failed the specific duty his rank exists to discharge.

## The Áelendan

In the mountainous interior live the [[lore-aelendanclt|Áelendan]], the People of the Pact: the ancestor tribes who never settled into the kingdom's cities and who keep the old ways from before the monarchy.

The Élavendri relationship to them is complicated and mostly unspoken. They are the source of every noble house's prestige, and they are also, from the cities' point of view, rustics who declined civilization. The Áelendan view is that the cities traded something away and have been decorating the gap ever since. Both sides are courteous about it, and neither is persuaded.

## Bards, and Why They Matter

The oral tradition is deeply embedded and the bards are legendary beyond the kingdom's borders. Poetry and storytelling are not entertainment here: they are how a house's history is held, how prophecy is transmitted, and how a judgment is made memorable enough to bind.

An Élavendri therefore listens to form as well as content. A thing said well is more likely to be believed and much more likely to be repeated, and a house that retains a great bard has bought something closer to a chancery than a musician. This has an edge to it—a satire composed by a bard of standing is a real weapon, and the law recognizes it as one.

## Magic as Manners

Magic is ordinary here in a way it is nowhere else in Ankaris. It is studied, practiced, discussed at table, and expected of the well-bred, and a noble who cannot at least follow a technical conversation about it is embarrassing in the way a Provenzian who cannot follow an argument is embarrassing.

The consequence is that the Élavendri are unimpressed by displays. Power casually shown is vulgar; power precisely applied is admired. A visiting mage who sets out to overawe an Élavendri court gets an attentive, appreciative and entirely unmoved audience.

The magic they study is the [[affiliation-zemorhodhis|Zemor Hodhis]], the Six Ways, as [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|the Thordimalon]] teaches it, and the houses' ties with the Order are very close: the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} and the Order's chapterhouses are where a well-bred Élavendri learns the art. The bond is a real point of tension with the Áelendan, who actively dislike the Order and do not tolerate its meddling in their sacred sites.

Because the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]] walk openly among them, the Élavendri also have a settled sense of proportion about their own arts. They know what a truly old practitioner looks like, and the knowledge prevents a certain kind of human vanity.

## Prophecy, and the Long View

Prophecy is a live institution and not a curiosity, and Élavendri culture is organized to receive it: houses keep records of utterances, bards transmit them, and a great many decisions are made with a half-remembered line in the background.

This produces the quality foreigners find most exasperating. An Élavendri is reluctant to close a question—he will leave an arrangement deliberately open, decline to name a date, and prefer a phrasing that can be read two ways. He is not being evasive. He has been raised among people for whom the meaning of an event is often established long after it happens.

## What Is Good, and What Succeeds

**Good is a thing asked for and kept: land held as a duty, a talent cultivated, the [Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} respected and the house fed. Evil is a thing taken that was not offered, and a talent or a people let go to waste.** An Élavendri does not weigh a deed by its force. Power casually shown is vulgar and power precisely applied is admired, and the test of a deed is whether it was asked for in the right form and kept in the right order. The oldest account [[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Colleges]] sing is of a people who sat at the edge of a wood and asked, and it is taught as a lesson in manners as much as in history.

Success is a house counted in the first rank: one that has produced artists and mages across two generations, whose claim to the old blood has been proved before the Colleges, and whose lord's people eat. In the cities it is a bard of standing at the table, a chair at court and a pupil who goes on to a College. In the country it is a grove-elder who counts a village as his own and a harvest that is not strange. Failure is the house quietly dropped from the first rank, the claim exposed before people who remember the genealogy differently, the satire sung by a bard of standing, and the hungry people of a lord who failed the one duty his rank exists to discharge. At the bottom is outlawry, which walks a person past the tree line.

The Élavendri value most art, stewardship, the old blood and a thing said well. They value least haste, display and the plain answer to a question that is still open. Closing a question early is not decisive but crude, and an arrangement left open is the sign of a house in no hurry to be wrong.

## The Man Admired

[[being-rnthrprdln|Ranthor Párdalen]] is the Élavendri's admired shaman, a man in his late forties who stands between the kingdom and the interior. He served the kingdom as a soldier and a guard and rose to significant rank. Captured in a campaign against hostile tribal forces, he was held by shamans who saw in him a spiritual sensitivity his training had suppressed, and who inducted him instead of killing him. When he was ransomed he returned to [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], left the royal service and made himself a shaman who bridges the martial and the mystical. He heals wounds, mediates disputes and steadies warriors before a fight, and he sits on the [Tribal Council of Shamans]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, where he is the primary liaison between the shamanic order and the kingdom's military and political leadership. A crown is tattooed on his chest, and he says only that he once served crowns and serves the tribe instead.

The Élavendri admire in him the oath sworn at every crowning, that the crown stops at the tree line, made flesh in one man: a soldier of the kingdom who learned where the kingdom ends. He is a man of few words and a dry humor, fair and not warm, who respects competence and honesty in any station and has no patience for pretense. His cost is that he belongs to neither side of the line he holds. Traditionalist shamans count his practical bent a betrayal of the mystical, his plain speech is out of place at a court where an open question is a courtesy, and the melancholy in him is that of a man who has seen the worst of the city and the wood and has made peace with neither.

## The Woman Admired

[[being-ylthklmrth|Jalith Kálmereth]] is the Élavendri's admired bard. She is a traveling singer born at [[place-liranel|Liranel]] to a family of performers, named Kálmereth for the clarity of her voice and keeping the name. She sings the ballads of the common folk from village to village and comes home to the timber town only between roads. Liranel gave her a subject: it is the town where people hire guides into the old wood, and where some families wait for someone who does not come back. She has sung in noble halls and been well paid there and has never stayed, and she has turned down a settled place at a lord's table more than once, because the formalities of court sit badly on her and she says so.

Her admiration is not a lesser form of Ranthor's. He holds the line between the kingdom and the interior by standing on it, and she holds the account of the ones who crossed it. The Colleges keep the genealogies of the houses that can pay for proofs, and her songs keep the families the genealogies leave out. She belongs to the [Minstrels' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"}, which gives her the right to sing for pay in any Élavendri market, and she is as much at ease at the market stall as at the high table. Her cost is the road. A bard who will not take a lord's table has no house, no patron and no College behind her, and the memory she keeps is held by no one but herself.

## What the Houses and the Groves Keep

An Élavendri can name the things that hold the kingdom's old institutions in place. **The genealogies** are kept by the Bardic Colleges, the kingdom's definitive authority on who descends from whom, and the proofs of descent they hold are among its most valuable documents. The Pact is the unwritten compact of the Áelendan with the land, sung and enacted through the seasons and held by the Tradition Keepers. [[lore-theasking|The Asking]] is the Sinalean lay of the Pelwar band that came over the eastern pass and asked the wood for a place in it; the Tradition Keepers hold it, and the Colleges sing it to the harp in nine turnings. [[lore-seventowers|The Seven Towers]] is a second Sinalean song, held the same way. **The grove-elder's circlet** is woven of living wood, tended through a lifetime and burned with its wearer. **The leaf-mark** is the tattoo on the inside of an initiate's wrist that records the home grove, renewed by its elder and fading with absence. **The tree line** is the limit the crown swears to at each crowning.

Each person carries smaller things. A hedge-affiliate carries a knotted cord of grove-blessed yarn, often under the clothes. A noble house carries a signet. A sun name goes in public, and a shadow name stays among intimates and at night.

## The Day Kept

On an ordinary day the religion is small and local. Officially the Élavendren are Aureldian, fervent in the worship of the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] in the Aureldian manner, with [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] the World-Weaver and [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]] the Veiled Dreamer held in special prominence. Beside that faith the common people keep the older beliefs and customs of the land, adapted rather than set aside, and the Aureldian priesthood officially opposes them and in practice turns a blind eye. A family that hears in the temple that Sélmoros keeps the gate of the dead still plants a tree for its dead and sings the lament at the cairn, and a village that keeps the temple's feasts keeps the hinge-days of the groves as well. The noble houses are held to a stricter account, since the priesthood expects them to follow Aureldian worship and give up the older beliefs. The Pact tradition is the Áelendan's, and the Élavendren respect it, though many noble houses, the lesser ones especially, still keep to it in secret. No Élavendren sets the gods and the older spirits of the land against each other. Sacred groves, standing stones and holy wells serve beside the temples. A household leaves a loaf, _tárneve_, at a birch, a well or a stone and does not wait to see it taken. A village healer or herbalist who walks in [[affiliation-groveconcl|the Grove Council]]'s tradition is the person a sick child is taken to, and a grove-elder is heeded before any sheriff.

The rest of the day is the house: the land's stewardship, the work of whatever art the household follows, and a meal at which magic is discussed as other peoples discuss the weather. An Élavendri listens to form as well as content, since a thing said well is more likely to be believed and repeated. A guest is seated by the order of the seats, _máldalon_, which is worked out again at every gathering, and the right of the guest under the roof, _háelalon_, is not argued.

## The Round of the Year

The turning of the seasons is marked by elaborate festivals that honor the gods and the older spirits of the land together. The ones that bind the kingdom are those where its old institutions meet.

- **The hinge-days** are the equinoxes and solstices, when the Grove Council gathers at the great groves. They are for deciding what the forests need, what the fae are owed and what the kingdom must refuse [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|the Order]].
- **The feast**, _kurnis_, is a gathering with food at which the genealogies are sung. A house gives it on its own occasions and a bard of the house sings the line. It is for renewing a claim of descent in the hearing of everyone who might dispute it.
- **The autumn fair at [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]]** brings tribal families down to camp on the lake shore rather than enter the walls, and the high country trades with the low. It is for trade face to face, and for a house's claim of descent to be heard by people who remember the genealogy differently.
- **The crowning** is where the monarch swears that the crown stops at the tree line. It is for saying again, in the basin, where the kingdom ends.

## Favor and the Tree Line

Reward is standing: a place in the first rank, a bard of note at the table, a College's proof of descent, a village that counts the grove-elder as its own. A great bard's song is the reward that lasts, since a deed sung well is remembered longer than a deed done.

Penalty runs from courtesy to the tree line. A house that produces no artists and no mages across two generations quietly stops being counted among the first rank. A bard of standing may sing a satire, and the law recognizes a satire as a weapon. A king who crosses a grove-elder finds his harvests strange, his hunts empty and his soldiers reluctant to march into forest country. A trespasser in sacred territory is warned once and escorted out, and one who returns or desecrates is dealt with harshly by the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]]. A person outlawed, _ánkenis_, is walked past the tree line and known to the realm only as one it has excluded.

## The Crown, the Circle and the Grove

Authority in [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]] has no single seat. The monarch rules the cities, the farms and the coast, with the officers of a feudal crown from Chancellor and Justiciar to Sheriff and Coroner. The Bardic Circle is the kingdom's memory and conscience, and the crown consults it on precedent, protocol and lineage. The Grove Council is the religious authority of the fae traditions, holds no charter and is older than the kingdom, and the rural population treats its word as binding. The Aureldian temples serve the gods of the twelve, and the Áelendan Wardens hold the sacred sites by a jurisdiction the crown does not challenge. The Thordimalon (the Arcane Order) is permitted and limited: it may hold its houses, teach and practice within the cities, and it may not extend that reach into the tribal lands, the sacred sites or the deep forest.

A case is tried by the royal courts in the cities and shires and by the grove-elder in the villages, and the Wardens' judgment on trespass is final inside sacred ground. The royal magistrate asks first whether a complainant had been warned. The Colleges keep the records of lineage and precedent, and the chancery keeps the crown's writs. The crown collects through its Treasurer and its sheriffs. When the answers conflict the settlement is slow and deliberate, with attention to precedent, prophecy and the counsel of [[lore-faecourts|the fae courts]]. The crown governs the interior only through the Wardens, who answer to the tribes, and a king believed to have taken the Order's side would face not a rebellion but the quiet judgment that he had stopped being of the old blood.

## What Becomes of the Dead

The Aureldian faiths teach that the soul passes through the spirit realm and that the Silent Judge [[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] keeps the gate. The Élavendri add what the land adds. The dead are part of the house, and the house is the whole descending line, living and not. The veil between the worlds is thin in Élavendre, and the dead lie in a country where it can be crossed. The Áelendan send a Death-Speaker to the owl-spirit of [[place-owlhollow|the Owl Hollow]] to ask whether the recently dead have gone.

That belief makes an ordinary day. A house names its dead at the feast, since the genealogies sung there are the dead in the one place where they are kept. A family plants a tree for one of the dead, _sélvosalve_, and sings the lament at the cairn, _nélthava_. A debt of honor, _molthon_, outlasts the person who owed it and falls on the line. A noble pays a College to prove descent because the proof is an inheritance of the dead as much as of the living, and a house that lets its talent lapse has failed its dead as well as its heirs. Money goes into the proof, the bard, the cairn, the tree and the patronage of the arts the house is judged by.

## Reaching the Dead

The living reach the dead through the bard and the grove. The bard sings the line at the feast, and the grove holds the thin places where a Death-Speaker or a grove-elder can ask. The lament is sung at the cairn. As elsewhere in the west, the temples of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] charge for funerals and tomb care and not for answers from the dead, and the Grove Council does not charge for what a grove-elder asks but expects the village to have left its offerings and spared its copses.

The dead reach the living through the land and the line. The thin places let something cross, and a village that cuts the wrong copse finds its children sickening. Whether the dead of a house answer is not a promise any institution makes. A Death-Speaker asks whether the dead have gone, which is a different question from asking the dead anything.

## What a Person Owes

Ask an Élavendri what a person owes, and he answers: the house first, and by house he means the whole descending line including the parts of it that are no longer living. Then the land in his keeping, which is stated as a duty and not a possession. Then his art, whatever it is, because a talent left uncultivated is held to be a debt unpaid.

Last he owes the Pact his respect—the old arrangement of the Áelendan with what was here before anyone, which is theirs to keep—and he will not explain it to a foreigner, partly from courtesy and partly because he is not entirely sure of the terms himself.

In return a person is owed the house's name and protection, the land's keeping by the lord whose rank exists for it, a hearing that is courteous and slow, and a place at the fire under _háelalon_. The crown owes its people the tree line held and its lords the stewardship that justifies their rank.

## The Weak

The house is the first resort, the lord the second and the village healer the third. A lord whose people go hungry has failed the specific duty his rank exists to discharge. The poor of the cities go to the temples and the groves, and the hedge-affiliate is the Council's eyes and ears in every village. A widow, an orphan or an old relation is placed by the house first and the village second. A child raised by another house is fostered, _íthanersa_, and a person of no line whom a house takes in is seated at its fire by the rule of the guest.

In a bad year the lord's stewardship is the first resort. Commoners live well by the standards of Ankaris, and a bad year draws on that surplus before it draws on anything else. The temples and the groves carry what the lord cannot, and so do the Order's chapterhouses, whose healing and legal scholarship the traditional institutions cannot always match. A village that turns the Order away may find itself without the medical and administrative help it was quietly receiving.

## Those Who Cannot Work

An injured or incapacitated person goes on being the house's, since the house includes the line and not only the able. A maimed soldier is fed by his lord's stewardship, a bard who cannot travel by the house that keeps her, and the old by the young. Hedge-affiliates and grove-trained healers close wounds, and a person who cannot labor is asked to do what he can in the art he follows. The old are the memory of the house, because the oral tradition is how a line is held.

## Shelter, Teaching and Defense

Shelter is the house, then the manor, then the temple and the grove. Teaching is the house's first and the College's after: the children of nobles are taught by tutors and by the Order's schools where the house can afford them, and by the bards and grove-elders where it cannot. A grove-elder is chosen in childhood and trained for decades. Defense is the crown's levy and the Wardens' jurisdiction. The eastern hills muster a levy against raiders every year, the Wardens hold the tree line, and the crown's hold on the interior is the Áelendan's consent.

## The Line at Every Age

The house is the unit, and its line is wider than the household. A child is born into a house and given its names, uses a sun name in public and acquires a shadow name among intimates. A young person learns the house's art and may be sent to a College or fostered into another house. An adult serves the land and the art, marries for the house's sake and sits in the order of the seats. An elder holds the memory and the claim. The dying are attended by the temple, the grove and the bard, and the dead are planted, sung and counted into the line.

Membership is by blood proved before the Colleges, by marriage, by fosterage and by the guest's seat. A house is the line and not the roof, so an Élavendri can belong to a house he has never lived in. The nuclear family is a small thing inside a large one.

## What the House Does Not Say

Every real culture has a blind spot its own thoughtful members argue about, and the Élavendri's is the descent. A claim to the old blood is the currency of the first rank, and it is maintained gracefully whether or not it is true. Thoughtful Élavendri dispute whether the Colleges' proofs are a courtesy or a fraud, and whether a kingdom whose crown rests on the old blood can afford to ask.

The sin is the welcome. The crown's cordiality to the Order is warm and sincere, the [University of Péravel]{.name data-type="affiliation"} dominates the capital, and the Order has taken artifacts out of the sacred sites for as long as it has been permitted into the kingdom. The Áelendan count the permission, and not any single theft, as the offense. The crown profits from an institution that is robbing its own ancestor-people, and the guides it provides are excellent, courteous and do not lead the Order anywhere that matters. Everyone concerned understands the arrangement, and the Élavendri call it diplomacy.
