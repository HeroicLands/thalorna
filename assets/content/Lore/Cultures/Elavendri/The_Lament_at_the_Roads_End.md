**The Lament at the Road's End** is a _nélthava_, a lament, that the market singers of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] sing for the diggers who went up to the [King's Cut]{.name data-type="place/site"} in the reign of [Hóuradel]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and did not come down ([[lore-sorvandaclose|The Sórvanda Closing]]). A _nélthava_ belongs at the cairn, and these dead have none that anyone below the mountains knows of; so it is sung where the broken ore-road comes down to [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]], on the first night of the autumn fair, by a singer who stands with his back to the lake and faces up the road. It is a song of the [Minstrels' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} and not of [[affiliation-bardicolgs|the Colleges]]. It is sung in ballad measure, four lines to the verse with the second and fourth rhyming, and the fair sings the names back with the singer at the end.

It names eleven: [Dalren]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the drover's son, [Moren]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the smith of [[place-valdun|Valdun]], the charcoal-burning brothers [Naldor]{.name data-type="being/npc"} and [Tenral]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, [Orlen]{.name data-type="being/npc"} the carter, [Denrik]{.name data-type="being/npc"} who owed money, [Salten]{.name data-type="being/npc"} off the river barges, [Halden]{.name data-type="being/npc"} who left a wife, [Kelda]{.name data-type="being/npc"} who baked for them, her son [Arvend]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who was fourteen, and [Jeldor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the king's engineer, who gave the orders and died with the rest. Singers on the coast name thirteen, and some at Elanmere leave the engineer out, so the count is not agreed, and the song asks for any name it lacks. Eleven rowans stand at the foot of the ore-road, set there by the diggers' mothers and wives, and a loaf is left on the stone among them on the first night of the fair by someone nobody sees.

The song is a satire as well as a lament, and it is sung against a crown. It sets the king who went up the mountain unasked and came down beside the diggers who were asked up by his crier and stayed, and it sets the proofs of descent the great houses paid for after the Closing beside eleven people whose line nobody sings. The Colleges do not teach it. No crown has forbidden it.

The poem is in common metre, rhymed ABCB.
