**The Line of the House of Hálvireth** is a _sírnernë_, a roll of names, as the bard of a lowland house sings it at a _lórdemon_, a feast at which the genealogies are sung. The house of **Hálvireth** holds river-meadows in the Bérav basin below [[place-beravel|Béravel]], and it gives this feast on the night a child of the house is named; the bard sings the line from the child up, so that the child is counted into it in the hearing of everyone who might dispute it. What stands here is the short line, the heads of the house only, which is sung at a naming; the long line, with every branch and every marriage, takes a night and is sung at a death.

The recital is in two forms. The bard's frame, which opens and closes it, is in rhymed couplets to the harp. The line itself is not composed and is not rhymed: it is said, each head in a set form, each line closing on the name that opens the next, exactly as the [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Colleges]] proved it, and a bard who changes a word of a proof has changed the proof. Each head is given one deed, the deed the house has chosen to keep, and the dead for whom trees stand are named with their trees. A debt of honor the line has never paid is sung at every feast until it is paid, and this line carries one, to a house whose line has ended.

The line was proved before the Colleges by **Árnolen** in the year the mountains closed ([[lore-ardwynclose|The Ardwyn Closing]]), when every house set the Colleges to prove its descent, and the proof joins the house to the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] through **Sírtala**, a woman of the Dhárgandi who came down to the basin and married a river trader. Above her the names are in the high tongue, and the house sings no further up the Dhárgandi line than three names, and hands the rest to the Tradition Keepers, whose it is. The Dhárgandi who come down to the autumn fair at [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]] keep no daughter of **Rônken** who went down to the basin. The house has sung her at the fair every autumn since **Vélthar** went up and sang the line there, and nobody at the fair has stood up.

: The Line of the House of Hálvireth {#line}

The bard:

```poetry {form=sirnerne meter="iambic tetrameter" rhyme=AABB lang=en}
Set down the cups, and shut the door,
and trim the lamps. I sing, before
the meat, the line; and not one word
of it is mine. It's to be heard
by those who'd sing it otherwise.
If you are one of those, then rise,
tonight, and say so in this hall,
or eat, and hold it, one and all,
another year. The child is here.
She has her name. Now hear her clear
into the line, from her, the least,
up to the first. Hear, house and feast.
```

The line:

```poetry {form=sirnerne lang=en}
Sílvera, named tonight, whose other name is not for this hall, child of Táeren.
Táeren, who holds the house, and keeps the river-meadows, and whose people eat, child of Méldoreth.
Méldoreth, who followed the art at Béravel and came home, and kept a harper twenty years and paid him, child of Óuravel.
Óuravel, in whose years the silver stopped going east, who built the living bridge over the mill-race, which stands, child of Dhéneth.
Dhéneth, who held the house in her own right, and in the year the rain came wrong unbarred the stores, child of Kálvorin.
Kálvorin, for whom the oak stands at the ford-gate, child of Thérseth.
Thérseth, who stood on the north shore the year the fleets came, and came home, child of Ráenor.
Ráenor, whom a bard of standing sang against, and who lived it down, and never named the bard, child of Ílthera.
Ílthera, who held a chair at court against the Ordo, and kept it, child of Gálveren.
Gálveren, who planted the birch in the low meadow for his brother, which stands, child of Vélthar.
Vélthar, who went up to the fair at Elanmere and sang the line there, and was heard, child of Sárhel.
Sárhel, who lost the harp-hall to fire and built it again in stone, child of Nérrin.
Nérrin, a harper, the house's own, who sat in the Circle, child of Árnolen.
Árnolen, who in the year the mountains closed set the College to prove the line, and paid, and the College sang it in this hall, child of Lóthel.
Lóthel, fostered in the house of Oréthan, who owed Oréthan a fosterling, and Oréthan's line ran out before it was paid; the debt is the line's, and owed; child of Méreval and of Sírtala.
Méreval, a trader on the river, who married Sírtala.

[Here the harp is loudest.]

Sírtala of the Dhárgandi, who came down from the hills to the basin and stayed, and kept the high tongue in her mouth all her life, child of Rônken.
Rônken of the Dhárgandi, child of Tháurmira.
Tháurmira of the Dhárgandi, of the hearth that was kindled from the Lodge.
And up the Dhárgandi line, which the Keepers hold and not this house, to Dhárgan, who sat.
And up past Dhárgan, to the snow on the pass, and the lamb in the coat.
```

The bard:

```poetry {form=sirnerne meter="iambic tetrameter" rhyme=AABB lang=en}
That is the line. The hall has heard.
Nobody stood. Nobody stirred.
Sílvera, you are in it now:
the debt to Oréthan, the bough
of the oak at the ford, the birch, the fire,
the chair at court, the hill, and higher,
the snow. It's yours. You keep it, then,
and keep it sung, and sing again.
Now fill the cups. The line is fed.
I've sung the living and the dead.
```

: The names in the line {#names}

The house sings the heads of its line by their sun names. The highland names above the join are sung as the highlands say them.
