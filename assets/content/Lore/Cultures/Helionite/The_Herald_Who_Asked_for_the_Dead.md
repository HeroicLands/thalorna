**The Herald Who Asked for the Dead** is the legend the priests of [[place-gamessanct|the Sanctuary of the Games]] tell at the heralds' altar on the evening before every meeting, when the heralds of the cities have come in and have not yet sworn. Pilgrims stand at the back and listen. It is the sanctuary's account of how the custom began that ends every war between the Helionite cities: the loser's herald walks out on the field under truce, asks for the dead in fixed words, and is given them ([[lore-firstgames|the First Games]]). The words are the one part of the legend that does not change from teller to teller, and the heralds at the altar say them aloud with the priest when he comes to them.

The herald is [Agelis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and the victor who gave him the dead is [Tálanos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. The priests keep the altar for no city in particular, and they keep the legend the same way: they never say which city Agelis spoke for or which city Tálanos led, and every city that sends a herald claims Agelis as its own. In the legend the crossing happens in the years after the first meeting, when the truce still held only for the days of the [Games]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and it is the sanctuary's explanation of how the herald's person came to be inviolable at every time and in every war.

The legend is told in prose, as the priests tell it, with the words of the asking set apart.
