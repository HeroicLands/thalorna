**The Morning Words**, _Ongelshar_, are what an [[lore-osketclt|Osket]] daughter who will hold a road is taught by the woman who holds it now. The name is the name of the instruction a guide-mother gives at first light, the word that says whether the road is open and ends the question; by extension it covers every short instruction she gives on the road, and the sayings below are those. Each is a thing to do. None gives its reason, because none of them is given with one, and a daughter who asks why is answered with the instruction again. They are taught walking, a few to a stage, from the last terrace up, in the order the road meets the need for them, and nothing of them is written down anywhere.

This is the teaching as [Nashken]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, guide-mother of the hearth of [Qarsolt]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, a lineage of [[place-suryadvar|Suryadvar]], gave it to her daughter [Sunash]{.name data-type="being/npc"} in the first month of the open season, on the road from the last terrace up past [[place-oskhelt|Oskhelt]], with a caravan on the road behind them and its master within hearing. A teaching given within a stranger's hearing is given as a stranger may hear it, and where a saying touches what lives in the range it says so and goes no further. At the end of the walk the daughter says the first saying back.

: Ongelshar {#ongelshar}

::: {.verse form=ongelshar lang=en work=ongelshar}
[Nashken walks in front, and Sunash behind her, at the last terrace.]{.direction}

Boots. Hands. Sky./
Look at them in that order. Then open the door.

Read the peak and not the sky./
The sky is over the lowland too.

[Sunash:]{.speaker} Why the peak?

[Nashken:]{.speaker} Read the peak.

At first light look once, and say what you saw./
Do not look a second time to say something else.

If the bird circles the peak at first light, hold the road./
Whatever the cloud is doing, hold it.

Give the morning to the master of the caravan as a thing to do./
He has not asked for anything more. If he has, he has not been answered.

A man who argues on the col is not argued with./
He is not taken. There is no second guide.

Seen is seen. Told is told. Read is read./
Keep them apart as the loads are kept apart on a narrow shelf./
A guide who says told as seen has sold a little of the road.

What your grandmother saw, you were told./
What I saw, you were told. Say it as seen when you have stood there.

Count the dead on the stage you are walking./
Walk past them at the pace of the load.

On a storm day light no fire in the open./
Rope the mules close, under the rock.

The yak knows the shelf. You know the yak./
The lowlander knows neither. Know where he is.

A man who will not give up his load at the steps carries it./
If the steps keep him, count him.

Where the summit priests say the blessing, take it./
We have seen it work. That is enough to know.

A caravan the priests did not bless is not taken down./
Do not take it down.

The warden names the fee. The hearth names the season./
A warden who names the season names it once.

Half of what lands at the far end./
Ask it there, when he has seen what the road left him.

The slab at the head of the water is the mountain's./
Step round it. Do not put a hand on it.

What lives in the range is not named on the road.

[Sunash:]{.speaker} Not to me?

[Nashken:]{.speaker} Not on the road.

Whoever reaches the door is fed./
Do not ask his name. Do not ask what he can pay./
Feed the one who has eaten at your door before. Feed him again.

One man is not fed. You know which one./
Do not open to him. Do not say his name at the door.

If the cord is lost, walk the road./
Walk it with no load, slowly, and look./
Take nobody over it until you have walked it.

If the morning was wrong, it is counted./
Then the next morning is said.

When your knees go, sit, and the road comes to the hearth./
An old guide who asks for one more crossing is told no. Tell him./
Do not tell the party why.

Your brother is taught the ground. You are taught the road./
He walks what you open. He stays where you close.

When the road is yours, say the morning once, and not again./
Your word ends the question.

[At the hail stage Nashken stops on the shelf and turns round.]{.direction}

[Nashken:]{.speaker} Say the first.

[Sunash:]{.speaker}/
Boots. Hands. Sky./
Look at them in that order. Then open the door.
:::
