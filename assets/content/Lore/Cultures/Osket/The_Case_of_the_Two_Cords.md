When a question touches two roads, the [[lore-osketclt|Osket]] lineages concerned meet at a col and argue it, and the **Speaker of the Cols** who convenes them is chosen for the meeting and holds nothing afterward. There is no larger body to appeal to. A meeting therefore settles what the lineages settle and leaves the rest where it lies, and the Osket keep their unsettled cases as tellings, a _tsumshar_ or col-talk, which the hearths concerned give again whenever a new question of the same shape comes before a meeting. The old case binds nobody. It is said so that everyone at the col hears again what was said last time and what was left.

**The Case of the Two Cords**, _Aukh wugh tsumshar_, is the oldest col-talk of the [[place-suryadvar|Suryadvar]] hearths. It is the case of [Orghat]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, road-holder of the hearth of [Utenkhar]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, who went up against his mother's morning word to bring down a party the snow had caught beyond the fork, and brought it down, the hearth of [Qarsolt]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} says, by a way of its own, on which he had no standing. Two of the party stopped, and the cords of both hearths keep them, each at a stage of its own road. The two hearths tell the walking each in its own way, and argue less over whether Orghat did right than over how each of them knows where he walked: the party's account came in the road-pidgin, which cannot say whether a thing was seen, told or guessed.

The telling below is the case as it was given at a meeting on a col of the upper road, convened by the Speaker [Omker]{.name data-type="being/npc"} over a later matter between the same two hearths, which is theirs and is not given here. [Tselbat]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, guide-mother of Utenkhar, tells for Utenkhar, and [Rankel]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, guide-mother of Qarsolt, for Qarsolt. The Speaker's words at the opening and the close are fixed, and are given in the tongue as well.

: The Speaker's opening {#opening}

::: {.verse form=tsumshar lang=en work=opening}
Two roads, one col./
The lineages have come up to it, and they will go home./
What is said here is said by the one who knows it, and she says how she knows it./
The Speaker holds nothing. When the lineages go home, neither does anyone.
:::

## What Utenkhar Tells

I am Tselbat of Utenkhar, and I hold the morning of Utenkhar's road and its count, and what I say of Orghat was told to me by the woman who held it before me, and to her by hers, back to the woman who was his mother. None of it I saw. I say so at the start, so the col knows it, and so that what Qarsolt says after me about how we know it is answered before it is said.

Utenkhar takes its parties up the great road under the village and over, and at the fork, where the road parts for the far edge and for the north-east, it takes them down the far-edge road. Qarsolt takes its parties down the other. Above the fork the two hearths walk the same ground; below it, on the far side, each has its own road and no standing on the other's. That is the shape of it. Everybody at this col has walked one of the two.

In Orghat's time the snow came early one year. It came in the last month of the open season, which it does not do, and it came while a party of Utenkhar's was over the col and two stages down the far-edge road beyond the fork, coming down. They were eleven lowlanders and four men of Utenkhar, with the loads. The men of Utenkhar knew the stages. They brought the party back up to the wall at the head of the far-edge road to wait it out, because the stages below were already under the snow past the knee and the shelf below the wall was not a shelf any longer. They sent nobody down, because there was nobody to send who would get down. That is told.

At the hearth the snow-watcher read the peak at first light, and the guide-mother of Utenkhar said the road was shut.

She was Orghat's mother. Her name was [Hülgh]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, which is thunder. She said it on the first morning of the snow, and on the second, and on the third. The road was shut. Nobody went up. Every morning she said it in the same voice, and every morning the men of the hearth stood in the yard with the ropes and heard it and went in again.

Orghat was the road-holder. You know what a road-holder answers for: every party he takes over his road. That party had been taken over his road, by his men, on his share, under his name. They were not down. He answered for them.

On the fourth morning Hülgh said the road was shut, and Orghat went up.

He took six men and the ropes and as much barley as six men can carry. He did not argue with her in the yard. He did not say that the party came before her word, though it does: the road first, then the party taken over it, then the guide-mother's word on the morning, and every child at every fire on the wall is taught that order. He did not say anything at all. He went out of the yard with the men behind him and went up into the snow, and Hülgh stood in the door and watched him go and said nothing either. That is told, and it is told at Utenkhar the same way every time, and nobody at Utenkhar has ever said that she called after him.

He went up through the snow for eighteen days, on a road of sixteen in the open months. He came to the col and over it and down to the fork, and down the far-edge road to the wall, and the party was there, eleven lowlanders and four of ours, alive, eating the last of the loads.

Here Qarsolt will tell you he did not bring them back the way he came. Qarsolt is right that he did not. I say it first. He did not take them back over the col, because the col had closed behind him in the eighteen days, with the snow lying deeper on it than when he came, and fifteen people with frozen feet do not go up through that. He brought them down. That is the whole of what a road-holder is for.

The far-edge road below the wall was gone under the snow. So he brought them back up to the fork, and from the fork he took them down.

Utenkhar says he took them down the far-edge road, by the high way round the shoulder above the buried stages, which is ours and which Utenkhar's men walked again with their own eyes in the open season after and found his rope-pins still in the rock. That is told, and the rope-pins were seen, by the men who walked it then. Qarsolt says something else. Qarsolt can say it.

Two of the party stopped. A lowlander whose name went down with the party, on the second day below the fork. And [Hülum]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a man of Utenkhar, which is snow, on the third day, going back up the slope for the lowlander's load. The rest came down. Thirteen. They came down to the far edge, alive, with frozen hands, and the lowlanders were taken into the towns, and the half of what landed was taken at the far end, which was not much, because most of it was in the snow, and the four of ours and Orghat and his six came home over the wall the next open season, when Hülgh said the road was open.

The party came down. Thirteen of fifteen. An Osket weighs a deed by the road: it is good if the party comes down. That is what is said, at every fire, and Qarsolt says it at its own fire. I do not ask for reasons. Neither did Hülgh.

Our cord ties Hülum and the lowlander at the second and third stage below the fork on the far-edge road, which is ours, where they stopped. Our cord has them there. Hülgh said them there at the closing, and our cord-keeper tied them where she said.

And on the morning the road opened, Hülgh came to the door of the hearth and read the peak and said to Orghat, in the yard, "The road is open." She said it to him. He walked his road thirty more seasons, and she gave him the morning every season she lived, and he never went up against it again, and he never once said a word about the four days. That is told. That is the hearth's word on what he did. It is the only word Utenkhar has ever had on it, and it is enough for Utenkhar.

That is Utenkhar's.

## What Qarsolt Tells

I am Rankel of Qarsolt, and I hold the morning of Qarsolt's road and its count, and I was told what I say, by the woman before me and hers before her, and none of it I saw either. Utenkhar has said so of itself. Let the col hear that I say it of myself as well, because the matter is how a thing is known, and I would not like to be caught saying told as seen at a col where I am about to say Utenkhar has.

Qarsolt does not say that Orghat should have sat at the fire. Let the col hear that first, too. A road-holder answers for his party. His party was up. He went. Qarsolt would have gone, in the same snow, against the same word, and Qarsolt's road-holder would have been the same fool for it, and the party might have come down. Qarsolt says it would have been the same deed at Qarsolt and Qarsolt would have had to answer for it at Qarsolt's own fire. That is not what we came to the col for, in Orghat's time or now.

What we came to the col for is our road.

Below the fork on the north-east side there is a way down that is the hearth's and no caravan's. Our caravans go down the long branch to the north-east, and no party of ours has ever been taken down this way. It drops off the height in two days to a wall of stones of ours, the low wall, under an overhang, where there is shelter, and from the low wall a man who knows it crosses back under the shoulder to the far-edge road, below its buried stages. I will not say the stages by name before Utenkhar. Utenkhar knows them anyway; that is the matter.

The party said, when they came down to the far edge, that they had come down from the fork in the snow by a quick way, down a slope and along under a rock and to a wall of stones under an overhang, in two days, and slept there. They said it in the road-talk to the people of the towns, who said it to the drovers, who said it to us. You know what the road-talk is. You know what it does not carry. A lowlander who says in the road-talk that he came down by a wall under an overhang has not told you whether he saw it with his own eyes, or was told it by the man in front of him, or thinks so because he was cold and there was a wall. The road-talk does not ask him and he does not say. Utenkhar's whole people was told by lowlanders who could not say how they knew. So was ours. Let the col hear that I say ours was too.

But here is what Qarsolt knows another way. In the next open season, when our men first went down our way off the fork that year, at the low wall under the overhang there were two lying close under it. One a lowlander. One with a rope of yak hair round him, of the twist that is Utenkhar's. They were under the snow that had come off the rock over the winter, and they were seen, by our men, with their own eyes, and the men came back and said so, and the guide-mother of Qarsolt then, whose name was [Shogme]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, had them tied into our cord, at the low wall, because that is where the road had them. Seen. That is not told and it is not the road-talk. It is the eyes of the men of Qarsolt.

So Qarsolt's cord keeps Hülum, a man of Utenkhar, and a lowlander whose name went down with his party, at the low wall on Qarsolt's way off the fork, where our men saw them. And Utenkhar's cord keeps the same two at the second and third stage of the far-edge road, where Utenkhar's guide-mother said they stopped, because Orghat's men told her so. Told. Orghat's men had come down in the snow too.

You have heard Utenkhar say its own men walked the high way the next season and saw Orghat's rope-pins in the rock. I do not say they did not. I say that rope-pins in the rock on the high way are rope-pins, and a man who put rope-pins on the high way in the snow might have pulled back to the fork when the shoulder would not go and come down ours. That is inferred, and I say it as inferred. Utenkhar says it the other way, and that is inferred as well, from pins. Neither of us saw Orghat on any shelf. What Qarsolt saw is two people under our wall.

Utenkhar's road-holder came down our way, without our morning, without our cord, with a party of lowlanders behind him who now know there is a quick way off the fork to the north-east and a wall under an overhang at the bottom of it. That is what we came to the col about in Orghat's time. A road walked by somebody without standing on it is a road a little less held. Every lowlander of that party went home to the towns knowing two days of a way no party of ours has ever been taken down. Nobody sold anything. Qarsolt has never said anybody sold anything, and does not now, and would ask Utenkhar not to hear it said. But nobody gave it either, and it went.

And our cord has two dead at the low wall who are not ours, and we did not take them over. Shogme had them tied. She said, when they were tied, "The road has them. They are ours now." She said it at our closing. We say it at every closing since. They are in our count.

That is Qarsolt's.

## What the Col Heard

At the meeting in Orghat's time the two hearths said what is said above, in more words and with more heat, and the Speaker of that meeting, whose name the tellings do not keep, said the closing words, and the lineages went home. Nothing was settled about the road. Qarsolt did not ask for anything to be paid, because there was no fee on a party nobody had taken over for a fee, and Utenkhar did not offer. Neither hearth untied anything. The two dead have been in both counts ever since, each at a different stage of a different road, and both hearths say them at every closing, each at its own fire.

It is told at both hearths, and has been told at every meeting where a road-holder of one went onto the road of the other, that Orghat and Shogme met once on the col above the fork in an open season, years after, when he was going over with a party and she was coming back from seeing one down. It is told that they stopped on the col, as two parties stop, and that she looked at his boots, and his hands, and the sky behind him, in that order. And it is told that neither said anything, and that both went on. Utenkhar tells it so. Qarsolt tells it so. It is the one piece of the case the two hearths tell in the same words.
