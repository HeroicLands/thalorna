**The Songs of the Salt Pans** are sung by the women of [[place-volsather|Volsater]] on the terraces above the town, where the wind scours the pans dry and the women rake the salt in the long days of summer while the men are out with the boats. They are sung in pairs: one woman sings a couplet, the woman at the next pan sings it back with one word changed, and the whole terrace takes the refrain. A couplet's second line comes back as the first line of the couplet after next, so the song walks forward by going back over itself, the way a rake goes over a pan. The songs have no instrument and no sílador. Nobody pays for them.

There are many, and every summer adds to them and loses some. These six are the ones the terraces sing at the start of every day's raking. The girl in them speaks to her mother, to the sea, to her friend at the next pan, and to nobody; the man is always out. The long sea song of [Lentize]{.name data-type="being/npc"} ([[lore-lastshrine|The Song of the Last Shrine]]) quotes the refrain of the third, and the second carries the sea at her window and the small word of the oath out of the long song; the terraces call the third [Aulira]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s, after the woman in the long song who held a lamp on the cut and did not wave.

The court singers of [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] have the cut songs too, and sing them at table with a lute as lyrics of courtship, the girl a lady at a window and the sea a rival suitor with a better name. On the terraces the sea has no name but the sea.
