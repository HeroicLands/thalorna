**Courtly love** is the code of conduct of the [[lore-provenzianclt|Provénzians]], and its influence runs across the whole [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]]. It governs romance, patronage and the lyric, and it is mostly about the second. It is the precise management of admiration, obligation, discretion and public regard, and a great deal of it passes between people with no romantic interest in one another whatever. It is how patronage is negotiated without either side saying the ugly part aloud. A Provénzian who plays it badly loses real money and real access.

## The Code {#the-code}

The code has no book. It is learned at a patron's door and in the hall of a great house, and every city keeps its own version of the finer points. Its main terms are the same everywhere.

**The vow.** A lover's vow is _tolmesa_. Its twin, _tolmeza_, is a vow made before a notary, and the difference is the code's whole point: a _tolmesa_ cannot be sued on. It binds a person's honor and nothing else, so it can be given where a contract would be ruinous, and broken at the cost of a name rather than a fortune.

**The love name.** A lover or a close friend is given a name no one else may use, and it is used only in private and never in public. A book is public, so no songbook writes one ([[lore-morningname|The Dawn Song of the Morning Name]]). Saying another's love name aloud in company is among the worst offenses the code knows.

**Discretion.** What the code permits, it permits in the forms. A lady promised to another house may be loved as the code allows, in song and attendance and regard, and the contract between the houses goes ahead before the notaries regardless. The code and the contract are kept separate, and confusing them is the commonest error a foreigner makes.

**Regard.** A compliment is a coin, and a direct refusal is a discourtesy. A great house refuses by not answering, and an exact and cutting thing can be said inside the ornament, where every Provénzian in the room hears it.

**Longing.** The feeling at the center of the lyric is _solmere_, the ache of absence for a person, a place or a time that is not coming back. Every Provenzal love song is built around it, and a sílador who cannot make a patron's hall feel it does not keep the patron.

## Patronage in the Forms {#patronage}

The relation that orders a Provénzian life is patron and client, and courtly love is the language it is conducted in. A client courts a patron's favor in the forms of a lover's suit: attendance at first light, a song made for the house, a gift given as though it were nothing. The patron answers in kind with regard, a place in the train or a commission. Neither names a price, and both know exactly what is being bought.

The síladors, the poet-singers of the courtly tradition, are the code's professionals. A great house keeps one, as it keeps a library, and a sílador's song for a patron is a public statement of the house's standing.

## The Courts of Love {#the-courts}

A complaint under the code goes to a **court of love**. A great lady or lord holds one in the hall of a house, sitting with the síladors and the guests the host invites, and hears a complaint that a vow was broken, a love name spoken, a song made to shame or a suit pressed past the forms. The court hears both sides, the síladors give their opinions in verse and prose, and the host gives the judgment.

A court of love has no law behind it. It cannot fine, imprison or annul, and the high courts at [[place-aureliane|Aurelane]] do not hear its judgments. Its only sentence is exclusion. A person the court finds against is not received in the houses that respect it, and in a city where patronage is livelihood that sentence is harder than most a magistrate can pass. A visitor who treats the code as a parlor game is indulged, and then quietly left out.

The courts that matter sit in the capital during the sittings of the [[affiliation-councilpeers|Council of Peers]], when the great houses are all at [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] at once. The most sought this season is the one [[being-itrolaldrvn|Itrola Lédravren]], Princess of Gefavel, keeps in her house there. A princess who lost her standing at court has a court of her own, and the Peers whose voices she needs come to it.
