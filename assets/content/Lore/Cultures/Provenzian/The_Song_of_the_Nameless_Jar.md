**The Song of the Nameless Jar** is a satire sung in the temple squares of the southern cities during the Festival of the Golden Harvest of [[affiliation-olvenia|Ólvenia]], when the merchants close their shops and the squares fill with singers. It is not sung at a table. The singers who sing it have no patron, which is why they can, and a sílador with a patron does not have it in his book. Its maker is not known; the song gives itself to a singer who asked a question at a great house in the spring and was not asked back.

Its subject is the quickened field at [[place-kyvarel|Kivarel]] ([[place-bldfldkyvarel|The Blood-field of Kivarel]]), whose ground grows the best fruit in the barony, and whose fruit goes into the finest conserves in the kingdom under no particular name. The song never names the field, the barony or the baron. It praises the conserve in every stanza, as a guest praises his host's table, and comes back every time to the one question a guest does not ask. Every compliment in it carries the charge.

: The Song of the Nameless Jar {#song}

```poetry {form=satire rhyme=ABAB lang=en}
Here is a jar of the finest fruit in the land,
the gold of it, the weight, the honeyed skin;
a house of standing keeps one near at hand
to show the guest what kind of house he's in.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

Look at the seal: the yard that put it by,
the factor's mark, the year, the weight, the price.
It does not name the field. And why ask why?
A courteous man does not ask questions twice.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

Ask any factor of the yields. He'll say
the weight per tree, how early the fruit will set,
how the blossom held the spring the frost took away
the next field's blossom. Which field? He'll forget.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

The tenants farm it well. They did not choose it.
Their fathers left it to them with the plough.
And who that held a field like that would lose it,
the best ground in the barony? Would you? Not now.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

Twice the Silent Judge's priest came up the vale
to offer them the rite, and place the dead,
and twice they thanked him, and they poured him pale
wine of the west, and twice they shook their head.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

For who can tell, if the dead were placed and gone,
whether the trees would bear the way they bore?
It may be the dead are what the fruit grows on.
The district cannot pay to find out more.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

The hardest workers in the barony
lie under the trees and draw no wage, no bread;
no patron fetched them home; no priest's decree
has placed them; and they pay the rent instead.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

I ate it in a great house at the feast
and asked my host, the way a stranger might,
what ground could grow it. He was not the least
put out. He said: The west has a kind light.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

That was the spring. It is the Harvest now.
I sing in the square and not at a great man's board.
I was indulged one evening, and somehow
was not asked back. Nobody said a word.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.

They bring the best jar to the Bountiful One
at the Harvest, gold on gold, and she is pleased.
The Silent Judge gets the fruit gone soft and done,
which is his due. The rest of his due is leased.
    Where did it grow? That isn't asked at table.
    Pass the jar, and praise it while you're able.
```
