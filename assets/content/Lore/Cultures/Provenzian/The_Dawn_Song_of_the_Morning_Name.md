**The Dawn Song of the Morning Name** is a dawn song of [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]], a lyric of two lovers parting at first light while a friend keeps watch for them on the wall. Three voices sing it, the lover, the lady and the watch, and the refrain says each time that the watch will soon cry the morning. The cry itself comes only in the last line the watch sings. The songbooks of the capital copy it with the prose the copyists set before a song to say why it was made, which they call the reason, and the two are given here as a songbook of Jalnasdal gives them.

The song is **Sirrelure**'s, made in his youth in the capital, before he went on the road and long before the patron he lamented ([[lore-karalzelmnt|The Lament for Karalze]]). The reason says what the song does not: that he gave the lady a love name, which is never spoken in public, and that the watch of Jalnasdal cries the morning from its walls every day as it comes off duty. The reason does not write the love name. Its readers draw their own conclusion from the last line.

: The Reason {#reason}

The reason of this song is that Sirrelure, a sílador of Jalnasdal, whose house this book does not name, loved a lady of a great house of the capital while he was a student at the academy and had no patron. The lady was promised by her house to a house of Kalveza, and the contract was before the notaries. She loved Sirrelure as the code permits, which is to say in the forms, and then beyond the forms, which the code does not permit, and he came to her by night in her house below the cistern stair and went from it before the market opened.

Sirrelure had a friend at the academy who served his year in the watch of the city, and this friend kept the wall above the cistern stair, and swore to Sirrelure that he would hold the morning cry until the last breath he could hold it, and then cry it. The watch of Jalnasdal, coming off the walls at first light, cries the morning from every tower in the one word, and the word is the city's word for morning, and every street hears it.

Sirrelure gave the lady a love name, as the people of the tongue give a friend or a lover a name no one else may use, and he used it only in the dark, as is proper. This book does not write it, since a love name is not used in public, and a book is public. In the song the lady says that the wall cries her name every dawn above the waking street and that nobody who hears it knows whose it is. Let the one who reads understand it as he can.

The contract was sealed at the end of that year, and the lady went to Kalveza and kept her own name there. Sirrelure left the capital, unplaced, and sang in the taverns of the coast and the march until a lord of Pirnebris took him into his train. This is why he was on the road. The song stayed in the capital, and the watch still cries the morning from the walls, and the singers at table sing this song after the hour when the lamps are put out.

: The Song {#song}

```poetry {form=dawn-song rhyme=ABAB lang=en}
THE LOVER
The lamp is low; the lane is going grey;
I hear the first carts on the river road.
Lie still a little. It is not yet day.
The night is lent us, and the night is owed.
    Soon the watch on the wall will cry the morning.

THE LADY
Lie still yourself. The carts are always early.
They bring the fish up for the market stalls.
I know the hour by them. I know it surely.
There is an hour yet before he calls.
    Soon the watch on the wall will cry the morning.

THE WATCH
Friend in the house below the cistern stair,
I keep the wall for you as I have sworn.
The light is on the harbor. In the square
they set the stalls. I cannot hold the dawn.
    Soon I must cry it from the wall: the morning.

THE LOVER
I gave you a name no other mouth may say.
Your house has never heard it, nor has mine.
I say it in the dark, and through the day
I keep it shut, the way a sealed jar keeps wine.
    Soon the watch on the wall will cry the morning.

THE LADY
You gave it me. And every dawn the wall
cries it aloud above the waking street,
and the carters hear it, and the stalls, and all
the city, and none of them knows whose. Go, sweet.
    Soon the watch on the wall will cry the morning.

THE WATCH
Friend, go now by the cistern stair, not the square.
Her steward's at the gate; her herald's out.
I've held it all I can. I cannot spare
another breath. I cry it now. Look out:
    Pramiza, cries the watch, and it is morning.

THE LOVER
I went down by the cistern. Behind me rose
the cry from every tower along the bay,
from every wall, her name, her name, and those
who heard it went on buying fish. And day.
```
