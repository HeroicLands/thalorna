[[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia Region]] has no throne, and it has never needed one. What it has is rivers, and every river has someone who holds it. A _zhuvar_, a river-prince, holds the landings, the mills, the ferries and the grain that comes down a river, and a Velanthian places a stranger by asking whose river the stranger lives on before asking anything else. The answer says what the stranger owes, who feeds him in a bad year, and which hall he would be sent to if he were taken.

## The River and the Prince

A prince is as strong as what his river carries. The princes the neighboring lands deal with most differ in exactly that. One holds the grain lowlands and their granaries. One holds the river-port and the merchant houses that crowd its quays. One holds the frontier forts on the edge of the Grukar forest. Each needs the others. Grain goes down to the port on the port's boats, the forts stay manned on the lowlands' grain, and the port stays safe behind the forts. The rivalry among them is constant and it is checked, because a prince who ruins a neighbor ruins his own supply.

The princely houses are old and intermarried, and a prince is addressed by his river as often as by his name. His power rests on the mint, the toll at the landing, the levy of the villages along the water and the riders of his hall. He does not rest it on a title that anyone outside his river would honor.

## The Hall and the Retinue

A prince keeps a _dvorad_, a retinue of riders and household sworn to his hall. They eat at his table, ride at his stirrup and carry his word to the villages, and a rider who leaves a prince's hall takes nothing with him but his horse and his name. The hall is also where a prince's _pravad_, his judge, sits. Every princely house recognizes one body of customary law, and a _pravad_ rules by it, so that a dispute between two rivers is argued from the same custom on both banks.

On the frontier the prince's riders do not stand the watch themselves. A _tverad_, a paid fort, is garrisoned by wardens who draw a prince's pay and answer to the warden of the fort, and the forts along the edge of the Grukar forest are such posts. Fur hunters go into the margins of that country in the cold months and come out again with their bales. The forts mark where the settled land ends, and the hunters do not go past them in numbers.

## The Hosts

Along the eastern and southeastern steppe margin live the Hosts: farmer-warriors who keep horses and sow grain and have taken from the steppe as much of its way of life as suits them. A Host is led by a _polvod_, called an ataman in the common speech of these pages, and the office passes in the founding line. A Host takes its name from its first _polvod_ or its river, and it keeps its own law, its own muster and its own battle-shrine, a portable altar carried to the gathering ground.

A Host wants three things: to be left alone, to be paid, and to be free to take what the frontier offers. It serves a river-prince for silver and grain, and its _polvod_ can walk away. The princes know it, and it is the fear they live with. A Host that changes sides takes the riders who hold the frontier and the toll roads that feed the princes' mints, and no levy of the heartland can hold the grass without it. A prince pays well, feasts the _polvod_ in his hall and marries into the Host's founding line where he can, because the alternative is to find out what the Host would cost on the other side.

Hosts deal with the steppe tribes across the frontier on their own account. They make their own peace, trade horses for silver and salt, and sometimes make their own war.

## The Monasteries and the Granaries

The grain-belt monasteries are houses of the [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]], large and endowed, with fields, mills and barns. A _bratar_ works the land and keeps the offices, and the _starbrat_ who heads a house is a figure of real weight: he or she sits between princes and Hosts as a mediator that neither side can accuse of taking the other's part. A _zhitnad_ is a granary, and in the grain belt the same word names the monastery that keeps it. The monasteries hold grain against bad years and bad neighbors, and a famine year shows how many people in a prince's lands eat from a monastic barn.

They also hold the letters. Monks copy the liturgies and the court's letters in the [[skill-helnkscrpt|Helónika Script]], the script the courtly tongue is written in, and a village scribe is almost always a monk's pupil. A prince who wants a charter written sends to the nearest house.

## The Merchant Houses

In the grain-belt towns the _torgad_, the merchant houses, live on credit. A house buys the harvest before it is cut, ships it down the river on its own boats and sells it in the south, and the profit is made on the delay between the two. The princes strike the coin, but the houses lend it, and a prince in a lean year owes more to a _torgad_ than any rider's pay. The houses are cosmopolitan and polyglot, and their ledgers and letters of credit cross every border the grain does.

## The Eastern Reach

The communities along the inland edge of the frontier, in the oasis-and-river country, are neither wholly Velanthian nor wholly of the steppe. They speak the [[skill-velanthlng|Velanthian Language]] with words of the [[skill-sowideslng|Sovidur Language]] in it, or Sovidur with Velanthian words for the plow and the mill, and they answer to the side that last had the better year. Their speech carries the vocabulary of a frontier that is crossed in both directions: _koser_, the safe-conduct and the toll for it, _lushik_, the ransom for a captive taken in a raid, _yadak_, the raid itself, and _semuk_, the guest under a tribe's protection.

## Names and the Tongue

A Velanthian carries a given name and a house name. A given name is a thing of the river country with a closing: _Brelovod_, _Dobrogor_, _Zhitena_, _Radora_. A house name says whose line it is or whose seat: _Brelovodich_ is the line of a founder named Brelovod, _Duvavesh_ is the house of the oak seat, _Volkarov_ the house of Volkar. The eastern reach gives some of its names a steppe word, and a name such as _Kodogor_ or _Sovidena_ places its bearer on the frontier as clearly as an accent would.

The courtly and liturgical tongue is [[skill-velanthlng|Velanthian]], unrelated to the tongues of the Nordlands or of Aureldia, and written in a variety of [[skill-helnkscrpt|the Helónika]]. The language note gives its sounds, its words and the lists the names are drawn from.

## Greetings and Manners {#greetings}

The greeting is _dobrogesh_, "good guest". The one who holds the place says it first: a warden on his quay, a keeper at a barn door, a householder at his gate, a prince in his hall. The one who arrives says it back, and in saying it takes the welcome. A stranger who answers nothing has not accepted the greeting. Three gestures go with the word, and each belongs to the people it suits.

**The hand on the chest** is the everyday greeting of villages, quays, markets and merchant houses. The palm lies flat on the breastbone while the word is said. It means that the speaker's word is straight and will be kept as a word given at the ford is kept. A merchant or a boatman offers it to anyone he deals with, and a stranger who gives the word without the hand is taken for a man who has not learned the country. A prince's retinue and the people of a prince's hall use it among themselves.

**The open palm** belongs to the riders of the Hosts and the people who deal with them. The fingertips touch the brow, and the hand then opens outward at shoulder height with the fingers spread and pointing up and the palm forward. It says that the rider sees the other and that his hand is empty of any weapon. On the open grass a rider is a bowshot off before he can be heard, so the newcomer lifts the palm while still at a distance, and the camp's holder answers it with his own and says the word when the newcomer is within hearing. A rider who rides into a muster without the open palm, or with a hand on his sword, is met as a raider.

**The bow** belongs to the monasteries and to formal petition. A _bratar_ or a monastery's _zhitnar_ bows from the waist with the hands folded in the sleeves, and he bows to every arriver to the same depth, a prince, a carter or a stranger, because a house that feeds anyone takes no side. A _starbrat_ is bowed to first by the monks of the house and returns a shallower bow. A petitioner at a prince's hall bows with a hand on the chest, holds the bow until the prince answers, and addresses him by his river before anything else. A prince answers with the word and does not bow.

## What Is Good, and What Succeeds

**Good is the granary full before the snow, the word given at the ford and kept at the ford, and the hall that feeds the people who sit at it. Evil is grain held back from a neighbor's famine, a river closed to a neighbor's boats and a retinue turned against the man who fed it.** A Velanthian weighs a deed by what it does to the river as a whole: a prince who gouges a rival harms the supply he needs next year.

Success is a river that carries more than it did, a granary that outlasts a bad year and a house, a Host or a hall that is still named a generation on. The princes value the Host that stays, the monastery that mediates and the merchant who lends fairly. They value least the neighbor who has to be asked twice.

## The Councils

No office joins the rivers. When the whole country must act, against a great steppe incursion, a Nordmal push in the west, a famine year or a dispute between two great houses, the princes call a council. It has no standing and no executive power. It advises, bargains and coordinates, and it ends when the danger does. The custom that every princely house recognizes is what a council argues from, and it is the only thing that outlasts one.
