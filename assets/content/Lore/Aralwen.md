An **arálwen** is a place where the material world and the spirit world lie thin against one another—close enough that things pass between them. They are the reason [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] has
the reputation it does. Almost all of the known ones are in its mountains and deep forests, and the
[[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] have lived alongside them since before the tribes had a name.

Inside one, the ordinary rules of the world are negotiable. The fae are met there rather than
elsewhere; the [[lore-thekindred|Kindred]] are close and attentive; and the effects reported by
people who come back are as often about time, distance and memory as about anything a sword would
help with. Arcane power runs strongest in them, which is precisely why they are dangerous and why
they are watched.

## Arálwen-Water

Some rivers are arálwen along their length. An **arálwen-water** runs partly in the material world
and partly in the spirit world, and the two courses are not the same river—the banks do not match,
and neither does the distance between two points on it.

These are held to be the strongest arálwain of all. A place is a doorway that stays where it is put;
a river is a road with one end in each world, and it goes somewhere.

## The Wardens

The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] exist for these. Their charge is the sacred geography
of the interior, and their doctrine is graduated: a trespasser who blunders in is warned once and
escorted out, and one who returns, or who desecrates, is dealt with finally. Their authority over an
arálwen is recognized both by tribal custom and by the crown of Élavendre, and it is older than
either—it descends from the Kindred Pact, the compact between the Áelendan and the land itself.

That has put them in open conflict with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]], which regards
the arálwain as the most interesting arcane phenomena in the west and has spent decades sending
surveyors to map them. Research camps are found abandoned. The Ordo deplores the incidents publicly,
tallies its losses privately, and sends more agents.

## Named Arálwain

- **Úqua-Arálwen**—a _slow_ arálwen of deep Terrae resonance, all ancient trees and dryads, whose
  time runs at its own rate: a day inside can be a month outside. Few who enter come out. Its flora
  is not merely large but occasionally mobile and hostile.
- **[[place-ataanwyrd|The At'aan Wyrd]]**—not an arálwen itself but a Kindred that holds one: the
  oldest the Áelendan deal with, counted fae-adjacent, and understood to have crossed the boundary
  long ago or to have been fae-kin from before the tribes came.

## The Name

_Arálwen_ is the [[affiliation-theoldway|Old Way]]'s word, worn down from an older **arallwyn**—"the other place." The doubled consonant simplified, the ending fronted as it does in Tanwen and
Gwalchen, and the stress settled where the acute now marks it. Áelendan shamen use the plural
**arálwain**; the Élavendren court, which prefers not to name such things precisely, says _the thin
places_.
