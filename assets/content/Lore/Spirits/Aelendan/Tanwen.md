- **Kind:** Hearth-kin, of [[lore-thekindred|the Kindred]]
- **Seat:** the great ceremonial hall at [[place-eshalshldg|Eshálosha Lodge]]
- **Territory:** the hall, its fire, and everyone under its roof
- **Posture:** Venerated
- **Met through:** [[skill-tanwensprt|Tanwen Spirit Power]]

Tanwen is the hearth-kin of the great hall at Eshálosha, and the oldest continuously tended fire
in Áelendan country—the Lodge has been inhabited since the earliest Pelwar settlement of the
interior, and the shamen hold that the fire has not gone out in all of it. She is the only one of
the named Kindred that is met indoors, and the only one every Áelendan meets, because every tribe
comes to Eshálosha to gather.

## Guest-Right

Her domain is the peace of the hall. What is under her roof is under her notice: no weapon carried
past her fire, no oath sworn at it and afterward broken, no guest received and then harmed.

This is the reason the Lodge's hospitality is described as generous and exacting in the same
breath, and why a violation—even an inadvertent one—ends a visit and is _not forgiven_. The
Áelendan are not being severe. They are not the offended party and have no standing to forgive on
her behalf. A guest who carries a blade into the ceremonial hall has not broken an Áelendan rule;
they have been noticed by Tanwen, and what follows is between the two of them.

Guests are told this on arrival. Outsiders routinely mistake it for a formality politely
expressed, which is the most common way for a visit to end early.

## Fire That Is Carried

Hearth-kin are unlike every other kind of Kindred in one respect: they do not exist without people.
A river-mother holds her water whether or not anyone comes to it. A hearth-kin comes into being
where people keep returning, and thins where they stop.

The Áelendan therefore reckon their settlements by the strength of the hearth-kin each can hold.

- **Eshálosha** holds Tanwen, of unbroken standing, because it has never been left.
- **Tribal villages** hold their own lodge hearth-kin—real, named within the tribe, and of
  generations rather than ages.
- **Semi-permanent camps**, returned to each season, hold a thin hearth-kin that shamen describe
  as sleeping between uses and needing to be woken. Waking it is the first thing done on arrival
  and takes precedence over shelter.
- **Temporary camps and the small grove shrines** hold none at all, and are not expected to. A
  night's fire is nobody's.

A village is not one place but a permanent hall with several satellites around it—hunting camps
returned to each season, fishing stands, high summer pasture, and the small shrines that are raised
for a purpose and let go. The hall holds the hearth-kin; the satellites hold thin ones or none, and
are worked out of the hall's fire.

Very occasionally two friendly villages share a satellite, and this raises the one question the
arrangement cannot avoid: whose fire is it. The custom is that whoever arrives first wakes the
camp, and the second party is then sitting at a hearth that is not theirs—guest-right in
miniature, a long way from any hall, and binding in exactly the way Tanwen's own hall is binding.
Áelendan who have quarreled elsewhere are notably careful with each other at a shared camp. Those
who are not careful are the reason the custom is remembered.

Where no hearth-kin can be raised, the Áelendan **carry fire** instead: an ember from a permanent
hearth, kept in a covered pot, so that the camp burns under the notice of a hearth-kin that is
elsewhere. A party far from home is understood to be sitting at its own village's fire, at a
distance, and to be bound by its protocols while it does.

When a new village is founded, its fire is kindled from a carried ember and not struck fresh. Where
the ember came from Eshálosha, the new hall's hearth-kin is held to be Tanwen's own, and the tribe
of that village stands in a particular relation to the Lodge ever afterward. This is as close as
the Áelendan come to a political hierarchy, and they would not call it one.
