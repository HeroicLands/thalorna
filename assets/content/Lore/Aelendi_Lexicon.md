# terran_analog: Áelendi is patterned on a conservative mountain branch of a family whose lowland daughters changed fast, with a deep layer of borrowings from a neighbor it shares nothing else with, in the way the Celtic tongues of the uplands kept old sounds and took words from their neighbors. At a gathering in [[place-eshalshldg|Eshálosha]], before anything is decided, a Tradition Keeper stands and recites the names. He begins with the band that crossed the eastern pass and comes down, founder by founder and clan by clan, to the people sitting at the fire, and he does not hurry, because a name left out has stopped existing. Every name he says is an Áelendi word, and so is every word he uses to join them. Half the words for the wood outside came from the Sinalë, and the Áelendan count them as their own.

This is the word-hoard of [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]], the tongue of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]. It is the daughter of ancient Vylari that left first and changed least: it still says the clicked stops the empire's speech gave up, and it lost only the sounds made with the lips. It is also the only daughter that took a whole second layer of words from a neighbor. The [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] of the deep wood taught the band that crossed the pass how to keep the wood, and the words came with the keeping: the trees, the water below the snow line, the veil and the thin places, the rites and the hand the Áelendan write in. The tables give every word with its source, the rules that turn an ancient word or a Sinalë one into Áelendi, and the pieces the names are built from.

The words are arranged by what they say about the people who use them. The longest tables are the Pact, the light and the wood; the shortest is the one on fighting, because the Áelendan fight rarely and only on their own ground.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has six columns:

- **Form** is the Áelendi word as it is cited: a noun in its nominative, an adjective in its masculine, a verb in its infinitive.
- **Source** says where the word came from, as the table of sources below sets out. An inherited word names its ancient Vylari form, a loan names its Sinalë form, and the Form is exactly what the changes make of it.
- **Class** gives the word's kind and, for a noun, its gender.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in Áelendi, which is often not its meaning in imperial Vylari.
- **Built from** is `—` for a word inherited or borrowed whole. Otherwise it names the Áelendi words and suffixes it is made of.
- **Attested** names a page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

Áelendi is written in these pages in the romanization the Ordo's scholars use, which follows the Áelendan scribes' own marks. One mark stands on the first vowel of a word of two syllables or more, because the stress always falls there: an acute where that vowel is short, a circumflex where it is long. A long vowel keeps its circumflex in the first syllable of a word of one syllable too. Nothing marks any later vowel, because no later vowel is long.

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind                     | Sounds like                                                                                           |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `t`, `d`, `k`, `g`      | consonant                | the plain stops; `g` is always hard                                                                   |
| `th`, `dh`, `kh`, `gh`  | consonant                | the same stops with a breath after them, never a hiss: _hothouse_, _madhouse_, _backhand_, _doghouse_ |
| `t'`, `d'`, `k'`, `g'`  | consonant                | the stops closed hard in the throat and released with a click                                         |
| `s`                     | consonant                | always hissed, never buzzed; the tongue's only hiss                                                   |
| `m`, `n`, `l`, `r`      | consonant                | as written; `r` is tapped                                                                             |
| `p`                     | consonant, in loans only | as written, and only in a word that came from Sinalë                                                  |
| `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` | short vowel              | _father_ said quickly, _pet_, _pit_, _pot_, _put_                                                     |
| `â`, `ê`, `î`, `ô`, `û` | long vowel               | the same vowels held twice as long                                                                    |
| `á`, `é`, `í`, `ó`, `ú` | stressed vowel           | a short vowel in the first syllable, which carries the stress                                         |
| `ai`, `au`, `ei`, `oi`  | diphthong                | _aisle_, _house_, _vein_, _coin_                                                                      |
| `ái`, `áu`, `éi`, `ói`  | diphthong                | the same, in the first syllable, which carries the stress                                             |
| `ae`, `áe`              | diphthong                | _ah_ gliding to _eh_; heard only in _áel_, first light, and the names built on it                     |

The tongue has no `f`, no `v`, no `b`, no `h` alone and no `w`: every sound the ancient tongue made with the lips fell away. A Sinalë `v` is said `p`, the one lip sound the Áelendan learned to make for their neighbors' words, and a Sinalë `w` is said `g`. So the yew, _eiva_ in the Sinalë's mouth, is _éipa_ in an Áelendan's.

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel, on one consonant, or on a stop with `r` after it. It closes on a vowel or on the consonants and pairs below; the pairs are what the ancient endings left when they fell.

| Edge                         | Sounds                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Pairs that may open a word   | `tr`, `dr`, `kr`, `gr`, `thr`, `khr`, `ghr`            |
| Consonants that close a word | `k`, `l`, `n`, `r`, `s`, `ks`, `lm`, `nd`, `th`, `rth` |

### Clusters

No more than three consonants stand together, and a pair of letters such as `th` or `k'` counts as one.

| Rule                                   | Count |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Most consonants together inside a word | 3     |

### Stress

The stress falls on the first syllable of every word, and has never moved in the whole of the tongue's remembered life.

| Rule            | Value          |
| --------------- | -------------- |
| Stress falls on | first syllable |

## From ancient Vylari

Eleven changes, in this order, make an Áelendi word of an ancient one. The lip sounds and `h` fell, and the vowels they had stood between ran together: like vowels into one long vowel, unlike ones into the first, lengthened. `y` fell the same way, `z` became `s` and `x` became `ks`. The old genitive plural became the clan ending, its vowel copied from the stem. The masculine nominative ending fell, leaving the bare stem, and the feminine one shortened. Then the stress moved to the first syllable, and every long vowel behind it was said short. So _prêdhâ_ (peace) is _rêdha_, _ghûlmôs_ (a horse) is _ghûlm_, and _sôrêthândis_ (those of one blood) is _sôrethendi_.

| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| A1   | `p ph b w h` → `—`                                                                           | anywhere                   |
| A2   | `y` → `—`; `z` → `s`; `x` → `ks`                                                             | anywhere                   |
| A3   | like vowels merge, long                                                                      | —                          |
| A4   | unlike vowels merge to the first, long                                                       | —                          |
| A5   | `ândis` → `di`                                                                               | word end and after `n`     |
| A6   | `ândis` → `ndi`                                                                              | word end and after a vowel |
| A7   | `ândis` → `Vndi`                                                                             | word end                   |
| A8   | `ôs` → `—`; `â` → `a`                                                                        | word end                   |
| A9   | stress moves to the first syllable                                                           | —                          |
| A10  | `â ê î ô û` → `a e i o u`                                                                    | unstressed syllable        |
| A11  | acute on a stressed short vowel, circumflex on a long one of a word of two syllables or more | —                          |

The clicked stops, the breathed `dh` and `gh` and the long vowels of the first syllable all pass through unchanged. That is what makes Áelendi the conservative daughter: hearing it, a scholar of the empire is hearing the ancient tongue with its lips closed.

## The Sinalë loans

When the band came over the eastern pass, about two thousand one hundred years ago, the Sinalë met them at the lowest pool and set a watch at Thalve, and what they taught was the wood: "You learn it: tree, path, pool. And there are places where it's thin." The words came with the teaching. They are thickest where the teaching was, in the trees, the water below the snow line, the veil and the rites, and they are absent from the herds, the kin, the body, the numbers and the oath, which the band brought with it. A second band of loans came later with the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldirno]], the Sinalë hand. The loans are almost all nouns. No pronoun, number, kin word, part of the body or ending was ever borrowed, and no given name, because names are inherited.

A Sinalë word is said in Áelendi by the changes below. Doubled vowels become long ones; `y` and `ë` become `i` and `e`; `rh` becomes `r`; an `h` before a consonant falls and lengthens the vowel before it, and any other `h` becomes `kh`; `v` becomes `p` and `w` becomes `g`; and the stress moves to the first syllable. A loan noun takes its gender from its last sound: feminine on `-a`, neuter on any other vowel, masculine on a consonant.

### Sinalë loans

| Step | Change                                                                                       | Where              |
| ---- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| S1   | `aa ee ii oo uu yy ëë` → `â ê î ô û î ê`                                                     | anywhere           |
| S2   | `y` → `i`; `ë` → `e`                                                                         | anywhere           |
| S3   | `rh` → `r`                                                                                   | anywhere           |
| S4   | `ah eh ih oh uh` → `â ê î ô û`                                                               | before a consonant |
| S5   | `h` → `kh`                                                                                   | anywhere           |
| S6   | `v` → `p`; `w` → `g`                                                                         | anywhere           |
| S7   | stress moves to the first syllable                                                           | —                  |
| S8   | acute on a stressed short vowel, circumflex on a long one of a word of two syllables or more | —                  |

The Áelendan do not count these words as borrowed. Asked, a Keeper says that they came with the keeping of the wood, and that a thing kept is part of the people who keep it. What the Áelendan refuse are the lowland words and the empire's: for a new thing from below they coin a word from their own roots, and a lowland word said in council is said once and corrected.

## Words

### The Pact, the oath and the keeping

The Pact is never written, so the words for it are the oldest in the tongue and the most carefully said. An Áelendan who swears does not promise to do a thing; he puts himself under _sêmnalis_, a compact between two kins that the whole valley is witness to, and a man who breaks one owes _kûthresa_, a forfeit his kin pays with him. The worst sentence the elders can give is _ênthodis_, the word the empire uses for exile. In the highlands it means being walked past the tree line and not let back, which is a death that takes longer.

The verb at the heart of the field is _tháurathen_, to keep. The empire's speech kept the same word for its palace guard. The highlands kept it for what a Warden does to a spring, a grove or a cairn: stands by it, turns away whoever comes for it, and answers for it to the kin. A keeper is _tháurand_, and his duty, the whole of it, is _tháuralon_.

### Kin, the clan and the name

A clan is its founder's name in the genitive plural, "those of" him, and the word for kin is built the same way: _sôrethendi_, those of one blood. Nobody in the highlands belongs to a household the way a Vylarian belongs to one, under a master who answers for him. The head of a household is _sôrkhand_, a man who speaks for the hearth in council, and the council can overrule him.

A child is _nûrison_ until it is named, and the naming is the event. The genealogies, recited by the Tradition Keepers at every gathering, are lists of names and nothing else, and to have a name in them is to exist. An earned name, _kíramarlen_, is sung once at the gathering after the deed and is the bearer's from then on. A name inherited through a clan is _ârlendi_, the same genitive plural again. The lowland houses that claim Áelendan descent claim it in exactly these words, and the Keepers are the only people who can say whether the claim is true.

### The lodge, the fire and the guest

The lodge is a fire with a roof over it. Every hearth keeps _k'áralson_, the kept fire, which is banked at night and never let die, and a household whose fire goes out borrows a coal from a neighbor and owes for it. Where a guest sits is settled before he is fed: _sîrothathen_ is to give a man his place at the fire, and _sîrothalon_ is the order of the seats, worked out again for every gathering.

Half the courtesy in this field came over the eastern pass from the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]], and the loans are the gentler half. The native _dûmaralon_ is guest-right as a duty: what the hall owes the man under its roof. The loan _éllepirsa_ is guest-right as a welcome, the word the Áelendan use for what passes between them and their neighbors in the wood. _Tárnepi_, bread, is a loan as well, because the first bread the band was given on the far side of the pass was Sinalë bread, and the offering is still made with it.

### Herds, pasture and the high grass

The Pelwar came out of the grasslands, and the highlands kept the grass words when they had little grass left to use them on. _Ghûlm_, the horse, is the same word the empire uses for its cavalry. The move up the mountain each spring, sheep and horses and families together, is _kômrelalon_. In the coastal speech the same word names a country estate with slaves.

### Mountain, snow and stone

The Áelendan live where the snow lies half the year, and the snow word is one they share with the empire: _nélamis_. The coastal speech means a thing seen on the far peaks. In the highlands it is the condition of life from autumn to the thaw, and winter is simply _nélamesa_, the snow-time. A valley is _nêralis_, a tarn in its corrie _thôlmara_, and a torrent in spate _îlaras_, the word the empire keeps for its one great river and declines as a god.

Stone is cut for two purposes, to raise as a boundary and to cover the dead. _Dôrkima_ is a pillar, _âlthokis_ a dressed block, and _grûmalon_ the cut in a hillside where the stones for a ring were taken, which is left open and never quarried again.

### Light, sky and the turning year

Here the two layers of the tongue lie side by side. The sun, the moon, the day and the month are inherited words, the ones the band brought over the pass. ### The fae, the thin places and the wards

Nearly every word here is Sinalë, and the Áelendan do not count a single one of them as foreign. The lore of the veil came with the keeping of the wood, and a thing that is kept is part of the people that keeps it. _Áudhi_ is the veil, _sírnasto_ a thin place where it wears, and _órthi_ a ward. The fae themselves are _pénallanu_, one at a time; the tongue has a plural for them and the highlands do not say it.

The art the empire calls magic is _símme_ here, the Sinalë word, and the Áelendi words for it share nothing with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]]'s. The Ordo's scholars, who come up the valleys to study what the tribes exist to keep, have never been taught these words, and this is the field they most want.

### Rite and the dead

A rite is done the way the Sinalë showed it, so its words are theirs, and the burial of the dead is a rite. _Sélporo_ is death, _ílparo_ grief, and _nélthapa_ the lament sung at the cairn. The Sinalë plant a tree for each of their dead, and the Áelendan have kept the custom and its word, _sélporosalpu_, beside their own cairns. A lingering spirit, one that will not go on, is _áipelo_. The binder who settles one is _náuldinno_, and the highlands keep two or three in every generation and do not speak of them.

### Song, speech and the letters

The Kindred Pact lives in the mouth, and the people who carry it are the Tradition Keepers, the _lôresand_: the word the empire uses for a lecturer at its University, and which here means a reciter, one who says the old words aloud at the gathering exactly as they were said to him. To recite is _lôresathen_; an account of what was done, which in the empire is a history book, is _ôldhemon_, a recitation that is never written down.

Letters are a borrowing, and late. The Áelendan cut a few words in [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thúravardh]], their own rune-row, and a stave of it is _sêmarson_, the word the empire uses for one letter of its script. What they write with ink they write in the Sinalë hand, the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldirno]], which they call _példirno_; its scribe is _példinno_, and a roll of names kept in it _sírnerno_. These words came with the hand, a band of loans later than the rest.

### The Wardens and the border

The Áelendan keep no army and have needed one rarely, and the war words they inherited have shrunk to the size of the border they hold. _Thrûmar_ is the empire's word for a general; here it is the one who leads the Wardens of a valley. _Thûrgeson_, a siege in the coastal speech, is the holding of a pass against whoever is coming up it. The word for peace is the one the whole family shares, _rêdha_, and in the highlands it means the quiet between neighbors more than the end of a war.
