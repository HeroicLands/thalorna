An [[affiliation-osketguides|Ösket]] lineage is a hearth, a herd, a high pasture and one road, and its tongue names each of them in a word of one syllable: _göich_ is the hearth, _pöich_ the yak, _ghauk_ the road held by descent. [[skill-osketlng|Ösket]] is related to nothing spoken in any lowland, and its words owe nothing to Vedyari. This page is the word-hoard of the tongue: its words, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives the Ösket and the places they hold.

A compound sets two words side by side with no linking material, the first saying which and the second saying what: _ghauk_ is the road and _wugh_ a knotted cord, so _ghaukwugh_ is the guide-cord, the record of a road. A personal name is a single word taken from weather, ground, beast or condition, so the words of the second table below are also the names the Ösket give their children.

The sound of Ösket is heavy and closed. A word may end on any consonant, the front rounded _ö_ and _ü_ are the vowels an outsider hears first, and the uvular _q_ is made far back in the throat. Long vowels are not distinguished.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the words the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly those words set side by side.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind      | Sounds like                                                                       |
| ----------------------- | --------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `p`, `t`, `k`           | consonant | the plain stops, unvoiced                                                         |
| `q`                     | consonant | a _k_ made far back in the throat                                                 |
| `b`, `d`, `g`           | consonant | the plain stops, voiced                                                           |
| `ts`, `ch`              | consonant | the _ts_ of _cats_, and _church_                                                  |
| `s`, `sh`, `h`          | consonant | _s_, _ship_, _h_                                                                  |
| `kh`, `gh`              | consonant | a rasp at the back of the mouth, as when clearing the throat, and the same voiced |
| `m`, `n`, `ng`          | consonant | the nasals; _ng_ as in _sing_                                                     |
| `l`, `r`, `w`, `y`      | consonant | the liquids and the glides                                                        |
| `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` | vowel     | _father_, _bet_, _machine_, _for_, _boot_                                         |
| `ö`, `ü`                | vowel     | the lips rounded on an _e_ and on an _ee_                                         |
| `ai`, `au`, `öi`        | diphthong | _eye_, _cow_, and _ö_ gliding to _ee_                                             |

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel, a single consonant or one of the pairs below, and closes on any consonant or one of the pairs below.

| Edge                           | Sounds                                                |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Pairs that may open a word     | `sk`, `st`, `kr`, `gr`, `bl`, `tr`                    |
| Consonants that close a word   | `any`                                                 |
| Pairs that may close a word    | `lt`, `rk`, `rt`, `nt`, `sk`, `lkh`, `rq`, `lq`, `nk` |
| Vowels that may stand together | `ai`, `au`, `öi`                                      |

### Clusters

| Rule                                   | Count |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |

## Making names

**A person** carries one word, most often of two syllables, taken from weather, ground, beast or condition: _Nushang_ is hail and _Yulmüm_ deep snow. The name is not inherited. What is inherited is the road, and a stranger is told the crossing first and the person second: _Sūryadvāra, Yölte_.

**A place** of the Ösket is older than the words on this page: [[place-oskhelt|Öskhelt]] and [[place-tsokhar|Tsökhar]] stand in the register with `—`. The crossings themselves are named in Vedyari, and the [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]] lexicon registers them.
