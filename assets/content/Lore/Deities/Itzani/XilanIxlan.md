Shilan Ish'lan is the Walker of Paths in the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]], the god of travel, trade, journeys, messengers, commerce and communication. His symbol is a footprint marked with a compass of cardinal stars. The [[affiliation-xilanixlan|Faith of Shilan Ish'lan]] serves him, and his ritual is [[skill-xilanixlan|Ritual: Shilan Ish'lan]].

## God of the White Roads

He is the god of travelers and the pathways they walk. The great causeway roads connecting the major city-states, the _ish'lan ch'upul_ (white roads), are understood as literal manifestations of his presence. They are raised above the landscape to prevent flooding, perfectly straight for hundreds of miles, with white stone surfaces that reflect moonlight for nighttime travel. To walk a white road is to walk in the god's own footsteps.

## Worship

The [Ish'lan Tsoqol]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} (traveler's blessing) is performed when caravans assemble for significant journeys. Priests anoint travelers with aromatic oils and provide charms inscribed with the god's glyph, and the blessing includes practical instruction: water sources, bandits and route conditions. A traveler setting out touches the shrine stone at the city's edge and leaves something small, a bead, a feather or a smear of blood from a pricked thumb; on returning he leaves something of the journey itself. Blood is given lightly and often, a thumb at a shrine or a few drops at a crossroads.

In the trading season great traveling festivals bring merchants from dozens of city-states together for ceremonies, trade fairs and the ceremonial races that celebrate his association with swift movement. Young men competing to become professional relay runners undergo trials during these festivals, and those who excel are recruited for the priesthood's message networks.

His priesthood is partially distributed and mobile. Road shrines dot the white roads, each with a resident priest who records travelers, provides refreshment and monitors the road for damage, and reports move from shrine to shrine by relay. Because the priests cross borders continually they are the tradition's habitual mediators.

## Relations

- [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]]: astronomers lay the lines that make a white road run straight, and the road priests carry the astronomers' corrections and observations between observatories.
- [[lore-kanixhakandty|K'anish Ha'kan]]: the cacao trade moves on his causeways and under his protection.
- [[lore-kayikchalixdty|K'ayik Ch'alish]]: the road priests supply the fire-temples with copal, ceiba and mahogany.
- [[lore-piqalkulqatdty|P'iqal Kul'qat]]: the builder-priests raise and repair the causeways, and the allocation of masons between new work and road repair is a standing annual quarrel.
- [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Ts'uqil Ish'bal]]: a traveler who dies on the road is the shrine keeper's charge until the death-walkers can be fetched.
