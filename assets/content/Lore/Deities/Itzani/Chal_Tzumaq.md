## Overview

Ch'al Tz'umaq—"beneath the night sky"—is the underworld of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itzáni]] faith, and its mouths are everywhere in [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]]. It lies beneath every cenote, cave and sacred spring, and the [[place-kankulrgn|K'ankul]] priesthood teaches that the sun itself passes through it each night on its way to rebirth at dawn. It is the domain of [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Tz'uqil Ix'bal]], the death god, and a priest of that faith explains it to a newcomer before anything else: it is not a place of punishment but of passage.

## The Passage

A death-walker of the [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Tz'uqil Ix'bal]] puts it plainly. Ch'al Tz'umaq is a labyrinth of chambers, trials and deceptions, and the soul must find its way through every one of them to reach the celestial realm beyond, where it emerges reborn. A soul that fails is lost and becomes one of the _tz'uqilob_, trapped in shadow. The priests can help, and they sell exactly that help: the **Speaker of the Trials** sits with the body through the night after death and names each chamber in the order the soul will meet it, and each deception in the order it will be offered, while the family listens without speaking. A family that can pay for the full recitation buys its dead a guided passage; one that cannot buys a shorter one. The priests sell memory, not mercy, and even the poorest households beggar themselves to pay.

That is why the gravest sentence the faith can pass is exclusion from the funerary rites. A soul left to face Ch'al Tz'umaq's trials unguided is, in the Ki'ichek reckoning, the one sentence that outlives the body.

## The Cenotes and the Threshold

Every cenote is at once a mouth of Ch'al Tz'umaq and the body of [[affiliation-xaqikhanal|Xaq'ik Ha'nal]], goddess of living waters, and the two priesthoods have settled city by city, over centuries and not without acrimony, which waters serve which rite on which nights. At the turning of the Tz'uqal Cycle, whole communities gather at the cenote mouths through the night for **the Taq'tzuqalil**, the Calling of the Dead, to welcome the recently dead into the underworld, and they bring their children so the children grow up unafraid of the mouth of the world.

The threshold was once guarded by [[lore-pikultzumaqdty|Pik'ul Tz'umaq]], the celestial owl, who concluded that the underworld was a trap rather than a passage and began pulling souls back out of it. The heretics who follow it, the [[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|Serpent Awakeners]], can draw the newly dead back from Ch'al Tz'umaq, and they do; a soul reversed before its trials are done comes back damaged, and the damage cannot be undone.
