Ts'uqil Ish'bal is the Death Jaguar of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]], the master of death, decay and the underworld. His symbol is a jaguar skull crowned with obsidian blades, an owl perched on the brow. The [[affiliation-tzuqilixbal|Faith of Ts'uqil Ish'bal]] serves him, and his ritual is [[skill-tzuqilixbal|Ritual: Ts'uqil Ish'bal]].

## The Jaguar and the Owl

He is depicted as a massive jaguar with eyes that see in absolute darkness, his skull ringed with obsidian blades. On his brow sits an enormous owl, [Pik'ik]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, his sacred companion and the embodiment of his patient aspect. The jaguar kills swiftly and the owl watches as the jungle slowly reclaims what death has taken; together they represent the full cycle of death. Death is not punishment but transition, and decay is not waste but renewal.

His domain is [[place-chaltzumaq|Ch'al Ts'umaq]] ("beneath the night sky"), the subterranean realm beneath every sinkhole, cave and sacred spring. It is a labyrinth of chambers, trials and deceptions through which the soul must navigate to reach the celestial realm beyond. A soul that reaches the other side emerges reborn; a soul that is lost becomes one of the ts'uqilob, trapped in shadow.

## Worship

His priests, the Ish'bal'ob (death-walkers), memorize the funerary codices, and a soul whose family has engaged the priests to perform the correct rites has a far better chance of navigating the underworld. A family that can afford a full recitation buys its dead a guided passage and a family that cannot buys a shortened one: the priests sell memory, not mercy. The Speaker of the Trials attends the body through the night after death, naming each chamber of Ch'al Ts'umaq in the order the soul will meet it and each deception in the order it will be offered, and the family listens and does not speak. Where the water is deep and still the dead are given to it, and elsewhere they are interred in the chamber walls.

At the turning of the [[lore-itsaniclndr#tsuqal|Ts'uqal Cycle]] the Ish'bal'ob perform the [Taq'tsuqalil]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} ("The Calling of the Dead"), a nocturnal ceremony in which communities gather at the mouths of the sacred pools to welcome the recently deceased into the underworld. Children are brought deliberately, so that they grow up unafraid of the mouth of the world.

His temples descend rather than ascend, with great staircases lined with carvings of jaguars and ancestors leading down to pool chambers. [[affiliation-pikob|The Owl Keepers]] (Pik'ob) are the contemplative branch of his service: they tend the Shik'tel K'anal (Sacred Groves) and teach acceptance of dissolution.

## Relations

- [[lore-xaqikhanaldty|Shaq'ik Ha'nal]]: every sacred pool is at once a portal to Ch'al Ts'umaq and the body of the goddess of living waters, and the two priesthoods have settled which waters belong to which rite and on which nights.
- [[lore-tzenultqandty|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]]: the solar god teaches that the cycle must be held and the death jaguar that everything within it is meant to end, and neither branch concedes a word of doctrine to the other.
- [[lore-naliktzuqaldty|Nal'ik Ts'uqal]]: his branch's closest working partner. A dream-keeper who receives a dream of the dead refers the dreamer to the death-walkers.
- [[lore-pikultzumaqdty|Pik'ul Ts'umaq]]: the tradition's most dangerous heresy came out of his household; in the traditional account he cast the owl out.
