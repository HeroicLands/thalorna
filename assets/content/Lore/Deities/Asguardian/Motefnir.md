_**The Maker**—a clay mould and an unfinished casting, work put down before it was done and never taken up again._

**Mótefnir** is the god every craft in the north invokes over the beginning of a piece of work, and the only one of the **Ten** whose own work can be met on a road. A Hofgydja keeping the great hof in [[place-vithgard|Vithgard]] states the faith's whole claim in one breath: he makes living things, he makes each of them once, and he does not come back for them. "Ask me where he works and I will tell you nobody knows," she says. "I have heard the laboratories described by men who swear they have stood in one. I have heard they are halls, and I have heard they are nowhere at all. What I can tell you is what comes out of them, because it walks."

The ladder is the ordinary one. A Blótmadr attends the blót and sets a tool on the stone before the rite; a Hofsmadr is given to a hof and keeps its workshops as well as its floors; a Godi or Gydja is ordained to keep the hof's rites and to bless a piece of work begun in it; a Hofgodi or Hofgydja consecrates hofs, ordains their godar and performs the great blót at a season's turning. Nothing of the land and no pontiff stands above that. What is unusual is the offering: a hof of Mótefnir takes work rather than cattle, and the shelves behind its altar hold the first work of every craftsman who has ever sworn there.

The [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|Dreadspawn]] are his, and the faith neither denies them nor apologizes for them. Each is made whole and made once; none of them breeds; many were let go before they were finished, which is the part his clergy teach as doctrine rather than embarrassment. "He is not careless," the Hofgydja says. "He is finished with a thing the moment he has learned what it had to teach him. That is not the same as cruelty, and it is not kindness either." Other halls draw the comparison with **Svartbrandr** and are corrected for it: the Destroyer unmakes on purpose, and Mótefnir unmakes nothing—he simply stops, and what he stopped working on keeps going.

What devotion costs is the standard a craftsman then has to hold himself to, which the faith sets higher than any guild does and enforces by refusing the blót to a man who has knowingly sold bad work. What it is held to win is not skill, which is earned, but the moment a maker recognizes the shape a thing wants to take. The standard telling of [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] gives him no death and no adversary, and his clergy decline to supply either. "The great halls can each name the end of their own god," the Hofgydja says. "He has not been told, or has not said. What I teach is that a world kindled out of the coals will need everything in it made again, and that somebody has to be left who knows how."
