_**The Healer**—sheaf of wheat and herb bundle, a peace that still has to be worked for every year it holds._

A valley infirmary's Gydja in [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]]'s passes keeps the plainest theology of the **Ten**: **Fródvin**'s peace is not the absence of war but the presence of a harvest, and the two are related only in that a valley fed well enough has less reason to raid its neighbor. "People hear peace-god and think I bless something that was already going to happen," she says. "I bless the grain that would rot in the ground without a hand on it, and the wound that would kill without one on it too. Neither waits for the god. Both wait for the hand."

Her [[affiliation-frodvin|Faith of Fródvin]] keeps no grand temples; its rare hofs are built onto the infirmaries that are the clergy's real work, and the blót is held outdoors where the fields or the sick can be reached without a wall between the god's attention and the thing it is meant for. A Blótmadr shares the harvest-offering and asks nothing further; a Hofsmadr labors at the herb-lore and the sick for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Godi or Gydja is ordained to keep a valley infirmary's rites and tend its sick in her own right, a clan member's equal under the land's own law. Nothing above a Hofgodi or Hofgydja presides, and the office belongs to whoever has consecrated the infirmaries of a whole district rather than to any single great hof.

The [[affiliation-gronverdir|Green Wardens]] are the faith's own paradox made visible—warriors sworn to a god of peace—and the Gydja does not find the pairing strange. "A Warden does not protect peace by refusing to fight. He protects it by fighting only what threatens it, and that is exactly what I do with a knife at a sickbed—I cut only what the wound requires and stop." The ordinary faithful ask for no more than the [[affiliation-frodvin|Blessing of the Fields]]; those seeking the god's particular favor undertake the [[affiliation-frodvin|Healing Trial]], curing a grievously hurt creature with nothing but what the land itself provides, or the [[affiliation-frodvin|Peacewalk]], bringing two feuding households to a settlement neither wanted to reach alone.

What devotion costs is years at a sickbed or a furrow with no guarantee either will be thanked for it; what it wins is a harvest that holds through a hard winter and, for the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]]' purposes, one fewer reason for the ting to call a muster. At [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] Fródvin goes down fighting [[affiliation-svartbrandr|Svartbrandr]] himself, and the valley hof does not pretend the ending is anything but a loss. "He loses to the thing that burns what he grows," the Gydja says. "I would rather my god lose that fight than never have fought it."
