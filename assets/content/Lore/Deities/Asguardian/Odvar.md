_**The All-Father**—raven feather and runestone, an eye paid away at a well for the water in it._

[[place-odholm|Ódholm]]'s rune-hall holds that **Ódvar** is the oldest of the **Ten** and the one who went looking for what the others simply knew. His is a seer's fit rather than a birthright: bought at **[[lore-minnir|Minnir]]'s well** with his own eye, and carried since in a face that sees less than it understands. His [[affiliation-odvar|Faith of Ódvar]] calls this the pattern every initiate repeats in smaller coin—a vigil kept, a comfort given up, a question answered only after its price is paid—and teaches that wisdom bought cheap is not wisdom, only information.

[[lore-hugvin|Hugvin]] and [[lore-munvin|Munvin]], his two ravens, are read two ways inside his own hall. The rune-casters hold them literal: thought and memory made bird-shaped, flown out each dawn and returned each evening with news a god who cannot walk among men has no other way to gather. Acolytes are taught the stricter reading only after ordination—that a rune-caster who waits on a raven's report has stopped doing the work the runes exist for, and that the birds are a teaching figure for the unordained rather than Ódvar's actual method.

Ódholm keeps the only rune-hall every **Nordheim** hof of the faith acknowledges as the first among them, and the rite its Hofgodi will not delegate is the **Rite of [[lore-minnir|Minnir]]'s Well**: a draft taken from a chalice that stands for the well itself, at the one hour of the year the rune-staves are recast for the coming season. "A man asks me what Ódvar knows that he does not," the Hofgodi says, "and the honest answer is nothing. He paid for what he knows, and most men will not pay." Below him the clergy keep the ladder every blót-faith of the Ten keeps: a Blótmadr shares the mead and asks nothing further, a Hofsmadr labors years at the rune-lore before any claim on the priesthood, and a Godi is ordained to cast the runes and keep a rune-hall's rites in his own right, a clan member's equal under the law of the land.

The bargain runs harder at the top than at the bottom. Where the [[affiliation-ordrstrmspkrs|Storm-Speakers]] test a postulant for a gift he already carries, the **Order of the Raven** and the **Order of the All-Seeing Eye** ask years at the rune-halls with no promise the gift is there to find, and most who enter leave Hofsmadr and rise no further. What the years buy is not power over wyrd but its shape: a rune-caster reads the current a choice will run in, never the choice itself. What a man is owed for an honorable death is a seat among the **Valdrengir** in [[place-valsalhall|Ódvarshöll]], training for a battle his own god has already told him is lost.

[[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] is where Ódholm's teaching turns grim rather than triumphant. Ódvar went to [[lore-minnir|Minnir]]'s well to learn what the world's end would cost, and what he learned was his own death: the wolf that slips its chain on that day takes him before any other god falls. His clergy do not soften this partway through a postulant's vigil. They teach it as the reason the vigil is worth keeping—a god who paid an eye to learn his own death and kept his hall open anyway is the pattern Ódholm asks every rune-caster to match, in whatever smaller coin his own life can offer.
