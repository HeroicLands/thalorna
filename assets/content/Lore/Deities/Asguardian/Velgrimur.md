_**Velgrímur, The Trickster**—serpent skin and shadowed glass, a favor asked quietly and repaid the same way._

**Velgrímur**'s hovs are hidden in the ordinary sense a Nordman understands hiding: tucked into the close-built quarters of [[place-malagna|Malagna]]'s port towns, known to those who need to know and to nobody else, and kept that way by a rule the [[affiliation-velgrimur|faith]] enforces harder than any of its rites. "The other nine ask a man to prove something to be admitted," the hov's Gudja says. "I ask only that he not be the reason we are found. Everything after that is negotiable. That is not." A man who causes a hidden hov to be discovered is refused the blet outright, cut from the clergy and from the secrecy that was the only thing protecting him in the first place.

A Bletmadur shares an offering most of the faithful never speak of beyond the hov's own walls; a Hofsmadur is given young and labors at the hov's secrecy for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Gudi or Gudja is ordained to keep a hidden hov's rites outright. A Hofgudi or Hofgudja consecrates the hidden hovs across Malagna's ports and ordains their gudar, and nothing of the land or any pontiff reaches above that—a ladder identical in shape to every other blet-keeping faith of the Ten of the [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian Pantheon]], run entirely in rooms the Ten's own law pretends not to notice.

[[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] keeps the god close through every saga where the two travel together, and his own faithful's open distrust of Velgrímur is a fact his hov's priesthood takes as a compliment rather than an insult. "They are right to watch him," the Gudja says of Thrunvald's clergy. "A god who is only ever useful when he can be seen coming is not the god I keep." The faith holds, as the rune-priests of other halls also teach, that the world needs the cunning that bends as much as the strength that breaks, and that a pantheon without Velgrímur in it would be a poorer and more brittle thing.

What devotion costs is a life that cannot be lived openly and a trust that has to be earned back after every scheme that goes wrong; what it is held to win is protection for exactly the kind of work no other faith of the Ten will bless—the lie told to save a household, the theft that feeds one, the infiltration that ends a siege without a battle. [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] is where the faith's own account turns darkest, and the Gudja does not flinch from it: the pantheon holds that when the cunning finally runs out, Velgrímur stands with [[lore-svartbrandurdty|Svartbrandur]] against his own kin. "I have heard every other hall's reading of that line," she says, "and I do not have a better one to offer in its place. I only know that he has not done it yet, and that his faithful keep honoring him regardless of what the end is supposed to hold."
