_**Solrún, The Golden One**—rose petal and silver coin, a wealth measured by how much of it has already left the hand that held it._

A feast-hall's Hofgudja in [[place-malagna|Malagna]] keeps the faith's one rule plainly stated: **Solrún** does not bless the gold a man keeps, only the gold he gives away, and standing among her faithful is read off a household's generosity rather than off its coffers. "Any fool can grow rich," she says. "What I watch for is who grows rich and still gives the feast." Her [[affiliation-solrun|Faith of Solrún]] names its highest rank, Hofgudi or Hofgudja, for women alone, and the hall's own Hofgudja does not treat this as an honor owed to her sex so much as a judgment on the work itself. "Giving-away is a woman's trade in most halls before it is ever a priest's," she says, "and the faith only wrote down what every bundi's wife already knew."

A Bletmadr shares the meal and the gold given away at her feast-halls and asks nothing further; a Hofsmadr labors at a hov's stores and rites for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Gudi or Gudja is ordained to keep a feast-hall's rites and the giving-away of its gold, a clan member's equal under the land's own law. The [[affiliation-hringsystur|Hringsistur]], her Ring-Sisters, guard both the halls and the gold that passes through them, sworn to the same bargain the Hofgudja preaches: make wealth, and give it away.

Her faith teaches that half of [[lore-odvardty|Odvar]]'s honored dead go instead to Solrún's own field, and the Hofgudja is plain that this is no lesser portion—the woman dead in childbirth, the oath-keeper dead with his word intact, the craftsman killed defending his work, none of them chosen by a battlefield, all of them seated at [[place-solvangrhall|Sólrunsholl]] on equal footing with anything [[place-valsal|Valsal]] holds. What the faith costs the ordinary household is a portion given away that it could have kept; what it wins is a name remembered for the giving rather than the keeping, and a seat on [[place-solvangr|Solvangr]] for a life that never needed a battle to earn it.

Her own fate at [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] is the one the skalds do not sing with any confidence, and the Hofgudja will not pretend otherwise. "The tellings give Odvar and [[lore-thrunvalddty|Thrunvald]] an ending because the tellings need a shape," she says, "and they go quiet on Solrún because nobody agrees, and I would rather my faith say so than invent her an ending to match the men's." What holds regardless is the morning the field empties: whatever becomes of her, her half of the dead stand in the same line as the other.
