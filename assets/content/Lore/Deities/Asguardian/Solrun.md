_**The Golden One**—rose petal and silver coin, a wealth measured by how much of it has already left the hand that held it._

A feast-hall's Hofgydja in **Malagna** keeps the faith's one rule plainly stated: **Sólrún** does not bless the gold a man keeps, only the gold he gives away, and standing among her faithful is read off a household's generosity rather than off its coffers. "Any fool can grow rich," she says. "What I watch for is who grows rich and still gives the feast." Her [[affiliation-solrun|Faith of Sólrún]] names its highest rank, Hofgodi or Hofgydja, for women alone, and the hall's own Hofgydja does not treat this as an honor owed to her sex so much as a judgment on the work itself. "Giving-away is a woman's trade in most halls before it is ever a priest's," she says, "and the faith only wrote down what every bóndi's wife already knew."

A Blótmadr shares the meal and the gold given away at her feast-halls and asks nothing further; a Hofsmadr labors at a hof's stores and rites for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Godi or Gydja is ordained to keep a feast-hall's rites and the giving-away of its gold, a clan member's equal under the land's own law. The **Hringsystur**, her **Ring-Sisters**, guard both the halls and the gold that passes through them, sworn to the same bargain the Hofgydja preaches: make wealth, and give it away.

Half of **Ódvar**'s honored dead go instead to Sólrún's own field, and the Hofgydja is plain that this is no lesser portion—the woman dead in childbirth, the oath-keeper dead with his word intact, the craftsman killed defending his work, none of them chosen by a battlefield, all of them seated at [[place-solvangrhall|Sólrúnshöll]] on equal footing with anything **Valsal** holds. What the faith costs the ordinary household is a portion given away that it could have kept; what it wins is a name remembered for the giving rather than the keeping, and a seat on [[place-solvangr|Sólvangr]] for a life that never needed a battle to earn it.

Her own fate at [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] is the one the skalds do not sing with any confidence, and the Hofgydja will not pretend otherwise. "The tellings give Ódvar and **Thrúnvald** an ending because the tellings need a shape," she says, "and they go quiet on Sólrún because nobody agrees, and I would rather my faith say so than invent her an ending to match the men's." What holds regardless is the morning the field empties: whatever becomes of her, her half of the dead stand in the same line as the other.
