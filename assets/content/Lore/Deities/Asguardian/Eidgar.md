_**The Just**—iron scale and oath ring, a law that costs the judge before it costs the judged._

**Eidgar** paid his own hand to chain **[[lore-vetrulfr|Vetrúlfr]] the Winter-Wolf**, in the age before any ting met to recite the deed, and his [[affiliation-eidgar|Faith of Eidgar]] reads the sacrifice as the whole of its theology in one image: a just binding costs the binder first, or it is not a binding anyone should trust. The **Hofgodi of Lögstead** puts it without ornament. "A man who judges and loses nothing by the judgment has not judged anything," he says. "He has agreed with himself."

**Lögstead** is where the kingdom's own ting sits, and the hof's blót is sworn into the same oath the assembly swears over it—the offering and the law bound together at one hearth. A Blótmadr shares that meal and nothing further is asked of him; a Hofsmadr labors at the hof's observances for years before any standing in the priesthood that judges there; a Godi is ordained to keep the hof's rites, swear its oaths, and judge in Eidgar's name, a clan member's equal under the land's own law. The faith's Hofgodi consecrates the hof itself and ordains the godar who serve under him, and nothing of the land or any pontiff reaches above that.

The hof's priesthood is not the [[affiliation-eidhond|Order of the Sworn Hand]], though the two keep the same roof at Lögstead and the same god. "**The Sworn Hands** ride to where the ting cannot reach," the Hofgodi says, "and I keep the ting that can. A knight enforces a ruling with his own sword; I bind the oath the ruling rests on, and that is the older and, I will say it plainly, the harder work." An Asguardian oath is sworn in public, before witnesses and under the god's own attention, and breaking one is held the gravest offense the faith recognizes—worse than murder, because a broken oath corrodes the trust an entire hall is built on rather than ending one life inside it.

What the faith asks of those seeking Eidgar's particular favor is the **Oathkeeper's Challenge**, holding to a difficult oath under real pressure to break it, or the **Sacrifice of the Hand**, giving up some real thing to prove the willingness the god himself once proved. What it promises is a hof's protection for a dispute honestly brought, and for those who die keeping their word, a place among the honored dead.

At [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]], Eidgar meets the adversary chained against this day, and both are destroyed together. Lögstead's priesthood does not call this a tragedy any more than Eidgar's own sacrifice was one. A binding that holds until the binder dies defending it is, in the hof's own reckoning, the only kind of binding Eidgar was ever going to keep.
