_**The Rime-Giant**—rime-scored bone and a chip of grey stone, a god who is not a person and does not pretend to be one._

An Adherent attached to **Holafell** for most of a long life gives the flattest theology any faith of the **Ten** teaches: **Hrímthur** is not a maker, never was one, and grants nothing because there is nothing in him shaped to grant anything. "The other nine have a god who listens," the Adherent says. "I have a god who is listened to, in the sense that the ground is listened to when a blade cuts into it. He does not answer. He was never built to." The world, on this account, was cut from a living giant's body in an age no telling claims to have witnessed, and what holds the two pieces of that act together—the ground and the gods who stand on it—is that the cutting is renewed rather than left to scar over.

The faith has no ladder to climb, only two standings and neither of them conferred by anyone. An Admirer holds the Rime-Giant's work in awe without joining anything, since there is no rite to enter and nothing is lost by staying outside one. An Adherent has attached himself to one of the scattered factions tied to Holafell, to the **Northern Hall in Targud**, or to one of the hörgar between them, and does that faction's work on its own terms—whatever standing it claims for what he has made or understood, since the roles here are "informal, fluid, and claimed rather than conferred" in the faith's own words, and no office sits above any of them to say otherwise.

What a faction does, at bottom, is keep the wound open on purpose. **The Renewal of the Wound** is cut into Holafell's rock at the turn of winter by whichever adherent the Holafell faction has put forward that year, together with as many of the [[affiliation-thursborn|Giant's Children]] as have traveled to see it done; the **Naming of the Stone** counts a hof's grey chips aloud against the roll of its dead and returns them to the floor. Hrímthur names no divine servants, keeps no messenger, and his Adherent takes a certain grim satisfaction in saying so. "Anything claiming to speak for him is speaking for itself," he says. "I have met men who tried. I have outlasted all of them."

What the faith costs is counted in cold rather than in coin: the **Cold Held**, a night and a day on bare rock above the snowline without fire, or the **Walk to Holafell** made on foot in winter by the high ground rather than the easier valley road. What it is held to win is not favor, since Hrímthur favors nobody, but a closeness to the one true thing under every other faith's claims—that the ground the Ten stand on, and the hall any god keeps, was built by a violence nobody has stopped committing. "I am not troubled by that," the Adherent says, "and I have stopped expecting anyone outside Holafell to understand why."
