_**The Destroyer**—ember and volcanic rock, a fire that is going to be struck eventually and had better be struck on purpose._

A forge-hof's Hofgodi in [[place-norgaad|Norgaad]] keeps the one distinction the rest of the pantheon insists on and his own faith works to earn: **Svartbrandr** stands among the **Ten**, honored rather than merely feared, because what he burns away is something the other nine gods need gone. "[[lore-nahilddty|Náhild]] hates that life exists at all," the Hofgodi says. "I do not serve that. Fire does not hate the ore it is burning the dross out of. It only burns." The claim is the faith's own and not the pantheon's settled judgment—Svartbrandr is, after all, the one who sets [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]]'s fire in the end—but it is the claim that keeps his clergy inside the Ten's law rather than outside it with Náhild's.

A Blótmadr shares a forge-hof's ember-offering and asks nothing further; a Hofsmadr labors at the bellows and the [[lore-fireweeks|Fire-Weeks]] for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Godi or Gydja is ordained to keep a forge-hof's rites and its Fire-Weeks outright. A Hofgodi or Hofgydja consecrates the forge-hofs, ordains their godar, and performs the great blót and the Fire-Weeks at a season's turning, and nothing of the land or any pontiff presides above that. The Fire-Weeks are the faith's defining ordeal, the [[affiliation-svartbrandr|Ashen Walk]] across hot coals that a postulant either survives on his own feet or does not undertake again, and the forges themselves do double work the Hofgodi will not let anyone forget—a blade quenched at a Svartbrandr hof is a weapon and a sacrament in the same stroke.

[[lore-eidgardty|Eidgar]]'s faith stands against his in every telling the skalds keep, order against the fire that ends it, and the Hofgodi accepts the framing without softening it. "He is right to oppose me," he says of Eidgar's clergy. "Something has to. I would think less of the faith if it did not." What devotion costs here is nearer and plainer than most—the [[affiliation-svartbrandr|Firewalk]] undertaken barefoot, the [[affiliation-svartbrandr|Flame's Trial]] survived in a volcanic waste with no guarantee of rescue—and what it is held to win is a strength proved rather than merely claimed, and standing among the warriors the god favors in the only coin that faith recognizes.

At [[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]], Svartbrandr rides out of [[place-eldheim|Eldheim]] with his fire-kin at his heel, cuts down [[affiliation-frodvin|Fródvin]], and burns what is left of the world before the ash of [[lore-heimsask|Heimsask]] falls. The Hofgodi will not call this victory, exactly, but he will not call it anything else either. "The fire was always going to be struck," he says. "My faith exists so that when it is, it burns for a reason rather than for nothing."
