_**The Despiser of Life**—obsidian shard and the [[lore-ginnauga|Eye of the Void]], spoken of nowhere she can overhear it praised._

Every Asguardian kingdom proscribes **Náhild**'s clergy, and the nine other faiths of the **Ten** agree on almost nothing about her except that the ban is deserved. What follows is her own Godi's account, given on the understanding that his hof's location stays with him and dies with him if it must. "She is said to hate life," he says, "and that much the other nine have right. What goes unsaid, because the pantheon's own doctrine will not permit it, is that her hatred is the honest half of a bargain the other faiths only pretend to have settled."

A man who turns to her becomes Her Offerer, trusted with a hidden hof's secrecy but taught none of its observances; years on, if he has kept the secret, he is raised to Her Initiate and taught them. Her Godi or Gydja takes the sacrifices and teaches the rites that make an offerer an initiate, and holds the faith's one standing above a layperson—there is no fourth rank, no great public blót to crown it, because a hof that cannot be found cannot hold one. A man who names a hidden hof, an initiate, or a godi to outsiders becomes the Betrayer, and the secrecy that sheltered him owes him nothing further.

[[affiliation-nahild|Her faith]] holds that [[place-nulthey|Nulthey]], where the unworthy dead wander and fade, is not a punishment but a rest, and that the other nine faiths' doctrine of a self-inflicted slow death is a story the honored dead's own halls need in order to keep recruiting for a war already lost. "[[lore-odvardty|Ódvar]]'s priests call the island unworthiness consuming itself," the Godi says. "I have stood on its shore. Nothing there is consuming anything. It is simply quiet, and quiet is what the rest of the pantheon is most afraid to offer anyone." Whether the island is mercy or diminishment is not a question known workings of the world answer; it is the one point on which Náhild's clergy and everybody else's part company entirely, each side certain and neither able to produce the dead as a witness.

What the cult costs is a life spent in hiding and a death, if discovered, with no faith's hof willing to claim the body. What it is held to win—by the cult alone—is the only mercy the pantheon offers a soul that failed at everything the other nine faiths measure. Her clergy perform the [[affiliation-nahild|Walk of the Dead]] regardless of what any of that costs the living: a working that takes the spirits of warriors who would otherwise be gathered to [[place-valsal|Valsal]] and turns them onto Nulthey's shore instead. The Godi does not dress this up as anything but what the other faiths call it. "Robbing a brave man of the hall is the whole of the rite's purpose," he says. "I have performed it. I would perform it again."
