_The Trickster—serpent skin and shadowed glass, a favor asked quietly and repaid the same way._

Vélgrímr's hofs are hidden in the ordinary sense a Nordman understands hiding: tucked into the close-built quarters of Malagna's port towns, known to those who need to know and to nobody else, and kept that way by a rule the faith enforces harder than any of its rites. "The other Nine ask a man to prove something to be admitted," the hof's Gydja says. "I ask only that he not be the reason we are found. Everything after that is negotiable. That is not." A man who causes a hidden hof to be discovered is refused the blót outright, cut from the clergy and from the secrecy that was the only thing protecting him in the first place.

A Blótmadr shares an offering most of the faithful never speak of beyond the hof's own walls; a Hofsmadr is given young and labors at the hof's secrecy for years before any claim on the priesthood; a Godi or Gydja is ordained to keep a hidden hof's rites outright. A Hofgodi or Hofgydja consecrates the hidden hofs across Malagna's ports and ordains their godar, and nothing of the land or any pontiff reaches above that—a ladder identical in shape to every other blót-keeping faith of the Ten, run entirely in rooms the Ten's own law pretends not to notice.

Thrúnvald keeps the god close through every saga where the two travel together, and his own faithful's open distrust of Vélgrímr is a fact his hof's priesthood takes as a compliment rather than an insult. "They are right to watch him," the Gydja says of Thrúnvald's clergy. "A god who is only ever useful when he can be seen coming is not the god I keep." The faith holds, as the rune-priests of other halls also teach, that the world needs the cunning that bends as much as the strength that breaks, and that a pantheon without Vélgrímr in it would be a poorer and more brittle thing.

What devotion costs is a life that cannot be lived openly and a trust that has to be earned back after every scheme that goes wrong; what it is held to win is protection for exactly the kind of work no other faith of the Ten will bless—the lie told to save a household, the theft that feeds one, the infiltration that ends a siege without a battle. Aldarlok is where the faith's own account turns darkest, and the Gydja does not flinch from it: the pantheon holds that when the cunning finally runs out, Vélgrímr stands with [[affiliation-svartbrandr|Svartbrandr]] against his own kin. "I have heard every other hall's reading of that line," she says, "and I do not have a better one to offer in its place. I only know that he has not done it yet, and that his faithful keep honoring him regardless of what the end is supposed to hold."
