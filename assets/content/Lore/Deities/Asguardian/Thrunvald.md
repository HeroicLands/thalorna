_The Thunderer—hammer stone and sea shell, the storm that reaches a coast before the raiders do._

Thrúnvald is held to be the strength the Ten put between the ordered world and whatever presses against it: giants, sea-chaos, and the raw force that would otherwise go unanswered. His hammer, Thrúnhamarr, is both the weapon that breaks that pressure back and the tool his [[affiliation-thrunvald|Faith of Thrúnvald]] uses to bless a ship before it clears the harbor mouth—the same object doing both jobs, because the faith draws no line between war and the voyage that war so often turns into.

Thrumufjall, the seamount hof on the Nordheim coast, is where that blessing is given its fullest form, and the Hofgodi who keeps it does not share the headland with a rival so much as with a second body entirely: the [[affiliation-ordrstrmspkrs|Storm-Speakers]] train on the same exposed summit, but a Speaker's weather-craft and a Hofgodi's rites answer to different vows. "A Speaker reads a storm and may call one," the Hofgodi says of the arrangement. "I offer the one that is already coming, and the ship that sails out blessed is no safer for it if the crew forgets why the blessing was asked." Below him the clergy keep the faith's shared ladder: a Blótmadr shares the sea-blessing at a coastal hof, a Hofsmadr labors at its nets and storm-rites before any claim on the priesthood, and a Godi is ordained to keep the hof's rites and bless its ships outright.

What the blót asks of the ordinary faithful is the Hammer's Blessing before a voyage or a fight; what it asks of those who seek the god's particular favor is harder—the Hammer's Trial, carrying a weighted hammer to exhaustion, or the Iron Vigil, standing a stormy night guarding a sacred site unsheltered. What it promises in return is Thrúnvald's protection on the water and, for a death met honorably in the god's own work, a place among the Valdrengir.

Thrumufjall's priesthood keeps one relationship the rest of the pantheon finds stranger than any doctrine: Vélgrímr rides beside Thrúnvald in a dozen sagas, rescues him as often as he embarrasses him, and is kept close by the god himself even as his own faithful will not extend the same trust. "The rune-priests of other halls say he is necessary," the Hofgodi allows, "and I have no quarrel with their reading of the myth. I have a quarrel with a man I cannot see coming, and so does every crew that has ever trusted him." The suspicion is the clergy's own, and Thrúnvald's hall does not pretend otherwise.

[[lore-aldarlok|Aldarlok]] gives the god a death as blunt as his hammer. He strikes down **Heimsormr** the World-Wyrm, walks nine steps from the blow, and dies of the wyrm's venom before a tenth. Thrumufjall's priesthood does not read this as a defeat to be explained away—nine steps after killing the thing that killed him is, in the hof's own accounting, the whole of what a victory against a doom already fixed can look like.
