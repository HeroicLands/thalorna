_The Flame of Renewal — a lean, burning figure standing within a ring of broken chains._

Rásikara is fire and the transformation fire compels — the burning that strips corruption away and the kindling that starts new growth. Where [[affiliation-mahajaya|Mahájaya]] preserves and [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyālendra]] shapes, Rásikara breaks, and Vedyari theology holds the breaking sacred rather than merely necessary: no forest-fire, no green flush of new shoots; no fever's crisis, no end to the sickness; no cremation, no release of the soul from what it has been. He is venerated for courage rather than comfort — the courage to meet what must be destroyed with the fire proper to the task, no more and no less.

A fire-brand of consecrated wood is carried in every rite of purging; a fragment of the Ring, a broken metal circle, is worn at the neck as a sign of willingness to be broken and remade; an ash-mark, a smear of consecrated ash on the brow, is traced before any work that destroys what came before — razing a condemned house, felling a diseased tree, cutting down a blighted harvest. Rásikaran households keep an unbroken hearth-flame carried from parent to child at each marriage, and to let it go out is a grave omen; an ember from it lights the pyre at a death in the family.

His shrines stand at a city's edge and at its cremation grounds rather than at its centre, and two of them are already named where his fire has gone specifically to work: the cremation shrine outside Dhanurkota, and the shrine at Rājapur's downstream village of Mukteśvara, where the dead of eleven villages are received. At Suvarṇagiri a whole stream on the mountain's back slope is his and is never panned, in open recognition that not every part of the mountain's wealth belongs to Mahájaya's order.
