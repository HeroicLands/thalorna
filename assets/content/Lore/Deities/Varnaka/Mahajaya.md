_The Eternal Preserver — a serene matriarch crowned with wheat, holding a balanced scale._

Mahájaya is preservation, harmony, and abundance — the steady influence by which a sown field ripens, a market keeps its word, and the year turns in its ordered round rather than flying apart. Where [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyālendra]] gives the world its shape and [[affiliation-rasikara|Rásikara]] breaks what has gone rigid, Mahájaya keeps the shape intact between the two; the [[affiliation-trimurtisampradaya|Trimūrti-sampradāya]] reads all three as one cosmic work seen from three sides, and the reading is nowhere more literally built than in a temple whose three chambers give her the same standing as her brothers.

Her scale is not the scale of judgement; it is the scale of measure — the honest weighing that keeps an exchange fair and a portion whole. A brass weighing scale sits on the household shrine beside a fresh sheaf of wheat cut at the start of each harvest, and a bowl of mixed grains stands as the household's small sermon on the wholeness that comes from many parts kept in right relation. Merchants touch the scale at the close of a market day and name the day's transactions, asking that nothing was gained by short measure; farmers scatter a handful of millet at a field's edge to her Nivara-Kshetras at each new moon.

Her greatest temples inland are working proof of her demand for accountable order rather than mere prosperity: at Dhanurkota her temple is the janapada's federating hall; at Suvarṇagiri three temples of equal size ring the gold-bearing mountain so that no single priesthood can claim its wealth, and it is under her name that the monthly Weighing of the mountain's gold is conducted in public, in front of anyone who cares to watch. She is invoked wherever grain is sown, a contract is sealed, or a court needs reminding that order is a discipline and not a birthright.
