_The Dreaming Host — slender, silver-robed figures moving together in a gentle dance, half-lit by moonlight._

The Svapnadēvas are a celestial court, a host of spirits who weave dreams, carry omens and stand between the mortal mind and the divine order.

The Varṇaka hold them servants of [[lore-goddreams|The God of Dreams]], the elder deity known under many names elsewhere in [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]. They do not name that greater deity directly and do not venerate it as the organised pantheons of the west venerate theirs. What is revered is the Host, the vessel through which the elder god's dream-power reaches the world. Seers, mystics and healers of disturbed minds are its particular devotees.

The Host is honoured at the edges of the day, at dusk and at dawn, and at no fixed hour. A dark moonstone, the dream-stone, is kept beneath the pillow of every sleeper in a devout household and replaced at each new moon with one freshly consecrated at a temple.

A silver mirror serves the rites of divination proper, the seer meditating on its polished face until the waking mind quiets. To catch one's own unprepared reflection in an uncovered mirror is a small misfortune, and mirrors are kept covered outside the moment of reading. A braided lotus-cord is worn about the wrist of a devotee keeping a dream-vigil, and is untied when the vigil ends and the dream is spoken aloud.

The Host keeps no priesthood. Its formal devotion is two ordeals. The Seven-Night Vigil is seven consecutive nights of light sleep in a temple chamber, each morning's dream reported, and few are completed without a senior seer's help. The Mirror Year is a year spent reading the silver mirror for any petitioner who asks, without refusal and without fee.

Both belong to the household devotee and to the [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]] alike, the mystical assembly built around the Host's service. The assembly's own dream-reading is this devotion kept as a vocation.
