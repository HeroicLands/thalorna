_The Dreaming Host — slender, silver-robed figures moving together in a gentle dance, half-lit by moonlight._

The Svapnadēvas are not one god but a celestial court, a host of spirits who weave dreams, carry omens, and stand as intermediaries between the mortal mind and the divine order. The Varṇaka hold them to be divine servants of [[lore-goddreams|The God of Dreams]] — the ancient elder deity known under many names elsewhere in [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]] — but the Varṇaka do not name that greater deity directly and do not venerate it in the manner of the west's organised pantheons; what is revered is the Host itself, the vessel through which the elder god's dream-power reaches the world. Seers, mystics, and healers of disturbed minds are their particular devotees.

The Host is honoured at the edges of the day, dusk and dawn, rather than at any fixed hour. A dark moonstone — a dream-stone — is kept beneath the pillow of every sleeper in a devout household and replaced at each new moon with one freshly consecrated at a temple. A silver mirror is used in the rites of divination proper, the seer meditating on its polished face until the waking mind quiets; to catch one's own unprepared reflection in an uncovered mirror is reckoned a small misfortune, and mirrors are kept covered outside the moment of reading. A lotus-cord, braided, is worn about the wrist of a devotee undertaking a dream-vigil and untied only when the vigil ends and the dream is spoken aloud.

Formal devotion to the Host has two ordeals rather than a priesthood of its own: the Seven-Night Vigil, seven consecutive nights of light sleep in a temple chamber with each morning's dream reported and few completed without a senior seer's help, and the Mirror Year, a year spent reading the silver mirror for any petitioner who asks, without refusal and without fee. Both belong equally to the household devotee and to the [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]], the mystical assembly built entirely around the Host's service, whose own dream-reading is this devotion practised as a vocation rather than as a rite undertaken once.
