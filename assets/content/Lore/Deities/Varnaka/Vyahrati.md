_The Keeper of Decay — a serene older woman crowned with wilting blossoms, holding a shallow bowl of gathered petals and dew._

Vyāhrati is endings, and the quiet transformation by which what has reached its term gives itself back to the world. She commands no great presence in the Vedyari imagination, and is not venerated for power; she is venerated for necessity. Without decay, the theology holds, there is no soil; without an ending, no beginning; without the honest release of what must be released, a house, a garden, and a soul all turn stale in the keeping. She is the gentle complement of [[affiliation-rasikara|Rásikara]]'s purging fire — where he breaks what will not yield, she receives what yields of itself — and her worshippers count that gentleness itself, not weakness, as her particular strength.

The petal bowl on the kitchen windowsill is the one shrine that crosses every household in Vedyara regardless of its primary devotion: the day's faded garland, a fallen leaf tracked in on a sandal, a spent seed-head, all gathered there and emptied ceremonially onto the compost each month with the formula of release. The dew-phial, a small glass vessel of water gathered at the autumn equinox, is kept for the Rite of Releasing, and a withered garland — deliberately wilted rather than fresh — is worn at rites of decay as a sign of willing acceptance. A household passing through a change of state, a child grown and gone, a partnership ended, a business sold, sets withered flowers at its threshold for a full day to mark the passage.

Her formal devotion asks little institutional structure and correspondingly little of a devotee who wants more: a year received at a temple garden, taking in whatever is brought for composting and returning it to the ground with the proper rite, or three days of silent witness inside a house emptied by death or dissolution before it passes to new keeping. Her shrines sit as modest alcoves beside the temples of louder gods, and her formal priesthood is thin — but no household's shrine is complete without her bowl.
