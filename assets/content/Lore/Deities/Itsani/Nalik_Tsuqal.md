Nal'ik Ts'uqal is the Dreamer in Moonlight, the god of dreams, visions, prophecy and the moon in the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]]. Her symbol is a crescent moon trailing threads of silver mist. The [[affiliation-naliktzuqal|Faith of Nal'ik Ts'uqal]] serves her, and her ritual is [[skill-naliktzuqal|Ritual: Nal'ik Ts'uqal]].

## The Gentle Power

She is unique among the Itsani gods: she does not demand blood, and accepts cacao, flowers, incense and the spoken word. She is gentle where other deities are fearsome, and she teaches and illuminates where others demand sacrifice. Yet she governs the visions that shape the decisions of kings, the prophecies that alter wars and the dreams that give glimpses of possible futures. In Ki'i'chek understanding dreams are real perceptions of reality: alternate timelines, possible futures and the movements of the gods through the celestial spheres. Her celestial influence is strongest at the dark of the moon, when the boundary between waking and dream grows thin and visions are most reliable.

## Worship

The faithful sleep in sacred chambers to receive visions, prepared through specific prayers and breathing practices. A petitioner is admitted for a stated question, sleeps under supervision, and on waking recites what was seen before speaking to anyone else. Words are the most precious offering, and the branch's festivals are recitations rather than sacrifices: an entire night of composed speech, cacao and flowers. A household sets flowers and a cup of cacao by the sleeping mat before an important day and speaks the question aloud into the dark.

Her dream-keepers, the Ts'uqal'ob, keep the [Codex Ts'uqal]{.name data-type="lore/literature"}, a vast library of recorded dreams organized by symbol, outcome and reliability. A Reader of Symbols interprets a dream against its patterns and states the reliability with the reading.

## Relations

- [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Ts'uqil Ish'bal]]: dreams of the dead are common, and a dream-keeper who receives one refers the dreamer to the death-walkers.
- [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]]: the two branches keep enormous classified archives of recorded phenomena and hold an amiable, unresolved rivalry.
- [[lore-tzenultqandty|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]]: the solar priesthood teaches that there is no grace in the cosmos, and a goddess who takes flowers and gives visions and asks nothing in blood is a standing theological irritant to it.
- [[lore-xaqikhanaldty|Shaq'ik Ha'nal]]: the two branches ask least and give most, and in many cities their temples adjoin.
