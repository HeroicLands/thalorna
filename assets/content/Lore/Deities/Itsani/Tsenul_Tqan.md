Ts'en'ul Tq'an is the Sun at the World's Edge, the god of order, cosmic law and the solar cycle in the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]]. His symbol is a radiant sun disk bisected by a horizon line. The [[affiliation-tzenultqan|Faith of Ts'en'ul Tq'an]] serves him, and his ritual is [[skill-tzenultqan|Ritual: Ts'en'ul Tq'an]].

## Sovereign of the Fifth Age

He is the greatest and most terrible of the Itsani gods: the sovereign of the [Fifth Age]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"}, the maintainer of cosmic order and the celestial emperor whose constancy prevents the return of chaos. He is not worshipped with comfort or mercy. He demands absolute fidelity to the cosmic cycle, and his worship is one of rigid duty and sacred obligation. In this theology the sun does not rise by divine benevolence but because blood has been spilled and mortals have kept faith with their burden. His priests preach that there is no grace in the cosmos, only the reciprocal cycle of gift and obligation. The Fifth Age will endure only as long as the accumulated debt of blood owed to the gods does not exceed what mortals have given.

## Worship

The solstices are the most sacred astronomical events of the solar year. At the winter solstice the Ts'enmah ascends the pyramid and draws his own blood into the eternal flames while priests chant the binding spells that hold the sun to its path of return. At the summer solstice priests sever the hearts of sacrificial captives at the pyramid's peak and pour the blood, collected in jade vessels, down the temple's great stone channels. Personal bloodletting, by piercing the tongue, the earlobes or the genitals with obsidian blades, is a privilege of the honored. Warriors who have captured enemies dedicate their captives' coming sacrifices in advance.

Every dawn a Ch'alishbal stands at the pyramid's eastern stair and speaks the sun's name as the first light reaches the summit. The Warden of the Debt keeps the ledger of blood given and announces the surplus or shortfall at the close of each twenty-day month. Vows made to him are the heaviest a Kì'ì'chek can undertake. The great solar pyramids are always the tallest structures in any city.

## Relations

- [[lore-kayikchalixdty|K'ayik Ch'alish]]: the closest and least troubled partnership. The two share the premise that the sun is the fire at the world's edge, and at the [K'ayik Kul'al]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} the Presiding Ch'alish Tq'an ascends the pyramid with the highest fire-priests beside him.
- [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]]: the solstice hours, the eclipse warnings and the fifty-two-year realignment come from her observatories, and the goddess of knowledge is held to know when the Fifth Age will end.
- [[lore-puqilchaqundty|P'uqil Ch'akun]]: the solar branch funds the binding rites and keeps a posture of wary containment.
- [[lore-tzuqilixbaldty|Ts'uqil Ish'bal]]: the solar god teaches that the cycle must be held and the death jaguar that everything within it is meant to end. In the traditional account the solar god's refusal to spend cosmic resources destroying a minor functionary of the death god's household left [[lore-pikultzumaqdty|Pik'ul Ts'umaq]] at large.
