P'uqil Ch'akun is the Feathered Storm-Serpent of the [[affiliation-itzanpnthn|Itsani Pantheon]], the god of storms, chaos, rain, destruction and cosmic dissolution. His symbol is a feathered serpent coiled within a thundercloud, jade-tipped lightning in its jaws. The [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|Faith of P'uqil Ch'akun]] serves him, and his ritual is [[skill-puqilchaqun|Ritual: P'uqil Ch'akun]].

## One God, Two Aspects

He is the most terrifying and most desperately worshipped deity of the Itsani. He is depicted as a vast serpent of jade and obsidian scales, plumed in iridescent feathers the colors of storm-cloud and lightning, coiled within a thunderhead that blots out the sun. In this theology he is not two gods but one: the same power that sends gentle rain to nourish the maize sends the hurricane that flattens cities. Creation and destruction are aspects of the same cosmic force, and rain and hurricane are born from the same sky. When the [Fifth Age]{.name data-type="lore/cosmology"} ends, the serpent will wake.

## Worship

In his nourishing aspect, worship is most intense in the dry season, when cities conduct the [k'ishkan ch'alish]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} (rain-calling ceremonies) on the highest pyramid peaks. Priests ascend in procession, chanting in archaic [[skill-kiichek|Kì'ì'chek Language]] and pouring sacred water. The rain-calling escalates by fixed stages as the dry season lengthens, and when the first rains come the celebration is immediate and ecstatic.

In his destructive aspect, worship is the [Ts'umaq'tsuqalil]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} ("Binding of the Serpent"), conducted when the god is believed most restless. It requires mass bloodletting, offerings of jade and maize, and ritual recitation of the names of all four previous ages and their destructions. Its purpose is twofold: to bind the serpent symbolically with chains of blood and word, and to give an outlet for the anxiety of living under an apocalyptic shadow.

Households keep a jade chip or a green feather at the doorpost against the wind. A hurricane season passed without loss is answered with offerings at the next rain-calling, not with thanksgiving, since the god is not thanked for restraint he may withdraw.

His water-priests, the ne'tq'an, serve his nourishing aspect. His serpent-keepers serve his binding aspect: they keep the sacred calculations of the celestial configuration of total dissolution in codices bound in serpent skin, stored in triple-sealed chambers.

## Relations

- [[lore-kanixhakandty|K'anish Ha'kan]]: the water comes from one branch and the seed from the other, and the two jointly bless the [Chuqtik]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} planting.
- [[lore-tzenultqandty|Ts'en'ul Tq'an]]: the solar branch funds the binding rites, and each Ts'umaq'tsuqalil reminds the city that solar sovereignty is temporary.
- [[lore-ixilanchalixdty|Ish'ilan Ch'alish]]: the serpent-keepers depend on her observatories for the calculations they keep sealed.
- [[affiliation-tzumaqkalanil|The Serpent Awakeners]] (Ts'umaq K'alanil): the Serpent Awakeners teach that the Fifth Age is irredeemably corrupt and that hastening his awakening would be a mercy, and they are hunted and suppressed.
