The diffuse creative presence the Nyáluba name, in the idiom of the griots, as **the Great
Brightness**—the source from which all spirits emerged and into which, at the far end of all
things, all spirits will return.

It is the one power in the tradition that receives nothing. **No offering is made to it, no song
is sung to it, and no shrine houses it.** It is acknowledged, not venerated: the name the
tradition gives to the ultimate horizon of being, and not a party to any pact. Active religious
life is directed at the spirit guides beneath it—see [[lore-nyalbsprts|Nyáluba Spirits]] and
[[affiliation-nyalbpnthn|the Nyáluba Way]].

The Nyáluba recognize no supreme god, and would not accept that this is one. The
griots say that Shorínya Ndáfo is where the guides came from, and that it is not the sort of
thing one talks to.

The Lobwezi and Ngonzi griots tell how the Brightness came differently, and neither telling is ruled true.

**The Lobwezi telling.** The Brightness was the first thing there was and the only one, a light with no edge. It did not make the guides. It gave of itself, and each guide is a piece of it that took the shape of the ground it fell on: the baobab where the water rises, the crocodile where the river bends. The Lobwezi read the Brightness as a root, patient and under everything.

**The Ngonzi telling.** The Brightness was a flight and not a root. It crossed the sky once, from the east, and the guides are the sparks it shed as it passed over the highlands, the eagle first among them. In this telling the Brightness has gone on, and it returns at the end of all things by the road it left.

Neither clan has ever brought the difference to the Great Lubemo: a question about something nobody talks to is not one the Pact needs answered.
