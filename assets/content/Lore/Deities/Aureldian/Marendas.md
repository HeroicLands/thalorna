Marendas, the Keeper of Sacred Wisdom, is the goddess of learning, language and invention in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]. Her symbol is an open book surrounded by glowing stars. The [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]] serves her.

## The Keeper of Sacred Wisdom

In the faith's teaching she illuminates the minds of scholars, craftsmen and leaders and guides them toward wisdom. To worship her is to take part in the pursuit of knowledge itself, whether it is medicine, mathematics, history, languages, craftsmanship or philosophical theory, and the devout are expected to master at least one field and to share it by teaching or by making works that benefit civilization.

The faith teaches her as the complement of [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] in the making of civilization: Marendas gives the understanding of natural and philosophical truth, and Árdavon gives the legal and moral frame within which that understanding can be kept and handed on. Its followers hold that [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]]'s creative force and [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]]'s generative power are expressions of divine knowledge made manifest, and some of its theologians study whether creation, growth and knowledge share a deep connection. It teaches that [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] recognizes that an educated people is a more prosperous one. Its teaching sets [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] against her, since the Black Flame rejects wisdom for raw will and power.

## Worship

Her temples are places of prayer and centers of learning at once, alive with scribes copying manuscripts, scholars debating philosophy, apprentices learning crafts and students conjugating foreign tongues. Households keep libraries and reading corners dedicated to her, and the faithful spend hours in study and contemplation. Offerings are books and scrolls given to temple libraries, new manuscripts commissioned, and the schooling of promising youth who could not otherwise pay for it.

Marendandi, the sixth month of the [[lore-commoncal|Vilérian Calendar]], is hers. At the spring equinox the week-long [Festival of Illumination]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} marks the return of the light with public lectures on philosophy, theology and natural science. Scholars from across the regions gather to debate the nature of reality, the proper order of society and the place of mortals in the cosmos; winners of the philosophical contests receive scrolls and manuscripts and prominent places in temple libraries. The festival ends with an oath-taking in which students commit themselves to the pursuit of knowledge and teachers pledge to pass wisdom on. At the beginning of autumn, when young people enter training in crafts and professions, the [Dedication of the Apprentice]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} brings apprentices before her priests for blessing and instruction in the intellectual and ethical foundations of their trades: a smith learns the geometry beneath architecture, a scribe the responsibility of recording knowledge accurately, a merchant the logic of fair trade.

## Priesthood and Temples

Her priesthood puts scholarship before hierarchy. The Mentara, the Master of Wisdom, is chosen by the vote of the senior scholars of the faith and is the final authority on doctrine, residing at the [Grand Temple of Illumination]{.name data-type="place/structure"} in a major center of learning. Below her the Daskeral, a body of senior scholars each specializing in a field of knowledge, serve as priests, teachers and researchers, each running schools and inquiries that are loosely coordinated and independent; they meet quarterly on matters of theology and where disciplines intersect. The Daskeni are ordained teachers in cities and settlements, running schools and mentoring apprentices, and are expected to keep advancing their own work. The Lerini, students and junior scholars, make up the base, and the road to ordination has no fixed length.

The faith's great libraries, the Notral Maldor, are the distinctive institution: centers of preservation and research maintained jointly by the priesthood and wealthy patrons, with thousands of manuscripts, and the ones in the [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia Region]] and the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] draw scholars from across western Ankaris on pilgrimage. Librarians hold real authority, since keeping and ordering knowledge is sacred work to the faith. The priesthood is funded by endowments, by fees for schooling and apprenticeship that are waived or reduced for the poor, and by royal patronage, and it deliberately avoids accumulating land or temporal power.

## Present

Árdavan judges consult the scholars of the Faith of Marendas on difficult legal questions, its schools operate under the protection of Árdavan law, and many temples of the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]] hold large libraries and host its scholars as permanent residents. Its scholars share mathematics, geometry, metallurgy and engineering with [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], and joint research has advanced architecture and metalwork; in major cities the temples of the two stand close together. Where [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] threatens, its priesthood works to preserve knowledge and support education, since a thinking population resists the simplifications of tyranny.
