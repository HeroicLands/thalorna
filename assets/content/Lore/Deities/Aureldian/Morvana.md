Morvana, the Mistress of the Waning Moon, is the goddess of decay, endings and transformation in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]. Her symbol is a wilting flower surrounded by mist. The [[affiliation-morvana|Faith of Morvana]] serves her.

## The Waning Moon

The faith teaches that Morvana oversees the natural cycle of death and renewal and reminds mortals of life's impermanence. Her domain in its teaching is the process and not the fact of ending: the gentle fading, the slow return to the earth that fed the living thing. The faith teaches acceptance, not consolation, and offers the dying no false promise of reunion.

[[lore-selmorosdty|Sélmoros]] is taught as the fact of death, the silent judge who cannot be negotiated with, and Morvana as the way to him; the faiths hold that Sélmoros is the gate and Morvana the passage through it. [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] is her counterpart in the wheel of the year: the faiths picture what Lusinia's season brings to growth as returning in time to compost under Morvana's. [[lore-lizelasdty|Lizelas]] is her gentle contrast, since Lizelas brings celebration and Morvana brings perspective: enjoy the feast because it will not last, and accept the ending because the feast was had. [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] is taught as the sudden shock that tears down what was, and Morvana the gradual return to earth that follows it, so the two are strained but understood by some theologians to stand together for transformation. [[lore-zunelasdty|Zunelas]] shares a subtle affinity with her, because death is sometimes described as the greatest dream.

## Worship

Worship is solemn and contemplative, acknowledgment in place of celebration. Her temples are built in autumnal colors of deep gold, burnished red and black and are hung with fallen leaves, withered flowers, empty branches and images of creatures in their last seasons. They are deliberately shadowed and inward, places to meet impermanence and accept it with grace.

Morvanandi, the ninth month of the [[lore-commoncal|Vilérian Calendar]], is in her keeping. In late autumn, as the land retires into winter, the seven-day [Festival of the Waning Moon]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} is kept in remembrance and acceptance. The faithful process through the cities with candles that darken day by day, bright on the first and dimmer after, until the last night is kept in near-total darkness. Families raise altars to their dead, speak their names and recount their deeds, in the understanding that every life is remembered and then forgotten in turn, and that this is just. In hospices and places of healing her priestesses perform rites for the dying that affirm their passage as sacred and their decay as a return to the earth.

Devotion is mostly private, undertaken by those facing loss, aging or their own death. Priestesses visit the sick in their last days and bring comfort and perspective, and the grieving pour out their sorrow at her altar, where the faith holds that it is heard and acknowledged. Philosophers and scholars often hold a personal devotion to her as the teacher of the deepest truths about existence. Offerings are the gifts of decay: autumn leaves, withering flowers, fruit past ripeness, turning wine, molded grain, made not from what is valued but from what must be let go. Some noble families keep her shrines in the family crypt, making annual offerings and speaking their ancestors' names.

## Priesthood and Temples

The Valmena, the High Priestess or High Priest, is typically an older priestess with decades of service who has survived personal loss and turned grief into compassionate service; she counsels the dying and interprets the goddess's will for the faith. Below her the Sarnori of Twilight, ordained priests, care for the terminally ill, counsel the bereaved and keep her temples, chosen for empathy, comfort with mortality and the ability to speak honestly of death without cruelty. Acolytes of Decay train in the care of the dying and in preparing bodies for burial rites, often for years in hospices; many never proceed to ordination and give their lives as nurses and caregivers in contact with the priesthood.

Her Hospices of the Waning Moon, found in every major city, shelter and comfort those in their final days and perform the funeral rites that prepare the dead for their passage. They are supported by grateful families, by endowments left by the dying and sometimes by noble patrons who look to her faith for a dignified death of their own.

## Present

The priesthoods of the Faith of Morvana and the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] work together at deathbeds, the Sélmoran priests invoking Morvana's acceptance as they guide the dying toward what their own faith teaches of Sélmoros's realm. Some temples keep joint altars with the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]], with fresh flowers set beside Morvana's withered ones, and her priestesses work with those of the [[affiliation-zunelas|Faith of Zunelas]] to help the dying find peace through visions and dreams.
