Lizelas, the Nurturer, is the goddess of fertility, growth and healing in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]. Her symbol is a blooming flower encircled by a crescent. The [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]] serves her.

## The Nurturer

The faith teaches that Lizelas ensures the fertility of people, animals and the land. It invokes her at planting, at childbirth, in rites of renewal and in sickness, and holds that her gifts extend to the restoration of bodies damaged by illness or wound. Her nature in its teaching is joyous, exuberant and sensual: she is depicted as celebrating abundance, pleasure, love and the creative power of life itself.

The pantheon pairs her with [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] in the full cycle of fertility and growth. Where Lusinia stands for patience, cultivation and the methodical work of creation, Lizelas stands for joy and the abundant flowering of life, and the two are invoked together at planting and harvest. [[lore-morvanadty|Morvana]] is her gentle contrast, not her enemy: the faiths teach together that flowers are to be enjoyed while they bloom and that they wilt and return to earth. Her relation to [[lore-olveniadty|Ólvenia]] is taught as a natural affinity, in which the farm Lizelas blesses produces the crops Ólvenia's merchants sell. She is taught as wary of [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] without hostility, since wild storms bring rain and forest fires clear ground for growth, and she is held to stand for nature brought into relationship with civilization.

## Worship

Her temples are built to capture light and are decorated with flowers, fountains and gardens of great beauty; where other temples are austere, hers are places of comfort full of fragrance, singing birds and the sound of water. Offerings are abundant and sensory: fresh flowers, honey, wine, sweet herbs and first fruits. Many homes keep a small shrine with blooming plants replaced as they fade, and gardens and herb-lofts kept for healing are dedicated to her. Pregnant women visit her temples to ask for an easy delivery and healthy children, the sick come to her priestesses for healing, those who have lost a child keep vigil at her shrines, and the young leave offerings before a first love.

Lizelandi, the first month of the [[lore-commoncal|Vilérian Calendar]], is in her keeping, and the year opens in it on the vernal equinox. In early spring, at the first signs of new growth, the seven-day [Festival of the First Bloom]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} fills the cities with processions of priestesses crowned in fresh flowers, the opening of new gardens and communal plantings in which all are invited to put seed in the ground. Marriages and handfastings are made during the festival in the belief that the union receives her blessing of fertility and lasting joy, young couples exchange flower garlands, and the priesthood blesses couples hoping to conceive. In late summer, as the first fruits ripen, families bring the first of their abundance to her temples for the [Blessing of the Harvest]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}. Part is left as an offering, and the remainder is given to the poor, because the faith teaches that abundance must be shared.

## Priesthood and Temples

The priesthood is led by the Lizena, the High Priestess, or occasionally High Priest, though the office is more often held by a woman. She is typically a middle-aged priestess renowned for skill in healing and gardening, and the faith regards her as healer, teacher and intermediary between the goddess and her people. Below her stand the Sarnori, the ordained priestesses and priests who serve as healers, midwives, gardeners and officiants at marriages and fertility blessings. The finest are sought by noble families and commoners alike. Acolytes of the Garden train for three to five years in herbalism, midwifery, healing and the cultivation of gardens, and many settle as independent herbalists or midwives while keeping their ties to the temple.

The House of Healing, a temple-hospital found in nearly every major city, is the faith's distinctive institution. It employs priestesses, nurses and servants, treats the poor and sick without regard for wealth or station, and is supported by the gratitude of patients, the sale of herbal remedies and the patronage of the nobility. Its finest houses are known across kingdoms as places where the dying are comforted and women in difficult childbirth are cared for by priestesses who have delivered thousands of children.

## Present

Her priestesses consult the scholars of the [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]] on the nature of the plants they cultivate, and the libraries of Marendas employ them as consultants on medical and botanical texts. The Faith of Lizelas works with the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] in festivals and healing. Sailors and merchants visit her temples before a journey to ask for safe travel, and her priestesses pray for favorable winds and for the harvests of those who will return, in a quiet partnership with the [[affiliation-pozeras|Faith of Pozeras]].
