Zunelas, the Veiled Dreamer, is the goddess of dreams, celestial visions and the veil between worlds in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]. Her name means the hallowed night in the [[skill-vylarilng|Vilari Language]]. Her symbol is a freestanding stone arch with a radiant star beyond. The [[affiliation-zunelas|Faith of Zunelas]] serves her.

## The Veiled Dreamer

The faith teaches that Zunelas brings divine inspiration and omens to mortals through their dreams, and it holds her patron to seers, oracles, healers of the mind and all who seek truth in the world beyond waking. The arch is depicted as the passage a dreamer takes between the waking world and the light beyond it. She is the Aureldian name for [[lore-goddreams|the God of Dreams]], a deity far older than any human pantheon, worshipped by the [[lore-flksenali|Senali Folk]] and [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]] since before humanity existed; the Asguardian peoples know the same deity as [[lore-bjarturdty|Bjart]].

In Aureldian teaching the other gods respect her, because she represents a realm that stands above the conflicts of the waking deities. She is taught as the quiet advisor, the voice of deeper wisdom speaking from dreams and visions when mortal councils are deadlocked, and nearly every faith consults her priesthood on matters of great importance. The faith holds that [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] brings clarity to waking disputes and she brings insight into the truths beneath them, and some theologians hold that the two together are the complete wisdom, justice balanced with mercy and order with intuition. With [[lore-marendasdty|Marendas]] she shares knowledge as the other path to it: Marendas is taught as seeking it by study and reason, and Zunelas as revealing it in dreams, visions and the unconscious mind. Death is sometimes described as the greatest dream, a sleep with no waking, which is why her faith and the [[affiliation-morvana|Faith of Morvana]] comfort the dying together. [[lore-karnavosdty|Karnavos]] is held to shatter civilization, and she only to remind mortals that truths exist beyond rational order; some renegade priests of Karnavos have tried to turn her worship into a cult of dangerous ecstasy, which her mainstream priesthood regards as a perversion of her nature.

## Worship

Worship is contemplative and private, centered on dreams, visions and deep rest. Her temples are sanctuaries of silence and shadow, draped in soft fabric in midnight and silver, perfumed with herbs that encourage sleep and built to quiet the waking mind. The faithful come to rest, to dream and to seek the goddess in visions. Her offerings are subtle and personal: a lock of hair bound with silver thread, a small stone from a beloved's grave, a cup of milk left before sleep, a written prayer sealed and buried beneath a sacred tree.

The temples are places of healing sleep. The sick, the injured and the spiritually troubled stay for nights or weeks under the care of her priestesses, resting and dreaming in the belief that they are under the goddess's protection, and the faith holds that she can mend what waking physicians cannot reach. Oracular shrines in major cities answer questions through dreams: a supplicant purifies, sleeps in the sacred precinct and wakes with dreams that the priestess-oracles interpret as the goddess's counsel. The Oracular Sanctuary at [[place-argenna|Argenna]] in [[place-provenzrgn|Provénzia]] draws pilgrims from across the continent, who trust that dreams received there come from the goddess herself.

She has no great public festival. Zunelandi, the eleventh month of the [[lore-commoncal|Vilérian Calendar]], is in her keeping, and in the darkest nights of winter, when the faithful hold the veil between waking and sleeping to be thin, they deliberately spend long hours in sleep and dreaming. Priestesses keep vigil and tend the sacred flames that light the temples through the dark hours, and many families sleep together in the most comfortable room of the house, taking their rest as a shared benediction.

## Priesthood and Temples

The Nirvena, the High Priestess or High Priest, is a title used regardless of gender and is typically held by an elderly priestess of visionary power whose dreams have guided kingdoms and whose wisdom is sought by monarchs and councils. The Nirvena is the faith's chief interpreter of the goddess's will and mentor to younger priests. Below the Nirvena the Ozeli, ordained priestesses and occasionally priests, are dream-healers and interpreters of vision, trained for years in lucid dreaming, in the reading of symbols and omens and in the care of those seeking guidance; they keep the sanctuaries, oversee purification and guide supplicants. The Dunari, acolytes and servants of the night, tend the temples through the dark hours, trim the sacred lamps, prepare the sleeping chambers and assist the Ozeli, in an apprenticeship of five to seven years that teaches them to enter deep meditation, to record their own dreams and to read the symbolic language in which the faithful believe the goddess speaks.

The priesthood keeps dream journals written across centuries, interpretive texts on common symbols and visions, and manuals for inducing lucid dreaming, in temple libraries where scholars sometimes spend months in residence. The Houses of Sleep are major temple complexes that serve as hospitals for the afflicted through sacred rest. They are supported by grateful patients, by the sale of dream interpretation to wealthy clients and by the patronage of the nobility.

## Present

The libraries of the [[affiliation-marendas|Faith of Marendas]] sometimes employ priests of the Faith of Zunelas to read the deeper symbolism of texts, and its oracular shrines consult the scholars of Marendas on what the goddess's messages mean. Its priesthood works with that of the Faith of Morvana at deathbeds, offering visions of peace and rest in place of fear of the end.
