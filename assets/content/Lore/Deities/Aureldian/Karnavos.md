Karnavos, the Wild Beast, is the god of chaos in the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]]: wildness, unpredictability, primal energy, and the wilderness no law reaches into. His symbol is antlers framing a swirling spiral. The [[affiliation-karnavos|Faith of Karnavos]] serves him.

## The Wild Beast

The faith teaches that Karnavos is the untamed force of nature and the disruptive change that growth requires. In its teaching forests thicken and darken, rivers break their banks and seasons shift without warning under his hand, and a rigid system that has gone stagnant is broken open by him so that something can begin again. He is taught to demand neither submission nor obedience; he is held to acknowledge the wild impulse in mortals that drives them across boundaries and toward change.

The pantheon holds him as real and necessary but dangerous. [[lore-lusiniadty|Lusinia]] stands for nature brought into order by cultivation, and the two are invoked in paradox: his storms destroy crops planted under her blessing and are also held to renew the soil and open ground for growth that neither could manage alone. He shares transformation with [[lore-morvanadty|Morvana]], who is inevitable ending and natural decay where he is violent disruption, and the two are called together at moments of transition, death joined to rebirth. [[lore-ardavondty|Árdavon]] is his opposite, and the faiths of each teach the pair as locked in eternal struggle with mortals caught between. The teaching of the [[affiliation-zunelas|faith of Zunelas]] has her regard him with caution, because a dream however wild ends in waking to order, and he is held to seek to shatter even that waking.

## Worship

His worship is neither celebrated nor forbidden. Scholars and theologians acknowledge it and some practice it privately because they hold the god necessary, and it takes place at thresholds, boundaries and wild places: crossroads shrines at the edge of towns, standing stones at the margin of a forest, isolated mountain peaks. Merchants setting out on uncertain voyages, adventurers, revolutionaries and philosophers who question established truth invoke him quietly at the hour a venture begins or an old order must break.

Rites fall at the boundaries between seasons and at midnight on the new moon. Karnavar, the eighth month of the [[lore-commoncal|Vilérian Calendar]], is his, and the autumnal equinox falls on its first day. Offerings are left at night at wilderness shrines: wild meat, untamed plants, things that have resisted cultivation. Some of the faithful keep ecstatic rites in secret groves, with dancing, drumming and the deliberate breaking of taboo as expressions of his wild blessing, and the priesthood teaches that such liberation is temporary and is balanced by a return to order.

## Priesthood and Temples

The faith has no temples in the conventional sense and no High Priest. Local shamans, hermits, eccentric philosophers and wilderness dwellers serve as informal priests, rarely ordained and often alone. The loose and shifting [Circle of the Threshold]{.name data-type="affiliation/fellowship"} gathers priests and philosophers at boundary places at seasonal transitions to exchange knowledge and debate the god's will; nothing it decides binds anyone, and every practitioner interprets the god freely.

The most respected rank is the Hermit of Chaos, an oracle and teacher recognized for insight into wild forces, who holds no official authority and may have no followers. Some live alone in the wilderness for decades, others wander cities as holy fools. Acolytes of the Threshold are largely self-taught, from books and solitary practice. In civilized cities a hidden shrine-keeper tends a small altar stone in a grove or behind a tavern, a place of last resort where the desperate or defiant leave offerings when no god of order will answer; many of them keep up the worship of a more conventional god in public.

## Present

The [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]] and the authorities it influences suppress the worship of Karnavos in many kingdoms as a threat to civilized order, while Árdavan theology admits that the god cannot be denied, only restrained. [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] courts his priests as kindred spirits of transgression, and the mainstream Karnavic priesthood refuses it, holding that Karnavos is transformative chaos and the Black Flame is destruction for its own sake.
