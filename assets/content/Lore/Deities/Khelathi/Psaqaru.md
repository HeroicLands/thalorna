![[icon-psaqaru|Faith of Psaq'âru]]{float=top-left size=medium}

_Shaper of the Eternal World—a chisel carving a star._

Psaq'âru is the supreme architect of creation, the divine craftsman whose hands and mind shaped the
cosmos out of formless void. Temple theology holds the universe to be his masterwork: every
mountain, every star, every creature exists because Psaq'âru conceived its form and brought it into
being through perfect artistry. He is shown with hammer and chisel, or as something more abstract—the invisible force of creative intention that reaches the world through human hands.

The distinctive claim of his faith is that **creation did not cease at the world's beginning**.
Every builder who raises a structure, every artisan who shapes raw material into beauty, every
architect who envisions a city yet unbuilt participates in the ongoing work. And the god grants not
success but _inspiration itself_: the ability to see potential in stone and wood, to understand
proportion, to perceive the hidden forms waiting inside materials.

Aû'Khelâthu's monumental architecture is understood as his presence made concrete. The builders of
the pyramids and the obelisks regard themselves not as servants of the Gar-Aû but as high priests of
Psaq'âru, their works prayers in stone—which is why the precision is itself devotional and why
innovation in form is not valued over perfection of it.

One strand of theology holds that **Psaq'âru made Uqa'â**—that the universe came into being through
Psaq'âru's creative will and that [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] is the animating principle moving through his
creation. It has never caused conflict; the priesthoods treat the aspects of creation as
complementary rather than ranked.
