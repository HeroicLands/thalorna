_The Keeper of Transitions—a jackal's head cradling a crescent moon._

Hezmuîri is the god of decay, mummification and transformation, and the compassionate guide of the
dead through the most fundamental of all transitions. To most minds decay evokes revulsion—a thing
to be denied and overcome. Khelâthi theology runs deeper: **decay is not meaningless annihilation but
necessary and sacred transformation**. The body that served its owner faithfully in life must be
transformed before the spirit can go on.

The symbol carries the argument. The **jackal** is a scavenger of the wild margins that moves
through the night with quiet dignity—in Khelâthi understanding not a defiler but a participant in
the natural cycle, a creature that knows bodies must return to the earth. The **crescent moon** is
night, and also renewal: the old becoming new.

His and [[lore-wethurdty|Wethûr]]'s domains divide cleanly. Hezmuîri holds the physical process—the
natron and the resins, the seventy days, the linen and the amulets at every joint—while Wethûr
holds the soul's journey and the tomb's sanctity.

He is honored more universally than almost any other god for a plain reason: essentially every
human being will eventually require his services. That universality is also an industry, and it made
his priesthood among the largest and most powerful in the empire.
