![[icon-qearet|Faith of Qe'âret]]{float=top-left size=medium}

_Keeper of Truth and Harmony—a feather atop a golden scale._

A Wazu at the archive door says it to every petitioner who comes hoping for mercy: "She does not do mercy. She does the scale." **Qe'âret** is the great balancing force of the cosmos, and she is **a goddess of absolute truth and immutable law, not of compassion or mercy**. Before creation chaos reigned; she imposed order on that primordial darkness and established the laws by which the universe operates and to which all creation must conform. To live in harmony with her is to live truthfully, honor one's oaths, speak with clarity and balance every action with restraint.

Her theology rests on one overwhelming doctrine: **the Weighing**. The deceased stands before the assembled gods and both of his accounts are opened. The temple account is read as a scribe took it, every entry closed or short, and beside it the gods set the account they kept themselves, which no witness attended and no tablet holds. A soul short on either is taken by **Álgit, the Devourer of the Dead**. A soul whose entries close passes onward into the blessed [[place-zulaten|Zulaten]].

[[lore-readingweigh|The Reading at the Weighing]] sets the two accounts side by side:

: The Weighing

```poetry {form=litany lang=en}
Before the assembled gods both accounts are opened.
Ours is read as it stands; theirs is set beside it.
The feather is on the scale, and nothing is bought at the scale.
The lord who endowed a temple late is read as his tenant is read.
```

That judgment is the foundation of Khelâthi ethics, and its force comes from one feature above all: **wealth cannot buy a favorable verdict**. A written entry can be closed late or bought out; the unwritten account can only be met, and nobody holds a copy to consult. Even the powerful fear it, which makes it a real if imperfect check on the worst abuses of power in a civilization otherwise steeply unequal.

She judges; [[lore-wethurdty|Wethûr]] does not. By the time a soul reaches his realm it has already been weighed.

The temples do not say whether the deceased may speak when his accounts are read, or in which register: attesting the written, petitioning the unwritten, or standing silent while the gods read both. They do not say what Álgit is either, whether a god, a beast or an office.
