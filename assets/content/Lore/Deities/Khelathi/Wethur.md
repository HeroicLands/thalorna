![[icon-wethur|Faith of Weth'ûr]]{float=top-left size=medium}

_Lord of Silent Passage—a black hawk with gold-tipped wings._

"He is not waiting to catch you," a household priest tells a nervous child at the door of a family tomb. "He is waiting to show you the road." **Weth'ûr** guides souls through the underworld, keeps the tombs and guards the boundary between the living world and the realm of the dead. He is _impartial and inevitable_, neither cruel nor kind. As the sun sets each day into the western horizon, every mortal passes westward into his realm, and nobody escapes him, bargains with him or cheats his dominion. He is a god of order, and no god of terror.

He does not judge. That belongs to [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]], and Weth'ûr's charge is to see that the soul comes before her in proper condition and properly prepared, and afterward is guided to its destiny. Where [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmu'îri]] presides over the transformation of the body, Weth'ûr presides over the passage of the soul, which is why their priesthoods share the mortuary temples and divide the work between the embalming table and the rite.

The theology holds that death is a transition, and the most important one in existence. From that follows the reorientation of values his faith teaches, and with it much of what outsiders find morbid in [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]]: the monuments one builds will crumble, the kingdoms one conquers will be conquered and the wealth one gathers will be inherited, while the afterlife is eternal. The elaborate care of the dead is a judgment about where value lies, and no obsession with dying.

In his theology grave-robbery is cosmic blasphemy as well as theft: it breaks the order he maintains and threatens the rest of the dead.

A soul **Álgit** devours is ended. Weth'ûr keeps only the weighed and the worthy, which is why the tombs of the condemned go untended.
