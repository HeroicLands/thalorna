![[icon-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'âr]]{float=top-left size=medium}

_Scribe of the Eternal Library—an ibis quill writing on a golden scroll._

Reth'Sa'âr is the custodian of all knowledge and the divine scribe, recording not merely the deeds of
mortals but the workings of the cosmos. In the **Eternal Library**—a celestial archive held to
exist simultaneously within and beyond the world—he inscribes every moment of creation with his
ibis quill, so that nothing of importance is ever truly lost. He is shown ibis-headed or crowned
with the lunar crescent, for he governs the moon's cycles along with the reckoning of time.

His faith teaches that knowledge is the greatest treasure and the surest path to virtue. Where other
gods grant wealth or martial prowess, Reth'Sa'âr offers **understanding**—the ability to perceive truth
beneath illusion. The scribal class of Aû'Khelâthu owes its existence to him, and since the scribal
school is the empire's one reliable ladder out of the fields, the god of records is also the god of
the only social mobility most Khelâthi will ever see.

He stands to [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] in reverence tinged with subordination: counselor and scribe,
recording the decrees and helping mortals understand the law the sun embodies. Temple theology gives
him a specific part in the nightly battle—it is Reth'Sa'âr who prepares Uqa'â for it, supplying the
magical knowledge and the strategic insight that sustain the cosmic order.

With [[lore-psaqarudty|Psaq'âru]] he is a complement rather than a rival: creation without knowledge is
chaotic, knowledge without creation sterile, and the temple legends show the two in constant
productive argument.

What the Eternal Library is held to contain that the temple archives at Khelunu do not is not stated.
