![[icon-rethsaar|Faith of Reth'Sa'âr]]{float=top-left size=medium}

_Scribe of the Eternal Library—an ibis quill writing on a golden scroll._

Every child admitted to a scribal school learns the same first lesson: "Write down only what is so. He is reading over your shoulder." **Reth'Sa'âr** is the custodian of all knowledge and the divine scribe, who records the deeds of mortals and the workings of the cosmos alike. In the **Eternal Library**, a celestial archive held to exist both within and beyond the world, he inscribes every moment of creation with his ibis quill, so that nothing of importance is ever truly lost. He is shown ibis-headed or crowned with the lunar crescent, for he governs the moon's cycles and the reckoning of time.

His faith teaches that knowledge is the greatest treasure and the surest path to virtue. Where other gods grant wealth or martial prowess, Reth'Sa'âr offers **understanding**, the ability to perceive truth beneath illusion. The scribal class of Aû'Khelâthu owes its existence to him. The scribal school is the empire's one reliable ladder out of the fields, so the god of records is also the god of the only social mobility most Khelâthi will ever see.

He stands to [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] in reverence tinged with subordination, as counselor and scribe, recording the decrees and helping mortals understand the law the sun carries. Temple theology gives him a specific part in the nightly battle: Reth'Sa'âr prepares Uqa'â for it, supplying the magical knowledge and the strategic insight that sustain the cosmic order.

With [[lore-psaqarudty|Psaq'âru]] he is a complement and not a rival. Creation without knowledge is chaotic and knowledge without creation is sterile, and the temple legends show the two in constant, productive argument.

What the Eternal Library holds that the temple archives at Khelunu do not is a question the temples leave unanswered.
