_Bringer of Tempests—a serpent coiled around a lightning bolt._

A river pilot who sees the sky go brass in late summer does not curse. He throws a pinch of incense into the wind and starts tying down the cargo. **Tjaq'ûr** is the god of storms, wind and the ferocious power of nature at its most violent. The distinction from [[lore-azuathisdty|Azu'âthis]] is doctrinally important and easily missed: where Azu'âthis is primordial chaos threatening all existence, Tjaq'ûr is the destructive and purifying power of natural phenomena, the thunderhead that shatters ancient trees, the gale that drives ships onto rocks, the lightning that burns away corruption and the wind that carries necessary change. He is a god within the order and no enemy of it.

He belongs to no one landscape. The [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] understand him as the breath and fury of the sky itself, at home over the cultivated valley, the mountains, the sea and the far grazing country: the late-summer thunderstorms over the river, the gales that churn the [[lore-vylarianclt|Vylarian]] coast and the squalls that come down off the eastern hills and flatten standing grain in an hour.

To worship him is to acknowledge that not all divine power flows through gentle channels, and that his tempests remind people nature runs on its own logic and not on human convenience. His worship is solemn and formal, with constant awareness of invoking a dangerous power, and his temples stand on high ground where lightning is likeliest to fall.

The priesthoods of Tjaq'ûr and [[lore-qearetdty|Qe'âret]] are on tense terms. Whether the quarrel is doctrinal, order against weather, or simply old is a question neither priesthood has answered.
