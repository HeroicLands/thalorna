_Mother of New Beginnings—a blooming lotus intertwined with a serpent._

Uznêra is the great mother goddess of Aû'Khelâthu: fertility, renewal and healing, the principle that
sustains all life. She is the river that floods each year and lays rich black silt across the
valley; she is the womb from which creation springs; and she is the healer who mends what is broken
and returns the wounded to wholeness.

Her symbol carries the whole theology. The **lotus** is renewal and beauty, emerging pristine each
dawn from the mud of the igelu; the **serpent** is transformation and the shedding of old forms.
Together they say that healing and fertility both require a willingness to let the old go—that
nothing is restored without something being given up.

Her teaching is the theological foundation of Khelâthi women's unusual legal standing. The doctrine
that **creation requires the balanced partnership of masculine and feminine divine principles** is
what underwrites women owning property, initiating divorce, pleading in the Genzet, practicing
medicine and magic, and serving as priestesses including in high office.

Against the more ascetic faiths she is a deliberate counterweight. Where they demand renunciation,
Uznêra's priestesses hold that the body is sacred, that its legitimate needs are the goddess's gift,
and that to deny them is to deny her. This is not license—it is the claim that life's generative
and healing forces are as divine as its abstract principles of order and truth.
