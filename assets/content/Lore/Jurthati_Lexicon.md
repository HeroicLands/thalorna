# terran_analog: Jürthāti is an island daughter of the Tāntsou stock, open-syllabled and mora-timed in the manner of Japanese beside a Chinese-shaped parent, and its inherited words lose the parent's -ng into long vowels as Japanese readings of Chinese do. The inherited words follow from Tāntsou by the change table; the island words coined here are made to the rules and are not the Japanese or Chinese word for their meaning.
---

The islands of [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jénchài]] speak the mainland's words with the music taken out. Where a Tāntsou minister sings _téng_ for heaven, rising through the syllable and closing on _-ng_, a Jürthāti fisherman says _tē_, the nasal gone into a long vowel and the rising tone left behind as a high pitch. A thousand years of separation have made [[skill-jurthatlng|Jürthāti]] a language of open syllables and even beats: every word closes on a vowel or on _n_, two consonants meet inside a word only where an _n_ stands first, and the verb at the close of a sentence carries the speaker's whole relation to the listener and the subject.

This page is the word-hoard of the island tongue. It gives the inherited stock as the change table makes it from [[doc-tantsoulexcn|Tāntsou]], the island words beside it, the endings of the honorific grid, the rules a new word or name is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in Jénchài.

Jürthāti holds two readings for much of its vocabulary. The **native reading** is the word as the islands say it, made from the mainland word by the changes below. The **learned reading** is the mainland pronunciation, written as the mainland writes it, tone marks and all, and kept for the realm's name, titles, rites, the names of castes and offices, and the set phrases of the court and the shrines. The learned reading keeps the _-ng_, the _zh_, the _l_ and the four tones the native reading loses, so _Ruìsing_, the ritual death, is a learned word whose native reading is _ruizī_. The word tables give the native reading; the learned forms the notes write stand in the table of names outside the pattern below.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word in its native reading.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief. Two glosses joined by _and, by the other pitch,_ are two mainland words the loss of tone has made alike in writing, which the pitch-accent keeps apart in speech. Two glosses joined by _and, at the same pitch,_ are two mainland words the islands say alike, which only the script keeps apart.
- **Built from** is `—` for a word taken whole. Otherwise it names the words and endings the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the building rules make of them.
- **Source** says where the word came from: `inherited` for a word the change table makes from its Tāntsou root, `island` for a word a note gives that has no Tāntsou root, and `coined` for a word this page makes so that the tongue can be used.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

Jürthāti writes five vowels, each short or long, and the mainland's consonants less _zh_, _l_ and _ng_. The mainland's four tones are a pitch-accent on the islands that the pages do not write: a word is said high or low, the pitch parts word pairs and carries no grammar, and where the loss of tone has left two words alike in writing, the pitch is often the only thing that tells them apart.

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind        | Sounds like                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `b`, `d`, `g`, `k`, `t` | consonant   | the plain stops; _g_ is always hard                                     |
| `ch`, `j`               | consonant   | _church_, _judge_                                                       |
| `ts`                    | consonant   | _cats_                                                                  |
| `f`, `h`, `s`, `z`      | consonant   | _fine_, _hat_, _sit_, _zeal_                                            |
| `sh`                    | consonant   | _ship_                                                                  |
| `m`, `n`                | consonant   | the nasals; an _n_ that closes a syllable is held for a beat of its own |
| `r`                     | consonant   | a tapped _r_, between _r_ and _l_                                       |
| `w`, `y`                | consonant   | _we_, _yes_                                                             |
| `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` | short vowel | _father_, _bet_, _ski_, _note_, _boot_, each cut short                  |
| `ā`, `ē`, `ī`, `ō`, `ū` | long vowel  | the same vowels held for two beats: a long _ah_, _ay_, _ee_, _oh_, _oo_ |

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, and closes on a vowel or on _n_.

| Edge                               | Sounds                                                     |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pairs that may open a word         | none                                                       |
| Consonants that close a word       | `n`                                                        |
| Vowels that may stand together     | `ai`, `ei`, `ui`                                           |
| Pairs that may stand inside a word | `nb`, `nd`, `ng`, `nj`, `nm`, `nn`, `nr`, `nw`, `ny`, `nz` |

### Clusters

Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel, or a vowel alone, and an _n_ may close it as a beat of its own; verse is counted in these even beats. So two consonants meet inside a word only where a syllable's _n_ stands before the next consonant, and the next consonant is voiced, as the voicing rule below makes it.

| Rule                                   | Count |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Most consonants together inside a word | 2     |

| 6    | `tu` > `tsu` | at the start of a word |
| 6    | `tū` > `tsū` | at the start of a word |

The rows of one step are one change, written out sound by sound.

1. **The tones become pitch.** The acute and the grave fall away. The macron stays as length: a vowel in the mainland's high level tone is a long vowel on the islands.
2. **The _-ng_ becomes length.** A closing _ng_ is lost into the vowel before it, which is held long: _téng_ gives _tē_, _jing_ gives _jī_, _wōng_ gives _wō_. A closing _n_ stays, and is held as a beat of its own.
3. **Gliding vowels settle.** _ao_ and _ou_ become long _ō_ and _ū_, _iao_ becomes _yō_ and loses its glide after _ch_, _uo_ becomes _o_, _ie_ becomes _e_, and a long _ēi_ becomes _ē_. The pairs _ai_, _ei_ and _ui_ stay.
4. **Two consonants change.** _zh_ becomes _z_ and _l_ becomes the tapped _r_.
5. **Before _u_**, an opening _h_ becomes _f_.
6. **Before _u_**, an opening _t_ becomes _ts_.

**Pitch.** A root in the high level or the rising tone is said high; a root in the falling or the low tone is said low. The pitch is not written. Where two roots fall together in writing, the pitch parts them when it differs, and nothing in speech parts them when it does not.

## Building words

A compound sets two words side by side, the modifier first, as on the mainland: _ho_, fire, and _shū_, water, make _hojū_, a hot spring. A name is written as one word; a title or an office keeps its words apart.

### Voicing

Inside a compound, a second part that opens on a voiceless consonant takes the voiced one. A word with such an opening stands alone in its plain form and closes a compound in its voiced form, and that compound form stands in the suffix table, where the guard holds it to this table.

| Plain | Voiced |
| ----- | ------ |
| `k`   | `g`    |
| `t`   | `d`    |
| `ch`  | `j`    |
| `ts`  | `z`    |
| `s`   | `z`    |
| `sh`  | `j`    |
| `h`   | `b`    |
| `f`   | `b`    |

The mountain _chai_ stands alone with its _ch_ and closes a compound as _-jai_: _jen_ and _-jai_ make _jenjai_, the Nine Mountains in the native reading. A word that opens on a vowel, a nasal, _r_, _w_, _y_ or a voiced stop closes a compound unchanged, and two words otherwise meet without change.

### Tiers and forms

[[skill-jurthatlng|Jürthāti]] places every act it names. A verb takes one of three tiers: the **humble** `-nū` for the speaker's own act and his household's, the plain stem for an act of no standing, and the **honorific** `-dān` for the act of anyone above the speaker. On the tier sits the **polite** `-rē` when the listener is owed courtesy, and on that the **witnessed** `-wū` when a third person of standing hears it. Three tiers, polite or plain, witnessed or not, make twelve forms of every verb, and the Honorific grid table below gives them for _wa_, to answer. The tier is decided by the subject, the polite form by the listener and the witnessed form by whoever else is in the room, so a retainer reporting his lord's answer to a guest in the lord's hall says _wadānrēwū_.

Counters are set on a number, and the number on what it counts: _wennō wo_, five warriors. A counter is chosen by the shape of the thing counted.

## Making names

**A person** of Jénchài carries the house name first and the personal name after it. Among the retainer classes the personal name is granted, most often at coming of age or for a service worth marking, and it is granted again when a man changes lords, takes vows or survives a defeat that ought to have killed him; the old name is not used afterward. A granted name is a compound of two words, as _Rēwei_, dawn-jade, or _Tsuija_, iron-blade, and its second part is voiced as any compound's is. The lower castes hold no personal name.

**A great lord's house** takes its name from a feature of its island, so that the name is also a place on the island: a peak, a strait, a hot spring, a stand of cedar. The feature closes on _-jai_, place, as _Yanjai_, Cloud Peak, and _Hojūjai_, Hot Spring Place.

**A retainer family** takes its name from the office it has held longest, and an office that keeps records closes on _-jū_, as _Beshejū_, Records of the Salt.

**A title, a caste or a rite** is written in the learned reading, and every such name the notes give stands under `kept` in the register and in the table below with its native reading where the words reach it. A new native name follows the voicing and suffix rules on this page.
