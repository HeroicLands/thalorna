_Ithasul_ (precursor stone) is the substance of every work [[lore-theithari|The Ithari]] left, on [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara Continent]] and everywhere else. The custodians of the island, the [[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]], know more of it than anyone in the world, and all of what they know is handling and procedure: how a work looks and feels, what state it stands in, what may be done beside it and what may not. What it is, and what the Ithari gave the works, they have never identified. The account of the Ithari themselves, and of the works that stand elsewhere on [[place-worldthlrn|The World of Thalorna]], is The Ithari.

## In the Hand

To the eye a living work is smooth, softly lustrous and one even gray, with no vein, grain, cloud or mark anywhere on it. To the hand it gives a little, the way good leather gives, and comes back when the hand lifts; the eye and the hand do not agree, and a Kaliharan child grows up with the disagreement. Some of it is warm to the touch and some faintly lit after dark. No work bears writing of any kind.

Nothing harms it. No tool, fire, flood or working of any tradition has ever marked one, and time leaves it as it was on the day the Ithari walked away. A work stands where it was made, and no number of hands or beasts has ever shifted one.

## The States of a Work

A work is found in one of these states, and the first three are alive.

| State             | To the eye and hand               | What it does                                                            |
| ----------------- | --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Working           | Gray, giving                      | Its one office, as it always has                                        |
| Working badly     | Gray, giving                      | Its office weakly, intermittently, in the wrong place or not every time |
| Stopped           | Gray, giving                      | Nothing at all                                                          |
| White             | Bone-white and hard, with no give | Nothing; the work is dead                                               |
| White and cracked | Bone-white, hard and cracked      | Nothing; the only kind that has ever let a piece go                     |

Nothing goes part white: the whitening takes a whole work at once, and a white work can be told across a valley. The signs of a work going wrong are all in what it does and none in what it is, so a work working badly gives no warning of how long it has, and a stopped work cannot be told from a living one until somebody needs it. Nothing anyone does moves a work from one state to another. No rite, procedure or working has ever caused, hastened, halted or reversed a stopping, a whitening or a cracking, and no dark work has ever been woken; the archive at [[place-veshvaan|Veshvaan]] says so.

## What a Custodian Can Do

A custodian can:

- perform a work's procedures exactly, as they were handed down;
- detect drift in a procedure, by setting the [Seasonal Recitation]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}'s fresh transcription against the archive;
- observe the state a work is in;
- open a work that has an oval, by its liturgy;
- take the held stone a cracked work gives up, and record the failure;
- convene, through a silkan, the council that decides whether a procedure may be adjusted.

A custodian cannot:

- read a slab whose procedure has been lost;
- identify what the Ithari gave the works;
- cut, drill, pry, joint or take the smallest thing off a work, the island having blunted every tool it ever owned in trying;
- force an oval, by any means;
- move, tip or lift a work or any part of one;
- replace any part, or make new ithasul;
- wake a dark work.

## The Openings

Where a work has a way in, the way in is an oval in the wall, with no jamb, lintel or sill, and it is no door. Nothing swings, slides or turns on a hinge: the substance draws back from the center, widening smoothly and without a sound, and closes again the same way.

On Kalihara an opening is procedure, and the Ithakan hold it. The liturgy is like every other: a sequence in the archaic tongue and hands laid on named places in a named order, learned by rote and performed the same way each time. To a foreigner at the end of a quay it looks like a blessing, and that is as much of it as the island has let a foreigner see. The custodians know that it opens a work; the archive does not say why. Opening a work by procedure is the one thing the works do that a Kaliharan can make happen and nobody else in the world can, and the Council knows what that is worth. Nothing opens one by force, and no work has been opened by violence in any record the island keeps.

## The Held Stones

A _kaan'sul_ (held stone) is a piece of ithasul small enough to carry in the hand, and the island keeps two kinds: living and dead.

**A living held stone** belongs to one working work, and it is handed down within the lineage that holds that work. Each is shaped to a purpose of its own. It is gray like its work, and in the fist it gives like the rest of the substance and comes back; that is how a custodian tells a true held stone from a carved imitation in the dark. It warms in the hand while its work is tended. When its work goes dark the stone goes cold, and when the work dies the stone is a dead held stone from then on. None was taken from its work, since nothing is ever taken from a living work, and the archive does not say where any of them came from.

**A dead held stone** is bone-white and hard, with none of a living work's gray and none of its give, and a custodian knows one by feel the moment it is in the hand. A white work that has cracked is the only kind that has ever let a piece go. It yields only what it yields: no blow takes a piece off, and none has ever been taken from a living work, nor tried for.

Every dead held stone ends in a sanctuary's keeping, and it comes there by one of two roads. Most come from the [Rite of Last Tending]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, when the custodians take a single held stone from a cracked white work and carry it back to the sanctuary, and the rest are surrendered. When a living held stone's work dies the stone is dead from that day, and the lineage that kept it gives it up to a sanctuary. Every sanctuary keeps at least one. Dead held stones are the island's reference pieces for what becomes of the substance after a work dies, and a kaan'sili learns on them to tell dead stone from living, a held stone in one hand and a living work under the other. Custodians still call a dead held stone a fragment as often as not, as though it had been broken from something; nothing is ever broken from a work, and the two words name the same piece.
