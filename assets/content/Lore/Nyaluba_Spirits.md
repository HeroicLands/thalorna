The spirits are the active substance of the [[affiliation-nyalbpnthn|Nyáluba Way]]. Beneath the
single diffuse source – [[lore-mwangadty|Mwánga-Kúbwa]], which receives nothing – it is the
**guides** that Nyáluba religious life is directed at. They are not gods and they are not
worshipped. In the language of the tradition they are **venerated, appeased, or warded against**,
each according to its character and the need of the moment.

## Character

The guides are particular. They are rooted in specific places or specific lineages, and their
power – which can be considerable – is local to the territory or the kin-line they inhabit. A
hippopotamus-spirit that wards a stretch of river is powerful on that stretch and nowhere else. A
baobab-spirit that holds a village's gathering-tree keeps that village and no other. An
ancestor attends the descendants of the clan that remembers their name.

They are not transcendent and do not preside over domains of existence in the manner of the
Aurèldían or Kemetían gods. They are of the world, and the Nyáluba deal with them as kinsmen –
close or distant, beloved or feared – whose territories the clan shares. Every guide has its own
temperament, its own protocols, and its own history of dealings with the clans whose lives it
touches.

## The Three Postures

A Spirit-Speaker's craft consists, in large part, of knowing which posture a given guide requires
and which posture a situation calls for. The categories are the tradition's own; a griot asked to
describe the people's dealings with the spirits will name all three without prompting.

- **Venerate** – the benevolent guides: honored with regular offering, greeted at the seasons,
  sung of in the clan's memory-songs. A river-mother whose waters are clean, a baobab that keeps
  the gathering-tree, an ancestor whose counsel still guides the clan, the hearth-spirit of a
  compound. Most of Nyáluba religious life consists of such relationships.
- **Appease** – the severe or exacting guides: their demands met, their protocols followed
  without error, their offerings rendered exactly as tradition requires. A crocodile-kin of a
  dangerous crossing, a drought-spirit placated before the dry season, the spirit of a ridge
  where an elder was struck by lightning. Appeasement is precise and unforgiving of mistakes.
- **Ward against** – the dangerous and the hostile: avoided where possible, sung away where not,
  kept outside the bounds of the clan's life by fire, charm, protective object and the exact word
  of warding. The restless dead of cursed ground, the spirits of a place where a great wrong was
  done, the enmity a rival clan earned generations past. One does not bargain with what one wards
  against.

## The Kinds of Guide

The tradition imposes no rigid taxonomy, but seven kinds recur across the clans.

- **Totems** – the ancestral guides of a clan's lineage, and the ones with formal cults of their
  own: [[affiliation-nyalblion|Lion]] (Mvuzi), [[affiliation-nyalbcroc|Crocodile]] (Kambezi),
  [[affiliation-nyalbeagl|Eagle]] (Ngonzi), [[affiliation-nyalbleop|Leopard]] (Sengala) and
  [[affiliation-nyalbbaob|Baobab]] (Bombwe). A totem is not an animal but the ancestral presence
  from which the clan traces its identity, and the clan's conduct is bound by its nature.
- **Land-spirits** – whose domain is a place rather than a lineage: keepers of gathering-trees,
  ridge-spirits of sacred heights, spirits of crossings, cairns and thresholds. Many of the
  oldest sites are held by land-spirits that were ancient when the first clans arrived.
- **Water-spirits** – of rivers, pools, waterfalls and the great inland lakes. Addressed as
  "mothers" or as "fathers" depending on character, and consulted on travel, crossing, drowning,
  fishing and cleansing.
- **Hunt-spirits** – of particular game and particular hunting grounds. Addressed before the
  hunt, thanked after a kill, and given a specific share. A hunter who returns full-handed with
  no offering has broken his clan's standing pact.
- **Weather-spirits** – of storm, rain, wind and sun. More mobile than the territorial guides and
  regarded as less reliable; addressed in the moment rather than on a standing basis.
- **Ancestor-spirits** – named ancestors of a clan, personal to a lineage. A clan's genealogy is
  in effect the roll of its ancestors, and the griot who holds the genealogy keeps the connection
  alive.
- **Hearth-spirits** – of the household, the compound and the communal fire. Every household has
  one; a great village hearth may have a powerful one of long standing.

## Standing Pacts

Each clan holds a **standing pact** with every guide whose territory or lineage overlaps its own.
The pacts are not written, because the Nyáluba do not write: they are held in the griot's memory
and transmitted through the clan's oral tradition.

A pact records, at minimum, the guide's name (or the clan's name for it), the territory or
lineage it holds, the ceremonies it expects, the offerings it accepts, the times of year at which
it must be honored, and the conduct it forbids. A clan that keeps its pacts prospers; one that
breaks them suffers in specific and attributable ways – a failed hunt, a drowning at a crossing,
a child born still, a fire that takes a compound. The Nyáluba do not regard these as
superstition. They regard them as consequence.

Pacts are inherited, and their maintenance is a central obligation of the clan's griots, elders
and Spirit-Speakers. When a clan moves – by migration, war, or the drying of its water – new
pacts must be formed with the guides of the new ground, and this is understood to be perilous
work that can take generations to settle.

## Outsiders

Outsiders have no pacts and cannot form them. What they can do is refrain from transgression: not
desecrating a grove, not hunting what they have not been permitted to hunt, not entering a
totemic precinct uninvited, not disturbing a cairn or a river-shrine. Most clans extend that
minimum reciprocity to respectful visitors. Those who transgress discover – sometimes slowly –
that the guides take offense, and that the clans do not intercede for a transgressor.
