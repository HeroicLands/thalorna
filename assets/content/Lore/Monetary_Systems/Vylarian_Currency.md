A money-changer at the Grand Market Circle in [[place-orzara|Ôrzâra]] sets out three coins on the felt of his table for every newcomer who asks what a Vylarian price means. A thick gold coin sits on the left, a thin silver one in the middle and a silver wedge on the right. "Gold, you will see once or twice in your life, and you will not be handed it," he says. "Silver is what the city runs on. And the wedge is what you spend. Learn the three, and you can be cheated only in the ordinary ways."

**Scope:** The Vylarian currency is the standard currency of western and central Ankaris. It is legal tender in [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] and [[place-helionis|Heliónis]], the territory under direct imperial governance and a province on paper. It is the practical standard of commerce in the [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldían kingdoms]] and [[place-byzariargn|Byzaría]], the post-imperial commonwealth members. It is accepted as a foreign currency, alongside the local Khelâthi system, in the **Northern Fertile Region of [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]]**: [[place-bethuargn|Bethûa]], [[place-okharisrgn|Okháris]] and [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu Region]]. Outside these regions moneylenders will exchange it, but it is not the dominant medium of exchange.

The imperial [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]] mints and authorizes the currency. Provincial mints in Vylarian and Heliónite cities strike Ardini and Ogdeli under Tarmex standards, and Soldeni are minted only at Ôrzâra. The post-imperial polities mint no Vylarian coin of their own. Their commercial life draws on the imperial coinage without producing it.

## The Three Coins

| Coin              | Metal  | Common Use                                                                                                                                                                  | Conversion                 |
| ----------------- | ------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Solden**        | Gold   | Substantial transactions—land sales, inheritance settlements, large commercial contracts, military commission purchases (denominated in Soldeni even when settled in paper) | 160 Ard                    |
| **Ardin** (_Ard_) | Silver | The standard unit of account for ordinary commerce; the everyday large-denomination coin                                                                                    | 1 Ard                      |
| **Ogdel** (_Bit_) | Silver | Physical 1/8 of a Ard; the everyday small-denomination coin                                                                                                                 | 1/8 Ard / 1,280 per Solden |

The Tarmex has fixed the ratio (Solden : Ardin : Ogdel :: 1 : 160 : 1,280) for two centuries, and everyone treats it as inviolable. Proposals to add intermediate denominations, a half-Solden or a 20-Ard coin, have all been defeated, and the simplicity of the three-coin system counts as one of its commercial virtues.

### The Solden

The Solden is a small, thick gold coin, about the size of a thumbnail and roughly 20 mm across, holding about 15 grams of fine gold. That is the gold worth of 160 Ard at the ratio of one weight of gold to twelve of silver, less the same small charge for striking that the Ardin carries. The obverse bears the profile of the reigning Ôrzâs (the Vylarian emperor), renewed at each imperial accession, and older coin remains legal tender. The reverse bears the imperial _ôrkhaimâ_, the eagle of the legions, over the inscription _Zêl ôrkhaima thâlentôr ûthrimê_ ("Beneath the eagle stand the borders"). The edge is milled to prevent clipping.

The Solden is rare in circulation. Perhaps 200,000 coins exist empire-wide at any one time, most of them in noble household vaults, the [[affiliation-tarmex|imperial treasury's]] strongroom at Ôrzâra and the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar's]] chapter reserves. Most commoners go their whole lives without holding one. Even most Aerean nobles handle few of them physically, because their wealth is denominated _in_ Soldeni but held in deeded estates, moneylender deposits or paper script. A merchant who pulls out an Solden to settle a bill at a market stall draws immediate attention, and the gesture is the financial equivalent of unsheathing a sword.

### The Ardin (Ard)

The Ardin, colloquially the _Ard_, is the workhorse of Vylarian commerce. It is a thin silver coin roughly 18 mm across and 1.5 grams in weight, struck three parts silver to one part alloy. The silver in it, a little over 1.1 grams, is the metal worth of one Ard less the mint's small charge for striking, and a pound of silver is worth 400 Ard. The obverse bears the imperial eagle and cipher. The reverse bears the value mark and the issuing mint's stamp: the Ôrzâra mint mark, or the provincial mint mark on Vylarian and Heliónite provincial issues. The edge is milled.

The Ard is the unit of account for everyday large commerce: a day's beer for a tavern of regulars, a craftsman's weekly settlement with his apprentice, the price of a working tool, a chicken at market, a night's lodging at a respectable inn. It is too large a coin for very small purchases, since one Ard buys four loaves of common bread, which is why the Ogdel exists.

"Ard" is universal in informal speech. The formal "Ardin" appears only in legal documents, formal contracts and imperial accounting. A Vylarian who says "twenty Ard" means twenty Ardini, and a contract writes it "twenty Ardini" or, in legal hand, "XX Ardini."

### The Ogdel (Bit)

The Ogdel, colloquially the _Bit_, is physically one-eighth of a Ard, cut from the parent coin along the eight-segment pattern struck into the Ard's reverse. A cut Ogdel is a wedge-shaped piece of silver, triangular in outline, weighing roughly one-eighth of a Ard (under a fifth of a gram) and holding one-eighth of its silver. Moneylenders cut them, shopkeepers cut them, and anyone with a sharp blade and a need to make change cuts them. A worn Ard with all eight Bits cut from it leaves a small central remnant, which by convention goes back to the moneylender or to the Tarmex for re-minting.

The imperial and provincial mints also strike Bits in advance: clean wedges of silver, more uniform in weight than hand-cut ones, with a small imperial cipher on one face. Mint-struck Bits are preferred wherever weight matters, and hand-cut Bits are accepted but sometimes weighed. The two forms are interchangeable in casual use, and only mint-struck Bits are recognized at face value in formal contracts.

The Bit is the coin of everyday life: a loaf of bread, a tankard of beer, a tip to a porter, a hot pie at a market stall. Most common people handle Bits daily, Argos weekly and Soldeni never.

## Paper Script (Brief)

Physical Solden coin is rare and physical Ard coin is impractical to carry in bulk, so large-value commerce moves on **paper script** and not on coin. Three forms of script circulate:

- **Retail notes**—issued by an individual moneylender against his own reserves and honored locally, at the issuing house and at houses with reciprocal arrangements.
- **Traveler's notes**—issued by a moneylender, drawn on the [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]], and honored at any moneylender of the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]] across the commonwealth.
- **Master notes**—issued directly by the Tarmex Orzan in imperial territory, or by a Danvar chapter in non-imperial territory, for the largest commercial settlements.

Script is the medium in which most large transactions are actually settled, including all but the most prestigious commission purchases. The [[affiliation-tarmex|Tarmex Orzan]] note holds the full mechanics, the issuance and redemption fees and the political economy of the system.

## Practical Notes

### Coin Condition

Coins are inspected on transaction. A receiving party may discount worn coin, whose milled edges have rubbed smooth or whose images have faded, by a fraction of a percent, though refusing recognizably genuine coin is rude and generally not done in respectable commerce. **Clipped coin**, from which someone has shaved bits of silver or gold, counts as functionally counterfeit if the clipping is significant. The receiving party absorbs small clippings as a cost of commerce. The Tarmex periodically calls in old or worn coin for re-minting, and commercial parties usually forward worn coin to their moneylenders for exchange.

Any transaction of consequence weighs hand-cut Bits. A small balance is part of every moneylender's equipment, and serious merchants carry one too. Mint-struck Bits are accepted at face value without weighing.

### Counterfeiting

Counterfeiting any Vylarian coin is a capital crime under imperial law in imperial territory and under the local equivalent in post-imperial commonwealth members, all of which criminalize counterfeiting the standard currency. The Tarmex employs specialist assayers who examine suspect coin. The Censors of the Tarmex investigate counterfeiting rings, and the Censors of the Tolvan prosecute when imperial revenue is implicated. Authorities typically break up major counterfeiting operations within a year or two of their start. Small individual counterfeiters sometimes last longer but rarely make coin convincing enough to circulate beyond their own region.

### Exchange with Foreign Currency

Vylarian moneylenders at frontier and trading-port locations run currency-exchange services. A traveler arriving from Aû'Khelâthu can exchange Khelâthi weight-pieces for Ard at the moneylender's posted rate. The metal in the pieces sets the par (a Ard carries the silver of half a Khelâthi silver qelu), and the moneylender's spread on top moves with trade flows and generally favors him over the foreign-currency holder. A traveler heading the other way faces the mirror situation. A spread of 3–5% on Khelâthi-Vylarian exchange is typical of the major trade routes, and less-traveled exchanges, Vylarian-Vedyaran for instance, carry substantially larger spreads.

### Carrying Coin

Large sums of coin are heavy. One thousand Ard (about 6.25 Solden) weighs about 1.5 kilograms (3 1/3 pounds). Ten thousand Ard (about 62.5 Solden) weighs 15 kilograms (33 pounds), a heavy load for one person, and a hundred thousand is a cartload. Gold carries the same value in a sixteenth of the weight, but Soldeni are rare. For practical purposes larger sums travel as paper script, and moving coin at scale takes an armed convoy, which only the imperial treasury, the Tarmex's bullion movements between strongrooms and the rare commercial-house transfer with no paper available can afford.

A merchant on the road typically carries a modest mix. He keeps a handful of Soldeni, concealed, for emergency liquidity, perhaps 20–50 Ard in a belt purse for daily expenses, and a sealed traveler's note for the bulk of his working capital, sewn into a pocket or carried by a trusted servant. The Bits pile up from the day's change.
