**Scope:** The Aû'Khelâthu currency is the standard currency of the **Northern Fertile Region of Xerathia**—the empire of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] itself, the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethûa]], and the kingdom of [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]]. Outside the Northern Fertile Region it is exchangeable as foreign currency, principally against the [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian system]] through moneylenders at the trading ports of the Vylarian Sea.

The system is **weight-based rather than coined**. Where the Vylarian Aerarium Imperii strikes named coins (Aurion, Argentus, Octus) bearing the Augustar's image and the imperial Aquila, the Khelâthi temples attest **standardized weight-pieces** of copper, silver, and gold—small ingots, rings, or stamped wedges of fixed mass, marked with the issuing temple's seal certifying their weight and metal purity. The pieces are not called coins. The gold and silver pieces are _attested metal of known weight_, worth the metal in them; the copper pieces are temple tokens, worth the face their seal attests rather than their bronze. All of them are accepted at face value in commerce throughout the Northern Fertile Region without need for re-weighing.

The system is administered by the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]]—the temple-treasury institution of the Khelâthi priesthood—which maintains the reference weights against which any piece can be checked, issues and redeems the token copper, holds the great granaries that anchor the empire's grain-based credit system, and issues the **temple chits** that serve as the Northern Fertile Region's equivalent of paper script. [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethûa]] and [[affiliation-okharis|Okháris]] do not maintain native monetary systems; they use the Khelâthi system through commercial institutions (the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|matriarchal trade-houses]] of Bethûa, the [[affiliation-crwntrdskhrs|crown-chartered trades]] of Okháris) whose credit is ultimately backed by correspondent relationships with specific Khelâthi temples.

## The Three Metals and Two Units

| Metal      | Unit           | Mass    | Metal content                         | Conversion                                         |
| ---------- | -------------- | ------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Gold**   | Gezan          | ~22.7 g | five parts in six gold (~18.9 g fine) | 1 gold gezan = 10 gold qelu = 10 silver gezan      |
| **Gold**   | Qelu (Qezelet) | ~2.27 g | five parts in six gold (~1.89 g fine) | 1 gold qelu = 1/10 gold gezan = 1 silver gezan     |
| **Silver** | Gezan          | ~22.7 g | fine silver                           | 1 silver gezan = 10 silver qelu = 10 copper gezan  |
| **Silver** | Qelu (Qezelet) | ~2.27 g | fine silver                           | 1 silver qelu = 1/10 silver gezan = 1 copper gezan |
| **Copper** | Gezan          | ~22.7 g | bronze; a token worth its face        | 1 copper gezan = 10 copper qelu                    |
| **Copper** | Qelu (Qezelet) | ~2.27 g | bronze; a token worth its face        | 1 copper qelu = 1/10 copper gezan                  |

The gezan is one weight for all three metals, about 22.7 grams, and the qelu is a tenth of it. The three metals interconvert at fixed temple-attested ratios—**1 gold gezan = 10 silver gezan = 100 copper gezan**—which is to say that a gold piece is worth ten silver pieces of the same weight, and a silver piece ten copper pieces of the same weight. Silver is the standard: a silver gezan is a gezan of fine silver, and every Khelâthi price is reckoned against it. Gold is attested at five parts in six fine, which makes the gold in a gold gezan worth exactly ten silver gezan at the ratio gold and silver hold in every market around the Vylarian Sea—one weight of gold for twelve of silver—so that the temple ratio and the metal agree. Copper is token money, described below. The ratios are reaffirmed annually by the Gar-Aû's seal at the New Year's festival and are inviolable across the empire. Where rare market conditions cause the ratios to drift in informal trade, the temples re-attest by edict, and the ratios snap back to canonical.

### Token Copper

The copper pieces are not worth their metal. They are struck in bronze by the [[affiliation-garmelnu|Gar'Melnu]] under [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] supervision, and the bronze in a copper gezan is worth little more than a twentieth of its face. The face is what the temple's seal guarantees, and the guarantee is backed by silver:

- **Issue.** Only the Gár-Hálzi issues copper pieces, and only in exchange for silver at face—ten copper qelu for a silver qelu, ten copper gezan for a silver gezan. The silver taken in is held at the issuing chapter against the copper outstanding.
- **Redemption.** Any Gár-Hálzi chapter redeems copper pieces for silver at the same face, on demand, so a purse of copper is always worth its face in silver. The Bethûan trade-houses and Okhárian crown-trade offices with correspondent accounts take copper at face and return it through those accounts.
- **Raw copper.** Copper without a temple seal—foreign ingots, hack-copper, foreign copper coin, a melted piece—is metal, not money. The chapters and the Gar'Melnu buy it by weight at the market price of copper, about one copper gezan of face for a pound of metal, which is what the same pound fetches abroad. Importing raw copper buys nothing it would not buy at home; only the seal turns bronze into face value, and the seal is not for sale.
- **Melting.** Melting copper pieces down returns little more than a twentieth of their face, so they stay in circulation as money.

Counterfeiting the copper is the one way to profit from the gap between face and metal, and the temples treat it accordingly (see _Counterfeit and Debasement_ below).

The Khelâthi people speak in _gezan_ and _qelu_ with the metal specified by context: "the field cost twelve gold gezan" or "the loaf was three copper qelu." The unit _qezelet_ is the formal liturgical form of _qelu_; everyday speech uses qelu, formal documents and temple records use qezelet. The distinction parallels the Vylarian distinction between Argentus (formal) and Argo (colloquial).

## The Standardized Pieces

The temples produce, attest, and circulate pieces of metal in standardized weights. The pieces are small ingots, rings, or wedge-stamped fragments—never round coins (the round-coin form is a Vylarian and Helionite convention that the Khelâthi consider foreign). Each piece bears the issuing temple's seal, an indication of metal and weight, and (on the larger pieces) the regnal cypher of the Gar-Aû in whose reign it was attested.

| Denomination       | Mass    | Argo-equivalent value | Typical use                                                                               |
| ------------------ | ------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Copper Qelu piece  | ~2.3 g  | 0.2 Argo (~1½ Bits)   | Daily small purchases—bread, beer, market goods                                           |
| Copper Gezan piece | ~22.7 g | 2 Argo                | Everyday medium purchases—meals, a night's lodging                                        |
| Silver Qelu piece  | ~2.3 g  | 2 Argo                | Wages, common commerce                                                                    |
| Silver Gezan piece | ~22.7 g | 20 Argo               | Significant purchases—weapons, tack, fine cloth                                           |
| Gold Qelu piece    | ~2.3 g  | 20 Argo               | Same value as silver gezan but lighter; used for ceremonial gifts and high-status payment |
| Gold Gezan piece   | ~22.7 g | 200 Argo (1¼ Aurion)  | Large purchases, ceremonial offerings, temple tributes                                    |

A trader's purse normally contains a mix sized to his transaction needs. Cheap purchases (bread, beer, market goods) are paid in copper qelu or copper gezan; medium purchases in silver qelu or silver gezan; large purchases call for gold or temple chits. The metal a trader chooses for a given payment is itself a social signal—paying in gold gezan for a small purchase is conspicuous and ostentatious; paying in copper for a large purchase is insulting. The cultural expectation is that the metal match the transaction's scale and dignity.

For "making change," the temples and most market stalls maintain small balances against which pieces can be weighed in case of dispute, but the standardized pieces normally pass at face value without verification. A piece of unknown provenance—hack-silver from foreign trade, irregular pieces from minor uncertified mints, recovered pieces from old hoards—is weighed and assayed at receipt and accepted at intrinsic value, often at a small discount for the friction.

## Conversion with Vylarian Currency

The conversion ratio between the Vylarian and Khelâthi systems is fixed by the metal in the pieces. An Argentus carries the silver of half a silver qelu, and an Aurion the gold of eight-tenths of a gold gezan, so the par of exchange is:

| Vylarian | Khelâthi                                           |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| 1 Bit    | 1/16 silver qelu (5/8 copper qelu)                 |
| 1 Argo   | ½ silver qelu (5 copper qelu)                      |
| 1 Aurion | 80 silver qelu (= 8 silver gezan = 4/5 gold gezan) |

Working the other way:

| Khelâthi       | Vylarian               |
| -------------- | ---------------------- |
| 1 copper qelu  | 0.2 Argo (~1½ Bits)    |
| 1 copper gezan | 2 Argo                 |
| 1 silver qelu  | 2 Argo                 |
| 1 silver gezan | 20 Argo                |
| 1 gold qelu    | 20 Argo                |
| 1 gold gezan   | 200 Argo (= 1¼ Aurion) |

A SoHL item priced at _X_ Argo costs **X × 0.5 silver qelu**, or X ÷ 20 silver gezan (or the equivalent in any other metal). A 6 Argo dagger is 3 silver qelu; a 240 Argo Mail Shirt is 120 silver qelu—12 silver gezan, or 1.2 gold gezan, or any equivalent combination. The conversion is mechanical: no SoHL item's value field needs to change for a Khelâthi campaign.

The Vylarian Argo trades within the Northern Fertile Region at a small discount against its theoretical silver-content value—typically 3–5%—because of the friction of accepting foreign coin in a system that prefers temple-attested local pieces. Conversely, a Vylarian moneylender at a frontier port honors Khelâthi silver and gold pieces at their metal value minus a similar 3–5% spread. Copper pieces hold their face only for someone who can return them to a Gár-Hálzi chapter: the changers at the ports take them at face less the same spread, and beyond the ports they are bronze, taken by weight. The spread is the basic income of the **changers**—the specialist moneylenders who operate at the conversion boundaries.

## Grain Credit and the Temple Granaries

In addition to the metal-piece system, Aû'Khelâthu maintains a parallel **grain-credit economy** administered through the temple granaries. Every selat's principal temples hold great granaries—vast clay-walled silos of wheat, barley, and other staples—and serve as the empire's grain banks. Workers' wages, soldiers' rations, and tax payments often pass through the granaries as **lagar of grain** (one lagar ≈ 76 liters, roughly a quarter of a year's grain ration for an adult).

The granaries maintain ledger accounts for individuals, families, businesses, and noble houses; a depositor brings grain at harvest and receives credit in his account, drawing against it through the year for daily rations or for conversion to silver-qelu payment when needed. The exchange rate of grain to silver fluctuates seasonally—grain is cheap at harvest, expensive in the late dry season before the next flood—but is publicly posted at every major granary and is generally honored across the empire on the day of transaction.

For the Northern Fertile Region as a whole, grain-credit accounts are _Khelâthi_—held at Khelâthi temples and convertible only through Khelâthi granaries. Bethûan and Okhárian merchants doing substantial business with Aû'Khelâthu often maintain grain accounts at temples in cities they trade with regularly; this is a practical commercial arrangement, not a religious one, and the temple priesthood is comfortable serving foreign clients as long as the relationship is correct.

For game purposes, grain credit is unlikely to be a routine player concern—characters carrying physical coin and using temple chits will handle most transactions. Grain credit becomes relevant for substantial commercial play (a player merchant character) or for stories involving the granary system (famines, granary robberies, harvest-festival politics).

## Temple Chits

For transactions too large to carry as metal—multi-gezan deals, cross-territory remittance, large credit extensions—the Northern Fertile Region uses **temple chits**, issued by the [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] and honored at any Gár-Hálzi chapter across Aû'Khelâthu (and, by correspondent arrangement, at any associated Bethûan trade-house or Okhárian crown-trade office).

A chit is a small clay-sealed clay tablet (for small chits) or a sealed papyrus letter (for larger ones), bearing the issuing temple's seal, the value (in silver or gold gezan), the recipient's name or "to bearer," and the temple's registered serial mark. Functionally similar to a Vylarian traveler's note. Theologically different: the chit is an act of the temple, and the small fee the temple charges (typically 2–3% on issuance, comparable to the Aerarium's 2%) is framed as a votive offering rather than as a commercial commission.

The chit system extends across the Northern Fertile Region through correspondent arrangements between specific Khelâthi temples and specific commercial institutions in Bethûa and Okháris. A Bethûan matriarchal trade-house holding a correspondent account with the great temple at Galezkara can honor Gár-Hálzi chits drawn on that temple, and can issue its own local chits backed by its Galezkara account. The arrangement is private and varies by relationship; the trade-houses with the strongest Khelâthi correspondent networks become the principal banking institutions in Bethûan commercial life, despite holding no formal "banking" charter.

For travelers, the practical rule is: large sums move on temple chits; small sums move as physical metal pieces; the conversion happens at the nearest Gár-Hálzi chapter, correspondent trade-house, or correspondent crown-trade office.

## Practical Notes

### The Cultural Significance of Metal

The choice of metal in a Khelâthi payment carries social meaning that has no equivalent in the Vylarian coin system. Copper is the metal of ordinary commerce, daily life, and small transactions—paying in copper is unremarkable. Silver is the metal of significant transactions and respectful dealing—paying in silver dignifies the recipient and acknowledges the transaction's importance. Gold is the metal of high commerce, ceremonial gift-giving, temple offering, and noble-to-noble exchange—paying in gold for a small purchase is conspicuous (and possibly insulting, suggesting that the recipient cannot make change), while paying in copper for a large purchase is contemptuous.

A Khelâthi merchant negotiating a substantial deal will often discuss the _metal_ of the payment as well as the _amount_—"twelve silver gezan paid in gold qelu" is a different transaction from "twelve silver gezan paid in copper gezan pieces," even though the face value is identical. The first signals respect and continuing relationship; the second signals reluctance, low expectation, and possibly final settlement of a strained partnership.

### Hack-Metal and Unattested Pieces

Pieces of metal of unknown provenance—hack-silver, hack-gold, irregular pieces from foreign mints, recovered pieces from temple hoards—circulate alongside the temple-attested standard pieces but at a discount. The receiving merchant or moneylender weighs the unattested metal at his own balance, assays it for purity, and accepts it at intrinsic value minus a friction fee (typically 5–10%, depending on how foreign the metal looks). Some changers and frontier moneylenders specialize in handling hack-metal; the better ones can read regional smithing styles and identify the metal's likely origin within minutes.

### Counterfeit and Debasement

Counterfeiting an attested temple piece is a sacrilegious crime under Khelâthi religious law and an ordinary crime under Bethûan and Okhárian civil law. The copper pieces, whose face far exceeds their bronze, are the commonest target. Penalties are severe—the Khelâthi convict counterfeiters of attested pieces are ritually executed at the New Year's festival. The temples maintain assay specialists who can detect debased metal in a piece by weight, sound, and (for the most experienced) by visual inspection of the cut edge. Large counterfeit operations are typically detected within months and prosecuted vigorously; small individual counterfeiters operate longer in marginal regions but rarely produce pieces convincing enough to pass repeated assay.

### Carrying Weight

Like the Vylarian system, large sums of physical metal are heavy. 100 silver gezan (2,000 Argo) weighs about 2.3 kg (5 lb); 1,000 silver gezan weighs nearly 23 kg (50 lb), a burden for a single traveler. Gold carries the same value in a tenth of the weight, but gold pieces are few and conspicuous. Wealth above the carryable threshold moves on temple chits or trade-house correspondent arrangements. A Khelâthi merchant's purse in serious commerce typically contains: a handful of gold pieces (qelu or gezan) for emergency liquidity, perhaps 20–50 silver qelu for ordinary transactions, a small bag of copper for daily small purchases, and a sealed temple chit for the bulk of his working capital.
