## Overview

A counting-house clerk of [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective]] teaches a new factor three things in order. "First, most of the money you will handle is paper. Second, the coin you do carry comes in three metals and a fixed ratio. Third, the gold and the silver are worth their metal wherever you take them, the copper is worth its face only where it was struck, and you had better know why."

The Vedyaran banking system is the money and credit the Collective runs across the five seats of the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]]. It stands apart from the Vylarian treasury, [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]], and the Khelathi [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] alike. Both have asked the Collective for formal correspondent recognition, the Collective has declined them for generations, and it keeps its own system self-contained.

- **Paper.** The Collective is the principal banking institution of Vedyaran commerce as well as the federation of its great hereditary trading families, the _kulinas_. Its letter of credit sits above the coined system: a house that trusts another house's paper moves value between janapadas and city-states without moving coin at all.
- **The five seats.** [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chendrapur]], [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyalendra]], [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharalaya]] through its capital, [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rajepur Janapoda]] and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Danurkota Janapoda]] share the system, and the Collective's standing above any one realm gives the banking infrastructure the whole subcontinent for its range. [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnageri]] keeps its own weighing and its own treasury and holds no seat.
- **Who strikes coin.** Kingdoms and city-states strike coin and the janapadas do not. Chendrapur alone strikes the gold and the silver, and every kingdom and city-state strikes its own copper. A janapoda's commerce runs on the tomra of its neighbors' markets and on Chendrapur's silver, cleared through the Collective and not minted locally.

## The Three Coins

The clerk draws the table on a slate and has the factor copy it before saying anything else.

| Coin                          | Metal  | Struck by                                                                                                               | Common use                                                 | Conversion |
| ----------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| [[miscgear-suvarna\|Suvorna]] | Gold   | [[affiliation-chandrapur\|Chendrapur]], from Suvarnageri gold bought at the **Weighing**                                | Land, dowries, tribute, the largest commercial settlements | 16 chendra |
| [[miscgear-candra\|Chendra]]  | Silver | The [[place-moonhouse                                                                    \| Moon House]], at Chendrapur | Ordinary formal commerce—contracts, tolls, rents           | 8 tomra    |
| [[miscgear-tamra\|Tomra]]     | Copper | Every kingdom and city-state, independently                                                                             | Everyday trade—bread, wages, market change                 | 1 tomra    |

A suvorna is therefore worth 128 tomra. The suvorna and the chendra carry their metal, so either is assayed and exchanged for its gold or silver wherever it goes, and the metal comes to its face: by its metal a suvorna is four-fifths of a Vylarian Solden and a chendra eight Ardin. The tomra does not leave the realm that struck it at face value. A Shikharalaya tomra pays Shikharalaya's taxes and temple offerings and is redeemed at Shikharalaya's treasury; in Chendrapur it is a scrap of copper.

## Exchange With What the Coin Actually Meets

[[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] borders no Vylarian territory, and Vylarian coin reaches it anyway, by two routes and at second or third hand. It comes overland out of the cities of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], where it is the principal medium and where the march caravans pick it up, and it comes third-hand through Khelathi intermediaries at the sea. It arrives as metal and not as money, at a discount nobody bothers to publish, and no Vedyaran exchange quotes it.

The coin a Vedyaran trader actually exchanges stands at the three edges of the region: the northern passes, the southern sea, and the march road round the wall's end. The clerk takes them in that order.

- **At the passes.** [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] runs on barter and tribal credit in its tribal interior and strikes no coin of its own, though its urbanized trading cities have begun keeping moneylenders "along Vedyaran lines." Vedyaran coin circulates there by the metal in it and at no fixed rate, so beyond [[place-suryadvara|Suryadvara]] a suvorna or a chendra is weighed and not counted, and a tomra is scrap copper.
- **At the sea.** [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] attests metal by weight and strikes no coin. Where Vedyara has a suvorna, a chendra and a tomra, the Empire has gezan and qelu of copper, silver and gold. A suvorna or a chendra reaching a southern port is assayed and exchanged for its metal at the Khelathi rate, which comes to its face: 64 silver qelu for a suvorna and 4 for a chendra, less the changer's spread. A tomra there is unsealed copper, bought by weight like any other. The Collective's frontier moneylenders apply the same discount to Khelathi gezan pieces moving the other way. [[lore-tantsouclt|Tantsou]] traders reaching the eastern coast carry their own bureaucratic ledger-credit and exchange through the Empire's frontier posts on the same principle.
- **At the march road.** [[place-marchroad|The march road]] round the broken end of the wall is the one frontier a loaded camel crosses without climbing, and the bulk of what enters or leaves Vedyara by land goes that way. The Dunhari side settles in goods, in animals and in silver taken by weight, so a Collective factor at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandyapur]] or [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] finances the season against letters and the coin never moves at all. The foreign pieces that do move east on it, Vylarian and Taruzelian, are weighed at the same counters and pass on their metal.

The clerk's last rule is the shortest: away from the treasury that struck it, the metal is the money.
