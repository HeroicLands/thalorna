## Overview

A counting-house clerk of the [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|Merchant Collective]] teaches a new factor three things in order. "First, most of the money you will handle is paper. Second, the coin you do carry comes in three metals and a fixed ratio. Third, the moment you leave the region, none of it is worth what its face says, and you had better know why."

The Vedyaran banking system is the money and credit the Collective runs across the five seats of the [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|Assembly of the Compact]]. It stands apart from the Vylarian [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]] and the Khelâthi [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gár-Hálzi]] alike. Both have asked the Collective for formal correspondent recognition, the Collective has declined them for generations, and it keeps its own system self-contained.

- **Paper.** The Collective is the principal banking institution of Vedyaran commerce as well as the federation of its great hereditary trading families, the _kulinas_. Its letter of credit sits above the coined system: a house that trusts another house's paper moves value between janapadas and city-states without moving coin at all.
- **The five seats.** [[affiliation-chandrapur|Chandrapur]], [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyālendra]], [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Vindhyālaya]] through its capital, [[affiliation-rajaprjnpd|Rājapur]] and [[affiliation-dhnrktjnpd|Dhanurkota]] share the system, and the Collective's standing above any one realm gives the banking infrastructure the whole subcontinent for its range. [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|Suvarnagiri]] keeps its own weighing and its own treasury and holds no seat.
- **Who strikes coin.** Kingdoms and city-states strike coin and the janapadas do not. A janapada's commerce runs on whichever city's or kingdom's coin its traders carry, cleared through the Collective and not minted locally.

## The Three Coins

The clerk draws the table on a slate and has the factor copy it before saying anything else.

| Coin                          | Metal  | Struck by                                                                                                               | Common use                                                 | Conversion |
| ----------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| [[miscgear-suvarna\|Suvarna]] | Gold   | [[affiliation-chandrapur\|Chandrapur]], from Suvarnagiri gold bought at the **Weighing**                                | Land, dowries, tribute, the largest commercial settlements | 16 candra  |
| [[miscgear-candra\|Candra]]   | Silver | The [[place-moonhouse                                                                    \| Moon House]], at Chandrapur | Ordinary formal commerce—contracts, tolls, rents           | 8 tāmra    |
| [[miscgear-tamra\|Tāmra]]     | Copper | Every kingdom and city-state, independently                                                                             | Everyday trade—bread, wages, market change                 | 1 tāmra    |

A suvarna is therefore worth 128 tāmra. The tāmra is the only coin whose mint varies by court, and its weight follows the mint only approximately. Ordinary trade does not tell a Chandrapur tāmra from a Dhanurkota one, and only a large settlement is weighed and not counted.

## Exchange With What the Coin Actually Meets

[[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] borders no Vylarian territory, and Vylarian coin reaches it anyway, by two routes and at second or third hand. It comes overland out of the cities of the [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate of Amradad]], where it is the principal medium and where the march caravans pick it up, and it comes third-hand through Khelâthi intermediaries at the sea. It arrives as metal and not as money, at a discount nobody bothers to publish, and no Vedyaran exchange quotes it.

The coin a Vedyaran trader actually exchanges stands at the three edges of the region: the northern passes, the southern sea, and the march road round the wall's end. The clerk takes them in that order.

- **At the passes.** [[affiliation-khzrncnfdrtn|Khazryn]] runs on barter and tribal credit in its tribal interior and strikes no coin of its own, though its urbanized trading cities have begun keeping moneylenders "along Vedyaran lines." Vedyaran coin circulates there by the metal in it and at no fixed rate, so beyond [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]] a tāmra or a candra is weighed and not counted.
- **At the sea.** [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] attests metal by weight and strikes no coin. Where Vedyara has a suvarna, a candra and a tāmra, the Empire has gezan and qelu of copper, silver and gold. A Vedyaran coin reaching a southern port is assayed and exchanged for its metal content at the Khelâthi rate and is never accepted at face value. The Collective's frontier moneylenders apply the same discount to Khelâthi gezan pieces moving the other way. [[lore-tanvuriclt|Tānvüri]] traders reaching the eastern coast carry their own bureaucratic ledger-credit and exchange through the Empire's frontier posts on the same principle.
- **At the march road.** [[place-marchroad|The march road]] round the broken end of the wall is the one frontier a loaded camel crosses without climbing, and the bulk of what enters or leaves Vedyara by land goes that way. The Dunhari side settles in goods, in animals and in silver taken by weight, so a Collective factor at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandhyāpur]] or [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] finances the season against letters and the coin never moves at all. The foreign pieces that do move east on it, Vylarian and Haradian, are weighed at the same counters and pass on their metal.

The clerk's last rule is the shortest: outside the five seats, the metal is the money.
