# terran_analog: Ki'ichek reads a Mayan register—glottal stops, ejective consonants, counting by twenties, titles built with a lord-ending and a servant-ending. The words on this page are made to the rules and none is the Yucatec, K'iche' or Nahuatl word for its meaning.
---

A scribe of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ix'ilankul]] learns two things before a single glyph: the counts, and the endings. A word in [[skill-kicheklng|Ki'ichek]] names a thing, and an ending says who stands toward it: _p'ilam_ is a glyph, and the _p'ilambal_ is the one who serves the glyphs; _tz'en_ is the sun, the _tz'enmah_ is the lord who rules under it, and the _tz'enbal_ is the priest who keeps its count. The same handful of endings make every title in a K'ich'chik court, so a ruler's whole household can be read from its titles alone.

This page is the word-hoard of the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]] city-states: the words the language note uses, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives to the continent's realms, gods, rites and scripts.

The sound of Ki'ichek is percussive. A stop may be said with a catch in the throat, written with an apostrophe after it, and a bare apostrophe is the catch alone, a glottal stop: _k'ayik_, _ne'_, _ix'bal_. Stress falls on the second-last syllable, and the five vowels never glide into one another.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun bare, a verb bare. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the pieces the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what they make.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Building words

Two moves make every built word on this page. **A compound** sets two words side by side, the first naming what the second is about: _ne'_ water and _tz'ik_ above make _ne'tz'ik_, an island, what stands above the water. **An ending on a word** says what part the word plays, and the endings stand below. A title takes its ending last, so _tz'en_ with _-il_ and then _-bal_ is _tz'enilbal_, the prince, the one who serves what belongs to the sun.

### Suffixes

| Suffix  | Stem  | Makes                                               | Example                                           |
| ------- | ----- | --------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| `-bal`  | added | the one who serves it, or a person of that standing | _p'ilam_ > _p'ilambal_, a scribe                  |
| `-mah`  | added | the lord of it                                      | _tz'en_ > _tz'enmah_, the Sun Lord                |
| `-alil` | added | one of its company                                  | _tz'om_ > _tz'omalil_, one who belongs            |
| `-al`   | added | the thing it rests in, or the one who tends it      | _tz'om_ > _tz'omal_, a foundation                 |
| `-il`   | added | belonging to it                                     | _tz'en_ > _tz'enil-_, of the sun, in _tz'enilbal_ |
| `-el`   | added | the thing its act makes                             | _k'otz-_ > _k'otzel_, the knotted cords           |
| `-ok`   | added | running with it, full of it                         | _ne'_ > _ne'ok_, flowing                          |

The grammar adds endings of its own that make no new word: a verb takes _ka-_, _na-_ or _ta-_ for present, past and future, and _-ix_, _-al_, _-um_, _-in_, _-alil_ or _-umil_ for the person; a noun takes _-ka_, _-il_, _-an_, _-uq_ or _-um_ for its case. The [[skill-kicheklng|language note]] sets them out in full.

## Words

### Sky, sun and the count of days

Four counts run at once, and each has its word: _tz'en_, the sun and its year; _tz'uqal_, the moon; _ix'ilan_, the stars; and _tq'an_, the count of 260 days that is also the word for an edge or a joining. A date is written as the meeting of several counts, and the fifty-two-year _K'ayik Kul'al_, the Living Flame, falls where _tz'en_ and _tq'an_ come back into phase. Zero, _tz'amal_, is a shell: the empty thing that holds a place.

### Rank, temple and court

A title is a word with an ending: _-mah_ makes the lord of a thing and _-bal_ the one who serves it. So the _tz'enmah_ is the Sun Lord and the _tz'enbal_ the priest who keeps his count; the _p'ilambal_ serves the glyphs, the _ch'alixbal_ serves the sacred, and the _ch'umbal_ carries a lord's spoken word along the roads. A people's members are _tz'omalil_, those who belong by the _tz'om_, the bond.

### Grammar words

Three short words say where a thing stands: _ch'al_ beneath it, _tz'ik_ above it, _xaq'al_ apart from it. The numbers count by twenties. One to ten are single words; eleven to nineteen are _olek'_, ten, followed by the unit as a second word, so _olek' lixe_ is thirteen. Above them stand the powers of twenty: _tz'ob_, _pel'ux_, _q'umpel_ and _xolemax_.

## Making names

**A K'ich'chik person** carries a compound of words, each element a word with its meaning, and the name reads as a short statement: _Ne' Ixkul_, water within the lake; _Tzul Ne'ok_, strength flows. A commoner carries two elements; three or more mark a noble, a priest or a person of great deeds, and such a name is shortened at home to its first element, never to a title or a connector. Titles are appended after the full name and never fused to it.

**A realm or a city** is named as a person is, by words set side by side: _Xelem_, the land of birds; _P'enqal Ch'akal_, the great grassland; _Ix'ilankul_, the seat of the stars, with _-kul_, the seat, closing the name of a city that rules.

**A god** is named by two words, a thing and its quality, and the Itzáni faiths carry those names whole: _K'ayik Ch'alix_, the sacred flame; _Tz'en'ul Tq'an_, the sun at the world's edge.
