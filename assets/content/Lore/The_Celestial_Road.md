A caravan-master explains the road to a young factor on his first crossing, and he begins with the arithmetic. Silk costs a hundred weights of silver at the Tānvüri customs-house and sells for four or five hundred at [[affiliation-yesilhan|Yeşilhan]]. The difference pays the tribes, the camels, the riders and the dead, and on a good crossing a little remains.

This note sets out the road: its route and length, the wells and the hand-offs, the beasts, the goods, what each stretch costs, who guarantees what, and why merchants pay all of it instead of going by sea.

## The Route

The road runs about 2,500 miles from the last fortified caravanserai of Byzaría's [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]] to the garrison and customs-house on [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]]'s western pass. It bends from well to well, so it is a tenth longer than the straight line. East-bound it crosses three of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]]'s zones.

| Stretch                      | Miles          | Days      | The ground                                                              |
| ---------------------------- | -------------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [[place-wlqtrrgn\|Welqator]] | 0 to 550       | 0 to 30   | grass and scrub; a spring every day or two; the easy stretch            |
| [[place-hskrrgn\|Hosikor]]   | 550 to 1,700   | 30 to 96  | the Long Dry, then sand seas and gravel; wells three to five days apart |
| [[place-idwkrrgn\|Idwakor]]  | 1,700 to 2,500 | 96 to 130 | stone desert and cold high steppe; stages of four to seven days         |

The rest of the Khazryn lies off the road. [[place-gwtkrrgn|Gwatekor]] is forest to the north, [[place-dkrqrrgn|Dikraqor]] is stone to the south, and the [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] beyond it is reached by a spur.

**Branches.** A northern branch comes down from Velanthia's eastern reach and joins the road before Qìso, carrying furs and amber. A southern spur leaves Qìso, crosses [[place-dkrqrrgn|Dikraqor]] in about twenty days and reaches the Tellumel. From there roads run west to Amradad and south to the pass-towns of [[place-meghadvara|Meghadvāra]] and [[place-suryadvara|Sūryadvāra]].

## Beasts

The two-humped camel carries the road. It takes cold and heat, goes five to seven days between wells with a full load, and eats what grows. The one-humped camel works the Tellumel and the Dunhari side. Horses carry the riders and escort, and are driven east as goods, since a horse walks itself to market. Mules and yaks work only the Grazian passes.

**No wheeled vehicle crosses**, for four reasons that any caravan-master gives. Draught oxen drink every day, and the wells are days apart. The sand seas swallow a wheel, and the gravel and the salt crust break an axle. There is no timber for a repair between the March and Tānvür. And the wells are dug, roofed and priced for camels. The March's paved road stops at the pass, and so does every cart.

## Goods

The cargo is light and dear. Westward go silk, fine porcelain packed small, brick tea, lacquer, jade, spices and medicines. Eastward go silver and gold by weight, amber, coral, glass, dyestuffs and horses. Nothing bulky goes the whole way. Grain, salt, timber and cloth by the bale are not worth the price of crossing, and the caravans that carry them travel a stage at a time and change hands at Qìso.

## Costs

Silver is counted in weights, and these figures give a scale for one camel-load of silk crossing west. They are the master's reckoning, and no tribe's price list.

| Item                                                       | Weights of silver |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| bought at the Tānvüri customs-house                        | 100               |
| passage through the tribes of Idwakor                      | 10                |
| passage through the tribes of Hosikor, the longest stretch | 17                |
| passage through the tribes of Welqator                     | 10                |
| riders, water at owned wells, fodder                       | 15                |
| the March's customs                                        | 5                 |
| losses spread over the fleet, about one string in eight    | 15                |
| **landed at Yeşilhan**                                     | **about 170**     |
| **sold at Yeşilhan**                                       | **400 to 600**    |

That margin is what makes the goods dear and the tribes rich. It also explains why caravans travel heavily armed even with passage bought: passage buys vengeance, not safety.

## Passage, Tribe by Tribe

No one sells the road whole. Each tribe sells its own stretch, a few days to a week or more of wells, and the master buys from every one: about thirty-five tribes between the March and Tānvür. A tribe's price covers three things.

- **Water.** The right to draw at the tribe's named wells and nowhere else.
- **A rider.** A _sowid_ of the tribe who joins the caravan, speaks for it to every herdsman he meets, and answers for it with his own life.
- **The tribe's word.** A promise of restitution if its own people rob the caravan, and of a feud fought on the caravan's behalf if outsiders do inside its range.

A caravan that has bought none of these, or leaves the named wells, is lawful prey to anyone who finds it. The master's real asset is his list of agreements: which tribes he has paid and which he owes, which rider is whose cousin, which two ranges are at feud this year and must be crossed in a particular order. Masters pass the lists to their sons.

**Who guarantees what:**

| Party                       | Guarantees                                                                                 | Owes                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| The tribe whose range it is | water at its named wells; a rider; restitution for its people's theft; vengeance on others | nothing beyond its range                                                                               |
| The rider                   | the route, the wells and the hand-off; his life as surety                                  | a rider who loses his caravan is not taken back by his tribe                                           |
| The caravan-master          | payment, in goods or in the credit of his backing house                                    | the road-peace: no hunting, no drawing at unnamed water, no fouling a well, no armed men off the track |
| The next tribe              | takes the caravan over against the _kiput_, the token the outgoing rider carries           | nothing until the token is shown                                                                       |

The token is a tally of cord or clay cut with the sign of the tribe that issued it. A string of tokens is a caravan's passport, and at the two hand-off wells the riders of one stretch give the caravan to the riders of the next in front of witnesses from both. Backing houses stand surety by the practice described in [[lore-kinhalcrdt|Kin and Hall Credit]], and a master who cannot pay a tribe in goods pays in his house's name.

## The Crossing

A loaded camel string makes 20 to 25 miles on a marching day, starting before dawn and halting in the heat. It rests one day in seven at a good well and three to five days at each of the larger toll-camps, while the price is argued and the beasts recover. That makes about 105 marching days and 20 to 30 halt days: **125 to 135 days end to end**, a little over four months. A caravan that pays every toll promptly and finds every rider waiting makes the crossing in 115. One that must go round a thin blood-field or wait out a sandstorm takes 150.

A courier with remounts and the right tokens crosses in about **45 days**. The tribes sell this service too, at a price only a court pays.

The road runs in spring and autumn. Summer kills beasts in the central sands, and winter closes Idwakor with snow. A caravan leaves the March at the spring equinox, to be across the sands before the heat, or at the end of summer, and reaches Idwakor at the first snows. Two fleets a year in each direction is the whole through traffic.

## Why Overland

A merchant in Tānvür can send his silk by sea, and few do. Every leg of the sea route is worse than the one before it.

1. **Pirates.** Strongholds on the islands between Tānvür and Vedyara harass the shipping of southern Tānvür, and make the first leg perilous.
2. **Vedyara.** Rounding the subcontinent is hard, and its passes are no road west: each of the six carries three or four caravans a year, and the guides keep half the value.
3. **The way on by land.** The shortest road from Vedyara's western end to Amradad crosses Dunhara, desert and tribal land, or goes through Aû'Khelâthu, where the [[lore-garauu|Gar-Aû]] takes a heavy cut on all goods bound for the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]].
4. **The way on by sea.** The Vylarian Sea opens to the ocean only through a narrow western strait, so a ship bound from Tānvür to [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] must sail round the whole of [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]], the longest voyage in the world.

The sea passage costs as many months as the camels and loses a larger share of what it carries. The Khazryn road is shorter, its losses are known, and every toll on it can be reckoned before the caravan leaves the March.

## Trouble on the Road

- A tribe has closed its wells to every caravan because its _dowek_ was killed by a rider of a neighboring tribe, and the rider's tribe has sworn that its range was never at fault.
- A great leader is gathering tribes along the road into an _orqwenoq_, and his first act has been to double every price.
- A caravan master has died on the march and the heir cannot read his father's list.
- A string has come out of the Long Dry with half its camels dead and its water gone, and a guide of the hidden-water bands at the edge of the sand is offering to sell it more than water.
