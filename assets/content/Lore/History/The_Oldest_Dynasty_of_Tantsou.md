Around 2500 BF a clerk of the Bureau of Records entered a dynasty, an emperor and a year in a register that the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] still keeps, and that entry is the oldest point at which any human institution can produce all three together. Every dynasty since runs from it, more than a dozen of them, and the archive that holds the entry is the oldest human archive anyone can still read.

The empire keeps two beginnings and treats them as one. The [[lore-celestlrdr|Celestial Order]] teaches that the first emperor was appointed at [[lore-greatcnvrg|the Great Convergence]], the zero of the [[lore-clndrstrlgy|Celestial Calendar]], some thirty-seven centuries before the Bureau's first entry. The Tanchéi explain the gap as a matter of custody: the mortal Bureau was not yet staffed in those early cycles, so the earliest dynasties' filings went upward into the celestial archive, and a record the mortal plane was never issued is no hole in the past. A provincial scholar who suggests otherwise in writing is not promoted.

The Bureau's first entry falls in a remarkable century. [[lore-khazarturn|Khazartúrn]], the greatest work of the Khazari, had been cut into its mountain for twenty-six centuries and had seventy years left before it fell. The oldest human archive and the greatest work of the Elder peoples stood in the world together for one lifetime, and neither record mentions the other: no Tantsou document of that century names Khazartúrn or the Khazari, and the Khazari account of the fall names no human realm at all.
