The **Zulaten** is the Khelâthi country of the dead, and it is not a rest. A soul that passes the weighing arrives somewhere it must eat, work and get on, exactly as it did in the valley, which is why the living send grain, tools and servants down with the body and keep up the offerings afterward. The road to it is the night half of the sun's circuit, the half that runs beneath the world while [[lore-uqaadty|Uqa'â]] crosses it from west to east in the dark.

"You will walk the sun's road, but without the sun," a priest of [[lore-wethurdty|Wethûr]] tells a widow waiting at the embalmers' door. "People ask me what the Zulaten is like. It is like the valley, with the same work and the same appetites, and the dead who arrive in credit are glad of every kindness they were owed." Only a soul whose accounts close arrives at all. One that is short on either is taken at the weighing by **Álgit**, the Devourer of the Dead.

The tradition of the [[lore-khelunulekha|Khelunu Lekhau]] insists the road is one road. Day and night are the two halves of a single journey, not two journeys, and a lekhau who commands only the first half can work only until sundown. The workings that protect the dead, guide the soul and ward the tomb belong to [[affiliation-garithnetau|Gar-Ithnetáu]], the House of the Zulaten, and to no other House.
