A ship bound for [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]] hears two registers of one tongue. On the quays of [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] and the smaller ports the islanders speak [[skill-kalihrlng|Kalihári]] quickly, threaded with the harbor pidgin; in the interior the custodians speak it slowly, and name their ranks and their tending in it: the _kaan'sili_ who watches, the _kaan'vaar_ who holds. This page is the word-hoard of both: the stems, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives on the island.

A Kalihári word is built of stems set side by side. _Itha-_ is still and _vanu-_ is water, and _ithavanu_ is still water, a lineage name. Where two stems meet in a custodial word, formal speech marks the seam with a catch in the throat, written `'`: _kaan-_ is to keep and _sili-_ is to watch, and _kaan'sili_ is the one who keeps by watching, a custodian in training. A handful of endings make a person of a stem or set an act going: _kula-_ is to flow, and _kulanei_ is one who flows.

The sound of Kalihári is open and liquid. Consonants are few and soft, nearly every syllable ends on its vowel, and vowels stand together freely, each its own syllable unless it makes one of the diphthongs the [[skill-kalihrlng|language note]] names. Stress falls on the second-last syllable.

## How to read the tables

Each word table has these columns:

- **Form** is the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its bare stem or an aspect. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone.
- **Class** says what kind of word it is, from the list below.
- **Gloss** is its meaning in brief.
- **Built from** is `—` for a root word. Otherwise it names the stems and the ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them, with the seam mark read past.
- **Attested** names the page that uses the word, or `—`.

## Sounds and spelling

### Letters

| Letter                  | Kind      | Sounds like                                                |
| ----------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| `k`, `t`, `p`           | consonant | the stops, said softly; the rarest sounds of the tongue    |
| `th`                    | consonant | the soft _th_ of _thin_                                    |
| `v`, `w`                | consonant | _v_, and _w_ as in _well_                                  |
| `h`, `s`                | consonant | _h_, and a soft _s_ heard chiefly in the custodians' words |
| `m`, `n`, `ng`          | consonant | the nasals; _ng_ as in _sing_                              |
| `l`, `r`                | consonant | the liquids; _l_ is flapped and _r_ lightly tapped         |
| `y`                     | consonant | _y_ as in _yes_                                            |
| `a`, `e`, `i`, `o`, `u` | vowel     | _father_, _bet_, _machine_, _note_, _flute_                |

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, and closes on a vowel or on one of the consonants below. A bound stem, written with a closing hyphen, closes on anything.

| Edge                         | Sounds              |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------- |
| Consonants that close a word | `n`, `l`, `r`, `th` |

### Clusters

Consonants stand apart, a vowel between each, inside a stem. Two meet where a stem closing on a consonant takes another stem, as in _kaan'sili_, and in a few old words such as _sílhari_.

| Rule                                   | Count |
| -------------------------------------- | ----- |
| Most consonants together inside a word | 2     |

### Marks and spellings read past

The pages mark stress with the acute and the seam of a custodial word with an apostrophe. Neither is a sound of the word itself: the seam mark is the catch formal speech makes between two stems, and plain speech runs the stems together. A word is read through the table below before it is held to the sound rules.

| Written | Read as | What it is                                  |
| ------- | ------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `'`     | —       | the seam between two stems, said as a catch |
| `’`     | —       | the seam mark, written curly                |
| `á`     | `a`     | the acute, a stress mark                    |
| `é`     | `e`     | the acute, a stress mark                    |
| `í`     | `i`     | the acute, a stress mark                    |
| `ó`     | `o`     | the acute, a stress mark                    |
| `ú`     | `u`     | the acute, a stress mark                    |

## Building words

Two moves make every built word on this page. **Stems set side by side** make a compound, the first stem marking the second: _koa-_ deep and _manu-_ root give _koamanu_, a deep root. In the custodians' words the seam between the stems is written `'`. **An ending on a stem** makes a person of it or sets its act in an aspect. The aspect endings are the three the [[skill-kalihrlng|language note]] gives for verbs; Kalihári marks how an act stands, ongoing, done or habitual, and leaves its time to the context.

### Suffixes

| Suffix   | Stem  | Makes                                                 | Example                                    |
| -------- | ----- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| `-nei`   | added | the one who does it, or who is marked by it           | _kula-_ > _kulanei_, one who flows         |
| `-an`    | added | the act going on, the ongoing aspect                  | _rith-_ > _rithan_, flowing                |
| `-a`     | added | the act done, the completed aspect                    | _rith-_ > _ritha_, has flowed              |
| `-athei` | added | the act as the nature of a thing, the habitual aspect | _rith-_ > _rithathei_, flows by its nature |

## Words

### The keeping

The custodians' words are compounds of a few old stems, and every rank of the [[affiliation-tharivaan|Ithári'kaan]] is a kind of keeping: by watching, by holding, by remembering what came before. The precursors themselves are _ithári_, those who came before, and the substance of their works is _ithári'sul_. The work of a lineage village is _sari'sul_, a tending-stone, and a piece of the substance small enough to carry is _kaan'sul_, a held stone.

## Making names

**A Kaliharan person** carries a given name and a lineage name, the given name first. A given name is often a stem with _-nei_, the one who does a thing: [[being-tvnrthr|Tavonei Reithuri]] is _tavo-_ with _-nei_, one who walks beside, a fitting name for a caravan guard. A lineage name is a compound of two stems of the island's ground and weather: _Reithuri_ is _rei-_ old and _thuri-_ wind. A given name of four syllables or more also has the short household name the [[skill-kalihrlng|language note]] describes, which is said and never written.

**A port** is a compound naming what the place is to the island: [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] is _ilo-_ door and _vanu-_ water, the water-door through which the world is admitted, and [[place-nalurithan|Nalurithan]] is _nalu-_ a river mouth with _rith-_ in its ongoing aspect, the running mouth on which the town stands.

**A byname** is a word of this page given to a stranger the port has no other name for. The old scholar of the pilots' observatory on [[place-telvari|Telvári]] is _Olitelinei_, the star-reckoner.
