## From the Spelling Guide to the Veldikko

- **Each consonant is its letter**, and its Roman spelling is lowercase. _p_, _v_, _m_, _t_, _s_, _n_, _d_, _k_, _h_, _l_ and _r_ are one letter each.
- **_ng_ and _dh_ are one letter each.** _N_ before _k_ is the letter _ng_ and then _k_. _Dh_ is the voiced fricative between vowels.
- **_g_ is _k_**, since Sinale has no _g_.
- **A sound with no letter is the nearest letter:** _b_ is _p_, _f_ and _w_ are _v_, _z_ is _s_, _c_, _q_ and _ch_ are _k_, _x_ is _k_ then _s_, and _j_ is the vowel _y_. No roman letter is left in the text.
- **_s_ followed by _h_ writes both consonants**, including where _-ho_ follows a stem ending in _s_.
- **A doubled consonant is its letter once, with the length bar beneath it.**
- **A vowel is the mark over the consonant it follows.** _A_, _o_, _u_, _e_, _i_ and _y_ are six marks, and the front vowel _ì_ is written with the _y_ mark.
- **A vowel at the start of a word, or sounded apart from the one before it, stands on the carrier.**
- **A doubled _aa_, _ii_ or _uu_ is one long mark**, and any other doubled vowel, such as _ee_, is two marks, the second on the carrier.
- **A diphthong is two marks over one letter**, in the order they are said: _ai_, _au_, _ia_, _eo_, _oi_, _ou_, _ui_, _uo_, _ei_, _ie_, _yi_ and _ye_.
- **Vowels are grouped from left to right**, a held vowel or a diphthong taken wherever it begins and each letter in one group: _kaai_ is the long _a_ and then _i_ on the carrier, and _koaa_ is _o_ and then the long _a_ on the carrier.
- **A hyphen is a word space**, and at the start or end of a word it writes nothing.
- **An acute is the stress sign after its vowel.** It marks a stress the common rule would not choose, as [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]] reads it: _ká_ is the vowel _a_ and then the stress sign. On a held vowel or a diphthong the acute sits on the first letter: _káa_ is the long _a_ and then the stress sign, and _kái_ is _a_, the stress sign and _i_. An acute on the second letter splits the pair, and the stressed vowel stands on the carrier: _kaá_ and _kaí_ are two vowels sounded in turn.
- **Pitch is written on a held vowel:** a caron is rising pitch and a circumflex falling pitch, as [[doc-prnncthlrn#tone|Pronouncing Thalorna]] reads them. The mark sits on the first letter of _aa_, _ii_ or _uu_, and the marked vowel is one long mark followed by the rise or fall sign: _onsâa_, _ovûu_. A tone on a short vowel, a vowel pair, a diphthong or the second letter of a held vowel has no spelling, and neither has a letter carrying both a tone and an acute. The groups are read before the tone is placed: _paaâa_ is the long _a_ and then the long _a_ with falling pitch on the carrier, but _paâa_ reads _aâ_ first, a tone on a held vowel's second letter, and has no spelling. Two toned held vowels in a row directly after a group that runs into the first of them, as in _paaâaâa_, have no spelling either. Ordinary writing may omit pitch.

Punctuation, quotation marks, verse boundaries and name marks follow the shared Roman standard. Literal glyph codes between `⟦` and `⟧` select the font’s signs directly.
