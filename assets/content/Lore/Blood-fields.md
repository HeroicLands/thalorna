Every people that has fought a great war knows the ground the war left behind, and every one of them tells a traveler the same thing first: do not cross it after dark. A **blood-field** is a place where so many died at one place and one time, unreceived by the rites their own people keep for the dead, that the boundary between the material world and the spirit realm tore, and the tear never closed. The battle ended. The field is still there, and it still has to be walked past.

This note sets out what the peoples of Thalorna agree a blood-field is, how they reckon its danger, and what each of them does about it. The facts are common to all of them; the explanations belong to each people's funeral rite, and each section below gives one people's account in its own terms.

## What a Blood-field Is

An [[lore-aralwen|arálwen]] is a natural thinness, old beyond reckoning, where the two worlds lie close enough to cross. A blood-field is an injury. It is made at a moment that can be dated, it takes its character from the battle that made it, and nobody keeps it the way the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] keep the arálwain.

## What Makes One

A blood-field needs many hundreds or thousands of dead in a few hours or days, and it needs most of them to go unreceived. The spirit realm is most dangerous to a soul in the moment after death, when the predators that hunt the unprotected are closest; a great many souls loosed in one place give that place a character of its own in the realm; and a soul that no god, ancestor pool or totem receives is hunted until it is consumed. A battlefield the dead were taken from in order leaves a battlefield. A battlefield the dead were left on, unburned, unburied, unnamed or unjudged, leaves a wound.

Each people explains the wound by the rite its dead went without:

- **Aû'Khelâthu:** the dead had no tomb, no name read aloud and no rites, so they had no continuation.
- **Vedyara:** no conch was sounded, no threshold lamp lit and no pyre kindled, so the way behind the souls never closed.
- **The Nordlands:** no grave-ale was drunk for them, so the dead ride with the Jól-Ride and never leave.
- **The Varokh:** no Shaman called the dead home to their totem.
- **Vylaria and Aurèldía:** the dead were not judged in order before [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]].

## What Does Not

Single deaths do not make a blood-field, and neither do small wars or burnings. A janapada's militia war, a Varokh feud between villages or a raid on a farmstead kills by the dozen, and the dead are carried home to their rites. A sickness that empties a village leaves a sad place and an unlucky one. A hall burned with its people inside it was its own pyre, and its dead passed. A cursed road and a sorcery laid on an enclave leave haunted or sealed ground of their own kind. None of these is ever called a blood-field, and a people that names one carefully knows the difference.

## The Five Kinds

A field's kind is fixed at its making and does not change unless somebody works on it. There are five, and every people that keeps a reckoning of its fields sorts them the same way.

- **Recurrence**, the commonest by far. The battle repeats: drums on a certain wind, lights along a treeline, ranks of figures seen at a distance and never close to.
- **The quickening.** The ground grows too well. Grass comes early and crops come heavy, and the fields are farmed because they are the best land in the district.
- **The silence.** Nothing sings there. No birds, no insects in summer, no sound but weather, and in some fields no flame that will stay lit.
- **The dread.** The field returns what was felt on it: panic, rage, or a terrible unwilling steadiness, coming on those who linger as though it were their own.
- **The withering**, the rarest. The land dies outward from the field: crops fail, beasts sicken and the soil sours, and the sickness spreads until it ruins a district.

## How Thin

A field's kind says what it does. How thin it runs says how dangerous it is, and one scale serves every people.

- **Slight.** By day the field is strange and no worse: a sound at the anniversary, a sight in the field's season. After dark it is frightening, and a traveler who crosses at night meets noises with no source, figures that are gone when looked at, and the dread of the place settling on the heart.
- **Moderate.** The kind comes on anyone who lingers, by day as well as by night, and sleepers within sight of the field dream the battle. After dark the visions are terrible: screams and moans out of the empty ground, the dead at their last work, sights that fill the mind with horror and do not leave it at dawn.
- **Thin.** Presences walk any night of the year, the field's season and standing water make them worse, and things come through that can harm the living. A field that has been worked on is always thin.

**The lesser fields break minds.** A slight or moderate field kills nobody by its own hand. It does not need to. One night on one is enough to frighten the stoutest traveler, and a person who stays too long, or comes back night after night, can come away with lasting harm: sleep that will not come, a mind that hears the field in every quiet, and in the worst cases madness and a breakdown nobody can mend. Every people that lives beside one keeps the same habits: nobody travels it at night, the road bends round it, and a traveler who must cross is told to go by day, go quickly and not look back.

**The thin fields kill.** The people who live beside them do not call them haunted. They call them cursed and dangerous, and they mean it as plainly as they would mean a flooded ford. They keep away, they warn travelers off, they bar the road or mark the edge, and every district with a thin field tells of people who went in and did not come out.

## What Crosses

From the field's side, two kinds of thing come through.

**The field's own dead**, worn down to their last fixation: men looking for their names, a column looking for a road home, a hunger for grain. On a slight or moderate field they are sight and sound and the dread they carry, and that is enough to do the harm the section above describes.

**The hunters.** The predator spirits gather where unreceived souls are, and a blood-field is a place where they never stop being. On a thin field some of them cross into this world in a semi-material form, and in that form they can act on it: they are seen close to, they take hold, they attack, and they kill. This is the reason a thin field is called cursed. A body found at the edge of one in the morning is a thing every such district has seen.

**Nothing living crosses bodily the other way.** A traveler on a blood-field never walks into the spirit realm, however thin the field. That is the plainest difference between a blood-field and an arálwen, which is a passage and goes somewhere: a boat that follows a second bank seen on a blood-field comes back to the same reach at dawn.

## Who Keeps One

Nobody, by office. No people has made anyone answerable for a blood-field the way the Wardens answer for the arálwain. Local custom tends some fields: a house that makes a yearly offering at the edge, a line of ash laid where the grazing stops, a cup poured at the anniversary, lamps set out by the families whose dead lie there. A sect may claim one as its own work, as the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agnī-panthā]] claims the fields of Vedyara. None of these is an office, and none of them obliges anyone to anything.

## What Ends One

A settlement rite performed over the field, in the rite of the people whose dead lie there. Every people that has one admits it works perhaps half the time, and every one of them costs something specific.

- In Aurèldía and Vylaria, the clergy of Sélmoros place the dead formally in the god's keeping.
- In the north, the grave-ale is drunk for the dead at last, with the reckoning spoken over it of who owes for them.
- In Vedyara, the Agnī-panthā burns the ground.
- In Aû'Khelâthu, a Reading is held for the unnamed, which cannot close a field whose names were never entered.
- Among the Varokh, a Shaman calls the dead home.

**A rite can also quiet a field for a set time without ending it.** A field hallowed for the days of an assembly stays quiet for those days, and a field half-settled stays quiet on one bank and not on the other. A quieted field returns to its kind when the time runs out, and it returns no gentler.

## How Rare

[[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] has more than anywhere else, and the [[lore-bloodfields|Blood-fields of Provènzia]] gives the reason. Elsewhere a people has two or three across its whole history, one to a great war at most.

**The Black Flame** works fields wherever it finds them. A field can be worked on—its kind roused and deepened, its thinness drawn out, and its ruin turned on the district around it—and the [[affiliation-blackflame|Cult of the Black Flame]] does that work anywhere a field lies. The [[place-bldfldyharvalen|Blood-field of Yhârvalen]] is the worked field every reader can name.

## Vedyara: the Shorukshetra

In [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] a soul goes on through a gap. When a man dies, [[affiliation-kalavrata|Kālavrata]]'s black conch is sounded, the threshold lamp his household lit when the illness turned is still burning, and until the pyre is kindled the way the soul has just gone stays open behind it. The pyre unmakes the body, the conch calls the soul on, and the gap closes. The [[affiliation-thresholdkeepers|Threshold-keepers]] put their one question inside that gap, and every Vedyari knows the gap is meant to close.

A _shorukshetra_ (blood-field) is ground where thousands died with no conch, no lamp and no pyre, so the gap behind them never closed. The Vedyari say the dead there are still in the doorway.

**The burning.** The Agnī-panthā claims the work, because its own fire was first kindled on a _shorukshetra_, and its rite is a burning of the ground: a fast, a confession of the district's sins before the ember, and grass-fires walked across the field in lines. It settles part of a field at best. The orthodox temples hold the unburned dead to be Kālavrata's affair and the burning presumptuous, and the quarrel is as old as the sect.

**No question is asked.** The Threshold-keepers' warrants forbid any question of war, and a dead soldier is not asked anything. Nothing on a _shorukshetra_ may lawfully be asked of its dead, which leaves the courts no way to learn from a field what happened on it.

**How the Vedyari live beside one.** A village near a _shorukshetra_ keeps indoors after dark and shuts its doors on the field's side. The roads bend round the fields, and a drover who must cross waters his beasts first and crosses at noon. The saying given to a traveler is the same in every district: "Cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls." A thin field is a _ghūrakshetra_ (cursed field), and nobody near one says the word after sunset. The road round it is barred at dusk, its edge is marked with stones, and the march companies tell new men the names of those who went in and were found at the edge in the morning.

Vedyara has three _shorukshetras_ in twenty-eight centuries, one to each age of war: [[place-oluratarna|Olūratarana]], where a king forbade the pyre to a rebel host about 300 BF; [[place-gajasthali|Gajasthalī]], where two armies broke among their own elephants about 270 AF; and [[place-lanthusthli|Lanthusthalī]], where the march kingdom died under the steppe host in 315 AF, the one _ghūrakshetra_ among them. Its militia wars are fought by hundreds, and the dead are carried home and burned, so no janapada battle has ever made one.

## Aû'Khelâthu: the Zelqezat

In [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Aû'Khelâthu]] a soul goes on because it is kept. The body is embalmed so the part of the soul that stays with it has a house, the name is cut in stone and read aloud so the part that is inscribed is never lost, and the rites see the rest across to the Weighing, where both of its accounts are opened before the gods, and Álgit, the Devourer of the Dead, takes a soul that is short on either. One that closes passes to [[place-zulaten|Zulaten]]. A Khelâthi who has no tomb, no name read aloud and no rites has no continuation at all. The whole civilization is built to keep that from happening to anyone.

A _zelqezat_ (blood-field) is ground where thousands died and none of it was done for them. The word means only "the place of a battle", because a battle whose dead were carried off, wrapped and read leaves no place behind: the Khelâthi say that every field in the valley has seen fighting and only these few remember it. The dead on a _zelqezat_ were never entered, so they are not lost souls in the ordinary sense. They are souls with nothing to be found by, and the predators of the spirit realm find them.

**The Reading for the unnamed.** The temples' answer is a Reading held at the field's edge by a priest of [[lore-hezmuiridty|Hezmuîri]], who reads aloud for the dead whose names were never written. It quiets a field for the night it is held. It cannot end one, because a Reading closes on a name and these have none; a family that can prove an ancestor lay on the field and bring his name to the edge is the one thing that settles any part of it, and in twelve centuries that has happened a handful of times.

**How the Khelâthi live beside one.** A _zelqezat_ is entered in no survey roll, so nobody farms it, nobody owns it and its render is remitted for as long as it lies there. The road goes round it, the villages on its side keep their doors shut after dark, and the house nearest it, by custom and never by office, makes an offering at the edge each year. A thin field is a _zelalgit_, the Devourer's place, because the things that come out of it are Álgit's kind and they hunt the living as they hunt the dead; boatmen will not moor beside one and the families that tend it go to its edge in a body, with lamps.

Aû'Khelâthu has three _zelqezat_ in twenty-eight centuries, all made by war and none older than the hill-nomads: [[place-zelhalzi|Zel-Halzi]], the Unmeasured Field, where the Tha'Ulgau destroyed the last Galezkara army in 1612 ST (499 BF); [[place-eleventhgran|the Eleventh Granary]] at Anlagh-Zetûn, where the dead of the granary rising were sealed in an empty granary without rites in 2378 ST, 268 AF; and [[place-chariotfld|the Chariot Field]] at Khelaga, where the chariot corps broke under a Vylarian army in 2487 ST, 377 AF, the one _zelalgit_ among them. The cursed Way of the False Uqa'â at Zu-Gezer is sealed ground and not a _zelqezat_, because a curse made it and not a slaughter.

## The Khazryn: Kèdoq and Henhulpedin

The [[skill-sowideslng|Sowides]] of the steppe lay their dead out. A body is carried to the laying-out ground (_yelos_), left with its face to the sky-father, and its name is spoken to the four winds, and the name is what the sky takes up. A soul no one named is not found by the sky, and the hunters find it first. A kèdoq (blood-ground) is a place where many died and none were laid out or named, and the steppe holds that the dead there are still waiting to be called. A thin one is a wèsuk (cursed ground), and the riders who guide a caravan past one do not say the word after sunset.

The Tellumi hold the same from the other side. A Tellumi dead is received by a Dēlkoshekh, who reads the name from the house's tablet while the household lights its lamps, and until the name is read the dead are not counted among the received. A _henhulpedin_ (blood-field) is ground where the names were never read, and a thin one is a _sirvelpedin_ (cursed field). [[place-henhulpedin|Henhulpedin]], outside [[place-zargandur|Zargandûr]], where the war-house fell in the Breaking, is the field that carries the word as its name.

**The three unions.** Each of the three great unions of the steppe made one: the first at [[place-jilaq|Jilaq]], where a city of strangers was killed and no rite was said for it; the second at [[place-wemaq|Wemaq]], where a tribe was killed and forbidden its laying-out; the third at [[place-qimod|Qìmod]], where a market town was killed on its fair day. The dead of a sacked town belong to a dozen peoples, which leaves the town's field with no one to settle it. The Breaking made the fourth, a lesser field.

**What quiets one.** A laying-out performed late for the dead of a field, with every name spoken to the four winds, quiets a blood-ground for as long as the winds carry the names. It needs the names, and a field of strangers has none to give. The Tellumi reading is the same rite in a Dēlkoshekh's voice, and in Zargandûr it is forbidden under the Ātárzád prohibition on the worship of Āhúrdáén, so the reader risks his life for it.

**How the steppe lives beside one.** Tribes mark nothing and wall nothing. The road bends, a rider turns a string off by force, a lamp is left at the edge on the anniversary, and the guides who would sell anything else will not sell a path across. The Tellumi paint stakes and shut gates.

## The Nordlands: the Vrellvöll

In the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]] a death is not finished until the grave-ale is drunk. The pyre, the ship or the howe takes the body, and the ale, drunk by the kin with the reckoning spoken over it, says who the dead man was, what he was owed and what he owed, and sends him on received. The dead who never got their grave-ale ride with the Jól-Ride, abroad on the worst nights of midwinter, and never leave. A _vrellvöll_ (blood-field) is ground where hundreds were denied it at once, and the halls hold that the ride never leaves such ground at all. The word is heard as a small last battle: _vrell-_, blood, and _-völl_, the field a battle is fought on, the word Vígvöll closes on.

**What ends one.** The remedy is the one the north uses for every claim. The grave-ale owed is drunk at last, with the reckoning spoken, and where the dead cannot be reckoned a door-court hears witness and rules them laid, as the Sworn Hand did for the nine at Flarnbrekka. Both cost something, because the reckoning names who owes, and the north keeps a debt for generations. [[place-domsey|Dómsey]] has never been settled for exactly that reason: the ale would say which crown's hird struck first in 287, and no king will be the one who says it. **A hallowing quiets a field for a set time.** The Landsgodi's walking of the stones closes Dómsey for the days of a sitting and lifting the peace opens it, and the island is the north's proof that a rite can hold a field shut without ending it.

**How the halls live beside one.** Nobody lives on Dómsey and nobody may; neither shore of [[place-skalfvatn|Skalfvatn]] fishes its anniversary month, and children learn the marching beat under the ice as the sound to run from; Targud's watch crosses [[place-dvulgvangr|Dvulgvangr]] by noon or not at all. Nobody keeps any of them by office. The Landsgodi pours one cup at the law-rock "for the ones the count left out", the Malagnan kin keep eleven hundred named stones and one blank one above the lake, and the fort-captains of Targud keep their musters off the ford after dusk.

Three fields in the north, one to each great war, and a fourth that northern hands made abroad. Dómsey, where the five kings' hirds killed each other on the strand in 287 and the ale was never drunk, a silence, thin, and held shut only at the sittings; Skalfvatn, where Malagna's king and eleven hundred men went through the ice in 466, a recurrence; and Dvulgvangr, where Targud's hird and its farmers held a ford against a Grukar horde for three days in 613 and were carried off, a dread. The [[place-bldfldyharvalen|Blood-field of Yhârvalen]] in Provènzia was made by the Nordheim fleets of about 520, and it is Provènzia's. Haulonna, Askholm and Ísdal are a sorcery on an enclave, a burning and a sickness, and the north calls none of them a _vrellvöll_.

## The Varokh: the Vrystgrund

Among the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] the dead go to their village's _wesk_ (totem), and it is the Shaman who sends them. At a burial or a pyre the Shaman calls the dead person by name into the _weskgrund_, the totem's land, where the ancestors are, and the kin keep the body warm until the call is answered. A soul nobody calls wanders outside the totem's land, where the _hrág_ (hungry spirits) hunt the weak, and the Varokh do not need to be told what becomes of it.

A _vrystgrund_ (blood-field) is ground where many died at once and no Shaman called them home: ground held by nobody, the opposite of the totem's land. The Varokh have two in their own forest, and neither holds Varokh dead. [[place-skathhreth|Skathhreth]], the harm ford on the Moldún, holds a Vylarian legion destroyed there in 291 AF, men who had no totem to be called to; it is a recurrence and the thinnest field in the forest, where the column is seen on the water at dusk and the _hrág_ gather in the thaw. [[place-grimak|Grimak]], the burned thicket in Skathwald, holds the Varokh the Grukar took before the nest was burned in 536, of whom nothing was left to call; it is a silence, moderate across the ring and thin in the hollow, and something in it calls Varokh names. A third field holds Varokh dead and lies outside the forest: [[place-blackmarshfld|the Black Marsh]] in Moktur, where a host of the hunger raids died in 520 with no Shaman among them, which Moktur keeps.

**What ends one.** A Shaman calls the dead home, by name, at the field. It needs the names and it needs a totem to call them to, and the forest's own fields have neither: the legion's dead have no totem and the Grukar's victims left nothing to name. Nobody has tried at either, and the Shamans say so plainly.

**How the villages live beside one.** No village stands within a day's walk of Skathhreth, and the ford is crossed by day and never in the thaw-week; the Shamans of the nearest villages lay a line of ash along its north bank each spring. Mahnrýth sings its lament at an empty mound and never at Grimak, and the grove council at Rádholt sounds the alarm if anyone is missing near the thicket. A feud between villages kills by the dozen and its dead are carried home and called, so no Varokh feud has ever made a _vrystgrund_.

## Aurèldía: the Blood-field

In [[place-aureldirgn|Aurèldía]] the dead are judged. The Threshold Keepers of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] perform the funeral rites and enter the dead in the temple records, and the Silent Judge receives each soul and weighs it without speaking. The judged go on to the Field of the Blessed, a region of the spirit realm that Aurèldían death-traditions have shaped over millennia. A blood-field is ground where thousands died and were not judged in order, and the clergy of Sélmoros end one by settlement: a formal placing of the dead in the god's keeping, which works perhaps half the time and is paid for by the district that asks.

**The ground, not the battle.** [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] has more fields than anywhere else, and its custom is to name the ground and let the battle go ([[lore-bloodfields|The Blood-fields of Provènzia]]). The battle at [[place-bldfldtiravlen|Tiravlen]] is known only from the Vylarian account, and no record says what war made [[place-bldfldkyvarel|Kývarel]] or [[place-bldfldcalvenza|Calvènza]].

**Élavendre has none.** Its thin places are [[lore-aralwen|arálwain]], natural and kept by the Wardens. Its wars were fought by not being where the army was, and [[lore-elvtribute|the war of the tribute]] and [[lore-ardwynclose|the Ardwyn Closing]] cost lives without a slaughter. The Áelendan dead are commended onward by their shamans, and the Death-Speakers go to [[lore-nyfainspr|Nyfain]] to ask whether they have gone.

**A rite must be the dead's own.** Sélmoros places in his keeping the dead who were his. The legion that died in [[place-horngorge|the Horn Gorge]] kept the Twelve, so a settlement could reach it, though no one has paid for one. The [[affiliation-saelvri|Saelvri]] did not keep the Twelve, and the rite they kept for their dead died with them, so no rite the living keep can settle [[place-hushwood|the Hushwood]]. It is the newest field in Aurèldía and the only one in living memory.

Aurèldía has eight fields: six in Provènzia, the Horn Gorge in Astúrath and the Hushwood on Aelwyth. The [[affiliation-twinbldrdr|Twinblade Order]] keeps stations at a few battlefields as guards at a threshold, and keeps none of them. The [[place-threesisters|Three Sisters]], where the Great War of Aelwyth ended, left no field, and nothing marks the ground.

## Heliónis: the Herald's Crossing

The Helionite cities are Aurèldían, and their account of the wound is the Aurèldían one: the dead were not judged in order before [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]]. What sets Heliónis apart is how seldom the account is needed. After a battle between Helionite cities the loser's herald walks onto the field under truce and takes the dead home, and every city buries its own to its own rites ([[lore-firstgames|the First Games]]). Sixteen centuries of wars among the cities have left no blood-field.

The exception is [[place-sourplain|the Sour Plain]], a withering made in 327 BF when a legion and a Korinthean levy died together under a working that turned back on its makers, on ground no herald could cross. The Sour Plain is thin. Its dead stand in their ranks on the anniversary, and its dead ground spreads a few yards in a bad decade. Korinthea has set a ring of boundary stones round it and reset them outward three times, and a judge of the Temple of Árdavon walks the ring each year. The settlement rite of Sélmoros has never been tried there: a Helionite temple is civic property, the rite needs an assembly's vote, and no Korinthean assembly has voted to say aloud what its city did.

## Byzaría: the Fort's Jar

The Byzarian League's records keep a field under the name of the fort that stands nearest it, and treat it as the garrison's business. Byzaría has one, [[place-thirstground|the Thirsting Ground]], made in 158 BF when a steppe host died of arrows and thirst between the spring-forts of the foothills ([[lore-heladwells|the Battle of the Wells]]). It is a silence, moderate: nothing sings there, a skin filled at dusk is empty by morning, and the field's own spring stops running for a night each year. The garrison of the nearest fort sets a full jar of water at its edge each evening and empties what is left at dawn. The monks of the eastern rite at Argýrteíkhos have tried the settlement rite of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] twice and failed, because the dead are steppe dead and the rite they needed was never said over them.
