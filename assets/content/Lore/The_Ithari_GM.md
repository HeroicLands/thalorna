> **GM-only document.** This note puts accounts and sites beside one another for the GM. Its conjectures are curiosities, not canon. None is the answer, every question stays open, and a campaign may use any of them or none.

## What this note is

A gray wall, an old account and a flooded terrace offer different kinds of evidence. This note gathers them without making them parts of a solved history. The people beside a work have their own concerns; the GM’s access to every note does not give those people the same access. Keep an observation, an explanation offered by someone in the world, and a possibility chosen for a campaign distinct.

There is no concealed solution here for a party to recover. Similar structures need not disclose a common purpose. A procedure that produces a repeatable effect need not explain either the procedure or the effect. A tradition may preserve an encounter faithfully without preserving its meaning. Use the questions to decide what the campaign explores, and leave the rest unanswered.

The Kaliharans never give outsiders their name or account. Their silence does not prevent independent research elsewhere. The scholarly conclusion is rare because comparison takes travel, records and patient work; it belongs to no birthright, initiation or exclusive faculty. Anyone with sufficient time and resources can reach it. It establishes neither the makers’ identity nor their purposes, arrival or departure.

The names express different kinds of knowledge. Do not put the island’s name into a foreign guide’s speech, or the Senali name into an unattributed narrator’s explanation. A local name may outlast every story that once accompanied it. None of the three names supplies the people’s own name for themselves.

## How people meet the works

**Locals** meet the site through its place in their lives. A gate-post may be touched before a journey; a patch of dangerous ground may be avoided. Their stories can disagree without making either teller a disguised expert. Give the party the local observation and its local interpretation separately. Familiarity with one stone does not yield a map of the others.

**Travelers and the curious** may notice a gray surface that looks like stone but yields under a fingertip, an unmarked curve or an oval opening without hinges. Some works stand open; others admit one visitor and refuse another. No dependable rule connecting admission with birth, training, intent or personal qualities has been found. Record what happens during the visit without turning that occurrence into a general key. Force has left no mark on the material in the accounts. A refusal can end an expedition without explaining the refusal.

The works’ ribs and shallow vaults suggest an inside, but not ordinary rooms. A resemblance to bone or shell does not identify an organism. Gray surfaces can be warm or faintly luminous, without the two properties reliably accompanying one another. No inscription on a work names its maker or announces its function. These are things a party may describe and compare; they are not instructions for operating a machine.

**Scholars** can assemble travelers’ descriptions, compare old drawings with a present visit, and distinguish a claimed miracle from a recorded change. Their limited synthesis is the one in The Precursors. Beyond it, rival suggestions remain questions. Are these buildings, instruments, living things, or objects for which those categories are inadequate? Does “magic” explain anything, or merely name an unfamiliar effect? Could an apparently idle work be doing something unobserved? Does the absence of wear tell us anything about its age or its condition?

**A party** can begin with the people who use the place, then seek earlier records of that same place, then pay for a journey to a second one. Travel, guides, copying, translation, lodging and permission to enter an archive cost time and resources. The [[place-rajavalilib|Rajavali Library]], for example, admits historians, but access to its correspondence shelf requires the Memory-Keeper’s word. That offers a concrete research obstacle without granting its keeper knowledge of every strange site. A comparison of two records may answer when a spring froze; it cannot establish why.

Let research produce bounded results: a description checked, a date disputed, a second route found, a local explanation contradicted by an older account. Neither a successful investigation nor a costly expedition obliges the GM to disclose the makers’ history. No written manual waits on a work’s surface. Kaliharan procedures and the island’s private account are not purchasable shortcuts around that limit.

### Reports without an identified work

[[place-nrthnhlds|The Old Holds of the Northern Taiga]] collects conflicting reports of masonry in the forest. Those reports do not establish a work of the same kind. A GM may make the holds lost Khazari halls, works of the teachers in the islanders’ account, or unrelated ruins. None of those possibilities follows from the reports, and the hunters’ stories connect the holds to nothing elsewhere. A ruin, a thin place or an unexplained landscape is not included above merely because its origin is uncertain. The island also has lesser tending-stones without individual site notes; [[lore-sarisul|The Tending-Stones]] gives their shared account. Neither an unlocated report nor that general account warrants inventing a further named site.

## Kalihara and its human civilization

The private account kept in [[lore-itharisul|Precursor Stone]] belongs to the custodians of [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]]. They call the teachers Ithari and describe knowledge received mind to mind, without spoken language. They place the arrival before 10,000 BF, the raising of their human civilization in the teachers’ time, and the departure at 7,300 BF. These dates and descriptions are the islanders’ testimony, not conclusions available from a gray wall.

Their account gives agriculture, medicine, governance, a vocabulary and a whole understanding of life together. [[affiliation-tharivaan|The Living Whole]] preserves the practice and the conviction that the gift was benevolent. The teachers are remembered as people with impatience and humor, not as gods demanding worship; their appearance, number and lifespan are not recorded. Did a people visit, or does the island’s word cover a profession or a crew? How much of the description belongs to the encounter, and how much to its long transmission?

The scattered human learning described in [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]] offers a contrast with the Kaliharan account. A complete gift raises questions that scattered borrowing does not. Was the civilization itself the purpose, a kindness alongside other business, or a society formed to keep something running? Why this island? Is the apparent completeness in the gift, in the way the island remembers it, or in the limits of its comparison? None of these possibilities supplies a hidden correction to the Kaliharans’ belief.

Where did the teachers come from? Was Thalorna their destination or a stop, and were the distant works part of the same undertaking as the island? The scatter does not establish what occupied them elsewhere, or whether the islanders knew its extent.

The teachers’ last disturbance and hurried additional instructions are central to the inherited account. Did something arrive, fail, end or demand their attention elsewhere? Were the additions meant to keep something open or close it? The surviving practice does not distinguish every late instruction from the earlier teaching. Departure might mean escape, recall, completion, abandonment or something that none of those words describes. Neither their destination nor their subsequent fate is settled.

The custodians can perform procedures without explaining them. They keep observations of gray works that behave unreliably or stop, of entire works turning white and hard, and of cracks in some white works. No known tending reverses the whitening. The lineages’ portable held stones and the sanctuaries’ dead reference pieces do not explain how the pieces first came to be separate from the works. Does a failure mark exhaustion, lost support, a completed task or a changed condition? Is a quiet work inactive? Similar appearances do not answer these questions.

The growing count of failures is held privately, with the silkan and the custodians of dark works; it is not knowledge every islander possesses. [[affiliation-silharicircle|The Sílhari Circle]] seeks principles beyond inherited procedures, while other custodians hold to the tending. Neither position proves what has gone wrong. The people’s later isolation and refusal of settlement are part of their history, not evidence of an instruction to remain isolated forever. Do not turn the account into an explanation of the island’s volcanoes, weather, currents or harvests.

## The Elder Races

The [[lore-flksenali|Senali Folk]] call the people they met _Ennosulkas Vennas_, “the Earlier Strangers.” In the Senali account they met them and received works from them. The account does not establish how that receipt occurred, what the works were for, or what either party understood by it. No transaction, device or teaching explains the encounter here.

[[lore-itharicntc|The Senali Meet the Earlier Strangers]] records a chance meeting, courtesy and no arranged second meeting. The separate claim of receipt does not establish a gift or exchange on that occasion. [[lore-senalircgnzd|What the Senali Recognized]] preserves the island’s report of recognition during the Senali visit to Kalihara, without explaining its source. Keep the limits of those accounts visible. The receipt does not identify the thing recognized, make the two occasions one event, or supply an unrecorded second encounter. What was received, and what did “received” mean to those involved? Does recognition concern the substance, a practice, or something else? How much is remembered but not said?

The [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari Folk]] arrive after the departure dated in the Kaliharan account. No meeting with them is established by that account. Their skill with stone does not establish knowledge of this substance. Silence by any elder people is not proof of ignorance, complicity or secret instruction. Did another people meet a work elsewhere? Would a remembered resemblance mean a shared origin? Leave those as possibilities, not missing chapters already known to the GM.

## Southern Kì’ìch’chik

[[place-southkiichchik|Southern Kì’ìch’chik]] contains works amid wastes and difficult journeys. The Quiet Sill and the Cold Spine offer observations of surfaces and passage, not a diagram of the country’s cause. A silent strip that sheds ash and a shrinking stretch of safe camping ground can matter urgently without revealing why either occurs.

Are the works malfunctioning, or doing precisely what they were intended to do? Is the land in its present condition because of them, despite them, or for reasons unrelated to them? Did a change in the land alter their behavior? Could a useful local effect be incidental to a purpose nobody here can perceive? The existence of other ruins, strange creatures or dangerous ground supplies no automatic connection.

For a journey, put the immediate question first: which ground can the party cross, what does a local account actually describe, and what has changed since it was recorded? A decision about shelter or a route can have consequences before anyone has a theory. Do not make the wastes a solved ancient accident, or the works a repair project with an implied correct setting.

## Using the Ithari anywhere

A single work can enter a campaign as an obstacle, shelter, disputed place or rumor. Begin with one observation and the people who live with it. If a second site enters play, let the connection be made through evidence. A scholar can bring a comparison to the party or need help obtaining one; the scholar’s hypothesis can remain wrong, incomplete or untestable. The GM’s index need never become a player handout.

Kaliharan knowledge can matter through an islander’s actions, a local observation the party witnesses, or an islander player character’s own inherited knowledge. It does not require a custodian to tell outsiders the private account. A Seeker abroad can need passage or seek another site while withholding the name and reasons. Questions from such a traveler may set a party investigating independently; they are not confirmation of the answers the party guesses. Keep an islander character’s knowledge distinct from what that character tells foreign companions.

[[scenario-klhrdvntrs|Kalihara Adventures]] offers campaign possibilities about failing works, foreign materials, competing researchers and intervention. Treat its proposed explanations and revelations here as questions for a chosen campaign, never as established answers: could an imported material matter at all? Could an arcane imitation reproduce an observed effect without explaining the original? Would a name in a carried message or an object in a sealed case be recognized, and why? Does a distant work offer a useful comparison or merely a seductive resemblance?

The larger possibilities stay equally open. Did the makers finish rather than leave unfinished business? Could a held stone from a white, cracked work help elsewhere, and what would taking it cost the community that keeps it? Could a liturgy reach anyone beyond the island? Would another elder people recognize the substance or offer help, and on what terms? Was some practice forgotten or refused, or is the notion of a lost original procedure itself mistaken? None establishes a cure, a return, a bargain or a monstrous duty that the party must perform.

Choose consequences for the party’s actions without guaranteeing a revelation or a rescue. A copied record can circulate; an expedition can disturb a local agreement; a stone can be fought over even if it helps no one. Preserve the possibility that the people remain unknown, that a work continues after the campaign ends, and that the party’s most consequential discovery concerns the living people beside it.
