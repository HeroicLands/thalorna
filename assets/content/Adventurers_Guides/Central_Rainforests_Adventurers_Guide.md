> The dugout comes round a bend in the grey before sunrise, and the river bank rises on your left like a wall of wet green. Mist lies on the water in rags. Pole-and-thatch houses stand raised a little off the ground along the top of the bank, and below them a man is lifting a basket-trap out of a weir with both arms, the water running off his wrists. A woman beside him is slitting fish with a shell and dropping the first one of the catch back into the current without looking at it. Smoke rises from three fires. Somewhere behind the houses a child laughs and is hushed, and a hornbill goes over with a sound like a rusty gate.
>
> The man sets the trap down on the bank and comes to the water's edge with his hands open and empty. He is slender, with a northern glass bead in one ear and the dark callus of a paddle across both palms. "_Melibo nudi_," he says, which welcomes an arrival, and then, in the same breath, "_Nimo Kobolu Lihola kedi_": I am Kobolu of the Lihola. He is the [[being-kobololihola|trade-keeper]] of this camp, and he does not ask what you want. He sets a bowl of smoked fish and a gourd of river water on the bank beside your boat, and waits until you have taken both.

The [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]] are a belt of unbroken equatorial jungle across the middle of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]], between the fertile north and the savannahs of the south. Two rivers carry what traffic there is: the [[place-luwabolu|Luwa Bolu]], the Old River, runs north through a gorge toward the sea, and the [[place-luwagbasa|Luwa Gbasa]], the Dark River, runs south through black water into flooded forest. Between them lies country that no outsider has mapped, held by tribes of a few hundred people each.

**The one thing to understand first: nothing stands above the tribe.** There is no king in the forest, no council of tribes and no law that runs from one river to the next. A tribe is a few bands that follow one leader, and each leader is made in the tribe's own way. A trader who speaks for a village at the landing is not its leader, and a leader who hands out gifts is not its chief. Alliances, feasts and marriages between tribes are events, and they do not last as institutions.

## What You Meet First

The forest peoples are the [[lore-bikoclt|Biko]], and they speak one small tongue, [[skill-ekpelulng|Ekpelu]], in four traditions that look alike from outside and differ greatly from within. The [[lore-luwandoclt|Luwando]] keep the rivers and make you a guest of the river for three nights. The [[lore-yesomaclt|Yesoma]] move through the deep interior and are met by chance, on a trail, before they are gone. The [[lore-kubindoclt|Kubindo]] keep a spirit-court each, and a stranger moves through their country like a guest in a house with many rooms. The [[lore-pewayeclt|Pewaye]] hold the northern and southern edges and trade with everyone. The [[lore-gorgfolkclt|Gorge-folk]] belong to none of the four: they carry canoes round the rapids for a share.

Every tribe has the same four standings: dependents, members, elders and one leader. You are a dependent until a tribe takes you in, and a tribe takes you in slowly.

## Where to Go

The Lihola hold the upper Old River from [[place-liholacamp|Lihola Camp]], and its trade-keepers move the forest's honey and ivory to the gorge, where the [[affiliation-morolatribe|Morola]] at [[place-morolalanding|Morola Landing]] carry cargo round the falls for a share and grade every trader by how he paid. On the Dark River the [[affiliation-luwalatribe|Luwala]] keep the last camp below the drowned plain and turn boats back from [[place-drwndzgrt|the Drowned Ziggurat]]. The northern and southern edges are the [[affiliation-kirolatribe|Kirola]] and the [[affiliation-balolatribe|Balola]], where [[lore-okharinclt|Okharik]] herders and the Sengala of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba]] bring iron, cloth and salt.

At the head of the Old River, in a valley no map shows, a tribe fed by its neighbors' fear keeps [[place-lpdkngcrt|the Leopard King's Court]]. The deep interior holds [[place-slntclrng|the Silent Clearing]], where nothing grows.

## What Gets a Newcomer Into Trouble

- **Cutting out the middleman.** The forest trades through inherited friendships, and a merchant who deals past the trade-keeper meets disease, disappearance or a trade that quietly stops.
- **Months, not years.** Fevers, fluxes and parasites kill outsiders who stay, whatever else they survive.
- **The fourth day.** A Luwando host asks nothing for three nights and then asks what you want. Have an answer.
- **The wrong river.** A forest person asked the way to the Ziggurat names a different river on purpose.
- **A gift refused.** A tribe that offers or sends a gift is making a statement, and a refusal is an answer.

## Ways In

Kobolu needs two paddlers to take a load of ivory down to the Morola's gorge landings in the fish run, when no Lihola can be spared, and the iron he brings back is the camp's stock for the year. A salvage crew on the Dark River has sent a woman ashore with iron to buy a guide, and the [[being-moroluwala|Luwala leader]] wants her turned away without a fight. The [[being-danokirola|leader of the Kirola]] needs someone to carry an answer to a herders' headman whose cattle are on the edge, and the [[being-lihobalola|leader of the Balola]] needs someone to ride to the Sengala post and learn why the iron is late.

A gift caravan for [[being-lpdkngldr|the Leopard King]] takes on porters, and its Speaker, [[being-dagbekpalula|Dagbe Kpalula]], tests each newcomer with small questions; [[being-ndokokpalula|Ndoko Kpalula]], the Court's war-leader, asks visitors what they saw on the Lower Terrace. The scenario [[scenario-trbtlpdkng|The Unseen King of the Spotted Court]] starts there, and [[scenario-hshcrclslnt|The Hush at the Heart of the Wood]] starts at the clearing.
