> Morning comes up the Bérav as a white mist, and your barge slides out of it under the first of the bridges. The arch is pale stone furred with a trained vine, its leaves still beaded with the night's rain, and the drip from them runs cold down your collar. On the quays of Béravel the bargemen in oiled gray wool walk their poles back along the gunwales without a word, and a crane creaks as it swings a bale of hill fleece onto the stones. The air smells of river weed, wet slate, and the woodsmoke of a glasshouse furnace somewhere upstream. Above the warehouses, towers of the same pale stone climb out of the mist with gardens on their terraces, and the gardens run into the wood behind the city without a wall to say where one stops.
>
> Nobody shouts on this quay. A University clerk in a dove-gray gown reads a manifest aloud in an even voice while a factor with a silver ring on every finger listens with her eyes half closed. Two apprentices from an instrument-maker's shop carry a harp frame wrapped in waxed linen and step around a puddle as carefully as around a sleeping dog. At the foot of the landing stage a tall woman in a moss-green cloak waits with a harp case on her back and a strip of birch bark knotted at her collar. She inclines her head before she speaks. "_Shâlor en kír a thél_," she says, half singing it, the pitch rising and falling: welcome, friend of the light. "I am Kélyra, a bard of the Colleges, and I sing for House Alveryn." Her eyes go to the dust on your boots, then to the road east out of the city, and she asks which way you mean to go.

[[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] is a woodland kingdom of about two million people on the western coast of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], and the only human realm there where the elder peoples walk openly. Its cities are pale stone, living vine and garden; its farms are rich; its forests are older than anything else in the west, and in places the world and the fae country lie close enough to cross between. Magic is studied, practiced and argued over at table. Behind the farmland rise the mountains of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]], the ancestor tribes who never came down into the cities, and somewhere in the deep wood live the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]].

**The one thing to understand first: the kingdom ends at the tree line.** The crown rules the cities, the farms and the coast. The sacred groves, the mountain interior and the deep forest belong to the Áelendan by ancient law, and the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Áelendan Wardens]] hold that line. A trespasser is warned once and escorted out. One who comes back is dealt with, and no royal magistrate hears the complaint.

## What You Meet First

The [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Kingdom of Élavendre]] is a monarchy of old noble houses, and each house works to trace its descent to the Áelendan; a demonstrable claim to the old blood is worth more than acreage. The [[affiliation-bardicolgs|Bardic Colleges]] hold the kingdom's genealogies and its memory in song, and the law recognizes a satire by a bard of standing as a weapon. The [[affiliation-groveconcl|Grove Council]] of druids speaks for the groves and the rural custom, and a village heeds its grove-elder before any sheriff.

Expect courtesy, allusion and a great deal of patience. People leave arrangements open, decline to name a date, and choose words that read two ways, because prophecy is a living institution here and a decision may wait on a half-remembered line. Power casually shown is vulgar; power precisely applied is admired.

The [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] holds the **University of Béravel**, the largest [[affiliation-panepistmm|Panepistemium]] campus outside the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]], and the crown welcomes it warmly inside the walls. An Ordo party that asks to go inward is given excellent guides, who show it real country and nothing of consequence. You pay in [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian coin]]: the Aurion, the Argo and the Bit.

## Where to Go

[[place-beravel|Béravel]] stands fifteen miles up the [[place-beravriver|Bérav]] where the tide stops. The University, the Colleges, the Grove Council and the Áelendan all keep houses there and fight their quarrel with patronage and students. In [[place-glanmor|Glanmor]], the timber town of [[place-liranel|Liranel]] stands where the crown's writ stops at the old forest. Its forest court issues licenses to cut, and its guides take visitors to the thin places; some guides stop at the margins, and the ones who do not are very well paid. [[place-elanmere|Elanmere]], a lake town at the foot of [[place-ardwyn|Ardwyn]], is where the high country trades with the low: at the autumn fair tribal families camp on the shore rather than enter the walls, and the boatmen will tell you where a net may not go down.

The dangers are real. Paths shift between visits, lights stand among the trees, and a fae lord may demand tribute of a village that cut the wrong copse. The [[lore-faecourts|Fae Courts]] are said to dwell in the deepest interior and receive only those they summon. Beyond the city walls the quarrel between the Ordo and the old institutions turns lethal: survey camps are found abandoned, notes scattered, and informants recant or vanish. On the eastern march, [[place-valdun|Valdûn]] musters the hill levy of [[place-brynavar|Brynavar]] against [[place-vrystwald|Varokh]] and [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] raiders every year.

## Ways In

A noble house hires you to escort its bard to the Elanmere fair, where its claim of descent must stand before people who remember the genealogy differently. An Ordo scholar in Liranel wants a guide who will not stop at the margins, and the Wardens already know her name. A grove-elder needs strangers to carry terms to a fae lord after a village cleared a copse for pasture and its children began to sicken. In the deep [[place-drenavrspn|Drenavar]] the nineteenth seat of the Grove Council has stood empty for a generation, and the signs now say its lost elder is coming home, or something wearing his shape.
