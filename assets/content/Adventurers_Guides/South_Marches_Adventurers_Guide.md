> The caravan reaches the eastern wells at dusk, and the first thing you hear is the horses. Two hundred of them stand in long lines at the picket ropes beside a walled well, small and rough-coated and shifting their weight, and beyond them black goat-hair tents run out into the thorn with a cookfire in front of each. The light is low and brown. The air smells of dung smoke, hot stone and wet leather; a boy is hauling a bucket up from the well hand over hand, and the water that slops out of it is gone into the dust before it lands. Men stand by the tents with spears and short bows, and none of them points a weapon at you, and none of them puts one down. A child leads a goat through the caravan, slapping the pack-animals out of its way.
>
> A broad man in a felted cloak, his beard braided with a strip of red wool, comes up the line of horses and stops at your lead camel without looking at the drover. "_okharé, ne thalé_," he says, a greeting that hails you as one of the people: Okharik one, greetings. "[Ukhedo]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Hasembe, tent-father of this well." He says it as if it settled something. He holds out a wooden bowl of soured milk, and he watches your hand take it before he says what an escort to the next stage costs.

The [[place-sthmrchsrgn|South Marches]] are the dry country south of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]], between the last of its irrigated strips and the edge of the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]]. There is no river. Seasonal streambeds come off the highland spurs and run north, full for a season, and a line of wells across the scrub holds the country together. Marcher clans move sheep, goats and small horses between the forage belts, free companies hold the old forts, and one road crosses it all. Bethua claims it on its maps and takes tribute from it, and holds none of it.

**The one thing to understand first: whoever holds the well holds the road.** The Marches have no wall, no toll-house and no customs, only water, and so the well is the law. Every well has a clan that holds it now, and the clans' right to it is the one the Marches recognize, whoever dug it; a company's hold on a fort lasts as long as its riders do. A caravan that wants to cross pays the one who holds the next well, and the question of who that is this season is the first thing a guide is asked.

## What You Meet First

The marcher clans are of the same stock as the frontier clans of [[place-okharisrgn|Okharis Region]] and speak a Marcher form of [[skill-okharclng|Okharik Language]]. Two tribes hold the country. The [[affiliation-dorumbetribe|Dorumbe Tribe]] graze the west, at the foot of the spurs, and carry the tribute to Bethua's frontier posts; the [[affiliation-hasembetribe|Hasembe Tribe]] graze the east along the road and sell riders and water to caravans. Each is led by a headman by descent and consent together, with the elders of its clans. Stock, wells and grazing pass from father to son, a clan's head is always a man, and a man bears arms as a matter of course.

The Marchers' ways are the reverse of Bethua's, and they know it. The matriarchy's ordering of the sexes is to a Marcher a standing offense, and a woman of Bethuan manner crossing the Marches meets a courteous coolness at every well.

Marchers do not own much that cannot be driven away, so hospitality is also accounting. You wait for leave before your beast drinks. A clan that has watered you owes you the water and no more, and a host's gift of soured milk is a challenge to be drunk, not a courtesy to be declined. Young men ride as light horse from boyhood and hire out to caravans and free companies, or raid the frontier when the grass is gone, and what they earn goes into the household's herd.

## Where to Go

The Hasembe's well-camp, [[place-hasembecamp|Hasembe Camp]], is where a caravan hires its riders; [[place-dorumbecamp|Dorumbe Camp]] lies at the foot of the spurs, where the elders sit and the tribute stock is gathered. Between them runs [[place-marchesroad|the Marches Road]], a track and not a built road, that crosses the drylands from the Bethuan frontier toward the grazing selatu of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]]. A caravan with a good guide takes about twenty days.

The wells are [[place-longwells|the Long Wells]], and the best of them are the Khelathi forts of [[place-khuqetzalu|Khuqet-Zalu]], a chain of broken mud-brick towers a day apart, each on a well and a standing cistern. The companies use them as lairs.

## Who to Fear

[[affiliation-frcmpnssthmrchs|The Free Companies of the South Marches]] are the Marches' marauders: bands of ten riders to a hundred, tolerated by the clans because they are useful against the north and because too many of the clans' own sons ride in them. The most feared are [[affiliation-jcklsthmrchs|the Jackals of the South Marches]], a company of the Houseless, women cast out of Bethua and eunuch-warriors cast out of its service, under [[being-argyrirjckl|Argirira the Jackal]]. They winter in the western stages and hit Bethuan targets by preference. The [[affiliation-spearsisterhood|Spear-Sisterhood]] looks at the forts from a distance and keeps a list of which are held.

The other dangers are the ordinary ones of dry country. The summers are long and fierce and the rains short; a streambed that was dry at noon can run at midnight, and a well that gave water last year may not give it this one.

## Ways In

A caravan master wants an escort past the eastern stages and does not want the Jackals to be the company that gives it. [[being-khandoruhsmb|Khandoru of the Hasembe]] is determined that they not be ([[scenario-nxtwllst|The Next Well East]]), and the Hirer of Riders at Hasembe Camp will name his price for a party that takes the contract. The Dorumbe tribute is late: the winter rains came short, [[being-mbareloddrmb|Mbarelo of the Dorumbe]] has to tell the frontier posts so, and the Tribute-Bearer needs someone to drive the stock there before the Hégissa of the southern cantons decides that the clans mean to pay nothing. Two clans of the Dorumbe claim the same cistern at Dorumbe Camp, and the Elder of the Wells wants a witness who has no clan to favor.

A scholar of [[place-khelunu|Khelunu]] wants squeezes of the stage-inscriptions at Khuqet-Zalu, and the company that holds the best-preserved fort wants to be paid for the visit. A merchant whose cargo cannot pass the Bethuan customs wants a guide for the whole length of the Marches Road. A young Hasembe horseman has come home from the Jackals' pay with more coin than a season of riding should earn, and his tent-father wants to know where it came from.
