> The escort met your party at the col, exactly there and not a step sooner, and for two days the soldiers have walked ahead of you in silence down a road of fitted stone. On the third evening the way-station rises out of the pines: a walled court of gray brick with green-glazed tiles on its roofs and red-lacquered gates, the last light catching on a bronze bell hung under the eaves. Inside the gate everything happens in order. Porters in undyed hemp set your bales down in a chalked square and step back from them. A soldier in lacquered scale checks a wooden tablet on a cord at a passing groom's belt, turns it over to the seal, and hands it back without a word. Through an open screen you see a long room of low desks, where clerks in dark blue robes write in columns with fine brushes, and you smell ink, pine smoke and steamed millet. No one raises a voice. Your guides from [[affiliation-hvarnguides|the Hvarn]], who will not come a step farther, are already being fed at a separate table.
>
> A woman in a scholar's blue robe, her hair pinned with a single plain comb, comes to the edge of the veranda and bows to you, precisely and not deeply. She greets you with _«lai nau!»_, good coming, the welcome a Tantsou gives a person received into their company. "[[being-kwisenregdtr|Second Daughter of the Registrar of the Clan of Moon Rises Over Still Water]], near [[place-weichai|Weichai]]," she says. "[[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]]." She waits. When it is plain that you have no caste to answer with, she inclines her head, leads you to the lowest end of a long bench, sets a bowl of millet and a cup of pale tea in front of you, and opens a fresh page in a bound register you are not allowed to see.

The [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] is the oldest continuously governed state in the world, a realm of river basins, terraced hills and crowded cities in the far east of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], with some 120 million people on its rolls. It holds that the cosmos was made by sorting, that the sorting is unfinished, and that every person's work is to keep the sorted world apart from the unsorted. Its Emperor is the Son of Heaven, holder of the [[lore-mandatehvn|Mandate of Heaven]], who reigns from the capital of [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]]; its gods are a celestial bureaucracy that the imperial bureaucracy mirrors; and its people live in eight castes that fix their trade, dress, marriage and the name, if any, they may bear. To the frontier clerk you are a document that arrived without a filing number.

The empire ends at [[place-tantsouwall|the Wall of the Ten Thousand Watchfires]], which runs thousands of miles from the far northern frontiers to the Grazian Mountains in the far south and has stood through many dynasties. To a Tantsou it is where the law ends and the sorted world with it: outside its gates are the barbarian lands of the Khazrin and its peoples. Nobody passes the wall, in or out, without proper papers and approvals.

**The one thing to understand first: a foreigner is unclassified until a bureau says otherwise.** Without classification a stranger cannot own property, sign a contract or seek the law's protection; a crime against him is no crime under the law, and a crime by him is a breach of the order, punished fast. The usual remedy is a bureau's classification, such as the trading classification a merchant receives with a tablet to carry, good for the length of his business. The highest is the Emperor's own decree, rare and kept for dignitaries, ambassadors and those who have done the empire an extraordinary service: it assigns the foreigner a caste, and his standing lasts exactly as long as the Emperor wills it, and his tablet, under the Emperor's own seal, is in practice a safe-conduct anywhere in the empire.

## The Eight and the Ledger

From the top: the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], sons of the Dragon, the Emperor's family and the Princes; the [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]], sorcerers ranked second and owned as property; the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] warrior nobility; the Tanchei scholars, who read omens, keep the sacred texts and run the administration; the [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihwo]] artisans and common soldiers; the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwen]] farmers; the [[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]] merchants, ranked below the farmers however rich they are; and the [[lore-noutsou|Nwitsou]], slaves and outcasts. Every traveler carries a _Zhau Mou_, a palm-sized hardwood tablet stating caste, clan, district and issuing seal. Forging one is punished by public execution.

Common faith is [[lore-ancstrwrshp|ancestor worship]], kept as a ledger: merit and default are recorded, carried and inherited, and a family that neglects its rites is in debt to its own dead. A Tantsou introduces himself by lineage and caste, in that order, and most people of the lower castes have no personal name at all. Manners are formal and exact. A Tantsou does not say no outright; he declines in ways a foreigner has to learn to hear. A Tanchei's reading of the omens can postpone a campaign.

## The Throne and the Four Princes

> From the quay at Zhowen you see the enclave before anything else: a long wall rising straight out of the river on its island, the palace roofs behind it, and archers walking its top. A bridge runs to it from each shore. On most days nobody is on either bridge except the petitioners whose names are on the day's list.

The capital is two cities, one on each bank of a wide river, each the mirror of the other street for street, with the walled enclave of the Zhazho on the island between them. Nobody but the Zhazho and their personal attendants may enter it. A person who sets foot on a bridge without written permission is shot dead by the archers on the walls. Everything outside the walls, the two shores, their offices and their caste quarters, is held for the throne by the Governor of Zhowen.

The Emperor on the throne, [[being-tengrauemp|Tengrau]], is young and newly crowned, and he is still finding the reach of his own power. The Retired Emperor, [[being-yanzhortrd|Yanzho]], lives in the background of the court. Only the Emperor's attendants and the Princes speak to him. Everyone else, a minister, a general or an ambassador, speaks to the Speaker, who comes out to the gate-court before the enclave wall, hears the question, whispers it to the Emperor and speaks his answer aloud. The young Emperor's chief counselor is the Caste-Registrar, keeper of the rolls on which every subject's caste is entered, and every Prince courts the Caste-Registrar for that reason.

The four Princes (_Zho Zhei_) hold the provinces and their armies, and each means to be the one who shapes the young Emperor. You know a Prince when you see one: nails grown almost impossibly long and lacquered in brilliant colors, a painted face and painted teeth. Their contest is [[lore-weizhairui|The Jade Fan Dance]] (_Wei Zhai Rwi_), the court's game of intrigue, and in it a rumor, a shaming or a misfiled petition does as much harm as an army. No Prince holds the lead for long. A reading of the stars by the right Tanchei can raise a family or destroy it, and the rise of one gifted Wandwo can tip the balance in a season.

- [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]], is the strongest and the most feared. His water-gifted Wandwo bring rain to the delta while the rain fails elsewhere, and his granaries sell rice to the Princes whose provinces have gone dry, at the price he names. Some at court say his sorcerers are behind the drought. Nobody has proven it.
- [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], is younger and rising fast, a reformer with the young Emperor's ear.
- [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]], and [[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]], are bitter rivals over an old marriage between their houses and an heir who died, and each takes the other's loss as his own gain.

The embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] has come asking for direct relations with the Empire. A foreigner reaches the Emperor only through the Speaker, so the court reads the request as a breach of the Speaker's protocol: it asks for a door that has never existed. Chengzho takes the embassy up openly, for the foreign weight it adds to his hold on the Emperor's ear, and the other three Princes each read it as a move against them. Granting it would shift power among the Princes, so the game decides it, and not the Emperor.

## A Hungry South and Pressed Frontiers

The south is hungry. Its rice harvests have failed several years running, smaller rivers lie dry in their beds, and in some districts the Nouwen have risen against their lords and the magistrates who collect the tribute. In the market town of [[place-jaunou|Jaunou]], at the heart of the famine, the district granaries stand open and empty. The Tanchei are divided over the cause, between a breakdown of classification in the domains of Earth and Water and a weakening of the Mandate itself, a reading no scholar puts in writing. In the capital the price of rice on both shores follows the granaries of Wongsen.

Every frontier is pressed at once. The steppe warlord [[being-wogenhenzhuo|Wogen Henzhwo]] holds the tribes of [[place-blackwilds|the Black Wilds]] together, and his riders are in the northern districts, where the wall also keeps out the undead hordes. The general [[being-tuijawengyan|Tuija Wengyan]], stripped of his rank and exiled, is in open revolt and holds gains in the border provinces. In the remote northwest the [[lore-chaizho|Chaizho]] range through the country a great earthquake broke. Across the eastern strait the fleets of the [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]] houses take tolls and ransoms and plunder the coast; the coastal magistrates want their captured officers back, and the court has no fleet that can clear the water.

## What the Registers Keep

The empire writes everything down, and its best-known works are written the way its offices write: as a reading, an annal, an account, a petition, a roll and a last record.

- [[lore-readingsorting|The Reading of the Sorting]] is read in every audit hall at the new year, and tells how the world was sorted, how it would end if the sorting failed, and why keeping it is everyone's work.
- [[lore-annaljenshou|The Annal of Jenshou]] is the district annal of [[place-jenshou|Jenshou]], in which a flood is written with the verb a Prince's office required, and the Tanchei schools still argue its two readings.
- [[lore-hearthaccount|The Hearth Account of the Third Terrace]] is a farming household's reckoning with its dead at midwinter, and the model the village Leigen teach an heir from.
- [[lore-petitionseals|The Petition with Every Seal]] is a hearth tale for the Moon Festival of a petition that went round every bureau of heaven, and came back with one seal too many.
- [[lore-unnumberedtwr|The Song of the Unnumbered Tower]] is the soldiers' song of the northern marches, of the tower with no number and three men written down right.
- [[lore-lastrecordiron|The Last Record of Iron Gate of the Winter Mountain]] is the last record of an officer who would not break a village's tablets, and never once says no.

## Places and Dangers

The empire is the throne's own basins around Zhowen and four provinces, each held by a Prince from a seat of his own: the rich delta and coasts of Wongsen, the terraced uplands of Mwonou in the southwest, the western basins of Doulwen where the caravan road comes in, and the cold north of Kwozheng behind its marches. Its cities and towns:

```sql
WITH RECURSIVE tantsou(place) AS (
  SELECT 'thalorna-note-place-tantsouregin'
  UNION
  SELECT n.address.canonical
  FROM notes n, tantsou r
  WHERE list_contains(n.data.parents, r.place)
)
SELECT address.slug AS _ref,
       name.full AS "Town",
       data.population.apparent AS "People"
FROM notes
WHERE type = 'place'
  AND subType = 'settlement'
  AND address.canonical IN (SELECT place FROM tantsou)
  AND data.population.apparent >= 1000
  AND state = 'full'
ORDER BY data.population.apparent DESC, name.full COLLATE NOCASE
```

- [[place-kwisen|Kwisen]], the frontier way-station beyond [[place-jnanadvar|the Scholars' Door]], feeds and houses every traveler for as long as the weather requires, and has kept its register without a gap for four hundred years. Below it the road runs down into [[place-mwonouplnd|The Mwonou Uplands]], terrace country, where the southern famine has its heart.
- At Jenshou, [[place-jenshouhall|The District Hall of Jenshou]] keeps the district's register, its annal and its seal, and [[place-tabletbank|The Tablet Bank]] carries the sealed boundary-stones of the upper villages and holds the river off the upper town.
- The western passes toward the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazrin]], where [[lore-celestialroad|the Celestial Roads]] end at a gate of the wall, are quiet and expensively garrisoned.
- The port cities keep foreign merchants in walled, watched and taxed quarters, such as [[place-wongsenfrgnqrtr|The Foreign Quarter of Wongsen]].
- [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]], the island realm across [[place-easternstrt|The Eastern Strait]], has raided the coast for more than a thousand years while the Empire calls it a rebellious province.
- [[affiliation-haluozhao|The Black Lotus Society]] trades in forged tablets, and the Jade Fan Dance draws in anyone useful.

## People to Meet

The registrar's daughter at the way-station, the soldiers of the escort and the clerks at their desks all have lives beyond your page in the register. Some of the empire's people hold its offices and its quarrels in their hands:

```sql
SELECT address.slug AS _ref,
       name.full AS "Person",
       data.occupation AS "Occupation"
FROM notes
WHERE type = 'being'
  AND data.culture = 'thalorna-note-lore-tantsouclt'
  AND subType IN ('npc', 'character')
  AND state = 'full'
ORDER BY name.full COLLATE NOCASE
```

At court you deal with the Caste-Registrar, [[being-cstrgstrtnts|Third Son of the Archivist of the Clan of Lanterns Along the Bank]], and on the shores with the Governor of Zhowen, [[being-gvrnrzhowen|Eldest Son of the Gate Captain of the Clan of the Heron on the Wall]], whose officers hold the rice barges at the canal locks until their seals are checked. In the uplands you meet [[being-jnshoregstr|Second Daughter of the Seal-Cutter of the Clan of Cold Spring Under Pines]], rotated in to keep the rolls at Jenshou, and [[being-jaunoumgstrt|Eldest Son of the Commandant of the Clan of Red Gate in Autumn]], the magistrate who reads the law in starving Jaunou. Most Tantsou you meet have no name to give you, only a description a clerk could file.

## What Gets a Newcomer into Trouble

- **Have your papers at the wall.** The wall's gates let nobody in or out without papers and approvals, and beyond the wall the empire's law, and its protection, end.
- **Carry your tablet.** Outside the district where you are known by face, an innkeeper, a quay officer or a street guard reads your tablet before anything else happens. Without one you are not entered, not given a bed and held the first time anyone asks.
- **Stay off the bridges.** In Zhowen a foot on a bridge without written permission is a death the archers deal on the spot, with no magistrate and no plea.
- **Do not ask to see the Emperor.** You speak to the Speaker, and the answer he gives is the answer on the record.
- **Never buy a tablet.** A tablet no bureau issued is forgery, and forgery is a public execution.
- **Listen for the refusal.** A Tantsou goes to great lengths to decline without saying no, and a direct refusal from you embarrasses you both.
- **Mind your classification's date.** A merchant whose classification runs out is an unclassified foreigner the morning after, and his goods stay in the warehouse when he cannot.

## Ways In

[[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|The Embassy from Dúnavarre to Tantsou]] carries a party east as part of a western embassy, into the middle of the Princes' game. Its eastern leg is the campaign frame [[scenario-doornvrexst|A Door That Has Never Existed]], which follows the embassy from the pass to the court's verdict through three adventures:

- **At the door.** At [[place-kwisen|Kwisen]], below the Scholars' Door, [[scenario-pgrgstrhld|A Page the Register Can Hold]] runs two hooks: weather shuts the col with a party still above the place where the escort waits, and the registrar's daughter finds the party has no caste to answer with and wants an account her register can hold.
- **At the chancellery.** At Zhowen the embassy's first business is paper: in [[scenario-flngnwrgn|A Filing in the New Reign]] two clerks of the chancellery come to the compound gate for the embassy's papers, each for a different hall, and the roll for the next hearing closes at sunset on the fourth day.
- **At the port.** At Wongsen, in [[scenario-tbltwrnghnd|A Tablet from the Wrong Hand]], a merchant's trading classification is about to expire with his goods still in a warehouse of [[place-wongsenfrgnqrtr|The Foreign Quarter of Wongsen]]. A tablet bought from the wrong person in the same quarter is either a forgery or a trap, and each answer has someone who wants it found.

If you come from the empire itself, you might be a Tanchei clerk rotated to a district that does not want you, as the registrars of [[place-jenshouhall|The District Hall of Jenshou]] are rotated, a [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] officer's younger son waiting on a name he has not been granted, or a grain merchant's clerk at a canal lock above [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], where a string of rice barges from Wongsen is held on a question of seals while the price rises on both shores. The frame's Ways In bring a party like this to the embassy from the other side of the gate.

A party that comes east by the Celestial Road meets [[place-tantsouwall|the Wall of the Ten Thousand Watchfires]] first, at the garrison and customs-house on the western pass, and goes no farther without a classification.
