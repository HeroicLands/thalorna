> The escort met your party at the col, exactly there and not a step sooner, and for two days the soldiers have walked ahead of you in silence down a road of fitted stone. On the third evening the way-station rises out of the pines: a walled court of gray brick with green-glazed tiles on its roofs and red-lacquered gates, the last light catching on a bronze bell hung under the eaves. Inside the gate everything happens in order. Porters in undyed hemp set your bales down in a chalked square and step back from them. A soldier in lacquered scale checks a wooden tablet on a cord at a passing groom's belt, turns it over to the seal, and hands it back without a word. Through an open screen you see a long room of low desks, where clerks in dark blue robes write in columns with fine brushes, and you smell ink, pine smoke and steamed millet. No one raises a voice. Your [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] guides, who will not come a step farther, are already being fed at a separate table.
>
> A woman in a scholar's blue robe, her hair pinned with a single plain comb, comes to the edge of the veranda and bows to you, precisely and not deeply. "Second Daughter of the Registrar of the Clan of Moon Rises Over Still Water, near Weichai," she says. "Tanchéi." She waits. When it is plain that you have no caste to answer with, she inclines her head, leads you to the lowest end of a long bench, sets a bowl of millet and a cup of pale tea in front of you, and opens a fresh page in a bound register you are not allowed to see.

The [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] is the oldest continuously governed state in the world, a realm of river basins, terraced hills and crowded cities in the far east of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]]. It holds that the cosmos was made by sorting, that the sorting is unfinished, and that every person's work is to keep the sorted world apart from the unsorted. Its emperor is the [[lore-mandatehvn|Son of Heaven]], its gods are a celestial bureaucracy that the imperial bureaucracy mirrors, and its people live in eight castes that fix their trade, dress, marriage and the name, if any, they may bear. To the frontier clerk you are a document that arrived without a filing number.

**The one thing to understand first: a foreigner is unclassified until a bureau says otherwise.** Without classification a stranger cannot own property, sign a contract or seek the law's protection; a crime against him is no crime under the law, and a crime by him is a breach of the order, punished fast. The usual remedy is a bureau's classification, such as the trading classification a merchant receives with a tablet to carry, good for the length of his business. A traveler classified by the Emperor's own decree carries a tablet under the imperial seal and goes where he likes.

## The Eight and the Ledger

From the top: the [[lore-zhazho|Zhazho]], sons of the Dragon, the Emperor's family and the Warlords; the [[lore-wanduo|Wánduo]], sorcerers ranked second and owned as property; the [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] warrior nobility; the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchéi]] scholars, who read omens, keep the sacred texts and run the administration; the [[lore-tsaihuo|Tsaihuo]] artisans and common soldiers; the [[lore-nouwen|Nouwén]] farmers; the [[lore-shuonong|Shuonong]] merchants, ranked below the farmers however rich they are; and the [[lore-noutsou|Noutsou]], slaves and outcasts. Every traveler carries a _Zhao Mou_, a palm-sized hardwood tablet stating caste, clan, district and issuing seal. Forging one is punished by public execution.

Common faith is [[lore-ancstrwrshp|ancestor worship]], kept as a ledger: merit and default are recorded, carried and inherited, and a family that neglects its rites is in debt to its own dead. A Tantsou introduces himself by lineage and caste, in that order, and most people of the lower castes have no personal name at all. Manners are formal and exact. A Tantsou does not say no outright; he declines in ways a foreigner has to learn to hear. A Tanchéi's reading of the omens can postpone a campaign.

## Places and Dangers

- The frontier way-station beyond [[place-jnanadvara|Jnanadvara]] feeds and houses every traveler for as long as the weather requires, and has kept its register without a gap for four hundred years.
- The western passes toward the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]], at the eastern end of the **Celestial Roads**, are quiet and expensively garrisoned.
- The port cities keep foreign merchants in walled, watched and taxed quarters.
- [[place-jenchairgn|Jenchai]], the island realm across the eastern strait, has raided the coast for a thousand years while the Empire calls it a rebellious province.
- The [[affiliation-haluozhao|Black Lotus Society]] trades in forged tablets, and the [[lore-weizhairui|Wei Zhai Rui]], the court's game of manipulation, draws in anyone useful.

## Ways In

[[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|The Embassy from Dunavarre to Tantsou]] carries a party east as part of a western embassy. A Vedyari astronomer crossing Jnanadvara needs guards and someone to stand beside him while he is classified. A merchant's trading classification is about to expire with his goods still in the warehouse. A tablet bought from the wrong person in a port quarter is either a forgery or a trap, and each answer has someone who wants it found.
