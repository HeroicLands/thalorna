> You come into [[place-kaljekor|The Golden Flame]], which everyone there calls Kaljekor, in the last heat of the afternoon, walking behind a herd. The dust the cattle raise hangs red-gold in the low sun, it is in your teeth and the creases of your hands, and the lowing of a thousand animals rolls ahead of you into the town. Then the pyramid stands up out of the haze, terrace on terrace of red sandstone, and every terrace is a market: awnings of striped cloth, copper pans flashing, bolts of linen and stacked hides, and at the top a thin column of smoke going straight up into the still air. The festival has filled the place. Herdsmen with spears and wool cloaks crowd the lower steps, foreheads smeared gray with ashmark; a priest-noble in a collar of beaten gold is carried through them in a chair, and nobody makes room until he is nearly on top of them. Merchants up from Au'Khelathu weigh silver on hand scales. Children run between the legs of the cattle, and somewhere a drum is keeping time for dancers you cannot see.
>
> A tall woman with close-cropped hair and a cloak of brown and white calfskin pushes out of the crowd, an ox-goad over one shoulder and a gourd in her hand. She counts the herd as it passes her, lips moving, before she looks at you. "_okharé, ne thalé_," she says, loud and cheerful, a greeting that hails you as one of the people: Okharik one, greetings. "[Ádhora]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, of the line of [Kithma]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, of the [Tálumeng]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, of Kaljekor." She pulls the stopper from the gourd with her teeth and hands it to you, and it is soured milk, cold from the shade, while her herders stand by watching to see you drink.

[[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] is a hot kingdom at the western end of [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]]'s fertile belt, with a long north coast on the [[place-vylarianse|Vilérian Sea]] and its harbor at Kaljekor, [[place-bethuargn|Bethua Region]] to the east, and the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], trackless except by its rivers, to the south. In the west the rain does the work that canals do elsewhere: barley, sorghum, flax, dates and figs grow on it, and the village tribes farm, fish and fell timber from fixed seats. Eastward the land dries to savanna, and the people follow cattle, sheep, goats and small sturdy horses between seasonal grazings on cycles so old they are a religious duty. Three temple-cities stand above it all, and the herds and the cities need each other.

**The one thing to understand first: wealth walks.** A rich Okharik's wealth is livestock that can be counted at a glance, raided, and never hidden, so a display of hospitality is also a display of accounts. Land is not owned; grazing is held by customary right, this clan in that valley in that season, and a clan that yields a right once has yielded it for good. Grazing rights are the commonest cause of bloodshed in the realm, and feuds are long and settled in cattle.

## What You Meet First

An Okharik names his lineage, then his clan, then his city, and places you by the same three. He belongs to a clan, which moves, and to a city, which does not and which keeps his festivals, his ironwork and his god. The compact between the two halves is the [Three-Flame Settlement]{.name data-type="lore/law"}: the cities give religious legitimacy, metal and trade, and the clans give the herds, the wool and leather, and the warriors. The rural clans often feel neglected, and the cities rarely notice.

The faith recognizes one god, [[lore-nkaruthardty|Nkaru'thar]], the eternal flame of creation, destruction and renewal, read through three cities and never as three gods. On an ordinary day a herdsman with a sick calf prays to the [[lore-zohira|zohira]], the spirits of fertility, storm, river and death whose shrines dot the grazing country. The festivals hold the realm together: clan leaders gather, disputes are heard, marriages are made, and herds change hands. Okharis strikes no coin; the [[lore-aukhlthcrncy|Khelathi weight-pieces]] of gezan and qelu pass everywhere.

## Where to Go

[[place-zarhanis|The First Flame]], Zarhanis in the old tongue, keeps the largest temple and [[miscgear-emberfirst|The First Ember]], which has never gone out; the King lives there beside the High Pyremant, and the Oracle keeps the temple's dreams. Kaljekor sits on the trade roads and is ruled by a council of priest-nobles; merchants swear oaths on [[miscgear-sclskaljekr|The Scales of Kaljekor]], its gilded beam. [[place-vuthraka|The Wild Flame]], Vuthraka, guards the southern frontier under the Warden of the Flame, who brands proven warriors in the [Trial of Flames]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}; the Captain of the Host keeps his quarters there, where the garrison and the clans' riders assemble. Beyond the settled country rise [[place-yandazhrng|The Mountains of the Spirits]], Yanda Zohiring, held to be the dwelling of the gods and the seat of the shamans' own oracle, [[lore-orclbonesspr|The Oracle of Bones]], and the shamans who claim to have reached it do not agree on where it is.

The dangers are the herds' dangers. Raiders take cattle, a clan pushes into a valley that is not its season, and the eastern clans pay tribute to Bethua for grazing that their grandfathers held free. Trade wealth feeds Kaljekor's ambitions while Vuthraka demands more for defense, and the King holds the balance by ceremony. South lies the rainforest, trackless except by its rivers, which nobody has charted.

## People to Meet

A few people stand at the center of the year. [[being-ewuratebara|Éwura Tébara]], the Oracle, answers the questions the realm puts to the flame, and the King, [[being-takorozanethar|Tákoro Zanethar]], will not call the Host, answer an embassy or set the tribute until she has spoken; this year she has given no answer. [[being-ngaluromara|Ngalu Rómara]], the Captain of the Host, counts the riders the clans have held back, and [[being-omaresonkara|Omare Sónkara]], the Warden of the Flame, answers the Oracle's silence by arming the frontier. At Kaljekor [[being-fumbarimakora|Fúmbari Mákora]], the Master of the Market, keeps the scales and weighs every promise sworn on them.

The festival moot seats the ten chieftains: [[being-kithmarembi|Kithma Rembi]] of the [[affiliation-khamundatrb|Khamunda Tribe]], [[being-mbikapanawe|Mbika Pánawe]] of the [[affiliation-wambuchitrb|Wámbuchi Tribe]], [[being-berathikarambi|Bérathi Kárambi]] of the [[affiliation-zhikandetrb|Zhíkande Tribe]], [[being-ezorojandaka|Ezoro Jándaka]] of the [[affiliation-yeshirotrb|Yéshiro Tribe]], [[being-kwathormbasu|Kwathor Mbasu]] of the [[affiliation-dongarutrb|Dóngaru Tribe]], [[being-mbelethorungi|Mbeleth Órungi]] of the Tálumeng, [[being-ngorombulawe|Ngoro Mbúlawe]] of the [[affiliation-ngorashatrb|Ngorasha Tribe]], [[being-ntembakasara|Ntemba Kásara]] of the [[affiliation-shokumatrb|Shókuma Tribe]], [[being-nyoralemberi|Nyora Lémberi]] of the [[affiliation-kirofatrb|Kirofa Tribe]] and [[being-omilotarombu|Omilo Tárombu]] of the [[affiliation-magaratrb|Magara Tribe]]. Each carries a village's dues, a grievance against the registers and a want a stranger can serve. The rest of the Okhariks a party is likely to meet:

## Ways In

The Oracle's silence has set the realm on edge, and parties enter by whichever door is nearest. Ngalu Rómara wants someone to ride the grazing country and learn which clans are keeping their horsemen at home, and the clans will not answer a Captain's question as they answer a stranger's. Omare Sónkara pays for scouts on the southern frontier, where the rainforest begins and nobody has charted it.

A clan of the [[affiliation-talumengtrb|Tálumeng Tribe]] hires you to drive its herd to the festival through country where a rival clan claims the grazing. The Khamunda and the Wámbuchi each carry a count to the temple granaries at Zarhanis: Kithma Rembi wants every jar entered at her own count, and Mbika Pánawe will not let this year's raised entry for grain stand. Either will take a witness who is nobody's kin. A merchant swore falsely on the Scales of Kaljekor, the year and a day is nearly out, and his family wants someone to find out what is coming for him; Fúmbari Mákora can say on which day he swore.

The Magara have lost a [Spirit Blade]{.name data-type="weapongear"} to raiders, and Omilo Tárombu, whose horsemen the Khelathi court, wants it found. A shaman of the Shókuma wants an escort into Yanda Zohiring and will not say what she means to ask.

## What Is Chanted and Told

The temple's chants are held by heart in the old register, and the clans' tellings are told at the fold gate, the cairn and the ford. These are heard most often:

- [[lore-hymnthrfires|The Hymn of the Three Fires]] is the creation hymn chanted at the Rite of Three Fires, and its refrain is known by everyone who has stood outside a temple court at the Rekindling: [One fire. Not three.]{.verse}
- [[lore-ashbornsong|The Song of the Burnt Ground]] is the Ashborn war-hymn, answered by the whole line: [We stand on ash.]{.verse}
- [[lore-guestprice|The Guest-Price]] tells why a stranger is handed soured milk at the fold gate, and what the Khelathi wrote down when they drank it.
- [[lore-scalesoath|The Oath at the Scales]] is the market tale behind the saying every trader at Kaljekor says before a weighing: [Dry salt. Thirsty cow.]{.verse}
- [[lore-bonesanswer|The Answer from the Bones]] is the Night of Ashes telling of a shaman's one question to the Oracle of Bones and the one word it answered.
- [[lore-emberroad|The Ember on the Road]] is the telling of a new fire carried home from Zarhanis, and of a valley paid for one coal.
