> You climb the last of the head-valley into [[place-nurvhrn|Nürvhrn]] in the blue hour after sunset, with the first hard frost squeaking under your boots and your breath freezing in your scarf. There is no village to see, only a long low hump of turf, white with old snow, running across the valley floor like a sleeping animal. Smoke leaks from a dozen holes along its back. A door of bound planks opens and lets out heat, the smell of yak butter and wet wool, and a roar of voices. Inside, the long house runs away from you into firelit dimness: hearth after hearth along a packed-earth floor swept clean as a table, children asleep in heaps of hides, a girl working grease into a coil of rope, two old men bent over a broken pack-saddle, a woman stirring a pot and arguing with three people at once without looking up from it. Nobody here has a room of their own, and nobody seems to want one.
>
> A broad man with frost still in his beard pushes through the crowd toward you. He speaks lowland Vedyari, slow and careful. "Hvrel, of the Klemvrd hearth. Fort-carrier." Then, without being asked: "The first Hvrel was found under the upper shelf of Jñānadvāra, eleven winters back." He takes your pack off your shoulders before you can object, sets you down at his hearth between his daughter and a sleeping dog, and puts a bowl of barley and butter in your hands. The janapadas' seal is in its box, he says, nodding toward the dark end of the house, and you can see it after you have eaten.

The [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] are nine thousand drovers who keep the two crossings of [[place-estrnreach|the Eastern Reach]], where the [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] swing east from [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]] toward [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]]. Through the open season their hearths are scattered across the high pastures with the pack-beasts; through the closed months the whole people lives at Nürvhrn, under one turf roof. They are poor by any lowland measure. They are also the best-connected people on the wall: they feed the one fort the temple-republics keep, they carry the scholars and envoys who cross by [[place-jnanadvara|Jñānadvāra]], and about forty lowlanders speak their tongue.

**The one thing to understand first: nobody owns a road here.** The Hvarn share the crossings out each winter. In the weeks after midwinter the hearth-heads sit in the middle of the long house and say aloud, in front of everyone, who walks which crossing in the coming season and what each hearth still owes another. The council has no power to compel anyone, and it needs none: nobody who ignored it would be given a road. A party hiring Hvarn guides is hiring whoever the winter council chose, and the fee goes to the guide's hearth.

## The Party and the Roll

Hvarn work is roped work, and the language marks in its verbs whether a man did a thing himself or as one of a party. A reach-guide answers to the council for anyone who does not come back. The drovers who kept the beasts on the lower stages are not held to have crossed at all.

The Hvarn go down for their dead. The bone-bringer recovers a body from the eastern gorges wherever recovery is possible, records where it was found, and recites the whole roll at the winter council, where anyone may correct him from the floor. Children are named from the roll. A Hvarn asked about his name answers with a place, and the roll is the best map of where the ground fails that anyone has.

## Places and Dangers

- [[place-nurvhrn|Nürvhrn]] is the winter house and the council's seat. Lowlanders come only for the renewal of the fort contract, every third year, and nobody has been invited to stay the winter in four centuries.
- [[place-jnanadvara|Jñānadvāra]], the scholars' door, takes a month and a half to reach Tānvür's western frontier, where a Tānvüri way-station records every traveler in a register no Vedyari has been allowed to see.
- [[place-sankhadvra|Shankhadvāra]], the conch-door, is the worst crossing on the wall: a shelf above an unmeasured gorge, two months to anywhere, two or three parties in a good year.
- [[place-sanghafort|The Sangha-fort]] on the conch-door col holds sixty men and lives on Hvarn pack-trains. [[affiliation-vindhyalay|Shikharālaya]] resents the arrangement, and the Hvarn decline to discuss it.
- The storms of the Reach come up the eastern valleys without warning, and a storm-reader's refusal ends a day's travel.

## Ways In

A party can come up with a lowland factor for the contract renewal, or be hired over Jñānadvāra with an astronomer and a crate of manuscripts. A hearth that lost a party has no road until the council says so, and its people need work, a witness or the body back. The roll records where every Hvarn dead was found; a body that is missing from the gorges, or a name that the roll cannot place, is a mystery the whole long house will want solved before midwinter.

| -------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| hearth name    | The family name a hearth gives, which changes when a person is taken into another |
| hearth-head    | Head of a hearth, sitting on the winter council and speaking for it               |
| winter council | The gathering of hearth-heads after midwinter that shares out the crossings       |
| winter-speaker | Presiding officer of the winter council, with no authority once it rises          |

| fort-carrier   | Holder of the contract that feeds the Sangha-fort                                 |
| bone-bringer   | Recoverer of the dead from the gorges and keeper of the roll                      |
| roll           | The recited record of the dead and where each was found                           |
| taken-in       | One sheltered by a hearth without being of its blood                              |
| unhoused       | One put out of the winter house for a fault the council has named                 |
