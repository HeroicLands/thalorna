> Dawn on the crossing is gray, and it stays gray. Fog lies on the water so thick that the ship's lantern makes a yellow room around the bow and nothing more; the deck is slick, the rigging drips, and the cold works through your cloak to the skin. Then the fog thins, and a brown moor rises out of it, heather and black peat cut in steps, and below it the sheltered anchorage of [[place-ravenmoor|Ravenmoor]]. Stone houses roofed in gray slate crowd the slope, peat smoke lies flat over the chimneys, and gulls scream over a fishing boat unloading herring in silver baskets. Nobody on the quay is in a hurry. A cooper hoops a barrel with slow taps of his mallet, two women in dun shawls mend a net between them, and a line of ships rides at anchor waiting its turn: Aldorathi cogs riding low under bales of fleece, and an Ondarethan trader with a red mantle painted on her stern.
>
> A boat brings the harbor officers out to you. The first aboard is a woman in a long coat of charcoal wool with a brass chain of office at her throat, followed by a clerk with a writing board and a young man carrying a tray lined with gray felt. She looks at the ship before she looks at anyone on it, then at you. "Welcome to [[place-eilwith|Eilwìth]], friend, and well met," she says, quietly, in the formal way, and again in the court register of the [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri Language]] a ship from the mainland is owed: "_Shalor en kir a thel_," welcome, friend of the light. "I am [Ívelin of Ravenmoor]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and I serve the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Synod]]." Her clerk dips his pen. She nods at the felt tray. "Your lading may bide. Hath your ship aught aboard that ye cannot explain?"

Eilwìth is a large island of about seven hundred thousand people off the western coast of [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris]], north-west of Élavendre, a long and dangerous sail from the mainland. Four realms share its coasts, a third of it is old forest that no plow has touched, and fog is the ordinary weather of the west. The island runs its affairs with the entire seriousness of a continent and almost no news of one: its lords hold court as though their precedence mattered beyond the next valley, and most Eilwìthans have no idea how small the island is.

**The one thing to understand first: every ship that crosses calls at Ravenmoor.** The Synod of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] mages that governs [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Dúnavarre]] requires every vessel between Eilwìth and the mainland to put in at Ravenmoor first, inbound and outbound. Its officers look for prohibited goods, and they record and often detain anything magical. The Synod meets every visitor before anyone else on the island does, and it is the only body on Eilwìth that knows how the island compares with the world.

## What You Meet First

The realms are [[affiliation-kngdmldrth|Aldorath]], the feudal breadbasket east of the great lake, where four in five people are serfs on manorial estates; Dúnavarre, the smallest and the most powerful, where the Synod rules through stewards and the [[affiliation-justbladeordr|Order of the Just Blade]]; the [[affiliation-repblcndrth|Republic of Ondareth]] in the west and south-west, a senate of mantled senators built in imitation of a Vilérian republic few there have seen, whose offices are for sale; and [[affiliation-jrldmstrmvld|Stormveld]] in the mountainous north, where Nordmen took the plain sixty years ago and work it with thralls. Every court keeps Arcane Order advisors, and every ruler knows the Synod can withdraw them.

An Eilwìthan attends the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian]] temple and also leaves something at the stone, the crossroads and the lake, and of all his duties those observances are the last he neglects. A farmer may say something naive about the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Empire]] and then name exactly which crossroads not to stand at after dark, in the words of [[lore-reevesthrees|the Reeve's Threes]]: "Three things not to be done after dark: to stand where the roads divide, to call the lake by name, and to count the standing stones." The coin is [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian]]: the Solden, the Ardin and the Bit.

## Where to Go, and What to Fear

[[place-wraymouth|Wreimouth]] is Dúnavarre's working harbor and [[place-dunmere|Dunmere]] Aldorath's walled seat. [[place-sunnerslade|Sunnerslade]] ripens a wine in the lee of [[place-ironfells|the Ironfells]] that has no business existing. In the high valleys of the Ironfells the [[lore-vardain|Vardain]], free remnant of the conquered north, live under the protection of the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]] of [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]].

The island's reputation abroad is wrong about its people and right about its land. There are places where the fae are strong, deep in the forest and in the wild north, and the borders of the realms bend around them. [[lore-grukaraelwyth|Grukar]] have lived in the empty third of the island for seven hundred years. The tribes of the [[place-pshtrwldrns|Peshtar Wilderness]] live outside every realm, and the forest peoples are the most warlike of them. [[place-hallowwood|Seirasto]] on the south coast is the forest of Eilwìth's [[lore-flksenali|Senali]], and it does not receive visitors.

## Ways In

- **A kiln that stays hot.** Border farms need the abandoned works of [[place-blackkilnpriory|Blackkiln Priory]] investigated. [[scenario-blackfireblkkln|Cinders in the Dead Pines]] begins with the smell of hot iron among dead trees.
- **Lamps on an empty road.** A free bed beckons from [[place-wenlornestage|Wenlorne Stage]], down the old road from Dunmere. [[scenario-lampswenlorne|Pale Lamps on the Crown Road]] asks what a guest owes a host.
- **A cure with a price.** A war-chief seeks help at [[place-nethermootholw|Nethermoot Hollow]], where the witch names her charges in years. [[scenario-yearsinoaks|Ten Winters in the Bark]] puts the bargain before the party.
- **A bell beneath the tide.** The commission wants its chest from [[place-threnhallow|Threnhallow]], and a pilot wants his missing son. [[scenario-bellsgreylvl|The Bells Beneath the Greilevels]] follows the causeway out at low water.
- **No signal from the pass.** A relief patrol climbs to [[place-sunderwynd|Sunderwiind]]. [[scenario-hangingdead|The Hanging Dead of the Ironfells]] begins when the road closes behind it.
- **Three nights in the wood.** [[being-neilwa|Neilwa]] asks for passage to her people’s dead; the Order posts the paths. [[scenario-seilvririte|Three Nights for the Hushwood]] can begin among the Seilvri or with a commission from the Order.
- **A boat and two cutters.** [[being-elfithwgdn|Elfith Wigden]] wants an account of Koldmere’s missing people and equipment. [[scenario-reedgillnets|Nets from Both Shores]] takes the party onto disputed reed-beds below [[place-reedgill|Reedgill]].
- **Horses for a name.** The highland breeders want their mares back, and [[being-kadrum|Kadrum]] wants the names of his brother’s killers. [[scenario-durraskcount|Forty Mares and One Name]] carries those offers between Stonyford and the Thornwold.
- **Service beyond the island.** The Synod needs staff for a diplomatic mission. [[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dunavarre to Tantsou]] opens its mainland adventures with a commission at Péravel.

## An Eilwìth Campaign {#campaign}

A company can begin at Ravenmoor with a commission and a reason to travel: recovering property, carrying petitions, serving an embassy or escorting people whom a court refuses to hear. Agree whose authority the characters accept, what payment they expect and which promises they make in their own names. A sealed letter has a different reach in Aldorath’s manors, the Durrask steads and the Seilvri woods.

The island’s roads and water connect the work. Dunmere gives access to the old southern road; the Greilevels lead out from Greiwater; Kaerwiind watches the approaches to the Ironfells. Koldmere and Stonyford put the company among people who need boats, grazing and riders while the realms demand service. Let a delivered message, a recovered object or a person brought home establish the next relationship. Each adventure has an outcome worth playing on its own.

The Order wages a crusade against the Seilvri, a people defending their homes. Characters serving the Order can confront what its commission requires; characters entering through Teilvari can help people whose rites and ground the kingdom denies. Choosing an employer does not decide a character’s conscience in advance.

A company leaving the island still passes through Ravenmoor. Embassy service carries its loyalties into foreign courts, where the Synod’s authority meets other laws and other sacred boundaries. Keep the consequences of the company’s promises with it, alongside the money and equipment it earns.

## Songs and Sayings

The common people of Eilwìth keep their customs in a few works, each tied to a road, a night or an office:

- [[lore-reevesthrees|The Reeve's Threes]] hold the custom of the Aldorathi manors in sayings a reeve learns by heart and says first at the manor court.
- [[lore-roadstops|The Ballad of the Road That Stops]] is the drovers' walking song of the Crown Road that ends at a bank of oaks, and the reason the highlands tell their children not to take it.
- [[lore-hildricplay|The Play of Hildrik and the Grukar]] is played in the Ashford inn-yards at the autumn fair, a war against the Grukar that the march never expects to end.
- [[lore-coldfordhead|The Head at Koldford]] is told at Wiindmarch wakes, and explains the loaf left by the dead and carried to the crossroads stone.
- [[lore-windownaming|The Naming at the Window]] is the widow's lament said over the black candle at the [[lore-silentpassage|Silent Passage]], for dead the wood took without graves.
- [[lore-kingswassail|The Wassail at Kingshollow]] is sung at the royal hall's door at midwinter, and asks the king for the one tree the village may cut.
