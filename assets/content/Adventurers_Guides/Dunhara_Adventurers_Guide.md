> The caravan comes into [[place-dunashir|Dûn Ashir]] an hour before dusk, when the heat lets go of the sand and the dunes to the south turn the color of an old copper pot. Palms crowd a hollow in the gravel plain, and the air under them is cool enough to make you shiver; it smells of wet earth, dung smoke and crushed dates. Black goat-hair tents run out from the water in long rows. Camels kneel and complain while boys haul dripping leather buckets up from the well. A woman in an indigo veil weighs a bolt of red wool against a sack of salt; a herder with sun-cracked hands drives goats through the press; a broad-shouldered warrior with a strip of deep-blue leather bound round his left arm watches the newcomers without seeming to. Behind the tents a man is reciting verse, and everyone within earshot has stopped talking to hear it.
>
> A lean man in a dust-colored robe steps out of the nearest tent and lifts a hand to you. "_Mehrûn parvandar sâzâdar_," he says, the blessing a Dunhari gives a traveler: the moon guides the truthful one. "I am Tavîshar, of the Afsharan. I am your guide for the next eleven days, and this is Afsharan water." He breaks a flat round of bread, presses a pinch of gray salt into the torn edge, and holds it out, and he does not lower his hand until you have eaten. Two of his kinsmen look up from the camel they are hobbling and nod once, as if a debt had just been entered. Only then does he ask your name, and who sent you.

The [[place-dunharargn|Dunhara]] is a desert the size of an empire with no empire in it. Sand seas, black mesas and dry lakebeds run from the scrub behind the eastern shore of the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]] to the marches of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara]], and across all of it perhaps a dozen great confederations and many small ones ride their seasonal circuits between the oases their grandfathers used. These are the [[affiliation-dunhartrbs|Dunhara Tribes]]. Each answers to its own sheikh and none to a king, and their caravans carry the southern overland trade from the sea coast to Vedyara. In a country where one well can decide a war, a person's word and a person's welcome are the only securities there are, and the Dunhari have built their whole law on those two.

**The one thing to understand first: salt binds.** Tavîshar's bread was a contract. A tribe that has fed you salt must protect you for a stated period after you leave, against its own blood if need be and at the cost of open war. The rule has no exceptions, because a desert where shelter depended on the guest's identity would be a desert nobody could cross. The same rule binds you. A guest who brings a feud into a host's tent has shamed the host, and the poets will say so.

## Who Speaks for a Tribe

A sheikh holds his place by descent, by his own standing and by the consent of the elders, and he loses it when any one of the three fails. Beneath him are the warriors who ride the raid and the caravan escort, and the poet-scholars who keep the tribe's law, genealogy and treaties in verse. When two tribes dispute a well or a death, each brings its recitation before poets of a third tribe, who judge which version is sound.

A blood-feud can run for generations, and it always has known exits: restitution in stock, a marriage, a shared pilgrimage, or a sheikh's formal apology to another sheikh. Once a settlement is recited in public, it binds both sides. Reputation weighs more than any of it. A shamed warrior keeps his life and loses marriage, the raid, the council and every courtesy of the camp.

The tribes keep the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] faith of fire and light, and an oath sworn at a fire is a legal act and a religious one at once. The god who matters most in camp is [[affiliation-bahramis|Báhrámiš]] of the storm. A wind-reader or rain-caller of the [[affiliation-strmcltsbhrms|Storm Cults]] sits in every camp of consequence, and a chieftain asks for a reading before he moves his herds. In the deep desert live the desert mystics, who fast alone in the open sand; one who returns with visions that prove true is followed for life.

## Places and Dangers

- [[place-stoneofranaz|The Stone of Ranâz]] is the hill-shrine where three tribal territories meet. The [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|Warrior's Circle]] gathers there each quarter to renew the indigo vâst-bahâr in the vat kept at the shrine, and the elders of the storm cults sit there at the season-turns to recognize a vessel of the god. Uninvited foreigners are received with courtesy and sent away with nothing.
- [[place-dunashir|Dûn Ashir]], the oasis seat of the tribes, is where a caravan hires its guide.
- The open sand kills fast. Summer heat is lethal within hours, winter nights freeze, and a windstorm can bury a camp in minutes.
- [[place-khuqetmiglet|Khuqet-Miglet]], in the southwest, is a desert-march the empire of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Aû'Khelâthu]] garrisons and the western clans contest, with toll and well-disputes on both sides.
- The [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Sultanate of Amradad]] holds the region's north-western corner on the sea coast. It is a separate kingdom of walled cities and a standing army, and the tribes admire its wealth and scorn its softness.

## Ways In

A party usually arrives as a caravan's escort. Escort duty passes from tribe to tribe as a caravan crosses each territory, and a dispute at a handover can strand the cargo between two sheikhs. Other troubles are already moving: a delegation petitioning the Warrior's Circle against a Dunhari chieftain, the marked stones that choose the Circle's Voice gone missing before a meeting, two storm-cult lineages giving opposite readings of the coming rains, and a [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] storm-shaman preaching where he should not.
