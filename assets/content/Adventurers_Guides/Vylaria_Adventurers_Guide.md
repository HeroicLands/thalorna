> The grain ship noses in to the Wharves an hour after dawn, and Magnápolis rises over you in tiers. Six hills of white marble, red tile and gilded bronze climb from the river, and the low sun sets the domes on the highest of them burning gold. Along the great avenue on the far slope the night's lamps still glow, cold and blue against the daylight, though no flame burns in them. The air is mild and already warm on your face. It smells of river mud and tar, of brine and gutted fish from the market sheds, of hot bread, and of the woodsmoke of a hundred thousand hearths.
>
> The quay stones are wet and worn hollow by centuries of feet. Porters swarm aboard before the lines are made fast, calling the count of sacks in a quick, rhythmic chant, and handcarts rattle away over the paving toward stairs so steep they are half ladders. At the foot of the gangplank a gray-haired watchman in a scuffed leather jack, a red horsehair crest on his helmet, leans on his cudgel and looks at every face that comes down. A Haradian factor in saffron-and-green striped silk argues weights with a customs clerk, who answers by reading the tariff aloud from a wax tablet, clause by clause, without once raising his voice. A senator's chair goes by on the river road on the shoulders of eight bearers, its purple curtains drawn, and the crowd parts for it without being asked. Beyond it a fountain at the heart of a vast paved circle throws its water higher than your masts.
>
> A man of middle years steps out of the press: a long, sun-browned face, green eyes, black hair receding from his brow, a good wool mantle with wax on its cuffs. He takes in your boots, your belts and your blades, and bows to exactly the depth they deserve. "_Dêlâ sormâkânis dáemzâ-tôr_," he says, the formal greeting of trade: the merchant grants you honor. "I am Harphôs Ôrchârenîs, chandler, of Aelissium, citizen of the Empire. Whom do you serve, and whose guests are you?" He listens to your answer with his head tilted, nods as if a small sum has come out right, and only then points to six crates of beeswax candles stacked on the quay. A steward on the Heights is waiting for them, four hundred steps up, and the Watch clears strangers from the Wharves at dusk.

The [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] once ruled most of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] and now holds four provinces on a warm peninsula above the [[place-vylarianse|Vylarian Sea]]: [[place-alyssa|Alyssa]] around the capital, [[place-hylen|Hylen]] that feeds it, [[place-vald|Vald]] that sails for it, and [[place-moktur|Moktur]] that guards it from the northern forests. Some 21 million people live under the Augustar, the emperor, and nearly all of them live under laws written in Vylari, judged in courts that allow appeal, and dated by a calendar that begins with the empire itself. The empire is smaller than its maps and poorer than its monuments, and it governs as though neither were true.

**The one thing to understand before anything else: in Vylaria, the forms are the government.** A magistrate whose writ nobody fears can still convene a court that everybody attends, because attending is what is done, and a surprising amount of the empire runs on exactly that. A Vylarian settles precedence early in any encounter and is easy once it is settled. Say who you are, whom you serve, and whose guest you are before anyone has to ask, and every door in the city opens a little wider.

## A Republic, an Empire, and a Long Afternoon

[[lore-vylarirpbl|The Vylarian Republic]] was founded around 650 BF by patrician families who overthrew the last king. It grew into the master of the Vylarian Sea, and in [[lore-helionscnq|the conquest of Heliónis]], from 335 to 312 BF, it broke the most powerful mages of the west with the **Dragon Riders**, warriors bonded to dragons whose presence makes spells misfire. Out of that war came the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]], chartered under the [[lore-lexarcana|Lex Arcana]] in 73 BF. In Year 1 a senator backed by the legions and the Ordo seized supreme power ([[lore-thefounding|The Founding]]), and the [[lore-commoncal|Vylarian Calendar]] of western Ankaris counts from that year. It is now 720 AF.

[[lore-empirdclne|The decline]] began about two centuries ago. [[place-provenzrgn|Provènzia]] and [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]] went their own ways, raiders pressed down from [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]], and twelve years ago [[affiliation-cnfdrtnhrdnstts|Harad]], the richest eastern province, won its independence when the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Aû'Khelâthu]] quietly paid for the rebellion and a Vylarian squadron was destroyed at [[place-tamzirshoals|Tamzîr Shoals]]. The defeat festers in the Senate and the officer corps. The Senate still lists the lost provinces among its territories.

## Who Holds Power

The Augustar rules in name, and four parties contest the power in practice. The Augustar and his court hold tradition and the loyalty of the armies. The [[affiliation-curiavylar|Senate]] holds the great families, debating in a bronze-doored chamber whose procedure is immaculate and whose authority erodes a little every decade. The military holds the swords, above all the [[affiliation-pratrgstrm|Praetar Augustarum]], the imperial guard in the [[place-urbsaquiln|Inner City]], who are best placed to guard an Augustar and best placed to unmake one. The Ordo Arcanis holds magic, and is woven deeper into the state every reign. Weak Augustars become puppets of the Senate or the Ordo; strong ones face conspiracies from both.

The old offices are still filled: a consul presides over the year, a praetor declares the law, a quaestor answers for the treasury's revenue, a censor keeps the citizen roll, and the Augustar is himself Pontifex Maximus, chief priest of the state cult. In the capital, the inspectors of the [[affiliation-aediles|Aediles]] can shutter a shop, fine its keeper, or order a building pulled down, and bribing them is one of the city's oldest industries. Below the Senate the daily state runs through the [[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|Imperial Scribal Guild]], the tax courts of the [[affiliation-curiafscls|Curia Fiscalis]], and the treasury of the [[affiliation-aerarimmpr|Aerarium Imperii]]. The capital beyond the Inner Wall answers to the five Curators of the [[affiliation-curiaurbis|Curia Urbis]], and the guilds answer to the [[affiliation-magnumclgm|Magnum Collegium]].

## Citizen and Resident

The deepest line in Vylarian society is not between rich and poor but between citizen and resident. Most people under the empire are residents, born to it and taxed by it with no share in it. A citizen holds the dole, a guaranteed ration of grain, and may live inside the capital's _pomerium_ by right, appeal his case up to the imperial tribunals, and expect a trial and a clean death, never the lash.

Citizenship is earned, not inherited, and for the common-born there is one road to it. A boy takes the legion's oath no earlier than his fifteenth birthday and serves twenty years, and if he lives he walks out a citizen at thirty-five. His sons are not citizens unless they walk the same road; only the nobility pass it heir to heir, and grants are rare. So the citizen body of Magnápolis is an old soldiers' city, and the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|City Watch]] that polices it is drawn from veteran-citizens.

> On the Heights the last light goes red behind the hills. The steward of the house counts the candle crates twice, then writes your names onto a waiver in a small, square hand, presses his master's seal into the wax beside them, and dates it. He does not hand it to you. He folds it into his own sleeve, and tells you to be at the kitchen door at first light.

A resident who wants to sleep inside the capital needs a waiver from the courts, almost always as the servant of someone who matters, and it ends when the service ends. That is why most of the city's working people live beyond the **Clearance**, the swept strip a hundred feet wide that rings Magnápolis where a wall would stand. The dole is also the throne's great fear. As the treasury thins, arrears and short measure creep into the ration, and a missed dole is the spark every prudent Augustar dreads. [[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vylarian Citizenship]] sets out the whole bargain.

## The Legions

Every Vylarian schoolchild learns the four words that open the military manual: _Sub Aquila stant fines_, "beneath the eagle stand the borders." Each legion carries an Aquila, a gilt eagle on a pole, and a legion that loses it is disbanded.

What a newcomer has to grasp about the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|legions]] is that each one holds two armies in one mess hall. The noble track buys its commissions, from _Cadetar_ at fifteen to _Legar_ in command, at prices set by the family's estate: Aurelian, Argentean, Aerean, or Adlect. The commoner track earns every rank from _Tironar_ to _Primar_, and no coin changes hands at any promotion. The two meet at a single rank, the _Praefar Castorum_, a commoner raised to officer for distinguished service perhaps twice in a decade. An eighteen-year-old _Subtribunar_ outranks a fifty-year-old _Primar_ on paper, and every unwritten rule of the army says he takes the _Primar_'s counsel. A _Subtribunar_ who ignores it and gets his men cut up is quietly sent home.

The reform after the Haradian war, an examination set by the _Dux Bellorum_ and a panel of generals, tightened only the commoner's road into the officer ranks, and the senior centurions count it an insult. Mercenaries, often Tarvénan or Nordmen, fill gaps in the line, and the loyalty of the auxiliaries has been a live question since Haradian units defected.

:::secret
**For the GM:** The gap between the tracks is the best tension in the empire: a noble who needs a centurion's counsel and cannot be seen to take it, or a _Primar_ failed by an examination rigged against him. The [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|military note]] carries six hooks built on it, among them a lost Aquila that may have been found.
:::

## The Ordo Arcanis

Magic in Vylaria belongs to the state. Under the Lex Arcana only members of the Ordo may practice, research, or teach arcane lore, and every child who shows talent must be reported and enrolled. A practitioner outside it is a rogue mage, hunted by the **Quaesitorium**, the Ordo's enforcement arm, whose Quaesitors prefer recruitment, then confiscation, then restriction, then prison, and kill only at the end of that list. Ordinary Vylarians regard the Ordo as one more arm of government; provincials find that unnerving.

The ranks run from _Initiatus_, a newly inducted child, through _Discipulus_ and _Adeptus_ to _Magister_, the governing rank. Above them the _Praelati_ sit on the **Concilium Magnum**, and the _Archmagister_ presides for life. Two rules bind every member: draw no attention to the Ordo, and never teach magic to an outsider, not even a single trick to a sister. Its chapter houses wear the seal of the [[affiliation-panepistmm|Panepistemium]] and teach law and mathematics to the children of the gentry. Most Vylarians never see a spell cast. The capital is the exception: glow-lamps light the great avenues, and in the [[place-palatimgnm|Grand Palace]] the clockwork legionaries and gilded provinces of the [[place-magngndorery|Grand Orrery]] parade past the throne at the great hours for every ambassador to see.

Two silences sit at the root of the Ordo, and scholars who pursue either are discouraged. In 75 BF an unchanneled release killed most of the Republic's arcanists, gathered with their books in the Alyssan country to be registered ([[lore-recensio|the Recensio of Alyssa]]). The land is fenced at the Ordo's charge to this day. Around 45 BF the Dragon Riders were disbanded and the **Eyrie of Mons Aquila**, under the palace hill, was sealed. The reason given is that the dragons made the Ordo's work impossible. The Helionite academies hold that there were never any dragons, and the [[place-alndntrblnds|Áelendan]] peoples hold that the mounts were drakes.

:::secret
**For the GM:** The dragons were real. In 330 BF the Republic took the workshop of a mage-warlord of [[affiliation-theradon|Therádon]] and found the souls of dead dragons bound in lead-sealed jars ([[lore-dragondead|The Dragon Dead at Therádon]]). Their return was the price of the war, on a lease renewed yearly and never written down ([[lore-thebargain|The Bargain]]); around 45 BF the return was refused ([[lore-ridersdisb|The Lease Refused]]). Something of the dragons' dead is still kept, and the debt is unpaid. When the **Praefectura Arcana** counted its holdings again after the release, the count came up short, and the Quaesitorium's first commission, to find out what happened at Alyssa and who did it, has never been closed.
:::

## The Gods and the Cult

The [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aurèldían Pantheon]] is the state faith, and its Twelve give Magnápolis the number of its great public squares. [[lore-janusdty|Árdavon]], keeper of the gates, lord of order and justice, is patron of the empire in name. On top of the pantheon sits the **Imperial Cult**, the veneration of dead Augustars as divine or half-divine. Its temples stand in every city, and taking part is a religious act and a declaration of loyalty in one gesture, and it binds subjects of every faith to one ritual of allegiance.

Beneath that order, Vylarian religion is busy and fickle. The capital is a bazaar of cults: [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] fire-shrines draw fashionable devotees, [[affiliation-asguardian|Asguardian]] warrior-cults draw officers, and Helionite mysteries draw curious aristocrats. The dead of the capital lie outside the _pomerium_ in the [[place-necropolismagnapolis|Necropolis]], tended jointly by the clergy of [[affiliation-thanatos|Sélmoros]], the Silent Judge, and the Asguardian priests of [[affiliation-nahild|Náhild]], whose deeper vaults are spoken of in lowered voices. The schismatic [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]], which holds that fire purifies and the strong should rule, works through hidden shrines.

## Magnápolis

[[place-magnapolis|Magnápolis]], the Great City, holds something under a million people on the [[place-sixhillsvyl|Six Hills]] above the [[place-vylarisrvr|River Vylaris]]. The walled Inner City on [[place-monsaquila|Mons Aquila]], closed to the common crowd, holds the palace, the Senate, and the bureaucracy. Below it the **Via Triumphalis** runs dead straight from the great gate, wide enough for a cohort to march twenty abreast, lined with columns set with the bronze beaks of captured warships. The **Twelve Circles**, each three hundred yards of level pavement where building is forbidden, hold markets, games, festivals, and the occasional riot. Four streets in five are stairs, so draft animals stay outside the Clearance and the city moves on foot, on porters' backs, and in pedicabs drawn by trotting runners.

Go to the [[place-circusmaximusmagnapls|Circus Maximus]] for the chariot factions, whose rivalries spill from the track into brawls in the street. Go to the [[place-magntheatredstr|Theatre District]] for masked players, late suppers, and intrigue, and to the **Grand Market Circle** for the banking houses of the [[affiliation-clgmrgntrrm|Collegium Argentariorum]]. The **Warrens** pack veteran-citizens into tenement _insulae_ of brick and timber above the Wharves. Beyond the Clearance the outskirts hold the smithies, tanneries, and gate-yards the city will not house, and most of the people who keep it running.

The gilt is wearing thin. The processional routes are immaculate; a street off them, the paving is patched in mismatched stone, fountains stand dry, and empty plinths mark where bronze was melted in a lean year. In confident years the Clearance is swept gravel; in lean ones shanties creep onto it, and Magnápolitans read the empire's fortunes by how far they have crept.

## The Four Provinces

Choose [[affiliation-provinclys|Alyssa]] for intrigue. Almost nothing there is more than a day's ride from a temple, a magistrate, and a tax-collector, and its towns and senatorial villas form an unbroken quilt around the capital on the best-kept roads in the empire. Its provincial capital is [[place-alyssar|Alyssar]], and its forges make **Vylarian Steel**, the alloy whose forging is a guarded secret and whose weapons command extraordinary prices across Ankaris.

Choose [[affiliation-provnchyln|Hylen]] for land and the people who own it. Its great families hold estates as large as valleys and send the capital its bread, wine, and oil through the market at [[place-aravantia|Aravantia]]; crossing a boundary stone there means passing from one lord's small world into the next. Choose [[affiliation-provincvld|Vald]] for the sea. [[place-mercavia|Mercavia]] is the busiest commercial harbor in the empire, with foreign merchant quarters, and [[place-castamar|Castamar]] builds the warships and holds the fleet command. Choose [[affiliation-provncmktr|Moktur]] for the frontier. Its military governors at [[place-tyrellan|Tyrellan]] rule like semi-independent warlords, even its market towns have walls, and some of its [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokh]] neighbors live inside the border while their cousins raid across it.

West of the provinces the **Cervaron Spine** rises, which the Senate lists as imperial territory and the [[place-alndntrblnds|Áelendan]] hold as the sacred dwelling of the [[affiliation-firstgods|First Gods]]. Merchants crossing its passes pay the clans an informal passage-gift, and nobody calls it a toll. To the north lies the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartûm]], Vylari-speaking and independent, whose people practice the Aurèldían faith with an intensity that makes Vylarian priests uncomfortable.

## Money and the Law

The [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vylarian currency]] is the standard coinage of western Ankaris. The Aurion is a thick gold coin worth 160 silver Argenti, rare enough that pulling one out at a market stall is the financial equivalent of drawing a sword. The Argentus, called the Argo, is what the city runs on: one buys four loaves of common bread. The Octus, called the Bit, is a wedge of silver cut from an Argo along the eight lines struck into its back, and it buys a loaf, a pint, or a porter's tip. Large sums move in script, paper notes issued by the moneylenders against the treasury.

The law is the empire's one unambiguous gift to the world: codified, written, and appealable. A Vylarian's first instinct in a dispute is to reach for a legal remedy where any other people on the continent would reach for a knife or a kinsman. He is often disappointed, and he tries anyway.

Several things get a newcomer into trouble. Remarking on the decay is a real discourtesy, true or not; Vylarians are far more caustic about it among themselves than a foreigner would dare to be. Crossing the Clearance anywhere but at a gate-road gets you taken for a thief. A foreigner who will not be placed makes his hosts uneasy. Patronage runs private life as thoroughly as law runs public life: a client attends his patron in the morning and carries his interests, and the obligation binds in practice if not in court.

## Ways In

A party can arrive by sea at Mercavia, by grain ship up the Vylaris, over the Cervaron passes, or down the old paved roads from provinces the empire no longer holds. A Vylarian character might be a veteran newly discharged a citizen, a _Subtribunar_ whose family bought a commission he never wanted, an _Adeptus_ of the Ordo sent to a provincial chapter, a scribe whose fine hand has drawn the wrong patron, or a resident whose waiver ends with a master's death. At the first gate, court, or dinner, three questions place a character in the empire: **Are you a citizen? Whom do you serve? Whose guest are you?**

Campaigns start well from the forms. A patron needs a client to stand beside him in court. A household's waiver is about to lapse, and its servants must find a new master or leave the city. A child in a market town has shown talent nobody reported. A _Primar_ wants proof that his examination was rigged. A grain convoy is late and the Circles are turning ugly.

:::secret
**For the GM:** A forged waiver, a commission bought with worthless script, a dole short by a measure, or an unregistered mage in a senator's household each tests whether the forms still hold, and each draws the Watch, the Quaesitorium, or the Senate's lobbies into the answer.
:::
