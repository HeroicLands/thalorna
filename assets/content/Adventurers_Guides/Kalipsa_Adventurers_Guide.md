Kalipsa Adventurer's Guide opens on the water:

> You come up on deck in the gray before dawn, after a night of open sea, and the first thing you see is the light: a steady yellow star low on the water ahead, where there should be no star. By the time the sky behind you is pale the island is under it, a dark hump with cliffs white with birds, and the light is a square tower on a headland. The master has hove to. Spray comes over the rail cold enough to make your teeth ache, the hull smells of tar and wet grain, and off the bow a line of black rocks shows and vanishes in the swell. Nobody on deck is talking. They are watching a small boat come out from under the headland, four oars and a man standing in the stern.
>
> He comes up the side as if it were a stair, a broad, gray-bearded man in an oiled coat with a brass badge on a cord at his throat, and he looks at the rocks before he looks at anyone. "_Fridma tiv thuv_," he says to the master, and then to you, because you are the stranger: peace to you. "[[being-glurvmilhvr|Glurvmil Hvurnaruk]], pilot of Kalipsa, of the lower quay." He puts out a hand for the master's papers, reads the name of the house that sent the hull, and nods at the rocks without turning his head. "Those are [the Hounds]{.name data-type="place/feature"}. I take you in, or you wait for the fog to lift and take yourselves. The fee is the same."

[[affiliation-calypsa|Kalipsa]] is a free port on a steep island in the cold sea, seventy-five miles off the northern coast of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] and a day and a night's sail from the mouth of [[place-liserebay|Lísere Bay]]. Some forty-five thousand people live on the [[place-calypsaisle|Isle of Kalipsa]], most of them in the city on its harbor, and the island feeds none of them. Kalipsa eats what it buys, pays tribute to nobody, owes fealty to nobody, and keeps a navy out of all proportion to its size to make sure it stays that way. Its seafaring houses govern it through the [[affiliation-captnscncl|Captains' Council]], and they measure everyone, themselves first, by the tonnage of their hulls.

**The one thing to understand first: in Kalipsa, a guest is not robbed.** The moment you have eaten at a Kalipsan table or been given a berth in the harbor you are under the city's protection, and a wrong done to you is judged twice as heavily as the same wrong done to a citizen. The rule runs the other way too. Bargaining here is the hardest on the northern coasts, and a bargain lost is no disgrace. Breaking the trust of a house that took you in is, and no hull in the harbor carries a stranger who has done it.

## What You Meet First

The pilot brings you in between the headland and the head of [[place-oldmole|the Old Mole]], and the harbor opens behind the mole as still as a pond. Before your hull touches the quay the Harbormaster's customs officers are aboard with a slate, taking the manifest and setting the dues. They are quick, exact and impossible to flatter. A cargo landed pays a modest tariff, a cargo passed on to another hull pays more, and a cargo left in a warehouse longer than a season pays so much that nobody leaves one.

> The customs officer is a young woman with ink on her fingers and a voice that carries the length of the deck. She asks what you brought, writes it down, asks whether you mean to sell it here or carry it on, and writes that down too. When you ask how long you may stay, she looks up for the first time. "As long as you pay your berth," she says. "Nobody here asks why."

The old city at the top of the slope speaks [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], and so do the docks and the villages. The markets of the lower quay speak [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]], the harbor pidgin of the inner sea, and anyone from Provénzia, Élavendre or the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] is understood without much trouble. The money is [[lore-vylrncrncy|Vilérian]]: the Solden, the Ardin and the Ogdel, and the paper of the [[affiliation-danvar|Danvar]] moneylenders, which is as good on the quay as coin.

The kitchens serve smoked fish with southern spice and olive oil. The wine comes from the terraces above the city and is, by its drinkers' own admission, indifferent. Weddings mix Nordmen drinking-songs with Aureldian hymns, and a wedding in the old city in winter goes on for three days.

## Where to Go

**[[place-calypsacity|The city]]** climbs from the quays to the walled old city in tiers of gray stone. The quay is where the business is: the customs house with the Council chamber above it, the syndicates' counting-rooms, the temple of [[affiliation-pozeras|the Faith of Pozeras]], the Wayfarer's, where every master gives before a voyage, and [[place-sealawhall|the Sea-Law Hall]], whose doors stand open on every hearing day for anyone who wants to listen. The lower quay is the coasters' harbor and the market, and the yards of [[affiliation-hsethraskumak|Thraskumak]] lie under the headland at the north end, where the navy's hulls are built.

**[[place-oldmole|The Old Mole]]** closes the harbor, a breakwater of great squared blocks laid without mortar, older than any date the city will admit to. Several of its stones are carved with characters nobody living can read. The city's tradition says refugees from a forgotten Helionite colony built it, and that is the only account there is. A storm this winter turned one of the old blocks and uncovered a carved face nobody had seen, and the masons and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s factor are arguing over it.

**[[place-lantrnklps|The Lantern of Kalipsa]]**, on the northern headland, has been relit every night for longer than anyone remembers. Its keeper's office is hereditary, and its keeper's word on what the light showed on a given night is taken at the Sea-Law Hall without a second witness.

**[[place-calypsashore|The South Shore]]**, over the ridge, is the island that is not the city: fishing villages in the southern coves, the dye-vats of [Vrellnes]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} that stain the rocks red, and the upland steadings where goats graze on the turf below the bare ridge. The villages speak an older Nordmal than the city, choose their own elders, and have no voice on the Council. After this summer's drought they are not pleased with the city.

**[[place-kalipsarun|The Kalipsa Run]]** is how most people arrive and how most leave: two days across from [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]] on the southern shore of Lísere Bay, carrying grain and fish west and everything else east, with a good deal of it never entered at the Élavendren crown's tally-house.

## The Council and the Houses

> On the morning of the census the whole city walks the quays. Every hull is dressed with flags at its berth, and the Customs Officer's tally-men go from berth to berth with their slates while the crews stand at the rails. A Councilor in a fur-trimmed coat stops beside you to watch a tally-man count a new coaster's tonnage, and says nothing at all until the man has written the figure down. Then he walks on without a word, and the master of the coaster spits over the side.

The Captains' Council is the heads of the seafaring houses, about a dozen great ones and twice as many lesser, and every house's voices are its tonnage at the spring census. A house that loses three ships in a season can lose its seat within the year, and a house that opens a new trade can win one. The Council elects a Harbormaster from its own members for a single three-year term, and no Harbormaster may serve the next. The present Harbormaster, [[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]], is a shipwright, and her term ends at the spring sitting of 720.

That election is what everyone on the quay is talking about. [[affiliation-hsetvarnidrok|Tvarnidrok]], the oldest house, leads the old houses and wants the office. [[affiliation-hsenorodari|The Norodari]], who rose in a decade on the fur trade through Sarmelis, lead the newer houses and want to stop them. Three Norodari hulls went down in the autumn fog, and whether [[affiliation-bondsyndcts|the Bond Syndicates]] must pay for them is before the Sea-Law Hall. The answer decides how many hulls the Norodari bring to the census, and so who wins.

The [[lore-sealaw|Sea-Law]] is the city's law of ships and cargoes, bonds and losses, and nobody has ever written it down. A small caste of professional arbitrators keeps it by recitation and recites it at every hearing. Its verdicts bind nobody outside the city, and they are honored across half the northern shipping world anyway, because a house that defies one finds that no syndicate in Kalipsa will write its bonds again.

## What Gets a Newcomer into Trouble

- **Breaking a guest's trust.** If a house has fed you or given you a berth, a wrong you do it is judged as heavily as a wrong done to you. Foreigners are refused the harbor for life for it.
- **Entering without a pilot.** A master who brings a hull past the Hounds without one pays the Council's fine if he lives, and the syndicates will not pay his bond if he does not.
- **Leaving cargo in a warehouse.** Past a season the tariff is ruinous on purpose. Sell it, ship it or give it away.
- **Invoking the Arcane Order.** The city's position is that the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s authority stops at the waterline. Discreet magic is nobody's business; a mage who makes it the city's business is asked to leave.
- **Assuming the villages are the city's.** The South Shore families hold their coves and their springs by their own custom, and they say so.

## Ways In

- **A count nobody bought.** The Harbormaster needs strangers to walk the quays beside the tally-men at the spring census and count every hull, because the competing houses dispute the count. [[scenario-kalipsahulls|The Third Night and the Spring Count]] joins the census to the pending losses.
- **The lost hulls.** The Norodari want proof that the pilots' boat was off its station on the eastern approaches the nights their hulls went down, and a witness from Sarmelis who saw the masters leave. Follow the evidence in [[scenario-kalipsahulls|The Third Night and the Spring Count]].
- **The locked spring.** The city's water-carters want an escort to the South Shore, where the elder of Vrellnes has barred the village spring-house, and the villages want witnesses to their claim that the springs belong to them. [[scenario-vrellnesspring|The Key Above the Dye Vats]] begins at the barred spring-house.
- **The carved stone.** The Order's factor will pay well for copies of the face the storm uncovered on the Old Mole, taken at low water before the masons wedge the stone, and the watch does not let anyone onto the outer ledge at night. [[scenario-oldmoleface|Before the Stone Is Covered]] gives the party a tide in which to make its copy.
- **The Run.** A Kalipsan factor at the outer quay of Sarmelis needs a cargo of furs carried across before the crown's cutters come down the bay, and his master is in the sheriff's hall. [[place-kalipsarun|The Kalipsa Run]] describes the crossing and its hazards.
