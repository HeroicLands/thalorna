> The mule track has climbed since before dawn, and now the sun clears the eastern ridge and lays a hard white light across the mountain's western face. The air is thin and cold enough to sting your teeth; frost still furs the thorn scrub in the shadows. Ahead there is only cliff—gray limestone streaked with rust and black where water runs, a few dark hollows, ledges crusted with lichen. Then a hollow puts out a thread of smoke that spreads flat along the rock and vanishes, and you see that the ledge under it is a sill, and the hollow a window, and the stain a flue. Your eyes go up the face and find another, and another, dozens of openings shaped to the rock's own cracks, until the whole cliff becomes a city looking down at you.
>
> At its foot, in a court of trodden earth below a door you had taken for a shadow, farmers from the terraces below unload carts of barley sacks, wheels of hard cheese and bundled charcoal while their oxen steam and stamp. Nobody shouts. The tally is kept by a short, broad figure with a slate and chalk dust in her beard, who marks each sack and does not look up. From somewhere inside the mountain comes a sound like slow rain: hammers, many of them, each struck a long breath apart. Then the door is full. The figure in it stands no higher than your chest and nearly as broad as the frame, in a leather apron scorched at the hem, an iron-gray beard braided into three cords and tucked through her belt. Her hands are square, scarred across the knuckles and very still. Above her head the lintel carries a line of short angled letters that follows a seam in the stone instead of a straight edge. "_Gaz_ **Gorzo**, _gaz gl-guráz tanvam_," she says—"I am Gorzo; I guard the gate as my trade"—and lays her palm on the carved name above her. She looks past you at the sealed tube of Tānvüri imperial orders on your employer's hip, then at the cliff behind her, then back at you, and waits for you to say what you want of the stone.

The [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazári]] are the second of the elder peoples, younger than the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] and ancient beyond any human reckoning. They came to Thalorna about 5300 BF as seven clans fleeing another realm, and they live in holds: cities found in the caves behind mountain cliffs, on every continent, far from human settlement. There are very few of them, and most humans never see one. A party does not usually belong to this people. It meets them at a gate: a door in a cliff, a weighing yard at the end of a mountain road, a Khazári engineer on an imperial work. What happens next depends on whether the party has understood the one thing every Khazár judges by.

**The one thing to understand first: the Khazári ask what the stone wants, and judge everyone by how little they force.** Good work follows the grain of the rock and opens what the mountain already almost holds; evil is forcing, a cut made where none was needed. A master is admired for how little she had to cut. The same measure falls on a stranger. A visitor who demands, hurries or pushes past a refusal has shown in one breath what kind of person he is, and he is not asked in. Being brought inside a hold is _tamk_, an act of trust, and it is rarely extended.

## Counting in Sixteens

> While Gorzo reads your employer's orders, the tally-keeper in the gate court, finishes the barley. She has set the sacks in rows of sixteen against the wall, and she walks the rows touching each one, then calls up to the door: "_Zik girm bek bozd._" Gorzo glances at the rows, nods once, and the farmer at the cart—who has been counting on his fingers in tens—shrugs and starts unhitching his oxen.

The Khazári count in sixteens. What the tally-keeper called is "two sixteen and four": two rows of sixteen and four sacks over, which a human clerk writes as thirty-six. Every number up to fifteen has its own word. Sixteen is girm, said after the count of sixteens and joined to what is left over by _bek_, "and". Sixteen sixteens, 256, is a vazath, a great count.

Sixteen and 256 are the Khazári round numbers, as ten and a hundred are for most human peoples, and 64, four sixteens, is a round figure too. Sixteen halves cleanly to eight, four, two and one, so ore, stores and gate-shares divide among houses with no fraction left over, and the craft-records reckon a measure of ore or a length of tunnel in _fadhaz_, sixteenths. A human clerk who reckons a hold's delivery in tens has his figures accepted, converted and quietly corrected.

These pages give Khazári counts as ordinary numbers. The sixteens appear only where the reckoning itself is the point.

## Where You Meet Them

Human scholars place holds in the mountains of every major continent: above [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]], in [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]], along [[affiliation-tanvurempr|Tānvür]]'s western border and in [[place-xerathia|Xerathia]]. The Khazári neither confirm nor deny any of it. Two places give a party a real door.

**In Tānvür**, the one human realm with a lasting relationship with the Khazári, the empire classifies them by what they do: most as artisans, engineers and scholars, a few as military officers and a very few as nobles. Their strongholds govern themselves, and the empire relies on their weapons, armor and fortifications. A party in imperial service can carry a commission to a stronghold's gate, serve beside a Khazári officer on the mountain border, or guard an engineer whose methods infuriate a human general. The [[lore-elderraces|Elder Races]] note sets out the terms.

**On Aelwyth**, [[place-vorgald|Vorgald]] stands in the eastern heights of the [[place-ironfells|Ironfells]], the westernmost hold the Khazári ever founded. Below it live the [[lore-vardain|Vardain]] of the [[place-vardainvalleys|Vardain Valleys]], the one human people that still farms for a hold. A caravan road climbs nearly six thousand feet to [[place-vargate|Vargate]], shut by snow a third of the year, whose market sells instruments, fine tools, wire and worked metal that the Vardain call their own work. Above the market, at the **Weighing Yard**, Vardain factors exchange the valleys' grain, cheese and fuel for what the hold makes. No outsider enters the yard, and no Khazár is seen below it.

> At Vargate a Vardain factor in a sheepskin coat counts your coin onto a cloth, folds the cloth over it and puts your sealed letter into her own satchel. She turns and walks up the road toward the yard without a word, and the boy at the gate of the yard steps aside to let her through and back across to stop you. The snow on the road above her is untrodden except by her own boots and the ruts of handcarts.

## How a Hold Lives

A hold is found before it is made. The Khazári take the caves a mountain already holds and adapt them, so great limestone caverns become halls, shrines, cisterns and storehouses, and digging is the exception. A hold begins with its fakar, its cave, found by a _fakarir_, a finder who reads a mountain from outside. Tunnels wind along the _varn_, the true line the rock runs, because a straight cut is proof that the stone was forced.

Ordinary life happens near the light, in shallow homes set into the face with openings shaped to the rock's cracks. Smoke leaves by natural fissures and disperses on the face, water comes from seeps and a hutavar, a cistern the rock already held, and passages inside the mountain link everything, so no road scars the face.

Depth carries weight. The deeper a room, the holier or more serious its purpose: the _hukhard_, the inmost hall; the _huzafal_, the shrines; the tombs and mines; and the famgadurákh, the refuge where the whole hold can be sealed in with stores, light and air. People go down for funerals, festivals, oaths and worship, and in war, and a Khazár who asks you to come down is telling you something serious is about to happen.

A hold grows nothing of its own. It eats what its farmers send up to the gate, and when the harvest fails, the elders open the refuge caches and share them out by need.

## Listening, the Cut and the Record

Before any cut, a worker listens. She taps the stone and hears how it rings, finds the seam and follows it with her hand—_luváz_, to follow a seam—and only then decides the day's work. Much of a working day is spent deciding not to cut. _Pazal_, patience, is a word of the workshop before it is a word of temperament, and _khasp_, restraint, is the virtue the workshops prize above skill. The finest work, a _khispath_, barely looks like work at all: a stair the rock nearly held, finished; a hall standing on the pillars the rock left. A narvavutám, a long tunnel cut along the grain that needs no prop, is a miner's masterpiece. A _sakap_, a prop, is a mark of shame on the working and on the name entered beside it.

A life climbs the same ladder. Children learn the hand form of [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]] on slate and learn to tell sound stone from rotten by ear. A _nabaf_, an apprentice, spends years in samgh, listening, sounding stone and watching masters decide where not to cut, and an apprentice who asks too early for a hammer is told to listen another year. Workers strike under a master's eye. Masters strike as a master does, _thummár_, and are trusted with the first cut of a passage. The old, whose hands fail before their ears do, teach listening and keep the records.

Everything is written down. The craft-records of every hold name each worker against each cut, and a finished piece carries its warrant, signed, dated and specified on the object itself in _dalkapuráz_, carved Pirzath, whose angled strokes cross the grain and whose lines follow the seams. Everyday writing is _milgath_, the flowing hand form. A gharp, a clumsy cut, goes into the record against its maker's name and stays in the stone after everyone who saw it made is dead.

Penalties rise in a plain order: a gharp entered against your name; mending your own forced cut while a master of another house watches; your tools set aside and a return to the apprentice's rung; and, for a broken oath sworn below, the loss of the right to go down at all. Past all of that lies exile, with no house, no hold and no place in the refuge. Some exiles live by selling to humans what a hold would not have sold.

## Elders, Houses and the Record

There is no lord inside the rock. A hold is governed by the elders of its houses sitting together, and the same elders lead the rites below. Every Khazár belongs to one of the seven clans—**Summágh**, **Dullák**, **Rummák**, **Gullád**, **Vurrán**, **Gurráz** and **Lummáv**, each named for its master craft—and to a house within it. A house name is a sentence about work: Gorzo's house, **Tanvaguráz**, is "the guarding of the gate".

Quarrels are tried in the deep hall, before the elders of a house, of the hold, or of several holds, as the quarrel requires. The archive's keepers read the bearing craft-records aloud, and where a record and an elder's memory disagree, the record stands.

## Puthakun and the Deep

The Khazári are not much given to worship. The one they venerate, and do not worship, is [[lore-khazararrv|Puthakun]], the servant of [[lore-goddreams|Luváth]] who led the seven clans out of **Humadhan**, "the middle place", held the passage against pursuit until the last clan was through, and sealed it behind them. Every Khazár alive descends from someone he brought out. His name is kept, his deed is remembered below, and good work is the thanks. Luváth they recognize as his master, the light that endures in deep places, and they ask little of the god.

Craft is the prayer, and listening before a cut is the commonest act of devotion. There is no priesthood apart from the masters and elders. The whole hold goes down to its _famgafudhán_, its deepest sanctuary, at the turning of the year to hear the year's craft-records read by lamplight, on the day of its finding, for the reading of the account of Khazártúrn's fall, and on oath-days, when an undertaking is sworn below and cut where it was spoken. Joy lives near the light, in the courts on the face, with singing that carries along the passages.

The dead go down into the mountain's keeping, and their work stays behind them. A dead Khazár answers for a clumsy passage as long as the passage is walked, and a descendant who mends a forebear's flawed cut sets the forebear's record right. At a funeral the hold keeps khullán, silence over the dead, broken only by the words also said over finished work: _Ramk kr-na-sumád, ramk na-thumár-aktor_, "the course has been raised; the course will be struck."

## Khazártúrn

The Khazári built one thing that all their other work is measured against, and lost it. [[lore-khazarturn|Khazártúrn]] was raised in 5123 BF from seven spurs of a mountain valley's cliff, finished as seven towers, one to each clan, the only work the whole people made together. It held the craft-records of an entire people and stood for close to twenty-seven centuries. In 2427 BF a Sinalë outlaw wielding the [[miscgear-secondvoice|Second Voice]], a relic that takes [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] from their spawners and commands them as an army, destroyed it and killed all thirty thousand of its people. The Khazári laid their dead in their own chambers, cleaned the city and sealed it. It is intact, in good order and full of its people, and its location is the one secret the Khazári keep as a secret.

The account of the fall is read aloud below in every hold at its appointed intervals. It explains the two things about the Khazári that a party will meet soonest. They have not spoken to the Sinalë since. And they kill Grukar on sight, everywhere, without deliberation, knowing that the Grukar were compelled and chose none of it: their doctrine is prevention, and Grukar are ammunition.

## At the Gate: Warnings

- **Do not ask about the inner passages.** A Khazár asked how a hold runs inside simply stops talking.
- **Do not touch the memorial objects**—the preserved tools and weapons of ancestors. No outsider may handle them, in Tānvür or anywhere else.
- **Do not bring a Grukar near a hold**, as a captive, a guide or a curiosity. The Khazári will kill it in front of you, and your standing dies with it.
- **Do not boast, hurry or display.** Haste and waste earn contempt; a straight line drawn where the rock curves earns more.
- **Do not offer a borrowed word.** Khazári make every word they need from their own skeletons, and borrowing is a small shame.
- **Say what you want of the stone, and accept the answer.** A master says what the rock will give and refuses the rest; arguing past that refusal ends the conversation.

## The Farmers at the Gate

Every hold needs farmers within reach of its gate, and when the Khazári arrived there were none anywhere. Around their first holds they taught the nearest peoples water-works, terracing and granaries, and bound them as subjects to mine, cut timber and herd. What those tribes carried away became the foundation of human metallurgy, stonework and writing. The Khazári maintain that nothing was given that mattered; [[lore-longnhrtnc|The Long Inheritance]] tells the rest.

The Vardain are the last of those arrangements. When the Nordmen took the plain below Vorgald sixty years ago and enslaved the Vardain who had fed the hold for millennia, the Khazári did not intervene and have not said why. They admitted the refugees who reached the valleys, and protect and feed from them to this day. The ethic that forbids a needless cut in stone does not reach people. Thoughtful Khazári argue about that inside the holds, and **Gisvin** of Tanvaguráz, who carried his hold's stores out through his own gate to a starving tribe and was exiled for it, is remembered in [[lore-balvafunad|The Going of the Exile]].

## Voices of the Holds

The Khazári keep no founding scripture, but copies of their records and songs open doors into the holds: [[lore-clanscrossing|The Crossing of the Clans]], their deep song of beginning; [[lore-clansquarrel|The Quarrel of the Clans]], on Khazártúrn's divided council; [[lore-sealingcity|The Sealing of the City]], its lament; [[lore-vargafukar|The Finding at the Far Edge]], on Vorgald's founding; [[lore-longtunnel|The Long Tunnel]], a water-line cut at **Magharathubád**; and [[lore-vazanez|The Sayings of the Masters]], read when an apprentice is admitted to strike.

## Ways In

Khazári characters are rare and suit a campaign built around them: an engineer or officer in Tānvüri service, a worker on a hold's errand, or an exile. Most parties meet the Khazári from outside, as a Vardain factor's escort on the Vargate road, a Tānvüri commission bringing a fortification design, Grukar hunters whose quarry ran toward a cliff that turned out to be a city, or treasure-hunters holding a blade only Khazári hands could have made. At a gate, ask three questions: **What do you want of the stone? What have you forced to get here? Who will speak for you inside?**

Campaigns start well from something a hold needs and will not leave the rock to fetch. A delivery from the farmers below has not arrived. A forebear's flawed passage in a human mine bears a Khazári name, and the house that owes its mending wants a party to guide its workers there. A Grukar tribe has budded in a valley within reach of a hold. An exile offers to sell a secret.

:::secret
**For the GM:** After the fall of Khazártúrn the Sinalë recovered the Second Voice, and the two elder peoples agreed it would be held in trust on Aelwyth by both together. Six ships left Élavendre with it and vanished without wreckage or survivors, and Vorgald has waited three thousand years for the delivery. A party that finds any trace of those ships becomes the most important human beings the Khazári have dealt with since, and the most dangerous to them.
:::
