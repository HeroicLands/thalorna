> On the seventh morning out from the last caravanserai of the Eastern March, the frost is still white on the grass when the camels are made to kneel. Ninety of them wait along the track, shaggy two-humped beasts shedding their winter wool in brown clumps, loaded with silver in sealed bags and amber packed in straw, and behind them a herd of horses steams and stamps. The air smells of dung smoke, crushed wormwood and wet felt. On a rise above a spring stand a dozen black tents and a pole with a horse skull lashed to its top, its empty eyes turned west toward you. Below them a boy whistles a flock of sheep away from the water, two women strain mare's milk through a cloth, and an old man with one gray braid sits on a saddle by a tent door, knotting a cord as he counts your camels.
>
> The woman who comes down from the tents is past fifty, broad and weathered, in a red felt coat belted with silver plaques. She walks past the caravan-master, stops in front of you, the stranger, and holds out a pinch of gray salt on the flat of her knife until you take it on your tongue. "_Yoleq_, semoq," she says: salt, guest. Then she touches her own chest. "Kesuqye, dowek of the Kweduhes, of the wells from this spring to the white stones three days east." She turns to the master, and her voice goes flat. "Your list says ninety camels. My old man counted ninety-one at the ford. Whose is the last one, and what does it carry?"

The [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn]] is the largest and emptiest country in Ankaris: about 2,350 miles of desert and dry grassland from the forests of [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] to the passes of [[place-tanvuregin|Tānvür]], and 1,400 miles from the northern taiga to the glaciers of the Grazian Mountains. About three million people live in it, most of them in a narrow strip at the foot of the mountains, and elsewhere you can ride for a week and meet nobody but the owners of the next well.

One road crosses it. [[lore-celestialroad|The Celestial Road]] carries Tānvüri silk west and the silver of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] east, on camels, about 2,500 miles and more than four months each way. The [[lore-sowidesclt|Sowides]], the independent herding tribes of the steppe and the sand, own its wells a few days at a time, and each tribe sells passage over its own stretch and nothing more.

**The one thing to understand first: in the Khazryn everything has an owner, and a stranger who has bought nothing is fair game.** The spring that looks empty belongs to a tribe camped two ridges away, and so does the grass your horses crop. Passage, water, guest-right and a guide are bought tribe by tribe, about thirty-five times between the March and Tānvür, and each purchase buys the protection of one tribe across one range.

## The Lay of the Land

The Sowides name the country by ranges, the ground a tribe grazes, and each shades into the next over a hundred miles.

- [[place-wlqtrrgn|Welqator]], "the grass range," is the western margin below Byzaría's [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]], green for six weeks in spring and dust by midsummer. Its tribes are the most practiced with strangers: one who comes without passage is stripped and sent back west, and killed only if he fights.
- [[place-hskrrgn|Hosikor]], "the sand range," is the heart: sand seas round a dead lake basin, wells three to five days apart, and the market-camp of [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qìso]] in the middle.
- [[place-dkrqrrgn|Dikraqor]], "the stone range," is a black plateau of mesas and ravines whose poor tribes escort caravans when they are not raiding them.
- [[place-idwkrrgn|Idwakor]], "the east range," climbs through stone desert to cold high steppe before Tānvür's passes, with the longest stages and the hardest bargain on the road.
- [[place-swoasisbelt|Tellumel]] is the irrigated strip at the foot of the mountains, and [[place-gwtkrrgn|Gwatekor]], "the forest range," fills the north.

Snowmelt feeds only the Tellumel; deep wells cut by a vanished people carry the road, and hundreds of small owned springs carry everyone else. Sandstorms bury a camp in an hour in late spring and cold winds close the north in autumn, so the road runs in spring and autumn only.

## The Road to Tānvür

> At [[place-yemokwell|Yemok]], thirty days out, tamarisks stand over a stone trough long enough for forty camels. The riders who brought you squat on one side of it and the riders of the sands on the other, and between them the master lays out his string of tokens, tallies of cord and clay each cut with a tribe's sign. A rider of the sands turns each one over and asks a question about it. Then both sets of riders lay their hands on the well-head stone and swear that the caravan came through whole, and the master pays again.

Caravans leave the March at the spring equinox or at the end of summer, two fleets a year each way. A loaded string makes 20 to 25 miles a day, marching before dawn and halting in the heat, and the crossing takes 125 to 135 days: 115 for a master who finds every rider waiting, 150 for one who must wait out a sandstorm or go round a blood land. A courier with remounts crosses in about 45 days, at a price only a court pays.

No cart crosses. Oxen drink every day and the wells are days apart, sand swallows a wheel, salt crust breaks an axle, and there is no timber for a repair. So the cargo is light and dear: silk, porcelain, tea, jade, spices and medicines west, silver, gold, amber, coral, glass and horses east. Silk bought for a hundred weights of silver at the Tānvüri customs-house sells for four to six hundred at [[affiliation-yesilhan|Yeşilhan]], and the difference pays the tribes, the riders and the losses, about one string in eight. The sea is worse, with pirates off Tānvür, the hard passage round Vedyara and the Gar-Aû's cut beyond it.

Each tribe sells three things: water at its named wells and nowhere else; a sowid (rider), who travels with the caravan and answers for it with his life; and the tribe's word, a promise of restitution if its own people rob you and of a feud on your behalf if outsiders do. Passage buys vengeance, not safety, so every caravan travels heavily armed. A master's real asset is his list of agreements: which dowek he has paid, which rider is whose cousin, which ranges are at feud this year.

Halfway across, Qìso, "the price," is a ring of black stones round a deep well until the fleets arrive, and then for ten days a city of felt tents holding fifteen thousand people. The tribes whose ranges meet there swear a peace on the well-stone, carry every quarrel to their jutjen (moot) to be priced, and take the hands of a camel thief.

## Owned Water

> You come over a rise at dusk to a dozen palms, a pool the width of a cart and nobody. Your guide gets down and walks to a pole with a horse skull on top, then to the oldest palm, where a sign is cut higher than a camel can rub, then to a flat stone over a shaft with no rope. He ties a strip of cloth to the pole, sits at the edge of the sand with his hands on his knees and tells you not to unload. At first light a dozen riders come in from every side at once.

A bedaq (owned water) is a spring or seep too small to settle, and every one belongs to a tribe as surely as a tent belongs to a family. It stands empty because the tribe grazes elsewhere and comes in its season, and the owners watch it from a ridge more often than a stranger knows. The well-stone, the skull-pole and the sign in the oldest palm all say the same thing: this water has a name.

The custom that saves you is plain. Tie a cloth to the skull-pole and wait without drawing, unloading or lighting a fire; that asks for guest-right. A semoq (guest) who has eaten a tribe's salt is protected even against the host's own kin until he leaves its range. The careless camper meets the owners at dawn. A party that only slept pays for the night in a blade, a beast or a day's labor. One that drew water owes for every skin and leaves someone behind as surety, and grazed beasts are forfeit. A party that fouled the well, cut a palm or drew steel pays in blood, and its survivors become thralls. [[lore-ownedoasis|The Owned Oasis]] sets out the whole custom.

## The Tribes

Each tribe answers to nobody above its own dowek (chief), chosen by the elders from the strongest line. The dowek cannot raise a raid, move a camp or sell passage without the moot's assent. A middling tribe holds three wells and a strong one a dozen.

A jadek (raid) drives off herds and takes captives for ransom; women, children and shamans are not killed, and no well is poisoned. A raid that kills opens a badwe (blood-feud) that can run for generations, until horses are paid or a marriage is made. A raid on a caravan that has paid the tribe's toll insults the tribe, and the tribe answers it. Taking from a caravan that paid nobody is collection.

Now and then one person, man or woman, forces many tribes together by the sword. That leader is an orqwen, and the union is an orqwenoq. It has no council, no heir and no law but the orqwen's word, and it breaks within a season of the orqwen's death, when every feud it closed opens at once. The tribes count years from the last great union, and the present year is the 173rd of the Silent Market. They expect a fourth and cannot say when it will come.

The Sowides honor the [[affiliation-ashanpnthn|Āsháian]] gods loosely over older sky-cults, above all [[affiliation-bahramis|Báhrámiš]] of the storm. A treaty is sworn before lightning, and the dead are left on the laying-out ground with their names spoken to the four winds.

## The Tellumel

Where the snowmelt reaches, the country turns within a day's ride to canals, date groves and walled cities older than any tribe's memory. The Tellumi came down from the Grazian peaks and kept two thousand years of records on clay, and ninety years ago the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Ātárzád]] took four of their seven cities. The southern spur reaches them from Qìso in about twenty days, and at the first gate a clerk presses your name into a tenyul (clay tablet) as it sounded to him. The [[doc-tellumiadvguide|Tellumi Adventurer's Guide]] takes you inside.

## Ruins and Labyrinths

Two vanished peoples left the oldest ruins. [[lore-basinbldrs|The Basin Builders]] farmed a lake in the Hosikor until the water failed around 1700 BF. Their lake is [[place-ewod|Ewod]], a salt flat whose crust breaks over the old canals; their stair-well [[place-qelod|Qelod]] goes down three hundred feet to something pale in the drain; their kings lie in the painted tombs of [[place-osikor|Osikor]], and rise when a chamber is breached. Later, [[lore-towercities|the Tower Cities]] lit towers along the first road so the strings could march in the cool. [[place-wilud|Wilud]] lies under a dune sea that uncovers one street a generation, and the [[place-ruinsarkor|Ruins of Arkor]] under the Desert of Hek'ar, where knights of the Black Flame fight one another for a relic.

Other ruins belong to no story anyone tells. The caverns of [[place-ruweq|Ruweq]] in Idwakor hold sweet cisterns and a painted script no one reads, and the tribes bar them as the country's lungs. [[place-roqid|Roqid]] is a Vylarian fort whose strongroom nobody has opened. Above the Tellumel the labyrinth of [[place-gurdi|Gurdi]] climbs into the mountain from a doorway forty feet high, and other [[place-grazruins|cut passages]] open wherever a glacier draws back. The tribe that owns the nearest water charges for each of them or bars the way.

## The Four Blood Lands

A kèdoq (blood-field) is ground where a great many died at once and nobody performed their rite. Four are known. Two are wèsuk (thin blood-fields), where the hunters the tribes call [[being-yiqnotud|Yiqnotud]] cross in half-material form and kill.

- [[place-jilaq|Jilaq]], chief of the Tower Cities, was killed by the first orqwen about 190 BF. Its towers light themselves on still nights, and the road bends three days south round it.
- [[place-qimod|Qìmod]], a caravan town on the spur, died in its market week about 548 AF. No sound carries in its lanes, no fire stays lit, and whoever sleeps inside is taken by the throat.
- [[place-wemaq|Wemaq]], the best water on the road, is moderate: palms fruit twice a year, and everyone who sleeps there dreams the second orqwen's massacre and keeps hearing it.
- [[place-henhulpedin|Henhulpedin]], outside Zargandûr, is where the Tellumi war-house died in the Breaking. Hooves gallop over it every night.

The [[lore-bloodfield|Blood-fields]] note explains how such ground is made and what quiets it.

## Creatures

Creatures gather at water. [[being-gntcmlsp|Giant camel spiders]] come in to a camp's scraps at night, the [[being-qelodud|Qelodud]] hangs in well shafts, and the [[being-orkesud|Orkesud]] shimmers like water on a clay pan and takes what comes to drink. On the sand the [[being-hosikud|Hosikud]] digs funnel traps that swallow camels, a rutting [[being-gwirador|Gwirador]] scatters a string, and the [[being-budeq|Budeq]] follows a caravan for days and settles on the weakest beast. [[being-numek|Numek]] pour out of the dead light-towers at dusk, and the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|region note]] lists the rest, the salt-spirit [[being-yolequd|Yolequd]] among them.

## The Mountains and the Forest

The [[place-graznmntns|Grazian Mountains]] close the south: year-round glaciers and enormous peaks, six crossings in two thousand miles, each open a few weeks in late summer and each leading into Vedyara. Hereditary guides of the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] and the [[lore-osketclt|Ösket]] take light goods over and keep half the value. Above the snow line a party without guides and furs does not come back.

[[place-gwtkrrgn|Gwatekor]] is spruce, bog and frozen river down to an icy coast, the same cold forest as upper Velanthia and Targud. About sixty thousand people live in bands that take seals on the coast and follow deer inland, with no chief who can bind another band. Hunters tell of walls and passages cut into a ridge and a sea-cliff doorway with its sill below the tide, the [[place-nrthnhlds|old holds]] that travelers credit to the Khazári or the Ithári.

## What Gets a Newcomer into Trouble

- **Draw at no water you have not paid for.** Tie the cloth and wait; the rope you let down proves what you meant.
- **Stay on the road and at the named wells**, and do not hunt or graze off the track.
- **Never draw a blade at a hand-off well or in Qìso.** The truce binds every tribe, and a stranger who breaks it dies outside the ring.
- **Guard your tokens as you guard your water.** A caravan without them waits until the tribes decide what it is.
- **Sleep nowhere within sight of Wemaq, Jilaq or Qìmod.**
- **Never ask one tribe to speak for another.** The steppe fears a man who claims to speak for all of them.

## Ways In

A party can sign on at the March as guards or drovers, or ride with a tribe as fosterlings or hired swords. A steppe character might be a dowek's younger son who lost a horse race and his place with it, a rider whose caravan was lost on his stretch, or a girl who carries her dead father's list of agreements in her head and must make a master honor it.

At the first fire, three questions place a character: **Whose salt have you eaten? Whose tokens do you carry? Whose water did you drink last night?**

Campaigns start well from the price of things. A caravan-master dies on the march and his heir cannot read his list. A tribe closes its wells because a neighbor's rider killed its dowek. A street of Wilud has opened, and a hidden-water band has sold the news to two buyers. A Byzarian factor at Qìso offers a fortune for one lens of tower-glass from Jilaq.

:::secret
**For the GM:** A rising orqwen is the campaign the Khazryn holds in reserve. Each dowek he reaches must swear under open sky or watch the tribe's wells fouled, and one who refuses is replaced by a cousin who will swear. A master's list of agreements names every tribe on the road and which of them are at feud, and a party that sells one to the wrong rider has handed him the order in which the road can be taken.
:::
