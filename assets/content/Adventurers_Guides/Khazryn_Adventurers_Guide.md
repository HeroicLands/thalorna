> You come to [[place-mehrnagord|Mehrnāgord]] across the salt pans at first light, when the crust underfoot is still cold and the whole flat glitters pink and white to the horizon. Ahead the oasis is a dark line of date palms and a mud-brick wall, its lowest courses older and paler than the rest, as though the town had been rebuilt on top of itself many times over. Inside the gate the air is thick with the smell of boiling palm-sugar and wet clay. Salt-cutters stack gray blocks on a donkey's panniers. Two gardeners argue over a sluice in low, exact voices. At the spring a young mother in a saffron shawl holds a swaddled baby up before a small shrine, and the old woman beside her sprinkles water on its forehead from a copper bowl. Nobody hurries, and nobody raises a voice.
>
> A man in a long gray coat sits under an awning near the gate with a wooden board across his knees, pressing marks into a damp clay tablet with a reed. He finishes the line before he looks up. He studies your face, then your boots, as if entering both. "Mithran," he says, and touches his chest. "My family's records were kept at Kethramír. I keep tablets for the house of this oasis." He turns the board so you can see the tablet, sets a fresh one beside it, and lifts the reed. "Your name, and the city you come from." He listens with his head tilted, says both back to you in his own soft accent, and presses them into the clay as they sounded to him.

The [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert]] is named for the Khazryn, and the name is one of the few things nobody has managed to take from them. They built seven walled cities at the great springs of the [[place-swoasisbelt|Southwestern Oasis-Belt]] and ruled them for most of three thousand years. Ninety years ago the [[affiliation-tribestrzd|Ātárzád]], who had come to them as refugees and been given land under the desert's law of hospitality, turned on their hosts and took the four greatest. The Khazryn call that war the Vēštákán, the Breaking. Three small oases still answer to Khazryn princes; four princely houses hold court in exile in [[affiliation-sultntmrdd|Amradad]]; and most Khazryn live as craftsmen, clerks, physicians and water-engineers inside cities their grandparents governed.

**The one thing to understand first: a Khazryn keeps the record.** The exiles do not seek revenge. They seek return, and they keep the case for it ready: genealogies, deeds, temple registers, the boundaries of fields nobody has farmed in three generations. A Khazryn who lets a genealogy lapse has done what the Breaking could not do by force. A stranger who asks which city a Khazryn comes from is asking which court his family's records were kept in, and the answer tells another Khazryn a great deal.

## Houses, Mōbads and the Water

The seven princely houses meet once a year as the Council of the Princes in Amradad, on the Sultan's courtesy, and coordinate a common position without governing anyone. The institution that governs daily life is the Mōbadate. A Mōbad studies fifteen to twenty years before ordination, in astronomy, mathematics, theology, law and at least three languages. In the four lost cities, where there is no Khazryn court, he registers marriages, judges inheritance and keeps the community's records under a foreign magistrate.

Formal worship belongs to [[affiliation-ahurdaen|Āhúrdáén]], the Keeper of Harmony, and no other god may take the weekly observance owed to him. The deepest love goes to [[affiliation-zurathra|Zúráthrä]], the Mother of the Oasis. Every spring and every Khazryn quarter has her shrine at its water, every wedding takes her blessing, and every child is presented to her within forty days of birth. The Ātárzád worship the Sacred Flame alone; they pulled down the great Zúráthrä shrine at Shirvan and built fires over its holy spring.

Under a magistrate, a Khazryn is courteous, exact and reserved. He keeps two calendars—the old Khazryn year of twelve thirty-day months and a five-day festival at the spring equinox, and the city's—and pays what is assessed on time and keeps the receipt. He does not discuss politics with an Ātárzád.

## Places and Dangers

- [[place-mehrnagord|Mehrnāgord]], on the western fringe, is the largest oasis still in Khazryn hands: thirty thousand people, salt pans and a cautious old prince.
- [[place-ushtraber|Ushtra-bēr]], in the deep north, keeps court forms forgotten everywhere else. Learn them before you go.
- [[place-vahurdash|Vahúrdash]] is a ruined city reclaimed from the sand in the last generation, four thousand people holding on among broken walls.
- **Shirvan**, **Ashkarad**, **Zargandûr** and **Kethramír** are the four lost cities, under Ātárzád rule. Their Khazryn quarters pay a water-tax no one else pays, may not worship Āhúrdáén in public and may not carry arms.
- Yázdín of Kethramír's commentary _On the Ungrantable Land_, which answers the Ātárzád claim, is required reading for a Khazryn student and contraband in the Ātárzád cities.

## Ways In

The exile houses fund shrines and schools and keep agents in the lost cities, so a party can be hired to carry a letter, a ledger or a person across a line the magistrates watch. Tablets that left Shirvan in the flight lie scattered across the exile courts, and every house wants the ones another holds. In the Shirvan quarter people believe a Hidden Mōbad still keeps the old rite near the spring; the Ātárzád search for him from time to time and have never found anyone. In Amradad, Khávar of Zargandûr trains with the Sultan's soldiers and gathers the militant exiles, and the Council has neither endorsed nor forbidden what he is preparing.
