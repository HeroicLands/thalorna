> The pilot came aboard at dawn, a silent Kaliharan who took the helm without asking, and for three hours you watched him thread the ship through water that boiled and slid sideways over reefs you could not see. Now the trade wind drops behind the breakwater and [[place-qasirah|Qasirah]] opens ahead in the full morning light: whitewashed warehouses along the quay, merchant palaces with carved wooden balconies above them, and behind it all the jungle rising green and black to volcanic peaks wrapped in cloud. The air is wet and hot as breath and smells of cloves, tar and low tide. On the quay Vedyari traders in white cotton argue over sacks of cardamom; a Haradian captain with a gold ring in one ear shouts at his crew in two languages; a gem dealer holds a green stone up to the sun and squints. Among them the Kaliharans move more slowly, tall and brown-skinned, in plain wraps dyed leaf-green and rust, watching more than they speak.

[[place-kaliharargn|Kalihara]] is a great tropical island far out in the southern ocean, jungle and volcanic highland under a single civilization that has faced inward for ten thousand years. Its people hold that they were raised into that civilization by the [[lore-theithari|Ithári]], who taught them and then left, and that they have tended what the Ithári left behind ever since. They have no gods and do not pray; they observe the world and tend their part of it. The interior is closed to every foreigner without exception. The world meets Kalihara at a handful of ports on the coast, of which Qasirah is the greatest, and even there foreign merchants keep to their own quarters under the eyes of the harbor-wardens.

**The one thing to understand first: the port is as far as you go.** A port Kaliharan is cosmopolitan, speaks three trade tongues and has more in common with a factor in [[place-helionis|Heliónis]] than with a custodian in the highlands, and he will not be admitted inland either. Nobody resents the arrangement, and nobody will explain the interior to you. An inland Kaliharan answers a direct question accurately and volunteers nothing else, and the ridgelines, the jungle and the seas around the island turn back anyone the Kaliharans have not chosen to admit.

## The Living Whole and Its Keepers

Kaliharans call the world one living whole, [[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]], in which every part's health affects every other's. Every Kaliharan keeps three observances: sílhari, the daily habit of watching some corner of the world; vaan'ari, the tending of a garden, grove or stretch of shore that is his to tend; and the exact keeping of inherited practice. A child is given a first sílhari manual at six or seven and fills them for life.

The Ithári'kaan are the custodians, trained from childhood in interior sanctuaries. They tend the structures the Ithári left—seamless gray works that give slightly under the hand—through liturgies whose original purpose has been forgotten. Some of these works seem to calm storms or turn the sea around the island. The custodians carry great influence and do not rule; secular councils govern, and how they are chosen is unknown to outsiders.

## Places and Dangers

- [[place-qasirah|Qasirah]], on the eastern coast, is a city of spice and gem merchants with an Aurèldían temple for the foreigners; its merchant councils meet in the **House of Factors**.
- [[place-vashuran|Vashurán]], [[place-korinthel|Korinthel]] and [[place-telvari|Telvári]] are smaller ports, more tightly controlled.
- The approaches need a Kaliharan pilot; the currents around the island behave in ways foreign sailors call uncanny.
- The voyage home is the dangerous half. The trade winds that carry a ship to Kalihara in a month block the direct return, and the long northern detour crosses the **Doldrum Sea**, where even good captains lose one voyage in eight.
- A household name belongs to the household. A foreigner who uses one is given the full name back and the conversation turns cold.

## Ways In

A party arrives as crew, guards or factors on a spice ship, and the work is at the boundary: a cargo held for a harbor-warden's inquiry, a foreign merchant who tried to buy a guide inland, or goods from the interior that should not be on a quay at all. The [[scenario-klhrdvntrs|Kalihara Adventures]] gather seeds for a campaign.

:::secret
**For the GM:** The Ithári works are failing. Effects once reliable are intermittent, a few works have stopped, and a few have gone bone-white and hard. The senior custodians know how far the failure has gone and keep it secret. If the works that calm the storms fail, the harvest goes with them; if the works that guard the approaches fail, the island loses its best defense.
:::
