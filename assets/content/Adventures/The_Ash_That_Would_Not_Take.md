At [[place-dahanaghat|Dahanaghat]] the urns come home too light and the dead stand in the river at night. The party must find who is selling the hour between the conch and the fire, what is being bought with it, and whether the ground can still be closed.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Stovira of [[affiliation-adanshreni|the Adanshreni]] needs a [Chinnasutra]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} cell removed and cannot be seen near it. The cell takes from houses in mourning, which the shrena forbids, and hides its takings where nobody looks. The contract is generous and will not be honoured in writing.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** A Danaja widow hires two reliable swords to stand beside her at her husband's pyre. She will not say why. Her husband's brother wants the household's keys, and a dying man's wishes are in dispute.
- **The leader.** The sabhra of a river village asks a chieftain to settle a quarrel with the pyre-keeper. The villagers' urns are light, and the keeper says the fire was thorough.
- **The wanderer.** A Jvelita of the [[affiliation-agmipantra|Agmi-pantra]] has been sent for to purge the ground, and finds the keeper's family waiting on the gallery to see what he brings. He asks a traveler to watch the river with him.

## Scene One: The Waiting

**Purpose.** The entrance and the guardians: show a funeral that goes wrong.

**Situation.** Dusk at the stair. A household has brought its dead down on a bier, the oldest son carrying the lamp. The gallery is full of mourners. The river is low and brown, and the first stars are on the water. The pyre-keeper stands at his slab with a splint of sandalwood in one hand. On the pedestal behind him the black conch is silent.

**NPCs and threats.** [Kholu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the pyre-keeper, is a heavy, soft-spoken man who will not meet anyone's eye, and he is late with the conch. The [Waiting]{.name data-type="being/creature"} are in the river. They are the unburned dead of the last three years, standing in the shallows up to their knees in the dress they died in, turned toward the stair. They do not stir while the mourners watch. When the conch is not sounded, they begin to walk. They do not attack the living. They reach for the bier, and for the coin in the dead hand, and they take what they reach. A mourner who stands between them and the bier is moved aside, gently, by many hands.

**Player choices.**

- _Sound the conch._ Anyone may lift the black conch from its pedestal and sound it. The Waiting stop. Kholu goes grey.
- _Give coin._ A coin of passage, or a tomra filed blank, placed in a Waiting hand with the dead one's name, sends that one on. Names are on the bier-cloths of the households at the stair.
- _Fire._ A Jvelita's ember kindled at the slab holds the Waiting back from the bier for as long as it burns.
- _Fight._ The Waiting do not tire, and each one struck rises when the party turns.
- _Slip away._ A party that goes into the reeds at dusk avoids the bier and the Waiting, and leaves the Landing's trouble for the next night.

**Clues and rewards.** Each of the Waiting holds a coin of passage, the coin it was never burned with. Someone has been keeping the coins from the fire. A little old woman in white at the edge of the gallery looks at the party for a long time, and leaves.

**Consequences.** A funeral that fails has its dead among the Waiting by morning. A party that sounds the conch has made an enemy of the keeper and a friend of the household.

**If bypassed.** A party that arrives after the funeral finds the slabs empty and the ash cold, and meets the keeper in the gallery counting coins.

## Scene Two: The Count of Coins

**Purpose.** A roleplay and a puzzle: find what the ledger and the urns say.

**Situation.** The next day. The gallery is quiet, and the keeper's family eats at the long table. On the table stands the great pair of scales, and beside them the keeper's ledger and a basket of the coins recovered from the ash. A Keeper of the Register of [[affiliation-thresholdkeepers|the Threshold-keepers]], [Satyadarshin]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is in the shrine, with two witnesses and a court's warrant.

**NPCs and threats.** Kholu is helpful and afraid. His wife and sons have the instincts of a family who think their house is being looked at. Satyadarshin, a lean man in grey with a witness-cord round his wrist, has come under warrant for an inheritance question. He refuses to put it, because the gap before the Landing's pyres has been open too long and nothing he asks would be answerable. He will say so to anyone who sits with him. The **widow in white** is back, sitting at the end of the bench with a cloth over her wrist.

**Player choices.**

- _Weigh the urns._ A body burned leaves ash of a known weight. The urns of the last year are two-thirds as heavy as they should be. The difference is wood-ash.
- _Count the coins._ Every coin of passage burned with a body is recovered from the ash and refiled. The basket holds fewer coins than the ledger has pyres.
- _Talk to the family._ The sons say what they were told. The wife says nothing and gives a bowl of rice to a stranger.
- _Question Satyadarshin._ He will give an answer to two witnesses. He knows that someone in the gap is asking more than one question, because the gap is crowded.
- _Follow the widow._ She leaves at dusk by a track that does not go to the road.

**Clues and rewards.** Kholu pays a lender he calls only the [Lamp-Seller]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. The lender takes bodies and leaves wood-ash. The count of coins gives the number: nine in the last season, all from households with money. Satyadarshin's account of the gap points to a practice the Keepers forbid and describe in detail.

**Consequences.** A party that makes the numbers public sets the janpoda's sabhra on the keeper. A party that keeps it quiet has days before the Lamp-Seller moves.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the count reaches the cenotaph without numbers and meets the cell's watch on the stair unprepared.

## Scene Three: The Cenotaph Stair

**Purpose.** A trick and a setback: the way down is a trap, and the keeper's kindness is its bait.

**Situation.** Beyond the gallery stands a small cenotaph of white stone, a roof on four pillars, to the unnamed dead of the river. A stair under it goes down to a low room where Kholu says the old urns are kept. The door is open. Torches are lit.

**NPCs and threats.** The Chinnasutra cell is in the cellar and has been waiting for visitors. Its leader, the widow in white, is [Nusha]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a handsome woman in her middle years who has buried three husbands and robbed three households during the burning of each. She is the cell's face at funerals. With her are four cutthroats from the river, and the **sentries**, a row of unburned dead in the sacking they were taken in, eyes open and mouths closed. They carry short cords of knotted silk. The stair is wet and the roof low. When the party is on the stair, the door shuts behind it.

**Player choices.**

- _Go down._ The party is expected.
- _Watch the stair._ A careful party notices that the torches are lit and no one has come up in an hour.
- _Take the keeper._ Kholu goes down the stair with the party, a knife at his back. He is more use alive.
- _Bribe the cell._ Nusha will deal. Her price is the party's silence.
- _Go in from the river._ The drain behind the slabs is a low tunnel that leads under the cenotaph. It is wet, dark and unguarded.

**Clues and rewards.** The cellar holds the cell's takings: plate, small jewels, the household keys of eight families, and a stack of letters of credit of [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective]]. A shelf of urns, each marked, holds the real ash of the bodies they were sold as.

**Consequences.** The sentries wake at the first blow. Nusha escapes by the river unless the drain is watched. If she is taken alive, she names the Lamp-Seller.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes straight to the Lamp-Seller by the river tunnel reaches the vault without the cell's goods and without the cell's enemies.

## Scene Four: The Lamp-Seller

**Purpose.** The climax: the fallen priest and the man he keeps in the gap.

**Situation.** Beyond the cellar, a second stair descends to a round vault lined with white clay. On a bed in the center lies a dying man in gold-threaded cloth, and over him burns a single threshold lamp. The old man is not dead. He has not died, for days, and his lips move. Around him stand a dozen of the Waiting in a ring, facing outward, a coin in every hand.

**NPCs and threats.** [Dakunata]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the Lamp-Seller, is a Retuja fallen, with the brand of a Patuta on his face and a hood to hide it. He was a priest of the temple school, trained to the passage, and he has learned how to keep a soul at the threshold and ask it things. He is quiet, exact and intensely courteous. He addresses the dying man by the polite name of his station, and the dying man answers him. The old man is [Dhanapati Ratnapala]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the head of a trading household of [[place-naughat|Naughat]], and the questions are of trade: where the money is, who is owed, what the cargoes were worth. [[affiliation-twilighths|The Twilight House]] has commissioned the answers. Its Factor, [Sandhyakirti]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a courtesan in grey-green silk, sits at the foot of the bed with a slate, writing. Dakunata fights with the Waiting and with a word that stops the heart.

**Player choices.**

- _Fight._ Dakunata is old and not strong. The Waiting are many and will not leave their ring.
- _Sound the conch._ The conch from the shrine, carried down, sounded in the vault, ends the gap for Ratnapala and for the Waiting. The old man dies, and the questions stop.
- _The one question._ Satyadarshin, if he is with the party, can put the warranted question. It is the only lawful question the dying man can be asked, and it is the one that exposes the Lamp-Seller.
- _Bargain._ The Factor offers money and the Twilight House's silence. The Lamp-Seller offers the Waiting's release if his work is left unfinished.
- _Burn the vault._ A Jvelita's ember in the clay kills the lamp, the gap and the work, and probably the Waiting.

**Clues and rewards.** The Factor's slate has the questions asked and the answers given, in the House's careful hand. Dakunata keeps a register of the soul-gaps he has opened, with the names of the clients who bought them. Ratnapala's last word, if he is allowed to speak it, is his own wish for his household.

**Consequences.** If the lamp is put out, the gap closes for this household and not for the Landing. If Dakunata lives, he goes to ground in another cremation ground.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes to the vault by the river tunnel finds it before the cell has been warned.

## Scene Five: The Closing

**Purpose.** The twist and the reward.

**Situation.** The lamp is out, and the Landing is not quiet. The dead stand in the river, hundreds of them now, the whole of the three years and more. The gap has been opened and shut so many times that the ground has torn. The Landing is a _shorukshetra_, and the Waiting do not leave.

**NPCs and threats.** The **river** is the threat: the stair is slick, the water is rising in the dark, and the Waiting are on every step. Kholu, if he lives, kneels in the water. Satyadarshin will not put a question on a blood-field, because nothing on it may be asked. The Jvelita, if there is one, says the only thing that closes a field is fire, and that it costs.

**Player choices.**

- _Burn the ground._ The Agmi-pantra's rite: every pyre slab alight at once, lit from the ember, with the names read aloud. It takes the night and the Jvelita's vessel. It works, as it works half the time.
- _Close it by rite._ The household of each of the Waiting is found, and the coin and the name given. It takes days and the cooperation of the janpoda.
- _Give it up._ The party leaves, and the Landing becomes a blood-field that spreads along the lower reaches of [[place-sarvedarivr|the Sarveda]].
- _Take the register._ Dakunata's register and the Factor's slate are worth more to some people than the Landing.

**Clues and rewards.** The cenotaph's store is returned to its families by the sabhra, and the shrena pays modestly, in chendra, for the removal of the cell. The grateful Danaja household of Ratnapala rewards the party with a letter of credit.

**Consequences.** What leaves the Landing in the party's packs is a register, a debt, a flame or a grudge, depending on what they did at the end.

**If bypassed.** The Waiting dissolve with the dawn, and the ground starts to close over the course of a season.

## Aftermath

- **The ground burned and the dead named.** The Landing is quiet. The Agmi-pantra is thanked at Naughat and cursed by the temple of the passage.
- **The ground left to itself.** The river villages move their dead to the sea-shore, and the Landing becomes a shorukshetra of the lower Sarveda.
- **The Twilight House.** The Factor leaves with her slate, and the House's name comes up in the sabhra of the janpoda.
- **The shrena.** The Sabhra of the Adanshreni has its cell removed and does not thank anyone for it.
- **Kholu's house.** The keeper's family loses the Landing to a younger branch.
