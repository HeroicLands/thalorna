A party crossing the [[place-vandstein|Vandstein]] highlands by the old road takes shelter in a guesthouse built into an abandoned Vylarian post. Its keeper cuts the bridge behind every party his kin send him, and in the cellars beneath the post a renegade mage takes travelers apart and builds servants out of what is left. The largest of them speaks with the voices of the people it was made from. The frightened girl who carries the bowls tells the party all of this, and every word she says is true: she is the mage, and she has been wearing that girl for two summers.

## Overview

North of the passes [[lore-legionsroad|The Legions' Road]] crosses a gorge on four Vylarian piers and climbs into the black pines of [[place-dunkelholt|Dunkelholt]], where a stone shell of an imperial post has been roofed again as a guesthouse. Its keeper, [Othric yra Vandwald wur Vandgrund]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has a wife the Master below has kept alive after a bear mauled her, and he pays for that by sending travelers down the cellar stair. The Master is [Siremos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Vylarian of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] whose house struck him from its rolls; the [Sekran]{.name data-type="affiliation"} wants him dead for the arts he practices, and its file describes an old man. His servants are [[lore-golemcrtr|Constructs]] of dead flesh, each held by a ring of signs cut into its skin, and the largest, the [Choir]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, hunts the near bank of the river at night. A thin young woman, [Sundrún yra Eichrúnd wur Erthhaven]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, serves at the keeper's table, and she is the party's best guide to all of it.

That is the story as the party first meets it, and every sentence of it holds. The section that follows, What Is Really Happening, says how the rest of it reaches the party.

Sundrún tells the party most of this in Scene Two, and each thing she tells it can also be found without her:

The sally passage the legions cut drops from a drain under the woodshed, runs beneath the cellars and comes out at the river. It is the second way into the Master's chamber, and the runoff that runs down it keeps the Choir out of it.

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the house—who Sundrún is, what sits by the brazier, what Othric's bargain buys, and what the Choir obeys—is in [[place-dunkelholt#truth|the truth of Dunkelholt]]. In short: Sundrún is Siremos, the old man in the cellar is his former body bound as a decoy, and every warning she gives is true, because a true warning herds a party toward the cellar better than a lie. This section covers what the truth does at the table: where the party can find it, how it lands on each route, and what the party wants once it knows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Sundrún's confession in Scene Two is a performance, and every fact in it is true. The party must be able to find each of those facts without her—at the cut rope, in the woodshed, in the factor's day-book and in the luggage—so that a party that never trusts her still knows what she would have told it, and a party that does trust her can check her.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal belongs in Scene Three or at the start of Scene Four, and it reaches the party by whatever route it took.

- **Through the loft and the stair.** [Athalwen]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, coming up the cellar steps, sees Sundrún's lamp behind the party and stops. She says that the one with the lamp is the one who cuts, that it was the old man once and has been the girl for two summers, and she asks the party to make her husband stop serving her.
- **Through the yard or the pines.** When the Choir has a member of the party in its arms and is about to tear what the Master means to use, Sundrún speaks to it. Every mouth on it turns toward her, and it goes still at her word.
- **Through Sundrún's way.** The party comes up through the grate onto the prepared table, and Sundrún climbs out behind it with the lamp and sets the lamp down at the head of the table.
- **Down the main stair.** The old man repeats himself, the edge of the ring of signs shows under the stitching at his throat when he turns, and the Choir does nothing he tells it until Sundrún comes in behind the party.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on a single detail and then stop talking. Athalwen's sentence, the Choir going still, the lamp set down at the head of the table: say it once, plainly, and let the players turn round in their chairs and look at the scenes behind them.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early, and never deny a guess that is right. A party that suspects Sundrún in Scene Two and confronts her before the cellar catches the Master with the Choir outside, the old man below and only the drain to run to. Let her drop the pretense when cornered, speak as the old scholar she is, and bargain from weakness. The party has earned an easier fight.

> [!WARNING]
> An early kill is not a closed route. A party that kills Sundrún on suspicion alone has killed the Master without proof. The Choir keeps the house on its last order, the old man goes on reciting at the brazier, Othric weeps with relief, and the manuscripts in Scene Five tell the party what it did. Let the evidence arrive afterward, and let the party face the Erthhaven kindred with it.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party wants something it did not want before. The girl it has been protecting is the one to stop, and she is at its side holding the light. [Arngar yra Skaldrith wur Thrastein]{.name data-type="being/npc"} learns that the hand that cut his brother is the one he has been guarding. Othric and Athalwen become people to free, and stop being gaolers to punish. Killing the old man achieves exactly what the Master wants, so the party has to decide whether to let that story stand and carry it south, or to carry the truth instead, which means the body of a young woman of the [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] on the cellar floor and a kindred at [[place-erthhaven|Erthhaven]] owed an account of it.

> [!TIP]
> A party hired by the Sekran, or carrying a mage of the Arcane Order, is the party the decoy was made for. Any hook can carry a second commission: a _sekeros_ (inquirer) at the border garrison pays a small retainer for news of an old Vylarian mage on the northern road.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A [[place-moktur|Moktur]] factor's agent at the border garrison says a fur consignment worth a small fortune went north with a [[place-vandgrund|Vandgrund]] guide last autumn and never reached [[place-waldburg|Waldburg]]. Her house wants the furs or the proof they are gone, and will pay for either.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A merchant party crossing to Waldburg hires the party as guards at Vandgrund, because [[affiliation-blckpnwlvs|The Blackpine Wolves]] work the road south of the gorge. The merchants have been told there is a good guesthouse on the way.
- **The leader.** A Vandgrund elder who has counted the parties that never reached Waldburg wants someone from outside the kindred to walk the road and say what is on it, because the guides who send parties north are her own kin.
- **The lone wanderer.** The guide, Arngar yra Skaldrith wur Thrastein, takes the job for less than it is worth. His brother [Arnric]{.name data-type="being/npc"} guided a fur party north on this road last autumn and did not come home, and Arngar does not mention it.

## The Opening

The party has crossed the gorge on the span and is climbing toward the pines when the span goes. Read or paraphrase:

> Behind you something cracks like a branch in frost, and then the river has the bridge. You turn in time to see the last of the planks tip off the piers, and the timbers drift past below you, turning slowly, end over end, toward the bend. There is no other crossing within a day. Ahead, across a clearing, smoke rises from a stone guesthouse among the pines, and a lamp burns in its window though it is barely dusk. Arngar stops on the path and looks at the building for a long moment. Every shutter on it is fastened from the outside.

> [!TIP]
> Let the bridge sit for a breath before anyone speaks. The opening's job is the first cold certainty that this is not good, and the lamp in the window is the first thing the party walks toward.

## Scene One: The Open Door

**Purpose.** Bring the party under the keeper's roof, make his hospitality the first thing that is wrong, and put Sundrún at the party's side.

> The door opens while you are still crossing the yard, before anyone has knocked. A broad man stands in it with the firelight behind him, wiping his hands on a cloth, and says the pot is hot and the beds are dry. Behind him a thin young woman lifts the lamp down from the window and holds it up to light you in.

**Situation.** The guesthouse is the old post's gatehouse and barracks range, roofed in bark and turf, around a yard with a stable and a stream that runs through a stone channel the legions cut. The keeper offers a fire, a pot of venison, dry beds and stabling, and he asks, kindly, who knows the party's road: who sent them, who expects them at Waldburg, and when. The shutters, he says, are fastened against the wind off the highland. That is true of the wind.

**NPCs and threats.** Othric is courteous, tired, and listens harder than he talks. He will not go into the cellar while guests are awake. Sundrún carries the bowls, and she interrupts him at the table to tell the party, too loudly, that the road is full of Wolves and they must keep their weapons by them even here. Othric sends her to the kitchen, and when she passes behind his chair he leans aside to let her by without looking at her. She came to Dunkelholt two summers ago with a merchant party and stayed when it left, or so the keeper says.

Othric sends her away because he is afraid of her, and because a girl who lingers among the guests is choosing which of them to use. He says what he was told to say about the merchant party. Sundrún's warning is true, and she wants the party armed, because an armed party is one that can kill an old man in a cellar.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Two things must happen at supper: Sundrún's warning, said too loudly, and Othric leaning aside as she passes behind him. Describe the second in passing, among the bowls and the bread, and move on.

> [!TIP]
> Play Sundrún as the one person in the house who is on the party's side. She is quick, thin, frightened for the party and right about everything, and her fear is real enough to pass: what she fears is a party that fails to go down the stair.

> [!WARNING]
> Give Sundrún no sinister tell beyond the ones this scene and the next name. A party that distrusts her from the first hour should do so on the evidence, never on the GM's tone of voice.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Seize the keeper and demand answers. He says nothing that matters and asks to be let go before dark, because he has a wife to see to.
- **Stealth.** Search the yard before supper: the stable, the woodshed, the icehouse dug into the bank.
- **Talk.** Answer the keeper's questions, or lie to him. A party that tells him someone at Waldburg expects it on a given day watches him go quiet and count.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the post itself: the gatehouse bars are on the outside of the guest doors, and new iron hasps hold the shutters.
- **Giving something up.** Refuse the roof and camp in the pines, which is exactly where the Choir hunts.

**Clues and rewards.** The rope ends at the span were cut, not frayed, and the woodshed holds a stack of new planks and coils of fresh rope enough to lay the span again. An iron grate in the woodshed floor drains the yard, and water runs loud beneath it. The stable holds the harness of far more beasts than it has stalls, the brass of several merchant houses on it. In the icehouse, under hides, lies a dead hound with a man's hands for forefeet and a band of signs cut around its chest, slashed through with a knife; it is the size of a calf and quite still.

**Consequences.** A party that answers the keeper's questions truthfully tells him how long he has before anyone comes looking. A party that lies well buys a night. The hound is the first thing the party sees that has no ordinary explanation.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that camps in the pines hears its own words repeated back from the trees after dark, in voices that are not its own, and something heavy circles the camp without crossing the stream the party has camped beside. Sundrún comes out at full dark with bread and the same warning, and begs them inside. While she walks from the house to the fire, the circling stops, and it starts again after she has reached the camp. The Choir is hers and lets her pass. A party that kills or binds the keeper at once finds the cellar door locked from beneath and Sundrún too frightened to say why, and the night of Scene Three begins without him.

> [!TIP]
> If the party camps, describe the stillness and nothing more. Do not have Sundrún remark on it. A player who asks why the thing let her by has found the first thread, and Sundrún's answer is true: it does not hunt her.

## Scene Two: The Stripped Beds

**Purpose.** Show the party what has happened to everyone who stayed here before them, and give it Sundrún's account and her route.

> The loft runs the length of the old barracks under a roof of bark and turf. There are eight beds in it. Seven are bare frames with their ropes still strung. The eighth has its mattress, and someone has made it up for you.

**Situation.** The guest rooms are the old barracks loft, up a stair with a door at its foot. The ticking of the one made bed is torn along both edges in rows of four, the width of a hand, where someone held on. In the eaves, under a loose board, is the luggage of the parties before: packs, saddlebags and strongboxes with the marks of several Moktur merchant houses, and a Varokh fur party's bundles. Each party's gear carries the tally-cord a Vandgrund guide ties on a bundle he is guiding, knotted with the party's count. The only guide's pack among them is Arnric's, of [[place-thrastein|Thrastein]]; no Vandgrund guide's pack is there. The Vandgrund guides who brought these parties walked home.

**NPCs and threats.** Arngar finds his brother's pack in the eaves. He knows it by a knot on the strap, and he goes very quiet. Sundrún comes up with water and, once the door is shut, talks. What she says is true in every word:

- The keeper's kin at Vandgrund send word of every party they guide north.
- The keeper cuts the bridge when one is due, so that nobody can turn back.
- The Master buys the travelers from Othric with his wife's life.
- She holds the lamp at the Master's table, because her hands are steady.
- The legions dug a way from the cellars to the river, and it comes up behind the thing that guards the stair.

She never says who the Master is. She says "the Master" and "below", and lets the party put the old man there that every story about a renegade mage prepares it for.

> [!WARNING]
> Sundrún never lies, and the reveal depends on it. If a player asks outright whether the Master is the man in the cellar, she answers with something true: the Master is in the cellar every night. If a player asks whether she is the Master, she laughs, and says that she is the one who holds the lamp.

> [!TIP]
> Her account should sound like a confession wrung out of a frightened girl. Let her hands shake on the cup, let her listen at the door, and let her ask the party to take her south with it when this is over. All of it is performance, and none of it is false.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Break down the cellar door now. It is oak bound in Vylarian iron, and the noise brings the keeper with an axe and wakes what is below.
- **Stealth.** Wait for the house to sleep, and go down by Sundrún's way.
- **Talk.** Get the whole story from Sundrún, which costs her the keeper's trust if he hears of it, or confront the keeper with the pack, which costs Arngar his patience.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the luggage. Among it is a Moktur factor's day-book in [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron Script]], its last pages written here.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the luggage and its silver where it is, so that the keeper finds nothing disturbed.

**Clues and rewards.** The factor's entries are plain and frightened, and each records something the party can set beside Sundrún's account:

- At the last farm their guide sent a boy ahead with a knotted cord, and the keeper met them at the door already knowing how many they were.
- The span went into the river the evening they crossed, and the keeper said the frost had taken it.
- The keeper asked twice who knew their road and when they were expected at Waldburg.
- One of his men saw something big standing at the stable stream at night and not crossing it.
- The factor heard the keeper on the cellar stair after dark, speaking to his wife below, and a woman's voice asking him to let her die. The keeper answered that it would be one more party, and then one more.
- The girl who serves carried the lamp down the cellar stair alone at midnight, and the screaming began after.
- A voice called the factor's clerk by name from the yard after the clerk had gone missing.

Then the hand changes. The last pages are in a smaller, practiced hand, and the last entry describes the drain under the woodshed: a passage the legions cut, too low for anything large, with water running in it, that climbs to a grate beneath the cellars behind the guard on the stair. If nobody in the party reads Semaron, Sundrún reads the book aloud without stumbling. Arngar, asked about the stream, says the tellings of the [[lore-varokhiclt|hrag]] hold that it will not cross running water, and that he never believed them. The luggage holds a modest weight of aurions and amber the party can take now.

The last pages are the Master's work, written into a dead man's book and left where the party would find it. They are bait, and they name her route.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The party must leave this scene with the drain route in hand, from Sundrún, from the day-book or from both. Every route through Scene Four is easier with it, and the one that ends on the second table runs through it.

> [!TIP]
> Let the order fall as it falls. A party that reads the day-book before Sundrún talks hears her confirm it; a party that reads it afterward can check her against it, and finds every word borne out. Either way the party knows the house from the evidence, and her confession is only the loudest copy of it.

> [!TIP]
> Describe the change of hand as a plain fact of the page, the way a player handling the book would see it. Do not dwell on it. The second reading belongs to Scene Four, when the jar labels show the same hand.

**Consequences.** Arngar stops being a guide and becomes a brother. From here he will go toward any voice that sounds like Arnric's, and he is right that one of them is. A party that confronts Othric forces him to choose between his guests and his wife, and he chooses his wife.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never climbs to the loft is put there anyway at bedtime, and finds the torn mattress on the only bed the keeper has made up for it. If the party never wins Sundrún's trust, she slips the factor's day-book under a door during the night, and the clues of the day-book reach the party that way; the book under the door is bait as surely as the book in the eaves. If Arngar is lost or left behind, the brother's pack is still in the eaves, and the voice of Scene Three uses his name instead.

## Scene Three: The Voice at the Stable

**Purpose.** The horror comes inside. The party learns it is penned in, that something in the cellar is coming up, and, on most routes, who has been holding the lamp.

> Deep in the night a voice in the yard calls Arngar by name. It asks him to come down and help with the horses; one of them is cast in its stall. Below the floor, at the same time, something starts up the cellar steps: one step, and a long drag; one step, and a long drag. On the landing outside the loft door, wood scrapes on iron as a bar goes home.

**Situation.** The voice in the yard is Arngar's own, or near enough that only Arngar knows the difference: it is his brother's. Othric, on the landing, has set the bar across the loft door from the outside. A little after the bar goes home it lifts again, and Sundrún slips in with her lamp, whispering that the keeper has gone to the yard and the party has until he comes back. From the loft she offers three ways: down the stair to the dining room and the cellar door; out into the yard; or round the back to the woodshed and the drain.

**NPCs and threats.** The Choir stands in the yard by the stream, a head taller than a tall man, its body joined from several bodies at seams that do not quite match, with mouths opening along its shoulders and throat. It repeats what the mouths last said: the factor's clerk asking who is there, a Wolf cursing, a woman calling for a child, Arnric asking for help with the horses. What comes up the cellar steps is Athalwen, Othric's wife, alive, her own body from the waist up and the rest of her somebody else's, which she cannot yet make walk. She is coming up because she heard voices, and in two winters she has heard no voice but her husband's and the Master's.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Break the loft door, or go through the roof turf and drop into the yard. Fighting the Choir in the open, in the dark, is the hardest thing the party can choose tonight.
- **Stealth.** Keep still and let the voice call. The Choir does not come in; it waits.
- **Talk.** Speak to Othric through the door, or to Athalwen on the stair. She is lucid, and she wants to die, and she asks the party to make her husband stop.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Draw the Choir to the stream and stand on the far side of it, where it will not follow.
- **Giving something up.** Let Arngar go to the voice, or hold him back by force and lose his trust for the rest of the night.

**Clues and rewards.** At the stream the Choir stops dead and paces the edge, which proves the day-book right. Up close, a ring of signs is cut around its middle, the same as the dead hound's, and the party can see where a knife would have to go. Athalwen knows that her husband keeps the cellar key on his belt and that the Master works behind a door marked with a sign that burns the hand that touches it.

**The reveal, on the stair.** If Sundrún is with the party when it meets Athalwen, Athalwen sees the lamp behind them and stops. She says it plainly: "The one with the lamp is the one who cuts. It was the old man once. It has been the girl for two summers." Then she asks the party to make her husband stop serving her. Sundrún, if she answers at all, says that every word she told them was true.

**The reveal, in the yard.** If the party is in the yard and the Choir has one of them in its arms, about to tear what the Master means to use, Sundrún calls out to it from the woodshed door. Every mouth on it turns toward her, and it goes still and lets its prisoner drop.

> [!TIP]
> After Athalwen speaks, give the players a silence and let them turn to the girl with the lamp. Sundrún has no fight in her body and knows it. She steps back toward the dark with the lamp held up between herself and the party, and she starts to bargain.

> [!WARNING]
> Choose the route before the reveal, not after it. If the party goes by the woodshed, Athalwen never reaches it and the Choir never needs Sundrún's word, so the reveal waits for the second table in Scene Four. Do not move Athalwen to the drain to land it early.

> [!TIP]
> Once the party knows, Othric is a man to free. A party that tells him it knows what Sundrún is hears the first honest words he has spoken: he gives up the cellar key and asks the party to carry Athalwen out of the house before anything else is done.

**Consequences.** If Arngar reaches the voice, the Choir takes him and carries him down into the cellar, and he is alive on the first table in Scene Four. If the party kills Athalwen, as she asks, Othric turns on the party with everything he has; if the party spares her and carries her with it, he opens the cellar for them. Either way there is no way back across the gorge, the shutters are fastened, and the Choir is between the house and the pines.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** Every path from here leads down. If the Choir takes anyone, its trail of blood runs to the cellar door, which it leaves open behind it. If the party breaks out of the loft and runs for the pines, the Choir drives it back toward the walls, and Sundrún is waiting at the woodshed with a lamp, at the head of the sally passage. A party that holds the loft until dawn finds the yard empty and the cellar door open, and goes down knowing someone was taken in the night.

## Scene Four: The Warded Room

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the Master and her greatest work, and the revelation of what the house has been for and who has been running it.

Read by the stair:

> The cellars are long vaults under the granary, lamplit and swept clean. In the outer vault the thing from the yard stands with its back to a door, and every mouth on it is shut. Cut into the lintel of the door behind it is a sign that hurts to look at for long.

Read by the drain:

> The passage climbs in the dark with water running round your ankles, until lamplight falls through a grate above you. You push it up and come out under a table. Its straps hang undone. Along its edge, on a clean cloth, instruments are laid out in rows: knives, hooks, saws, needles already threaded, one set to a place, and the places are as many as you are. Across the room an old man by a brazier looks up and begins to speak.

**Situation.** The cellars are the legions' strongroom, long vaulted chambers under the granary. The Choir stands in the outer vault. Beyond it, behind a door with a ward-sign cut into its lintel, is the Master's operating chamber: the first table, with straps, where the night's taken traveler lies awake and bound; racks of instruments; jars labeled in a small, practiced hand; a brazier; and the second table, set over the drain grate and laid out for the party. A narrow old man sits by the brazier, wearing a young man's hands, one eye blue and the other brown, his throat stitched where a fresh run of skin has been laid in. He speaks in four sentences, always in this order, and begins again at the first when he reaches the last:

1. "You are welcome. Sit; the floor is cold."
2. "I am Siremos. I was of the Arcane Order once, and the Arcane Order would like me dead."
3. "Everything you carry can be mended. Lie down, and I will show you."
4. "There is no need for the swords."

He is the Master's former body, a construct with its ring of signs under the stitching at its throat. On Sundrún's way she climbs out of the grate behind the party with the lamp and sets the lamp down at the head of the second table, where she stands at every working.

> [!WARNING]
> Never let the old man improvise. He does not answer questions, he does not react to wounds, and he does not change the order of his four sentences. The first time a player notices the repetition is a clue; a GM who has him say something new to keep a conversation going spends it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Whatever route the party took, this scene must put Sundrún and the lamp in the room and make the party see her for what she is. If the reveal landed in Scene Three, this scene is the reckoning; if it did not, it lands here.

**NPCs and threats.** The Choir guards the outer vault and obeys the Master's word, which means it obeys Sundrún. When the old man tells it to take the party, it does not move; when Sundrún tells it, it does. Sundrún is no fighter in a body that is not her own. Her ward burns the hand that opens the inner door, two smaller servants joined from dogs and the parts of men wait under the tables for her word, and the Choir comes to her voice. She bargains readily and truthfully: she can close any wound the party carries tonight, she can make Athalwen walk, and she will cut Arnric's mouth out of the Choir and give it to his brother to bury. When Arngar wavers she has the Choir say Arnric's last words again. If the fight turns against her, she goes down the grate and along the passage to the boat at the river, and the last thing she tells the Choir is to keep the house. A construct does what it was last told for as long as its signs hold.

> [!TIP]
> Play the Master unmasked as the old scholar behind the girl's face: courteous, precise, unhurried and faintly amused, speaking Varokh with a Vylarian's turns. She is honest about what she is and what she wants, and her best argument to the party is that the old man is right there, and nobody south of the passes needs to know the difference.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight through the Choir in the outer vault, at the cost of whatever its arms close on.
- **Stealth.** Come up through the drain grate by Sundrún's way, under the ward and behind the Choir, which never enters the passage because water runs in it.
- **Talk.** Bargain with the Master for the captive's life, or for Athalwen's death, or turn Othric against her by making the keeper see that his guests now know.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Lure the Choir into the runoff channel, where it stops; or break the ring of signs while it stands still.
- **Giving something up.** Let one of the party hold the Choir's attention, so another can reach the signs.

**Clues and rewards.** The mouths of the Choir are the travelers who stayed here. When Arngar hears his brother's voice from one of them saying the last thing Arnric said, the guide knows him, and so does the party. The jar labels are in the hand of the day-book's last pages. The old man carries no key and no purse; the strongroom key is on Sundrún's belt.

**Met prepared.** A party that knows about the signs, the water and the passage, and knows who holds the lamp, can take Sundrún the moment she climbs out of the grate behind it, before the Choir turns, and cut the Choir's ring while it stands in the channel or at the door it was told to hold. When the ring is broken, every mouth closes at once, and it falls.

**Met unprepared.** A party that comes down the stair knowing none of it meets the Choir head on. It can still survive by retreating into the sally passage, where the Choir will not follow, and coming up again under the second table, or by fleeing down the passage to the river, where the boat is moored. Killing the old man ends nothing: the Choir keeps the house, and Sundrún stands in the doorway with the lamp.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that kills the old man and believes the work is done has given the Master what she wanted. That is a legitimate ending. Sundrún weeps with relief, thanks the party, and asks to go south with it. Do not correct the party; let Scene Five and the manuscripts do it, or let the party leave without reading them.

**Consequences.** A party that breaks the Choir's signs frees its mouths; they say nothing more. A party that kills Sundrún kills the Master, and leaves the Choir standing on its last order unless it breaks the ring too. A party that kills only the old man has closed the Sekran's file for her. A party that bargains with the Master and lets her live leaves her at work.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party driven out of the cellars by the Choir finds itself in the sally passage, which ends at the river and the boat. It can escape by water now and come back by daylight, when Sundrún is upstairs with the bowls and the Choir stands motionless in the outer vault until it is called. The old man is at the brazier by day as by night, awake, in the same chair. A party that never reaches the operating chamber hears the captive screaming from it, which brings it back.

## Scene Five: The Strongroom and the River

**Purpose.** Pay the reward, give the party the proof of what the Master is, and get the party out before the next span is laid and the next party crosses it.

> Behind the iron door the legions hung, the strongroom is dry and orderly. Bags and bales stand along the walls in rows, each row with a merchant's mark chalked above it, and at the far end, on a shelf of its own, is a cedar chest.

**Situation.** Off the operating chamber, behind an iron door the legions hung, is the strongroom. Its key is on Sundrún's belt or Othric's. Inside are the goods of every party that stayed at Dunkelholt, sorted and kept, because the Master sells them south through Vandgrund to pay for materials. The merchant party Sundrún came with two summers ago never left: its goods are here, in a row of their own.

**NPCs and threats.** If the Choir still stands, it keeps the house, and the party has to leave by water or by the Choir's leave. If Othric lives, he can be talked out of his post or left to it. His kin at Vandgrund are already guiding the next party north.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Carry out everything the boat will hold.
- **Stealth.** Take only what is easily carried, and leave the rest for whoever comes.
- **Talk.** Persuade Othric to go back to Vandgrund and tell the elders what his kin have been doing.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Burn the cellars behind the party, so nobody works there again.
- **Giving something up.** Return the furs to the Moktur house that lost them, or the brother's pack to his village.

**Clues and rewards.** Bags of silver aurions, sorted by the house they came from; amber in rough lumps from the Varokh fur parties; bales of prime marten and sable pelts for the river trade; and the Master's manuscripts in the cedar chest, his surgical notes and the workings he used on the dead, each worth a great deal to the Sekran and dangerous to anyone else who reads them. The notes run for years in an old man's crabbed hand and change two summers ago to a young, practiced one, the hand of the jar labels and the day-book's last pages. The last working in the old hand is the taking of a living body, its subject written down as a Varokh woman of about twenty, sound, with steady hands. A riverboat is moored at the foot of the sally passage, big enough for the party and its goods and whoever it rescued.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The cedar chest is the proof. A party that suspected Sundrún finds it confirmed here; a party that killed only the old man finds out what it did. Put the last working where the party will read it.

**Consequences.** The manuscripts draw the Sekran, which pays for them and then asks who else has read them; the last working changes what the Sekran's file says it is hunting. The furs belong to houses that will pay to have them back. A party that leaves the cellars intact leaves them for the next mage who needs a quiet place.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that escaped by the river in Scene Four without the strongroom can come back by boat on a later day, through the sally passage, with the Master gone or upstairs; the strongroom key is on Othric's belt or Sundrún's, and Athalwen knows where both of them sleep.

## Aftermath

- **If the Choir was broken and the Master killed.** The road north of the passes is quiet, and the villages of Vandstein go on calling what hunted it a hrag. Arngar takes his brother's pack home to Thrastein. A young woman of Erthhaven is dead by the party's hand, and her kindred is owed an account: with the manuscripts and Arngar's word the party can give one, and without them her kin seek blood payment before their Other Chief.
- **If the party killed only the old man.** The Sekran's file on Siremos closes, and a young woman with a lamp takes service at another roadhouse farther along another road. If the party took her south, she leaves it at Waldburg with thanks.
- **If the Master escaped, unmasked.** She moves farther north and starts again, and a party that knows her face is the first thing she looks for.
- **If the Choir still stands.** It keeps the house it was told to keep, and the near bank of the gorge has a hunter on it at night that no village can name.
- **If Othric went home.** The Vandgrund elders call a reckoning on the guides who sent parties north, and the families concerned are the same ones the War Chief fears to drive away.
- **If the manuscripts went to the Sekran.** The Arcane Order pays well, and opens a file on the party. If the last working went with them, the Sekran's file on Siremos describes a young Varokh woman.
