A party crossing the Vandstein highlands by the old road takes shelter in a guesthouse built into an abandoned Vylarian post. Its keeper cuts the bridge behind every party his kin send him, and beneath the post a renegade mage takes travelers apart to mend his own failing body and to build servants out of what is left. The largest of them speaks with the voices of the people it was made from.

## Overview

North of the passes the [[lore-legionsroad|Legions' Road]] crosses a gorge on four Vylarian piers and climbs into the black pines of [[place-dunkelholt|Dunkelholt]], where a stone shell of an imperial post has been roofed again as a guesthouse. Its keeper, **Óthwin yra Vandwald wur Vandgrund**, has a wife the mage below has kept alive after a bear mauled her, and he pays for that by sending travelers down the cellar stair. **Sîrêmôs**, the mage, is a Vylarian of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Ordo Arcanis]] whose house struck him from its rolls; the Sekran wants him dead for the arts he practices. His servants are [[lore-golemcrtr|constructs]] of dead flesh, each held by a ring of signs cut into its skin, and the largest, the Choir, hunts the near bank of the river at night.

Three facts carry the adventure, and the party can learn each of them in more than one place:

| What the party needs to know                           | Where it can be learned                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The keeper cuts the bridge behind his guests.          | The cut rope at the span; the stacked planks and new rope in the woodshed; Sundwyn; the factor's day-book |
| The made things stop when the signs cut in them break. | The dead hound in the icehouse; Sundwyn, who has held the lamp while he cut them; the mage's manuscripts  |
| The Choir will not cross running water.                | The factor's day-book; the hrág tellings the guide knows; the Choir's own behavior at the stable stream   |

The sally passage the legions cut from the cellars to the river is the alternative way to the mage's chamber, and the runoff that runs down it is why the Choir never uses it.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Moktur factor's agent at the border garrison says a fur consignment worth a small fortune went north with a Vandgrund guide last autumn and never reached [[place-waldburg|Waldburg]]. Her house wants the furs or the proof they are gone, and will pay for either.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A merchant party crossing to Waldburg hires the party as guards at Vandgrund, because the [[affiliation-blckpnwlvs|Blackpine Wolves]] work the road south of the gorge. The merchants have been told there is a good guesthouse on the way.
- **The leader.** A Vandgrund elder who has counted the parties that never reached Waldburg wants someone from outside the kindred to walk the road and say what is on it, because the guides who send parties north are her own kin.
- **The lone wanderer.** The guide, **Arnrád yra Skáldrith wur Thrástein**, takes the job for less than it is worth. His brother **Arnulf** guided a fur party north on this road last autumn and did not come home, and Arnrád does not mention it.

## The Opening

The party has crossed the gorge on the span and is climbing toward the pines when the span goes. Read or paraphrase:

> Behind you something cracks like a branch in frost, and then the river has the bridge. You turn in time to see the last of the planks tip off the piers, and the timbers drift past below you, turning slowly, end over end, toward the bend. There is no other crossing within a day. Ahead, across a clearing, smoke rises from a stone guesthouse among the pines, and a lamp burns in its window though it is barely dusk. Arnrád stops on the path and looks at the building for a long moment. Every shutter on it is fastened from the outside.

## Scene One: The Open Door

**Purpose.** Bring the party under the keeper's roof, and make his hospitality the first thing that is wrong.

**Situation.** The guesthouse is the old post's gatehouse and barracks range, roofed in bark and turf, around a yard with a stable and a stream that runs through a stone channel the legions cut. The keeper opens the door as the party crosses the yard, before anyone has knocked. He offers a fire, a pot of venison, dry beds and stabling, and he asks, kindly, who knows the party's road: who sent them, who expects them at Waldburg, and when. The shutters, he says, are fastened against the wind off the highland. That is true of the wind.

**NPCs and threats.** **Óthwin** is courteous, tired, and listens harder than he talks. He will not go into the cellar while guests are awake. **Sundwyn yra Eichrúnd wur Erthhaven**, a thin young woman who carries the bowls, interrupts him at the table to tell the party, too loudly, that the road is full of Wolves and they must keep their weapons by them even here. Óthwin sends her to the kitchen. She came to Dunkelholt two summers ago with a merchant party and stayed when it left, or so the keeper says.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Seize the keeper and demand answers. He says nothing that matters and asks to be let go before dark, because he has a wife to see to.
- **Stealth.** Search the yard before supper: the stable, the woodshed, the icehouse dug into the bank.
- **Talk.** Answer the keeper's questions, or lie to him. A party that tells him someone at Waldburg expects it on a given day watches him go quiet and count.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the post itself: the gatehouse bars are on the outside of the guest doors, and new iron hasps hold the shutters.
- **Giving something up.** Refuse the roof and camp in the pines, which is exactly where the Choir hunts.

**Clues and rewards.** The rope ends at the span were cut, not frayed, and the woodshed holds a stack of new planks and coils of fresh rope enough to lay the span again. The stable holds the harness of far more beasts than it has stalls, the brass of several merchant houses on it. In the icehouse, under hides, lies a dead hound with a man's hands for forefeet and a band of signs cut around its chest, slashed through with a knife; it is the size of a calf and quite still.

**Consequences.** A party that answers the keeper's questions truthfully tells him how long he has before anyone comes looking. A party that lies well buys a night. The hound is the first thing the party sees that has no ordinary explanation.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that camps in the pines hears its own words repeated back from the trees after dark, in voices that are not its own, and something heavy circles the camp without crossing the stream the party has camped beside. Sundwyn comes out at full dark with bread and the same warning, and begs them inside. A party that kills or binds the keeper at once finds the cellar door locked from beneath and Sundwyn too frightened to say why, and the night of Scene Three begins without him.

## Scene Two: The Stripped Beds

**Purpose.** Show the party what has happened to everyone who stayed here before them.

**Situation.** The guest rooms are the old barracks loft, up a stair with a door at its foot. The loft has eight beds. Seven have been stripped to the frames. The eighth still has its mattress, and the ticking along both edges is torn in rows of four, the width of a hand, where someone held on. In the eaves, under a loose board, is the luggage of the parties before: packs, saddlebags and strongboxes with the marks of several Moktur merchant houses, the tally-cords of Vandgrund guides, and a Varokh fur party's bundles.

**NPCs and threats.** **Arnrád** finds his brother's pack in the eaves. He knows it by a knot on the strap, and he goes very quiet. **Sundwyn** comes up with water and, once the door is shut, talks: the keeper's kin at Vandgrund send word of every party they guide north, the keeper cuts the bridge when one is due so that nobody can turn back, and the man below, whom she calls the Master, buys the travelers from Óthwin with his wife's life. She holds the lamp at the Master's table because her hands are steady, and she knows the way the legions dug from the cellars to the river.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Break down the cellar door now. It is oak bound in Vylarian iron, and the noise brings the keeper with an axe and wakes what is below.
- **Stealth.** Wait for the house to sleep, and go down by Sundwyn's way.
- **Talk.** Get the whole story from Sundwyn, which costs her the keeper's trust if he hears of it, or confront the keeper with the pack, which costs Arnrád his patience.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the luggage. Among it is a Moktur factor's day-book in Sêmarion script, its last pages written here.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the luggage and its silver where it is, so that the keeper finds nothing disturbed.

**Clues and rewards.** The factor's last entries record that the keeper asked twice who knew their road, that one of his men saw something big standing at the stable stream at night and not crossing it, and that a voice called the factor's clerk by name from the yard after the clerk had gone missing. Arnrád, asked about it, says the tellings of the hrág hold that it will not cross running water, and that he never believed them. The luggage holds a modest weight of aurions and amber the party can take now.

**Consequences.** Arnrád stops being a guide and becomes a brother. From here he will go toward any voice that sounds like Arnulf's, and he is right that one of them is. A party that confronts Óthwin forces him to choose between his guests and his wife, and he chooses his wife.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never climbs to the loft is put there anyway at bedtime, and finds the torn mattress on the only bed the keeper has made up for it. If the party never wins Sundwyn's trust, she slips the factor's day-book under a door during the night, and the clues of the day-book reach the party that way. If Arnrád is lost or left behind, the brother's pack is still in the eaves, and the voice of Scene Three uses his name instead.

## Scene Three: The Voice at the Stable

**Purpose.** The horror comes inside. The party learns it is penned in, and that something in the cellar is coming up.

**Situation.** Deep in the night a voice in the yard calls Arnrád by name and asks him to come down and help with the horses, one of them is cast in its stall. It is Arnrád's own voice, or near enough that only Arnrád knows the difference: it is his brother's. Below the dining room, at the same time, something starts up the cellar steps, slowly, one step and a long drag, one step and a long drag. Óthwin, on the landing, is pulling the loft door shut and setting the bar across it from the outside.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Choir** stands in the yard by the stream, a head taller than a tall man, its body joined from several bodies at seams that do not quite match, with mouths opening along its shoulders and throat. It repeats what the mouths last said: the factor's clerk asking who is there, a Wolf cursing, a woman calling for a child, Arnulf asking for help with the horses. What comes up the cellar steps is **Athalrún**, Óthwin's wife, alive, her own body from the waist up and the rest of her somebody else's, which she cannot yet make walk. She is coming up because she heard voices and she has not heard any but her husband's in two winters.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Break the loft door, or go through the roof turf and drop into the yard. Fighting the Choir in the open, in the dark, is the hardest thing the party can choose tonight.
- **Stealth.** Keep still and let the voice call. The Choir does not come in; it waits.
- **Talk.** Speak to Óthwin through the door, or to Athalrún on the stair. She is lucid, and she wants to die, and she asks the party to make her husband stop.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Draw the Choir to the stream and stand on the far side of it, where it will not follow.
- **Giving something up.** Let Arnrád go to the voice, or hold him back by force and lose his trust for the rest of the night.

**Clues and rewards.** At the stream the Choir stops dead and paces the edge, which proves the day-book right. Up close, a ring of signs is cut around its middle, the same as the dead hound's, and the party can see where a knife would have to go. Athalrún knows that her husband keeps the cellar key on his belt and that the Master works behind a door marked with a sign that burns the hand that touches it.

**Consequences.** If Arnrád reaches the voice, the Choir takes him and carries him down into the cellar, and he is alive on the Master's table in Scene Four. If the party kills Athalrún, as she asks, Óthwin turns on the party with everything he has; if the party spares her and carries her with it, he opens the cellar for them. Either way there is no way back across the gorge, the shutters are fastened, and the Choir is between the house and the pines.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** Every path from here leads down. If the Choir takes anyone, its trail of blood runs to the cellar door, which it leaves open behind it. If the party breaks out of the loft and runs for the pines, the Choir drives it back toward the walls, and Sundwyn is waiting at the woodshed with a lamp, at the head of the sally passage. A party that holds the loft until dawn finds the yard empty and the cellar door open, and goes down knowing someone was taken in the night.

## Scene Four: The Warded Room

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the mage and his greatest work, and the revelation of what the house has been for.

**Situation.** The cellars are the legions' strongroom, long vaulted chambers under the granary, lamplit and clean. The Choir stands in the outer vault. Beyond it, behind a door with a ward-sign cut into its lintel, is the Master's operating chamber: a table with straps, racks of instruments, jars, a brazier, and a second table where the night's taken traveler lies awake and bound. **Sîrêmôs** sits by the brazier, a narrow old man wearing a young man's hands, one eye blue and the other brown, his throat stitched where a fresh run of skin has been laid in. He is courteous, he is in pain, and he talks while he works.

The sally passage enters the operating chamber from below through a drain grate under the second table. The runoff from the yard stream runs down it to the river.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Choir** guards the outer vault and obeys the mage's word. **Sîrêmôs** is no fighter. His ward burns the hand that opens his door, and he has two smaller servants, joined from dogs and the parts of men, that wait under the tables. He bargains readily: he can close any wound the party carries tonight, and he can make Athalrún walk. Killing him does not stop his works. A construct does what it was last told for as long as its signs hold, and the last thing he tells the Choir, if he sees death coming, is to keep the house.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight through the Choir in the outer vault, at the cost of whatever its arms close on.
- **Stealth.** Come up through the drain grate by Sundwyn's way, under the ward and behind the Choir, which never enters the passage because water runs in it.
- **Talk.** Bargain with Sîrêmôs for the captive's life, or for Athalrún's death, or turn Óthwin against him by making the keeper see what his wife has become.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Lure the Choir into the runoff channel, where it stops; or break the ring of signs while it stands still.
- **Giving something up.** Let one of the party hold the Choir's attention, so another can reach the signs.

**Clues and rewards.** The mouths of the Choir are the travelers who stayed here. When Arnrád hears his brother's voice from one of them saying the last thing Arnulf said, the guide knows him, and so does the party.

**Met prepared.** A party that knows about the signs, the water and the passage can come up through the grate, reach the mage before the Choir turns, and cut the Choir's ring while it stands in the channel or at the door it was told to hold. When the ring is broken, every mouth closes at once, and it falls.

**Met unprepared.** A party that comes down the stair knowing none of it meets the Choir head on. It can still survive by retreating into the sally passage, where the Choir will not follow, and coming up again under the table, or by fleeing down the passage to the river, where the mage's boat is moored. The mage's death does not end the fight; the Choir keeps the house.

**Consequences.** A party that breaks the Choir's signs frees its mouths; they say nothing more. A party that kills the mage and leaves the Choir standing leaves it to hunt the near bank forever, held by its last order. A party that bargains with Sîrêmôs and lets him live leaves him at work.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party driven out of the cellars by the Choir finds itself in the sally passage, which ends at the river and the mage's boat. It can escape by water now and come back by daylight, when the mage sleeps and the Choir stands motionless in the outer vault until it is called. A party that never reaches the operating chamber hears the captive screaming from it, which brings it back.

## Scene Five: The Strongroom and the River

**Purpose.** Pay the reward, and get the party out before the next span is laid and the next party crosses it.

**Situation.** Off the operating chamber, behind an iron door the legions hung, is the strongroom. Its key is on the mage's belt or Óthwin's. Inside are the goods of every party that stayed at Dunkelholt, sorted and kept, because the mage sells them south through Vandgrund to pay for his materials.

**NPCs and threats.** If the Choir still stands, it keeps the house, and the party has to leave by water or by the Choir's leave. If Óthwin lives, he can be talked out of his post or left to it. His kin at Vandgrund are already guiding the next party north.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Carry out everything the boat will hold.
- **Stealth.** Take only what is easily carried, and leave the rest for whoever comes.
- **Talk.** Persuade Óthwin to go back to Vandgrund and tell the elders what his kin have been doing.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Burn the cellars behind the party, so nobody works there again.
- **Giving something up.** Return the furs to the Moktur house that lost them, or the brother's pack to his village.

**Clues and rewards.** Bags of silver aurions, sorted by the house they came from; amber in rough lumps from the Varokh fur parties; bales of prime marten and sable pelts for the river trade; and the mage's own manuscripts in a cedar chest, his surgical notes and the workings he used on the dead, each worth a great deal to the Sekran and dangerous to anyone else who reads them. A riverboat, the mage's own, is moored at the foot of the sally passage, big enough for the party and its goods and whoever it rescued.

**Consequences.** The manuscripts draw the Sekran, which pays for them and then asks who else has read them. The furs belong to houses that will pay to have them back. A party that leaves the cellars intact leaves them for the next mage who needs a quiet place.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that escaped by the river in Scene Four without the strongroom can come back by boat on a later day, through the sally passage, with the mage gone or asleep; the strongroom key is on the mage's belt or on Óthwin's, and Sundwyn knows where both men sleep.

## Aftermath

- **If the Choir was broken and the mage killed.** The road north of the passes is quiet, and the villages of Vandstein go on calling what hunted it a hrág. Arnrád takes his brother's pack home to Thrástein.
- **If the mage lives.** He moves farther north and starts again, and a party that crossed him is the first thing he looks for.
- **If the Choir still stands.** It keeps the house it was told to keep, and the near bank of the gorge has a hunter on it at night that no village can name.
- **If Óthwin went home.** The Vandgrund elders call a reckoning on the guides who sent parties north, and the families concerned are the same ones the War Chief fears to drive away.
- **If the manuscripts went to the Sekran.** The Ordo pays well, and opens a file on the party.
