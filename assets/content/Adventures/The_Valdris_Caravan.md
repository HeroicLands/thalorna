Three weeks ago a shipment of [Merchant House Valdris]{.name data-type="affiliation/venture"}, eight wagons of the house's most valuable goods, went into [[place-ravenswood|Ravenswood]] on a road [[being-orshkrnlkt|Órashkar Naulakit]] has driven a hundred times, and it never came out the other side. Nobody has found a wheel, a body or a broken harness. The house blames the [Ravenswood Brigands]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"}, who have hunted Órashkar's loads ever since he refused them their tribute. Órashkar searched the road himself and found nothing a band of brigands leaves behind. Now the house that was to receive the load has taken its loss to an arbitrator, and the hearing sits in Krisamar in ten days. House Valdris means to answer it by naming the brigands, because a load taken by raiders on the agreed road is a loss the two houses share, and it has hired armed men to go into the wood and take its goods back from them.

## Overview {#overview}

The adventure runs over ten days, from the morning Órashkar decides to ride the road again to the hearing at the arbitrator's table in [[place-chrysamar|Krisamar]]. Its clock is the hearing. The arbitrator asks first what took the load, and the answer decides who bears the loss: both houses, House Valdris alone, or a third party. Before the hearing sits, the house's hired guards go into Ravenswood after the brigands, which puts the house's own charter at risk, and on the eighth night the moon is dark again, as it was the night the caravan vanished, and the trail in the hills goes cold.

This is a disappearance with three possible answers, and the scenario does not choose among them. The convoy left the road inside the wood, at a turning somebody hid. Where it went from there is the Game Master's choice: [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]], a rival house, or the dangers of the mountain country. Each explanation has its own clues, written below as unplaced hooks. The Game Master picks one before play and places only its clues, and the party finds the turning and the hill track whichever is true.

**The terms.** The arbitrator's scribe reads the terms aloud before anyone argues, and they set four outcomes:

- A load taken by raiders while the convoy keeps to the agreed road is the road's loss, and the two houses share it.
- A load lost after the convoy leaves the agreed road by its own master's choice, with no raider and no fraud putting it off, is the loss of the house that sent it.
- A load lost by fraud is the loss of whoever committed the fraud.
- A load whose loss has no cause shown is the loss of the house that sent it.

Where two outcomes seem to fit, the cause that put the convoy off the road decides: a convoy forced off it by raiders is the road's loss, and a convoy sent off it by a fraud is the fraud's loss, even though its master turned the wagons himself.

**What each party to the loss wants:**

| The Ravenswood Brigands                         | to be left alone in their wood, and paid by the teamsters who pay                  | the house's armed men, and the thing in the wood that takes their sentries     |

**The guards and the charter.** The factor's contract sends armed guards into Ravenswood to take the house's goods back from the brigands, and he calls it recovery. Private revenge voids a Bizarian charter. The brigands do not have the goods, in any version, so a ride against them is revenge on men who took nothing, and the consignee's factor says so at the hearing. A party that stops the guards saves the house from its own factor.

The clock assumes the waystation lies about two days' ride from Krisamar and a morning's drive for loaded wagons short of the wood, a shorter ride on horseback, the fork in the hills half a day's climb from the road, and, for a rival house, the market town half a day past the fork. The Game Master sets the real distances and moves the days to match, keeping their order.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

### What is true in every version

**The convoy.** Eight wagons of House Valdris left the waystation at dawn under the house's convoy master, with drivers of the [Teamsters' Guild]{.name data-type="affiliation/guild"} and the guards the house hired for the road. Órashkar planned the route and did not ride with it. Whether any of the young teamsters he trains drove in it is the Game Master's choice. None of the convoy has come home.

**The turning.** The convoy reached the wood about midday, and brigand sentries on the wood's edge saw it go in. It never passed their camp, farther along the road, and the brigands took it to have stopped for the night. That night was the dark of the moon, and the brigands keep to their fires on dark nights. Between the wood's edge and the camp, a gully track runs off the road through the holly, and the convoy took it. Somebody cut brush and dragged it across the turning behind the last wagon. Órashkar rode past it on his own search, looking for wreckage on the road, and did not see it.

**The hill track.** The track climbs out of the wood up the gully and into the hills, and in the hills it forks. Wagons leave ruts in the gully that three weeks of weather have softened and not erased.

**What did not take it.** The Ravenswood Brigands did not take the convoy, in any version, and they did not see where it went. The [[being-shdwstlk|Shadowstalker]] of Ravenswood is not the cause in any version: it hunts the brigands, it does not leave the wood, and it has never touched a caravan or a teamster. The teamsters' argument about it is part of the road, and the spirit itself is in the deep wood after dark.

### The three explanations

The Game Master chooses one of these before play. Only the chosen explanation's clues are placed; the other two stay rumors in the inns, and the party can follow them as far as the evidence the Game Master has placed allows. Nothing in the setting names one of them as true.

**A. The Black Flame.** Two guards joined the convoy at the waystation the night before it left, in place of two of the house's hired guards who had fallen out over pay. They were followers of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]], whose faith has a following among caravan guards and hill bands in the eastern country. At the turning a warband of the faith waited in the holly, and the two guards drew their blades on the convoy master from behind. There was no struggle on the road because the drivers and the other guards, surrounded, did as they were told. The warband hid the turning and made the drivers drive the wagons up the gully track into the hills, and it holds them alive. On the night of the theft, the dark of the moon, it kept the Night of Ashes at the fork and burned the house's seals and manifests in its bonfire. On the eighth night, at the next dark of the moon, it burns the wagons that carry the house's marks, loads the goods onto its own mules, and moves on toward the eastern hinterland with the load and the drivers. Under the terms this is raiders on the agreed road: the convoy left it at blade-point.

**B. A rival house.** A rider met the convoy master at the waystation the night before it left, carrying an order under the seal of House Valdris. The seal was counterfeit. The order told the convoy master to leave the road inside the wood by the gully track, to hide the turning against the brigands, and to take the load over the hills to a market town, where the house's buyer would meet it, and to send no word home until the buyer came, so that the brigands would think the load lost. The convoy master knew the seal and obeyed it. At the town's gate the agent of a rival house met the convoy as the buyer's man, showed the order's countersign, and took the wagons and the load into a warehouse in the merchants' quarter on the order's authority. He paid the convoy master and the drivers, in House Valdris's name, to wait at an inn by the drovers' gate for the buyer's payment and their release. From the warehouse the goods go out with the house's marks scraped off and the rival's seal on the bales, sold in a quarter of the town the drivers have no reason to visit. They are at the inn, alive, waiting for the word, and the convoy master believes he is obeying his house and can describe the man he handed the load to. On the eighth night the rival's agent sends what is left of the load on to the east, so that the last of the house's goods leave the market, and tells the drivers that the house releases them; they scatter to find work on the eastern roads. Under the terms this is fraud.

**C. The dangers of the mountains.** The convoy master knew the brigands were waiting for any load of Órashkar's, and he took the gully track to go round their camp by the hills, a way Órashkar never drives. His own drivers hid the turning behind them so that the brigands could not follow. The mountain country took the convoy on the track. Whether a band of the mountain marauders who raid the wagons on the hill roads fell on it past the fork and pitched the emptied wagons into a gully, or the ground itself took it, in a flood down a gully or a slide on the slope, is the Game Master's choice. The wagons lie in a gully in the hills where nobody on the road can see them, and the dead lie with them or in the herders' graves. Any survivors are hurt, and they are held by the band or sheltered by the hill herders, as the Game Master chooses; the herders send no one down to a road the brigands hold. On the eighth night the band moves on with what it took, or the herders send the survivors down another road. Under the terms this is a convoy that left the agreed road by its master's choice.

> Choose before play, and keep the choice. Every scene below gives the clues common to all three explanations and the clues of each one; place only the chosen explanation's clues. The party can be wrong, and the hearing can rule on a wrong answer.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal has two halves, and the party finds them in order:

- **The convoy left the road.** The brigand sentries, the hidden turning and the ruts in the gully prove it. This clears the brigands, and it is enough to stop the house's armed guards, if the guards' captain is shown the turning before they ride on the seventh day.
- **What took it in the hills.** The chosen explanation's evidence proves it, and only that evidence decides the hearing.

The first half cuts against the house's case. A convoy that left the road by its own choice is the house's loss alone, so the house's factor wants the turning kept quiet and the party to bring him raiders. A party that brings the turning alone gives the arbitrator a convoy that left the road and nothing to say why. The factor then asks why Órashkar's route took a convoy through a wood where it can vanish, and the hearing turns on the teamster.

### What the party wants afterward

Once it knows, the party holds the one account that decides the hearing. The house wants the loss shared or moved, and the turning forgotten. The consignee wants its price. Órashkar wants the truth about his road, and his protégés home. The brigands want the house's guards out of their wood, and the party is the only body that can make the house believe they did nothing.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

The scenario fixes the turning, the hill track and the terms, and leaves these to the table:

- **What took the convoy.** The Black Flame, a rival house, or the mountains. The setting's own notes say nobody can answer, and the scenario keeps that: choose one for this table, and place only its clues.
- **For the Black Flame: where the warband comes from.** The faith is strong in the hinterland towns of the [[place-eastrnmrch|Eastern March]] and the holds of the high passes, and the scenario names no warband and no Flame-Warden. A party that reaches the warband's camp before the eighth night finds the drivers there; after it, the warband has taken them east, and whether they live is the Game Master's choice.
- **For a rival house: which house, and why.** A house that wants Valdris's trade, a house that wants Órashkar's name ruined so that Valdris loses its teamster, or a house that simply wanted the load are all supported by the clues. One candidate is in the notes: Merchant [Castellan Thorne]{.name data-type="being/npc"} works to ruin Órashkar's reputation, and the [Merchant House of Thorne]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} is a powerful house that the notes tie to a killing the official records clear it of. Whether Castellan Thorne belongs to that house, and whether either is behind the fraud, is the Game Master's choice. The agent who sold the load is a house factor's man and nobody the notes name. Which market town lies beyond the hills is the Game Master's choice.
- **For the mountains: what took it.** A band of marauders, a flood in a gully, or a slide. With a band, there is a fight to be had past the fork and goods to recover; with the ground, there are only the dead to bring home and the survivors the herders kept.
- **Whether Órashkar's protégés drove in the convoy.** The scenario runs either way. If they did, Órashkar rides at a pace his stamina does not bear, and he falls behind in Scene Three unless the party slows for him.
- **The scenario's assumptions.** The convoy is eight wagons, and the night of the theft was the dark of the moon. The house's town and the consignee are not named, and the hearing sits in Krisamar, Órashkar's town, where the house's factor keeps its business with him. The four outcomes of the terms are the scenario's, and so is the ruling that the guards' ride is private revenge. The waystation a morning short of the wood, the gully track, the fork and the market town past it are the scenario's, not fixed features of Ravenswood. A Game Master who changes any of these keeps the chain by keeping a hidden turning inside the wood and a hearing that needs a cause.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The trader: the old teamster.** Órashkar asks the party to ride the road with him, because his eyes missed something on the first search and he knows it.
- **The courtier: a witness for the guild.** [[being-zhambelinake|Zhambelina Keramakit]], head of the Teamsters' Guild, wants someone who is not a teamster to see the road, so that the guild can stand behind Órashkar at the hearing with more than its loyalty.
- **The warrior: the house's guards.** The house's factor hires the party among the armed guards who ride into Ravenswood on the seventh day, and the party rides east ahead of them to see what it has been hired to fight.
- **The scholar: a fee to settle it.** Priests of the faiths of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian Pantheon]] have offered, for a fee, to settle whether the shadowstalker took the convoy. One of them hires the party as an escort into the wood, and what the party finds there answers the question better than the rite.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The warehouses of the Krisamar harborfront shut their doors at dusk, and the old man's workshop behind them is the last light on the lane. Harness hangs from every beam, and a wagon wheel lies on the bench with its iron off. Órashkar Naulakit sits beside it with his walking stick across his knees and a map of the eastern road open in front of him, held flat by two horseshoes.
>
> "I drove that road a hundred times," he says, without looking up. "When the wagons did not come in, I rode it again, all the way through the wood and back, and I found nothing. Eight wagons do not go into the air. So I missed something, or I am too old to see it. I mean to know which."

> Open on the map. Órashkar has drawn the road through Ravenswood from memory, every bend and gully, and the turning is not on it.

## Scene One: Órashkar's Table

**Purpose.** The commission. Put the party in Krisamar with the people who need the answer, and give it the stakes and the clock.

**Situation.** Órashkar has decided to ride the road again and asks the party to ride with him. The house's factor has told the Teamsters' Guild that the house is reviewing his service, and the hearing sits in ten days.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **[[being-orshkrnlkt|Órashkar Naulakit]]**, the League's most dependable teamster, in his last working years, measured and dry, who trusts people of good name and has never left the road in Ravenswood.
- **[[being-zhambelinake|Zhambelina Keramakit]]**, head of the Teamsters' Guild, who keeps the guild's roll of the drivers in the convoy and values Órashkar's counsel.
- **[[being-bruzanazghig|Bruzanaz Ghigidek]]**, one of the League's major traders, whose family Órashkar helped when she was young, and who is loyal to him.
- **The house's factor**, who runs House Valdris's business in Krisamar and believes the brigands took the load.

**Player choices.**

- _Ask Órashkar:_ the convoy's size, its master, its guards, and the route.
- _Read the guild's roll:_ Zhambelina shows the party the drivers' names and their masters.
- _Call on the factor:_ hear the house's case, the terms, the hearing's date and the guards' contract.
- _Ask Bruzanaz:_ she trades in the same goods and hears where they are sold.

**Clues and rewards.** The convoy's size, eight wagons, and its master, in every version. The terms, from the factor, who reads them as a reason to name the brigands. The factor's contract with the armed guards, and its date. Then only the chosen version's:

- **The Black Flame (A).** Two of the house's hired guards quit over pay at the waystation the night before the convoy left, and the convoy master hired two others there. The two who quit came back to Krisamar and complained of it in the teamsters' inns, where Órashkar heard it and thought nothing of it.
- **A rival house (B).** Bruzanaz has heard that goods of the house's kind are offered in a market town beyond the hills under another house's seal, and she took it for a coincidence.
- **The mountains (C).** Órashkar says the convoy master asked him once, years ago, whether the hill tracks round Ravenswood were passable, and that he told him to stay on the road.

**Consequences.** The factor agrees that the guards' captain holds his men if he is shown proof that the convoy left the road before they ride on the seventh day. Bruzanaz offers to buy back anything of the house's the party can find, at her own risk. Zhambelina warns the party, quietly, that a house which sends armed men after the wrong people stakes its charter on it.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party hired by the factor or the priests meets Órashkar on the road east, riding alone at a pace that is too much for him.

## Scene Two: The Wood Road

**Purpose.** The turning. Take the party into Ravenswood and let it find what Órashkar missed.

**Situation.** From the waystation the road crosses open country for a morning and runs into the oaks and holly. The brigands' camp lies along it, farther in, with a great fire burning day and night and sentries in pairs with their backs to it. The turning is between the wood's edge and the camp.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The waystation keeper**, who saw the convoy leave at dawn three weeks ago and remembers who came and went the night before.
- **Ravenswood brigands**, nervous, fewer than they were, and furious at being blamed. They demand tribute from the party as from any load, and double it if Órashkar rides with it.
- **The [[being-shdwstlk|Shadowstalker]]**, in the deep wood after dark, hunting brigands, never two in a night. It has never touched a caravan or a teamster. A party that camps by a brigand fire sees it take a sentry.

**Player choices.**

- _Ask the waystation keeper:_ who joined the convoy, who called on it, and when it left.
- _Parley with the brigands:_ they swear they never saw the convoy pass their camp, and their sentries on the wood's edge saw it go in about midday.
- _Search the road by daylight:_ a careful search between the edge and the camp finds cut brush dragged across a gully mouth, gone brown, and the ruts behind it.
- _Go into the wood at night:_ meet the shadowstalker, or the brigands' sentries, or both.

**Clues and rewards.** The brigands' sentries and their account, and the turning, in every version. From the keeper: two guards who joined the convoy at the waystation, one of whom washed at the trough with his shirt off and had a burn on his shoulder in the shape of a clenched fist (A); a rider who came the night before with a sealed order for the convoy master and left before dawn (B); the convoy master's question, the night before, about the hill tracks round the wood (C).

**Consequences.** A party that finds the turning holds the first half of the reveal. Shown to the guards' captain before the seventh day, it stops the house's guards; the party can show him itself, or leave Órashkar at the waystation to do it while it climbs into the hills. Órashkar, shown the turning, stands over it a long time and says nothing.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that misses the turning hears from the brigand sentries that the convoy never passed their camp, and the stretch of road it can have left is a short one. A search of it by daylight finds the turning.

## Scene Three: The Hill Track

**Purpose.** The answer. Follow the track out of the wood to the fork, and find what the chosen explanation left there.

**Situation.** The gully track climbs out of the wood into the hills, and the ruts lead to a fork half a day's climb from the road. What waits at the fork and beyond it depends on the explanation the Game Master chose. Hill herders graze the slopes along the way.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Hill herders**, who saw wagons on the track three weeks ago and want nothing to do with whatever came after.
- **The chosen explanation's people**: the warband of the Black Flame and its captive drivers (A); the drivers waiting in the market town half a day past the fork, and the rival's agent (B); a band of mountain marauders, or nobody but the dead and the herders who sheltered the living (C).

**Player choices.**

- _Follow the ruts:_ to the fork, and on.
- _Ask the herders:_ what they saw, and what they heard at night.
- _Watch before acting:_ a party that watches the camp or the market for a few hours learns how many it faces.
- _Fight, bargain or free:_ depending on what is there.

**Clues and rewards.**

- **The Black Flame (A).** At the fork, a great ring of ash with half-burned wax seals in it bearing the house's mark, and the warband's camp a few hours on, where the drivers are held and the wagons stand. The herders saw a bonfire on the hill on the dark-moon night. Before the eighth night the wagons still stand in the camp with the house's marks on them; after it they are ash, and the warband has gone east with the load on its mules and the drivers with it.
- **A rival house (B).** The ruts run past the fork to the market town, where the drivers wait at the inn by the drovers' gate for the buyer's payment, and the convoy master carries the counterfeit order in his coat and describes the buyer's man who took the load. In the merchants' quarter the house's goods are for sale from a warehouse with its marks scraped off and another house's seal on the bales, and the house's wagons stand in the warehouse yard. Before the eighth night the bales are there to be seen and the drivers are in the inn; after it the bales have gone east, only the sale is recorded, and the drivers have scattered.
- **The mountains (C).** The ruts run past the fork and stop. A search of the gullies beyond finds the wagons at the bottom of one, smashed or plundered as the Game Master chose. With a band, the band's camp holds the goods and any captives; with the ground, the herders have the hurt survivors and have buried the dead. Before the eighth night the band is at its camp and the survivors are where they were taken; after it the band has moved on, and the herders have sent any survivors they kept down another road.

**Consequences.** Whatever the party brings away is its evidence at the hearing: drivers who can speak, the counterfeit order, wagons, seals, goods. Órashkar, if his protégés are among the living, sees them home before anything else.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that reaches the fork after the eighth night finds the trail cold and only one witness the Game Master chooses: a driver who slipped away, a herder who saw it all, or a bale left behind in the market. One witness is enough to tell the story and not always enough to prove it.

## Scene Four: The Arbitrator's Table

**Purpose.** The ruling. Bring the account before the arbitrator and let the terms decide who bears the loss.

**Situation.** The hearing sits in Krisamar on the tenth day. The arbitrator's scribe reads the terms aloud before anyone argues. House Valdris's factor and the consignee's factor sit on either side. The arbitrator asks first what took the load, and then whether the house's guards rode.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The arbitrator**, named by both houses, whose standing rests on being thought fair, and who asks for the terms before a name.
- **The house's factor**, who means to name the brigands, or raiders of any kind if the party can show them, and wants nothing said of a convoy that left the road by choice.
- **The consignee's factor**, who wants the price, doubts any account that shares the loss, and names the guards' ride private revenge if they rode.
- **Órashkar**, who is asked whether his route was sound, and answers it plainly.
- **Bruzanaz Ghigidek**, who testifies to the sale beyond the hills if the party asks her (B).

**Player choices.**

- _Tell it all:_ the turning and the hills, with the evidence brought back.
- _Tell the turning only:_ clear the brigands, and leave the hills unproven.
- _Keep something back:_ for the house, for Órashkar, or for a price. In the mountains version, keeping back the turning keeps the house's claim of raiders alive and leaves Órashkar's route in question.
- _Stay silent:_ let the factor name the brigands.

**Clues and rewards.** None left. The reward is the ruling.

**Consequences.** The arbitrator's ruling, by what the party shows:

- **The Black Flame proved.** Raiders forced the convoy off the agreed road, so the two houses share the loss. Órashkar's route was sound and his name is clear, and the house keeps him. The ruling goes to the League's officers of the Eastern March as word of a warband working the eastern road.
- **A rival house proved.** The loss is a fraud's, and falls on the rival house. The arbitrator sends the counterfeit seal to the guilds, and the rival's agent is struck from the roll, his contracts void. House Valdris keeps Órashkar and owes the party.
- **The mountains proved.** The convoy master left the agreed road by his own choice, against Órashkar's advice, so the whole loss falls on House Valdris. Órashkar's route was sound, and the house keeps him.
- **The turning proved, the hills not.** The brigands are cleared. The convoy left the road and no cause is shown, so the whole loss falls on House Valdris. The factor asks why the house's teamster planned a road on which a convoy can vanish, and the house keeps Órashkar only on the factor's word, until the cause is known.
- **Nothing proved.** The factor names the brigands, the arbitrator asks for proof, and there is none. No cause is shown, so the whole loss falls on House Valdris, and the house ends Órashkar's service after twenty years.
- **The guards rode.** Whatever else is ruled, if the guards went into the wood on the seventh day, the arbitrator names their ride private revenge against men who did not take the load. The ruling on the loss stands. House Valdris's charter is suspended and the house's other contracts are void, Órashkar's service among them, until the house makes its peace with the guilds.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** The hearing sits without the party; see If the Party Does Nothing.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- The house's armed guards ride into Ravenswood on the seventh day, burn a brigand camp and lose men to the brigands in the holly. The road through the wood closes to every teamster for the rest of the season.
- On the eighth night the trail in the hills goes cold, and nobody looks for it.
- At the hearing the factor names the brigands and has no proof. The whole loss falls on House Valdris, and the arbitrator names the guards' ride private revenge. The house's charter is suspended and its other contracts are void.
- The house ends Órashkar's service after twenty years, and the Teamsters' Guild hears that the League's most dependable teamster lost eight wagons on his own road.
- The brigands take their losses out on the road, and on any load with Órashkar's mark.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **If the drivers came home.** The guild has its drivers back, and Órashkar's protégés, if they drove, are back in his workshop. Órashkar owes the party, and he pays his debts in work and counsel.
- **If the Black Flame was found.** A warband of the faith knows the party's faces and has lost a load. Órashkar is of the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]], and its order asks the party what it saw.
- **If a rival house was found.** A house of the League has lost a factor's man to the roll and its name in the guilds, and it knows who brought the seal to the table.
- **If the mountains took it.** The dead are brought home or lie in the herders' graves, and the convoy master's choice is the guild's lesson for its apprentices: in Ravenswood, a teamster stays on the road.
- **If the brigands were cleared.** The brigands owe the party something they do not like owing, and the vendetta with Órashkar stands.
- **If the guards were stopped.** House Valdris keeps its charter because the party showed the turning in time, and its factor knows it.
- **If the house's guards fought in the wood.** The brigands are fewer and angrier, every teamster on the eastern road pays for it, and House Valdris cannot trade under its charter until the guilds restore it.
