At the first frost the men of the [[affiliation-ghtkrtrb|Ghotakur]] leave their winter valleys at the forest's edge and go north into [[place-gwtkrrgn|Ghotaqor]] to mend the wall, and the valleys are held by women, boys and old men until the thaw. This autumn a camp of the [[affiliation-svghrtrb|Savaghur]], broken by last year's ice winter, has come to the mouth of a Ghotakur valley with too few beasts to live through the cold on its own ground. The two tribes have been at feud over these valleys for three generations, and the last time a Savaghur camp came into this valley the Ghotakur drove it out into the snow. The Savaghur camp head has not come to raid. He has come to end the feud, and he does not know how to ask.

## Overview {#overview}

The adventure runs over the weeks from the first frost to the first snow that closes the grass, on the northern grass of the [[place-hskrrgn|Ghoshiqor]] where it meets the forest. Its clock is the cold. Every night is colder, the Savaghur camp's beasts are dying at the valley mouth, and once the snow lies the camp either has shelter or does not.

This is a quarrel between two tribes over ground both need, with a third tribe offering a way out and a stranger's banner offering another. There is no villain. The valley-mother holding the valley is right that her tribe cannot lose it; the Savaghur camp head is right that his camp dies without it. What the party can change is whether they find an ending the custom allows before the snow or the young riders decide for them.

**The ways the quarrel can end**, and what each needs:

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

[Savaghjilaq Savaghur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, head of the Savaghur camp, lost half his beasts in the ice winter and more this summer. His own valley is open to the north wind and will not carry what he has left through another hard winter. He has brought the camp to the mouth of the Ghotakur valley to ask for shelter as guests. He cannot ask: while blood is owed between two kindreds, a man of one does not eat in the tent of the other, and guest-right begins with salt. The only roads out of a feud are a payment of horses or a marriage, and he has no horses.

His young riders have a third road. The riders of a dovaq gathering the steppe under his banner have offered the Savaghur herds to replace the dead ones if the tribe swears, and the young men say a camp that took the Ghotakur valley would bring the man something worth the herds. They are waiting for the first snow and for Savaghjilaq to fail.

In the valley, [Jiboqlun Ghotakur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the valley-mother, sees a Savaghur camp at her valley mouth the week her men left, as it was in the ice winter. She has boys, old men, a few women who can shoot, and the memory of the last time.

North at the wall, a long stretch that fell last winter has fallen further, and [Ghotaqtoliz Ghotakur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the dovaq, has sent a man back to the valleys for every boy who can carry a stone.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Play the custom as hard ground, not as an obstacle the party can argue away. Nobody in either camp breaks it, and the party's offer of a meal shared across the feud is refused politely and finally by both sides. The scenario is about finding the ending the custom allows.

### How the turn lands

The turn comes when the party understands what Savaghjilaq wants. It lands at whichever scene the party first speaks with him, and it lands hardest if the party has already armed the valley against him. From then on the question is no longer how to keep the Savaghur out, but whether the valley-mother can be brought to take them in on terms the custom allows, before the young riders move.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the turn lands, the party is a go-between, the one role nobody in either camp can take. It needs a match, a witness, and Jiboqlun's consent; or it needs Khilodlun to take the camp; or it needs to stop the young riders long enough for one of those to happen. The man from the wall turns the valley-mother's arithmetic: her valley needs men more than it needs to be empty.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A fur trader of [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] pays the party to winter with the Ghotakur and buy their sable before the Jobizur fur party does, and the Ghotakur sell only to people who have held a valley with them.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** Ghotaqtoliz hires the party at the first frost to hold a valley camp until the snow, because every man the valley can spare is going north.
- **The leader.** [[being-khilodlunbii|Khilodlun Biilokur]], dovaq of the [[affiliation-jbzrtrb|Jobizur]], wants someone who belongs to neither tribe to carry her offer to the Savaghur camp, and to bring back its answer before the snow.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer has a debt to [[being-tijodorquns|Tijodorghun Savaghur]], the Jobizur's old shaman, who was born Savaghur and asks the wanderer to go and see what has become of his birth tribe's camps.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The frost came in the night. In the morning the grass at Jiboq is white and stiff, the spring steams, and the men of the Ghotakur stand in a long line in front of the shaman with sleds behind them, packed with axes, rope and dried meat. One by one they put a hand in the spring and say the words. Nobody watching speaks.
>
> When the last has sworn, they go. The line goes north into the trees without looking back, the sleds hissing behind it, and the women turn to the valleys before the last man is out of sight.
>
> Two days later, from the ridge above the valley you are holding, you see smoke at the valley mouth: a dozen tents, a thin herd, and nobody coming in.

> [!TIP]
> The opening's work is the emptiness the men leave. Name a boy who is now the oldest male in the valley, and an old man who cannot draw a bow any more and does not say so.

## Scene One: The Valley

**Purpose.** Ground and people. Give the party the valley it is holding and the people in it, and set Jiboqlun's fear before the Savaghur appear.

**Situation.** The valley runs back into the forest edge, sheltered by larch on both sides, with a creek, winter grass and a dozen tents. The valley-mother holds it: herds, water and the word of the camp. She has fourteen people who can hold a bow and none who have fought.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Jiboqlun Ghotakur**, the valley-mother, a woman past sixty, small and quick, who was a girl in this valley in a winter the Savaghur came. She tells the party which way the Savaghur come and where to put the boys.
- **Her granddaughter**, twenty, who shoots better than anyone left in the valley, and whose mother died in the ice winter's cold.
- **The boys and old men** of the camp.

**Player choices.**

- _Prepare:_ set watches, choose ground, teach the boys. Jiboqlun approves of anything that keeps the herds behind the camp.
- _Parley:_ ask why the valley matters so much. Jiboqlun answers with the wall: it is the nearest grass to the wall's road, and the men come home to it at the thaw half-dead.
- _Look:_ walk the valley. It holds more grass than the camp's herd needs.

**Clues and rewards.** The valley carries more beasts than the Ghotakur have. Jiboqlun's account of the ice winter: the Savaghur camp came in while the men were north, she drove it out into the snow, and she does not know how many of them died on the way home.

**Consequences.** The party knows the ground and the valley-mother's mind before the smoke appears.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that arrives after the Savaghur meets Jiboqlun with her bow strung, and she gives the party the valley in a sentence.

## Scene Two: Smoke at the Valley Mouth

**Purpose.** The other camp. Bring the Savaghur to the valley and let the party see them clearly.

**Situation.** The Savaghur camp pitches at the valley mouth, on open grass where the wind comes straight off the steppe. It does not come in. On the second day Savaghjilaq rides toward the first tents alone and unarmed, stops short, and rides back.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Savaghjilaq Savaghur**, camp head, a heavy man of about fifty, grave and ashamed. He will speak to a stranger. He will not speak to a Ghotakur, because there is nothing he can say to one while blood is owed.
- **His eldest son**, twenty-four, unmarried, who keeps the camp's few horses alive.
- **The young riders**, a knot of them under Savaghjilaq's nephew, who sit apart and watch the clouds.
- **The Savaghur women**, slaughtering beasts that will not last the week.

**Player choices.**

- _Parley:_ ride out to the camp. Savaghjilaq gives the party salt; the party is a guest of the Savaghur from that moment, which the valley-mother notes.
- _Watch:_ count the camp and its beasts from the ridge.
- _Threaten:_ ride out armed and tell the camp to move. Savaghjilaq does not move, and the young riders mark the party's faces.

**Clues and rewards.** Savaghjilaq's request, if he trusts the party: shelter for the winter as guests, and he knows he cannot ask it. The state of the herd. The son. The young riders' talk of the gathering man's herds, which a guest in the camp hears at the fire.

**Consequences.** The party carries the request to Jiboqlun or does not. Jiboqlun's first answer is no: a Savaghur in her valley is a Savaghur in her valley, guest or not, and the feud forbids the salt.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never rides out sees Savaghjilaq turn back twice more; on the third day he sends his son with a lamb, which the valley-mother sends back.

## Scene Three: The Jobizur Grass

**Purpose.** The way out the steppe offers. Take the party to the Jobizur, where the third road and the old shaman are.

**Situation.** The Jobizur winter camps lie two days' ride west, where the tribe is coming down to its winter wells. Savaghur families taken in by the Jobizur live among them and keep their name. Khilodlun is at her winter camp, and Tijodorghun is with the circle.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **[[being-khilodlunbii|Khilodlun Biilokur]]**, the Jobizur dovaq, warm and shrewd. She offers the Savaghur camp what she offers every family: come in, keep your name, take Jobizur grass. She does not say what it costs, and she knows the party knows.
- **[[being-tijodorquns|Tijodorghun Savaghur]]**, seventy-seven, the eldest shaman of the circle, born Savaghur. His eyes fail at night, and he knows the old marriages of the northern grass better than anyone living.
- **Savaghur families** among the Jobizur, who want their kin to come in.

**Player choices.**

- _Parley:_ carry Khilodlun's offer back to Savaghjilaq; ask Tijodorghun what ends a feud between the Ghotakur and the Savaghur.
- _Ask for the sky:_ Tijodorghun reads the sky for the Savaghur if asked by someone who belongs to neither offer; what he reads is the referee's, and he says it plainly.
- _Bargain:_ ask Khilodlun to send horses for a feud-payment. She sends horses only to a camp that has come in.

**Clues and rewards.** Tijodorghun, asked how the feud can end, says a feud over these valleys has ended before: a Ghotakur daughter married into a Savaghur line in his grandmother's grandmother's time, before the present feud began, and the two tribes shared the valleys for a generation after. He names the line, and it is Jiboqlun's. He will witness a marriage, if one is made, under open sky.

**Consequences.** The party carries back two ways out: Khilodlun's, which ends the Savaghur camp's name, and Tijodorghun's, which ends the feud.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that does not go west meets a Jobizur rider at the valley mouth in Scene Four, carrying Khilodlun's offer, and an old shaman's message that a marriage has closed this feud before.

## Scene Four: The Man from the Wall

**Purpose.** The north's demand. Bring the wall into the valley and change the valley-mother's arithmetic.

**Situation.** A wall-sworn man, [Voghaltoliz Ghotakur]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, comes out of the forest on foot, three weeks after the men left, with a frostbitten hand and a message. The stretch of the wall that fell last winter has fallen further. Ghotaqtoliz wants every boy of the valleys who can carry a stone sent north at once. Voghaltoliz will not say what came through the gap before the men closed it, and his face says enough.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Voghaltoliz Ghotakur**, wall-sworn, about thirty, exhausted. He says what the dovaq asks and no more.
- **Jiboqlun**, who must choose between sending the valley's boys north and holding the valley against a Savaghur camp.
- **The young Savaghur riders**, who see a Ghotakur come out of the forest alone and know what it means.

**Player choices.**

- _Advise:_ tell Jiboqlun what the party has learned of the Savaghur camp.
- _Escort:_ take the boys north to the wall's road and back, leaving the valley thinner.
- _Bargain:_ propose that Savaghur men go north with the boys, which only a marriage can make possible.

**Clues and rewards.** The wall needs men; the Savaghur camp has men and no herds; the valley has grass and no men. Jiboqlun sees it the moment it is said aloud, and says she would rather die.

> [!NOTE]
> The wall stays offstage. What lies beyond it is the north, and the dead there do not lie still; this adventure says no more. Voghaltoliz's silence is the scene's weight, and the referee does not need to fill it.

**Consequences.** The young Savaghur riders decide that the valley is at its emptiest, and plan to ride in at the first snow.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** If the party is away when Voghaltoliz arrives, Jiboqlun sends the boys north with him, and the valley is held by old men and women when the snow comes.

## Scene Five: The First Snow

**Purpose.** The decision. Bring the two camps face to face with the snow coming down.

**Situation.** The first snow begins in the afternoon. The young Savaghur riders saddle at dusk. Savaghjilaq, if the party has carried anything back to him, rides to the first tent of the valley with his son and stops where he stopped before.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Jiboqlun**, with her granddaughter beside her.
- **Savaghjilaq** and his son.
- **The young riders** under Savaghjilaq's nephew, who ride whatever their camp head says.
- **Tijodorghun**, if the party brought him, standing bareheaded in the snow to witness.

**Player choices.**

- _Make the match:_ bring Jiboqlun to name her granddaughter to Savaghjilaq's son under open sky, with a shaman to witness. The feud ends when it is spoken, the Savaghur camp eats the valley's salt that night, and the son and the camp's men go north at the next hard frost.
- _Send them to the Jobizur:_ persuade Savaghjilaq to take Khilodlun's offer and ride west in the snow. The feud stands, and the Savaghur camp is a Jobizur camp in all but name by spring.
- _Hold the valley:_ stop the young riders with bows, words or the camp head's authority, and leave the Savaghur at the mouth.
- _Stand aside:_ let it happen.

**Clues and rewards.** None. The reward is the ending, and who owes the party for it.

**Consequences.** The ending the party reached runs into the winter; see Aftermath.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** The young riders ride in at dusk; see If the Party Does Nothing.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- The Savaghur camp waits at the valley mouth. Its beasts die one a night.
- Voghaltoliz takes the valley's boys north to the wall.
- At the first snow the young Savaghur riders ride into the valley, and Jiboqlun and her granddaughter shoot. Somebody dies on each side, and the camp takes the valley.
- The young riders send word to the gathering man's riders that a Savaghur camp holds a Ghotakur valley, and the herds come with the thaw.
- The Ghotakur men come home at the thaw to a valley held by their enemies, and the feud has a new winter to tell.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **If the match was made.** The feud ends with the words, and the two camps share the valley until the thaw. Savaghur men go north to the wall under the wall-oath, and some come back wall-sworn. Ghotaqtoliz, at the thaw, must decide whether the Ghotakur keep a peace their valley-mother made, and the moot at Jiboq argues it all spring.
- **If the Savaghur went to the Jobizur.** Khilodlun's tribe is a camp larger, the Savaghur a camp smaller, and the other tribes of the northern grass count her dependents again.
- **If the valley was held.** The Savaghur camp loses what is left of its herd in the snow. Its young riders swear to the gathering man in spring, and they remember the party's faces.
- **If blood was shed.** The feud has a fresh debt on each side, and the Ghotakur men come home to it.
