## Overview {#overview}

[[being-neilwa|Neilwa]] needs three nights in [[place-hushwood|The Hushwood]] to keep the rite for the Seilvri killed there. [[being-arvanir|Arvanir]] cannot spare the fighters to hold it. Across the posts, [[being-merraddovvrn|Sir Merrad Dovavren]] hires scouts at [[place-kelivren|Kelivren]] for the coming road into [[place-teilvari|Teilvari]]. He also offers Arvanir a meeting under truce. The party can enter through either commission and learn what accepting it commits them to.

The adventure runs during the preparation for the summer campaign of 720. The Hushwood lies east of the Seilvri’s remaining woods, in western Dúnavarre.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The [[affiliation-justbladeordr|Order of the Just Blade]] wages a war of extermination against the [[affiliation-saelvri|Seilvri]], a peaceful people defending their homes. The Order’s knights can be brave, courteous and generous to their own dependents while carrying out that war. Merrad honestly offers protection to those who submit, and kills those who refuse. His terms extinguish the Seilvri’s independence and rites.

Neilwa’s rite can settle the field. She needs access and protection, not a conqueror’s permission to know her own dead. [[being-edrintalvvl|Edrin Talvavel]] has never heard her account; he considers her rites worthless. [[being-orvandkthvl|Sir Orvand Kéthavel]] presses for a final muster and treats delays as failures of duty. Arvanir wants his people alive and fears losing their remaining fighters over graves the Order already holds.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The choice is concrete: whose passage does the party secure, what does it promise to obtain that passage, and who bears the cost afterward? Settling the field does not end the crusade or restore the stolen valleys.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The Order’s door.** Merrad hires the party to carry his offer of truce into Teilvari and survey the approach for the road. He pays openly, gives written terms and expects an honest report.
- **The Seilvri’s door.** Neilwa asks the party to escort her past the posts and keep her at the burial ground for three nights. She offers food, shelter and the households’ guidance; she cannot buy an army.
- **The witness.** A party already traveling between the estates and the forest can carry both messages. Arvanir wants outsiders present when Merrad states his terms, and Merrad wants witnesses that he keeps them.

## The Opening {#opening}

At the Order’s door, an ox-cart of axes waits outside Kelivren’s gate while Merrad reads the truce terms aloud. At the Seilvri door, Neilwa lays out the things she carries for the rite while Arvanir counts the fighters who must stay at the valley heads. In either opening, put the other commission within reach immediately: the message is already being carried, and the party can ask to hear its recipient’s answer.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### The terms at the road’s end

Merrad meets the party at the last completed stretch of road. He explains the Order’s offer without euphemism: come out, kneel at Árdavon’s threshold and accept settlement on an estate. Edrin undertakes the reception. The burned clearings beside the road show what refusal costs.

**Choices and evidence.** The party can accept the survey, refuse it, or limit its service to carrying the truce message. Merrad accepts a plainly stated limit before hiring; secretly marking a route for the Order while claiming neutrality is a betrayal if discovered. Ask exactly what the party records. A map of the household paths helps the next campaign even if no sword is drawn today.

**If bypassed.** The written terms travel with the message, and the posts repeat them. The party can reach the Seilvri without speaking to Merrad, but cannot later claim a safe-conduct it never obtained.

### The gathering in Teilvari

Neilwa and Arvanir receive the message at the households’ gathering. Arvanir questions a scout about every mark made on the way in. Neilwa describes the dead and the three nights. Their disagreement concerns whom they can risk, not whether the killing occurred.

**Choices and evidence.** The party can offer to escort Neilwa without drawing away the bowmen, ask Arvanir for a guide to the edge, or undertake to negotiate access. Neilwa consents to a limited escort; she does not agree to conversion as a device for fooling the Order. Arvanir lets volunteers leave with her, but refuses an order to empty the valley defenses.

**If bypassed.** A party loyal to the Order can remain at the posts and receive Neilwa’s request there. It still hears her terms from her, instead of only from Edrin’s account of them.

> [!TIP]
> Give each commission its full practical attraction. The Order has food, pay and an honored place for an escort. The Seilvri know the forest and offer trust they cannot safely replace. Neither employer owns the party’s next decision.

### A passage that can be kept

The Hushwood’s paths are posted from the estate side. Merrad’s own patrol knows the time of the promised meeting. A party seeking lawful passage must obtain a specific promise: Neilwa and her escort enter without conversion, remain three nights and leave unmolested. Edrin objects to recognizing the rite; Merrad asks why a commander should let an enemy cross his line.

**Three routes.** A witnessed truce is possible if the party persuades Merrad that keeping the dead and testing the wood’s danger serves the limited meeting he already offers. He can grant this brief passage without renouncing the campaign. Alternatively the party can guide Neilwa around the posts from Teilvari, leaving tracks and risking interception. A third choice is to disclose the attempt to the Order and keep Neilwa out. That preserves the party’s contract and leaves the field unsettled.

**If talks fail.** The patrol does not vanish. The party can withdraw, try the forest route, or confront Merrad at the margin. Show the opposed numbers in words: a small escort cannot hold the Order’s road against its companies. A fight buys a withdrawal or an opening, not possession of the whole forest.

### Three nights at the burial ground

Neilwa names the dead and keeps her people’s observance. The first night tests whether the escort can remain together in the silence. The second tests its supply and watch: low lamps and voices at the edge of hearing pull attention away from the living. The third tests the passage agreement, as the posted patrol comes to count who remains in the wood. The rites themselves belong to Neilwa; the party’s work is light, shelter, food and keeping interruption away.

**Choices and consequences.** Bring Edrin close enough to hear with Neilwa’s consent, keep him outside, or carry her account back afterward. He can witness an observance without admitting its theology. If the party fights the patrol, Neilwa must decide whether she can finish while the escort holds the approach. If she is driven out, she must return for a complete observance; no partial night creates a new shortcut.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not turn the field into an excuse to require the Seilvri’s conversion. Its dead are theirs. Their own rite settles it, as the Hushwood’s account establishes.

**If the party leaves.** Neilwa will not remain without protection against the posts. She withdraws with the surviving escort, and the field remains. A party that secures a promise and then abandons her has broken a particular trust, with witnesses.

### The report and the next road

After the third night the silence loses its hold: the field is settled, though its graves and the record of the killing remain. The escort leaves by the passage it secured or must find its way back through the woods. Merrad wants his report and any survey; Arvanir asks which household paths it contains. Orvand wants a useful road, not an account that questions the campaign.

**Choices and rewards.** The party can give the Order an accurate account of the rite while refusing a map of occupied clearings, publish the killing’s witnesses, conceal the route, or fulfill the original survey. State the consequences separately. A truthful report may preserve Merrad’s respect and still cost future work from the Marshal. A map may earn full pay and expose homes. Seilvri gratitude buys guidance and refuge, not command over their war-leader.

**If no rite is completed.** The final meeting still occurs. Ask what the party reports about the refusal and whose words it carries. A failed escort can leave a useful account; a successful survey can deepen the war.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The muster gathers at Kelivren. The road advances in summer, the households retreat farther into Teilvari, and the Hushwood continues to draw danger. Neilwa looks for another escort while Arvanir spends the fighters he cannot replace.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The rite is kept.** The dead are settled. The Order still holds the taken valleys, and the Seilvri still need food and room to live.
- **Merrad keeps a witnessed passage.** The party has a promise it can cite in a later negotiation. It has no general immunity for Seilvri travelers.
- **The route becomes a survey.** The next road follows information the party supplies. Name the clearings affected from the party’s actual report.
- **The party exposes the campaign.** Veldareth and the Synod become possible audiences for living witnesses. Neither response is guaranteed by the adventure.
