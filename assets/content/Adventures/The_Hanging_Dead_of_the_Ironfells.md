A rockslide shuts the gorge behind the party below [[place-sunderwynd|Sunderwynd]], a watch-post on the southern approach to an [[place-ironfells|Ironfells]] pass. The garrison hangs in a dead pine and from the post's own beams, and the one man left on the ground holds the door with a crossbow. The thing that took them is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]] that lives in the heights of the stonework and never sets foot on the ground, and at dusk the heights fill with the voices of the men it took. The garrison's daybook says the voices are its own. They are not: the hung are alive, and the captain has been calling his last man by name for three nights.

## Overview

Some weeks ago a made thing came down out of the high country and into Sunderwynd. The garrison called it the **Hangman**. It takes men from overhead, by reach or by rope, carries them up, and hangs them in high places: the dead pine above the gorge, the tower's beams, the vault of the cistern beneath the yard. It keeps to the heights, moves across walls, ceilings, beams and the hollow places in the masonry, and does not touch the ground. On open ground, with nothing above it to climb, it does not come.

The relief patrol from [[place-caerwynd|Caerwynd]] passed the party on the gorge trail this morning, going up. The party reaches the post's crag in the afternoon. The garrison is gone except for **Kenric Brandley**, a patrolman who has survived three nights by lying flat in the open yard after dark. He watched his captain taken, and since then he has heard his captain call him by name from the tower every night. He has not answered.

The way down is shut by the slide. The way up is the pass, under snow by nightfall. The way out is the post's drainage tunnel, which runs from the cistern's low end through the crag to the gorge face below the slide, and the cistern is where the Hangman feeds. Beyond the cistern's far wall lies the pay vault, walled up by an earlier captain during a raid and never opened since.

What the Hangman was made for, and by whom, stays unknown.

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the post—what the Hangman keeps, why the hung call, who is still alive and where, and where the pay vault's door is—is in [[place-sunderwynd#truth|the truth of Sunderwynd]]. In short: the Hangman has no voice. The voices in the heights are the taken, living, hung by ropes under the arms and watered in the cistern every dusk so that they last, and it lets them call because calling brings rescuers within reach. **Captain Aldmund Hardwick** hangs in the tower's roof-room above his own table, and the voice Kenric has refused for three nights is his.

This section covers what that truth does at the table: where the party can find it, how it lands on each route, and what the party wants once it knows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Kenric is honest and wrong. Everything he says about the Hangman's rule is so; only his reading of the voices is mistaken, and he believes it. The party must be able to find each true fact without him, so that the one wrong thing he tells them is the only thing that rests on his word alone.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal belongs in Scene Three, at dusk, when the Hangman makes its round of the pine, the tower and the cistern and the hung wake one by one and call. Whatever route the party is on, a voice does something a mimic cannot.

- **In the yard.** The captain calls for Kenric again. Anyone who asks the voice a question gets an answer to it, true and new. Asked anything about the post, the captain answers it and then says what he found on the afternoon before he was taken: "The vault door lieth beside the mason's mark. 'Tis set dry. Tell Caerwynd."
- **On the cistern route.** By lamplight, a man hanging over the water opens his eyes. His chest rises. The rope runs under his arms.
- **On the wall.** A patrolman in the pine calls for his sergeant: "Wilric! Wilric, art thou there?" Nobody in the party has heard the name. From the grating over the cistern, below the yard, a weak voice answers: "Here, lad. Hold on."
- **If a companion was taken.** The companion's own voice calls from above, answers the party by name, and asks for a knife.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on the voice and then stop talking. Let the voice answer a player's question, plainly and in pain, and let the players put the dripping boots, the crack and the drip on the table together for themselves. Kenric's face does the rest.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early. A party that climbs the trap in the captain's ceiling in Scene Two, or lays a hand on a hung man in the cistern by day, finds him warm and breathing. The reveal lands there, the party has the whole of the dusk to plan for, and the Hangman knows someone has been in its larder.

> [!WARNING]
> Never play a voice as mockery, as an echo of the party's own words, or as a repetition of what it said before. A voice that only repeats is the mimic Kenric believes in, and a GM who plays it that way confirms his reading and spends the reversal.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party no longer wants down. It wants up. The rescue is in the Hangman's own ground: the tower's roof-room, the dead pine over the drop, and the vault of the cistern above black water. The drainage tunnel is still the way out, but leaving by it now means leaving living men behind. Kenric has three nights of a captain he did not answer, and he goes up the tower ladder if the party will not. Under the leader's hook, the kinsman is alive.

> [!TIP]
> Play Kenric after the reveal as a man who has stopped being afraid of the right thing. He wants to answer the captain now, at once, from wherever he stands, and the party has to decide whether to let a voice in the yard draw the Hangman to the tower.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each puts the party on the gorge trail below Sunderwynd.

- **The thief.** Every smuggler who crosses the pass knows the post keeps the toll, and one of them has heard that a captain walled up the pay vault in a raid and died without telling anyone where. The thief goes up the gorge to look, behind the relief patrol.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** Caerwynd sends the party up with dispatches and a mule of stores, a day behind the relief patrol, to be signaled back with the post's news.
- **The leader.** The post's captain, **Aldmund Hardwick**, is the leader's kinsman or old comrade, and his signals have stopped. The leader goes to see why, and takes companions.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is crossing the Ironfells by the eastern pass, alone, late in the season, when the slide comes down behind.

## The Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The slope gives way behind you with a long grinding roar, and when the dust clears the trail you climbed this afternoon is gone under a fan of broken rock, trees and earth from wall to wall of the gorge. There is no way down. Above you, where the gorge opens toward the watch-post on its crag, a dead pine leans out over the drop. Three bodies hang from its branches. One of them wears the colors of the patrol that passed you this morning, six men climbing in good spirits to relieve the post. Its boots are still dripping.

> [!TIP]
> Say the last sentence and move on. The boots are the first clue, and the party should carry them up the trail as a horror, not as a question.

## Scene One: The Doorway

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Put the party at the post with no way back, meet the one man left, and put the crack and the captain's voice in the party's ears.

> The trail climbs out of the gorge under the dead pine and ends at a gatehouse with its gate standing open. Beyond it is a walled yard, a square stone tower and a long barrack range. Nothing moves in the yard but a cart lying on its side. From behind the cart a voice says, quite steadily, "Stop there."

**Situation.** In the yard, behind the overturned cart, **Kenric Brandley** crouches with a loaded crossbow leveled at the gate. It is late afternoon and the light is going. He will not let anyone come closer until he is sure they are alive.

**NPCs and threats.** **Kenric** is a patrolman of the garrison, young, filthy, hollow-eyed and steady-handed. He gives his test in the older speech: "Stand ye where the light is, every one, and show me your feet upon the stones. Speak your names, and tell me what ye ate this morn." A sound from above sends him flat to the ground.

Once he trusts the party he tells what he saw, briefly. He was on the tower stair three nights ago with the captain at his table above, writing. Something scraped overhead, the lamp went over, there was a crack in the dark, and when Kenric got up the stair the chair was empty. That night the captain's voice called him by name from the tower, asking him to come up and help. Since then the voice has called every night, and it says things the captain never said: that the snow is starting on the pass, that the lamp in the barracks has gone out. "It watcheth us," Kenric says, "and it learneth." He has not answered it, and he has not been inside a roofed room since.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Two things must reach the party here or in Scene Two: the crack in the dark, and a voice that reports what it sees. Give them as Kenric gives them, as proof of the thing's cunning, and do not dwell on either.

> [!WARNING]
> Kenric believes every word he says. Do not let him hesitate over the voice, wonder aloud whether the captain lives, or look away when the tower is mentioned. A party that suspects the truth should suspect it from the boots, the crack and the voice's news, never from his manner.

> [!TIP]
> Play the test as the most important thing in Kenric's world. He watches feet, not faces, and the first person who steps onto the stones and stands there is the first person he lowers the crossbow for.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ rush him. He shoots the first one through the gate and is then overpowered. The party has a wounded comrade, and Kenric, once he sees them bleed, weeps with relief.
- _Stealth:_ come over the yard wall from the gorge side while others talk at the gate. A climber on the wall is the one thing Kenric is looking for, and he swings the crossbow at the wall, not the gate.
- _Talk:_ take his test. Standing in the open with their feet on the ground satisfies him at once; he has never seen the thing stand.
- _Cleverness:_ notice what the test is for. The party, asked to show its feet, can ask him why, and he says what he knows: it does not come down, and it has never once crossed the open yard.
- _Giving something up:_ throw down weapons and walk in with hands open. Kenric lowers the crossbow, and the weapons lie in the gateway, under the arch, where something could reach them from above.

**Clues and rewards.**

- **It keeps to the heights and never touches the ground.** From Kenric's test. From the hanging pine, out of reach of anything that walks.
- **The voices.** From Kenric's account: the captain's voice, the crack, the voice's news of the snow and the lamp.
- **The open yard is the one safe ground.** From Kenric, who has slept in it three nights.

**Consequences.** Kenric, trusted, becomes a guide: he knows the post, the cistern, the drainage tunnel and the captain's daybook. Kenric, shot or driven off, is lost to the party, and is found later hanging in the cistern, alive.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that avoids the post and tries to climb past it on the gorge wall finds the wall loose and the light going, and something moves on the rock above them; the only shelter is the post. If Kenric dies at the gate, the captain's daybook in the tower holds the post's story in the captain's own hand up to the last entry, the gouges on the ceilings tell the rest, and the party hears the captain's voice for itself at dusk. The open yard teaches its own lesson in Scene Three, to anyone who sees what the Hangman does not do.

## Scene Two: The Barracks

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Show what happened to the garrison and give the way out, while the thing itself stays unseen, and put the daybook and the drip in the party's hands.

> The barrack range is long and dim, and it smells of cold food. Every bunk has been torn apart. Overhead, across the plaster and the beams, run deep scores in sets of four, as if something heavy had hung up there by hooks and dragged itself along. The floor is swept clean.

**Situation.** The barrack range has bunks on both walls and a kitchen at one end. The bedding is shredded and flung about. Meals sit half-eaten on the table, a spoon still in a bowl. The gouges run over every bunk. In the captain's room in the tower are the daybook, open, and a patrol sketch pinned to the table. Above the table is a trap in the boards of the ceiling, closed, with a ladder lying under it, and the gouges run up the tower wall to its edge. A slow drip of water falls through the boards beside the trap and has spread a stain across the daybook's last page, though the day is dry.

**NPCs and threats.** The Hangman is in the post, unseen. Now and then, from inside the walls, comes a sound of something shifting, a scrape and a pause. Kenric will not come under the roof; he stands in the doorway and talks.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The daybook's last entry, the drip and the closed trap must reach the party together, in the captain's room. Describe the drip as a plain fact of the room, like the spoon in the bowl, and let the party decide whether to look up.

> [!WARNING]
> The trap is the one way the reveal can come before dusk. Do not close it, lock it or put the party off it. A party that goes up finds the captain, and that party has earned the reversal early; see the tip under How the reveal lands.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ search the post room by room, all together. The party sees the gouges spread from room to room, along the ceilings, up the tower wall to the trap, and into a ventilation shaft in the tower wall, too narrow for a man.
- _Stealth:_ go quietly and listen. A party that is quiet hears the scrape in the masonry move from the barrack wall to the tower and stop, as if listening back. Under the trap in the captain's room, a quiet party hears a man's slow breathing in the roof-room above, and then nothing.
- _Talk:_ Kenric tells how it went: the first men lost on watch on the wall, then men taken from bunks with their mates asleep in the next, then the captain from his own table in the tower, with Kenric on the stair below.
- _Cleverness:_ read the captain's daybook. Its earlier pages record the post's searches for the pay vault's door, room by room. Its last pages are in the older speech of a frightened man writing exactly: "It cometh not down. I have watched it these three nights, and it hath not set foot to floor nor ground. It dippeth them in the cistern ere it hangeth them, and none knoweth why. The cistern is its lair. I will draw the men into the yard at dark." The last entry, below it, is a single line under the stain from the drip: "This afternoon I found at last the do". It ends there, mid-word.
- _Giving something up:_ go into the cistern's mouth with a lamp, alone, to look. Only the stair is seen, wet, and the sound of dripping, before something in the dark ahead goes still.

**Clues and rewards.** Each of the essential facts has more than one source:

- **The cistern passage leads out past the slide.** From the patrol sketch, which marks the drainage tunnel from the cistern's low end to the gorge face below the trail. From Kenric. From the draught of cold air that comes up the cistern stair.
- **It never touches the ground.** From the gouges on every ceiling and none on any floor. From the daybook. From the sketch itself: beside the tunnel, someone has drawn a long body with too many joints and hooked limbs, and no feet. The drawing ends where blood soaked the page.
- **The cistern is its lair.** From the daybook. From the dripping boots in the pine. From the wet stair.
- **It dips the men it takes.** From the daybook. From the boots in the pine. From the wet ropes Kenric has seen drawn up the cistern stair at dusk.
- **The pay vault lies behind the cistern's far wall.** From the daybook, which records the post's searches for its door. From an old mason's mark cut on the cistern's far wall, matching one on the tower's foundation stone.

**Consequences.** The party knows where it must go and what waits there. Night is coming.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that skips the barracks and goes straight for the cistern finds the gouges on the cistern's vault and the wet ropes hanging from it, and the sketch is in Kenric's coat if he lives. If the daybook is missed, Kenric remembers what the captain said aloud about the dipping and the yard. If the sketch is lost, the cold draught and the sound of water running out of the cistern's low end mark the tunnel as plainly. A party that never enters the captain's room hears the drip's meaning at dusk instead, when the captain's voice comes from the tower.

## Scene Three: The Arrow-Slit

**Purpose.** The horror draws close, and the reversal lands. Show the Hangman, prove that no room is secure, close the way up and the way down, and let a voice answer.

> Dusk comes on fast. Snow begins on the pass above, fine and dry. Something heavy goes down the cistern stair, slowly, and after a while comes back up, and the sound of it dripping crosses the yard wall and goes away toward the gorge. A while later, from the pine over the drop, a man's voice calls a name. Later still, from high in the tower, another says, "Kenric."

**Situation.** Dusk is the Hangman's round. It goes down to the cistern, then out to the pine and back, then up the tower, and the round takes the better part of an hour. In the barrack range, a long shape crosses the outside of an arrow-slit on the gorge side, from left to right, smoothly, as if walking on the air beyond the wall; there is no ground there, only the drop. Moments later, a coil of rope on the floor jerks and runs upward, through a smoke-hole in the roof, lifting whatever is tied to it: a pack, a mule's lead, or a careless companion who has looped it round a wrist. The party hears movement inside the masonry around them, close, in the thickness of the wall.

As the round goes on, the voices begin, one after another as each hung man is watered and wakes. The captain calls Kenric from the tower, and says the snow has started. From the pine over the gorge a younger voice calls for someone named Wilric. Up the cistern stair come other voices, fainter, asking for water, for light, for a knife.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Hangman**, glimpsed but not seen whole: a long dark shape, too many joints, hooked limbs, quick and silent. It can reach any room with a roof, a beam, a shaft or a gap in the stone. It cannot reach the open yard. It makes no sound but the scrape of its hooks. When anyone moves toward a voice, the scrape in the masonry moves the same way.

**Kenric, after the voice answers.** He goes white, then gets up from the stones. He says, once, "Three nights," and then he wants to answer, and to go up. He does not wait for permission unless someone holds him.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must put a voice in the party's ears that answers what it is asked, or speaks to another voice. Everything after it—the rescue, the choice in Scene Four, the captain's knowledge in Scene Five—rests on the party knowing the hung are alive.

> [!WARNING]
> Keep the Hangman silent. It has no voice of its own, and a single word from the shape at the arrow-slit gives the party back the mimic, and the reversal with it.

> [!TIP]
> Let the voices start one at a time, in the order of the round: the cistern first, then the pine, then the tower. A party that notices the order has the round, and the round is the clock the rescue in Scene Four runs on.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight it at the smoke-hole or the shaft. It has the height, and the party does not. It takes a wound and withdraws into the wall. In the quiet after, the captain's voice asks from the tower whether anyone is hurt.
- _Stealth:_ go out to the open yard with Kenric and lie flat through the night. The Hangman paces the yard's walls, but it does not come down. The voices call all night. The snow falls. By morning the pass is shut, and the party is cold and spent, and has listened to living men for a night.
- _Talk:_ answer the voices. They answer back. The captain says where he hangs, that the rope is under his arms, and that he cannot feel his feet, and he asks for Kenric. As the party talks, the scrape in the masonry moves toward the tower and settles there, beside the man who is calling.
- _Cleverness:_ use its rule against it: fight it only from open ground, light the yard, draw it to a place where the only way at the party is down.
- _Giving something up:_ cut the rope and let the pack or the mule go. The companion on the other end is the harder choice: a quick blade on the rope saves him and drops him to the floor, hurt.

**Clues and rewards.** The Hangman's rule, proved: it does not cross open ground and does not come down. The direction it goes when wounded, always toward the cistern. The round, and how long it takes. The voices, and what they are. On the yard route, the captain's word that the vault door lies beside the mason's mark, set dry.

**Consequences.** Snow closes the pass by morning. The slide closes the gorge. The way out is the drainage tunnel, and the tunnel runs through the cistern. Staying means another night of the round, and living men above who will not last forever.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that went straight down into the cistern at dusk meets the round there: the rope jerking up into the vault, the shape crossing above the lamplight, and a hung man over the water opening his eyes as the Hangman passes. A party that stays in the yard and waits for rescue hears the patrolman in the pine call for Wilric, and Wilric answer him from the cistern grating, and the captain call for Kenric until dawn. A companion taken by the rope is alive, hung in the cistern, and calls the party by name.

## Scene Four: The Cistern

**Purpose.** Confrontation. Meet the Hangman in its feeding chamber, let the party use what it learned, and make it choose whom to bring out.

> The stair goes down wet into the dark. At the bottom is a long low vault on squat pillars, black water at the far end and shallow water at the near, and from every rib and pillar hang men on ropes, their feet a little above the water. Some of them are turning. One of them, near the stair, lifts his head.

**Situation.** The cistern is half-full of black water at the high end and shallow at the low end, where the drainage tunnel opens. The vault is hung with the taken: men of the garrison, the relief patrol, a smuggler and his pony, strung by ropes under the arms from the pillars and the ribs, their feet over the water. Some are dead. Some breathe: the relief patrol's sergeant **Wilric** and three of his men, and two men of the garrison. Some of the stones of the vault are cracked and loose; the Hangman's weight, night after night, has worked them free of their mortar. In the far wall, by the mason's mark, the stone looks newer. The Hangman is overhead. The captain is not here; he hangs in the tower.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Hangman**, seen at last as it unfolds from among the vault's ribs: longer than three men, jointed like a stick insect and a man at once, its limbs ending in hooks, a band of dark metal sunk into one of them and cut with marks nobody can read. It makes no sound at all. It attacks from above, across walls, beams and narrow openings, drops, lifts and climbs away. On the floor it is slow and its reach is short. It goes after anything that falls from its ropes, living or dead, at once.

> [!WARNING]
> Cutting a living man's rope drops him into the deep end with his arms bound to his sides. Make that plain the first time a blade goes to a rope, before it cuts, never after. Bringing the living down alive means lowering them, which needs two pairs of hands and the time the Hangman allows.

> [!TIP]
> Make the choice in this scene about time, not about whether the party can win. Every man lowered is a round of the fight spent, and the round of the tower and the pine is still going on above. Let the party count the living and decide.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight it in the vault. It has every advantage overhead. A party that fights it only from the shallows, and strikes only when it comes down to strike, wears it down at cost.
- _Lower the living:_ one takes a hung man's weight while another cuts, and they wade him to the shallows. Each man brought down costs the time of a blow struck or taken, and the Hangman comes for the ones on the floor.
- _Bring down the vault:_ the loose stones of the failing ribs can be levered or struck free. A section of the vault brought down drops the Hangman into the water and the rubble, on the floor, where it can be killed. The hung beneath that section come down with it, and the cistern may come down further than intended.
- _Force it into the open:_ draw it up the stair after a voice or a lure and into the yard, where nothing stands overhead. There it sprawls, slow and blind with fury, and it can be finished.
- _Go up first:_ climb to the captain in the tower while the Hangman is busy in the cistern, or works the pine. Lowering him through the trap takes two people and a rope, and the Hangman comes to the tower the moment it hears him moved.
- _Run for the tunnel:_ fight through to the low end and into the drainage tunnel, which is too tight for it to follow quickly. The party escapes, and the Hangman stays, and so do the hung.
- _Cleverness:_ cut down the dead. They fall into the deep water, and the Hangman goes after them, hook and limb, as it goes after anything that leaves its ropes; for a few moments it is low and busy, and the living can be lowered.
- _Giving something up:_ Kenric offers himself as the voice to draw it, standing in the yard and answering the captain at last; or one of the party does.

**Clues and rewards.** The newer stone in the far wall, by the mason's mark, is the pay vault's door. A companion taken in Scene Three hangs here, alive. Wilric, if he is lowered alive, knows the relief patrol's names and which of them lives.

**Consequences.** If the Hangman dies, the post is quiet. Its body is light for its size and smells of nothing. If it is driven off or escaped from, it stays in the heights of the post with whatever it still holds, and Sunderwynd cannot be held.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes down unprepared, without knowing the Hangman's rule, can still run for the tunnel, and its low ceiling and tight bends slow the thing behind them. A party that flees back up the stair has the open yard as refuge until the next night. The round is the other way in: while the Hangman works the pine at dusk, the cistern and the tower are both unwatched, and Kenric, who has listened to three nights of it, says how long that lasts; without him, the order in which the voices wake gives it.

## Scene Five: The Pay Vault

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Pay out the treasure, weigh it against the living, and send the party down the mountain.

> Behind the stone is a short dry passage and a low room cut in the crag. Lamplight finds iron-bound chests along one wall and, laid across them, a sword in a scabbard gone green.

**Situation.** Behind the newer stone, a short passage leads to the pay vault. The captain who walled it up hid here what he could not carry out under attack: the toll chests, the garrison's pay, and his own sword. Captain Hardwick, if he lives, knows the door is a single slab set dry behind a skin of lime, and a bar under its lower edge lifts it out; without him, breaking through the facing takes an hour of noisy work. Beyond the cistern's low end, the drainage tunnel runs down through the crag, cramped and wet, and comes out on the gorge face below the slide, where a climb brings the party to the trail and the long way down to the forest steadings.

**NPCs and threats.** Kenric, if he lives, wants the captain carried down before anything else, and the post's silver taken to Caerwynd after. The living the party brought down are weak, cold and unable to climb unaided.

> [!TIP]
> Put the silver and the living in the same tunnel. Every man carried is a chest left, and the party should feel the trade without the GM naming it.

**Player choices.**

- _Take everything:_ the silver is heavy, and the tunnel and the climb are hard. With the living to carry, most of it stays behind or goes down the gorge.
- _Keep faith:_ carry the living, the toll and the pay to Caerwynd with Kenric. The garrison pays a reward, and the crown remembers.
- _Divide it:_ Kenric is one man, wounded and spent. He can be talked round, or left.
- _Bury the dead:_ cutting down the dead and laying them in the yard costs the rest of the day, and earns Kenric's lasting loyalty.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **garrison's pay**, a chest of crown silver.
- The **toll chests**, in coin and in kind: spice, amber and a bolt of fine cloth taken from a smuggler's ponies.
- The **captain's sword**, a fine blade given by the crown to the captain who walled the vault, with his name and the crown's mark on the blade. His family wants it back, and pays.
- From the Hangman, if it died: the band of dark metal, cut with marks no scholar on Aelwyth has read.
- From Caerwynd: a reward for every man of the garrison and the relief brought down alive.

**Consequences.** The party comes out on the gorge face below the slide, with the forest below and the snow above.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that escapes through the tunnel without opening the vault can come back in the spring with Caerwynd's men, if the Hangman has left the post or been killed. If it has not, the vault waits behind its wall, and so does the thing in the cistern, with whatever it kept.

## Aftermath

- **If the Hangman is dead,** Caerwynd regarrisons Sunderwynd, and the pass is open in the spring.
- **If it lives,** the post is abandoned, and a dead pine above the gorge carries new men each season, and on still evenings the trail below hears them. Smugglers use the other pass.
- **If the captain was brought down,** the first thing he asks for is Kenric, and what he says to him nobody else hears. He names Kenric in his report as the man who held the post.
- **If the captain was left,** Kenric goes back up the gorge with the first party Caerwynd sends in the spring, whatever the orders.
- **If living men were left in the cistern,** Caerwynd hears it from the ones who came out, and asks the party why.
- **If the band of metal is taken to a scholar,** the marks on it match nothing in any library on Aelwyth. The Synod at Ravenmoor asks to see it.
- **If Kenric lives,** he does not serve under a roof again.
