A rockslide shuts the gorge behind the party below [[place-sunderwynd|Sunderwynd]], a watch-post on the southern approach to an [[place-ironfells|Ironfells]] pass. The garrison is dead, hung in a pine and from the post's own beams, and the one man left alive holds the door with a crossbow. The thing that killed them is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]] that lives in the heights of the stonework, calls in dead men's voices and never sets foot on the ground. The way out runs through its feeding chamber.

## Overview

Some weeks ago a made thing came down out of the high country and into Sunderwynd. The garrison called it the **Hangman**. It takes men from overhead, by reach or by rope, carries them up, and hangs them in high places: the dead pine above the gorge, the tower's beams, the vault of the cistern beneath the yard. It dips them in the cistern first, for reasons nobody knows. It calls in the voices of the men it has taken, and it learns new voices quickly.

It keeps to the heights. It moves across walls, ceilings, beams and the hollow places in the masonry, and it does not touch the ground. On open ground, with nothing above it to climb, it does not come. Brought down to the floor, it is slow, clumsy and badly placed, and it can be fought.

The relief patrol from [[place-caerwynd|Caerwynd]] passed the party on the gorge trail this morning, going up. The party reaches the post's crag in the afternoon. The garrison is gone except for **Kenric Brandley**, a patrolman who has survived three nights by lying flat in the open yard after dark. He watched his captain die, and then heard his captain call him by name.

The way down is shut by the slide. The way up is the pass, under snow by nightfall. The way out is the post's drainage tunnel, which runs from the cistern's low end through the crag to the gorge face below the slide, and the cistern is where the Hangman feeds. Beyond the cistern's far wall lies the pay vault, walled up by an earlier captain during a raid and never opened since.

What the Hangman was made for, and by whom, stays unknown.

## Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The slope gives way behind you with a long grinding roar, and when the dust clears the trail you climbed this afternoon is gone under a fan of broken rock, trees and earth from wall to wall of the gorge. There is no way down. Above you, where the gorge opens toward the watch-post on its crag, a dead pine leans out over the drop. Three bodies hang from its branches. One of them wears the colours of the patrol that passed you this morning, six men climbing in good spirits to relieve the post. Its boots are still dripping.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each puts the party on the gorge trail below Sunderwynd.

- **The thief.** Every smuggler who crosses the pass knows the post keeps the toll, and one of them has heard that a captain walled up the pay vault in a raid and died without telling anyone where. The thief goes up the gorge to look, behind the relief patrol.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** Caerwynd sends the party up with dispatches and a mule of stores, a day behind the relief patrol, to be signalled back with the post's news.
- **The leader.** The post's captain, **Aldmund Hardwick**, is the leader's kinsman or old comrade, and his signals have stopped. The leader goes to see why, and takes companions.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is crossing the Ironfells by the eastern pass, alone, late in the season, when the slide comes down behind.

## Scene One: The Doorway

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Put the party at the post with no way back, and meet the one man left, whose terror is the first clue.

**Situation.** The trail climbs out of the gorge under the dead pine and ends at Sunderwynd's gatehouse. The gate is open. In the yard beyond, behind an overturned cart, **Kenric Brandley** crouches with a loaded crossbow levelled at the gate. It is late afternoon and the light is going. He will not let anyone come closer until he is sure they are alive.

**NPCs and threats.** **Kenric** is a patrolman of the garrison, young, filthy, hollow-eyed and steady-handed. He saw **Captain Aldmund Hardwick** taken up into the barrack rafters and heard his neck go; that night the captain called to him from the tower in his own voice, asking him to come up and help. He has not been inside a roofed room since. He gives his test in the older speech: "Stand ye where the light is, every one, and show me your feet upon the stones. Speak your names, and tell me what ye ate this morn." A sound from above sends him flat to the ground.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ rush him. He shoots the first one through the gate and is then overpowered. The party has a wounded comrade, and Kenric, once he sees them bleed, weeps with relief.
- _Stealth:_ come over the yard wall from the gorge side while others talk at the gate. A climber on the wall is the one thing Kenric is looking for, and he swings the crossbow at the wall, not the gate.
- _Talk:_ take his test. Standing in the open with their feet on the ground satisfies him at once; he has never seen the thing stand.
- _Cleverness:_ notice what the test is for. The party, asked to show its feet, can ask him why, and he says what he knows: it does not come down, and it has never once crossed the open yard.
- _Giving something up:_ throw down weapons and walk in with hands open. Kenric lowers the crossbow, and the weapons lie in the gateway, under the arch, where something could reach them from above.

**Clues and rewards.**

- **It keeps to the heights and never touches the ground.** From Kenric's test and his account. From the hanging pine, out of reach of anything that walks.
- **It speaks in the voices of the dead.** From Kenric's story of the captain.
- **The open yard is the one safe ground.** From Kenric, who has slept in it three nights.

**Consequences.** Kenric, trusted, becomes a guide: he knows the post, the cistern, the drainage tunnel and the captain's daybook. Kenric, shot or driven off, is lost to the party, and is found later hanging in the tower.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that avoids the post and tries to climb past it on the gorge wall finds the wall loose and the light going, and something moves on the rock above them; the only shelter is the post. If Kenric dies at the gate, the captain's daybook in the tower holds his story in the captain's own hand, up to the last entry, and the gouges on the barrack ceilings tell the rest. The open yard teaches its own lesson in Scene Three, to anyone who sees what the Hangman does not do.

## Scene Two: The Barracks

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Show what happened to the garrison and give the way out, while the thing itself stays unseen.

**Situation.** The barrack range is a long room with bunks on both walls and a kitchen at one end. The bedding is shredded and flung about. Meals sit half-eaten on the table, a spoon still in a bowl. The floor is clean. Across the ceiling, over every bunk, run deep gouges in the plaster and the beams, sets of long parallel scores as if something heavy hung there by hooks and dragged itself along. In the captain's room in the tower are the daybook and a patrol sketch pinned to the table.

**NPCs and threats.** The Hangman is in the post, unseen. Now and then, from inside the walls, comes a sound of something shifting, a scrape and a pause. Kenric will not come under the roof; he stands in the doorway and talks.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ search the post room by room, all together. The party sees the gouges spread from room to room, along the ceilings, and into a ventilation shaft in the tower wall, too narrow for a man.
- _Stealth:_ go quietly and listen. A party that is quiet hears the scrape in the masonry move from the barrack wall to the tower and stop, as if listening back.
- _Talk:_ Kenric tells how it went: the first men lost on watch on the wall, then men taken from bunks with their mates asleep in the next, then the captain from the middle of the room, in lamplight, in front of everyone.
- _Cleverness:_ read the captain's daybook. Its last pages are in the older speech of a frightened man writing exactly: "It cometh not down. I have watched it these three nights, and it hath not set foot to floor nor ground. The cistern is its lair. I will draw the men into the yard at dark." The last entry ends mid-word.
- _Giving something up:_ go into the cistern's mouth with a lamp, alone, to look. Only the stair is seen, wet, and the sound of dripping, before something in the dark ahead goes still.

**Clues and rewards.** Each of the essential facts has more than one source:

- **The cistern passage leads out past the slide.** From the patrol sketch, which marks the drainage tunnel from the cistern's low end to the gorge face below the trail. From Kenric. From the draught of cold air that comes up the cistern stair.
- **It never touches the ground.** From the gouges on every ceiling and none on any floor. From the daybook. From the sketch itself: beside the tunnel, someone has drawn a long body with too many joints and hooked limbs, and no feet. The drawing ends where blood soaked the page.
- **The cistern is its lair.** From the daybook. From the wet boots of the hanged. From the dripping stair.
- **The pay vault lies behind the cistern's far wall.** From the daybook, which records the post's searches for its door. From an old mason's mark cut on the cistern's far wall, matching one on the tower's foundation stone.

**Consequences.** The party knows where it must go and what waits there. Night is coming.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that skips the barracks and goes straight for the cistern finds the gouges on the cistern's vault and the wet ropes hanging from it, and the sketch is in Kenric's coat if he lives. If the daybook is missed, Kenric remembers what the captain said aloud. If the sketch is lost, the cold draught and the sound of water running out of the cistern's low end mark the tunnel as plainly.

## Scene Three: The Arrow-Slit

**Purpose.** The horror draws close. Show the Hangman, prove that no room is secure, and close the way up and the way down.

**Situation.** Dusk. Snow begins on the pass above. In the barrack range, a long shape crosses the outside of an arrow-slit on the gorge side, from left to right, smoothly, as if walking on the air beyond the wall; it does not touch the ground, because there is no ground there, only the drop. Moments later, a coil of rope on the floor jerks and runs upward, through a smoke-hole in the roof, lifting whatever is tied to it: a pack, a mule's lead, or a careless companion who has looped it round a wrist. The party hears movement inside the masonry around them, close, in the thickness of the wall.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Hangman**, glimpsed but not seen whole: a long dark shape, too many joints, hooked limbs, quick and silent. It calls from the roof in Captain Hardwick's voice, and, if it has heard them, in the voices of the party themselves, repeating what they said in the barracks. It can reach any room with a roof, a beam, a shaft or a gap in the stone. It cannot reach the open yard.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight it at the smoke-hole or the shaft. It has the height, and the party does not. It takes a wound and withdraws into the wall.
- _Stealth:_ go out to the open yard with Kenric and lie flat through the night. The Hangman paces the yard's walls and calls, but it does not come down. The snow falls. By morning the pass is shut, and the party is cold and spent.
- _Talk:_ answer the voices to find where it is. The voice moves as it answers, and it is never where the speaker was.
- _Cleverness:_ use its rule against it: fight it only from open ground, light the yard, draw it to a place where the only way at the party is down.
- _Giving something up:_ cut the rope and let the pack or the mule go. The companion on the other end is the harder choice: a quick blade on the rope saves him and drops him to the floor, hurt.

**Clues and rewards.** The Hangman's rule, proved: it does not cross open ground and does not come down. The direction it goes when wounded, always toward the cistern.

**Consequences.** Snow closes the pass by morning. The slide closes the gorge. The way out is the drainage tunnel, and the tunnel runs through the cistern. Staying means another night, and the Hangman learns voices.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that went straight down into the cistern at dusk meets the same signs there: the rope jerking up into the vault, the shape crossing above the lamplight, the voices from the stone. A party that stays in the yard and waits for rescue sees, at noon, a figure on the trail below the slide waving, and hears it call in the relief patrol's sergeant's voice; nobody comes. A companion taken by the rope is alive, hung in the cistern, and the party can still reach them there.

## Scene Four: The Cistern

**Purpose.** Confrontation and revelation. Meet the Hangman in its feeding chamber, let the party use what it learned, and give it ways to win or to get past.

**Situation.** A wet stair from the yard leads down into the cistern: a long low vault on squat pillars, half-full of black water at the high end and shallow at the low end where the drainage tunnel opens. The vault is hung with the dead: men of the garrison, the relief patrol, a smuggler and his pony, all strung by ropes from the pillars and the ribs of the vault, their feet over the water. Some of the stones of the vault are cracked and loose; the Hangman's weight, night after night, has worked them free of their mortar. In the far wall, by the mason's mark, the stone is newer. The Hangman is overhead.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Hangman**, seen at last as it unfolds from among the vault's ribs: longer than three men, jointed like a stick insect and a man at once, its limbs ending in hooks, a band of dark metal sunk into one of them and cut with marks nobody can read. It does not speak in its own voice, if it has one. It attacks from above, across walls, beams and narrow openings, drops, lifts and climbs away. On the floor it is slow and its reach is short.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight it in the vault. It has every advantage overhead. A party that fights it only from the shallows, and strikes only when it comes down to strike, wears it down at cost.
- _Bring down the vault:_ the loose stones of the failing ribs can be levered or struck free. A section of the vault brought down drops the Hangman into the water and the rubble, on the floor, where it can be killed. The cistern may come down further than intended.
- _Force it into the open:_ draw it up the stair after a voice or a lure and into the yard, where nothing stands overhead. There it sprawls, slow and blind with fury, and it can be finished.
- _Run for the tunnel:_ fight through to the low end and into the drainage tunnel, which is too tight for it to follow quickly. The party escapes, and the Hangman stays.
- _Cleverness:_ cut the hanging ropes. The dead fall into the water, and the Hangman goes after them, hook and limb, in a way it does not for the living; for a few moments it is low and busy.
- _Giving something up:_ Kenric offers himself as the voice to draw it, standing in the yard and calling; or one of the party does.

**Clues and rewards.** The newer stone in the far wall, by the mason's mark, is the pay vault's door. A companion taken in Scene Three hangs here, alive.

**Consequences.** If the Hangman dies, the post is quiet. Its body is light for its size and smells of nothing. If it is driven off or escaped from, it stays in the heights of the post, and Sunderwynd cannot be held.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes down unprepared, without knowing the Hangman's rule, can still run for the tunnel, and its low ceiling and tight bends slow the thing behind them. A party that flees back up the stair has the open yard as refuge until the next night. The Hangman leaves the cistern each dusk to hang its dead in the pine above the gorge; Kenric, or the daybook, knows how long it is gone, and that is time enough to open the pay vault and reach the tunnel while the vault is empty.

## Scene Five: The Pay Vault

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Pay out the treasure and send the party down the mountain.

**Situation.** Behind the newer stone, a short passage leads to the pay vault, a dry room cut in the crag. The captain who walled it up hid here what he could not carry out under attack: the toll chests, the garrison's pay, and his own sword. Beyond the cistern's low end, the drainage tunnel runs down through the crag, cramped and wet, and comes out on the gorge face below the slide, where a climb brings the party to the trail and the long way down to the forest steadings.

**NPCs and threats.** Kenric, if he lives, wants the post's silver taken to Caerwynd and says so. He also wants the captain's body taken down.

**Player choices.**

- _Take everything:_ the silver is heavy, and the tunnel and the climb are hard. Some of it stays behind or goes down the gorge.
- _Keep faith:_ carry the toll and the pay to Caerwynd with Kenric. The garrison pays a reward, and the crown remembers.
- _Divide it:_ Kenric is one man, wounded and spent. He can be talked round, or left.
- _Bury the dead:_ cutting down the hanged and laying them in the yard costs the rest of the day, and earns Kenric's lasting loyalty.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **garrison's pay**, a chest of crown silver.
- The **toll chests**, in coin and in kind: spice, amber and a bolt of fine cloth taken from a smuggler's ponies.
- The **captain's sword**, a fine blade given by the crown to the captain who walled the vault, with his name and the crown's mark on the blade. His family wants it back, and pays.
- From the Hangman, if it died: the band of dark metal, cut with marks no scholar on Aelwyth has read.

**Consequences.** The party comes out on the gorge face below the slide, with the forest below and the snow above.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that escapes through the tunnel without opening the vault can come back in the spring with Caerwynd's men, if the Hangman has left the post or been killed. If it has not, the vault waits behind its wall, and so does the thing in the cistern.

## Aftermath

- **If the Hangman is dead,** Caerwynd regarrisons Sunderwynd, and the pass is open in the spring.
- **If it lives,** the post is abandoned, and a dead pine above the gorge carries new bodies each season. Smugglers use the other pass.
- **If the band of metal is taken to a scholar,** the marks on it match nothing in any library on Aelwyth. The Synod at Ravenmoor asks to see it.
- **If Kenric lives,** he does not serve under a roof again, and he does not go back up the gorge.
