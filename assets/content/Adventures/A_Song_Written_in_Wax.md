## Overview {#overview}

[[place-sngngtrn|The Singing Tarn]] lies in a corrie at the head of the best summer meadow in its part of [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]]. On still evenings voices rise from the black water and sing in no tongue anyone knows, and a listener who answers walks into the tarn. This summer it takes a herd boy, Tamrik, who goes up after a stray at dusk. His mother, [[being-rupenamother|Rupena]], sits at the shore every evening outside the Wardens' line, and the watch twice carries her down.

On the ridge above the corrie an [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] scholar, [[being-palvenorsel|Palven Orsel]], waits for a still night with hired guards, a clerk who writes music and a jar of wax for the guards' ears. He wants the song on paper. The party is hired to guard the clerk through one still night at the shore, against the watch and against the tarn.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The song.** The singers take only one who answers. A listener who stays silent, however long and however near, is never taken, and wax in the ears keeps a man from answering. The danger is the song answered without meaning to: a hum, a companion's name, a child's voice joining in.

**The taken.** Those who walk in are not drowned. They come up on the islet at the dark of the moon, standing in a ring, singing with the others in their own voices, and they go down again before first light. Tamrik is in the ring. The pale thing seen on the islet on a clear night is the ring. The Tradition Keepers' list of the taken is a list of the living, and the Keepers have never told the herders so.

**The counter-song.** The Wardens of the meadow sing a counter-song, loud, plain and long, until the tarn falls silent for the night. It does not drive the singers off. It gives the tarn a song it has no answer to. Nobody taken has ever been brought back, and nobody among the Áelendan has tried.

**The scholar.** Palven holds that the song is wind across the corrie lip, shaped by the water. His clerk, [[being-lisraventha|Lisra Venthal]], has heard the song and does not agree. She writes music and has the best ear in Péravel, and she is afraid that she will answer. The wax she is to wear in her ears is made for the guards, not for her, because Palven needs her to hear.

**The watch.** [[being-aldrethtirin|Aldreth]] of the [Tírindi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} sits the shore alone this week. His partner has fallen ill with a fever, and a Tradition Keeper must come from [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] to teach the counter-song to a replacement. Until then Aldreth sings it himself, and he is tiring.

**Shardbeaks.** The birds of the high meadows perch on the herders' cairns and dive at anyone who carries a working or a worked thing. A clerk's pen and a scholar's instruments both count.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The mercenary.** Palven hires the party to guard Lisra and keep the watch from the shore for one still night.
- **The healer.** Rupena asks the party to sit with her at the shore one evening when the Wardens are elsewhere.
- **The messenger.** Aldreth needs someone to fetch a Tradition Keeper from Ésalosa before the next still night, and someone to sit with him until she comes.
- **The wanderer.** A herder at the meadow cairns will pay for word of the boy, living or dead, and has been told by the Wardens to ask no one.

## The Opening {#opening}

The ridge above the corrie, at the hour the sun leaves the peaks. Palven has his instruments laid out on a blanket and a wax tablet on his knee. Lisra stands apart, with her hand to her throat.

> "Two hours of quiet water and the song begins," Palven says. "Lisra writes it down, the guards hear nothing, and nobody goes in. It is the simplest contract I have offered this year."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Meadow

**Purpose.** The herders, the cairns and the grief.

**Situation.** The high meadow below the corrie, grazed short to a line of cairns and long above it. Herders bring their flocks down before dusk.

**NPCs and threats.** Rupena, who has not slept since Tamrik went. The herders, who will not talk to an Order guard. A shardbeak on a cairn.

**Player choices.** Ask the herders what they know, sit with Rupena, walk the line, or go straight to the ridge.

**Clues and rewards.** The herders say the drowned sing with the others and that a mother who listens long enough hears her child. They say the Keepers do not deny it. A herder shows the party the line of cairns and tells them the Wardens carry an outsider down once.

**Consequences.** A party that treats the herders with respect has an ally at the shore, and one that does not has the herders' dogs on its heels.

**If bypassed.** Rupena goes up alone at dusk and the watch carries her down a third time.

### Scene Two: The Ridge

**Purpose.** The contract, the clerk and the wax.

**Situation.** Palven's camp on the ridge: a tent, a table, the jar of wax and a rack of ruled tablets. Eight guards.

**NPCs and threats.** Palven, brisk and patient. Lisra, quiet and afraid. The guards' captain, who has done this work before and has cut his own ears.

**Player choices.** Take the contract, ask why the clerk has no wax, warn Palven, or tell Aldreth.

**Clues and rewards.** Lisra tells the party in a low voice that the song is not wind, and that the clerk who hears it does not always write what she hears.

**Consequences.** Palven's contract holds if the party shares what it learns with him. It does not hold if the party shares it with Aldreth.

**If bypassed.** Palven hires the captain's men for the night and puts wax in his own ears.

### Scene Three: The Still Night

**Purpose.** The song and its answer.

**Situation.** The shore, a narrow beach of gray shingle, on the one night of the month when the water is perfectly still. The watch's fire pit stands well back, facing away from the water. A bare islet of gray stone lies a long bowshot out.

**NPCs and threats.** The singers. Aldreth, who sings the counter-song until his voice breaks. Lisra, who writes without wax. A guard who forgets himself and hums.

**Player choices.** Stand with Aldreth and sing the counter-song, guard the clerk, stop Lisra from writing, or stay silent and watch.

**Clues and rewards.** A silent party sees the pale ring on the islet and the herd boy among it, singing in his own voice. The counter-song falls silent at last. Lisra's pen has written a song the Order has never held.

**Consequences.** A guard who answers walks in. A clerk who answers is lost unless somebody holds her. A party that keeps silent keeps everyone.

> [!WARNING]
> The song is answered by accident. A hum, a name called to a friend, a child's voice. Anyone at the shore who is not wearing wax is at risk, and the clerk is the person Palven needs unwaxed.

**If bypassed.** The guards fall to humming, and two walk in.

### Scene Four: What the Keepers Keep

**Purpose.** The choice about the song and the boy.

**Situation.** The morning after, at the shore with the Wardens and the Order's camp, or at Ésalosa Lodge with a Tradition Keeper who has come down.

**NPCs and threats.** Palven, who wants the song. Aldreth, who counts it as something taken from an Araelwen. The Keeper, who has the list of the taken and will not say what it is.

**Player choices.** Give Lisra's pages to Palven, give them to the Wardens, burn them, or tell Rupena what the ring on the islet is.

**Clues and rewards.** The Keeper confirms that every name on the list is alive at the dark of the moon. She asks the party to tell no herder. She does not ask them to tell Rupena that Tamrik is dead.

**Consequences.** A party that tells Rupena what it saw gives her a living son at the dark of the moon and a reason to sit at the shore every month. The Wardens carry her down each time.

**If bypassed.** Palven sends the pages to Péravel by courier, and Rupena sits at the shore every evening until the snow.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Palven's guards hold the shore for one night without a watch to stop them. Two guards hum and walk in. Lisra writes the song and nearly answers it. Aldreth, worn out, sings the counter-song badly, and a third guard is taken. The Wardens carry Rupena down a third time. Palven sends the pages to the University, and the Order's theorists decide the song is wind.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The Order holds a written song of the Singing Tarn, and the Wardens count the pages as taken from an Araelwen. Lisra's ear is never quite the same. The ring on the islet gains a few voices. The Keepers' list grows by whatever names the party's night adds. Rupena sits at the shore at the dark of the moon, or she does not.
