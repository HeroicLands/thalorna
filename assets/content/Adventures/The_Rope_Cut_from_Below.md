## Overview {#overview}

[[place-hldhr|The Held Hour]] is a bowl of pale marble above the tree line on [[place-drenavrspn|the Drenavar Spine]], lit all day and all night by a sun that stands where it stood. Two [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] sit at its lip with their backs to the light. In a fold of the pasture below the ring of mark-stones, out of their sight, an [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] party is camped under a crown license to survey the Spine and no leave from anyone who keeps it. Its fellow, [[being-hetharpelth|Hethar Pelthal]] of the [[affiliation-univperavel|University of Péravel]], hires the party as guards for the season. The contract names him and a season and no ground. His first task for them is to keep the watch busy for an hour while a man goes down on a rope.

The man has gone down already. The party reaches the bowl as the Order's camp is beginning to understand that.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The bowl.** Time on the floor runs far faster than time at the lip. An hour on the lip is the better part of a week below, and a day on the lip is close to a season. The light never moves, so nobody inside can tell how long he has been there, and hunger and sleep are the only clocks.

**The surveyor.** [[being-taldorlidre|Taldor Lidren]] went down on the rope early on the morning before the party arrives, with a sighting-rod and a water-clock, meaning to be drawn up in an hour. On the floor he waited a month for the rope to move. He cut it from below himself, because it hung slack and he took the slackness for abandonment. He is alive, thin, bearded and nearly mad, living on the last of his stores and the rain that runs down the rock, and he keeps a tally of his sleeps on the stone. His notes are the most exact record of an Araelwen the Order has ever had, and he knows it. His colleagues have stood at the lip for half a day.

**The camp.** Hethar knows the bowl disturbs time and does not know by how much. His water-clock went down with Taldor, and a clock carried into the bowl runs at the bowl's pace and tells its bearer nothing he does not already feel. The fellow's own instruments, left on the lip, tell the truth, and nobody has read them. Hethar wants Taldor's notes more than he wants Taldor, and does not say so to his guards.

**The watch.** [[being-marennedhar|Marenne]] of the [Tírindi]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} and her partner [[being-orpethward|Orpeth]] have the lip. It is summer and the herds are up, and the third Warden of the watch is down the mountain with a sick child. Marenne has seen the Order's tracks below the ring, and she is one Warden short of turning them back by force.

**The law.** Warden leave cannot take an outsider into the bowl, and taking out anything from an Araelwen, a page of notes included, is desecration. A party that descends is warned once. The royal magistrates hear no complaint from anyone hurt at an Araelwen, and their first question is whether he was warned.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The rope was cut from below. Whoever pulls on it finds it slack, and the cut end is clean. Nobody in the camp has looked at it, because they have been watching the Wardens.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief or the scout.** Hethar needs the Wardens' attention off the lip for an hour, and pays half in advance.
- **The Warden's friend.** Marenne asks the party to sit the lip with her through a summer day, and to turn back the Order's camp.
- **The mercenary.** Hethar's guard captain, [[being-ermintarve|Ermin Tarvenel]], has lost a man down the cleft and wants the party to go after him, no questions asked of him.
- **The leader.** A courier of the University carries the party a letter from the Fitter of Expeditions, asking for the surveyor's notes by name and for nothing else.

## The Opening {#opening}

The Order's camp in the fold of the pasture, an hour before the Wardens change the watch. Hethar sets out the contract on a folding table with a water-clock beside it.

> "One hour," he says. "The man is on a rope and a rope is an hour. Keep the Wardens at the ring and nobody is hurt."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Camp

**Purpose.** The contract, the camp and the first sign that something is wrong.

**Situation.** Hethar's camp below the ring: a surveyor's tent, a cart-load of rope, and a rope that runs up the slope and over the lip. Hethar and Ermin are not as calm as they were at breakfast.

**NPCs and threats.** Hethar, scholarly and anxious. Ermin, who wants the work finished before he has to explain a missing man. The camp's guards, who have been told the Wardens warn once.

**Player choices.** Take the contract, ask what the rope is for, or ask where the man is.

**Clues and rewards.** The rope has been slack for what the camp thinks is half a day. The man was due up in an hour. A clock on the lip table reads half a day, and one of the guards says the sun at the bowl never changes.

**Consequences.** A party that asks the right questions learns that nobody in the camp has pulled the rope, for fear of the Wardens.

**If bypassed.** Ermin's men haul on the rope at the change of the watch and bring up the cut end.

### Scene Two: The Lip

**Purpose.** The Wardens, the law and the ring.

**Situation.** The ring of mark-stones and the lip beyond it. Marenne and Orpeth sit with their backs to the light, and warm afternoon sun falls on anyone at the edge whatever the hour.

**NPCs and threats.** Marenne, courteous and exact. Orpeth, young and very tired. Shardbeaks perch on the mark-stones and dive at whoever carries a working or a worked thing, the Order's instruments included.

**Player choices.** Warn the Wardens, create a diversion, walk up and ask, or offer to sit the watch.

**Clues and rewards.** Marenne says what the Wardens say to everyone: once, plainly, at the ring. She will not say what is in the bowl, but she will say that no Áelendan goes down and that a herdsman went down once and came up gray. If the party sits the watch, she lets a day pass at the lip without anyone coming near it, and the party sees how long a day is.

**Consequences.** A party that deceives the Wardens is a party the Wardens know on sight afterward.

**If bypassed.** The Order's guards rush the lip at the change of the watch, and Orpeth is hurt.

### Scene Three: The Cleft

**Purpose.** The descent and the bowl.

**Situation.** The one way down is a cleft in the rim with a scramble of fallen blocks. A cairn on the last block was built by no Áelendan. On the floor, a sighting-rod, a coil of cut rope, a tin cup and a ring of stones round old ashes.

**NPCs and threats.** The bowl itself. A party that goes down lives longer below than the lip sees, and a party that goes down without a rope held above it has nothing to climb back on. Taldor, who does not recognize the people who find him.

**Player choices.** Descend, send one, drop a line from the lip, or leave the surveyor.

**Clues and rewards.** The tally on the stone is long, and the stone around it is scored with the days. Taldor hands over his notes without being asked, and begs the party not to let go of the rope.

**Consequences.** A party that spends the better part of a week on the floor comes up to find that only an hour has passed at the lip. It is hungrier, wearier and grayer than the lip's clock can account for, and the Order's camp reads that clock.

> [!WARNING]
> A water-clock carried into the bowl runs at the bowl's pace and tells the bearer what he already feels. Only a clock left on the lip tells the truth. A party that trusts the clock in its hand cannot tell that every hour its companions wait at the lip costs it nearly a week of food, sleep and nerve below.

**If bypassed.** Ermin's men bring up the rope, cut, and Hethar sends someone else.

### Scene Four: The Notes

**Purpose.** What to do with an exact record of a forbidden place.

**Situation.** The lip, the camp and the Wardens at the ring, with a surveyor in a blanket and a bundle of notes.

**NPCs and threats.** Hethar, who wants the notes. Marenne, who counts them as something taken from an Araelwen. Taldor, who has not slept properly for what he thinks is a month. The Order's guards, who are paid to stand between a scholar and a Warden.

**Player choices.** Give the notes to Hethar, to Marenne or to the Fitter's courier, burn them, or walk off with them.

**Clues and rewards.** The notes are the sole thing in the world that sets out what the bowl does by the hour. Whoever holds them holds a map of how to survive in the Held Hour, and how to be lost in it.

**Consequences.** The Order sells the notes to the highest bidder in Péravel, or the Wardens destroy them at the lip, or Taldor keeps them. Each outcome changes who is willing to go down the next time.

**If bypassed.** Hethar takes the notes by the rope and the Wardens let the camp go for the sake of the man.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Ermin's men haul on the rope at the change of the watch, bring up its cut end and send a second man down. Orpeth is hurt at the ring. Marenne rings the alarm to the Spine, and a company of Wardens arrives within three days. The second man comes up gray and silent after a quarter of an hour at the lip, and Taldor is never seen again.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Taldor, if he lives, carries the Held Hour in him for the rest of his life. The notes sell for a fortune at Péravel or are burned at the ring. The Wardens close the pasture to the Order, and the crown receives a petition from the Grove Council. The fellow who hired the party writes to the Fitter of Expeditions, and the Fitter hires more guards.
