A landslide strands the party below a forgotten binding temple in the eastern rainforest of Ne'ok Ba'ate. A priestess of the storm-serpent's serpent-keepers has opened the temple's sealed chamber to recover a seal-stone, trusting a register that calls its guardian dead. The guardian is a Living Work of the Builder, one of a kind, and it is awake, and it is hungry.

## Overview

[[place-kulqattzumul|Kul'qat Tz'umul]], the Red Steps, is a binding temple of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|P'uqil Ch'aqun]] raised by the builder-priests of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|P'iqal Kul'qat]] and shut so long ago that the forest has taken its road. The serpent-keepers of Ne' Tz'umul have sent **Ix'mel Tz'uma Ch'upul**, a Warden of the Sealed Chambers, to bring back its seal-stone and its codices before the seals are next inspected. The Warden of the Ruins records the temple's guardian as failed. It is not. **Ch'al Kul'iq**, the strength beneath, is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]] set over the sealed chamber, and when Ix'mel raised the barrier it came up the stair behind her.

Three facts carry the adventure, and the party can learn each of them in more than one place:

| What the party needs to know                                | Where it can be learned                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| It keeps to the builders' stone, and bare earth is safe.    | The ferns pressed flat only along the buried paving; the carvings' border of fitted stone; Ix'mel; the guard |
| Blood spilled on stone draws it.                            | The carvings of the offering bowls; the dead porter's trail; its own turn toward a wound in Scene Three      |
| The shaft and its gate, or the carrying pillar, can end it. | The carvings; the machinery in the gate gallery; Ix'mel; the builder's glyph cut on the pillar itself        |

Its own sign goes with it everywhere: lamp flames lean toward it before it comes.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Ne' Tz'umul jade-dealer has heard that the serpent-keepers sent a Warden into the eastern forest with porters and an empty chest, and wants to know what she is bringing back.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A master merchant hires the party to escort two baggage animals of cacao and feather-work over the eastern trail to the coast before the worst of the rains.
- **The leader.** A Ha'nalmah of the eastern valleys sends the party to find out why the serpent-keepers' party has not come back down the trail, because his district supplied its porters.
- **The lone wanderer.** One of the Warden's guards, **Ch'imik Ha'nal**, is the wanderer's cousin, or friend, or debtor, and has not come home.

## The Opening

The rain has not stopped for three days. Read or paraphrase:

> The hillside lets go above you with a sound like a drum heard underwater, and when it is over the trail is gone, and both baggage animals with it, somewhere down the gorge in the brown water. Behind you there is only mud and broken trees. Ahead, through the rain, red steps climb out of the forest toward a temple on the shoulder of the mountain. Something lies across the lowest stair. Your guide, Ha'ik Tz'alu, goes a few steps closer and stops. It is a human hand. Then the water running down the stair turns it over, and there is nothing attached to it.

## Scene One: The Lowest Stair

**Purpose.** Get the party onto the temple, past a man who has been told to let nobody up.

**Situation.** The red stair climbs a stepped platform eight times a person's height to a temple doorway. Halfway up, under the stair's overhang, sits a wounded temple guard with a spear across his knees. Beside the gate at the top of the first flight lies his companion in a cuirass of hardened hide and wooden slats, face down. The guard's orders are to keep the stair, and he keeps it.

**NPCs and threats.** **Tz'okum Ne'ok** is a guard of the serpent-keepers' household, with a broken arm bound in his belt and a fever coming on. He bars the stair, threatens, then bargains. His companion, **Ch'imik Ha'nal**, is dead. Something moves through the forest below the platform while the party argues: a long line of ferns bending flat, steadily, the way grass bends under a dragged weight, and the smell of wet stone. Nothing can be seen making it. The guard looks at it and lets the party up.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Take the stair from a feverish man with one arm.
- **Stealth.** Climb the platform's side terraces under the cover of the rain, out of his sight.
- **Talk.** Persuade him, or wait until the moving ferns persuade him.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the moving line. It follows the run of the processional way, and the ferns either side of it do not stir.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the dead man where he lies, or take the time to carry him up out of the rain, which earns the guard's trust and costs the party the light.

**Clues and rewards.** Ch'imik's cuirass is scarcely dented, and under it he is crushed flat, the bones broken small inside unbroken skin; the guard says a stone fell on him in the landslide, and does not believe it. The red on the steps is old plaster wash where the rain runs over it, and new blood where it does not. At the foot of the platform, where the line of ferns ended, a culvert mouth the height of a crouching person opens in the stone and breathes cold air.

**Consequences.** A party that wins Tz'okum gains his knowledge of the temple's upper rooms and his word that the Warden is somewhere inside. A party that forces him leaves a wounded man alone on the stair with something in the forest below.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that goes around the platform instead of up it finds the slope closed by the landslide on one side and the swollen gorge on the other, and the line of bending ferns keeps pace with it along the paving. Night and the rain drive it to the only roof there is. If it never speaks to Tz'okum, he is gone from the stair when it next passes, his spear lying on the step with its shaft snapped, and the culvert at the foot of the platform has a fresh smear at its lip.

## Scene Two: The Blank Wall

**Purpose.** Show the party what happened inside, and give it the carvings that explain the place.

**Situation.** The sanctuary is a pillared hall open to the stair, dim and loud with rain on the roof. Its lamps are overturned and their oil has run in the cracks of the floor. In the middle of the floor is a square shaft under a pivoting stone lid. A line of bloody footprints, a porter's bare feet, runs across the hall and ends at the back wall, which is one blank slab of stone. The slab does not quite meet the floor. There is a gap under it a hand high, and the blood goes into the gap.

**NPCs and threats.** **Tz'ilil Nal'ik**, a boy attendant of the serpent-keepers, is wedged into a niche between two pillars and will not come out. He says the Warden raised the wall to go down for the Storm's Eye, the seal-stone, because the register said the guardian was dead; that something came up the stair behind her; that the porters dropped the wall; that the pin broke; and that one of them did not get out of the way. The Warden is still inside the wall, he thinks, in the gallery where the pin goes.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Try to lever the slab. It moves, a little, and something below it moves too.
- **Stealth.** Find the service stair inside the thick wall that the boy says leads to the gallery, and go down quietly.
- **Talk.** Get the boy out of his niche and talking, which takes patience and food.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the carvings that run around the hall at eye height.
- **Giving something up.** Pour a little blood at the lip of the shaft, as the carvings show the priests doing, and watch.

**Clues and rewards.** The carvings show priests lowering the barrier while a shape reaches under it; priests pouring bowls at the lip of the shaft while the shape lies across the lid and the lid tips; and a border of fitted stones around every scene, which the shape never crosses. One pillar near the back of the hall carries a builder's glyph, cut deep, for the pillar that carries the roof. Blood poured at the shaft's lip brings, after a long while, a scrape from below, and every lamp flame in the hall leans toward the shaft.

**Consequences.** A party that pours blood learns the lure works, and lets the guardian know where the party is. A party that levers the slab lets it know too.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reads the carvings meets them again in the gallery in Scene Three, where the same scenes are cut above the machinery as instructions. A party that never finds the service stair is found by Ix'mel, who comes up it bleeding and shouting for help with the pin. If the boy is never coaxed out, the Warden tells the same story herself.

## Scene Three: The Broken Pin

**Purpose.** The guardian comes near, and the party learns it has more than one way to reach them.

**Situation.** The service stair winds down inside the wall to the gate gallery, a narrow stone room with the barrier's counterweight hanging in a slot, a beam and pin that hold it, and a squint that looks down onto the descending stair beyond the barrier. **Ix'mel Tz'uma Ch'upul** kneels at the beam, trying to drive a hardwood pin into a socket where the obsidian pin broke off. She has a lamp, a satchel and a bad cut on her forearm. Beyond the barrier something heavy scrapes, once, and stops. Then the same scrape answers from behind the party, from the passages at the foot of the service stair. The sealed chamber has a second way out, and the culvert at the foot of the platform is it.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ix'mel Tz'uma Ch'upul**, Ix'mel to her household, is proud, frightened and quick. She opened the chamber because the register told her the guardian had failed, and she did not tell her porters what the carvings showed because she did not believe them. She wants the Storm's Eye, which she did not reach, and she wants to live. **Ch'al Kul'iq** is in the passages and on the stair. It does not hurry. Up in the sanctuary, Tz'okum, if he is still alive, begins to shout and then stops.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Hold the gallery door against whatever comes up the service stair.
- **Stealth.** Put out the lamp and keep still. It feels a tread on stone, and a still body on stone is harder for it to find.
- **Talk.** Make Ix'mel tell the whole truth, including what the register said and what she did not tell the porters.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Pin the barrier with something that will hold, which means a spear-shaft, a sword or a crowbar driven into the socket and left there.
- **Giving something up.** Use Ix'mel's wound, or a volunteer's, to lay a trail of blood away from the gallery.

**Clues and rewards.** Ix'mel confirms how the shaft lid turns and how the pit gate drops, and that the gallery holds the levers for both. She knows the paving rule, because the specification is in her branch's copy of the plans, and she knows which pillar carries the sanctuary roof, because the builders mark it on every temple they raise. When she bleeds, every lamp flame in the gallery leans toward the stair.

**Consequences.** A barrier pinned with a party's weapon holds, and the party has one weapon less for Scene Four. A barrier left unpinned rises behind the party as soon as the guardian finds it, and the sanctuary is open to it. The way back down the red stair is unsafe now, because the guardian has come up through the culvert and its paving runs to the foot of the stair.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** Whatever happens here, the guardian comes into the sanctuary. If the party never reaches the gallery, Ix'mel's pin fails, the barrier rises, and Ch'al Kul'iq comes up the main stair into the hall where the party stands. If the party flees the temple, the lightning shows a shape on the red stair below it, and the only bare ground in reach is the landslide mud; the party can wait there through the night, soaked and watched, and climb back at dawn to a sanctuary with one fewer person in it.

## Scene Four: Between the Lightning

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the Living Work, in its own building.

**Situation.** The storm is overhead. The sanctuary is dark between flashes, and each flash shows a different piece of the hall: a pillar, the shaft lid, the boy's niche, and then part of something that was not there in the last flash. The guardian enters from the barrier stair, the service stair or the culvert, wherever the party is not. It is seen only in fragments: a curve of grey hide plated like river-stone, wider than the doorway it came through; a weight settling; a pillar shifting on its base.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ch'al Kul'iq** seizes one person at a time, lies upon them, and draws back into a passage too narrow for anyone to follow, dragging the victim with it. It returns. It is drawn to blood on stone and to a tread it can feel. It has wits of its own and will not cross the shaft lid twice in the same way. **Ix'mel** works the gallery levers if the party asks her to and has kept her trust, and runs for the river landing if it has not.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight it as it comes, which is possible and very costly.
- **Stealth.** Keep still between flashes and let it pass, then reach the gallery or the stair.
- **Talk.** Bring Ix'mel to the levers and Tz'ilil out of his niche, so that someone is on the pins when it matters.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Lure it onto the shaft lid with blood, draw the pin, and drop the pit gate behind it. Or break the pillar that carries the roof, and bring the sanctuary down on it.
- **Giving something up.** The lure needs fresh blood, and a party that has none to spare has to make some.

**Met prepared.** A party that knows the lure, the levers and the paving can choose the ground: blood on the lid, someone at the gallery levers, and the rest still and silent among the pillars, well back from the lid. When it settles across the lid the pin is drawn, the lid turns, and it drops; the pit gate falls behind it and closes it off from its chamber. Breaking the carrying pillar works too, and brings the roof down on it and on everything in the hall; the barrier stair is buried, and the chamber below can be reached only by the culvert.

**Met unprepared.** A party that knows none of this can still live. Bare earth is the refuge, and it learns that when the first person to stumble off the paving into the mud is not followed. It can wait out the night there, and the guardian, once it has fed, draws back to its chamber and lies still for a long while, which is when the party can go in.

**Consequences.** A guardian dropped into the pit is alive and shut in, and the pit gate is all that holds it. A guardian under the fallen roof is buried with the sanctuary and the barrier stair. A guardian that has fed and gone back below is still there.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party driven off the platform reaches the processional way and the river landing, where the paving ends at the water's edge. The guardian will not leave the stone, so the landing's mud is safe, and the culvert from the landing back to the chamber is the way in once it sleeps.

## Scene Five: The Storm's Eye

**Purpose.** Pay the reward, and let the party out of the forest by the river.

**Situation.** The sealed chamber is a low vault cut into the bedrock beneath the sanctuary, reached by the barrier stair or the culvert. It is full. Generations of offerings lie in it where they were thrown down the shaft or carried in: jade, gold, obsidian, cloth rotted to dust, bones. On a shelf in the back wall, in a stone box under a lid of jade, are the serpent-keepers' codices, bound in serpent skin, and on the box lies the Storm's Eye.

**NPCs and threats.** If the guardian lives, this is where it sleeps. **Ix'mel** knows the offerings. Some of what lies here is wealth; some of it runs the temple. The rack of long obsidian blades by the stair are spare pins for the barrier and the lid, and the jade weights in the wall niches are the counterweights' trim. A party that takes those leaves the barrier and the pit gate unable to hold.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Take everything, defenses included, and leave the temple open.
- **Stealth.** Take only what Ix'mel calls safe, while the guardian sleeps or is shut away.
- **Talk.** Bargain with Ix'mel over the codices and the Storm's Eye, which her branch wants and the party has earned.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the register's error into the record: tell the Warden of the Ruins what the party found, for a builder's fee.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the codices for the serpent-keepers, or carry them out for someone who will pay more.

**Clues and rewards.** Jade ear-spools, pendants and plaques of ritual quality; gold ritual vessels, heavy and old; obsidian blades of fine work that are not pins; and the Storm's Eye, a jade disk the size of a palm with the serpent's eye cut through it. The codices are the serpent-keepers' calculations of the end of the age. They are worth more to the branch than the rest of the chamber together, and more again to the heretics the branch hunts.

**Consequences.** A party that hands the Eye and the codices to Ix'mel earns her branch's gratitude, which is real and watchful. A party that keeps the codices has something the serpent-keepers will want back and the Tz'umaq K'alanil will kill for. A party that took the pins has left Ch'al Kul'iq a way out.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that fled to the landing without the chamber can come back by the culvert on a later night, when the guardian has fed and lies still, and Ix'mel, if she lived, knows the hour of it. The processional stair leads from the platform down to a sheltered landing on the river, where a canoe left by the Warden's party is drawn up under the trees, and the river runs down to the coast.

## Aftermath

- **If the guardian is shut in the pit.** The temple holds its Living Work again, and the register at Ne' Tz'umul is corrected, if anyone tells the Warden of the Ruins.
- **If the sanctuary came down.** The Red Steps are a mound under the forest, and the builder-priests enter it in their register as a collapse, under load, in the rains.
- **If the guardian fed and lives free.** It keeps to its stone, and the stone runs to a river landing that traders can reach.
- **If the codices reached the serpent-keepers.** Ix'mel presents the Storm's Eye at the next inspection before witnesses of two other branches, and her branch remembers who carried it.
- **If the codices went elsewhere.** The serpent-keepers begin to look for the party.
