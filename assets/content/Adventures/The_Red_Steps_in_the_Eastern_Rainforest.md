A landslide strands the party below a forgotten binding temple in the eastern rainforest of Ne'ok Ba'ate. A priestess of the storm-serpent's serpent-keepers has opened the temple's sealed chamber to recover a seal-stone, trusting a register that calls its guardian dead. The guardian is a Living Work of the Builder, one of a kind, and it is awake, and it is hungry. The priestess says she never reached the seal-stone. The guardian, which goes wherever the seal-stone goes, says otherwise.

## Overview

[[place-kulqattzumul|Kul'qat Ts'umul]], the Red Steps, is a binding temple of [[affiliation-puqilchaqun|P'uqil Ch'akun]] raised by the builder-priests of [[affiliation-piqalkulqat|P'iqal Kul'qat]] and shut so long ago that the forest has taken its road. The serpent-keepers of Ne' Ts'umul have sent **Ish'mel Ts'uma Ch'upul**, a Warden of the Sealed Chambers, to bring back its seal-stone, the Storm's Eye, and its codices before the seals are next inspected. The Warden of the Ruins records the temple's guardian as failed. It is not. **Ch'al Kul'iq**, the strength beneath, is a [[lore-dreadspawncrtr|dreadspawn]] set over the sealed chamber, and when Ish'mel went down for the Eye it came up the stair behind her.

The party learns how the guardian works from several sources, and each fact can be had more than one way:

| What the party needs to know                                | Where it can be learned                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| It keeps to the builders' stone, and bare earth is safe.    | The ferns pressed flat only along the buried paving; the carvings' border of fitted stone; Ish'mel; the guard |
| Blood spilled on stone draws it.                            | The carvings of the offering bowls; the dead porter's trail; a scrape from below when blood is poured         |
| The shaft and its gate, or the carrying pillar, can end it. | The carvings; the machinery in the gate gallery; Ish'mel; the builder's glyph cut on the pillar itself        |

Its own sign goes with it everywhere: lamp flames lean toward it before it comes.

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the temple—what the guardian is, what the Storm's Eye does to it, and what happened on the stair—is in [[place-kulqattzumul#truth|the truth of Kul'qat Ts'umul]]. In short: Ish'mel reached the Eye. It is in her satchel, and she says she never got that far, because the porters died for it and her branch's standing at the next inspection rests on it. The guardian is not hunting the party. It follows the Eye, and the party is standing next to it. This section covers what that truth does at the table: where the party can find it, how it lands on each route, and what the party wants once it knows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Everything Ish'mel tells the party about the barrier, the levers, the paving and the register is true. The party must be able to find each of those facts without her—in the carvings, the gallery machinery and the pillar's glyph—so that the Eye is the only thing that rests on her word alone.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal belongs in Scene Three, and it comes from the guardian's own conduct. It comes to Ish'mel by every way it has, and it ignores everything else.

- **In the gallery.** The scrape answers from beyond the barrier and then from the foot of the service stair, closing on the room where Ish'mel kneels. A party that lays a blood trail away from the gallery hears the scrape pass the end of the trail and keep coming.
- **In the sanctuary.** A party that never went down hears Ish'mel coming up the service stair, and every lamp flame in the hall leans toward the stair door before she reaches it, then turns to follow her across the floor.
- **With Ts'ilil.** The boy, coaxed out and fed, or frightened by the lean of the flames, says what she told him not to: when she came up the stair she had it in her hand, the round green one.
- **By the culvert.** A party that crawled the culvert in Scene Two has already seen the empty jade box. Ish'mel's denial lands on people who know better.

If the reveal has not landed by Scene Four, the guardian makes it: between the lightning flashes it goes for whichever group holds the satchel, every time, and passes the others by.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early. A party that suspects the satchel in Scene Three and asks to see inside it gets a refusal, then a rush of argument, and finally the Eye on the stone in front of it, with every flame in the room leaning toward it. That party chooses what to do with the Eye before the guardian chooses for it.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on the flames and then stop talking. Say which way they lean, each time, and let the players notice that it is always toward Ish'mel.

> [!WARNING]
> Never let the guardian take a step that the Eye does not explain. It may take someone in its way, and it is drawn to a tread or to blood, but its direction is always toward the satchel. If it chases a party member who is nowhere near the Eye, the reveal has nothing to stand on.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party is no longer only trying to live through the night. It wants the Eye, and Ish'mel will not give it up. With it the party can lay the lure on the lid and drop the guardian into the pit, or throw the Eye down the shaft and send the guardian home to the chamber, or leave Ish'mel on the stone with her satchel and walk into the mud. The question is no longer how to beat the guardian; it is what to do about the woman beside them.

> [!TIP]
> Play Ish'mel's refusal as reasoned and desperate. She names the porters, and the inspection, and the two other branches whose witnesses will watch her present nothing. She offers the party a share of the vault. She does not threaten anyone until someone reaches for the satchel.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Ne' Ts'umul jade-dealer has heard that the serpent-keepers sent a Warden into the eastern forest with porters and an empty chest, and wants to know what she is bringing back.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A master merchant hires the party to escort two baggage animals of cacao and feather-work over the eastern trail to the coast before the worst of the rains.
- **The leader.** A Ha'nalmah of the eastern valleys sends the party to find out why the serpent-keepers' party has not come back down the trail, because his district supplied its porters.
- **The lone wanderer.** One of the Warden's guards, **Ch'imik Ha'nal**, is the wanderer's cousin, or friend, or debtor, and has not come home.

> [!TIP]
> The thief's hook gives one player a reason to watch the satchel from the first scene with Ish'mel. Let that player watch it, and answer every question about it with what can be seen: a leather satchel, heavy, held by the strap, never set down.

## The Opening

The rain has not stopped for three days. Read or paraphrase:

> The hillside lets go above you with a sound like a drum heard underwater, and when it is over the trail is gone, and both baggage animals with it, somewhere down the gorge in the brown water. Behind you there is only mud and broken trees. Ahead, through the rain, red steps climb out of the forest toward a temple on the shoulder of the mountain. Something lies across the lowest stair. Your guide, Ha'ik Ts'alu, goes a few steps closer and stops. It is a human hand. Then the water running down the stair turns it over, and there is nothing attached to it.

> [!TIP]
> Let the hand sit. The opening's job is the first cold certainty that something on this mountain kills people, and the red stair is the only roof in reach.

## Scene One: The Lowest Stair

**Purpose.** Get the party onto the temple, past a man who has been told to let nobody up.

> Halfway up the red stair, under the overhang of a landing, a man sits with a spear across his knees and one arm strapped to his chest. He watches you climb the first steps and does not get up. "Go back down," he says. "Nobody comes up."

**Situation.** The red stair climbs a stepped platform eight times a person's height to a temple doorway. Halfway up, under the stair's overhang, sits a wounded temple guard with a spear across his knees. Beside the gate at the top of the first flight lies his companion in a cuirass of hardened hide and wooden slats, face down. The guard's orders are to keep the stair, and he keeps it.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ts'okum Ne'ok** is a guard of the serpent-keepers' household, with a broken arm bound in his belt and a fever coming on. He bars the stair, threatens, then bargains. His companion, **Ch'imik Ha'nal**, is dead. Something moves through the forest below the platform while the party argues: a long line of ferns bending flat, steadily, the way grass bends under a dragged weight, and the smell of wet stone. Nothing can be seen making it. The guard looks at it and lets the party up.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The line of moving ferns must go along the buried paving and nowhere else. It is the party's first sight of the stone rule, and the first sight of the guardian moving toward the temple.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Take the stair from a feverish man with one arm.
- **Stealth.** Climb the platform's side terraces under the cover of the rain, out of his sight.
- **Talk.** Persuade him, or wait until the moving ferns persuade him. He says the Warden is inside and that the stair is his to keep until she comes down. Asked what happened, he says something came up the stair and lay on Ch'imik, and that it went past him.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the moving line. It follows the run of the processional way, and the ferns either side of it do not stir.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the dead man where he lies, or take the time to carry him up out of the rain, which earns the guard's trust and costs the party the light.

**Clues and rewards.** Ch'imik's cuirass is scarcely dented, and under it he is crushed flat, the bones broken small inside unbroken skin; the guard says a stone fell on him in the landslide, and does not believe it. The red on the steps is old plaster wash where the rain runs over it, and new blood where it does not. At the foot of the platform, where the line of ferns ended, a culvert mouth the height of a crouching person opens in the stone and breathes cold air.

> [!TIP]
> Ts'okum's "it went past me" is true and worth nothing yet. Say it flatly, as a feverish man's puzzle, and let it wait for Scene Three.

**Consequences.** A party that wins Ts'okum gains his knowledge of the temple's upper rooms and his word that the Warden is somewhere inside. A party that forces him leaves a wounded man alone on the stair with something in the forest below.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that goes around the platform instead of up it finds the slope closed by the landslide on one side and the swollen gorge on the other, and the line of bending ferns keeps pace with it along the paving. Night and the rain drive it to the only roof there is. If it never speaks to Ts'okum, he is gone from the stair when it next passes, his spear lying on the step with its shaft snapped, and the culvert at the foot of the platform has a fresh smear at its lip.

## Scene Two: The Blank Wall

**Purpose.** Show the party what happened inside, give it the carvings, and put the boy's one sentence in its ears.

> The sanctuary is a pillared hall, dim and roaring with rain on the roof. Lamps lie overturned, their oil run into the cracks of the floor. A line of bloody bare footprints crosses the hall and ends at the back wall, which is one blank slab of stone. The slab does not quite meet the floor. The blood goes into the gap.

**Situation.** The sanctuary is open to the stair. In the middle of the floor is a square shaft under a pivoting stone lid. The barrier slab hangs a hand short of the floor, and splinters of black obsidian lie at its foot. A carved band runs around the hall at eye height.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ts'ilil Nal'ik**, a boy attendant of the serpent-keepers, is wedged into a niche between two pillars and will not come out. He answers almost nothing. He says the Warden went down for the Storm's Eye, because the register said the guardian was dead; that it came up the stair behind her; that the porters dropped the wall. Pressed for more, he puts his face against the stone. He thinks the Warden is inside the wall, where the pin goes.

> [!WARNING]
> Ts'ilil does not say whether the Warden reached the Eye, and a GM who lets him answer that question here gives the reversal away. He has been told to say nothing about it. If asked straight out, he says he did not see, and looks at the floor.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Try to lever the slab. It moves, a little, and something below it moves too.
- **Stealth.** Find the service stair inside the thick wall that leads to the gallery, and go down quietly.
- **Talk.** Get the boy out of his niche and talking, which takes patience and food. He gives the same three sentences, and asks whether the porters will be found.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the carvings that run around the hall at eye height.
- **Giving something up.** Pour a little blood at the lip of the shaft, as the carvings show the priests doing, and watch.
- **By the culvert.** Crawl the culvert from the foot of the platform. It runs under the sanctuary to the sealed chamber.

**Clues and rewards.** The carvings show priests lowering the barrier while a shape reaches under it; priests pouring bowls at the lip of the shaft while the shape lies across the lid and the lid tips; and a border of fitted stones around every scene, which the shape never crosses. One panel shows a rayed disk laid on the shaft lid with the shape lying across it. One pillar near the back of the hall carries a builder's glyph, cut deep, for the pillar that carries the roof. Blood poured at the shaft's lip brings, after a long while, a scrape from below, and every lamp flame in the hall leans toward the shaft.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The disk panel must be described among the others, as one carving of several. It is the clue a party will remember in Scene Four, and it only works if it was seen and passed over.

**The culvert.** A party that crawls the culvert comes out in a low vault cut into the bedrock beneath the sanctuary, heaped with generations of offerings, and hears something breathing slow and heavy in the pit beneath the shaft. On a shelf in the back wall stand two stone boxes. One holds the serpent-keepers' codices, bound in serpent skin. The other, under a lid of jade, is empty, and the lid has been moved: the dust on the shelf is scraped, and there is none on the lid.

> [!TIP]
> Keep the culvert party's visit short and frightening. The breathing in the pit does not stop. A party that takes something from the vault now takes it past the pit, and the guardian, fed and still, lets it go.

**Consequences.** A party that pours blood learns blood draws it, slowly, and lets the guardian know where the party is. A party that levers the slab lets it know too. A party that saw the empty box carries it into Scene Three.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reads the carvings meets them again in the gallery in Scene Three, where the same scenes, the disk panel among them, are cut above the machinery as instructions. A party that never finds the service stair is found by Ish'mel, who comes up it bleeding and shouting for help with the pin, and every lamp flame in the hall leans toward her as she comes. If the boy is never coaxed out, he speaks his three sentences to Ish'mel, in the party's hearing, when she comes up, and she tells him to be quiet.

## Scene Three: The Broken Pin

**Purpose.** The guardian comes near, and the party learns whom it is coming for.

> The service stair winds down inside the wall to a narrow stone room. A counterweight hangs in a slot; a beam crosses it, and a pin socket gapes empty in the beam. A woman kneels at it with a lamp beside her, hammering a stick of hardwood into the socket with the flat of a stone. A leather satchel hangs from her shoulder, and her other hand never lets go of its strap.

**Situation.** The gate gallery holds the barrier's counterweight, the beam and pin that hold it, the levers for the shaft lid and the pit gate, and a squint that looks down onto the descending stair beyond the barrier. **Ish'mel Ts'uma Ch'upul** kneels at the beam, trying to drive a hardwood pin into a socket where the obsidian pin broke off. She has a bad cut on her forearm. Beyond the barrier something heavy scrapes, once, and stops. Then the same scrape answers from behind the party, from the passages at the foot of the service stair. The sealed chamber has a second way out, and the culvert at the foot of the platform is it.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ish'mel Ts'uma Ch'upul**, Ish'mel to her household, is proud, frightened and quick. She says she opened the chamber because the register told her the guardian had failed, and that she did not tell her porters what the carvings showed because she did not believe them. Both are true. She says she never reached the Storm's Eye: the thing came up the stair while she was still going down, and she ran. That is false. The Eye is in her satchel. She wants to live, and she wants to reach the river landing with the satchel. **Ch'al Kul'iq** is in the passages and on the stair, and it does not hurry. Up in the sanctuary, Ts'okum, if he is still alive, begins to shout and then stops.

> [!TIP]
> Play Ish'mel as someone the party wants on its side. She knows the machinery, she is brave at the beam, and she is right about everything except the satchel. When she rebinds her arm, do it in front of the lamp.

> [!WARNING]
> Ish'mel does not react to the flames, the satchel or the boy's sentence. If a player asks what is in the satchel, she says codices and her branch's copy of the plans, tiredly, and that a Warden of the Sealed Chambers lets nobody else carry them. A party that distrusts her should do so on the evidence, never on the GM's tone of voice.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Hold the gallery door against whatever comes up the service stair.
- **Stealth.** Put out the lamp and keep still. It feels a tread on stone, and a still body on stone is harder for it to find. In the dark, the scrape still comes toward the corner where Ish'mel crouches.
- **Talk.** Make Ish'mel tell the whole truth. She gives the register, the porters and the carvings, and holds to the Eye. Fetch Ts'ilil, or confront her with what he said, and the boy, seeing her, says that she had it in her hand.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Pin the barrier with something that will hold, which means a spear-shaft, a sword or a crowbar driven into the socket and left there.
- **Giving something up.** Use Ish'mel's wound, or a volunteer's, to lay a trail of blood away from the gallery. The scrape reaches the end of the trail, pauses, and comes on toward the gallery.

**Clues and rewards.** The gallery carvings repeat the hall's, the disk panel among them, as instructions above the levers. Ish'mel confirms how the shaft lid turns and how the pit gate drops, and that the gallery holds the levers for both. She knows the paving rule, because the specification is in her branch's copy of the plans, and she knows which pillar carries the sanctuary roof, because the builders mark it on every temple they raise. When she bleeds, every lamp flame in the gallery leans toward the stair, and they lean again, with her arm bound and dry, when she moves to the other side of the room.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must show the guardian coming toward Ish'mel and nobody else. The flames, the scrape that ignores the blood trail, and the boy's sentence are three ways to show it; use whichever the party's choices reach, and at least one of them.

**Consequences.** A barrier pinned with a party's weapon holds, and the party has one weapon less for Scene Four. A barrier left unpinned rises behind the party as soon as the guardian finds it, and the sanctuary is open to it. The way back down the red stair is unsafe now, because the guardian has come up through the culvert and its paving runs to the foot of the stair. A party that knows about the Eye now has to decide what to do about Ish'mel before the guardian reaches her.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** Whatever happens here, the guardian comes into the sanctuary, because Ish'mel does. If the party never reaches the gallery, Ish'mel's pin fails, the barrier rises, and she runs up into the hall where the party stands with Ch'al Kul'iq on the stair behind her. If the party flees the temple, the lightning shows a shape on the red stair below it, and the only bare ground in reach is the landslide mud; the party can wait there through the night, soaked and watched, and at dawn find that the shape never came near the mud at all. It went up the stair, after Ish'mel, and the sanctuary has one fewer person in it.

## Scene Four: Between the Lightning

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the Living Work, in its own building, and the choice about the Eye.

> The storm is overhead. Between flashes the hall is black. A flash: a pillar, the shaft lid, the boy's niche. Black. A flash: the same pillar, and beside it a curve of gray hide plated like river-stone, where there was nothing a breath ago.

**Situation.** The sanctuary is dark between flashes, and each flash shows a different piece of the hall. The guardian enters from the barrier stair, the service stair or the culvert, wherever is nearest the satchel. It is seen only in fragments: a curve of gray hide plated like river-stone, wider than the doorway it came through; a weight settling; a pillar shifting on its base.

**NPCs and threats.** **Ch'al Kul'iq** goes for whichever group holds the satchel, every time. It takes whoever is in its way, lies upon them, and draws back into a passage too narrow for anyone to follow, dragging the victim with it. It returns, toward the satchel. It is drawn to blood on stone and to a tread it can feel, and it has wits of its own: it will not cross the shaft lid twice in the same way. **Ish'mel** works the gallery levers if the party asks her to and has kept her trust, and runs for the river landing with the satchel if it has not.

> [!TIP]
> If the party splits up in the dark, move the guardian toward the group with the satchel between every flash, and say so in what the flash shows. Two flashes are usually enough.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight it as it comes, which is possible and very costly.
- **Stealth.** Keep still between flashes and let it pass, then reach the gallery or the stair. It passes anyone who does not hold the Eye.
- **Talk.** Bring Ish'mel to give up the Eye, or to the levers, and Ts'ilil out of his niche, so that someone is on the pins when it matters.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Lay the Eye on the shaft lid, draw the pin, and drop the pit gate behind the guardian. Or throw the Eye down the shaft and let the guardian follow it home. Or break the pillar that carries the roof, and bring the sanctuary down on it.
- **Giving something up.** Leave Ish'mel on the stone with her satchel and walk into the mud.

**Met prepared.** A party that knows the lure, the levers and the paving can choose the ground: the Eye on the lid, with or without blood, someone at the gallery levers, and the rest still and silent among the pillars, well back from the lid. When it settles across the lid the pin is drawn, the lid turns, and it drops with the Eye beneath it; the pit gate falls behind it and closes it off from its chamber. A party that throws the Eye down the shaft hears it go, and the guardian turns from the satchel's holder in mid-stride and goes back down. Breaking the carrying pillar works too, and brings the roof down on it and on everything in the hall; the barrier stair is buried, and the chamber below can be reached only by the culvert.

> [!WARNING]
> Laying the Eye on the lid and dropping the guardian costs the Eye. It goes into the pit, and the pit gate shuts it in with the guardian. Make that plain when the party plans it, so that Ish'mel's objection is part of the choice and not a surprise after it.

**Met unprepared.** A party that knows none of this can still live. Bare earth is the refuge, and it learns that when the first person to stumble off the paving into the mud is not followed. Whoever holds the satchel on the mud is safe too, and the guardian waits at the edge of the paving, as close to the satchel as the stone allows, until dawn. Ish'mel, if she is on the mud, says nothing all night.

**Consequences.** A guardian dropped into the pit is alive and shut in, with the Eye under it, and the pit gate is all that holds it. A guardian sent home by the Eye down the shaft lies in its chamber again, as the serpent-keepers left it. A guardian under the fallen roof is buried with the sanctuary and the barrier stair, and the Eye is wherever it was when the roof came down. A guardian still following the Eye follows it to the edge of the paving and no farther.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party driven off the platform reaches the processional way and the river landing, where the paving ends at the water's edge. The guardian will not leave the stone, so the landing's mud is safe. If Ish'mel reaches the landing with the satchel, the guardian comes down the processional way behind her and stops where the paving ends, and lies there.

## Scene Five: The Storm's Eye

**Purpose.** Pay the reward, settle the Eye, and let the party out of the forest by the river.

> The vault is low and cold and full. Jade and gold lie heaped where they fell from the shaft above, among cloth gone to dust and bones gone brown. On a shelf at the back stand two stone boxes, one shut and one with its jade lid set crooked on top.

**Situation.** The sealed chamber is a low vault cut into the bedrock beneath the sanctuary, reached by the barrier stair or the culvert. It is full. Generations of offerings lie in it where they were thrown down the shaft or carried in: jade, gold, obsidian, cloth rotted to dust, bones. On a shelf in the back wall, in a stone box, are the serpent-keepers' codices, bound in serpent skin. Beside them stands a smaller box under a lid of jade, empty.

**NPCs and threats.** If the guardian was sent home by the Eye, this is where it lies, on the Eye, and the party goes quietly or not at all. **Ish'mel** knows the offerings. Some of what lies here is wealth; some of it runs the temple. The rack of long obsidian blades by the stair are spare pins for the barrier and the lid, and the jade weights in the wall niches are the counterweights' trim. A party that takes those leaves the barrier and the pit gate unable to hold.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Take everything, defenses included, and leave the temple open.
- **Stealth.** Take only what Ish'mel calls safe, while the guardian sleeps or is shut away.
- **Talk.** Bargain with Ish'mel over the codices, and over the Eye if it is still above ground.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the register's error into the record: tell the Warden of the Ruins what the party found, for a builder's fee.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the codices for the serpent-keepers, or carry them out for someone who will pay more.

**Clues and rewards.** Jade ear-spools, pendants and plaques of ritual quality; gold ritual vessels, heavy and old; obsidian blades of fine work that are not pins; and the codices, which are the serpent-keepers' calculations of the end of the age. They are worth more to the branch than the rest of the chamber together, and more again to the heretics the branch hunts. The Storm's Eye, a jade disk the size of a palm with the serpent's eye cut through it, is wherever Scene Four left it.

**Consequences.** A party that hands the codices to Ish'mel earns her branch's gratitude, which is real and watchful. A party that keeps them has something the serpent-keepers will want back and the Ts'umaq K'alanil will kill for. A party that took the pins has left Ch'al Kul'iq a way out.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that fled to the landing without the chamber can come back by the culvert on a later night, when the guardian has fed and lies still. The processional stair leads from the platform down to a sheltered landing on the river, where a canoe left by the Warden's party is drawn up under the trees, and the river runs down to the coast.

## Aftermath

- **If the guardian is shut in the pit with the Eye.** The temple holds its Living Work again, and the Eye stays with it. Ish'mel goes home with the codices and without the seal-stone, and the inspection goes against her branch.
- **If the Eye went down the shaft.** The guardian lies in its chamber as the serpent-keepers left it, and the register at Ne' Ts'umul is corrected, if anyone tells the Warden of the Ruins.
- **If the sanctuary came down.** The Red Steps are a mound under the forest, and the builder-priests enter it in their register as a collapse, under load, in the rains.
- **If Ish'mel took the Eye downriver.** Ch'al Kul'iq lies at the landing where the paving meets the water, and traders who put in there do not put out again. Ish'mel presents the Storm's Eye at the next inspection before witnesses of two other branches, and her branch remembers who carried it.
- **If the party took the Eye or the codices.** The serpent-keepers begin to look for the party, and wherever the Eye last left the builders' stone, the guardian lies at the edge of it.
