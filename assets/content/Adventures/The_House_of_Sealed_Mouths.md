A temple scribe needs the burial records of a dwindled house, and the records are at its funerary estate in the desert west of the river, at the end of a canal that is going dry. The party goes up the last of the water and finds the estate's household waiting at the gate with their mouths sewn shut. The steward is courteous, the relief boat is coming, and nobody leaves. The party believes it has walked into a dead man's larder. It has walked into his accounts: the steward never needed the party dead, only its names, and he has them on his forged releases before the first night is out.

## Overview

Read [[place-lutithnet|Lut-Ithnet]] first, and read [[place-lutithnet#truth|what is really there]] before running any scene: who the steward is, what the stitches do, why the house needs witnesses, and why a witness who can speak is a danger to him. The adventure assumes the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] ledger-ethic: an entry left open follows a person to [The Weighing]{.name data-type="lore/theology"}, a release closes an entry only when it is declared before witnesses, and at [[lore-readingweigh|The Reading at the Weighing]] for a dead person anyone the dead owes may stand and refuse to release him. That refusal is the thing the steward has sewn shut.

The adventure runs in five scenes. Each can be met with force, stealth, talk, cleverness with the house itself or a sacrifice, and each states how play carries on if the party skips it, fails it or cuts it short.

**The story as the party first meets it.** A steward who keeps to the shade and will not eat, a household of the sewn dead, a ledger that counts the party, a needle at the keyhole: the party reads a house that means to kill it and raise it, and every sentence of that holds.

**The story as it is.** The party is the villain of someone else's story. [Gezuhati]{.name data-type="being/creature"} needs living strangers on the witness lines beneath the releases he forged for the dead of [Zu'Lemetu]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, and the party's names are on them by the end of the first night, from the receipt signed at the gate or copied from the temple's letter. To the dead of the house, the party are the witnesses who closed their mouths for good. The needle at the keyhole is for the party's own mouths, so that it can never disown the witnessing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The reversal changes what the party wants. Before it, the party wants to get out. After it, walking out is the worst thing the party can do: it leaves every release standing, the steward's accounts closed, and a false entry in each character's own account at the Weighing. What the party wants now is to go down into the hall and unsay its witnessing aloud before the dead.

### How the Reveal Lands

The reveal lands in Scene Three, and it reaches the party by whatever route it took through the night.

- **The second look.** At the end of Scene Two the steward has the newest jar carried to the guest chamber, as the records the temple asked for. When the party looks into it again in the night, the empty witness lines beneath the seven releases carry its names, every one, in the steward's hand, and the travelers' tablet has them too. A character who signed the receipt finds his own signature among them, copied line by line.
- **The steward's answer.** In the passage, by the needle, he does not lie. Asked why the mouths come last, he says that the house has entered its guests as witnesses, that every release on the shelf is witnessed by the temple's agents, and that a witness who can speak can unsay.
- **The skiff.** A party camped at the basin is brought the jar in the small hours by [Thakete]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, who crawled out through the drains with it. She cannot read it. She watched the steward at the lamp with the party's letter, copying, and she says so.
- **The canal bank.** A party that ran carries the jar, because it is what the party came for, and at the sealed head-gate at dawn, with the attendants standing on the bank behind it, it has the light to read it by.

> [!TIP]
> Land the second look on a single detail: a character's own name, in a hand that is not his, on the line beneath a dead woman's release. Read it out and stop. The players need a moment to turn over everything they have assumed about the needle.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early, and never deny a guess that is right. A character who reads the sacred hand and looks hard at the receipt in Scene One has found the trick. Let the steward smile and say the receipt goes beneath the old mistress's last account, which is true; a party that refuses to sign on that basis has earned the advantage of knowing what the needle is for before it arrives.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not let the steward explain the disowning. He never volunteers that a witness can unsay in the hall, and the party learns where a disowning counts from its own knowledge of the Reading, from the cook's _no_, and from the hall itself. A GM who has him say it hands the party its answer.

### What the Party Wants Afterward

Leaving no longer saves anyone. Walking out with the party's names on the releases closes every account the household holds against the steward, and in the valley a false entry damns its witness with its maker, so every character carries the steward's crime to the Weighing. Breaking the tablets does not help: the entry is made, and only the witness can unsay it. What the party wants now is to disown the witnessing aloud, before the dead who can hear it, before the steward sews it shut. The Asking in Scene Four stops being one clever option among many and becomes the point of going down.

## Hooks

A temple scribe of [[affiliation-wethur|Weth'ur]], [Gezedafu]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, holds the older burial records of Zu'Lemetu and has received no new tablets from its estate in years. The house's heirs, a cousin line in a river town, mean to go to law over the estate, and the court wants the estate's own records. Gezedafu hires the party to go up the canal, collect the tablets and bring them back, and he pays a share of whatever salvage the heirs grant on the recovered estate. He gives them a reed skiff and a letter under the temple's seal that names each of them as the temple's agents for the records, and he warns them that the canal is failing.

Other entries suit other heroes:

- **The thief.** A boatman says the house was rich once and the vault in the rock was never opened for an heir.
- **The mercenary.** The heirs hire a guard for the scribe's errand, to see that nothing leaves the estate before the court sits.
- **The kinsman.** A bondsman's family has had no word from a son and a daughter in service at the estate for two seasons. The daughter is Thakete and the son is [Wenlegir]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.

> [!TIP]
> Every hook puts the party's names on the temple's letter. A party that came on another hook travels with Gezedafu's agent, or carries the letter as the price of the skiff.

## The Opening

Read or paraphrase this as the skiff grounds:

> The hull scrapes, slows, and stops. Behind you the last of the canal is already drawing back into cracked black mud, and the river is most of a day downstream. Ahead, under cliffs that have put the whole bay in shadow though the sun is still high, a white house stands over a dry stone basin. Seven people wait on the stair to its gate, in clean linen, perfectly still. None of them opens their mouth.
>
> As you climb out, you see why. Their lips are sewn shut with dark cord, neatly, in small stitches, the way a careful hand closes a seam.

> [!TIP]
> Let the stitches sit before anyone speaks. The opening's job is the first cold certainty that this is not good, and the party should climb the stair already sure it knows what kind of house this is.

## Scene One: The Gate

**Purpose.** To establish the errand and the isolation, to give the party one disturbing detail that still has an ordinary explanation, and to take its names.

> The stair climbs to a bronze gate in a white wall. The seven stand on it in two lines, unlit lamps in their hands, and their eyes follow you a moment after you pass. At the top the gate opens from within, and an old man comes out of the shadow of the gatehouse to meet you. He stops where the shade stops.

**Situation.** The seven attendants stand on the basin stair in two lines. When the party reaches the top, the steward meets it at the gate and does not step into the light on the stair. On a stand in the gatehouse lies a tablet with a reed pen beside it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- Gezuhati. A very old man, upright, with a scribe's ink-stained fingers and a voice like a well-kept ledger. He wears the linen of a great house's steward and a seal-cord at his belt. He apologizes for the household's condition at once and gracefully: the servants have taken a vow of silence in mourning for their mistress, and they sewed their own lips at her burial, as the old families sometimes do. Nobody does, but it sounds as though somebody might. He offers shelter, water and food until the relief boat comes. He sent a man downriver when the water dropped. Asked when that was, he begins to answer, stops and says that the days run together in a house of mourning.
- **The seven attendants.** Men and women of the household, clean and well dressed, with stitched mouths and eyes that follow movement a beat late. They smell faintly of washing-salt and resin. Under their tunics, at the wrist and throat, is linen wrapping. They obey the steward's least gesture and do nothing else. They do not attack anyone in this scene.

> [!TIP]
> Play the steward as the most reasonable man in the valley. He is tired, correct and sorry for the state of things, and he wants the temple's business done properly. Everything about him that is wrong should come from where he stands and what he does not eat, never from his manner.

**Player choices.**

- _Present the letter._ The temple's seal changes his manner. He becomes eager to help, reads the bearers' names twice over, and offers the records that very evening. Then he asks for the temple's receipt, and holds out the tablet from the stand with the pen already inked: one short line in Qalezu under a block in the sacred hand. A character who reads only Qalezu sees an acknowledgment that the temple's agents were received.
- _Accept._ The party walks in and the gate closes behind it. The steward shows them the guest chamber, the well and the record room, and asks whether one of them reads Qalezu.
- _Refuse and camp at the skiff._ The steward bows and leaves them a water jar. At dusk the attendants come down the stair, light their lamps and stand round the skiff at a little distance, facing it, until dawn. The house holds the only water.
- _Look before entering._ A character who skirts the wall toward the cliff sees the tomb galleries, the offering tables bare and dusty, and the drain mouths in the basin wall, each big enough for a crawling man, with a dark crust on their lips.
- _Force._ The gate is bronze on a bar and the wall is twice a man's height. The steward does not resist an entry. He is grieved by it, and he remembers it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The steward must come away from this scene with the party's names. A party that presents the letter gives them to him. A party that keeps the letter in its packs finds, before morning, that its seal has been lifted with a warm blade and pressed down again.

> [!WARNING]
> Describe the receipt as a receipt. Do not linger on the block in the sacred hand, and do not ask which character signs with any weight in the voice. A party that suspects it should do so because a character read the tablet, never because the GM paused.

**Clues and rewards.** The stitches, the salt smell and the wrappings. The steward keeps to shade and does not eat with his guests. The bare offering tables tell anyone raised in the valley that the house's dead have not been fed in years, which is a disgrace no living steward would allow in front of a temple scribe. The receipt, signed or refused. The letter's lifted seal, for a party that did not hand it over.

**Consequences.** A party that enters is inside the walls at dusk. A party that camps outside meets Scene Two's clues on the sand in the night: see below. A party that forced the gate has made the steward cautious, and he moves Wenlegir's preparation forward by a night.

**If bypassed or refused.** Thakete slips out of the house through the drains after dark and crawls across the dry basin to the skiff, and the attendants turn their faces toward her but do not move. She brings a water skin and her brother's name, and points back at the house and down. In the small hours the skiff's water jar is found empty and cracked, and there is no other water this side of the river.

## Scene Two: The Record Room

**Purpose.** To discover that the household is dead, that the records lie, and that a living servant is hiding in the house.

> The record room is shelved to the ceiling with clay jars, each stoppered and marked by generation, the oldest under a skin of dust and the newest clean. The steward sets three of the newest on the table himself, trims the lamp beside them, and goes out. One of the household takes its place in the doorway, and stays there.

**Situation.** The record room holds the house's tablets in clay jars sorted by generation. The steward brings the newest jars and leaves the party to work, with a single attendant standing in the doorway. Along the passage is a door with a new bronze lock, and behind it a preparation room where the dead of the house waited for the hall below in better years. It is still in use.

**NPCs and threats.**

- Gezuhati. Before he leaves, he asks a favor of the party. A few household releases want a witness, and a stranger with no tie to the house is the proper witness for them. He sets the tablets out, with a reed pen, and asks a character who reads Qalezu to set his name beneath them. He is patient about it and comes back to it more than once.
- Thakete. A young bondswoman of the household, alive, gray-faced and thin, hidden among the wrapped bodies on the preparation room's shelves with a bundle of linen pulled over her. She has not been sewn. She has been fed on: she tires walking across a room. She is terrified of the steward and of being heard.
- **The attendant in the doorway.** It watches. It does not stop anyone reading. It turns its head to follow any character who goes into the passage.

> [!TIP]
> Play Thakete with almost no words. She whispers the steward's near name, _Gezu_, as the household always called him, and her brother's name, and she points down. Asked about the stitches, she touches her own mouth and shakes her head. Asked where the steward sleeps, she points down again. Everything else the party learns by looking.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the records._ The burial records are in the sacred hand and the releases in Qalezu. A character trained in one reads only half, and the party has to put the halves together. The halves show seven deaths in the last few years, each one somebody the party met on the stair, with its own closing release of the steward written beside it, and beneath each release an empty line for a witness.
- _Witness the releases._ A character who signs sets his own name beneath the steward's work. It counts for something in Scene Four.
- _Refuse to witness._ The steward accepts the refusal with perfect courtesy and does not ask again. He has no need to.
- _Open the preparation room._ The lock is new and good. A character can pick it while the attendant's attention is drawn off, or break it noisily, or find the key on the steward's seal-cord, which is a matter of getting close to him.
- _Talk to Thakete._ She says what she can, which is very little, and she takes a character's hand and leads it to the basket of cord and needles on the shelf beside her.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The last release in the jar is for the man the steward sent for the relief boat, and his death is entered a month before the day the steward says he set out.
- An earlier tablet, in the old mistress's own hand, records the physician's finding that the steward was dying. No death follows it.
- A fresh tablet opens an entry in Wenlegir's name, and its closing day is already written in.
- The newest tablet opens an entry for _travelers come by the canal_, with a count matching the party's number exactly and the names left blank.
- In the preparation room, a basket of cord stiff with resin, and curved needles.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The party must leave this scene having seen the empty witness lines and the travelers' tablet with its names blank. Scene Three's second look depends on the first. If the party never reads the records, the jar comes to the guest chamber anyway, and the first look and the second are the same look.

> [!TIP]
> Let the travelers' tablet read as a death list. The party's count, a blank for each name, an entry opened and not closed: it reads as the steward counting his next dead, and that reading is the one the reversal turns over.

**Consequences.** A party that finds Thakete has a guide to the house and a person to protect, and she slows them. A party that leaves her hidden finds her gone by morning, taken below, and her name entered after her brother's. At the end of the scene the steward has the newest jar carried to the guest chamber, as the records the temple asked for.

**If bypassed.** If the party never reads the records, Thakete comes to the guest chamber after midnight, and the jar is already there. If they never find Thakete, the knocking under the guest-chamber floor in Scene Three is Wenlegir, and a character who answers it hears him say his name and his sister's. If they never reach the house at all, Thakete brings the jar to the skiff in Scene Three.

## Scene Three: The Needle at the Keyhole

**Purpose.** To show that the threat is active and aimed at the party, that the stitches are the binding, and that the party is already entered in the steward's accounts.

> Through the floor comes a faint, irregular knocking, and then, some time after midnight, a scraping at the door. A needle comes through the keyhole, draws back, and comes through again a finger's width along, pulling dark cord after it. Someone in the passage is sewing the door shut, seam by seam, in small neat stitches, and the cord is stiffening as it dries.

**Situation.** Night. The guest chamber sits over the preparation hall, and the newest jar stands in its corner where the steward had it set. When the party opens the door or looks out, the seven attendants are standing shoulder to shoulder in the passage, and one has the needle.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The attendants.** Tireless, silent and strong as the dead are strong. They do not strike first. They stand in the way, they take hold, and they carry a struggling person below if enough of them get a grip.
- **The one that tears.** When challenged, the oldest of them, a big man in a cook's apron, raises his hands to his own face and rips the stitches out. His mouth opens wide and he screams with no breath behind it, a sound like wind in a jar. Then he turns his head, looks at the character who signed the receipt or the releases, or at the one who carried the temple's letter if nobody signed, and his mouth shapes the refusal from the litany of the Reading: _no_. He falls, and he does not get up.
- Gezuhati. He arrives at the end of the passage with a lamp, as courteous as at the gate. He regrets the noise. He explains, as a host explains a house rule, that the canal is closed, that the house has entered its guests, and that guests of the house remain with the household. He does not lie about any of it now. He says the party will be very comfortable, and that their mouths will be closed only at the very end.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must put the second look in the party's hands: its own names on the witness lines. Through the jar in the room, through the steward's answer, or through Thakete at the skiff, the reveal lands here, before anyone goes below.

> [!TIP]
> Play the cook's look as the most frightening thing in the scene. The dead man spends his one breath of freedom on the party, not on his master. Describe where he looks, say the word, and let the players work out why.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the jar._ The second look. The witness lines are filled, and the names are the party's.
- _Ask the steward._ He answers what he is asked: why the house needs them, what the releases are, why their mouths come last. He does not say what a witness can do about it.
- _Force._ The attendants can be fought in the passage, where only two can come at a time. They do not tire and do not flee. Destroying them is possible. It is slow and loud, and the steward watches it with interest.
- _Cut the stitches._ A character who saw the cook fall can go for the stitches with a knife. Each attendant whose stitches are cut screams its breathless word, at the party, and falls. The steward stops being courteous the first time this happens.
- _Stealth and the roof._ The guest chamber's window gives onto a flat roof and from there onto the cliff path above the tomb galleries. The galleries open onto the hillside, and a character walking the cliff path hears something moving in them.
- _Run for the river._ Over the wall and down the canal bank. The attendants follow at a walk, all night, never closing and never falling back.
- _Parley._ The steward offers terms: witness every release on the shelf in the party's own hands, and the party may leave on the relief boat. There is no relief boat, and the party's names are on the releases already. A character who reads him well knows he is offering what costs him nothing.
- _Give someone up._ He accepts Thakete in exchange for a night's peace and keeps the bargain to the letter: the party is left alone until dusk tomorrow.

> [!WARNING]
> Running for the river is the route that closes most. A party that reaches the river and keeps going leaves its names on the releases and the steward's accounts closed, and it can only undo that by coming back. Make the cost plain through what the party already holds, the jar and the cook's _no_, and do not stop the party going.

**Clues and rewards.** The stitches hold the dead to him, and with them cut, the dead refuse him and the party's witnessing both. The knocking under the floor is Wenlegir, alive, signaling with his heels from a natron bed. The steward's words in the passage leave no doubt what he is, and the names in the jar leave no doubt what the party is.

**Consequences.** By the end of this scene the gate is barred from inside, and the party knows it is a prisoner. Every attendant destroyed is one fewer at the end, and the steward knows the count. A character carried below arrives in Scene Four on a table, unsewn, with until dusk.

**If bypassed.** A party camped at the skiff is brought the jar in the small hours by Thakete, who crawled out through the drains with it clutched to her chest. She cannot read it. She saw the steward at the lamp with the party's letter, copying, and she says so. A party that ran for the river reaches the canal's head-gate at dawn and finds it closed and sealed in fresh mud with the estate's seal. The river is still a long way off, and the attendants are standing on the bank behind them; the jar the party carried has the daylight to be read by. A party that shut itself in hears the stitches go in round its door, and at the darkest hour the door is cut out of its frame and the attendants take the nearest sleeper below. Either way the next scene is the preparation hall, entered by day through the drains or at night as a captive, a rescuer or a witness come to unsay.

## Scene Four: The Preparation Hall

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the steward on his Hallowed Ground, before the dead of the house, where everything the party learned can be turned against him and where its own witnessing can be unsaid.

> The hall is long and low, and it goes back into the cliff further than the lamps reach. Natron beds run down one side, tables down the middle, and on every bed and every shelf lies a wrapped body with its mouth sewn. On the main table an old woman lies wrapped to the throat. Her lips are stitched shut, and her eyes are open, and they are turned toward the stair you came down.

**Situation.** The preparation hall is long, low and cut back into the cliff. Natron beds run down one side and embalming tables down the middle, with gutters in the floor that drain into the dark mouths of the drains. Bronze partitions hang on tracks from the roof beams, so that the hall can be closed into bays for the seventy days of the first grade. At the far end is a stone niche, freshly cut, with a lamp in it and a name above it: Gezuhati's own. Above the main table is a light-shaft, closed with a new bronze shutter bolted from below. On the shelves and the beds lie the bound dead of the household who have not risen yet, every mouth sewn. On the main table lies [Zabegulu]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, the old mistress.

The steward stands at the second table with a curved needle in his hand, finishing Wenlegir. Wenlegir lies in the salt to the chest, alive, his mouth sewn halfway.

**NPCs and threats.**

- Gezuhati. In his own hall he lets the cloak slip. He is taller than he was upstairs, very pale, and the shadow round him is deeper than the lamps account for. He has the strength of his kind and the old steward's patience, and he would rather never fight. He sends the attendants and the risen dead between himself and anyone who comes at him, and works on. He has cord and needles enough for the party. Destroyed anywhere else, he gathers again here after a season or a year. Destroyed here, he is ended.
- **The attendants and the risen dead.** Whatever is left of the seven, and as many of the bound household as he wakes. He wakes them by touching their stitches, one at a time, and it takes him a breath for each.
- [Lizulu]{.name data-type="being/creature"}. The embalmer who made him, bound on a shelf near the light-shaft, sewn like the rest. Lizulu does not move at the steward's touch, because the steward never woke him. His working is cut into the edge of the main table in the sacred hand: _the servant answers his master while the mouth is closed._

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The hall is where the creditors are. Every release the party is entered on names one of the dead lying here, and a disowning said aloud in this room is said before the parties to the entry. Describe the bodies as an audience.

**Player choices.**

- _Disown the witnessing._ A character stands in the hall and declares aloud, before the dead, that his name on the releases is false: that he signed what he did not see, or that his name was copied without his hand. A release whose witness disowns it is no release. Each disowning reopens the entries it was set beneath, every one of them an account against the steward, and he feels each one go. He stops working on Wenlegir at the first and comes at the speaker, because a witness who can speak is the one thing in the hall he cannot sew fast enough.
- _Hold the Asking._ A character who knows the litany of the Reading can read the forged releases aloud from the tablets and ask each dead creditor whether it requires no more. A creditor whose stitches are cut answers _no_. With each refusal entered, the steward is weaker, and he knows exactly what is being done to him. This is what he did all of this to prevent.
- _Cut the stitches._ Every sewn mouth cut open in this hall screams its word and falls, and the hall is full of sewn mouths. Cutting Zabegulu's is the stroke that matters most. She says her _no_ plainly, in a dry clear voice, looking at him, and then lies still.
- _Open the light-shaft._ Somebody has to reach the shutter, draw the bolts and throw it open, while the dead try to stop them. Between mid-morning and mid-afternoon it drops a shaft of sun across the main table, and the steward cannot cross it without great cost. Out of those hours it shows only stars.
- _Use the partitions._ The bronze screens run on their tracks with a long shriek. Closed in the right order, they shut the steward into a bay with nobody between him and the party, or shut the dead out of the bay where Wenlegir lies, or keep him from a character who is still speaking.
- _Use the drains._ The drains are tight crawl-ways, and the dead fit them badly. A party can come in by them, go out by them with Wenlegir, or lure part of the household into them and close a partition behind.
- _Force, at night._ This is the unprepared fight, against a tereb on his own ground and a hall of the dead. It can be survived. It is not likely to be won by force alone.
- _Force, by day._ A party that comes up the drains by day finds him in his niche, slow and dulled by the hour. The dead are still on their feet.
- _Wake Lizulu._ Cutting the embalmer's stitches lets him speak for one breath. He spends it on his _no_, looking at the steward, and lays his hand flat on the working cut into the table's edge.
- _Bargain._ The steward offers the vault, the heirs' records and Wenlegir for the party's silence and departure. He means it, as far as the canal head, and he means _silence_ exactly.

> [!TIP]
> Let the disowning be loud and slow. A player who stands and says the words in character, over the shriek of the partitions and the dead coming off the shelves, has done the thing the whole adventure was built toward. Give each declaration a beat, and show the steward feel it.

> [!TIP]
> A party that disowns and also cuts the stitches gets both halves of the account reopened at once: the witness unsays and the creditor refuses. That is the strongest play in the hall, and a party that has worked it out has earned it.

> [!WARNING]
> Bargaining for silence is a closed route dressed as an open one. A party that takes the steward's terms leaves without disowning, and its names stay on the releases. Let the players take the deal if they want it, and let the aftermath say what it cost.

**Clues and rewards.** Lizulu's working, the niche and the shutter make the earlier clues fit together. Wenlegir, cut from the salt and unsewn, can walk with help. The steward's seal-cord opens the vault and the canal's head-gate.

**Consequences.** Ending him here, by any combination of light, blades, refusals, disownings and the hall's own fittings, ends him for good, and the risen dead fall where they stand. Driving him off without ending him leaves him to gather again at his niche in a season or a year. A party that flees without fighting leaves the household risen and its names on the releases, and finds the steward waiting courteously at the gate the next time it comes.

**If bypassed or failed.** A party that never comes down the stair is brought down: the attendants carry the sleepers below at the darkest hour, and the scene begins with someone on a table, unsewn, the light-shaft over their head and the dead of the house all round. A party beaten in the hall escapes through the drains into the dry canal bed. The steward does not follow into the open. By the next mid-morning he is in his niche, and the hall can be reached while he lies there, and a disowning said in it then counts as surely as one said at night.

## Scene Five: The Vault and the Way Back

**Purpose.** The treasure, the truth on record and the road out.

> Behind the hall, in the rock, is a door sealed with the mark of Zu'Lemetu, the wax old and unbroken. Beyond it the air is dry and still, and the lamplight finds gold.

**Situation.** Behind the hall a sealed door in the rock carries the seal of Zu'Lemetu. The steward's seal-cord opens it, or a long night with chisels. Inside is the endowment's store: the house's burial goods held back for heirs, and the true accounts. In the gatehouse wall above, behind a false offering niche, is the steward's provision store, kept full for the household's living, which is to say for his own use of them.

**NPCs and threats.** Whatever the party left alive or unended. If the steward lives, he is somewhere in the house, and the vault is the one place in it he wants to keep.

**Player choices.**

- _Take the treasure._ Gold burial ornaments, lapis-inlaid vessels for oil and wine, and a silver scribe's case worked with the house's mark, which is the old mistress's own.
- _Take the true accounts._ The endowment's books in Zabegulu's hand identify the rightful heirs and show what the steward spent. They are what the temple scribe wanted, and they are worth more to the heirs than the gold.
- _Open the head-gate._ The steward's seal-cord breaks the estate's seal on the canal head. Water comes down the channel in a day and floats the skiff.
- _Walk out._ The provision store holds water jars and dried food enough to carry the party, Thakete and Wenlegir along the canal bank to the river in a day.
- _Release the dead._ Every stitch cut in the house and the galleries lets one of the household refuse its master and lie down. A priest of Weth'ur can be brought later to say the words; until then the dead lie unread.

**Clues and rewards.** The treasure and the accounts. The tablets with the party's names on them, which the party can break or carry home; breaking one does not unsay it, and only a disowning made in the hall does. If the steward is ended, the house is silent, and its lamps go out one at a time over the first night, though nobody puts them out.

**Consequences.** Returning the accounts to Gezedafu and the heirs earns a substantial salvage reward, granted in the temple's presence and entered. A disowning made in the hall is entered at the temple like any other declaration when the party reports it, and Gezedafu enters it gladly, because the temple's name was on the releases with theirs. Leaving them means the heirs never prove the estate is theirs.

**If bypassed.** A party that fled with Wenlegir and nothing else can come back by day, through the drains, with the steward in his niche. The vault does not move, and neither do the dead in the hall. If the steward lives, he has moved the true accounts into his niche, and they must be taken from under him.

## Aftermath

- **The steward ended, the accounts returned.** The heirs hold a Reading for the household at the temple, and the scribe reads the forged releases aloud as false. The party is paid from the salvage. Thakete and Wenlegir go home, and the estate's galleries are fed again.
- **The steward ended, the dead left sewn.** The house is still, but it is a house of the unread dead, and the herders on the margin say its lamps light themselves on the old mistress's death-day.
- **The steward driven off.** He gathers again in his niche within a season or a year, a little worse in body and stronger in will, and he knows the party's names from the tablets.
- **The steward left in possession.** The relief boat that Gezedafu sends when the party does not return finds the canal running again, the gate open, and eight people on the stair.
- **The witnesses.** Every member of the party is on the releases, signed or copied. Each one who left without disowning in the hall carries a false entry into his own account, and a false entry damns its witness with its maker at the Weighing. While the releases stand, the steward's accounts with the dead of Zu'Lemetu are closed, and the party closed them.
