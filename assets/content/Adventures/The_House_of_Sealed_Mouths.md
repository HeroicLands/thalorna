## The House of Sealed Mouths

A temple scribe needs the burial records of a dwindled house, and the records are at its funerary estate in the desert west of the river, at the end of a canal that is going dry. The party goes up the last of the water and finds the estate's household waiting at the gate with their mouths sewn shut. The steward is courteous, the relief boat is coming, and nobody leaves. The adventure runs in five scenes. Each can be met with force, stealth, talk, cleverness with the house itself or a sacrifice, and each states how play carries on if the party skips it, fails it or cuts it short.

Read [[place-lutithnet|Lut-Ithnet]] first. The adventure assumes the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelâthi]] ledger-ethic: an entry left open follows a person to the Weighing, and at a dead person's [[lore-readingweigh|Reading]] anyone the dead owes may stand and refuse to release him. That refusal is the thing the steward has sewn shut.

### The Truth Behind the House

**What happened.** **Gezuhatî**, steward of the house of **Zu'Lemetu**, kept its funerary estate for forty years and spent its endowment. The offerings to the house's dead lapsed one by one, which in the valley is a default against creditors who cannot chase it. When a physician told him he was dying, he saw his account plainly: every dead member of Zu'Lemetu was owed, every servant who had seen the books was a witness, and any one of them could stand at his Reading and say no.

He did not die. The estate's embalmer, **Lizulu**, a Lekhau of [[affiliation-garithnetau|Gar-Ithnetáu]], prepared him through the rite that makes a [[being-tereb|tereb]], and he went into it willing. Then he silenced the creditors. The old mistress, **Zabegulu**, was the first. Lizulu was the last who could have spoken for the house. One by one the household died in the preparation hall and rose again as [[being-damut|damutu]], and each had its lips sewn shut with resin-soaked cord before it rose. The stitches are the working: Lizulu bound the dead to answer their master _while the mouth is closed_. In the record room Gezuhatî enters each death in the sacred hand and, beside it, a release in the people's hand: _I require no more of the steward._

**What he wants now.** Gezuhatî feeds by drawing out the life of the living, and the household's living are almost spent. The canal failing was his doing: he had the head-gate at the river closed and sealed with the estate's seal, so that nobody leaves and nobody comes. Then a temple asked for the burial records, and he saw a better use for the visitors it sent. Strangers with no tie to the house make good witnesses to a release. Once they have witnessed, he has no further need of them alive.

**What remains unexplained.** Lizulu's working took hold more firmly than the rite should allow. The dead stand at the foot of the stair all night with their faces to the cliff, as if listening for something in the galleries, and the house's lamps burn whether anyone fills them or not. The adventure does not say why.

### The Hook

A temple scribe of [[lore-wethurdty|Weth'ûr]], **Gezedafu**, holds the older burial records of Zu'Lemetu and has received no new tablets from its estate in years. The house's heirs, a cousin line in a river town, mean to go to law over the estate, and the court wants the estate's own records. Gezedafu hires the party to go up the canal, collect the tablets and bring them back, and he pays a share of whatever salvage the heirs grant on the recovered estate. He gives them a letter under the temple's seal and a reed skiff, and he warns them that the canal is failing.

Other entries suit other heroes:

- **The thief.** A boatman says the house was rich once and the vault in the rock was never opened for an heir.
- **The mercenary.** The heirs hire a guard for the scribe's errand, to see that nothing leaves the estate before the court sits.
- **The kinsman.** A bondsman's family has had no word from a son and a daughter in service at the estate for two seasons. The daughter is **Thakete** and the son is **Wenlegir**.

### What the Party Hears First

Read or paraphrase this as the skiff grounds:

> The hull scrapes, slows, and stops. Behind you the last of the canal is already drawing back into cracked black mud, and the river is most of a day downstream. Ahead, under cliffs that have put the whole bay in shadow though the sun is still high, a white house stands over a dry stone basin. Seven people wait on the stair to its gate, in clean linen, perfectly still. None of them opens their mouth.
>
> As you climb out, you see why. Their lips are sewn shut with dark cord, neatly, in small stitches, the way a careful hand closes a seam.

## Scene One: The Gate

**Purpose.** To establish the errand and the isolation, and to give the party one disturbing detail that still has an ordinary explanation.

**Situation.** The basin stair climbs to a bronze gate in the estate's white wall. The seven attendants stand on the stair, in two lines, with lamps unlit in their hands. When the party reaches the top, the gate opens from within and the steward comes out of the shadow of the gatehouse to meet them. He does not step into the light on the stair.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Gezuhatî.** A very old man, upright, with a scribe's ink-stained fingers and a voice like a well-kept ledger. He wears the linen of a great house's steward and a seal-cord at his belt. He apologizes for the household's condition at once and gracefully: the servants have taken a vow of silence in mourning for their mistress, and they sewed their own lips at her burial, as the old families sometimes do. Nobody does, but it sounds as though somebody might. He offers shelter, water and food until the relief boat comes. He sent a man downriver when the water dropped. Asked when that was, he begins to answer, stops and says that the days run together in a house of mourning.
- **The seven attendants.** Men and women of the household, clean and well dressed, with stitched mouths and eyes that follow movement a beat late. They smell faintly of washing-salt and resin. Under their tunics, at the wrist and throat, is linen wrapping. They obey the steward's least gesture and do nothing else. They do not attack anyone in this scene.

**Player choices.**

- _Accept._ The party walks in and the gate closes behind it. The steward shows them the guest chamber, the well and the record room, and asks whether one of them reads the people's hand.
- _Refuse and camp at the skiff._ The steward bows and leaves them a water jar. At dusk the attendants come down the stair, light their lamps and stand round the skiff at a little distance, facing it, until dawn. The house holds the only water.
- _Look before entering._ A character who skirts the wall toward the cliff sees the tomb galleries, the offering tables bare and dusty, and the drain mouths in the basin wall, each big enough for a crawling man, with a dark crust on their lips.
- _Force._ The gate is bronze on a bar and the wall is twice a man's height. The steward does not resist an entry. He is grieved by it, and he remembers it.
- _Present the letter._ The temple's seal changes his manner. He becomes eager to help, and he offers the records that very evening.

**Clues and rewards.** The stitches, the salt smell and the wrappings. The steward keeps to shade and does not eat with his guests. The bare offering tables tell anyone raised in the valley that the house's dead have not been fed in years, which is a disgrace no living steward would allow in front of a temple scribe.

**Consequences.** A party that enters is inside the walls at dusk. A party that camps outside meets Scene Two's clues on the sand in the night: see below. A party that forced the gate has made the steward cautious, and he moves Wenlegir's preparation forward by a night.

**If bypassed or refused.** Thakete slips out of the house through the drains after dark and crawls across the dry basin to the skiff, and the attendants turn their faces toward her but do not move. She brings her account, and her brother's danger, to the party where they are. In the small hours the skiff's water jar is found empty and cracked, and there is no other water this side of the river.

## Scene Two: The Record Room

**Purpose.** To discover that the household is dead, that the records lie, and that a living servant is hiding in the house.

**Situation.** The record room holds the house's tablets in clay jars on shelves to the ceiling, sorted by generation. The steward brings the newest jars himself, sets a lamp beside them and leaves the party to work, with a single attendant standing in the doorway. Along the passage is a door with a new bronze lock, and behind it a preparation room where the dead of the house waited for the hall below in better years. It is still in use.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Gezuhatî.** Before he leaves, he asks a favor of the party. A few household releases want a witness, and a stranger with no tie to the house is the proper witness for them. He sets the tablets out, with a reed pen, and asks a character who reads the people's hand to set his name beneath them. He is patient about it and comes back to it more than once.
- **Thakete.** A young bondswoman of the household, alive, grey-faced and thin, hidden among the wrapped bodies on the preparation room's shelves with a bundle of linen pulled over her. She has not been sewn. She has been fed on: she tires walking across a room. She is terrified of the steward and of being heard.
- **The attendant in the doorway.** It watches. It does not stop anyone reading. It turns its head to follow any character who goes into the passage.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the records._ The burial records are in the sacred hand and the releases in the people's hand. A character trained in one reads only half, and the party has to put the halves together. The halves show seven deaths in the last few years, each one somebody the party met on the stair, with its own closing release of the steward written beside it.
- _Witness the releases._ A character who signs has entered his name in the steward's account. It counts for something in Scene Four.
- _Refuse to witness._ The steward accepts the refusal with perfect courtesy and does not ask again. From this moment he has decided the visitors stay.
- _Open the preparation room._ The lock is new and good. A character can pick it while the attendant's attention is drawn off, or break it noisily, or find the key on the steward's seal-cord, which is a matter of getting close to him.
- _Talk to Thakete._ She whispers the steward's near name, _Gezû_, as the household always called him, and then cannot stop: he brings them back, but they come back wrong. He sews them first, and then they get up. Her brother Wenlegir is in the salt below. When the stitching is finished he dies, and then he gets up too. It is to be done tomorrow at dusk.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The last release in the jar is for the man the steward sent for the relief boat, and his death is entered a month before the day the steward says he set out.
- An earlier tablet, in the old mistress's own hand, records the physician's finding that the steward was dying. No death follows it.
- A fresh tablet opens an entry in Wenlegir's name and leaves the closing day for tomorrow.
- The newest tablet opens an entry for _travelers come by the canal_, with a count matching the party's number exactly and the names left blank.
- In the preparation room, a basket of cord stiff with resin, and curved needles.

**Consequences.** A party that finds Thakete has a guide to the house and a person to protect, and she slows them. A party that leaves her hidden finds her gone by morning, taken below, and her name entered after her brother's. A character who witnessed the releases is a witness to false entries, and in the valley a false entry damns the witness with the one who made it.

**If bypassed.** If the party never reads the records, Thakete comes to the guest chamber after midnight. If they never find Thakete, the knocking under the guest-chamber floor in Scene Three is Wenlegir, and a character who answers it hears him say his name and his sister's. If they never reach the house at all, Thakete's account at the skiff carries the essential facts, and the entry with their number on it is waiting in Scene Five for anyone who reads it.

## Scene Three: The Needle at the Keyhole

**Purpose.** To show that the threat is active and aimed at the party, that the stitches are the binding, and that leaving is harder than staying.

**Situation.** Night. The guest chamber sits over the preparation hall, and through the floor comes a faint, irregular knocking. Some time after midnight a scraping starts at the door. A needle comes through the keyhole, draws back, comes through again a finger's width along, and pulls dark cord after it. Someone in the passage is sewing the door shut, seam by seam, in small neat stitches, and the cord is stiffening with resin as it dries. When the party opens the door or looks out, the seven attendants are standing shoulder to shoulder in the passage, and one has the needle.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The attendants.** Tireless, silent and strong as the dead are strong. They do not strike first. They stand in the way, they take hold, and they carry a struggling person below if enough of them get a grip.
- **The one that tears.** When challenged, the oldest of them, a big man in a cook's apron, raises his hands to his own face and rips the stitches out. His mouth opens wide and he screams with no breath behind it, a sound like wind in a jar. Then he falls, and he does not get up. His mouth shapes a word as he goes down. It is the refusal from the litany of the Reading: _no_.
- **Gezuhatî.** He arrives at the end of the passage with a lamp, as courteous as at the gate. He regrets the noise. He explains, as a host explains a house rule, that the canal is closed, that the house has entered its guests, and that guests of the house remain with the household. He does not lie about any of it now. He says the party will be very comfortable, and that their mouths will be closed only at the very end.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ The attendants can be fought in the passage, where only two can come at a time. They do not tire and do not flee. Destroying them is possible. It is slow and loud, and the steward watches it with interest.
- _Cut the stitches._ A character who saw the cook fall can go for the stitches with a knife. Each attendant whose stitches are cut screams its breathless word and falls. The steward stops being courteous the first time this happens.
- _Stealth and the roof._ The guest chamber's window gives onto a flat roof and from there onto the cliff path above the tomb galleries. The galleries open onto the hillside, and a character walking the cliff path hears something moving in them.
- _Run for the river._ Over the wall and down the canal bank. The attendants follow at a walk, all night, never closing and never falling back.
- _Parley._ The steward offers terms: witness every release on the shelf, and the party may leave on the relief boat. There is no relief boat. A character who reads him well knows he is offering what costs him nothing.
- _Give someone up._ He accepts Thakete in exchange for a night's peace and keeps the bargain to the letter: the party is left alone until dusk tomorrow.

**Clues and rewards.** The stitches hold the dead to him, and with them cut, the dead refuse him. The knocking under the floor is Wenlegir, alive, signaling with his heels from a natron bed. The steward's words in the passage leave no doubt what he is.

**Consequences.** By the end of this scene the gate is barred from inside, and the party knows it is a prisoner. Every attendant destroyed is one fewer at the end, and the steward knows the count. A character carried below arrives in Scene Four on a table, unsewn, with until dusk.

**If bypassed.** A party that ran for the river reaches the canal's head-gate at dawn and finds it closed and sealed in fresh mud with the estate's seal. The river is still a long way off, and the attendants are standing on the bank behind them. Thakete, if she came, says that the drains in the basin wall run under the house into the preparation hall, and that the steward lies there by day. A party that shut itself in hears the stitches go in round its door, and at the darkest hour the door is cut out of its frame and the attendants take the nearest sleeper below. Either way the next scene is the preparation hall, entered by day through the drains or at night as a captive or a rescuer.

## Scene Four: The Preparation Hall

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the steward on his Hallowed Ground, where everything the party learned can be turned against him.

**Situation.** The preparation hall is long, low and cut back into the cliff. Natron beds run down one side and embalming tables down the middle, with gutters in the floor that drain into the dark mouths of the drains. Bronze partitions hang on tracks from the roof beams, so that the hall can be closed into bays for the seventy days of the first grade. At the far end is a stone niche, freshly cut, with a lamp in it and a name above it: Gezuhatî's own. Above the main table is a light-shaft, closed with a new bronze shutter bolted from below. On the shelves and the beds lie the bound dead of the household who have not risen yet, every mouth sewn. On the main table lies Zabegulu, the old mistress, wrapped to the throat, her lips sewn, her eyes open.

The steward stands at the second table with a curved needle in his hand, finishing Wenlegir. Wenlegir lies in the salt to the chest, alive, his mouth sewn halfway.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Gezuhatî.** In his own hall he lets the cloak slip. He is taller than he was upstairs, very pale, and the shadow round him is deeper than the lamps account for. He has the strength of his kind and the old steward's patience, and he would rather never fight. He sends the attendants and the risen dead between himself and anyone who comes at him, and works on. Destroyed anywhere else, he gathers again here after a season or a year. Destroyed here, he is ended.
- **The attendants and the risen dead.** Whatever is left of the seven, and as many of the bound household as he wakes. He wakes them by touching their stitches, one at a time, and it takes him a breath for each.
- **Lizulu.** The embalmer who made him, bound on a shelf near the light-shaft, sewn like the rest. Lizulu does not move at the steward's touch, because the steward never woke him. His working is cut into the edge of the main table in the sacred hand: _the servant answers his master while the mouth is closed._

**Player choices.**

- _Force, at night._ This is the unprepared fight, against a tereb on his own ground and a hall of the dead. It can be survived. It is not likely to be won by force alone.
- _Force, by day._ A party that comes up the drains by day finds him in his niche, slow and dulled by the hour. The dead are still on their feet.
- _Open the light-shaft._ Somebody has to reach the shutter, draw the bolts and throw it open, while the dead try to stop them. Between mid-morning and mid-afternoon it drops a shaft of sun across the main table, and the steward cannot cross it without great cost. Out of those hours it shows only stars.
- _Use the partitions._ The bronze screens run on their tracks with a long shriek. Closed in the right order, they shut the steward into a bay with nobody between him and the party, or shut the dead out of the bay where Wenlegir lies.
- _Use the drains._ The drains are tight crawl-ways, and the dead fit them badly. A party can come in by them, go out by them with Wenlegir, or lure part of the household into them and close a partition behind.
- _Cut the stitches._ Every sewn mouth cut open in this hall screams its word and falls, and the hall is full of sewn mouths. Cutting Zabegulu's is the stroke that matters most. She says her _no_ plainly, in a dry clear voice, looking at him, and then lies still.
- _Hold the Asking._ A character who knows the litany of the Reading can read the forged releases aloud from the tablets and ask each dead creditor whether it requires no more. A character who witnessed the releases in Scene Two can stand and declare the witnessing false, and a release whose witness disowns it is no release. With each refusal entered, the steward is weaker, and he knows exactly what is being done to him. This is what he did all of this to prevent.
- _Wake Lizulu._ Cutting the embalmer's stitches lets him speak for as long as a man can speak without breath. He tells the party what he made, where the steward's ground is, and that the binding fails if the mouths are opened. He asks them to finish what he cannot.
- _Bargain._ The steward offers the vault, the heirs' records and Wenlegir for the party's silence and departure. He means it, as far as the canal head.

**Clues and rewards.** Lizulu's working, the niche and the shutter make the earlier clues fit together. Wenlegir, cut from the salt and unsewn, can walk with help. The steward's seal-cord opens the vault and the canal's head-gate.

**Consequences.** Ending him here, by any combination of light, blades, refusals and the hall's own fittings, ends him for good, and the risen dead fall where they stand. Driving him off without ending him leaves him to gather again at his niche in a season or a year. A party that flees without fighting leaves the household risen, and finds the steward waiting courteously at the gate the next time it comes.

**If bypassed or failed.** A party that never comes down the stair is brought down: the attendants carry the sleepers below at the darkest hour, and the scene begins with someone on a table, unsewn, and the light-shaft over their head. A party beaten in the hall escapes through the drains into the dry canal bed. The steward does not follow into the open. By the next mid-morning he is in his niche, and the vault can be reached through the hall while he lies there.

## Scene Five: The Vault and the Way Back

**Purpose.** The treasure, the truth on record and the road out.

**Situation.** Behind the hall a sealed door in the rock carries the seal of Zu'Lemetu. The steward's seal-cord opens it, or Lizulu's knowledge, or a long night with chisels. Inside is the endowment's store: the house's burial goods held back for heirs, and the true accounts. In the gatehouse wall above, behind a false offering niche, is the steward's provision store, kept full for the household's living, which is to say for his own use of them.

**NPCs and threats.** Whatever the party left alive or unended. If the steward lives, he is somewhere in the house, and the vault is the one place in it he wants to keep.

**Player choices.**

- _Take the treasure._ Gold burial ornaments, lapis-inlaid vessels for oil and wine, and a silver scribe's case worked with the house's mark, which is the old mistress's own.
- _Take the true accounts._ The endowment's books in Zabegulu's hand identify the rightful heirs and show what the steward spent. They are what the temple scribe wanted, and they are worth more to the heirs than the gold.
- _Open the head-gate._ The steward's seal-cord breaks the estate's seal on the canal head. Water comes down the channel in a day and floats the skiff.
- _Walk out._ The provision store holds water jars and dried food enough to carry the party, Thakete and Wenlegir along the canal bank to the river in a day.
- _Release the dead._ Every stitch cut in the house and the galleries lets one of the household refuse its master and lie down. A priest of Weth'ûr can be brought later to say the words; until then the dead lie unread.

**Clues and rewards.** The treasure and the accounts. The tablet with the party's number on it, which the party can break or carry home. If the steward is ended, the house is silent, and its lamps go out one at a time over the first night, though nobody puts them out.

**Consequences.** Returning the accounts to Gezedafu and the heirs earns a substantial salvage reward, granted in the temple's presence and entered. Leaving them means the heirs never prove the estate is theirs.

**If bypassed.** A party that fled with Wenlegir and nothing else can come back by day, through the drains, with the steward in his niche. The vault does not move. If the steward lives, he has moved the true accounts into his niche, and they must be taken from under him.

## Aftermath

- **The steward ended, the accounts returned.** The heirs hold a Reading for the household at the temple, and the scribe reads the forged releases aloud as false. The party is paid from the salvage. Thakete and Wenlegir go home, and the estate's galleries are fed again.
- **The steward ended, the dead left sewn.** The house is still, but it is a house of the unread dead, and the herders on the margin say its lamps light themselves on the old mistress's death-day.
- **The steward driven off.** He gathers again in his niche within a season or a year, a little worse in body and stronger in will, and he knows the party's names from the tablet.
- **The steward left in possession.** The relief boat that Gezedafu sends when the party does not return finds the canal running again, the gate open, and eight people on the stair.
- **The witness.** A character who signed the releases and never declared them false carries a false entry into his own account, and a false entry damns its witness with its maker at the Weighing.
