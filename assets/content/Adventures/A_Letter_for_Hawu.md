## Overview {#overview}

Every petition that reaches the throne goes out again by the same roads. The Emperor does not leave [[place-zojai|Zojai]], so his answers travel, and every road from [[place-lengwou|Lengwou]] runs over ground that [[affiliation-muwojaidmn|Muwojai Domain]] keeps and across one river at [[place-zujufrry|The Zuju Ferry]]. The toll-keepers there enter the name of every party that goes over, with its size and its errand when it states one, and they do not break seals.

[[being-henzoshuiwo|Shuiwojai Henzo]] has petitioned for the vacant office of Teng Ja, and [[being-weilengjnch|Weileng]] has answered with a sealed letter that only the Emperor and the hands that sealed it have read. It is on its way to [[place-hawu|Hawu]]. Until it arrives Henzo does not know what the Emperor asks of him, and he does not trust the hands that carry it. [[being-chaiwomuwoj|Muwojai Chaiwo]], who keeps the ferry, the passes and the roll, has his own reasons to want the courier watched.

The party enters as the escort Chaiwo hires at [[place-kowu|Kowu]], as the hand Henzo pays to meet the courier on the far bank, or as travelers who share the ferry with a man who gives his name at the landing and says nothing else.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The courier.** A messenger of the [[being-jnchhsguard|Imperial Household Guard]] carries the letter. He may not cross under another name, and the roll at the landing will hold his. Nothing about him is secret, and everything he carries is.

**The road.** Chaiwo's toll-keepers see every name. The Strait bloc wants the roll read, the loyalists want the gate kept shut, and the independent houses want to cross without being watched. Each of them has asked something of Chaiwo's household this season.

**The garrisons.** Chaiwo has no grown heir, and three of his retainers are named as possible successors. Each has received an approach from a different bloc, and each would admit it if asked. One of them has accepted, and Chaiwo cannot tell which; the referee chooses. The three hold the garrisons of the passes on the far side of the ferry, each a [[being-mwjgrrcapt|Garrison Captain of Muwojai]], and the one who has accepted shows it in his garrison rather than in his speech: his men's arrears are paid in new silver that no stipend from Kowu covers, they wear new winter coats, and whenever someone of note passes his gate he sends a runner to the house that bought him.

**Henzo's side.** Henzo pays for the letter to reach him unopened, and he pays a second time for knowing who touched it on the way. A letter delivered to him with a broken seal, or not at all, tells him something as well.

**What the Sealed Letter asks.** The letter withholds the office and asks Henzo for more silver toward the court's budget, and it names no price at which the office would follow. It is Weileng's way of deferring: while the office stays unconferred, both blocs need him.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The seal is the scenario. Nobody in it is a villain, and nearly everyone wants to read the letter before Henzo does. The referee decides whether the seal reaches Hawu whole, and the party's choices decide what each side learns from the attempt.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- Which of the three captains has accepted, and which bloc paid him.
- Whose agent waits on the far bank: Henzo's, or another house's.
- What Henzo does with the Emperor's answer once he has read it.

## The Clock {#clock}

- **Day One.** Chaiwo commissions the party at Kowu.
- **Day Two, midday.** The courier gives his name at the Zuju landing and crosses. By evening the far-bank agent has met him.
- **Days Two and Three.** The three gates on the road over Zijai. The weather closes the pass at dusk, so the courier lodges at one gate for the night. The bought captain's runner leaves that night.
- **The day the courier reaches Hawu.** Henzo reads the letter in his hall. The runner's house has the report first.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The warrior: the escort from Kowu.** Chaiwo wants a party to see the courier safely over the river and to learn whether anyone waits on the far bank.
- **The courtier: Henzo's hand on the far bank.** Henzo pays a party to meet the courier on the far bank and bring the letter to Hawu unopened.
- **The infiltrator: the roll at Zuju.** A house's agent wants to know who crossed in the last month, and a clerk's price is a letter delivered to Kowu.
- **The trader: the same ferry.** A party crossing on its own business shares the boat with a man in a plain coat who gives his full name at the landing and says nothing else.

## The Opening {#opening}

The landing at Zuju at midday, with a clerk's brush squeaking down a roll. A man in the plain coat of the household guard waits his turn behind a carter's rice and a priest's mule, holding a lacquered case against his chest with both hands.

> "Name," says the clerk, and the man gives it, in the full form, without lowering his voice. Three heads at the toll-house door turn at once.

A party of travelers begins here and plays Scene Two at once. A party hired by Chaiwo or by Henzo plays Scene One first and reaches this moment at the start of Scene Two.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Hall at Kowu

**Purpose.** Meet the lord who keeps the road, and learn what he wants watched.

**Situation.** Kowu, the castle town of Muwojai, on the lower slope of [[place-zijai|Zijai]]. The toll of the ferry comes here to the lord's granary. Chaiwo, sixty-one, sits in his hall with the scar on his neck turned toward the door, and receives the party slowly.

**NPCs and threats.** Chaiwo, who is dutiful and hard to hurry, and who does not say that he cannot tell which of his own retainers has accepted a bloc's offer. The household retainers who attend him, who carry word to the garrisons on the far side.

**Player choices.** Accept the commission to see the courier over and find who waits on the far bank, ask what he knows about the roll, or ask why he does not use his own men.

**Clues and rewards.** Chaiwo does not use his own men because he cannot name the ones he trusts. He pays in a pass that opens the gates of the district.

> [!NOTE]
> Every house keeps an envoy at Kowu, because a message for the Emperor passes through the town first. A party seen leaving the hall with Chaiwo's pass is seen by more than his household.

**Consequences.** The party leaves Kowu with Chaiwo's commission and the pass, and the household knows both, so word reaches the garrisons ahead of it.

**If bypassed.** Chaiwo's household sends the courier on with its own guard, and the party meets the same road without his protection.

### Scene Two: The Landing at Zuju

**Purpose.** The crossing, the roll and the first sign that someone has been asking about the letter.

**Situation.** The village of [Zuju]{.name data-type="place/settlement"}, about three hundred people on the bank, with a landing on each side and a toll-house at the nearer one. The courier gives his name, and the clerks enter his size and his errand when he states one.

**NPCs and threats.** The clerk at the landing, a [[being-zujutollkpr|Zuju Toll-Keeper]], who is the lord's servant and sees every name. The courier, who is silent and exact. A [[being-jnchagent|Jenchai House Agent]] in a plain coat, who has been asking at the landing which parties came through this month.

**Player choices.** Cross with the courier under their own names, cross under the lord's pass and let the roll name Chaiwo, ask the clerk who has been reading the roll, or follow the agent.

**Clues and rewards.** A clerk will answer a question about the roll for a letter delivered to Kowu. The agent works for a house the party can name if it reads the badge on his coat or follows him to his lodging.

**Consequences.** The roll holds the party's name and the courier's. Whoever reads it later learns that they crossed together.

**If bypassed.** The courier crosses alone and the roll holds his name.

### Scene Three: The Far Bank

**Purpose.** Learn whether anyone waits, and meet them.

**Situation.** The landing on the far side and the road that climbs from it. Someone is waiting. The referee chooses who from the houses that have asked about the letter, an agent of the [[affiliation-shuiwojaidmn|Shuiwojai Domain]] among them.

**NPCs and threats.** The one who waits, another house agent. If it is Henzo's, he offers to take the letter on to Hawu in the party's place and to pay for the favor. If it is another house's, he offers to look at the seal and promises to put it back as he found it.

**Player choices.** Hand the letter over, refuse, offer to carry it on with the agent as witness, or turn the agent in to the first garrison.

**Clues and rewards.** The one who waits knows the courier's name and the hour he crossed, which only the roll can tell. Someone has read it.

**Consequences.** Whoever holds the letter at the top of the first slope holds it for the rest of the road.

**If bypassed.** The courier meets the waiting man alone, and the letter goes on or does not.

### Scene Four: The Passes

**Purpose.** The garrisons, and the retainer who has accepted a bloc's offer.

**Situation.** The road over the far side of Zijai, where Chaiwo's garrisons hold the passes. It goes through three gates, each held by one of the three retainers the lord has named as a possible successor. The courier carries the case with both hands. The pass Chaiwo gave the party opens the gates, and each captain reads it carefully.

**NPCs and threats.** The three captains, each polite and watchful. Their men, each a [[being-tsaihwosldr|Tsaihwo Soldier]], who stand at the gates. The weather in the pass, which closes the road at dusk.

**Player choices.** Show the pass, take another road round a garrison, ask a captain whether a bloc has approached him, or lodge at a gate and watch what its captain does.

**Clues and rewards.** Every captain admits, if asked, that a bloc has approached him, so the approach tells the party nothing. The acceptance shows: at one gate the men wear new winter coats and talk of arrears paid in silver, and the captain asks the courier to wait while a runner goes down the road to a house. The two captains who refused have nothing new at their gates, ask only for the courier's name and let him go. A party that follows the runner learns which bloc bought the captain.

> [!TIP]
> Play the three captains alike in speech. Each reads the pass slowly and hands it back with both hands, and each answers a question about his loyalty with a statement of his duty. The difference lies at the gate and not in the words, so a party that only listens learns nothing here.

**Consequences.** The party knows which of the three retainers has accepted a bloc's offer, and which bloc. Chaiwo knows it only if the party tells him.

**If bypassed.** The courier takes the lower road, the captain who accepted sends his runner all the same, and his report goes to the house that bought him.

### Scene Five: The Hall at Hawu

**Purpose.** Deliver the letter, and see what its arrival does.

**Situation.** Hawu, the strait-coast castle town of Shuiwojai, with the naval yards and the sixty ships of the fleet. Henzo receives the courier in his hall on a shoulder of [[place-rojai|Rojai]] and breaks the seal where the retainers can see.

**NPCs and threats.** Henzo, hearty and calculating, who laughs readily and whose eyes do not join in. His retainers, who watch his face. The courier, who delivers the letter and asks that its receipt be entered in the hall's roll.

**Player choices.** Stay and watch him read, give an account of the road, tell him who touched the seal, or leave before he asks.

**Clues and rewards.** Henzo pays for faithful delivery and for each name he has not heard before. Any party that brought the seal whole earns his regard, and one that brought it broken earns his questions.

**Consequences.** Henzo knows the Emperor's answer, and what he does with it is for the referee to decide. Every season the office stays unconferred, his silver buys a debt and not the office.

**If bypassed.** The courier delivers the letter alone and the party hears the hall's answer from the quay.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The courier crosses under his name and rides on with a guard of one. Whoever waits on the far bank reads the seal, or Henzo's agent takes the letter on to Hawu. Chaiwo learns nothing about his retainers. The roll at Zuju is read by the house whose clerk is paid most, and every bloc knows by the end of the month that the Emperor has answered and that nobody can say what the answer is.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The seal whole.** Henzo has the Emperor's answer unopened by any hand but his own. He is in the party's debt and says so in the hall.
- **The seal broken.** A house has read the letter, and the Strait bloc knows that its answer has been seen. Henzo asks the party who touched it.
- **A retainer named.** Chaiwo learns which of his possible successors accepted a bloc's offer, and which bloc paid him. He can name his successor from the two who refused, or outbid the bloc for the third.
- **The roll read.** Every house that pays a clerk learns who crossed and when, and the toll-keepers at Zuju keep the party's names.
