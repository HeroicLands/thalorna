## Overview {#overview}

On pension day the pay-hall of the Spire Circle in the [[place-warrensorz|Warrens]] pays half a ward, runs out of coin and shuts its doors. The queue does not leave. The veterans on the stairs of Nerval have no rent without the pension, and the rent-men of House Zelmaris go up the stairs the next morning. By nightfall every power in [[place-orzara|Orzara]] that claims the crowd is in the street. The party has one afternoon and one night to learn why the hall ran short, and then to decide what to do with a truth that would calm the queue if anyone dared say it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The pension is borrowed. The throne pays it each month with silver drawn against the great houses' loans, and the quarter's rollover has not yet cleared. The Tarmeks Orzan paid the Dole desk's warrant in part this month, and the desk paid the pay-halls in the order of their bearers' route until the chests ran out. The Spire Circle hall is last on the route. The silver for the rest of the warrant comes in three days, when the rollover closes.

Nobody can say so. To tell the queue the silver is coming is to tell it where the silver comes from. [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]], the veterans' tribune, knows the pension is borrowed and has known for four years: his sister's son is a scribe of the Dole desk and showed him the Tarmeks's note on the back of the warrant. If he says it, the stairs either refuse to believe him or believe him, and either way he is finished.

Beneath the shortfall lie the city's other secrets, and the queue brings each of them into the open.

- **The dead on the roll.** The citizen roll carries about four thousand dead names whose pension the ward clerks draw every month. A ward short of coin is a ward where the living and the dead compete for the same chest.
- **The night tallies.** The Watch sells a night inside the line to sleepers without papers, through the cookshops, and the old soldiers who keep the stairs take a share.
- **The eighth stories.** Forty Zelmaris tenements carry a floor of timber above the seventh, framed inside the roof-line and signed off as laundry space by a paid _peltros_.
- **The knife.** A society of officers and veterans of the eastern legions, meeting on a River Circle stair, means to kill the Khelathi factor on the quays on the night of the dole, so that the throne's Khelathi loan dies with him. Sathemar has neither given his word nor refused it.

> [!TIP]
> The scenario's question is not why the hall ran short, which the party can learn by evening. It is who tells the queue what, and what each telling costs. Present each power's offer as reasonable from where it stands.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A widow says her dead husband's pension is still drawn every month at another ward. She wants the man who draws it found tonight, while the clerks are counting what they have left.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** The steward of [[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]] hires guards to walk with the rent-men on Nerval tomorrow morning.
- **The leader.** [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]] asks a party to find out from the Dole desk in Arvon whether the short hall is a clerk's error or the beginning of something, and to be back before the queue decides for itself.
- **The wanderer.** A frightened young veteran finds a party of strangers in a cookshop and says that the killing is set for the night of the dole, on the quays. He wants the tribune told.

## The Opening {#opening}

Noon on the Spire Circle. The pay-hall's doors are shut and barred, and a clerk has chalked a notice on them that says nothing. Several hundred citizens stand in the lane in their legion's order, by stair, with their tokens in their hands. A preacher of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] stands on the steps of a cookshop across the Circle and does not yet speak.

> Nobody is shouting. The old men stand in their files, the way they stood in a camp, and the women hold the children's hands and do not let go.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Queue at Noon

**Purpose.** Establish the stakes and the people who claim the crowd.

**Situation.** The queue waits. Sathemar arrives and speaks to the files by stair, and they hear him. The Watch's picket from the Spire Circle house stands at the end of the lane.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]] promises what he always promises. A club captain of [[affiliation-zargoi|the Irons]] moves through the crowd handing out bread, on [[being-rzmsplthn|Razemos Pelthen]]'s orders. The preacher waits for the bread to run out. The picket's sergeant is one of those who sell the night tallies. A queue that turns on the hall is a riot that a small party cannot stop, only steer.

**Player choices.** Help Sathemar hold the files, follow the Irons' bread to whoever paid for it, or go up to Arvon for the answer.

**Clues and rewards.** The Irons' bread was bought this morning, before the hall closed, which means Razemos knew.

**Consequences.** Every hour the queue stands, the Irons' standing rises and the tribune's falls.

**If bypassed.** The queue stands until dusk on Sathemar's word, and he spends most of what it is worth.

### Scene Two: The Dole Desk

**Purpose.** A puzzle and a temptation: learn the truth, and see it is unsayable.

**Situation.** The Dole desk in the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]] in Arvon. Clerks in gray stoles count the remaining chests. Sathemar's nephew is among the scribes.

**NPCs and threats.** The chief of the desk, who will say nothing; the nephew, who will say everything to his uncle's messengers in a corridor; and a clerk of the Tarmeks bringing a note from [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]].

**Player choices.** Read the warrant, question the nephew, or carry the Tarkelos's note.

**Clues and rewards.** The warrant, and on its back the Tarmeks's note of the sum drawn against the house loans. The chief's route-list shows the Spire Circle last. The Tarkelos's note to the desk says only: _three days._

**Consequences.** The party now knows what the tribune knows. The Tarkelos learns who has seen the warrant.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that copies the warrant holds the most dangerous paper in the city, because it proves the pension is borrowed. Every faction that learns of it wants it, to use or to burn.

**If bypassed.** Sathemar's nephew carries the news to his uncle himself, at night, and the tribune decides alone.

### Scene Three: The Night on the Quays

**Purpose.** An action scene with a choice of victims.

**Situation.** The night of the dole on the [[place-wharvesorz|Wharves]]. [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]] leaves the Strangers' Row for a dinner on a moored hull, as he does every month. The society's men wait among the porters.

**NPCs and threats.** Four or five veterans with knives and no skill at murder; a fight with them is short and ugly, and they will not run. The factor's own guards are few. Pickets of [[affiliation-cleanshlds|the Clean Shields]] at the moorings above the gate watch and do not move unless the river gate is threatened.

**Player choices.** Warn the factor, stop the knife, let it happen, or bring the Watch.

**Clues and rewards.** One of the society carries a letter from an officer of the eastern legions, unsigned, naming the night.

**Consequences.** If the factor dies, the throne's Khelathi loan dies with him, the hawks of the Seldan rejoice, and the Row is closed for a season. If he lives and learns who saved him, the party has a creditor's friendship and a society's hatred.

**If bypassed.** The referee decides whether the knife finds him. Either way the city hears of it before the rent-men climb.

### Scene Four: Rent Day on Nerval

**Purpose.** Show the landlord's side and what the city stands on.

**Situation.** Morning. The rent-men of House Zelmaris go up the stairs of Nerval with the steward. Tenants without the pension cannot pay, and the steward's orders are to take a token or a tenant.

**NPCs and threats.** The steward; the rent-men; tenants who bar their doors; and a widow on the sixth floor whose cracked party-wall grows every rain.

**Player choices.** Walk with the rent-men as hired, stand with the tenants, or climb past the seventh floor to see what is there.

**Clues and rewards.** Above the seventh floor of several buildings, inside the roof-line, are framed rooms with sleepers in them. In one block a fire-wall between wards has been cut through and bricked over with a timber door.

**Consequences.** A party that knows about the eighth stories knows that the next fire on Nerval crosses wards within an hour, and that [[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]] would pay anything to keep it quiet until the Seldan lifts the height limit.

**If bypassed.** The rent-men take tokens from the tenants who cannot pay, and the queue at the pay-hall is longer the next pension day.

### Scene Five: The Three Days

**Purpose.** The climax: hold the city until the silver comes.

**Situation.** The silver arrives on the third day. Until then the queue returns every noon, and each power offers to end it on its own terms.

**NPCs and threats.**

- [[being-rzmsplthn|Razemos Pelthen]] offers the crowd to the claimant who pays for the bread, and asks the party to carry the offer.
- [[being-trzmsbrkrts|Torzemos Brakarates]]'s preachers tell the Warrens that the empire is weak and the faith is strong.
- [[being-kholsamelint|Kolsame Linthimis]] of the Watch wants names, so that her sweeps have someone to sweep.
- [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]] must say something on the third noon, and asks the party what.

**Player choices.** Persuade the tribune to tell the truth, help him keep the promise, buy the queue three days of bread, or let it break.

**Consequences.** The choice sets the tribune's standing, the Irons' price and the Black Flame's audience for the rest of the reign.

**If bypassed.** On the third day the silver comes and the hall pays. The Warrens remember who fed them.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The queue stands on Sathemar's word for two noons. On the second evening the Irons' bread runs out, the preacher speaks, and a stair on the Spire Circle stones the pay-hall's shutters. The Watch puts a hundred sleepers without tallies across the Clearance to show the Veramus of Order numbers. The society's knife goes out on the quays. On the third day the silver comes, the hall pays, and the tribune promises again that the pension is untouchable.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Every claimant's household now knows the price of the Warrens' crowd and who sells it. The warrant, if the party copied it, is either in the party's hands or in someone else's. The tribune owes the party his standing or blames them for its loss. The Dole desk shortens the bearers' route by one hall next month, and the Spire Circle is no longer last.
