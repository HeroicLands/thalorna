Every quarter day the throne of [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylaria]] borrows the silver that pays the veterans' pension, and every quarter day the lenders ask a little more for it. This frame runs through one such season in [[place-orzara|Orzara]]. The reigning Orzas, [[being-zrmlshlrkls|Zarmelas Helarkales]], is seventy-eight and failing, and he has adopted nobody. Three men hope to be named, the Guard expects a donative from whoever is, and the granaries hold about a week of bread. Every great house in the city takes what it can from a throne it cannot afford to see fall, each sure the others will hold the line.

## The Situation and the Stakes {#the-situation}

**The debt.** The throne pays the pension with silver borrowed from the great houses of [[affiliation-danvar|the Danvar]], rolls the loans over as they fall due and puts nothing by. [[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]], whose house holds more of the throne's paper than any other, asks a rider at every rollover: the guild's auditors kept out of the great houses' chapter books, and the throne's share of the two-percent fee cut. [[being-krznsthlrts|Korzenas Thalurates]] means to offer the next rollover himself, cheaper and without riders, and take her place in a single quarter. The Imperial _Tarkelos_, [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]], negotiates every loan and keeps the only true account of what is owed.

**The succession.** The Orzas has no son, and by custom an heirless Orzas adopts the man who follows him. The three spoken of on the Heights are [[being-klvnsmrdvns|Kolvenas Mardovanes]], the Senate's choice and the grain's; [[being-hlzrsvndrn|Halzoras Vendaren]], a legate and the Guard's favourite; and [[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]], poor, pious and the old houses' man. Each house lends partly on the hope that its man is named and its paper honoured by the next reign. The donative is the fuse. The Guard Camp expects one on the day of the adoption, the expected man has promised one and paid nothing, and the soldier offers coin now.

**The bread.** About a week of grain stands in the state granaries, and nothing stands behind it. The Grain House of [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] sells the city its bread from Hilen and wants a duty on foreign grain. [[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]]'s shipping house brings the only grain that can reach the capital faster, and wants trade free. A low river at Ostenna or a short dole on the Circles brings a queue, and a queue that waits too long is a riot.

**The outside creditors.** On the Strangers' Row the Khelathi factor, [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]], offers the throne grain on credit and silver for its paper. [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Au'Khelathu]] financed Taruzel's war against the empire, and it spent more on that war than its treasury admits, so it wants returns now. The [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] lends to the throne against the customs of [[place-aelissium|Elissara]], collected by its own clerks: a foreign creditor at the capital's harbour.

**The other powers.** [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Thordimalon]] keeps the Orrery, the lamps and the wards, the empire's face to every envoy who lends to it. [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] has patrons in the Seldan, officers in the legions and preachers on every Circle, and its High Flame-Warden, [[being-trzmsbrkrts|Torzemos Brakarates]], has warned every claimant's household what a promise to proscribe it would cost.

**The knives.** The officer corps and the veterans have not forgiven the loss of Taruzel. A war of revenge is possible for the legion and impossible for the treasury, so revenge comes as assassination: a society of veterans of the eastern legions, meeting on a stair of the River Circle, means to kill the Khelathi factor before the throne takes his loan.

**The companies.** The legions are thin, and three hired companies serve the empire beside them. [[affiliation-cleanshlds|The Clean Shields]], three hundred strong under [[being-shttrkssm|Shattar Kassumi]], hold the grain road and the barge moorings above the river gate, and are paid on the day by a paymaster nobody names.

The stakes are a reign's succession, the credit of the whole west's clearing, and a city of citizens who live on a pension and a week of bread.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The frame's balance is its engine. None of the oligarchs wants Orzara broken, because their fortunes are the throne's debt and their houses stand inside its walls. The danger is that their takings add up past what the city can bear, or that one of them misjudges the margin. When something catches fire, the same houses that stoked it have the means to put it out, and each names a price for doing so.

## Who Moves It {#who-moves-it}

[[being-zrmlshlrkls|Zarmelas Helarkales]], the Orzas
: Patient, dry and dying. He has no cash. He can proscribe a house, raise one, and sell offices, titles and grants, and he sells them quietly. The succession is his last collateral, and he spends it slowly.

[[being-klvnsmrdvns|Kolvenas Mardovanes]], the expected man
: A former governor of Hilen whose brother's house will not pay his donative until the adoption is sealed. He wants to reign as his own man, and has gone to the Strangers' Row after dark for the money.

[[being-hlzrsvndrn|Halzoras Vendaren]], the soldier
: A legate who brought his legion home whole from the Taruzelian war. His donative is Veldis silver, and he has signed a letter promising the throne's paper honoured whole.

[[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]], the old blood
: Keeper of the Masks of the Imperial Cult, descended from five Orzoi and owning almost nothing. He has secretly offered Nartun the proscription of the Black Flame in return for its knights and temple coin.

[[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]], the Imperial _Tarkelos_
: The keeper of the true accounts. He knows which houses could not survive an audit, and his price is his own family raised.

[[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]], the throne's banker
: She cannot let the throne default and cannot let the city burn. She has an answer ready for the night it does.

[[being-krznsthlrts|Korzenas Thalurates]], the debt broker
: He buys the old houses' debts for their votes and the throne's paper through factors, and wants a daughter married to the next Orzas.

[[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]], the Grain House
: The best host on the Laurel, who profits from every shortage and needs the next Orzas to owe the Grain House his bread.

[[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]], the Khelathi factor
: Courteous, patient and under instruction to get returns. He lends against revenues a throne cannot easily repudiate.

[[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]], the shipping house
: Free trade's voice on the Wharves, whose hulls carry cargoes taken at sea.

[[being-ylzmsmlvrts|Yalzemas Molvarates]], the tax-farmer
: She offers three years of Hilen's land tax now for the right to collect it for ten, and her advance is Khelathi silver.

[[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]], tribune of the stairs
: He promises the veterans that the pension is untouchable, and knows it is borrowed.

[[being-mrznszlmrs|Merzanas Zelmaris]], landlord of Nerval
: His rents fall due the day after pension day, and his tenements carry eighth stories the law forbids.

[[being-rzmsplthn|Razemos Pelthen]], Master of the Irons
: He sells the Warrens' crowd to whichever claimant promises the accession games.

[[being-shttrkssm|Shattar Kassumi]], captain of the Clean Shields
: Taruzel-born, cashiered in the war's first month, and promised the citizenship by a paymaster he will not name.

[[being-trzmsbrkrts|Torzemos Brakarates]], High Flame-Warden
: He reads the grievances the Night of Ashes is meant to burn, and wants a reign that will not press the Ashen Temple.

The bodies
: [[affiliation-sorvan|The Imperial Guard]] and its watch-captains; the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]]; [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks Orzan]]; [[affiliation-citywatchorz|the City Watch]], veteran-staffed and scorned by the Guard; the houses of [[affiliation-mardovanes|Mardovanes]], [[affiliation-varkalenis|Varkalenis]] and [[affiliation-thalurates|Thalurates]]; the League's clerks at Elissara; and the society on the River Circle stair.

## The Arcs

The frame gathers three Orzara scenarios and points at the provinces, each turning one strand of the knot.

1. **The bread.** [[scenario-sndbnthcrst|The Sand Beneath the Crust]] opens the reserve and finds it short. It sets the Grain House, the shipping house and the Khelathi factor bidding to fill a gap nobody may admit to.
2. **The pension.** [[scenario-hlfwrdshrt|Half a Ward Short]] puts a queue on the Spire Circle at noon and the veterans' society on the quays at night.
3. **The donative.** [[scenario-prmsndcoin|The Promise and the Coin]] takes the party into the Guard Camp's cookshops, where the watch-captains weigh a promise against coin.
4. **The provinces.** The succession reaches every province. [[scenario-rdtsrks|The Road to Soraks]] follows a dying governor's seal up the north road. [[scenario-rtbfrwddng|The Rite Before the Wedding]] stands on the field whose harvest the Grain House has sold forward. [[scenario-lttrnbdypnd|The Letter Nobody Opened]] finds a recall the capital believes delivered. [[scenario-hllsndrfrtrs|The Hulls Under the Fortress]] puts the war party's best voice in danger on both sides of the strait.
5. **The holy kingdom.** [[scenario-bxsldkngsrng|A Box Sealed with the King's Ring]] carries Nartun's quarrel, and the old blood's offer, to the Orzas's door.

The arcs run in any order, and each moves the knot whether or not the party finishes it. A party that closes one gap opens another, because the house that pays for the closing names its price.

## Ways In {#ways-in}

1. **For a house.** A great house hires the party to take a piece of leverage before the adoption is announced: a ledger, a letter, a witness, a cargo or a vote. Every house has a job, and every job harms another house the party may need later.
2. **For the stairs.** A veteran's family on the River Circle hires the party over a widow's token, a let stair or a pay-hall that closed its doors at noon, and the trail leads up the hill.
3. **For the throne.** The Orzas's secretary wants honest eyes in a claimant's household, and pays from the sale of a patent the party is asked to carry.
4. **From outside.** A League clerk at Elissara, a Khelathi factor on the Row or a Taruzelian factor behind a veteran's name wants something carried, counted or kept quiet, and pays in coin the city's own houses cannot match.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- The quarter day comes. Amarza Veldis gets her rider, or Korzenas Thalurates undercuts her, and either way the throne rolls the debt over at a higher price and the Seldan sees a third of what is owed.
- The river runs low, the reserve is opened, and the porters reach sand in the first hour. The throne buys grain on borrowed money, and the house that sells first has the city's gratitude and its silver together.
- A pay-hall comes up short. Sathemar Ardelen holds the queue with a promise he cannot keep, and the Irons and the Black Flame's preachers each take something from the afternoon.
- The watch-captains of the Guard weigh promise against coin, and more of them lean toward the man who pays now. The Orzas grows weaker. Three deeds lie in his secretary's strongbox, unsealed.

## The Secret {#the-secret}

The Orzas knows he has months, and knows the succession is worth most to him while it stays open. Every house lends on the hope its man is chosen, and the moment he adopts, the houses whose man was not chosen stop lending and begin to buy the Guard. Three adoption deeds lie drafted in his secretary's strongbox, one for each claimant, and none is sealed. He reads them in the evenings. This frame does not say which he seals, or whether he seals any. The referee decides, and may let the party's actions decide.

Beneath the succession lies a shared danger. The Tarkelos's true ledger records the debt the Seldan never sees. It also records three things that could each end a great house: the House of Veldis has issued about a fifth more notes than its reserves hold; the Tarmeks has advanced a third of the price of three Olkassa harvests not yet grown to House Mardovanes; and the Steel Guild delivers fewer watered blades each year at the same price. None of the three houses knows the others are exposed. Rivals in everything else, they each need the Tarkelos silent, and each would expose the others first.

The Clean Shields are Amarza Veldis's. The house pays the company through an account at its own chapter, and when the fire comes she means to sell the city the only armed company outside the legions' command, at the price of the next reign's signature.
