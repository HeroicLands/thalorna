## Overview {#overview}

About 401 AF Vilérian legions killed one another in a wheat-field on the Olkassa estate in [[place-hylen|Hilen]]. [[place-olcassafld|The field]] has borne its grain a fortnight early and half again as heavy ever since, and it is farmed in silence for the state granaries that feed the capital. [[affiliation-ganothandis|House Ganothandis]] holds the estate. Its heir, [Zetimas Ganothandis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is to marry the daughter of [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]], master of the Grain House, and he wants the field settled by the rite of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] before the wedding. The party comes to Hilen on a wedding errand and stays for the rite. The grain of the next three harvests is already sold, and the oath the province's masters swore against civil war rests on the same granaries.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The forward sale.** House Mardovanes has sold the next three Olkassa harvests forward to the state granaries at a price no other field could meet, and [[affiliation-tarmex|the Tarmeks]] advanced a third of it on borrowed money. If the field is settled and its quickening ends, the Grain House cannot fill the contract, and the throne has paid for bread that does not exist. The master of Ganothandis agreed to the sale. Zetimas does not know of it. Nethoras hopes the rite fails.

**The rite.** Sélmoros's rite of settling works perhaps half the time, and nobody knows whether settling the field would end its harvest. This scenario does not say. The referee decides at the field, by the rite's own rules, or lets the party's deeds decide.

**The oath.** Two springs ago the masters of Hilen's great houses swore at the Speakers' Stone that when the Orzas dies, no grain is to be weighed at [[place-aravantia|Aravantara]] for any claimant who brings a legion inside the capital's boundary. They call it the Compact of the Stone. The master of Ganothandis swore first. The list of the masters who swore, each name in its master's hand, lies in a box under the Orzas's image in the provincial temple, in the keeping of the _zalvos_. One morning last harvest the yard clerks refused the Mardovanes factor's wagons until noon, as a trial. Every claimant would pay for that list.

**The watchers.** [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Thordimalon]]'s surveyors want to measure the field, and the steward turns them away at the gate. A preacher of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] in Orzara tells the poor that the capital's bread is milled from a battlefield.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The rite's outcome is the referee's, and whether the field's harvest ends with it stays unknown to everyone in the setting. What the scenario establishes is who gains and who loses on each outcome.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A rival house of the Street of the Houses wants a copy of the list in the _zalvos_'s box, and says only that it is "a list of names in their own hands".
- **The mercenary or soldier.** [[being-klvnsmrdvns|Kolvenas Mardovanes]] hires guards to escort his niece's betrothal gifts up the river road to the Ganothandis townhouse at Aravantara, and to bring back the signed acknowledgment before the next sitting of the Seldan.
- **The leader.** [[being-ylzmsmlvrts|Yalzemas Molvarates]] wants her bid for the ten-year contract on Hilen's land tax carried to the capital before the Seldan sits on it, and a house of the province wants the same party to arrive late.
- **The wanderer.** [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] hires a party to escort Sélmoros's priests to the field and report to him, and is plainly hoping the rite fails.

## The Opening {#opening}

The river road into Hilen at harvest, between estates whose walls run for miles. The gift-wagon carries a chest of silver plate, a bolt of purple and a pair of carved masks. The wagoners are Mardovanes men, and they talk of the wedding as if it were a harvest.

> The wheat stands gold to the horizon on both sides of the road. On one estate the reapers are already in the field, two weeks before their neighbors.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Street of the Houses

**Purpose.** Introduce Hilen's houses, the outsider governor and the oath.

**Situation.** The Ganothandis townhouse at Aravantara accepts the gifts and gives the party supper. The _therkos_, [[being-lnthmsrvls|Linthimas Orvelis]], the first in a generation with no estate in Hilen, is a guest, and suspects that every petition he hears has been agreed before it reaches him.

**NPCs and threats.** The master of Ganothandis, gracious and tired; his heir Zetimas, eager and devout; the _therkos_; and [[being-ylzmsmlvrts|Yalzemas Molvarates]], whose bid the houses at the table argue against, because a farmer who pays ten years in advance collects ten years' worth.

**Player choices.** Listen at table, carry a word for the _therkos_, or hear the tax-farmer's offer.

**Clues and rewards.** A master's toast to "the Stone" makes the table go quiet. The yard clerks' refusal of the Mardovanes wagons is told as a joke at the factor's expense.

**Consequences.** The houses learn what the party is for, and the _therkos_ asks them to come back.

**If bypassed.** The party delivers the gifts at the door and is sent on to the field.

### Scene Two: The Priests on the Road

**Purpose.** A journey, and the tension of an escort whose employer hopes it fails.

**Situation.** Three priests of Sélmoros travel to the Olkassa estate with the rite's things. Nethoras's steward rides with them.

**NPCs and threats.** The senior priest, who has done the rite twice and seen it work once; the steward, who finds reasons to delay; and tenants on the road, angry at the estates that buy out the free farmers at [[place-messora|Messora]]. With them walks [[being-rvthrbrmztr|Revothar Baremzator]], a circuit priest of Lizelas from [[place-polthara|Polthara]] who keeps grain moving to the hungry and knows every tenant village by name. The tenants are hungry, not dangerous, unless the steward provokes them.

**Player choices.** Hurry the priests, let the steward delay them, or speak for the tenants.

**Clues and rewards.** The steward carries a letter from Nethoras telling him to "let the priests be slow, not stopped". Revothar knows that the Olkassa wheat goes to the capital's granaries and none of it to the villages.

**Consequences.** Delay brings the priests to the field after the reapers have cut the first rows, which the senior priest says makes the rite harder.

**If bypassed.** The steward has his way, and the priests arrive late.

### Scene Three: The Field

**Purpose.** The place itself, and its stillness.

**Situation.** The field at dawn: heavy wheat, workers who do not speak while they cut, and a steward who turns the Thordimalon's surveyors away at the gate.

**NPCs and threats.** The estate's reapers; the steward of Olkassa; two Thordimalon surveyors waiting at the gate with a writ from Orzara. The quickening itself is no danger to a party that does not dig. A party that does dig finds the dead of both eagles, and the reapers will not work beside them.

**Player choices.** Let the surveyors in, keep them out, or carry their questions to Zetimas.

**Clues and rewards.** The reapers' foreman says the field has never failed. The surveyors' writ names the field as a matter for the Order, which the estate denies.

**Consequences.** A party that admits the surveyors earns the Order's thanks and the estate's anger.

**If bypassed.** The surveyors wait at the gate through the rite.

### Scene Four: The Rite

**Purpose.** The climax, with an outcome nobody can predict.

**Situation.** The priests perform the rite of settling at noon in the middle of the field, with Zetimas, the steward and the reapers watching.

**NPCs and threats.** The priests; Zetimas, who wants it to work; Nethoras's steward, who does not; and whatever the field holds, which is the rite's to answer.

**Player choices.** Protect the rite, disturb it, tell Zetimas of the forward sale before the priests begin, or stand back.

**Clues and rewards.** None the party lacks by now.

**Consequences.** If the rite works, Zetimas is content, and nobody yet knows whether the next harvest will come early and heavy. If it fails, Zetimas delays his wedding or marries anyway. If he learns of the forward sale first, he may stop the rite himself or break the betrothal.

> [!WARNING]
> Telling Zetimas of the forward sale is the single choice in this scenario that can break the Olkassa marriage, and with it the Grain House's tie to Ganothandis. Let it be the party's, and let the masters at Aravantara learn who told him.

**If bypassed.** The rite takes place without the party, and the referee decides its outcome.

### Scene Five: The Box Under the Image

**Purpose.** An optional heist, and the province's leverage over the capital.

**Situation.** The provincial temple at Aravantara, where the _zalvos_ keeps a box under the Orzas's image.

**NPCs and threats.** The _zalvos_, devout and alert; temple servants; and the masters' men, who watch the temple in harvest week.

**Player choices.** Steal the list, copy it, sell its existence, or leave it.

**Clues and rewards.** The list, each master's name in his own hand, Ganothandis first.

**Consequences.** Whoever holds the list knows which houses would starve the capital to keep its legions apart. Every claimant would buy it, and every master would kill to keep it.

**If bypassed.** The list stays in its box.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The priests arrive late, the rite is performed, and the referee decides its outcome. Zetimas marries the Grain House's daughter in the autumn, knowing nothing of the sale. Yalzemas's bid reaches the Seldan, and the houses of Hilen speak against it. The Compact of the Stone stays sworn and secret. The Thordimalon's surveyors go home and write that they were refused.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

If the field's harvest fails, the Grain House cannot fill its contract and the throne's advance stands against nothing, and the house must find grain elsewhere or confess. If it holds, the Olkassa marriage binds the expected man's house to Ganothandis's grain. A party that holds the list of the Stone holds the one paper that tells a claimant which houses would stop the capital's bread against him.
