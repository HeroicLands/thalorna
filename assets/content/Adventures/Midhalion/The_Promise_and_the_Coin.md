## Overview {#overview}

Whoever the old Orzas adopts owes [[affiliation-sorvan|the Imperial Guard]] a donative on the day, and the throne has no cash to pay it. [[being-klvnsmrdvns|Kolvenas Mardovanes]], the man the court expects to be named, has promised one and paid nothing. [[being-hlzrsvndrn|Halzoras Vendaren]], the soldier and the Guard's favourite, offers the officers coin now. [[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]] has nothing to offer the Guard but his blood and his piety. The watch-captains who hold the gates of [[place-thaurzurgalis|the Guard Camp]] are weighing promise against coin, and on the night the Orzas dies, the gates open for whoever they choose. The party is hired by one claimant's household to learn what the watches are saying. Each thread it pulls leads to a claimant's silver and to the price he paid for it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

- **The expected man's empty purse.** House Mardovanes will not pay Kolvenas's donative until the adoption deed is sealed. Its silver is tied to the forward sale of three Olkassa harvests, which Kolvenas does not know. He has gone twice after dark, in a closed chair with no badge, to the Khelathi factor [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]] on the Strangers' Row, to borrow the donative against the grain contract of his own reign. Methisi has asked what lien the next Orzas could give that the Orzas after him could not repudiate. Kolvenas has not answered.
- **The soldier's coin.** Halzoras's donative is [[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]]'s, lent through the house's chapter at [[place-mercavia|Delalara]] in his brother-in-law's name. Her price is a letter in his own hand: the throne's paper honoured whole, and the Danvar's auditors kept out of the great houses' chapter books. The letter waits at the Veldis chapter in Orzara to be sent south. Amarza has also told him that an armed company will stand on the grain road for the claimant who honours the debt. He has guessed it is [[affiliation-cleanshlds|the Clean Shields]], and has met their captain, [[being-shttrkssm|Shattar Kassumi]], on the river road at night.
- **The old blood's offer.** Dalmeras has sent word to Nartun that he will proscribe [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] if its knights and temple coin stand behind his adoption. The Holy Mother has agreed to nothing. The High Flame-Warden, [[being-trzmsbrkrts|Torzemos Brakarates]], has warned every household what such a promise costs, and his Scorchmasters are listening for it.
- **The deeds.** Three adoption deeds lie drafted in the Orzas's secretary's strongbox, one in each name, none sealed. Each claimant has been told, or believes, that he is favoured.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The adoption is never settled here. The scenario ends with the watches' loyalties shifted, never with an heir named. The Orzas lives through it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A chair-bearer of the Strangers' Row gate has noticed the same closed chair let through after the gate is locked. He offers a party the night of the next visit, and the name of the man inside, to whoever pays most.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** Halzoras's steward hires a party to escort a chest of coin from the rented house on the Heights to a cookshop below the Guard Camp at night, and to see it handed to a named sergeant and nobody else.
- **The leader.** Kolvenas sends a party, as his steward's guests, to a supper of Guard officers in a cookshop below the camp, to learn which watch-captains still speak of his promise and which have stopped.
- **The wanderer.** The priest of the Sacred Forge who carried Dalmeras's first offer up the holy road has come home with a fever and talks in it, in a pilgrims' hostel below Antel where anyone can hear.

## The Opening {#opening}

A cookshop in the lane below the Guard Camp, in the evening, where officers of the Guard drink off duty and their sergeants drink at the next table. The supper is paid for by somebody's steward, and everybody knows whose.

> The officers toast the old Orzas's health, and then the toast goes round again, and each man names the health of someone different.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Officers' Supper

**Purpose.** Introduce the watches and the weighing.

**Situation.** Six watch-captains and their sergeants at supper. The talk turns to the donative within the hour.

**NPCs and threats.** The captains are proud, underpaid and courted by every house in the city. One speaks for Kolvenas's promise and asks where the money is. Another toasts the legate and drinks too much. A third says nothing at all and leaves early.

**Player choices.** Listen and count, flatter, provoke, or follow the captain who leaves early.

**Clues and rewards.** The count: two watches hold to the promise, three lean to the coin, and one is waiting. The captain who leaves early goes to a house on the Heights that is no claimant's.

**Consequences.** The captains remember who paid for supper, and the house that paid learns what the party heard.

**If bypassed.** The party hears the same count, less precisely, from a sergeant at the next table for a price.

### Scene Two: The Chest at Night

**Purpose.** An escort, and a look at whose coin moves.

**Situation.** A chest of coin goes from the rented house on the Heights down to a cookshop below the camp, after the lamps.

**NPCs and threats.** Halzoras's steward; the named sergeant; and men who know the chest is moving. The robbers are hired toughs a capable party beats off; their employer is the danger.

**Player choices.** Deliver it as hired, follow it after delivery, or count what is in it.

**Clues and rewards.** The coin is fresh Ardin in bags stamped with a Delalara chapter's mark. The robbers were paid by a steward of the Grain House, who wanted to know how much there was.

**Consequences.** The Grain House learns the size of the legate's purse. A party that opened the bags is known to have done so by morning.

**If bypassed.** The chest arrives, and the waiting watch begins to lean.

### Scene Three: The Closed Chair

**Purpose.** A discovery: the expected man's secret.

**Situation.** The Strangers' Row gate after locking. A closed chair with no badge is let through, the Watch sergeant on duty looking the other way.

**NPCs and threats.** The chair-bearers; the sergeant; Methisi's door-wardens. Kolvenas inside the chair. A society of veterans on the River Circle watches the Row for its own reasons.

**Player choices.** Watch, follow, confront, or carry the news to someone.

**Clues and rewards.** Kolvenas goes in and comes out an hour later. A servant of the factor's house will sell what was said for a good deal: a loan for the donative, against the grain contract of his reign.

**Consequences.** This is the one fact that turns the Grain House against its own claimant. Every buyer of it is dangerous: the Grain House, the soldier's household, the hawks of the Seldan, and the veterans' society, which would add a second name to its list.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that sells this to the River Circle society has marked Kolvenas for death. Make sure the table knows what the society is before they sell.

**If bypassed.** Kolvenas keeps his visits secret this season.

### Scene Four: The Fevered Pilgrim

**Purpose.** The third claimant, and the Black Flame's reach.

**Situation.** A pilgrims' hostel below the Old Hill. The priest of the Forge talks in his fever of the holy road, the Mother's Hall and a promise.

**NPCs and threats.** The priest; the hostel-keeper; Dalmeras's steward, who comes to carry him home; and two Scorchmasters of the Ashen Temple who come to listen. They are dangerous in a fight in a narrow room, and they would rather not fight at all.

**Player choices.** Carry the priest to the house on the Old Hill, let the Scorchmasters hear him, or take him somewhere else.

**Clues and rewards.** The fever's words, which name no claimant and need none: a priest blessed for the road, a Holy Mother, an oil and a proscription.

**Consequences.** If the Scorchmasters hear, the Black Flame is Dalmeras's enemy before he is adopted, and his steward's assurances to the High Flame-Warden are known for lies.

**If bypassed.** Dalmeras's steward brings the priest home, and the secret holds for now.

### Scene Five: The Weighed Promise

**Purpose.** The climax: the watches decide which way they lean.

**Situation.** A watch-captain asks the party, as people who have been everywhere this week, for proof that the expected man's donative exists before his watch weighs it against the legate's coin.

**NPCs and threats.** The captain; and every claimant's household, each of which knows the party has been asked.

**Player choices.** Bring proof, bring a lie, bring the truth about where the coin comes from, or refuse.

**Clues and rewards.** Whatever the party has gathered becomes evidence here: the Delalara bags, the closed chair, the fevered priest's words.

**Consequences.** The watch leans one way. Each claimant knows whether the party helped or harmed him, and the Guard's count moves by one watch.

**If bypassed.** The captain waits, and the coin keeps coming.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The coin keeps coming and the promise stays unpaid. By the season's end four watches lean to the legate and two hold to the expected man. Kolvenas's need drives him back to the Strangers' Row a third time, and Methisi asks his question about the lien again. The fevered priest recovers, and the Scorchmasters hear of his journey in the next Night of Ashes sack. The Orzas lives, reads his three deeds in the evenings and seals none of them.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The party leaves with a claimant's gratitude and two claimants' suspicion. It may hold one of the city's most dangerous facts: a Khelathi loan for a donative, a banker's signed letter, or a promise to Nartun. Each is worth most in the days before the adoption, and each puts the party in someone's way.
