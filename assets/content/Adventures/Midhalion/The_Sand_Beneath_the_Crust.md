## Overview {#overview}

A dry late summer leaves the [[place-vylarisrvr|River Vylaris]] low, and the night's grain barges reach [[place-orzara|Orzara]] short. The Veramus of the Grain is about to open the state granaries on the River Circle to hold the price. Two of those halls hold sand beneath an arm's depth of wheat, and the city's week of bread is a day or more shorter than its council believes. The party is drawn in by a porters' quarrel, a late barge or a house's errand, and arrives at the moment when knowing about the sand is worth most. Every house that can fill the gap wants to know first, and none of them wants the city to learn it in a queue.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The granary-keeper of the two halls nearest the river gate sold their reserve grain three lean winters ago, a little at a time, through a broker in the [[place-grandmarketorz|Grand Market]]. He replaced the bottom of each bin with sand under a crust of grain. A seal-cutter in the [[place-gildedqrtr|Gilded Quarter]] cut him a copy of the granary's seal-stamp, so that whenever a hall is opened before a witness he breaks the true seal and remakes it, and the witness sees nothing wrong. He means to buy the grain back after a good harvest and has never had the coin. He acted alone.

The shortage is worth money to everyone who learns of it in time.

- **The Grain House.** [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] profits from every shortage and wants a duty on foreign grain. He does not know about the sand. Told, he is shocked first and thoughtful after, because the house that sells the throne grain first has the city's gratitude and its silver together. His own house cannot survive a hard audit either: it has sold three Olkassa harvests forward to the granaries on an advance from the Tarmeks.
- **The shipping house.** [[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]] brings the only grain that can reach the capital faster than Hilen's, and wants trade free and the Khelathi contract. His hulls carry cargoes taken at sea and sold on in Sazumun, and the Grain House would ruin him with that if it knew.
- **The Khelathi factor.** [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]] offers the throne grain on credit. His fleet lies a week at anchor off the river mouth while lighters carry its grain upriver, and someone has paid a Vaskelen lighterman to learn which night the hulls lie closest to the reed-beds.
- **The treasury.** [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]] must buy whatever grain is missing on borrowed money. He knows the Grain House's advance stands against grain not yet grown.
- **The banker.** [[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]] cannot let the city riot. A bread riot could end with a reign that repudiates its predecessor's paper.

The reveal lands at an opening, when a bin is broken and the porters' shovels strike sand. A party that has followed the clues reaches it first and decides who hears.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The sand is a fact and the scenario never resolves what the city does about it. The party's choice of whom to tell, and when, decides which house profits and how long the queue waits.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A porters' guild-master suspects that one night gang carries one sack in forty to a warehouse on no tablet. He hires the party to work a night on the quays and follow the sack.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] hires guards to ride up the river road, find his barge strings at [[place-ostenna|Ostenna]] and bring their masters' tallies to the Laurel before the Grand Market opens.
- **The leader.** A Khelathi grain cargo landed on the Strangers' Row is said to be spoiled. The Grain House's steward pays a party with standing to buy a sack and bring it to him, and the Row's factor watches who comes.
- **The wanderer.** A warehouseman's boy at Ostenna has found something in a grain sack that is not grain, and he brings it to the first strangers he meets.

## The Opening {#opening}

The party reaches the Wharves after midnight, when the barges land. The horn sounds, the gangs run sacks from the moorings to the halls by lamplight, and the tally-clerks chalk each load. Above the river gate the pickets of [[affiliation-cleanshlds|the Clean Shields]] watch the moorings, and the [[affiliation-citywatchorz|City Watch]] watches them. Tonight eleven barges are due and six come in.

> The river is low enough to smell. The sacks come up the steps two to a man, and the tally-clerk at the hall door has stopped calling the count aloud.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Sack That Goes Astray

**Purpose.** Draw the party into the grain trade and show how much of it nobody records.

**Situation.** A night gang runs sacks from a late barge to the halls, and one man in forty turns off down an alley toward a warehouse whose door bears no tablet.

**NPCs and threats.** The porters' guild-master, the gang's foreman and the tally-clerk. The warehouse belongs to a broker of the Grand Market who buys from the granary-keeper, and his men guard it. A fight with them is a quick scuffle a party wins easily, and it brings the Watch.

**Player choices.**

- _Follow the sack_ to the warehouse and count what is inside.
- _Bribe the tally-clerk_ for the halls' true counts, which do not match the tablets.
- _Report the gang_ to the guild-master, who wants the thief, not the trade.

**Clues and rewards.** The warehouse holds good wheat in sacks bearing a granary's mark, sold back into the market. The broker's ledger names the granary-keeper of the two river-gate halls as a seller three winters running.

**Consequences.** The broker learns he is watched and warns the granary-keeper, who begins to look for a way out of the city.

**If bypassed.** The same ledger is found later, in Scene Three, in the seal-cutter's keeping, where the broker sent it for safety.

### Scene Two: The Barges at Ostenna

**Purpose.** Show why the river runs late, and that the city's grain market is played on purpose.

**Situation.** Upriver at [[place-ostenna|Ostenna]], a barge has waited eleven days in the midstream line while later ones loaded. Boatmen, warehousemen and toll-clerks find reasons not to sail, not to load and not to pass a manifest. A toll-clerk was pulled from the river below the Landing last week.

**NPCs and threats.** The three trades of the Landing, and one broker's agent who pays them all and sells the timing of each slowdown to a second broker. The drowned clerk's fraternity wants to know who killed him; it did not.

**Player choices.**

- _Break the slowdown_ by force or coin, and bring the Grain House's barges down early.
- _Find the drowned clerk's list_ of refused manifests, hidden in the bronze tariff board's hollow back in the Toll House.
- _Leave it_ and ride back with the tallies.

**Clues and rewards.** The list names which brokers paid for which delays. The Grain House is not among them. The barges are late because two brokers in Orzara make money on a short city.

**Consequences.** Grain broken loose at Ostenna reaches the city before the reserve is opened. The price falls a little, and the opening is put off by days.

**If bypassed.** The barges come down a week late, and the reserve is opened on schedule.

### Scene Three: The Seal-Cutter's Window

**Purpose.** A puzzle: learn how good seals hide an empty hall.

**Situation.** A seal-cutter's shop in the Gilded Quarter, its window full of signet rings. The seal-cutter cut the granary's stamp and has been afraid ever since.

**NPCs and threats.** The seal-cutter is no fighter, and he will talk to anyone who promises him a way out of the city. The granary-keeper visits him when frightened.

**Player choices.**

- _Persuade or frighten him_ into telling what he cut and for whom.
- _Wait for the granary-keeper_ and follow him home.
- _Buy the copy stamp_, which proves the forgery to anyone who compares it with the true one.

**Clues and rewards.** The copy stamp, and the seal-cutter's account of every opening the keeper remade.

**Consequences.** A party holding the stamp holds proof that the city's count is false. Every house in the city would pay for it.

**If bypassed.** The granary-keeper keeps his nerve until the opening.

### Scene Four: The Bidders

**Purpose.** A roleplay: the houses' prices for knowing first.

**Situation.** A party with proof can carry it to anyone. Each door it knocks on opens a different bargain.

**NPCs and threats.**

- [[being-nthrsmrdvns|Nethoras Mardovanes]] on the Laurel offers a fortune for silence until his house's grain is ready to sell, and asks nothing about his own advance.
- [[being-mrndsvskln|Marendos Vaskelen]] on the Wharves offers to fill the gap from the sea, if the party keeps the Grain House out of it.
- [[being-mthszrtml|Methisi Azeret'Mulu]] on the Strangers' Row offers grain on credit tomorrow, against a lien the next Orzas cannot repudiate.
- [[being-drvnsrlln|Dorvanis Arelliani Septarius]] in Arvon listens, thanks the party and asks them to tell nobody else, because he must buy the missing grain before anyone knows it is missing.
- [[being-amrzvlds|Amarza Veldis]] on the Heights asks only how long the city has, and whether the Watch knows.

**Player choices.** Sell to one, play two against each other, give it to the Tarkelos, or take it to the [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] in public.

**Clues and rewards.** Coin, favor and enemies, in proportions the party chooses. Each bidder lets slip something of its own: the Grain House's forward sale, the Corsair cargoes, the factor's anchorage, the Tarmeks's true debt.

**Consequences.** The house the party chooses moves first. A party that goes public starts a queue at every bakery on the Circles before nightfall.

> [!WARNING]
> Going public is a real choice, and it turns this scenario into [[scenario-hlfwrdshrt|Half a Ward Short]] at once. Let it happen.

**If bypassed.** The bidders learn of the sand at the opening, all at once, and the first to reach the Tarkelos sells.

### Scene Five: The Opening

**Purpose.** The reveal and the reckoning.

**Situation.** The Veramus of the Grain opens the river-gate halls before witnesses at dawn. The granary-keeper breaks the seal. The porters go in with shovels.

**NPCs and threats.** The Veramus, the porters, the witnesses, the granary-keeper, a Watch picket under [[being-kholsamelint|Kolsame Linthimis]], and a crowd that gathers when word spreads. [[being-sthmrrdln|Sathemar Ardelen]], the veterans' tribune, arrives with men from the River Circle stairs. A crowd at the halls is dangerous to anyone it blames, and a party standing next to the keeper may be blamed.

**Player choices.** Shield the keeper, hand him over, speak to the crowd, or slip away with the proof.

**Clues and rewards.** None the party lacks by now. The reward is the city's opinion of them.

**Consequences.** The porters reach sand in the first hour. What the crowd is told, and by whom, sets the price of bread on the Circles for a month.

**If bypassed.** The opening happens without the party, and the city learns of the sand from the porters.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The river stays low and the reserve is opened. The porters reach sand in the first hour, the Veramus closes the halls, and the city hears of it by noon. The throne buys the missing grain on borrowed money while the queues form, and the first house to sell is paid twice: in silver now, and in gratitude at the adoption. The granary-keeper is found in the river, or leaves the city, and the seal-cutter is never asked anything.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The city's week of bread is shorter by two halls until someone pays to fill them. If the Grain House fills them, its claimant's standing rises in the Seldan. If the shipping house fills them, the tariff bloc loses its strongest argument. If the Khelathi factor fills them, the hawks call it a chain and the veterans' society on the River Circle has one more reason for its knife. Whoever the party served remembers it, and so does every house the party passed over.
