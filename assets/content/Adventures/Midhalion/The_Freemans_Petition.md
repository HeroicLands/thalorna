## Overview {#overview}

When Taruzel's war began, the rebels' council promised the flotilla's captains the prize money of every imperial ship and cargo they took, to be paid once the Confederation stood. Six warships were taken at the Shoals alone. Nothing was paid. The guilds outlawed the flotilla's masters, and the [[affiliation-hitusuni|Hitusuni]] of Sazumun hold the war's prize cargoes against a bundle of warehouse receipts that the [[affiliation-freecaptains|Free Captains]] carry on one hull. [[being-nzzntrshn|Nazzuna Tarshuni]] keeps them, together with the promise itself. Presented to the [[affiliation-grandcouncil|Grand Council]] by a freeman, the promise and the receipts would be read. Presented by a Free Captain, they would be burned with her. She chooses the party.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**What the satchel holds.** Nazzuna's satchel holds the receipts and, folded among them, the original of the rebels' promise under the council's seal, signed by men who sit in the Grand Council now. No other Free Captain has seen the original. A copy lies in the ledger of [Yazila Ilammuni]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the captains' lender in [[place-pashinar|Pashinar]]. The [[affiliation-auricompct|Auric Compact]] has learned that a copy exists and its enforcers have begun calling on factors there, but nobody yet knows that the original sails with the receipts.

**Who wants it burned.** One of the cargoes the receipts record still lies under the Hitusuni seal in the deepest warehouse on the quay at [[place-sazumun2|Sazumun]]. If a freeman proves it is there, [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]]'s house is a thief on its own seal, and no house in Taruzel survives that. The Compact holds the Confederation's war debt and wants no new claim on the treasury. [[being-trmnqzkn|Tirmana Qizikuni]] presses for an audit of the treasury, and a claim for six warships' prize money on the morning of the audit is a question she would rather not have asked.

**How the hall works.** The Arch-Consul, [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]], sets the order of business and gives it to the Clerk the night before each sitting. The Clerk's senior copyist sells a second copy before dawn, and this session the buyer is [[being-llmnblnn|Ellamuna Balinuni]]. Danashu has promised the veterans' petition the first reading of the summer session, which [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]] carries, and has told nobody. Zilmar's hall informs for the Compact.

**What follows reading.** A petition read in the Council is in the Council's record and cannot be unread. It does not have to be paid. The scenario offers the party the reading, never the payment.

> [!TIP]
> Every power that learns of the satchel offers the party a better bargain than Nazzuna can: a pardon, a fortune, a seat, a ship. Make each offer fair from where its maker stands, and let the party feel what they are being asked to sell.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** A Merchantry broker offers a party a sum that would buy a house on the height to learn where Nazzuna Tarshuni lies at anchor, and asks no questions about why they know her.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** Nazzuna takes a Compact merchantman with the party aboard as passengers and offers them passage to any cove they like in exchange for a service.
- **The leader.** [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]] wants a party with standing in Besikar to stand behind him at the Arch-Consul's residence when he brings the veterans' petition, so that the Arch-Consul sees the veterans have friends who are not veterans.
- **The wanderer.** A freeman of Besikar who paid the copyist's fee for his own petition has been given a number and nothing since, and wants to know whether the Arch-Consul has ever seen it.

## The Opening {#opening}

The deck of a Compact merchantman at dawn, with a Free Captain's hull alongside. The boarders are courteous. Their captain wears a satchel across her body, and she asks the passengers one at a time whether they are freemen.

> "I need someone nobody has outlawed," Nazzuna says, "and who keeps their word. Are you either?"

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Choice at Sea

**Purpose.** Hand the party the satchel and the danger.

**Situation.** Nazzuna explains what she wants: the promise and the receipts lodged as a petition in the Grand Council Hall at Besikar, in a freeman's name, before the summer session. She will put the party ashore wherever they choose.

**NPCs and threats.** Nazzuna, blunt and proud; her crew, who think she is mad to trust strangers; the merchantman's master, who will report all of it at the next port.

**Player choices.** Accept, refuse, ask for the copy in Pashinar as well, or bargain.

**Clues and rewards.** The original promise, with its seal and signatures. Nazzuna names Yazila Ilammuni and the Pashinar counting-room.

**Consequences.** The merchantman's master reports the boarding, and the Compact, the Hitusuni and the Qizikuni each learn within days that a party carries something for the Free Captains.

**If bypassed.** Nazzuna finds the party ashore and asks again.

### Scene Two: The Counting-Room at Pashinar

**Purpose.** Optional: the copy, and the Compact's enforcers.

**Situation.** Yazila Ilammuni's counting-room above the ferry quays, with the factors' houses on the slope above. Enforcers of the Compact have begun calling on factors.

**NPCs and threats.** Yazila, careful and frightened; [[being-pzrshilmmn|Pazirush Ilammuni]], who keeps the door and the table of a factor's house on the slope and knows every caravan master in the town; the Compact's enforcers, who are hard men in a narrow street and would rather frighten than fight.

**Player choices.** Take the copy for safekeeping, leave it, or lead the enforcers away.

**Clues and rewards.** The ledger's copy agrees with the original word for word. Pazirush tells the party which enforcers have called and when.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the copy draws the enforcers after it.

**If bypassed.** The enforcers find the ledger within the month.

### Scene Three: The Offers

**Purpose.** A roleplay: the powers bid for the satchel.

**Situation.** On the road to Besikar, or in its inns, each power that has heard sends someone.

**NPCs and threats.**

- A Hitusuni factor, sent by [[being-ashurazhitus|Ashuraz Hitusuni]], offers a fortune to see the receipts burned, and quietly arranges for the party to be robbed if they refuse.
- An agent of the Compact offers the party's names off every list of debtors in the Confederation, and asks only that they lodge nothing.
- A clerk of [[being-trmnqzkn|Tirmana Qizikuni]]'s asks what the satchel holds and offers to carry it into the Hall in the house's own name, at a time of the house's choosing.

**Player choices.** Refuse all, sell, delay, or play them against each other.

**Clues and rewards.** Each offer tells the party what its maker fears. The Hitusuni fear the warehouse, the Compact the treasury, and the Qizikuni the audit's timing.

**Consequences.** A refused Hitusuni factor sends men. They are dangerous in an alley at night and cautious in daylight.

**If bypassed.** The offers come all at once in Scene Four.

### Scene Four: The Warehouse

**Purpose.** Optional: the proof that makes a receipt into a theft.

**Situation.** The deepest Hitusuni warehouse on the quay at Sazumun, where one of the flotilla's cargoes still lies under the house's seal.

**NPCs and threats.** The warehouse's keepers; the house's seal-men; and the city's watch, which takes the Hitusuni's part. Breaking in is a heist a careful party can pull off, and a fight inside is one it loses.

**Player choices.** Find the cargo and leave it, mark it, or bring a witness.

**Clues and rewards.** The cargo, with its original marks under the Hitusuni seal, matching a receipt in the satchel.

**Consequences.** With this proof, the petition is no longer a claim but an accusation.

**If bypassed.** The receipts go to the Hall unproven, as claims.

### Scene Five: The Lectern

**Purpose.** The climax in the Grand Council Hall.

**Situation.** Besikar, the Clerk's rooms and the Arch-Consul's residence before the summer session. The order of business is set the night before each sitting and sold before dawn.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-dnshkmn|Danashu Kumuni]], who wants a quiet year; the Clerk and his senior copyist; [[being-llmnblnn|Ellamuna Balinuni]], who reads the list before Danashu has had breakfast; [[being-zlmrzzn|Zilmar Ezzinu]], who would put the satchel at the head of the veterans' petition, and whose hall informs for the Compact.

**Player choices.** Lodge the petition with the Clerk, take it to Danashu, join it to Zilmar's, or buy the second copy and learn where it stands.

**Clues and rewards.** The Clerk's register gives the petition a number and the date of its lodging, which makes it part of the Council's record.

**Consequences.** A petition lodged by a freeman is read when the Arch-Consul's list reaches it. Danashu has promised the veterans' petition first place. A separate petition needs its own place on his list; the party can ask him for one, while the buyer of the second copy knows the order early enough to press for a change.

> [!WARNING]
> Joining the satchel to Zilmar's petition puts it in the hands of a man who informs for the Compact. The party may not know that yet.

**If bypassed.** The satchel stays on Nazzuna's hull until the Fleet Review, where she means to read it to the coast herself.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Nazzuna keeps the satchel and carries it into the narrows on the morning of the [[lore-fleetreview|Fleet Review]], meaning to read the promise from the water. The Compact's enforcers find Yazila's ledger in Pashinar and burn it. The Hitusuni keep the cargo under seal, and Danashu keeps his promise: the veterans' petition is read first in the summer session, before the debt and the toll. Without Nazzuna's satchel, it carries none of the Free Captains' proof.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A petition lodged in the Council's record outlasts every house that wants it gone. If it is read, the Council must answer a claim signed by its own members, at the same session as the audit and the toll. Whether it pays, refuses or delays is the Council's business, and the halls and the coves watch what it does. The party is known to the Free Captains as people who kept their word, and to three houses as people who would not sell.
