## Overview {#overview}

In the holy city of [[place-nartum|Nartun]] the lower city says the high priesthood will sit in judgment on the king, [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]], before winter. [[being-vrthlszrkms|Varthelas Zerkemis]], High Priest of Sélmoros, is counting the votes, and the Holy Mother, [[being-ilvnssrkls|Ilvenas Sarkelis]], who anointed the king, is the one hand that can take the oil back. Around the city the forest is dying of the Withering. The temples call it judgment, the barons blame the priors' cutting, the Black Flame calls it a sign against the Order, and the woodsmen fear something older. The party enters on a forest errand or a pilgrim's road. They are foreigners the temples do not know, and that is why the king chooses them to carry a sealed box to Orzara.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The charge and its reason.** Varthelas means to charge the king with the Withering, laid at his door because the rites are neglected. His reason is the undercroft. Below the stables of the chapter house over the gate, knights of [[affiliation-sacredflame|the Order of the Sacred Flame]] hold worshippers of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] carried out of the Hilen march by night, on warrants the Holy Mother signs. A temple examiner judges them in closed session. The king has not been told, because he would stop it. Several high priests want a king who would let the takings go on, and the king's elder cousin, [Ormelas Daronis]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, has told Varthelas he would.

**The king's fear.** Kelvaras knows the judgment is real and that Varthelas has at least four votes. He does not know why. His one strong ally outside the kingdom is the empire Nartun refused. In the spring he wrote to the Orzas in his own hand: if the high priesthood moves to unmake an anointed king, he will acknowledge Orzara's throne as the kingdom's overlord in name, receive an imperial envoy and open the holy road to the empire's officers, in return for the Orzas's word that the legions will not suffer a deposition. The letter lies sealed in a cedar box in his bedchamber. If he sends it and it is known, he gives the high priesthood its judgment and the kingdom its first civil war since the break.

**The claimant's offer.** This spring and again at midsummer, envoys of a Vilérian claimant came up the holy road, the first a priest of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]] traveling as a pilgrim. The claimant, [[being-dlmrsvrklns|Dalmeras Varkalenis]], offers to proscribe the Black Flame when he reigns if Nartun's knights and temple coin stand behind his adoption. The Holy Mother has agreed to nothing. The temples lend no coin to any throne before the proscription, and she will hear of knights only when the claimant swears it in the Mother's Hall with his hand on the oil. The Black Flame has real power in the empire, and the promise may never be delivered. She has asked [[being-sldrsplvrn|Saldoros Pelvaren]], Master of the Order, how many knights could ride to Orzara. He answered that none could while the gate stood.

**The Withering.** The Withering is a fungus. It travels on felled timber with the bark on, on the crews' saws and axes and on wagon wheels, enters a living tree through a fresh wound, and follows the contract crews from valley to valley. Peeling and burning the bark where a tree falls, burning the brash and resting a valley the blight has reached would slow it. Several priors have seen that it follows the contracts, [[being-mlknssrvths|Melkanas Sarvothis]] among them, and none has said so. A forester sent Varthelas a report with a map two summers ago, and Varthelas put it in his chest.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> None of the kingdom's people knows the Withering's cause, and the scenario keeps it so. A party may gather proof, but proof is not agreement: the temples, the barons, the Black Flame and the woodsmen each have a reason to reject it.

> [!WARNING]
> The judgment does not happen within this scenario, and the Orzas never answers the box. The scenario ends with the box delivered, burned, opened or kept, and the votes moved.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The thief.** The prior's cousin in the lower city has been robbed of a strongbox he cannot report. [[being-mlknssrvths|Melkanas Sarvothis]] hires a party to recover it without the king's sergeants, and is anxious that nobody count it.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]] wants to know who has been buying his steward drinks at the Charcoal Wagon and asking about his debts, and hires a party to find out.
- **The leader.** A priest-noble house hires a party to learn which way the high priests mean to vote, and the king's own men want the same answer.
- **The wanderer.** [[being-brnksksrns|Branakis Kaseranis]], a charcoal-burner of [[place-kalderenna|Kalderenna]], needs someone to prove his clamps are sound before the estate's court sits, because the buyers who undercut him want the Order's knight to find him in breach.

## The Opening {#opening}

The holy road climbs through dying forest: gray stands among green, the leaves of the sick trees the wrong color. At the gate of the holy city the knights of the Order take every traveler's name.

> A knight in white writes the party's names in a book, slowly, and asks where they are lodging. He does not write the answer down.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Charcoal Grounds

**Purpose.** Show the dying forest and the quarrel over it.

**Situation.** Kalderenna's sawpits and clamps, on the prior's estate. The prior's forester has marked a stand whose leaves are the wrong color.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-brnksksrns|Branakis Kaseranis]], stubborn and skilled; the contract crews, who resent him; the Order's knight who keeps the estate's rules. A brawl with the crews is rough and short, and the knight ends it.

**Player choices.** Defend Branakis's clamps, follow a contract crew's wagons, or walk the marked stand.

**Clues and rewards.** The dying stands lie along the valleys the crews cut last year. The old burners peel their timber where it falls, and their grounds are healthiest.

**Consequences.** A party that maps it has the forester's proof again, and nobody yet who wants it.

**If bypassed.** The forester's map is mentioned in Scene Three.

### Scene Two: The Baron's Valley

**Purpose.** The barons' side, and a man in debt to the temple.

**Situation.** [[place-dremenna|Dremenna]], under the last uncut valley on the estate. [[being-ardmsvlthmn|Ardemos Velthamen]] hunts with the king and denounces the priors' cutting in the king's hall.

**NPCs and threats.** Ardemos, proud and two years behind with his rent; the prior's agent, who comes to ask again whether a crew may enter the upper valley; and Ardemos's son, who rides at night with the knights of the [[place-ashenmere|Ashenmere]] chapel without his father's knowledge.

**Player choices.** Help the baron refuse, help him find the coin, or learn where his son rides.

**Clues and rewards.** The prior's offer in writing: the rent and the loan forgiven for the upper valley. The son's horse is lathered at dawn, and he wears the chapel's white.

**Consequences.** If the baron learns where his son rides and what the knights bring back, he tells the king, and the king learns of the undercroft.

> [!WARNING]
> This is one road by which the king can learn of the warrants. If he learns, the Holy Mother lets the judgment come to a vote.

**If bypassed.** The baron says nothing in the king's hall this season.

### Scene Three: The Mother's Hall and the King's

**Purpose.** A roleplay between two halls, one above the other.

**Situation.** The holy city. The party has standing enough, from Scenes One and Two or from its patron, to be received in both halls.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-ilvnssrkls|Ilvenas Sarkelis]] in the Mother's Hall, who asks the party about the empire's claimants. [[being-vrthlszrkms|Varthelas Zerkemis]], who wants witnesses that the Withering falls where the rites are neglected. [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]] in the hall below, who asks foreigners what the lower city says of him.

**Player choices.** Carry the forest's evidence to the high priest, the king or the Mother; learn the votes; or say nothing.

**Clues and rewards.** Varthelas keeps the forester's report in his chest. The Holy Mother's envoy from the claimant is lodged at the Charcoal Wagon, and someone has searched his room.

**Consequences.** Evidence carried to Varthelas goes into his chest. Carried to the king, it gives him a defense the high priesthood must answer. Carried to the Mother, it becomes another lever she holds.

**If bypassed.** The halls go on watching each other.

### Scene Four: The Cedar Box

**Purpose.** The king's request, and its weight.

**Situation.** The king's bedchamber, at night. Kelvaras gives the party a cedar box sealed with his private ring, to be put into the hands of the Orzas's secretary and no one else, and tells them to burn it unopened if the Order's knights stop them on the road.

**NPCs and threats.** The king, frightened and dignified; his Lord of the Seal, who has not read the letter; the knights on the gate.

**Player choices.** Accept, refuse, open the box, or sell its existence.

**Clues and rewards.** Opened, the letter offers the empire the overlordship of Nartun in name.

**Consequences.** A party carrying the box carries the kingdom's civil war in its baggage.

**If bypassed.** The box stays in the bedchamber.

### Scene Five: The Gate

**Purpose.** The climax on the holy road.

**Situation.** The city gate held by the Order, with the chapter house over it. [[being-sldrsplvrn|Saldoros Pelvaren]] stops the party, or does not, depending on what the Holy Mother knows.

**NPCs and threats.** Saldoros, the knights at the gate and the undercroft beneath them. A fight with the Order's knights at their own gate is one a party loses.

**Player choices.** Bluff, bargain, burn the box, hand it over or flee down the road.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that talks its way into the chapter house hears voices from below the stables.

**Consequences.** If the box reaches Orzara, the empire has a lever over Nartun. If the Order takes it, the Holy Mother has her judgment. If it burns, the king is no safer and no worse off. If the party finds the undercroft, it holds the empire's casus belli.

**If bypassed.** The box never leaves the city.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The cedar box stays in the bedchamber. Varthelas goes on counting votes, and the Holy Mother does not yet decide whether to let them be cast. The prior sends a crew into another valley, the Withering follows it, and the temples raise that village's dues. The claimant's envoy waits at the Charcoal Wagon for an answer the Mother has already given.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The judgment, the Withering and the claimant's offer all outlast the scenario. A party that carried the box has given an Orzas who is dying a reason to look north. One that found the undercroft holds a fact that could start the war between the empire and Nartun. One that mapped the Withering holds proof that every faction in the kingdom has a reason to refuse.
