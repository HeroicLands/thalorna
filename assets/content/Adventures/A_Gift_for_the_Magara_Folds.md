## Overview {#overview}

[[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] is waiting. At [[place-zarhanis|The First Flame]], Zarhanis in Okharik, the Oracle [[being-ewuratebara|Éwura Tébara]] has given no answer to the question the King put to her this year, whether he calls the Host. Until she answers, [[being-takorozanethar|Tákoro Zanethar]] may not call the Host, answer an embassy or set the tribute. The tribute the village tribes carry up to the temple-cities rose again at this year's festivals, and the chieftains count what they pay against what the cities return, and the count comes out short.

The [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] gives them something to compare it with. Okharis owes the empire nothing, so the Khelathi houses ask for horsemen, not tribute. Their embassies wait in the guest-houses of Zarhanis for an audience the King cannot grant, and their gifts go on past the cities to the chieftains, where the terms are better than the cities' own. One Khelathi envoy has waited long enough. He means to ride to the [[affiliation-magaratrb|Magara Tribe]], whose horsemen are the most sought in the realm, and name his price at the fire of their chieftain, [[being-omilotarombu|Omilo Tárombu]].

At [[place-vuthraka|The Wild Flame]], Vuthraka, the garrison's yearly draw of riders has come in late and short. [[being-ngaluromara|Ngalu Rómara]], the Captain of the Host, counts the Magara draw against the registers at Zarhanis, and the two disagree by forty horses.

The party enters as the honest guide the King sends with the envoy, as the Captain's eyes at the chieftain's fire, or as outsiders Omilo asks to read the envoy's terms.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The silence.** The scenario does not explain the Oracle's silence, and nothing in it ends the silence. Every faction reads it for its own purpose: the priests of Zarhanis hold that the Flame asks the realm to wait, the Warden of Vuthraka that the frontier must be armed against whatever the Oracle will not say, and the chieftains that the god does not stand behind the cities' ledgers. A party that asks the Oracle about the envoy gets no answer either.

**The envoy.** He speaks for a Khelathi house that needs horsemen and will not say which. The referee chooses among the reasons the houses give: a general who wants a force he trusts, a temple that distrusts its garrison, or an ally against a governor who holds back his render while the Khelathi flood fails. He is courteous, patient and honest by his own law, and he travels with a scribe trained in a temple school.

**The envoy's terms.** The gift is linen, bronze and a gold collar weighed out in gezan. The envoy's scribe is a temple witness, and his roll already holds the terms: the gift is an advance on the hire of a company of Magara riders, and the scribe enters it the moment the chieftain takes it. In Khelathi law a stranger's undertaking must be witnessed and written, and an open entry follows its maker until it is closed, so the envoy holds this to be plain fairness. At the fire, under Okharik hospitality, a chieftain who takes the gift owes riders in an archive he never sees. The Magara do not write. [[lore-guestprice|The Guest-Price]] remembers a Khelathi embassy whose hospitality at the fold was booked as tribute, and a party that reads the terms aloud at the fire makes the telling true in front of the whole tribe.

> [!WARNING]
> The envoy hides nothing by his own law, and the scribe's roll is the only place the terms are written. Let the party learn what the scribe enters before the fire, since reading the terms aloud is the one act that changes what the gift means.

**The draw.** Each tribe owes the garrison at Vuthraka a yearly draw of riders and their horses. This year the draw comes in late and short while the chieftains weigh the Khelathi offers. [[being-omaresonkara|Omare Sónkara]], the Warden of the Flame, reads a late draw as an insult to the Flame, and he sends a Tribute-Warden with Soldiers of Fire to bring the Magara draw in.

**The forty horses.** At this year's festival the Magara handed forty young horses to the Tribute-Wardens as the draw's mounts. The Chief Scribe at Zarhanis entered them as the tribe's tribute, so the registers show the tribute in full with young horses among it, while the Captain's tally-sticks, kept against the draw, still count forty horses owed. The Magara gave the horses once, and the cities have counted them twice. The draw's riders wait at the paddocks without horses, since a rider without his horse is no part of a draw, and the Magara say nothing, because they hold that the cities ought to notice for themselves. Nobody has stolen anything, and every count is honest.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The King cannot act and the Oracle does not speak. The scenario ends at the Magara fire, when the envoy's gift is taken or refused and the draw is settled or broken, and either outcome is carried up to the next moot.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The wanderer.** The King cannot grant the envoy an audience, and he wants an honest guide to take him to a chieftain's fold.
- **The soldier.** Ngalu Rómara wants someone at the chieftain's fire when the price is named.
- **The thief or the herder.** The Captain's tally-sticks and the registers at Zarhanis disagree by forty horses, and he wants the mounts at the Magara folds counted.
- **The leader.** Omilo Tárombu has been asked for a company of horsemen, and he wants an outsider to read the terms before he answers.

## The Opening {#opening}

The outer court of the temple at Zarhanis in the morning. Pilgrims, delegates and envoys stand in a queue that has not moved in days, waiting on an Oracle who has not spoken. A Khelathi in a linen mantle edged with blue sits apart on a folding stool he has brought himself. A young scribe beside him holds a sealed case on his knees.

> "I have waited forty days for a word from your King," the envoy says, in careful Okharik. "Your chieftains took my gifts in one."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Outer Court

**Purpose.** Meet the envoy, see the waiting realm, and take a commission.

**Situation.** The First Flame keeps the largest temple in Okharis and [[miscgear-emberfirst|The First Ember]], which has never gone out. The King lives here beside the High Pyremant. Chieftains come up to the moots with the tribute they owe and a count of what they have been asked for, and the Chief Scribe's registers hold what is owed and what is late. Khelathi envoys wait in the guest-houses for an answer the King cannot give.

**NPCs and threats.** Tákoro Zanethar, patient, courteous and watchful, who hears a speech to the end and knows each chieftain's dues from memory. Éwura Tébara, the Oracle, still and slow to speak, who holds that no one may speak the answer in the Flame's place. The envoy and his scribe. Delegates in the queue, who watch every stranger the King receives.

**Player choices.** Take the King's charge to guide the envoy, ask to read the Magara entry in the Chief Scribe's registers, keep the Oracle's queue in order as she asks and note who comes to ask what, or hire on with the envoy directly.

**Clues and rewards.** The King cannot answer the envoy and does not forbid him the road. He asks the party to see that the envoy reaches the fold safely and to tell him what is said there. The registers show the Magara tribute entered in full this year, young horses among it. The envoy calls his gift a gift and his scribe calls it something else, in the Khelathi tongue, when he thinks no Okharik is listening.

**Consequences.** The party rides with the King's word, which protects them on the road and binds them to report.

**If bypassed.** The envoy leaves for the Magara folds without a guide, and the party catches him up on the grass.

### Scene Two: The Wild Flame

**Purpose.** Hear the frontier's side and the Captain's count.

**Situation.** Vuthraka guards the southern frontier where the grassland meets the wall of the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]]. The Warden asks more of the realm this year and reads the Oracle's silence as a reason to arm. The garrison drills before dawn and waits, because neither the Warden nor the Captain may call the Host without the King. A young warrior of a village tribe who came for [The Trial of Flames]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} has been turned away without a reason.

**NPCs and threats.** Ngalu Rómara, plain, patient with men and impatient with excuses, who can say from memory how many horses each tribe owes and tends to read a late rider as a disloyal one. Omare Sónkara, the Warden, formal, humorless and fair, who drills the garrison himself. A Tribute-Warden and a file of Soldiers of Fire making ready to ride for the Magara folds.

**Player choices.** Take the Captain's commission to count the Magara horses, ride with the Tribute-Warden, ask the Warden what he means to do if the Magara refuse, or ask the turned-away warrior what the clans say about the draw.

**Clues and rewards.** The Captain's tally-sticks show the Magara forty horses short of the draw, and he lays them beside his copy of the registers. The Warden says that a tribe which takes Khelathi gifts and holds back the draw has chosen whom it rides for. The turned-away warrior says the Magara gave their best young horses at the festival and have nothing left to draw.

**Consequences.** The Tribute-Warden rides a day behind the party, or with them, and expects to find the draw ready.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the folds without the Captain's count, and the Tribute-Warden's arrival is a surprise.

### Scene Three: The Fold Gate

**Purpose.** Reach the Magara, receive their hospitality, and count the horses.

**Situation.** [[place-magaravlg|Magara Village]], the seat of the Magara Tribe, whose paddocks run out onto the grassland. The tribe breeds and breaks horses, and its families keep the [Spirit Blades]{.name data-type="weapongear"}. Buyers from the temple-cities come each year to choose from the young horses. A stranger at the fold gate is handed soured milk before anyone asks his business.

**NPCs and threats.** Omilo Tárombu, who broke his first horse at eight and wants his riders spent only when the King calls the Host. The War-Leader, who takes the riders out. The Blade families, who expect their standing kept at every council. Herders who count every horse that passes them.

**Player choices.** Drink at the gate and keep the guest's peace, count the paddocks, ask the herders where the best young horses went, or sit with Omilo before the envoy does.

**Clues and rewards.** The paddocks hold the year's young horses, forty fewer than a full year's breeding. The herders name the festival day the Tribute-Wardens chose forty and led them away. Omilo asks the party to read the envoy's terms to him before he answers, because a written thing he cannot read is a thing he cannot weigh.

**Consequences.** The party is under the Magara's hospitality, and the guest's peace binds them as it binds the envoy.

**If bypassed.** The party arrives with the envoy at the fire and has no count of its own.

### Scene Four: The Soldiers at the Fold

**Purpose.** Bring the cities' demand and the Khelathi offer to the same fire.

**Situation.** The night before the envoy names his price. The Tribute-Warden and his Soldiers of Fire ride in to collect the draw and find a Khelathi envoy under the Magara's hospitality, with his gift unpacked in the chieftain's house. The Magara riders stand along the paddocks, and the Soldiers of Fire stand by their own horses with their spears grounded.

**NPCs and threats.** The Tribute-Warden, who has a count to bring home and an envoy to report. The Soldiers of Fire, few and far from Vuthraka. The War-Leader and the Magara riders, who outnumber them. The envoy, who does not understand why the camp has gone quiet.

**Player choices.** Speak for the Tribute-Warden, speak for the Magara, keep the envoy out of sight, or put the forty horses in front of everyone.

**Clues and rewards.** The Tribute-Warden carries the same register entry the party read at Zarhanis and does not know what it means for the draw. A party that has both counts can show the Tribute-Warden that the horses were given once and counted twice.

**Consequences.** If blood is drawn at the fold, the [Three-Flame Settlement]{.name data-type="lore/law"} between the city and the tribe breaks on this ground, and the moot hears it as a war.

**If bypassed.** The Tribute-Warden reaches the fire in the morning, while the envoy is naming his price.

### Scene Five: The Price at the Fire

**Purpose.** The envoy names his price, and the Magara answer.

**Situation.** Omilo's fire in the morning, with the elders, the Blade families and the War-Leader seated, the envoy and his scribe across the fire, the gift laid out on a hide, and the Tribute-Warden standing at the edge of the light. The envoy names a price for a company of riders. The scribe has his roll open.

**NPCs and threats.** Omilo, who must answer before his tribe. The envoy, honest by his own law. The scribe and his roll. The Tribute-Warden and the Soldiers of Fire. The War-Leader, whose riders would be the company.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the terms aloud._ The tribe hears what the gift is, and Omilo answers the whole of it.
- _Let the gift be taken._ Omilo takes the gift, commits to nothing, and the scribe writes.
- _Settle the draw first._ The party shows both counts, the Tribute-Warden carries home the word that the forty were given, and the War-Leader sends the riders he has.
- _Escort the envoy away._ The party takes the envoy back toward Zarhanis before he names his price, and the King hears that his guide was honest.

**Clues and rewards.** The King pays in his favor and a seat at the next moot's edge. The Captain pays for a true count. Omilo pays in horses, and the envoy in gold weighed out at the Khelathi standard.

**Consequences.** Whatever is decided at the fire goes up to the next moot as the Magara's answer, and the other chieftains measure their own by it.

**If bypassed.** Omilo takes the gift without hearing its terms, and the Tribute-Warden reports what he saw.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Omilo takes the envoy's gift and commits to nothing, as the chieftains take every Khelathi offer, and the scribe writes. The Tribute-Warden finds a Khelathi envoy at the fire and a draw forty horses short, and reports both to Vuthraka. The Warden asks the King to act, and the King cannot. At the next moot the cities name the Magara as a tribe that sells its riders, the Magara name the cities as a crown that counts a gift twice, and the other chieftains listen to both. In the season after, the envoy's house sends to the Magara again, and its messenger brings the scribe's roll.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The terms read.** Omilo refuses the gift or names a price of his own, and the account the chieftains keep, the Guest-Price, is the one they set beside it that winter. The envoy returns to Zarhanis with nothing, and Khelathi gifts reach the other chieftains more carefully.
- **The gift taken.** The Magara have the linen and the gold, and the envoy has what his scribe wrote. The tribe learns what that is when the envoy's house sends for it.
- **The draw settled.** The registers and the tally agree, the riders go to Vuthraka with what horses the tribe can spare, and the Captain counts the Magara as loyal. The Chief Scribe's registers are read more closely at the next moot.
- **Blood at the fold.** The Warden has his insult, the Magara have their grievance, and the King, who cannot act, has a quarrel at the heart of the Three-Flame Settlement.
