## Overview {#overview}

A tunnel-well, a _katelun_, in the dry southern interior of [[place-bethuargn|Bethua Region]] has stopped running. Three nights ago the water stopped coming out at the mouth below the village. The barley on the village's strip stands in the ear, and the green is going out of it from the far end inward.

[[being-izetmu|The Izet-Mu]], the chief engineer-priestess of the [[affiliation-orderwaters|Order of the Waters]], is in the south on the Order's round and was at [[place-soredia|Soredia]], a day's ride from the line, when word of the dry mouth came. She called together the Soredia gang that keeps the line and rode ahead to the mouth herself, and she does not send the gang down. The roof has fallen somewhere in the last shafts above the village, and she does not put men under a roof she does not understand. She wants to know whether the collapse was an accident, and she asks strangers, who belong to no House and no gang on the line.

The party can learn how the roof fell, who was told that it might, and who has been drinking the line's water.

**The repair.** Once the Izet-Mu sees the cut, or is shown proof of it, she sends the gang down the next morning to cut a turn around the fall and wide of the cracked stretch. The gang pulls the set stone and blocks the branch as it goes down, and the water reaches the mouth two days later. The gang goes down no earlier than the morning of Day Three, when it arrives.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The upper ground.** Upslope of the village and to one side of the line lies grassland between the strips, unsown because no water reaches it. This season the holder of that ground has put it under barley, off the gate-roll, to sell to the Khelathi patron in the grain sale the [[affiliation-councilhouses|Council of Houses]] presses the Metrissa for, and a [[being-tradefactor|Trade-House Factor]] has bought the crop standing at the firm price the quay quotes this year. Who the holder is belongs to the Game Master. She acts through a woman overseer, and neither of them comes to the mouth.

**The cut.** Before the sowing, and after the Order's sisters had walked the line for the season, the holder hired a few diggers of the Soredia gang that keeps the line to work for her. A man makes no contract in his own name, so she hired them through the women they are under, and the wife of the head digger made the bargain and took the pay in Khelathi weight-pieces. The diggers went down one of the last shafts above the village, where the tunnel runs shallow, cut a short branch from the gallery wall running downhill to the upper field, and set a stone in the gallery to turn part of the flow into it. All season the line has run low at the mouth and the upper field has run green.

**The crack.** While he was setting the stone, the head digger put his lamp to the roof just below the cut and saw a thread, a crack running down the gallery and up into the gravel. He came up and told his wife. She took it to the overseer, and the holder sent back word to finish. They finished, and nobody told the Order.

**The fall.** Three nights before the party arrives, the roof came down at the lower end of the cracked stretch. Nobody was under it. Between the cut and the fall the crack still runs along the roof, opened wide. The fall blocks the gallery, the water backs up behind it, and nearly all of it now runs out of the branch. The village's mouth is dry, and the green past the line is greener than ever.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The answer to the Izet-Mu's question is neither of the two she fears. Nobody meant the roof to fall, and it did not fall by itself. The cut brought it down, and the holder was told it might. The cut and the crack are in the ground. Who saw the crack and who was told is known only to the people who kept it from the Order, and the man who saw it cannot stand before the Order except through his wife.

**The Order's rule.** At the head of every new mother-well the eldest sister present reads [[lore-risakostest|The Testimony of Rísakos]], with the note the Hiora Thériria added to the Order's copy in her own hand: a crack seen is said, by whoever sees it, and the work stops. The Izet-Mu has heard it read at more mother-wells than anyone living, and that rule is why she waits.

> [!WARNING]
> The Izet-Mu takes the [[affiliation-sacredcollege|Sacred College]]'s case for keeping the grain as the whole of the truth. A field sown off the roll to sell into the Khelathi sale is the strongest argument she has heard against the sale, and once she knows of it, she wants it heard in Bethura. Let the party see that before it decides how much to tell her.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- **Which line, and which village.** Any tunnel-well of the dry southern interior a day's ride from Soredia. [[place-sehela|Sehela]] is not the village: its line runs and its fields are green.
- **Who holds the upper ground.** A Junior House that grazes the grassland, or the House that holds the village's own strip, taking water from its own tenants' shares. The clues serve either.
- **What the holder answers for.** The fields belong to a House, the water to the Order, and the _katelun_ to the realm. Water taken out of turn answers to the Order, and damage to the line to the Hégissa of the southern cantons, who holds the realm's courts there. The penalties are the Game Master's.
- **The wife's fate.** Whether the Order goes on giving her household work once her part is known.
- **The wife in person.** She is the one witness the Game Master brings to the table in person. The head digger and the gang are not met: the Izet-Mu keeps her gang from strangers and answers for it. Asked who keeps the line, she names the Soredia gang and its head digger's household at any time. Once she has seen the pick handle, she keeps him at the camp and takes nothing from him except through his wife.
- **Day Eight.** If the gang goes down blind, whether the head digger speaks before it reaches the cracked stretch, and whether the roof comes down again.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar: a second pair of eyes.** At Soredia the Izet-Mu wants someone who can read a gate-roll and a line's book and has no place in either, to say what the two show together. A party hired there rides in behind her and reaches the mouth on Day One, and the opening is her briefing at the shaft.
- **The warrior: hands for the shafts.** The gang is two days away, and the Izet-Mu wants the last shafts above the fall looked at now, by people strong enough to climb out of one fast.
- **The courtier: a word for the village.** The levy women want a stranger to stand with them before the Izet-Mu and say for the village that it gives her until Day Four.
- **The trader: a crop to inspect.** A Trade-House Factor riding out before the harvest to look over a crop the House has bought standing wants company on the road. They reach the village on Day One, where the factor lodges and goes up to the field on Day Three.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The track runs beside a row of low earth rings in the grass, a shaft every few hundred paces, each capped with stones, and ends at a stone lip in the ground below a village of mud brick. Nothing comes out of the lip. The channel from it to the first field has dried and cracked into plates. Beyond, the barley stands in the ear, and the far rows have gone the color of straw.
>
> A woman in a linen robe sits on the rim of the last shaft with her legs over the edge and dust to her knees. She does not get up. "I have a gang two days away," she says, "and I am not sending it down. Tell me why the roof fell, and I send it."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Dry Mouth

**Purpose.** Establish the stakes and the first clues.

**Situation.** The mouth of the line and the village above it, on Day One. The Izet-Mu has the line's book and the village's gate-roll open on her knees. Women of the village gather at the edge of the browning rows.

**NPCs and threats.** The Izet-Mu; women of the village who drill with the levy and are at home this season, not at the posts, each a [[being-levywoman|Levy Woman of the Southern Cantons]], spears in hand and their barley dying behind them.

**Player choices.** Read the book and the roll with the Izet-Mu, or go among the women.

**Clues and rewards.** The book shows the season's walk and no crack. The gate-roll shows the mouth running about a third low all season; the village took it for a low mother-well in a dry year. The women point upslope at the green past the line, where nobody has ever sowed, and name whose ground it is.

**Consequences.** The heads of the households meet that night and give the Izet-Mu until Day Four either way, and the levy women carry their word to the mouth in the morning. A party that stands with the levy women can speak for the village afterward; one that does not is a stranger the village watches.

**If bypassed.** The levy women show the party the green field on Day Four (Scene Three).

### Scene Two: Down the Shafts

**Purpose.** Find the cut.

**Situation.** The last shafts above the village, from Day Two. A shaft is a narrow hole with footholds, and the gallery at its bottom is low enough that a tall person stoops. Water stands knee-deep behind the fall. From the shaft-heads the upper field shows plain.

**NPCs and threats.** No one is down there. The bad air at the fall drops a lamp's flame small and blue and a careless party where it stands, and under the cracked stretch a heavy step brings gravel down.

**Player choices.** Go down shaft by shaft and keep a lamp line, wade the backed-up water toward the fall, or follow the line on the surface to the shaft nearest the green.

**Clues and rewards.** A fresh branch cut into the gallery wall runs off downhill toward the upper field, with a set stone turning the water into it. A broken pick handle with the Order's mark lies in the mud. Below the cut the crack runs along the roof to the fall, opened wide: the roof was failing, though nothing in it says who saw it first. Shown the handle, the Izet-Mu says that only the Soredia gang that keeps this line has the Order's tools here, so the cut is her own gang's work, and she points the party to its head digger's household at Soredia.

**Consequences.** The repair begins as the clock gives it. A party that goes down past a blue flame comes up short of breath and sick; one that stays down is carried out.

**If bypassed.** The gang finds the cut on Day Eight, after it has begun to cut toward the fall.

### Scene Three: The Upper Field

**Purpose.** Find who drinks the water, and keep the village from a wrong it pays for.

**Situation.** The green field on the upper ground, where nobody works while the ears fill. Before Day Three the outlet runs open; from Day Three it lies under heaped brush. The factor is at the field on Day Three and lodges in the village after it. On Day Four the levy women come up the slope with spears.

**NPCs and threats.** The Trade-House Factor, come to look at a crop the House has paid for; the levy women, who mean to tear the crop out and fill the outlet.

**Player choices.** Pull the brush off the outlet, talk to the factor, stand between the levy women and the field, or let them do what they came for.

**Clues and rewards.** The outlet runs under the brush until the gang blocks the branch. Asked by anyone who looks like a buyer or an officer, the factor shows the purchase under the holder's seal. The factor knows nothing of the outlet until shown it, and then trades the truth about the purchase for the House being left out of what follows.

**Consequences.** Filling the outlet gives the village nothing back, because the fall still blocks the way down. A party that stops the levy women and shows them the outlet earns the village's trust; otherwise Day Four goes as the clock gives it.

**If bypassed.** Day Four goes as the clock gives it, and the party has no proof of the sale the field was sown for.

### Scene Four: The Diggers' Street at Soredia

**Purpose.** Learn who was told about the crack.

**Situation.** [[place-soredia|Soredia]], the town of well-sinkers and tunnel-diggers, a day's ride from the line, and the household the Izet-Mu named. The head digger is away with the gang; his wife holds the household and answers for him.

**NPCs and threats.** The head digger's wife, who made the bargain.

**Player choices.** Put the cut to her plainly, offer her a reason to speak, or bring her to the mouth to stand for her husband before the Izet-Mu.

**Clues and rewards.** The household holds Khelathi weight-pieces it has no Order work to account for, and from Day Five newly paid ones besides. Before Day Six, faced with the cut, she says that she made the bargain, that her husband saw the crack, and that she carried it to the overseer. From Day Six she has taken the overseer's money and denies all of it, unless the party gives her a reason the money cannot outweigh: the Order learns of the cut either way, and a household that speaks first keeps more than one that is found out.

**Consequences.** A man cannot stand before a court except through the woman responsible for him, so the head digger's word reaches the Izet-Mu only if his wife brings it.

**If bypassed.** The party has the cut and the holder but not the crack's witness.

## Endings {#endings}

On Day Seven the Izet-Mu sits on the rim of the last shaft and hears the party. The holder does not come; she answers when she is summoned.

- **The cut, the holder and the witness.** With the wife standing for her husband, the Izet-Mu has the whole of it. She has the branch blocked if the gang has not already, enters the fall as caused by a cut the holder ordered finished after she was told of the crack, and summons the holder before the Order for the water and before the Hégissa of the southern cantons for the line. The wife's part goes into the book with the rest. The Izet-Mu carries the case to the Sacred College as evidence of what the sale makes Houses do.
- **The cut and the holder, without the witness.** The Izet-Mu has the branch blocked if the gang has not already, and the holder pays for the water. Summoned, the holder says she never knew a cut could bring down a roof, and nobody can say otherwise. The fall is entered as caused by the cut, and the diggers' households keep their work. The Izet-Mu carries the field to the Sacred College all the same.

If the Izet-Mu has seen the cut by the reckoning, the repair reaches the mouth before the end of Day Ten and the strip is harvested. Without the cut, Day Eight goes as under If the Party Does Nothing. A party that let the levy women have their way on Day Four leaves the village owing the holder's House for the torn crop, whatever the reckoning finds.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The levy women tear out half the upper crop on Day Four, and the overseer's rider buys the wife's silence on Day Five. On Day Eight the Izet-Mu, out of time, sights a turn through the stretch her book calls sound and sends the gang down, with the head digger in it. Whether he speaks before the gang reaches the crack, and whether the roof comes down, is the Game Master's. If it does, the strip is lost; if it does not, the gang finds the cut and blocks it, the water comes on Day Ten, and the Izet-Mu learns of the cut but not who was told of the crack. The rest of the upper field is reaped, and its grain goes north with the surplus the Council of Houses and the Sacred College argue over.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Whenever the Izet-Mu learns of the field, she carries it to the College, and the Houses that hold grassland between the strips are asked in the Council of Houses whether they have sowed it. The village remembers who stood between its spears and the upper field.
