A fire that needs no fuel burns in a kiln in the dead pines, tended by the dead of a Black Flame cell. A Republic that paid for the priory wants it quietly silenced, and a Peshtar chief wants it left alone. The party goes in for the iron the fire has made, and must decide what to do with the fire.

## Overview

[[place-blackkilnpriory|Blackkiln Priory]] is the ruin of a cell of the [[affiliation-blackflame|Black Flame]] raised with Ondarethan money to burn out Peshtar raiders. Its Flame-Warden, **Gerolt Sarne**, fed the first raiders he caught to his kiln, and the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]] came in with them. The fire burns black and the cell's dead tend it, because the Shadow still uses them to carry offerings. Beneath the chapel is a crypt of the cell and, beyond it, the iron the fire has made.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Thornbury factor who deals in odd metal will pay well for the black iron that is said to cool in the old kilns, and gives a map of the charcoal road.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** An Ondarethan border magistrate hires a squad to carry out a night patrol that ends at the kilns. He does not say whose money is buried there.
- **The leader.** A Peshtar war-chief whose clan burned the priory sends a chieftain to see whether the fire has spread. The clan's dead lie in the dead pines.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer carries a brand or an old burn that has begun to ache, and a priest of the Sacred Forge says the cure lies at Blackkiln.

## Scene One: The Dead Pines

**Purpose.** Show the fire's reach and put the dead on guard.

**Situation.** The charcoal road runs between pines gone grey and brittle. Cairns mark each furlong and each cairn has a charred bone set in its top. The heat reaches the road at dusk and the air smells of hot iron. In the clearing, a figure in blackened mail kneels at the chapel door, a long sword across his knees.

**NPCs and threats.** The **cinder-sentry** is the cell's dead gatekeeper, a charred man in the cell's mail who kneels as if in prayer and rises when a living step crosses the threshold. He is slow and strong, shedding ash at every step. His blade is hot enough to scorch wood. The pines around him shed dry needles that catch easily, and the clearing is a tinderbox.

**Player choices.** Force: cut the sentry down, taking burns from his sword. Stealth: slip past while he kneels at his prayers. Guile: speak the Black Flame's challenge, if the party has learned it from a captive. Parley: kneel with him and ask for the Flame-Warden.

**Clues and rewards.** The sentry's mail carries the cell's mark, a mailed fist in flame, and a stamp of the Republic's border office on the inside of the gorget. The sentry says, if asked, that the Warden is below and that the fire is hungry.

**Consequences.** A party that fights in the clearing may set the pines alight. A party that kneels with the sentry is passed in.

**If bypassed.** A party that enters through the split east wall comes into the chapel from the side, and the sentry follows them in.

## Scene Two: The Watcher in the Nave

**Purpose.** Give the party the priory's history and a hard choice about the one living thing in it.

**Situation.** In the chapel's nave, under a patch of surviving roof, a man in a grey robe sits on a stool beside a cold brazier. **Tamsen Orlick** is a lay brother of Sélmoros who has kept a vigil at the priory for eleven years, sent by his order to see that the fire does not spread. He has a sack of salt, a bell, and a half-written record.

**NPCs and threats.** **Tamsen Orlick** is gaunt, courteous and tired. He fears the fire and feels responsible for the dead, who he believes can be released if the fire is quenched. He is lying about one thing: he has been feeding the fire himself, small offerings of his own blood, to keep the dead quiet, and the fire has begun to answer him.

**Player choices.** Force: seize the priest and leave him bound. Stealth: leave him sitting and go past. Guile: question him until the lie breaks. Parley: ask him honestly what he has done.

**Clues and rewards.** His record names the Warden, the money that paid for the priory and the border magistrate who delivered it. He has the key to the crypt door. He knows that the fire answers only to a name and that the name is Sarne's.

**Consequences.** If he is exposed, he breaks and asks to be taken out. If he is trusted, he opens the crypt. If he is left, the fire takes him in the autumn.

**If bypassed.** A party that ignores him finds the crypt door locked and his key on his belt.

## Scene Three: The Braziers

**Purpose.** Make the fire itself the puzzle.

**Situation.** The crypt is a vault of black stone with three braziers set in niches along its length and a stair beyond them. The braziers are cold. The air is hot and still, and the walls are warm to the touch. A tablet by the stair gives the Black Flame's order of rites in blackened letters.

**NPCs and threats.** The fire is the threat. A brazier lit in the wrong order flares black and the dead stir in the walls. The **ash-brothers**, Aspirants of the cell, rise from the niches as grey, silent figures that move in the dark without light, and they put out any lamp. The only light in the crypt is the party's own, and every lamp is put out in turn.

**Player choices.** Force: fight the ash-brothers in the dark. Stealth: cross the crypt along the wall by touch. Guile: light the braziers in the order the tablet gives, reading it as the cell's rites. Parley: speak the cell's rites over the dead and ask for passage.

**Clues and rewards.** The tablet's order is first the brazier of ash, then the brazier of iron, then the brazier of flame, and the ash-brothers kneel when it is done. A wall niche holds a coffer with a few coins of the Republic.

**Consequences.** A wrong order sets the dead against the party. A right one gives passage and takes from the party a measure of warmth, a chill that lasts a season.

**If bypassed.** A party that crosses the crypt by the roof arrives at the stair with the ash-brothers behind it.

## Scene Four: The Kiln

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the fire and the Warden.

**Situation.** The stair opens into the largest kiln, a stone dome with a pit at its center. The black fire burns in the pit. In its heart stands a figure in a Flame-Warden's mail, glowing as iron glows. Sarne has been burning for two generations.

**NPCs and threats.** **Gerolt Sarne** is a vast, heat-wavering shape of black mail and white-hot bone. He speaks in a voice like a bellows. The Shadow has taken his god's name and given him back a worse one, and he believes he is feeding the Forge-Lord. He fights by touch: where he strikes, the air ignites. The cell's dead stand around the pit and feed the fire.

**Player choices.** Force: fight the Warden at the pit's rim. Stealth: starve the fire by breaking the cell's offerings. Guile: offer the Warden an offering that is not a life, such as the iron of his own forge. Parley: tell him the Republic has abandoned him.

**Clues and rewards.** The black fire can be quenched only by water carried from a spring that has never been touched by the Shadow, or by rites of Sélmoros spoken for the Warden by name. Sarne's mail carries a stamp of the Sacred Forge, from his first ordination, before he turned.

**Consequences.** If the fire is quenched, the dead fall and the kilns cool. If Sarne is slain and the fire kept, the black fire passes to the nearest flame-priest who touches it. If the party flees, the pines catch.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves the kiln unreached is chased up the stair by flame and a voice that knows their names.

## Scene Five: The Black Iron

**Purpose.** Pay the reward and name its cost.

**Situation.** Behind the kiln's wall is a smaller chamber with three bars of dark iron on a cooling rack, still warm. An iron box stands in the corner with the Republic's stamp on its lid. The air is cold and dry.

**NPCs and threats.** None, unless the fire survives, in which case the bars hum.

**Player choices.** Force: take the bars and the box and run. Stealth: copy the papers in the box. Guile: sell the papers to the border magistrate's rival. Parley: take them to the Sacred Forge's smith-priest.

**Clues and rewards.** The bars are black iron, worth a few dozen Ardin each to a smith who knows what to do with them and carrying a touch of the Shadow in the metal. The box holds the Republic's contract for the priory: a list of the sums paid and the names of the magistrates who signed. One name belongs to a Senator who still sits.

**Consequences.** Anyone who carries the bars for long dreams of fire. The contract can ruin a Senator, or be sold back to him.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves the chamber hears of the box from the smith-priest within the year.

## Aftermath

- **If the fire is quenched.** The pines begin to green over the following years. The Peshtar clans leave the kilns alone and the Sacred Forge asks for the stones.
- **If the contract reaches the Senate's enemies.** A Senator falls, and the Black Flame's shrines in Ondareth lose a patron.
- **If the fire is kept.** The Black Flame sends a Scorchmaster for the iron.
- **If the party fails.** The fire spreads along the pines to the border farms.
