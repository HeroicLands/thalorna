## The Roll Beneath the Hill

A cohort of the old legion of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] went into a hill in [[place-auldalon|Áuldalon]] in the war at [[place-valdun|Valdun]] and never came out. Its _Lusar_ demanded shelter of the thing that kept the hall, and the thing gave exactly what was demanded. The cohort holds the door against all comers, and the roll of those who hold it grows by anyone who eats the bread.

### Hook

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each leads to [[place-kptfr|The Kept Fire]].

- **The thief.** A Vylarian collector in [[place-beravel|Péravel]] pays for a cohort's standard, and the drovers say the cohort's pay-chest is still in the hill with the banner. The only trouble is the old soldiers who guard it.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A clerk of the _felzos_'s house wants proof a cohort held rather than deserted, so that its descendants can claim the arrears. A veteran who can read a muster-roll and a sword's wear is worth a fee and a share.
- **The leader.** A drover family from the southern hills asks the party to bring back a boy who followed a lamp into the fold three years ago. The boy is counted at the door.
- **The lone wanderer.** A bard of [[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]] at the hill's foot asks for company. She has the lament's last verse and needs to hear whether the cohort still answers it.

### Overview

The cohort's _Lusar_, [Solkimos]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, led it into the fold on a winter night, starving and harried, and demanded shelter in the Sovereign's name. The Giver, [Dáimela]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"}, the thing that keeps the hall, gave a fire, bread and a roof. The _Lusar_ swore to hold the place against all comers while the Sovereign's name stood. The Giver took the oath exactly.

The [Kept]{.name data-type="affiliation"} do not age, do not sleep, and do not leave. They are not dead, and nothing of [[lore-theshadow|The Shadow]] is in them. They eat the hall's bread and answer the roll. A stranger who eats at the fire is entered on the roll and owes the hall its watch.

### Scene One: The Door

**Purpose.** Meet the sentry, learn the hall's grammar, and show that every approach costs something.

**Situation.** The path to the door is swept stone, laid in the Vylarian way. The door is a slab hung on iron, and before it a sentry stands in the rotted harness of a legionary, spear grounded, helm tilted, as alert as a man on the first night of a watch. His challenge is in [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], a clipped phrase that ends on a rising note. If the party answers, he counts them aloud.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Sentry** is the cohort's youngest man, and his face is as young as the day he took the post. He is dutiful, afraid, and very fast with a spear. He lowers it when the challenge is answered wrong. Behind him the door opens inward on a corridor lit from within, and four more Kept stand in the passage with their shields locked.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ the Kept are soldiers, trained, and the hall refills their number each dusk. The party can break the door, and the cohort turns out from the hall to meet them.
- _Stealth:_ a smoke-vent at the hill's crown opens into the hall's rafters. A climb in the dark gets a thief past the sentry, and past the roll, and into the hall as an uncounted man.
- _Guile:_ the watchword is a Vylari phrase from the legion's last year in the hills. A party that has the phrase from the _felzos_'s house or the lament answers the challenge as a relief detail. A party that bluffs a courier's errand holds the sentry for one question only.
- _Parley:_ ask for shelter instead of demanding it. The sentry has not heard the word in centuries and does not know what to do with it, and he lets the party in on that confusion.

**Clues and rewards.** The sentry's harness bears the cohort's mark. The watchword, if found, is useful in every scene. The vent is the way out when the door is not.

**Consequences.** A party that breaks the door is entered on the roll as hostile, and the _Lusar_ greets them as such. A party that answers the watchword is treated as officers.

**If bypassed.** If the party avoids the sentry by way of the vent, they arrive in the hall's rafters during the feast and must find a way down.

### Scene Two: The Table

**Purpose.** Teach the rule of guest-right and offer the party the choice that matters.

**Situation.** The great hall is warm, long and roofed with turf. A fire the length of a river-barge burns down the middle, and the Kept sit at the long benches in their rotted harness, eating. The cook, a grey-armed man in a leather apron, carries bread to every guest. A seat stands at the head of the hall with no one in it, a plain chair of black wood, and nobody looks at it.

**NPCs and threats.** [Thelvaran]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the senior sergeant, sits nearest the party and speaks Vylari with a hill accent. He tells them, kindly, to eat. The _Lusar_ watches from the far end and does not rise. The Kept are neither hostile nor friendly. They are bored, loyal, and desperate for news.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ none that helps. A fight at the table is a fight with a hall of soldiers in full harness and no fear of death.
- _Stealth:_ pocket the bread and eat nothing. A thief who palms the loaf and drinks only from his own skin is a guest who has declined, and nobody can say otherwise.
- _Guile:_ share the party's own food with the Kept. A loaf passed hand to hand is a gift between equals, not a meal at the hall's expense. It does not bind the party, and it binds the sergeant to a small debt.
- _Parley:_ ask the sergeant what he would do if the watch were relieved. He answers, after a long silence, that he does not remember what he did before it.

**Clues and rewards.** The cook's tally-sticks show the roll's length. The sergeant's debt buys a candid hour about the _Lusar_. The empty chair is the Giver's, and anyone who sits in it is asked, once, what they want.

**Consequences.** A character who eats the bread is on the roll. The roll is called at the third watch.

**If bypassed.** A party that keeps clear of the table sleeps in the corridor, among the shields, and is woken for the roll as unfed guests.

### Scene Three: The Roll

**Purpose.** Spring the trap: someone is on the roll, and the roll is called.

**Situation.** At the third watch a horn sounds and the Kept stand to their posts. The _Lusar_'s clerk reads names off a wax tablet. Each name called is answered by its owner stepping into line. The names are those who ate. Every name called, living or Kept, goes to the post.

**NPCs and threats.** The clerk, a man with a stained thumb and a flat voice, reads the roll without looking up. The Kept are not cruel in this. They step to their posts, and so does any guest whose name is called, with no more will of his own than a man in a dream.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ stop the clerk. The roll does not need him: the names are written in the hall's wood.
- _Stealth:_ get the called character out of line before the post closes on him. A friend with a rope and a shoulder can drag a walking man off the floor, and the hall resists as the tide resists a swimmer.
- _Guile:_ answer for the called man in a different voice. The roll takes the name from whoever says it, and the called man is then absent and counted anyway.
- _Parley:_ ask the _Lusar_ to strike a guest from the roll. He refuses a request from a stranger, and grants an order from an officer.

**Clues and rewards.** The hall's rule: the roll closes at dawn. A man still on it at dawn stays. The tablet lists the cohort's number, and the _Lusar_'s name is on it as present.

**Consequences.** A character placed at a post is lost until the party breaks the roll, relieves the watch or buys him free. The post holds by a guest's own will, which weakens as the night goes.

**If bypassed.** If nobody ate, the roll is called on the Kept alone, and the party passes the night as spectators. The hall is quiet and the _Lusar_ is ready.

### Scene Four: The High Seat

**Purpose.** The confrontation, and the party's choice about how the watch ends.

**Situation.** At the head of the hall, before the black chair, the _Lusar_ receives the party. Solkimos is a spare man in an officer's harness, with a face carved by cold and by the weight of a duty never discharged. He knows the party is not a relief. He asks, with perfect courtesy, whether they will take the door.

**NPCs and threats.** The _Lusar_ and the Kept at his back, and the empty chair that is not empty. The Kept fight in good order with locked shields, the sergeant on the right, and the _Lusar_ with a short sword he has not drawn in a long time. The Giver does not move, and the air near the chair is a little thicker than elsewhere.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ kill the _Lusar_, and command of the cohort passes to the one who took it. The Kept obey the new commander, and the commander is bound to the watch. A party that wins by sword is told, gently, to hold the door.
- _Stealth:_ steal the banner from the standard-rack in the _Lusar_'s alcove while the Kept stand to the roll. Without the standard the cohort cannot muster, and the _Lusar_ must treat with whoever holds it.
- _Guile:_ present credentials. A character who is an officer with a recognized commission, or who forges the _felzos_'s seal and the watchword, can formally relieve the watch. The _Lusar_ examines the seal, and his sergeant checks the paper. A convincing forgery works. A poor one starts the fight.
- _Parley:_ ask the _Lusar_ to ask. Only he can release the cohort, by asking the Giver for it. A Vylarian _Lusar_ does not ask a barbarian spirit for anything. A party that makes him see the last night he stood in the door, and how the Giver answered the demand, may make him say it.

**Clues and rewards.** The Giver answers an asking and nothing else. If the _Lusar_ asks, the black chair is occupied for a moment by a figure of firelight and old bread, who says "Go." The watch ends.

**Consequences.** The Kept's years come due when the watch ends. They are not dust. They are old men, and they die in the gray before dawn, most of them on the hill's slope with their faces to the east. The _Lusar_ dies last.

**If bypassed.** A party that cannot bring the _Lusar_ to the high seat leaves by the vent. The roll stays open, and the party's missing man stays on it.

### Scene Five: The Pay-Chest

**Purpose.** Reward the party and turn a lost cohort into a claim.

**Situation.** Behind the standard-rack, in a stone-lined cell, the cohort's pay-chest is bolted to the floor. It holds the legion's last pay, in Ardin of the old striking, still legal tender and worth as much as any Ardin. The banner is in the rack, rotted but legible. The _Lusar_'s tablets of record lie beside the chest, and they show that the cohort held its post and was never recalled.

**NPCs and threats.** The Kept, until the watch ends. After it ends, nobody.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ take the pay-chest and leave. The silver is a few hundred Ardin, a great sum for any hero.
- _Stealth:_ leave the silver and take the tablets. The tablets are worth more to the _felzos_'s house than the chest is to a thief.
- _Guile:_ sell the banner to the collector and the tablets to the _felzos_'s clerk, and bring the arrears claim to the family that owns it. The claim pays out over years.
- _Parley:_ give the Kept their last request, which is that someone carry the _Lusar_'s tablets to the _felzos_'s house and read them aloud. The reading is the cohort's discharge.

**Clues and rewards.** The pay-chest, the banner, the tablets, the sergeant's sword. The revelation is the Giver's: it did not hold the cohort. The _Lusar_ demanded and swore, the Giver took the oath exactly, and the door stood open the whole time. Nobody had asked it for anything but a roof.

**Consequences.** What the party does with the tablets decides whether the cohort's name is cleared. The _felzos_'s house pays the arrears to the descendants and an honorarium to the party, in Ardin, and files the cohort as having died at its post.

**If bypassed.** If the party leaves the watch unrelieved, the chest stays bolted, and the Kept go on.

### Aftermath

- **If the Lusar asked,** the watch ends. The hill is quiet, the fire goes out, and the drovers drive their flocks through the fold.
- **If a party member took command,** the hall has a new _Lusar_ who cannot leave. The Kept obey, and a rescue is a later story.
- **If the party relieved the watch by seal,** the Kept march out under the standard and die in the dawn on the slope. The _felzos_'s house acknowledges the relief and pays the party for it.
- **If the party robbed the chest,** the fire burns on, and the drovers tell the story of the thieves who ate.
- **The tablets** reach Péravel either way: someone carries them, or someone sells them.
