A lake of black ice north of the refugee fortress does not thaw, and the dead who freeze on it stand up again. Under the ice stands a hidden hof of the corpse-goddess, kept by people who came to Draskborg for shelter and found a gentler master. The party crosses the ice to close the place where the Eye of the Void leaks into the world, and finds that closing it costs more than a fire.

## Overview

[[place-dvilgvatn|Dvilgvatn]] lies a day north of [[place-driskborg|Draskborg]], up the river and beyond the last burned farm. The ice at its center is black and will not melt. Under it, a hidden hof of [[affiliation-nahild|Náhild]] stands with its roof-ridge a man's height below the surface, kept by [[affiliation-nalok|Nálok]] under a **Dróttstjóri**, **Gnuldrthýra**, who was a Targud bóndi's wife before the burning of her farm. The lake is a seep of [[lore-ginnauga|the Eye of the Void]]: [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] comes through it in a cold that does not warm, and the dead who freeze there rise as **Ginnbörn**. Nálok cuts shards of the black ice to set in howes elsewhere in the north.

Draskborg's wardens see the dark from the tower. The fortress is full of people who have lost everything, and some of them walk north in the night.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Draskborg gate-warden pays a thief to bring back a woman who walked out of the fortress with a child, and who is said to have taken the fortress's seal-ring with her. The ring is not why he wants her back.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The hirdman who keeps the tower wants a patrol sent up the river to learn what is wrong with the north. He has no men to spare, and he will pay hirdman's rates to anyone who goes.
- **The leader.** A hersvald whose steads on the river lost their herders to the north offers the party the muster of his bóndi for a winter's work, and asks it to come back with answers.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer's lost kin, thought dead on the burned ground, are seen standing at the lake's edge in a dream, looking up. The dream repeats until the wanderer goes.

## Scene One: The Black Ice

**Purpose.** Show the lake's danger, and the safe way across.

**Situation.** The grey ice at the shore is safe. Past a ring of bone-cairns it turns black, and the black is cold in a way the weather does not explain. Stars are few in the sky over it. A track of pale ice leads toward the center, marked by cairns of knucklebones.

**NPCs and threats.** The ice is the guardian. It takes the heat out of a person who walks on the black, a little at a time, so that the cold arrives as a kind of peace. The **Ginnbörn** stand on the ice at intervals, their limbs ice, their faces still those of the people they were. They do not hunt. They stand until a living thing comes within reach and then they move, fast.

**Player choices.** Force: cut a path across the black on a sledge, which is fast and cold. Stealth: follow the bone-cairn track at night, where the Ginnbörn stand with their backs to the shore. Guile: tie a rope to the shore and cross in turns, so that no one is alone on the ice. Parley: speak to a Ginnbörn by the name it had, which sometimes slows it.

**Clues and rewards.** The bone-cairns are Nálok's marks. A person who looks closely finds a lamp-hook on each, where a servant of the hof hangs a lantern to find the way.

**Consequences.** A party that steps on the black loses warmth and strength. A party that stays on the pale track keeps both.

**If bypassed.** A party that comes up the frozen river channel instead meets the hof's servants at the camp before the ice.

## Scene Two: The Offerers' Camp

**Purpose.** Meet the people who serve the hof, and see why they do.

**Situation.** On a stone shoal at the lake's edge stands a camp of low turf huts. A dozen people live there: bóndi and thralls from the burned east, a few Draskborg runaways, a hunter with a frostbitten hand. They carry out the hof's work in a dull, sad silence and share a kettle. They are **Her Offerers**, and the hidden hof has asked nothing of them yet but labor.

**NPCs and threats.** **Braldvir**, the camp's elder, is a bóndi who lost his farm and sons to the Grukar and was turned away from the fortress for want of room. He is polite, tired, and unafraid. He does not know what the hof does with the shards. The Dróttmadr who runs the camp, **Anthynda**, is a lean woman who watches the party and does not speak. The hunter has the beginnings of the black in his hand.

**Player choices.** Force: seize the camp, which the people fight with whatever they hold. Stealth: pass the huts at night while they sleep. Guile: pose as new offerers who have lost their farms, which is nearly true. Parley: tell Braldvir the truth about the shards, and the Ginnbörn, and what happens to the dead at the lake.

**Clues and rewards.** Braldvir knows the way to the hof and the cut in the ice where the stair begins. If he is told the truth about the shards, he guides the party. The hunter's hand shows what the lake does to a living person.

**Consequences.** A party that kills the camp makes enemies of every refugee at Draskborg who had kin there. A party that turns Braldvir has a guide, and a camp that scatters.

**If bypassed.** A party that skirts the camp finds the stair by the Ginnbörn's trodden track, which is easier to follow than it ought to be.

## Scene Three: The Looking-Ice

**Purpose.** A trial of the hof, and the lake's gaze.

**Situation.** The stair into the hof is cut through clear ice, and the ice under it is as clear as glass. Each person who goes down sees the sky below their feet and a face in it: the face of someone they have lost, looking up. The dread and hopelessness the Eye of the Void sends into the world is strongest here. The lamp the party carries gutters.

**NPCs and threats.** The ice itself is the threat. A person who stops on the stair and answers the face finds their breath freezing on the step, and the step holds them. The faces speak without sound.

**Player choices.** Force: go down without looking and without stopping. Stealth: cover the face of each person on the stair with a cloth. Guile: keep a lamp burning and speak aloud the names of the living, which is the Jól custom against the dark and works here too. Parley: speak to the face, tell it the truth, and ask it to let the person go.

**Clues and rewards.** The names of the living, spoken in a steady voice, keep the ice from taking hold. A lamp lit in the doorway of the hof will not go out. A person who is held on the stair can be freed by someone who speaks their living name.

**Consequences.** A party that falters on the stair leaves one of its number in the ice until the climax, or after.

**If bypassed.** A party that comes into the hof by the Ginnbörn's tunnel from the side meets the climax with the stair behind it unfaced.

## Scene Four: The Hof of Black Glass

**Purpose.** The confrontation with Nálok and the leak itself.

**Situation.** The hof is a long hall of ship-timber and black glass. At its far end is an altar cut from a single block of the black ice, and in the floor before the altar is a round of dark that is not the floor. The cold coming off it is the cold of nothing. **Gnuldrthýra** kneels at the altar, chipping shards from the block with a bone chisel. Ginnbörn line the walls.

**NPCs and threats.** **Gnuldrthýra** is a woman in her forties with a grey streak in her hair and black eyes, and she serves the goddess with a refugee's gratitude: Náhild gave her a place when none was given. She believes in the quiet that Náhild holds out to the dead. The Ginnbörn move when she lifts a hand. The round of dark is the leak, and it draws on the living as the party stands near it.

**Player choices.** Force: break the altar, which closes the seep for a season and sends the Shadow lashing through the hall. Stealth: slip the bone case of finished shards from the altar's foot and carry it out. Guile: convince Gnuldrthýra that her Order is feeding something that will eat the hof, which is true. Parley: tell her that the fortress would have kept her husband's name, and ask her to stop.

**Clues and rewards.** The altar is the block the leak feeds. Without the altar, the dark has nothing to gather around and thins, but it does not close. The Ginnbörn are ice to the bone, and a burning brand makes them give ground. The shards of the bone case are Nálok's seed for other howes.

**Consequences.** A party that breaks the altar frees the hof from its master but not the lake from the Eye. A party that kills Gnuldrthýra leaves the Ginnbörn masterless, and masterless Ginnbörn walk. A party that persuades her loses her to the lake, because she will not leave the dark.

**If bypassed.** A party that takes the shards and leaves sees the hof's lamps go out behind it, and the black spreads a hand's breadth in a night.

## Scene Five: The Cache and the Choice

**Purpose.** Pay the reward and name the cost.

**Situation.** Behind the altar is a store-room cut into the ice. It holds the possessions of the lake's dead: frozen purses, cloaks, weapons and a child's wooden horse. Beneath them lies a roll of hazel tally-sticks, one for each howe a shard has been set in.

**NPCs and threats.** If Gnuldrthýra lives, she follows the party and begs it to leave the cache, because the dead of the lake are hers to keep.

**Player choices.** Force: carry the goods out on a sledge. Stealth: take only the tally-sticks. Guile: sort the goods and return them to the families who lost them. Parley: bring the goods to Draskborg and ask the hirdman what is to be done.

**Clues and rewards.** The goods are modest: hack-silver, a few Vylarian Argenti, a chain of amber, the swords of dead raiders. The tally-sticks name the howes where shards are set, among them the fell of [[place-unthmylsfell|Unthmýlsfell]]. Among the Ginnbörn at the door stands a woman in a Draskborg cloak, and the child's wooden horse in the cache is hers.

**Consequences.** The shards in the howes are Nálok's work, and the lake is the Eye's. Closing the hof does not close the seep, and a ring of Draskborg's wardens must be set to watch the north.

**If bypassed.** A party that flees without the cache hears of the tally-sticks from the hirdman, when a patrol finds the hof open and empty.

## Aftermath

- **If the altar was broken and the hof burned.** The leak does not close, but it stops spreading. The wardens of Draskborg set a watch on the north shore and light the beacon toward the lake as well as the river.
- **If Gnuldrthýra was persuaded.** She leaves the lake and walks into Draskborg, where the gate-warden opens the gate for her as he opens it for all of them.
- **If the tally-sticks were taken.** The Raven Guard learn where the shards lie, and the work of cleansing the howes begins.
- **If the party failed.** The black spreads, and Draskborg takes in a new kind of refugee: the ones who walked out of the north with ice in their limbs.
