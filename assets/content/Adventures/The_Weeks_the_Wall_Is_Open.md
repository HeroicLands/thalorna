Every crossing of [[place-sthrnwall|the Southern Wall]] opens for weeks and shuts for months. Everything that must cross the wall, whether caravan, envoy, runaway or packet, crosses in the same few weeks or waits a year, and whoever holds a pass in those weeks holds the year. This frame runs through one open season at [[place-suryagarha|Suryagarha]] and the passes on either side of it. In that season the king of [[affiliation-shikhalaya|Shikhalaya]] means to have his son acclaimed while he lives, an [[affiliation-osketguides|Osket]] guide-mother wants a man dead before the thaw, and the priests' one garrison on the conch-door stands half manned.

## The Situation and the Stakes

**The window.** The caravanserai under Suryagarha fills for two months and stands empty for ten. A pass-token is cheap and the date on it is not, because the clerk at the customs-house dates it as he pleases, and a caravan that crosses late sells to buyers who have already bought. [[place-tamradvar|The Copper-Door]] shuts a week before [[place-suryadvar|the Sun-Door]], and [[place-meghadvar|the Storm-Door]] gives the shortest window of all before the monsoon cloud comes back onto the col.

**The acclamation.** [[being-vkrbrvrjyr|Vikrabir Virajyara]] holds the throne by a single afternoon's vote of the [Martial Council]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}, which acclaimed him over his elder brother [Jagasvar]{.name data-type="being/npc"}. The two councils do not agree who comes after him. The [Priestly Court]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} holds that Jagasvar's son [Kankamar]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is owed the throne. Vikrabir wants his own son, the **Yuvarava**, acclaimed in his lifetime, which the kingdom has never done. He needs the far chieftains at Suryagarha, and they can reach it only in the open weeks. The Ganoka's hour for the acclamation falls in the closed season by the computed year and a day into the open season by the sighted one.

**The sixth road.** [[place-gudesroad|The Guides' Road]] is on no register, and the crown's formal position is that it does not exist. [[being-nshng|Nushang]] of [[place-tsokhar|Tsokhar]] showed it to a lowlander for gold enough to restock her hearth's herd twice. If he walks it, sells it or takes anyone over it before the window opens, it is known below the shrines, her hearth is put out of the Osket, and the road stops being the Osket's.

**The rock.** The smithing clans of [[place-slvrgorges|the Silver Gorges]] lease their ore to [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective]] by a right older than [[lore-passcharter|the Charter of the Pass-Road]], and the crown collects on the road and not on the rock. The Collective's foreman has warned its overseer four times in writing that the new faces below the water-line are unsafe, and the men below them are bonded.

**The fort.** [[place-sangrafort|The Sangra-fort]] on [[place-shankhadvr|Shankhadvar]] is the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|janpodas']] only standing garrison, kept since [[lore-conchdoor|the Conch-Door Incursion]]. This year the janpoda whose turn it is has sent half its men. Shikhalaya calls the fort an intrusion at every [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"}, and a fort of thirty is the best argument it has had.

The stakes are a reign's succession, a road that pays more by the pound than anything that crosses at Suryadvar, and whether the wall's oldest arrangements hold for one more season.

## Who Moves It

[[being-vkrbrvrjyr|Vikrabir Virajyara]], Maharava of Shikhalaya
: Patient, dry and hard to surprise. He wants an heir acclaimed in his lifetime, so that no council can undo his own acclamation with the next.

[[being-asvrshmbtn|Asvari Shambatan]], senior clan-warden of Suryadvar
: A Samonta with a bench on the Martial Council. Her vote is wanted, and she wants the warden's fee kept in her clan's hand and out of the crown's.

[[being-ishbdptkshn|Ishobi Diptakshana]], Ganoka of Shikhalaya
: She has reckoned the hour for the acclamation by both years and set them side by side. A court that crowns on the wrong day wants to know whose reckoning it was.

[[being-viduliyadan|Viduliya Danasur]], high priestess of Jnanasura at Suryagarha
: She wants her temple's east window glazed, and a gift from either council would pay for it. Each council knows what a gift buys in an acclamation year.

[[being-nshng|Nushang]], guide-mother of the hearth of Shomber
: She sold the sixth road and knows exactly what follows if it is found out. She wants the man she sold it to dead before the window opens.

[[being-ubrn|Ubren]], a carrier of the hearth of Randug
: The pass-guide caravan masters ask for by name. He has never been given the road, and the thaw has brought a frame with a Collective seal out of the ice on the col he carried on.

[[being-krvlydbr|Krivalaya Adibar]], bow-merchant of Danurkot
: He went over the sixth road once, hooded, and has offered a year's profit three times to send bows the same way. The shrines have begun asking which guide took him.

[[being-tarvikatamrak|Tarvika Tamraksha]], the Collective's overseer in the Gorges
: She holds the four letters and the lease. The lease is the clans' and the danger is the Collective's, and she does not know which of them answers for the men below.

[[being-dlzhvrstn|Delzh Vrestun]], Turn-keeper of the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]]
: She suspects one hearth has taken Shikhalayan silver into its winter store, against a draw the council has not made. The Hvarn supply the Sangra-fort.

The bodies
: The Martial Council and the Priestly Court, forty paces apart at Suryagarha; the customs-house and its clerks; [[place-pssshrines|the Pass-Shrines]], whose register is not a customs document; the Osket lineages and the Hvarn hearths; the smithing clans; the Sangra-fort's commander in the first of his three years; and the [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]] quarter, which hears what happens beyond the wall months before the south does.

## Ways In

1. **On the road.** A caravan master's pass-token is dated for the last week of the season, and he hires the party to get the date moved at the customs-house without his name on the request. The clerk who can move it has not named a price, and every clerk is watched in an acclamation year.
2. **Up the mountain.** Nushang feeds every stranger who reaches her door and watches every one of them eat. She chooses the party because they are not of the blood, and sends them below the shrines with a name that is not the man's, a face and a town.
3. **In the citadel.** Ishobi needs this year's sighting brought down from [[place-surtatempl|the Surta temple]] before the councils sit. Both councils want to know what it says before she does.
4. **On the conch-door.** The Sangra-fort's commander asks the party to go down into the Bharava circuit and bring up the rest of his garrison before the window closes, and to say nothing at Suryagarha about how many men they found there.

## If the Party Does Nothing

- Vikrabir calls both councils in the open weeks. The Priestly Court refuses to sit for a living king's successor, and the Martial Council acclaims the Yuvarava without it. Shikhalaya has an heir acclaimed by one council and refused by the other, and Kankamar's household begins to count the far chieftains who stayed home.
- The man Nushang showed the road takes his first party over it at the thaw. By the time the western doors open, every lineage knows. The hearth of Shomber is put out of the Osket, and the sixth road has a second keeper below the wall.
- A new face in the Gorges falls in during the open weeks. The clans say the Collective worked it and the Collective says the clans leased it, and the crown uses the dead to press its claim to the rock.
- The Sangra-fort goes through the season at thirty men, and Shikhalaya has its argument ready for the next Mahva-Melka.

## The Secret

The man Nushang showed the road was paid by the **Koshadyoksha** of Shikhalaya, the minister who answers for the kingdom's revenue. The crown cannot tax a road it says does not exist, and the Koshadyoksha means to hand whoever reigns next a road already learned: its stages, its shelves and the guides who walk it. The man lodges at Suryagarha under the name he gave Nushang and works as a clerk in the customs-house, among the men who date the tokens.

Vikrabir does not know. He has kept the crown's position on the sixth road as the three reigns before him did, and he needs the clan-chieftains, who would read a crown that holds the Guides' Road as a crown reaching for the whole wall. A party that learns the truth has three people to tell, and each telling ends differently. Nushang settles it in the Osket way. Vikrabir must choose between his acclamation and a road worth more than Suryadvar. The Martial Council hears it as proof that the crown means to take everything the wardens hold.
