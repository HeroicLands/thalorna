## Overview {#overview}

[[place-fndr|The Fen Door]] is a cleft in a mound of peat and stone deep in [Olvalonke]{.name data-type="place/feature"}, the wet wood behind the [[place-lisere|Lísere]] shore, and on dark nights fell things come through it. The [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] keep it with the oldest form of their keeping: two sit by the cleft through every night with spear and dog and a banked fire. The watch is one Warden now. Her partner was killed at the cleft at midwinter, and no relief can come down from the mountains until the passes open.

An [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Order]] scholar, [[being-ilsevandsrdl|Ílsevand Sérdalnde]], has heard at Kalipsa that the Door is thinly kept. He hires guards on the Lísere coast and means to camp at the mound for a fortnight of dark nights and watch what comes through, and the contract has the guards keep him safe from the Wardens first. The party is hired. [[place-harnasel|Harnasel]], the last beacon village before the road leaves Lísere, is where the guards buy bread and ask the way.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The Door.** What came through at midwinter is still on this side. It killed the Warden who went at it alone with fire and the wrong song, and it did not go back. It is a long, low thing, black and wet like the bog, with too many joints and no face, and it moves under the moss as a man moves through water. By day it lies in the black water of Olvalonke. On dark nights it hunts along the edge of the forest toward the shore, and it does not come into the light of a beacon. Harnasel has lost a dog and a child's pony, and has said it was wolves.

**The watch.** The Warden, [[being-keldiradhar|Kéldira]] of the [Dhárgandi]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, cannot hunt the thing and keep the watch at once, and keeping the watch comes first. While she sits by the cleft, nothing else comes through. If the watch is left for a night, something else does.

**The scholar.** Ílsevand holds that the mound's cold is bog vapor rising through a fault, and that what the coast sees on dark nights is marsh light. His guards' task is to keep the Warden away from the mound while he watches. If they do, the Door is unwatched.

**The village.** [[being-solmerekalme|Sólmere Kálmereth]], who keeps Harnasel's beacon, knows what the village owes the watch and what the Order's guards are asking. She takes hands for the Door from anyone but the Order's. Half the village would send word up the Wardens' path that the Order is coming. The other half remember that the path is forbidden to every stranger and that the trade with the watch holds only while the village stays out of the Wardens' business.

**The coast.** Smugglers of the Kalipsa trade use the forest's tracks by night and do not want company. Varokh war-parties land on the shore and some come inland. A party that spends a fortnight of dark nights near the Door meets one or the other.

**The law.** An Araelwen is forbidden to every outsider. Warden leave never takes a stranger to the mound. A party that comes to help the watch does so on the Warden's invitation, at the place she names, and not before.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar's guards.** Ílsevand hires the party at the Lísere coast for a fortnight of dark nights.
- **The lone watch.** Sólmere carries Kéldira's word to any party that asks and is not the Order's: the watch needs hands for the dark nights.
- **The fallen Warden.** The Warden killed at midwinter lies under a cairn on the dry ground, and his kin in the mountains want his spear carried up to them before the thaw.
- **The forest edge.** Harnasel's charcoal-burners want someone to sit a night at the end of the Wardens' path with a lantern and see what the wolves are.

## The Opening {#opening}

The yard behind the bake-house at Harnasel, at the hour the first loaves come out. Ílsevand's quartermaster counts out the month's bread for the guards and asks for it to be delivered to the end of the path.

> "A fortnight," the quartermaster says. "Bread to the path and the scholar to the mound. The Warden is a courtesy, not a hindrance."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Quay and the Contract

**Purpose.** The hire, and what the contract does not say.

**Situation.** [[place-sarmelis|Sarmelis]] or [[place-hendrel|Hendrel]], at an inn on the Coast Road. Ílsevand lays out a fortnight of pay and a list of supplies.

**NPCs and threats.** Ílsevand, tidy and polite. His quartermaster, who knows the way. A Kalipsan captain at the outer quay who sells and buys without looking up.

**Player choices.** Take the contract, ask what the scholar is watching for, or ask the beacon families what the Door is.

**Clues and rewards.** The beacon families say the Door is a place to keep out of and a duty to feed. A fisherman says the mist over Olvalonke holds a low slow light on still nights, and the beacon is kept lit to answer it.

**Consequences.** A party that asks gets told the Door is a sacred place and that the Wardens forbid every stranger the path.

**If bypassed.** The quartermaster hires sellswords who ask nothing.

### Scene Two: Harnasel

**Purpose.** The village that feeds the watch.

**Situation.** Harnasel, in its two halves: boat-houses and net-lofts under the point, and the bake-house, cooper's yard and charcoal row a quarter mile inland, joined by one cart-track.

**NPCs and threats.** Sólmere, broad and slow-spoken. The charcoal-burners, who have lost a dog and a pony. The bakers, who sell the Order bread and watch where it goes.

**Player choices.** Buy the bread, ask the way, tell Sólmere the contract's terms, or ask her for the Warden's word.

**Clues and rewards.** Sólmere does not point to the path. If the party says what the contract says, she carries the Warden's list to them: the watch needs hands for the dark nights, and she cannot offer a bed, a fire or an explanation.

**Consequences.** A village that learns the party is the Order's keeps its doors shut. A village that learns the party will help the Warden holds its tongue.

**If bypassed.** The path is found by the charcoal-burners' dogs, and the Order's guards go in with the village's eyes on their backs.

### Scene Three: The Turf Hut

**Purpose.** The Warden, the watch and the contract's cost.

**Situation.** The dry ground among the alders: a turf hut, a woodpile and a ring of stones round a fire that is never let go out. A mound, half grown with black moss, and a cleft between the roots of two black alders.

**NPCs and threats.** Kéldira, thin and wary, with two dogs. The cold of the mound, which smokes breath in summer. The bones a little way off that have been burned and have not burned.

**Player choices.** Keep her from the mound as the contract says, sit the watch with her, warn her, or lead Ílsevand to the dry ground.

**Clues and rewards.** Kéldira tells the party what the watch does and why it is never pulled. She names the thing that killed her partner and says it did not go back. She cannot say where it is.

**Consequences.** A party that keeps her from the mound leaves the Door open to whatever comes next.

**If bypassed.** Ílsevand's guards pen her in the hut. The dogs are poisoned.

### Scene Four: The Dark Night

**Purpose.** The thing in the water, the door and the choice.

**Situation.** The edge of Olvalonke at the moonless dark. The beacon is lit, and the hunting thing comes to the edge of its light.

**NPCs and threats.** The long black thing, which moves under moss. Kéldira, who cannot leave the cleft. Ílsevand, who wants to watch. Smugglers or a Varokh scout, who complicate the shore.

> [!NOTE]
> The long black thing is run as a [[being-mrlrkr|Mirelurker]]: it lies submerged by day and strikes from the edge of the water at dark.

**Player choices.** Hunt the thing at the forest edge, hold the beacon, keep the watch for Kéldira so she can hunt, or protect the scholar.

**Clues and rewards.** The thing does not come into the beacon's light. Fire and the wrong song killed a Warden. Kéldira knows the right song and can sing it only if she is not alone.

**Consequences.** A party that keeps the Door while Kéldira hunts the thing has done what the watch cannot, and made an enemy of the Order's contract.

> [!WARNING]
> While Kéldira sits at the cleft nothing else comes through. If the watch is left for a night, something does. A party that hunts the thing and leaves the cleft empty has traded one fell thing for another.

**If bypassed.** The thing takes a guard at the edge of the forest. Ílsevand writes that it was a bog light.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Ílsevand's guards keep Kéldira from the mound for six nights. On the sixth something else comes through the cleft, and the thing in the water takes two guards at the forest edge. Harnasel keeps its beacons lit and its doors shut. Kéldira is found at dawn at the mouth of the cleft, alive, with a broken spear. Ílsevand writes that the cold of the mound is a bog vapor.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The Door is kept, or it is not. The thing in the water is dead, or it is still on the coast. The village bakes bread for the watch or for the Order's quartermaster, and not for both. The Wardens count whoever kept the scholar from the mound as an enemy of the keeping, and whoever kept the Door as a friend.
