## Overview {#overview}

The city’s carts return to the spring-house of Vrellnes on [[place-calypsashore|The South Shore]]. Its elder, [[being-hvurnvrthm|Hvurnrinna Vrathumak]], keeps the key. [[being-krildtvlgr|Krildthira Tvulgaruk]] would rather send strangers who can talk than send a squad. The party arrives between a practical need for water and a claim of ownership neither side accepts from the other.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

Autumn rain replenishes the springs, but it does not settle the dry summer’s dispute. The village holds its spring by custom; the city claims the island’s springs as the island’s government. The shore has no Council seat. Neither claim is proved simply by bringing more guards.

Hvurnrinna also hides half the season’s dye in a cellar beneath the spring-house. A city factor has paid for it and has been told it went over the ridge. She means to sell it again to a higher-paying Provénzian buyer at Dvonvík in spring. This is a real wrong. It does not establish the city’s ownership of the spring or cancel the village’s need for water.

[[being-brildmdvrn|Brildmil Dvurnumak]] sells his village’s water and buys winter grain with the proceeds. [[being-thruldsklf|Thruldmil Skulfendik]] is ready to discuss a price and wants a city market for his oil. Neither speaks for Vrellnes. [[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]] has not instructed the watch to force the door.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Krildthira asks the party to escort the returning carts and report what an agreement would take.
- Hvurnrinna asks outside witnesses to hear the village elders state their customary ownership before the watch acts.
- Travelers carrying a complaint about the missing dye can investigate it alongside the water dispute, provided they say whose claim they carry.

## The Opening {#opening}

> Empty casks stand in a cart beside the vats. The elder holds up a key dark with years of handling. Behind her, the door stays shut. She asks whether the strangers have come to buy water or to take a spring.

The carters have money for water at the city’s price. They have no order transferring the spring. Hvurnrinna has the village’s support and the key; she has no power to speak for every cove.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### State the commission

Before the carts reach the door, Krildthira makes the party’s authority explicit. It may escort, negotiate and report. It may not pretend that an undecided complaint is a warrant to seize a spring. A party hired by Hvurnrinna has a different duty: witness the claims faithfully, without promising to settle them by force.

**Choices and evidence.** Ask for the exact terms of any purchase, demand authority for a search, or decline a commission whose limits the employer will not state. The complaint and the lack of further instructions can be checked with Hrundselda’s office.

**If bypassed.** The question is asked at the door. A false assertion of authority may get a cask filled today and create a charge against the party when the office hears it.

### Hear the shore without making it one voice

At Dvonvík, Brildmil shows what the water payment buys. At Vrellnes, Hvurnrinna shows the vats that need the spring. Thruldmil treats a sale as a bargain to negotiate, not a surrender of the shore. Hold the meeting at the quay or the spring-house; each elder answers only for the village that chooses them.

**Choices and clues.** Compare a one-time sale, a seasonal quantity and a claim of continuing access. The elders can agree on a witnessed transaction while disagreeing about ownership. A party that writes a promise should read its exact words aloud before anyone accepts it.

**If bypassed.** Hvurnrinna can still bargain for Vrellnes, but cannot bind the other villages. Brildmil resists any announcement that his own sale was a mistake made on everyone’s behalf.

### What is below the floor

A request to inspect the spring leads past the cellar steps. Dye jars stand below, sealed and marked for the season. A willing inspection can reveal them; a forced entry reveals them too and begins a separate dispute over the entry. Copies of the paid order and the village’s delivery claim identify why they matter.

**Choices and consequences.** Confront Hvurnrinna privately, preserve the seals and have witnesses count the jars, or use the discovery to blackmail her into opening the spring. She admits the higher offer when shown the evidence. She argues that the village needs the money; that argument does not deliver the goods already sold.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Keep both questions on the table. Exposing the dye fraud does not prove the water belongs to the city. Defending the spring does not make the second sale honest.

**If the cellar stays hidden.** The party can settle today’s water purchase without knowing of the jars. The city factor’s complaint remains, and the spring dispute returns when the Provénzian buyer reaches Dvonvík.

### Fill a cask without conceding a village

The next carts wait while the party carries a proposed agreement. A workable narrow settlement names the water to be sold, the price and the priority of household and vat use, and says that the sale does not settle ownership. Hvurnrinna can accept that as a village transaction; Hrundselda can authorize payment while reserving the city’s claim. Neither is compelled to accept the proposal.

**Other choices.** The party can escort empty carts home with the evidence; help the village hold the door; or force it. Force obtains water only while force remains. A permanent title judgment or a new Council seat requires the city’s institutions and cannot be granted by the party’s wording.

**If talks fail.** Krildthira receives a report. She can keep her watch at Dvonvík and seek an instruction, leaving room for a later hearing. The immediate price is a lost cart journey and continued hostility, not automatic slaughter.

### Account for the dye

The jars can go to the buyer already owed them, be held for a witnessed reckoning, or disappear into another sale. Returning them leaves Hvurnrinna answerable to her village for the lost second price. Concealing them binds the party to her deception. A claim over trade can go to the Sea-Law Hall; a charge of theft or violence belongs to the city’s courts.

**Rewards.** The party earns the escort fee or the elders’ hospitality and a useful reputation for distinguishing promises. A practical water bargain keeps the vats working and gets casks over the ridge. It does not make everyone friends.

**If bypassed.** The dye buyer continues the claim, regardless of which side gets water. The party’s statements about what it saw become evidence in that later dispute.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The carts return empty, Hvurnrinna presses the other villages to lock their doors, and the watch receives another complaint. The hidden jars remain available for the promised spring sale, turning a dispute over water into a dispute over goods as well.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A limited purchase can hold through one dry season without settling the larger claim. A forced door makes future purchases require escorts. An exposed fraud can remove the elder’s freedom to bargain while leaving the village’s customary case intact. Let the villagers answer for their choice of elder; outsiders do not acquire that office by finding the jars.
