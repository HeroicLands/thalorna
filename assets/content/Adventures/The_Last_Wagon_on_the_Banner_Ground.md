## The Last Wagon on the Banner Ground

A small caravan carrying cut gems and a merchant's strongbox leaves [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] for the wells of the west, with the party as its escort. Its guide takes it off the road by a herders' track, a wheel breaks, and the guide rides away. The wagons are standing on [[place-lanthusthli|Lanthusthalī]], the cursed field of the march, with the sun on the hills. The adventure runs in five scenes, and the last adversary is the field itself. Each scene can be met with force, stealth, talk, cleverness with the ground or a sacrifice, and each states how play carries on if the party skips it, fails it or cuts it short.

Read [[place-lanthusthli|Lanthusthalī]] and [[lore-bloodfield|Blood-fields]] first. The adventure takes the field as canon gives it: a thin _shorukshetra_ of the dread kind, whose panic comes on anyone who lingers, and across which the hunters of the unreceived dead come in a form half made of the place, seen close to, able to take hold of a man and kill him. Every Vedyari on the march knows the saying: _cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls._

### The Truth Behind the Field

**The racket.** The company that holds the Ashvapada stage is a season in arrears, and its captain, **Krthirāya**, collects what he is owed from the traffic. He sells a caravan its escort, recommends a guide, and the guide, **Kārasha**, takes the wagons off the road short of the eastern chain post, by a herders' track he says goes round the field's northern side and saves half a day. The track runs onto the field instead, and across it toward the road. Just short of the southern stones it crosses a stony gully that breaks wheels. Kārasha times the crossing so that the wheel breaks near sunset, and rides out by the way he came, which by daylight he can do.

**The false line.** The march marks the field's edge with a ring of whitewashed stones. On the southern side, where a caravan flees toward the road, Krthirāya's men have lifted a long stretch of the stones and set them again well inside the ring. The real edge runs on beyond, through a cleft in a rock spur. A caravan that runs for the stones stops at the false line, thinks itself safe and dies there in the night. At first light the captain's men come in at a trot with their mules blindfolded, take the goods and leave the dead where the march expects to find them, at the edge of the stones.

**What remains unexplained.** What crosses on Lanthusthalī after dark is not the captain's. He uses it, as the companies use it against raiders, and he does not understand it. Neither does the adventure. The hunters stay away from a burning line of grass for as long as it burns, and gather round any pyre lit on the field, and nobody on the march can say why.

### The Hook

**Sundārka**, a gem merchant of the river country, is taking a small caravan west to the Dunhari oases: three wagons, his drivers, two guards of his own and a strongbox chained to the bed of the lead wagon. He hires the party at Ashvapada to ride with it, because the company escort he paid for is only a guide and a promise that the company's riders patrol the stage. The guide is Kārasha, a lean, cheerful man with a good horse, who knows a way round that saves half a day and brings the wagons to the chain post before sunset.

Other entries suit other heroes:

- **The investigator.** The court at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandhyāpur]] has heard that a company captain drives caravans onto the field at dusk. Its witnesses are dead, and it needs someone to ride a caravan through the stage and see.
- **The debtor.** A character owes Sundārka money, and riding his escort settles the debt.
- **The kinsman.** A character's brother rode as a driver for **Pāllivata**, a cloth factor whose caravan vanished on this stage last dry season.

### What the Party Hears First

Read or paraphrase this as the wheel goes:

> The lead wagon lurches in the gully, and the wheel breaks with a sound like a bone. The wagon sags beside a whitewashed stone. Kārasha looks back once at the broken wheel, then turns his horse and rides away north at a canter, and he does not look back again. The sun is touching the hills of the Descent.
>
> Then everyone knows it at once, the drivers, the guards, the merchant, all of you: horsemen are gathering in the hills behind, a great many of them, and the line has gone, and there is nowhere to run. You turn and look. The slopes are empty, gold in the last light. The certainty does not go away.

## Scene One: The Broken Wheel

**Purpose.** To fix the practical problem, the isolation and the first fear, while the party can still believe there is a simple way out.

**Situation.** The wagons stand in a shallow gully on stony ground. A single whitewashed stone stands beside the lead wagon, and a line of others runs away east and west across the slope. Kārasha said the road was just over the rise beyond them. The draft oxen are bawling and straining at their traces. Far off to the east, on the road where it bends south round the field, the chain post is a small square of wall with a fire in front of it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The panic.** It comes on everyone who lingers, the party included, and it feels like one's own fear. It makes people want to run, alone, in any direction, and run now.
- **The animals.** The oxen bolt in the first minutes unless they are blindfolded or cut loose. A team that bolts drags its wagon over in the gully.
- **Dhūlaka.** The lead wagon's driver, an old hand, pinned when the wagon lurched. His leg is broken below the knee. He cannot walk and he knows it. He knows the field by reputation, and the saying, and he says it aloud.
- **Sundārka.** A soft-spoken, stubborn man. His strongbox is chained to the wagon bed with a lock to which only he has the key, and he will not leave it.
- **Vijesa.** A young company soldier at the chain post, too far away to be heard more than in pieces. He shouts and waves: leave the wagons, run. The wind takes most of it. One phrase carries clearly: _not those stones_.

**Player choices.**

- _Run now._ Abandon the wagons, carry Dhūlaka and cross the line of white stones beside them. The panic makes every step feel like the right one. On the far side of the stones it does not lift.
- _Fix the wheel._ There is a spare spoke in no wagon, and a wheel takes time the sun does not leave.
- _Save the animals._ Blindfold the oxen and they stand. Cut them loose and they are gone into the dusk, and the wagons stay where they are.
- _Signal the post._ A fire, a mirror in the last light, a horn. Vijesa sees it. He does not cross the stones after dark. Nobody in the companies does.
- _Persuade Sundārka._ He can be talked into leaving the box, forced to leave it, or helped to carry it. It takes two strong people.

**Clues and rewards.** Vijesa's warning. The panic, which stays with anyone who steps over the stones. The white stone beside the wagon is freshly whitewashed and stands loose on the surface, with no bedding round its foot. Older stones further along are crusted with lichen and sunk into the ground. A character who looks for it sees that Kārasha's horse left the track exactly where the gully begins, as if he knew where to stop.

**Consequences.** Whoever bolts alone is the first to meet the dark. Loose oxen are found later, in the night, in a way that tells the party something is out there with them.

**If bypassed.** A party that runs at once crosses the stones, climbs the rise and finds no road beyond it, only more of the field going down into the dusk, and the shape of the shelter on the next rise. Whichever way it runs, the wagons are the one thing on the slope that does not move, and the dark comes on before any other landmark. A party that goes back for the wagons meets Scene Two there. A party that does not finds Pāllivata at the foot of the rise instead, where he crawled, still gripping his spoke.

## Scene Two: The Man Under the Wagon

**Purpose.** To discover that this has happened before, to find a route, and to learn the field's rules from someone who died of them.

**Situation.** Dusk. The lead wagon's broken wheel has come down on something in the gully. Under the wagon, wedged among the stones, is a body dried by a year of sun and wind, in the remains of a factor's coat, both hands locked round a broken wheel spoke. The wreck of a second wagon, stripped of everything that could be carried, lies half buried in the gully a few paces on.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The dead man.** Pāllivata, the cloth factor who vanished last dry season. Prying the spoke from his grip is hard. His fingers do not give until they break.
- **The panic.** It is stronger now the light is going. People start to argue in whispers and to look over their shoulders at the slopes.
- **Dhūlaka.** Feverish and lucid by turns. He heard the story of Pāllivata's caravan at Ashvapada. The drivers said nobody came out of it, and that its guide came back alone.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the spoke._ Pāllivata scratched words into the wood with a knife point, in a cramped hand: _Stones lie. Not the near ones. Shelter south, past the dead tree. Roof. See the whole ring from roof. Do not answer. They use our voices._ Below, scratched over and over: _Kārasha_.
- _Use the spoke._ It fits the broken wheel, roughly. A wagon that moves can carry Dhūlaka and the strongbox, slowly, and the wheel holds as far as the shelter.
- _Search the wreck._ The goods are gone. The wagon's harness has been cut, not broken. Someone came in by daylight and took what was worth carrying, and left the dead.
- _Search Sundārka's papers._ His escort receipt from Ashvapada carries the company captain's seal and a note recommending Kārasha by name.
- _Look south._ In the last light one dark, square shape stands on a rise, with a dead tree beside it.

**Clues and rewards.** The spoke. The receipt. The proof that wagons break in this gully on purpose. A character raised on the march recognizes the shape on the rise as a stone shrine older than the field's ruin, and knows it has a flat roof.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the spoke has a moving wagon, a slow one. A party that leaves Pāllivata where he lies may hear him later. Something out there uses his voice, calling for help from the gully, in the dark.

**If bypassed.** If the party never reads the spoke, Dhūlaka tells them the story of the shelter, or the scratched words on the shelter's roof beams in Scene Four say what the spoke said. If the party never reaches the wagon, the receipt in Sundārka's coat and the ledger in the shelter carry the proof of the diversion.

## Scene Three: Dark Between the Wagons

**Purpose.** To bring the threat close, to take someone, and to prove that the white stones nearest the wagons are no protection.

**Situation.** The sun goes down behind the Descent and the dark comes fast. Between the wagons, at the edge of the lamplight, figures start to pass: men on foot, running, in the dress of an old host, helmets gone, looking back over their shoulders. They pass by and are gone, and more pass. Then one does not pass. It stops, at the edge of the light, and turns, and comes closer. Close to, it is wrong: too much of it is the color of the dry ground and the dead grass, and the face is a shape the dark has made out of where a face should be. It reaches into the light.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The hunters.** The predators of the unreceived dead, crossing in a form half made of the field. They come close, they take hold and they drag a person off into the dark. They come thickest round anyone who is bleeding, and Dhūlaka is bleeding. Steel and fire hurt them. Neither hurts them the way it hurts a man.
- **The field's own dead.** The running men, Marupāla's beaten host, worn down to the one thing they still do: running for the road. They do not attack. They draw the eye away from what does.
- **The voices.** Out of the dark come voices the party knows: a companion's voice, a mother's, Pāllivata's, the merchant's, calling a name and asking for help.
- **Senatha.** Sundārka's younger guard, steady until now. He hears his brother's voice calling from the stones, and he answers it, and he goes.

**Player choices.**

- _Hold the wagons._ Circle them, build up the fire and fight from the wagon beds. The hunters come in and go out of the light and take what they can reach.
- _Move for the shelter._ Carry Dhūlaka on the moving wagon or on a litter, roped together, lamps out or lamps lit.
- _Go after Senatha._ His scream comes back across the slope and ends. A party that follows the sound finds him at the line of white stones, laid along it with his face to the road, dead. The stones that should have saved him are around his body.
- _Burn the grass._ The dry grass takes at once in the wind off the Descent. The hunters do not come through a burning line. The fire does not last, and if the wind turns it comes back at the wagons.
- _Do not answer._ A party that has learned the saying, from the spoke, from Dhūlaka or from any Vedyari among them, can hold its tongue and keep its people from answering. Roping people together, singing, counting aloud and holding hands all help.

**Clues and rewards.** The stones beside Senatha are the loose, new ones. Further along the slope, at the edge of the light, the field's own dead keep running south, past the false line, and on.

**Consequences.** Senatha is lost. If his body is left on the field, the hunters gather round it. Every person who answers a voice is in the next scene's danger first. A party that burned the grass has bought time, and drawn Vijesa's attention from the post.

**If bypassed.** A party that stayed at the false line instead of the wagons meets this scene there: the figures come along the line of stones, and the voices call from the wagons behind. If the party never moves at all, the hunters pull the wagons apart round them over the first watch, and the one structure on the field, the shelter on its rise, is all there is left to run to.

## Scene Four: The Roof and the Break

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the field. The party must keep the survivors together, find the true edge, and carry Dhūlaka to it through the hunters.

**Situation.** The shelter is a squat stone shrine on a rise, older than the field's ruin, roofless at one end, its flat stone roof whole at the other. The stumps of banner poles stand in sockets along its parapet. Inside, against the back wall, is a leather satchel. From the roof, under the stars, the whitewashed stones of the ring catch what light there is, and the whole ring can be seen as a pale broken loop on the dark slope. On the southern side the loop has a bite out of it: a long stretch of stones bulging inward, well inside the curve of the rest. Beyond the bite, where the curve would run on, two lines of stones climb to a cleft in a rock spur and stop on either side of it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The field.** The panic is at its worst on the rise. People want to scatter, each certain that their own way out is the only one. The voices are close now, under the parapet. The hunters come up the rise in numbers.
- **The cleft.** The real line runs through a narrow break between two rocks, wide enough for two people side by side. The hunters can get into it, and in the cleft they can hold a person bodily and pull.
- **Dhūlaka.** He has to be carried. He is weakening, and the hunters know it.
- **Sundārka.** He wants the strongbox carried too, and on the roof, with the field in his head, he is ready to fight for it.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the ring._ From the roof, the true line is plain to anyone who looks for the shape rather than the nearest stone. The cleft is the nearest point of it.
- _Search the shelter._ Pāllivata's satchel holds his caravan ledger, with the escort fee paid to Krthirāya's company and Kārasha's hire entered on the captain's word. On the roof beams, scratched with a knife, are the same words as the spoke.
- _Follow the dead._ Marupāla's running men cross the slope below toward the road. They pass the false line without pausing and stop at the true one, and stand there, looking at the road they cannot reach. Following them is true. It also takes the party down among them, where the hunters are.
- _Follow the offerings._ The Khazryn leave cups, knotted cloths and bowls at the stones for their own dead. They are at the true stones and nowhere along the bite.
- _Look for the soldier's fire._ If the party signaled Vijesa in Scene One or burned grass in Scene Three, a fire burns now on the road side of the cleft, where he has carried it against orders and sits by it with a horn. Other lights move on the field too. His is the one that stays still.
- _Fight the retreat._ Down the rise and across the slope, with Dhūlaka carried, the party in a close knot, fire at the back. The hunters press hardest at the cleft, where the rocks give them hold.
- _Burn a line._ Fire set across the slope behind the retreat holds the hunters back for as long as it burns. The wind decides how long that is.
- _Light a pyre._ The hunters gather round a pyre lit on the field, unexplained and unfailing. Burning Senatha's body, or the wagons, or the wagons with Senatha on them, draws them off long enough to reach the cleft. Burning the wagons burns Sundārka's goods, and he knows it.
- _Leave something._ A party can leave the strongbox, or leave Dhūlaka, or leave somebody at the cleft's mouth to hold it while the rest go through. Each is a real choice, and each works.
- _Hold the roof till dawn._ A party with fire, a strong position and nerve can stay on the roof all night. At first light the hunters thin and go, and the panic stays.

**Clues and rewards.** The ledger, which with the receipt makes a case at law. The true line. A character who stands on the far side of the cleft feels the panic lift like a weight taken off the chest.

**Consequences.** Whoever is pulled from the knot in the cleft is lost unless someone goes back for them at once, into the rocks, among the hunters. A party that reaches the road with Dhūlaka alive has beaten the field. Sundārka's gratitude depends on what was burned and what was carried.

**If bypassed or failed.** A party that never reached the shelter, or never read the ring, can still find the cleft by the offerings, by Vijesa's fire or by the running dead. A party that failed at the cleft and fell back holds whatever ground it can until dawn, and walks out through the cleft by daylight with the panic on it and the dead counted. Either way the party reaches Scene Five, at a cost proportionate to what it learned.

## Scene Five: Beyond the White Stones

**Purpose.** The treasure, the proof, and the men who come to collect.

**Situation.** Dawn, on the road side of the line. The field behind is empty, gold and quiet. Vijesa is there if he came, grey with sleeplessness. Sundārka sits on the strongbox, or on nothing, and looks back at the slope. Then, along the herders' track from the north, comes a string of mules at a trot, blindfolded, with four company riders and Kārasha on his good horse, coming to collect.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Kārasha and the collectors.** They expect the dead. They are armed and in a hurry to be off the field before the panic gets into them, and they do not want a fight on it.
- **Sundārka.** Alive and paying. He opens the strongbox and pays generously, in silver and cut gems, and more for any guard or driver the party brought out.
- **Vijesa.** A witness. He saw the guide ride away, and he saw the false line from the post for a season without understanding it.

**Player choices.**

- _Ambush._ The collectors ride onto the field in daylight, slowed by blindfolded mules, and the party knows where they will stop. Kārasha bolts first.
- _Let them go in._ Stay hidden, let them reach the wagons and find nothing they expected, and take them coming out.
- _Take Kārasha alive._ He talks quickly, and he names the captain.
- _Bargain._ The collectors offer a share to keep quiet. The party can take it, refuse it, or take it and testify anyway.
- _Go to the court._ The ledger, the receipt, Vijesa's word and the moved stones themselves, which anyone sent to look can see.

**Clues and rewards.** Sundārka's payment in silver and cut gems. Pāllivata's ledger and Sundārka's receipt, which together are the proof the court at Sandhyāpur lacks. Kārasha, if taken. The road west, or back to Ashvapada.

**Consequences.** The court that hears the case pays for the evidence and wants witnesses who will stand. The captain has a company, and the company is owed.

**If bypassed.** A party that walked out by the road and never met the collectors still has the ledger and the receipt. The court's case is thinner without Kārasha, and the captain has time to have the stones put back.

## Aftermath

- **The captain brought to court.** The march loses a captain and a company goes unpaid for a season, and the crown's officer at Ashvapada has to answer for what he passed. The stones are reset on their old line, and the companies say nothing about it.
- **The captain warned.** The false line is gone within the week. The guide is not seen again on the road, and another one, as cheerful, takes his place.
- **Dhūlaka carried out.** He walks with a stick by the next caravan season and tells the story at every well. Lost, he lies on the field and is not received, and his family asks the Agnī-panthā to burn the ground for him, which the orthodox temples forbid.
- **Senatha.** If his body was burned on the field, his family is told he went with a pyre. If it was left, it is found at the edge of the stones in the morning, as the march expects.
- **Pāllivata.** His family learns where he died. A character who brings his spoke home brings them the last thing he wrote.
