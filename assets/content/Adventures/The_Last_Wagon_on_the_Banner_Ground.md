A small caravan carrying cut gems and a merchant's strongbox leaves [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] for the wells of the west, with the party as its escort. Its guide takes it off the road by a herders' track, a wheel breaks, and the guide rides away. The wagons are standing on [[place-lanthusthli|Lantustali]], the cursed field of the march, with the sun on the hills. Every sign that night says a crooked company has sold the caravan to the field, and it has. So has the man who hired the party: the merchant is the court's man, riding the stage as bait to take the company's collectors with the goods in their hands, and the party is the blades he needs alive at dawn.

## Overview

The adventure runs from sunset to the next morning in five scenes, and the last adversary is the field itself. Each scene can be met with force, stealth, talk, cleverness with the ground or a sacrifice, and each states how play carries on if the party skips it, fails it or cuts it short.

Read [[place-lanthusthli|Lantustali]] and [[lore-bloodfield|Blood-fields]] first. The adventure takes the field as canon gives it: a thin _shorukshetra_ of the dread kind, whose panic comes on anyone who lingers, and across which the hunters of the unreceived dead come in a form half made of the place, seen close to, able to take hold of a man and kill him. Every one of the [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]] on the march knows the saying: _cross where the sun can see you, and do not answer anything that calls._

> [!TIP]
> Pace the night by the light. Scene One runs in the last of the sun, Scene Two at dusk, Scene Three in the first dark, Scene Four from midnight to the gray before dawn, and Scene Five at sunrise. Say where the light is at the start of every scene.

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the field—the company's racket, the false line, what crosses after dark, [Pallivata]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s caravan and the court's commission—is in [[place-lanthusthli#truth|the truth of Lantustali]]. In short: [Krtiraya]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s company sells caravans to the field through its guide, [Karasha]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and a stretch of moved stones, and its riders collect the goods at dawn as the field's leavings. [Sundarka]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, whose gems were lost in Pallivata's caravan, carries the court's commission to be caught in the same trap and come out of it alive. He asked for Karasha by name and hired the party because holding the wagons until the collectors come needs blades. He told nobody, and he does not know that the southern stones were moved. This section covers what that does at the table.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The party must reach Scene Three believing that the company betrayed it and nobody else, and that Sundarka is a stubborn, frightened merchant guarding his gems. By the end of Scene Four it must be able to see that he brought it onto the field on purpose. Every act of his has two readings: play each one as the first.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal lands in Scene Three or Scene Four, when the party wants to move, and it reaches the party by whichever route it took.

- **At the wagons.** When the party moves for the shelter or the cleft, Sundarka refuses to leave the wagons, and says why before he can stop himself: they hold here until dawn, because the collectors come at dawn and must find him alive with the box.
- **In his papers.** A party that searches him closely, or takes his coat from him when he is hurt, finds a second paper folded under the receipt and sealed with the court's seal at [[place-sandhyapur|Sandyapur]]: a commission to ride the stage and take whoever comes to collect.
- **At the shelter or the cleft.** [Vijesa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, at his fire, asks the first of the party to reach him whether the court's man is alive.
- **From [Dulaka]{.name data-type="being/npc"}.** Feverish on the litter, he says that he heard Sundarka ask the captain at Ashvapada for Karasha by name, and thought it odd at the time.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on one line, then stop. Let the players go back over the night themselves: the receipt he showed them, the oil, the eyes on the north track, the box he would not leave. Then let them look at Senata.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early, and never deny a right guess. A party that reads "as requested" in Scene Two and confronts Sundarka with it gets the truth: he admits the commission, shows the court's seal and tells them the collectors come at dawn. The party then plans the night with what he knows, and the false line is still there for it to find.

> [!WARNING]
> Before Scene Three, Sundarka never says "dawn", "collectors" or "court". His refusal to leave the box must read as greed, and his eyes on the north track as fury. If he explains himself in Scene One, the reversal is spent before the field has done its work.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party is torn between surviving the night by the cleft and finishing a sting it never agreed to, at the false line where [Senata]{.name data-type="being/npc"} died. Dulaka's broken leg and Senata's death sit on Sundarka's account, and the party knows it. The ways open to it are plain: leave him and the box and run for the cleft; carry him through against his will; name a price, since the court's silver is in the box; or finish the sting on its own terms. The dawn collectors are now a known target, and Scene Five's ambush is planned, not lucky.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that abandons Sundarka on the field closes the court's route to the captain: the collectors find a living merchant with no blades round him, and the court never hears from him. The ledger and the receipt still make a case if the party carries them out. Let the party choose, and let the Aftermath follow from what it knew when it chose.

## Hooks

Sundarka, a gem merchant of the river country, is taking a small caravan west to the oases of the [[lore-dunhariclt|Dunhari]]: three wagons, his drivers, two guards of his own and a strongbox chained to the bed of the lead wagon. He hires the party at Ashvapada to ride with it, because the company escort he paid for is only a guide and a promise that the company's riders patrol the stage. The guide is Karasha, a lean, cheerful man with a good horse, who knows a way round that saves half a day and brings the wagons to the chain post before sunset.

> [!TIP]
> Play Sundarka at Ashvapada as a careful man, not a nervous one. He pays the party well and promptly, he asks how they fight in the dark, and he buys oil.

Other entries suit other heroes:

- **The investigator.** The court at Sandyapur has heard that a company captain drives caravans onto the field at dusk. Its witnesses are dead. It asks a character to ride the stage and see, and to ride with a gem merchant named Sundarka who leaves Ashvapada that week. The court does not say why that caravan.
- **The debtor.** A character owes Sundarka money, and riding his escort settles the debt.
- **The kinsman.** A character's brother rode as a driver for Pallivata, a cloth factor whose caravan vanished on this stage last dry season.

## The Opening

Read or paraphrase this as the wheel goes:

> The lead wagon lurches in the gully, and the wheel breaks with a sound like a bone. The wagon sags beside a whitewashed stone. Karasha looks back once at the broken wheel, then turns his horse and rides away north at a canter, and he does not look back again. The sun is touching the hills of the Descent.
>
> Then everyone knows it at once, the drivers, the guards, the merchant, all of you: horsemen are gathering in the hills behind, a great many of them, and the line has gone, and there is nowhere to run. You turn and look. The slopes are empty, gold in the last light. The certainty does not go away.

## Scene One: The Broken Wheel

**Purpose.** To fix the practical problem, the isolation and the first fear, while the party can still believe there is a simple way out.

> The oxen are bawling and throwing their heads in the traces. Far off to the east, where the road bends south round the field, a small square of wall stands with a fire in front of it, and a figure on the wall is waving both arms.

**Situation.** The wagons stand in a shallow gully on stony ground. A single whitewashed stone stands beside the lead wagon, and a line of others runs away east and west across the slope. Karasha said the road was just over the rise beyond them. Far off to the east, on the road, the chain post shows its fire.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The panic.** It comes on everyone who lingers, the party included, and it feels like one's own fear. It makes people want to run, alone, in any direction, and run now.
- **The animals.** The oxen bolt in the first minutes unless they are blindfolded or cut loose. A team that bolts drags its wagon over in the gully.
- Dulaka. The lead wagon's driver, an old hand, pinned when the wagon lurched. His leg is broken below the knee. He cannot walk and he knows it. He knows the field by reputation and the saying, and he says the saying aloud.
- Sundarka. A soft-spoken, stubborn man. His strongbox is chained to the wagon bed with a lock to which only he has the key, and he will not leave it. While everyone else stares at the hills, he stands on the wagon tongue and looks north, along the track Karasha took, and he does not look away for long. His wagons carry six jars of lamp-oil.
- Vijesa. A young company soldier at the chain post, too far away to be heard more than in pieces. He shouts and waves: leave the wagons, run. The wind takes most of it. Two things carry clearly: the merchant's name, and _not those stones_.

> [!TIP]
> If a player asks how the post knows the merchant's name, Sundarka answers at once, a little sharply: the stage sends word to the posts of every caravan it takes money from. That is true of the stage, and it is not why Vijesa knows.

**Player choices.**

- _Run now._ Abandon the wagons, carry Dulaka and cross the line of white stones beside them. The panic makes every step feel like the right one. On the far side of the stones it does not lift. Sundarka will not go, and says that no caravan that ran from this field has lived. He is right.
- _Fix the wheel._ There is a spare spoke in no wagon, and a wheel takes time the sun does not leave.
- _Save the animals._ Blindfold the oxen and they stand. Cut them loose and they are gone into the dusk, and the wagons stay where they are.
- _Signal the post._ A fire, a mirror in the last light, a horn. Vijesa sees it. He does not cross the stones after dark. Nobody in the companies does.
- _Persuade Sundarka._ He can be forced away from the box, or helped to carry it, which takes two strong people. He cannot be talked out of it. He argues for staying at the wagons with fire, and offers his oil.

**Clues and rewards.** Vijesa's warning. The panic, which stays with anyone who steps over the stones. The white stone beside the wagon is freshly whitewashed and stands loose on the surface, with no bedding round its foot; older stones further along are crusted with lichen and sunk into the ground. A character who looks for it sees that Karasha's horse left the track exactly where the gully begins, as if he knew where to stop. The oil jars, and the merchant's eyes on the north track.

**Consequences.** Whoever bolts alone is the first to meet the dark. Loose oxen are found later, in the night, in a way that tells the party something is out there with them.

**If bypassed.** A party that runs at once crosses the stones, climbs the rise and finds no road beyond it, only more of the field going down into the dusk, and the shape of the shelter on the next rise. Whichever way it runs, the wagons are the one thing on the slope that does not move, and the dark comes on before any other landmark. A party that goes back for the wagons meets Scene Two there. A party that does not finds Pallivata at the foot of the rise instead, where he crawled, still gripping his spoke, and Sundarka, who stayed with his box, is at the wagons when the party next sees them.

## Scene Two: The Man Under the Wagon

**Purpose.** To discover that this has happened before, to find a route, and to learn the field's rules from someone who died of them.

> The sun is gone and the slope has turned gray. Under the lead wagon, where the broken wheel has come down among the stones, a hand is showing: brown and dry as leather, closed round a length of wood.

**Situation.** Dusk. Under the wagon, wedged among the stones, is a body dried by a year of sun and wind, in the remains of a factor's coat, both hands locked round a broken wheel spoke. The wreck of a second wagon, stripped of everything that could be carried, lies half buried in the gully a few paces on.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The dead man.** Pallivata, the cloth factor who vanished last dry season. Prying the spoke from his grip is hard. His fingers do not give until they break.
- **The panic.** It is stronger now the light is going. People start to argue in whispers and to look over their shoulders at the slopes.
- Dulaka. Feverish and lucid by turns. He knows what the drivers at Ashvapada said: that nobody came out of Pallivata's caravan, and that its guide came back alone. He knows no more than that.
- Sundarka. He knows the dead man's name before anyone reads it, and goes quiet. Asked, he says he knew Pallivata from the river country.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the spoke._ Pallivata scratched words into the wood with a knife point, in a cramped hand: _Stones lie. Not the near ones. Shelter south, past the dead tree. Roof. See the whole ring from roof. Do not answer. They use our voices._ Below, scratched over and over: _Karasha_.
- _Use the spoke._ It fits the broken wheel, roughly. A wagon that moves can carry Dulaka and the strongbox, slowly, and the wheel holds as far as the shelter.
- _Search the wreck._ The goods are gone. The wagon's harness has been cut, not broken. Someone came in by daylight and took what was worth carrying, and left the dead.
- _Ask Sundarka for his papers._ At the name Karasha he produces his escort receipt himself, angrily, as proof that he was cheated. It carries the company captain's seal and a note recommending Karasha to him, "as requested". He folds it back into his coat, over something else.
- _Look south._ In the last light one dark, square shape stands on a rise, with a dead tree beside it.

> [!WARNING]
> Read the receipt's note out in full if a player asks to read it, and do not stress the last two words. A player who catches them has earned the early guess.

**Clues and rewards.** The spoke. The receipt. The proof that wagons break in this gully on purpose. A character raised on the march recognizes the shape on the rise as a stone shrine older than the field's ruin, and knows it has a flat roof.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the spoke has a moving wagon, a slow one. A party that leaves Pallivata where he lies may hear him later. Something out there uses his voice, calling for help from the gully, in the dark.

**If bypassed.** If the party never reads the spoke, the dark shape on the southern skyline is still there to be seen, and the scratched words on the shelter's roof beams in Scene Four say what the spoke said. If the party never reaches the wagon, the receipt in Sundarka's coat and the ledger in the shelter carry the proof of the diversion.

## Scene Three: Dark Between the Wagons

**Purpose.** To bring the threat close, to take someone, to prove that the white stones nearest the wagons are no protection, and to show that the merchant will not move.

> The sun goes down behind the Descent and the dark comes fast. Between the wagons, at the edge of the lamplight, men are running past: men on foot in the dress of an old host, helmets gone, looking back over their shoulders. They pass, and more pass. Then one does not pass. It stops at the edge of the light, and turns, and comes closer.

**Situation.** Close to, the thing that stopped is wrong: too much of it is the color of the dry ground and the dead grass, and the face is a shape the dark has made out of where a face should be. It reaches into the light. Sundarka has his oil jars broken out and his drivers building the fire high between the wagons, and between the runs of the hunters he climbs onto the lead wagon and looks north.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The hunters.** The predators of the unreceived dead, crossing in a form half made of the field. They come close, they take hold and they drag a person off into the dark. They come thickest round anyone who is bleeding, and Dulaka is bleeding. Steel and fire hurt them. Neither hurts them the way it hurts a man.
- **The field's own dead.** The running men, [Marupala]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}'s beaten host, worn down to the one thing they still do: running for the road. They do not attack. They draw the eye away from what does.
- **The voices.** Out of the dark come voices the party knows: a companion's voice, a mother's, Pallivata's, the merchant's, calling a name and asking for help.
- Senata. Sundarka's younger guard, steady until now. He hears his brother's voice calling from the stones, and he answers it, and he goes.
- Sundarka. He will hold the wagons. When anyone proposes to move, he refuses, and if pressed he says why: they hold here until dawn, because the collectors come at dawn and must find him alive with the box.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must establish two things: the stones round Senata's body are the loose, new ones, and Sundarka will not leave the wagons for any reason the party can give him. Whatever else happens tonight, describe both.

**Player choices.**

- _Hold the wagons._ Circle them, build up the fire with the merchant's oil and fight from the wagon beds. The hunters come in and go out of the light and take what they can reach. This is what Sundarka wants.
- _Move for the shelter._ Carry Dulaka on the moving wagon or on a litter, roped together, lamps out or lamps lit. Sundarka refuses to come; this is where he says the line about the collectors.
- _Go after Senata._ His scream comes back across the slope and ends. A party that follows the sound finds him at the line of white stones, laid along it with his face to the road, dead. The stones that should have saved him are around his body.
- _Burn the grass._ The dry grass takes at once in the wind off [[place-wstrndscnt|The Western Descent]], and faster with oil. The hunters do not come through a burning line. The fire does not last, and if the wind turns it comes back at the wagons.
- _Do not answer._ A party that has learned the saying, from the spoke, from Dulaka or from any Vedyari among them, can hold its tongue and keep its people from answering. Roping people together, singing, counting aloud and holding hands all help.

> [!TIP]
> If the party never proposes to move, let Sundarka's slip come when a hunter takes a driver from the edge of the fire and somebody says they must get off the field. He answers that nobody leaves before the collectors come, and then hears himself.

**Clues and rewards.** The stones beside Senata are the loose, new ones. Further along the slope, at the edge of the light, the field's own dead keep running south, past the false line, and on. Sundarka's words about the collectors.

**Consequences.** Senata is lost. If his body is left on the field, the hunters gather round it. Every person who answers a voice is in the next scene's danger first. A party that burned the grass has bought time, and drawn Vijesa's attention from the post. A party that knows about the collectors now knows the herders' track is their road, and that they come at first light.

**If bypassed.** A party that stayed at the false line instead of the wagons meets this scene there: the figures come along the line of stones, and the voices call from the wagons behind, Sundarka's among them, though Sundarka is alive at his fire. If the party never moves at all, the hunters pull the wagons apart round them over the first watch, and the one structure on the field, the shelter on its rise, is all there is left to run to; Sundarka goes only if he is carried, and says why as he is.

## Scene Four: The Roof and the Break

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the field. The party must keep the survivors together, find the true edge, decide what it owes the merchant, and carry Dulaka through the hunters.

> The shelter is a squat stone shrine on the rise, roofless at one end, its flat roof whole at the other, with the stumps of banner poles along the parapet. From the roof, under the stars, the whitewashed stones of the ring catch what light there is, and the whole ring lies below you as a pale broken loop on the dark slope.

**Situation.** Inside the shrine, against the back wall, is a leather satchel. From the roof the ring's southern side has a bite out of it: a long stretch of stones bulging inward, well inside the curve of the rest. Beyond the bite, where the curve would run on, two lines of stones climb to a cleft in a rock spur and stop on either side of it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The field.** The panic is at its worst on the rise. People want to scatter, each certain that their own way out is the only one. The voices are close now, under the parapet. The hunters come up the rise in numbers.
- **The cleft.** The real line runs through a narrow break between two rocks, wide enough for two people side by side. The hunters can get into it, and in the cleft they can hold a person bodily and pull.
- Dulaka. He has to be carried. He is weakening, and the hunters know it.
- Sundarka, if he came. On the roof, with the field in his head, he demands that the party hold the rise until first light and then go back down to the wagons with the box, so that the collectors find him there. He is ready to fight for it.
- Vijesa, if he came, at his fire on the road side of the cleft.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the ring._ From the roof, the true line is plain to anyone who looks for the shape rather than the nearest stone. The cleft is the nearest point of it.
- _Search the shelter._ Pallivata's satchel holds his caravan ledger: the escort fee paid to Krtiraya's company, Karasha's hire entered on the captain's word, and a consignment of cut gems carried for the house of Sundarka. On the roof beams, scratched with a knife, are the same words as the spoke.
- _Follow the dead._ Marupala's running men cross the slope below toward the road. They pass the false line without pausing and stop at the true one, and stand there, looking at the road they cannot reach. Following them is true. It also takes the party down among them, where the hunters are.
- _Follow the offerings._ The people of the [[place-khzryndsrtrgn|Khazryn Desert Region]] leave cups, knotted cloths and bowls at the stones for their own dead. They are at the true stones and nowhere along the bite.
- _Look for the soldier's fire._ If the party signaled Vijesa in Scene One or burned grass in Scene Three, a fire burns now on the road side of the cleft, where he has carried it against his captain's orders and sits by it with a horn. Other lights move on the field too. His is the one that stays still. If the party did neither, his fire is there anyway by midnight, later and smaller.
- _Fight the retreat._ Down the rise and across the slope, with Dulaka carried, the party in a close knot, fire at the back. The hunters press hardest at the cleft, where the rocks give them hold.
- _Burn a line._ Fire set across the slope behind the retreat holds the hunters back for as long as it burns. The wind decides how long that is.
- _Light a pyre._ The hunters gather round a pyre lit on the field, unexplained and unfailing. Burning Senata's body, or the wagons, or the wagons with Senata on them, draws them off long enough to reach the cleft. Burning the wagons burns Sundarka's bait, and he knows it.
- _Leave something._ A party can leave the strongbox, or leave Dulaka, or leave Sundarka with his box, or leave somebody at the cleft's mouth to hold it while the rest go through. Each is a real choice, and each works.
- _Hold the roof till dawn._ A party with fire, a strong position and nerve can stay on the roof all night. At first light the hunters thin and go, and the panic stays. This is what Sundarka asks for, and from the roof the party can watch the herders' track.

> [!TIP]
> Vijesa's question is the surest of the reveal's routes, because his fire is at the cleft whatever the party did. Give it to the first character who reaches him, before he asks anything else: "Is the court's man alive?"

**Clues and rewards.** The ledger, which with the receipt makes a case at law, and the gems in it that were Sundarka's. The true line. A character who stands on the far side of the cleft feels the panic lift like a weight taken off the chest.

**Consequences.** Whoever is pulled from the knot in the cleft is lost unless someone goes back for them at once, into the rocks, among the hunters. A party that reaches the road with Dulaka alive has beaten the field. What the party now owes Sundarka, and what he owes it, depends on what was burned, what was carried and who was left.

**If bypassed or failed.** A party that never reached the shelter, or never read the ring, can still find the cleft by the offerings, by Vijesa's fire or by the running dead. A party that failed at the cleft and fell back holds whatever ground it can until dawn, and walks out through the cleft by daylight with the panic on it and the dead counted. A party that has not yet learned what Sundarka is learns it at Vijesa's fire, or at dawn when the collectors come and he walks out to meet them. Either way the party reaches Scene Five, at a cost proportionate to what it learned.

## Scene Five: Beyond the White Stones

**Purpose.** The treasure, the proof, and the men who come to collect.

> The sun comes up behind the road. The field behind you is empty, gold and quiet. Along the herders' track from the north comes a string of mules at a trot, blindfolded, with four riders and a man on a good horse at their head.

**Situation.** Dawn. Vijesa is there if he came, gray with sleeplessness. Sundarka sits on the strongbox, at the wagons or on the road side of the line, or on nothing, and looks north. The riders are company men, and the man on the good horse is Karasha, coming to collect.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Karasha and the collectors.** They expect the dead. They are armed and in a hurry to be off the field before the panic gets into them, and they do not want a fight on it.
- Sundarka. He wants them taken with the goods in their hands and himself alive beside the goods, which is the one thing that makes them robbers at law and not salvagers. If he is with the party, he says so, and his commission makes the taking lawful.
- Vijesa. A witness. He saw the guide ride away, and he has seen the dead of three caravans found at the southern stones.

**Player choices.**

- _Ambush._ The collectors ride onto the field in daylight, slowed by blindfolded mules, and the party knows where they will stop. Karasha bolts first.
- _Let them go in._ Stay hidden, let them reach the wagons and lay hands on the box, and take them there. A living Sundarka on the box when they do is the sting complete.
- _Take Karasha alive._ He talks quickly, and he names the captain.
- _Bargain._ The collectors offer a share to keep quiet. The party can take it, refuse it, or take it and testify anyway.
- _Go to the court._ The ledger, the receipt, Vijesa's word and the moved stones themselves, which anyone sent to look can see.

> [!TIP]
> A party that learned the truth in Scene Three or Four should have the means to plan this scene: where the track comes in, how many riders, and what Sundarka needs. Let the plan work if it is sound. The collectors are not the climax; the field was.

**Clues and rewards.** Sundarka opens the strongbox and pays: his own cut gems, and sealed bags of silver stamped with the court's seal, which is how the court pays its blades. He pays generously, more for any guard or driver the party brought out, and more again for the collectors taken. Pallivata's ledger and Sundarka's receipt, which together are the proof the court at Sandyapur lacks. Karasha, if taken. The road west, or back to Ashvapada.

**Consequences.** The court that hears the case pays for the evidence and wants witnesses who will stand. The captain has a company, and the company is owed. A party that left Sundarka on the field and comes out without him is paid by nobody, and is a witness the court did not commission.

**If bypassed.** A party that walked out by the road and never met the collectors still has the ledger and the receipt. The court's case is thinner without Karasha, and the captain has time to have the stones put back.

## Aftermath

- **The captain brought to court.** The march loses a captain and a company goes unpaid for a season, and the crown's officer at Ashvapada has to answer for what he passed. The stones are reset on their old line, and the companies say nothing about it.
- **The captain warned.** The false line is gone within the week. The guide is not seen again on the road, and another one, as cheerful, takes his place.
- Sundarka. The court has its case and recovers his loss out of the captain's goods. Whether it asks how many of the caravan came out alive depends on whether the party tells it. Senata's family hears from the party, or from nobody.
- **Dulaka carried out.** He walks with a stick by the next caravan season and tells the story at every well, and in his telling the merchant asked for the guide by name. Lost, he lies on the field and is not received, and his family asks the [[affiliation-agnipantha|Agmi-pantra]] to burn the ground for him, which the orthodox temples forbid.
- Senata. If his body was burned on the field, his family is told he went with a pyre. If it was left, it is found at the edge of the stones in the morning, as the march expects.
- Pallivata. His family learns where he died. A character who brings his spoke home brings them the last thing he wrote.
- Vijesa. His captain knows who carried a fire to the cleft. The court can take him into its service, or leave him at the post.
