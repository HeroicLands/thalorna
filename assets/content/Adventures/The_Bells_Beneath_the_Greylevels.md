The tide cuts the party off at [[place-threnhallow|Threnhallow]], an abandoned house of the [[affiliation-selmoros|Faith of Sélmoros]] on the seaward edge of [[place-greylevels|The Greylevels]]. A bell rings under the water before each disappearance. The dead of the house, the Levels' drowned, rise with the tide and come wherever the water comes, and the Threshold Keeper who taught them to rise commands them from the flooded crypt. Everything the party meets says the house must be broken. It must not, or not yet: the drowned have worked the house's sluice-gates at every ebb since the Keeper raised them, the lower grazing of the Levels is dry because of them, and every way the party has to break the house puts the sea back over the Levels at the next spring tide.

## Overview

Threnhallow was built to give the Levels' drowned their rites. When the house could not pay for labor on its sluice-gates, Keeper [Leofwald]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, its Threshold Keeper, altered the rite of Sélmoros so that the drowned in his crypt rose to work them. [[lore-theshadow|The Shadow]] came through the opening he made. His novices raised him with his own rite when he died, and he is one of the [[being-tereb|Tereb]] now, a dead man who wears his former self: courteous, grave, very nearly the Keeper he was. His congregation is the house's drowned, [[being-damut|Damut]] who go where they are driven, and the bell is what drives them.

The rule of the house is the water. The drowned come only where the water comes, and a body dragged clear of it falls still. Leofwald rings the bell below before he sends them for someone, and the one they take is drowned in the crypt and brought back to keep the house. The drowned call through the drains in the voices of the brethren and of those taken before, and a living soul who answers is counted among the keepers.

The adventure runs from one dusk to the next dawn, across a single spring tide. Its clock is the water: it rises through the house all night, turns in the small hours, and drains away at the ebb only if somebody opens the gates. Every scene below gives several ways through and says how play carries on if the party skips, fails or cuts short what the scene offers.

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the house—what Leofwald did, why the bell rings, what the drowned do at every ebb and what breaking the house costs—is in [[place-threnhallow#truth|the truth of Threnhallow]]. In short: Leofwald is right. The drowned have opened and shut the sluice at every ebb since he raised them, and the lower grazing has stayed dry for a generation because of them. They wear out, as every damut does, and the bell rings when Leofwald sends them for a living keeper to replace one. Silencing the bell, unmaking Leofwald and laying the drowned down each end the takings and stop the gates, and within a fortnight the next spring tide comes through the cut and over the Levels. The Levels have destroyed Leofwald twelve times, and each time the sea came over the lower grazing until he rose again in the crypt months later. He is on his thirteenth and last life, he knows it, and he cannot afford a fight. Destroying him now is final. This section covers what that truth does at the table.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The party must reach Scene Three believing the drowned are a congregation of hunters, and must be able to see by the end of Scene Four that they are a work-gang. Everything they do at the turn of the tide is the gates: they break off, go down to the wheel and work it. Keep that consistent on every route, and the turn does the revealing.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal lands at the turn of the tide, late in Scene Three or in Scene Four, and it reaches the party by whichever route it took.

- **From the bell-loft.** A party that waits out the flood in the tower is there when the tide turns. The drowned in the nave stop, turn as one, and file down through the vestry floor. [Mildith Barnley]{.name data-type="being/npc"} puts her finger on the last mark of her tally and makes another beside it. Lowering a lantern down the bell-shaft, the party sees them at the far end of the crypt, hands on the wheel, and hears the gates groan open and the cut begin to run.
- **In the crypt.** A party fighting below when the tide turns watches the drowned let go of whoever they hold, turn their backs and wade to the wheel, and wind it, while Leofwald waits with the rope slack in his hand. Asked, he says what they are doing and why. Everything he says is borne out by the wheel, the plans and the book.
- **From the embankment.** A party that leaves by the landward door at the ebb stands on the bank as the light comes and sees the outfall running, the cut clear, the lower grazing dry and the steadings' lamps lit behind a gate that nobody living has turned in years. Below the mound the gear grinds, and stops.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on the turn and then stop. The drowned walking away from a fight to go to work is the whole of it. Let the players go back over the scenes behind them themselves: Aldgar's grumble, the bright handles, the book in its many hands, Godric worried about the tide.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early, and never deny a right guess. A party that reads the plans and the book in Scene Two and suspects the drowned are keeping the gates can test it: watch the cut from the tower at the turn, or go down to the wheel at the ebb. Let the test work. The party has earned the chance to choose its order before the bell rings again.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not let Mildith or Godric explain the gates. Mildith counts what she sees and does not know what it means; Godric says the tide is coming and nothing else. Leofwald may say it all in Scene Four, but only after the party has seen the turn or the wheel. If any of them states it before the turn, the reversal is spent as a speech, not found.

> [!WARNING]
> Silencing the bell is the quickest way to stop the house, and the quickest way to stop the gates. A party that cuts the rope before the turn still meets the reveal: at the ebb nothing goes down, the water stands in the crypt, and from the tower or the embankment the cut is brim-full and creeping over the lowest ditches by dawn. Do not let the reveal wait on the party leaving the bell alone.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party wants to stop the takings without drowning the Levels. The endowment chest stops being loot and becomes the remedy: its deeds settle what the commission owes the house, and its coin pays living hands for the gates. Other answers are open. The party can work the gates itself until help comes, at every ebb, through a fortnight to the next spring tide. It can bargain with Leofwald to keep the house with the sea's own drowned and take no more of the living, which works the gates worse and wears his keepers faster. Mildith can carry word to the faith, which can send brethren. The order of the party's acts matters now: whoever silences the bell or lays the drowned down before living hands are at the wheel has flooded the lower grazing. Leofwald is on his last life, so his end is final: once he is destroyed, nothing drives the drowned to the wheel again, and living hands at the gates are the only way the Levels stay dry.

> [!TIP]
> Give the party a living face for the Levels. If Aldgar is with them, his steading is on the lower grazing; if not, the lamps the party saw from the embankment belong to the fowler who rowed it out. A fortnight is a long time to work a wheel at every ebb, and a short one to get a commission to pay.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each leads to Threnhallow on a rising tide.

- **The thief.** The commission of sewers' endowment chest sits on the mound, unclaimed, and a [[place-greywater|Greywater]] lawyer wants the rolls inside it for a lawsuit. He says nothing of the silver vessels the house held, which the thief has heard about elsewhere.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A Levels family hires an escort to bring home a jeweled reliquary of the healers of the [[affiliation-lusinia|Faith of Lusinia]] that it left in the house's keeping. The pilot they hire insists on going out by day and coming back before the turn, and the tide turns early.
- **The leader.** The pilot [Aldgar Wraymon]{.name data-type="being/npc"}'s son rowed two travelers out to the mound and did not come back. Aldgar heard the bell. He asks the leader to come with him, and he is the one at the oars in the opening.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is crossing the Levels on the causeway at low water, misjudges the tide, and reaches the mound with the water at the wanderer's knees. The pilot in the opening is a fowler who sees the wanderer and rows over, against his own judgment.

## The Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The pilot stops rowing. The causeway you were following across the flats is gone, the last stones of it sinking out of sight under brown water that was not there a quarter-hour ago, and the tide is still coming in, fast and silent across the mud. Ahead, a stone house stands above the reeds on its mound, roofless at one end, no light in it. The pilot does not look at it. Beneath the boat, deep in the water, a bell rings once. Something knocks against the underside of the hull, twice, as if asking to be let in.

> [!TIP]
> Let the knock sit before anyone speaks. The opening's job is the cold certainty that this is not good, and that the water belongs to whatever is under it.

## Scene One: The Steps

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Put the party on the mound with the way back gone, and show the first sign that the house's danger comes with the water.

> The boat grounds against steps cut into the side of the mound, slick with weed. Above them the chapel's west doors are shut. Behind you, inland, the last of the light lies flat across a long dry grazing, and a steading's lamp is lit on it.

**Situation.** As the party climbs out, the painter, the boat's mooring line, goes taut and pulls, hard, toward deep water. If nobody holds it, the boat goes out into the channel and does not come back. Through the gap between the west doors the party sees that they are barred on the inside with pews stacked to the lintel. Somebody inside was keeping something out. A side door into the cloister stands open. Inside, on the dry flagstones of the cloister walk, a line of wet bare footprints leads away from the barred doors and stops at an iron drain-grate in the floor. An overturned lantern lies beside the grate, its glass cracked. The wick is still warm.

**NPCs and threats.** Aldgar Wraymon, or whichever pilot or fowler brought the party, is tight-lipped and wants to leave at once; with the boat gone he cannot. He knows the house's story as the Levels tell it and believes every word. Whatever pulled the painter is not seen. A hand on the line feels it pull in slow, patient tugs.

> [!TIP]
> Give Aldgar his grumble while he looks back at the lamp on the grazing, the way a man says what he says every market-day: the lower grazing has been dry since the house went quiet, and the commission takes the credit for it. Say it once and move on. It returns at the turn of the tide with its second meaning.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ haul the boat back in against the pull. A strong group wins it, and a pale arm lets go of the keel at the last moment and sinks out of sight. Its palm is worn raw, and the skin is rust-red to the wrist. The boat is saved, and the party has seen a drowned man.
- _Stealth:_ go round the mound by the reeds to find another way in. The reed-margin is soft and sucking, and every channel the party crosses is deeper than it looks. Below the mound on the seaward side an outfall of cut stone opens on a channel running clear to the sea, and iron gates stand in it, shut. They find the vestry window, small and unbarred, and the footprints come to meet them in the corridor beyond.
- _Talk:_ get Aldgar to say what he knows. He says the bell rings before somebody is taken, that the dead come up out of the flats and call in the voices of the taken, that the brethren were good men before, and that nobody from the Levels comes out here after dark. He tells the Levels' telling of Leofwald as he heard it, and believes the dead worked the gates for the bad year and then turned to taking. He says the Levels have killed the Keeper more than once, with a fowler's spear, with a priest the commission paid and with their own hands after a hard winter, and that the Keeper always comes back, and the water with him. Nobody goes out against him now.
- _Cleverness:_ read the footprints. They come up out of the drain, cross the floor to the barred doors, and go back down. The floor between is bone dry, and the prints are wet only where they are.
- _Giving something up:_ let the boat go and keep everyone together on the steps. The pull stops the moment the line is dropped.

**Clues and rewards.**

- **The water is their road.** From the footprints, which come and go by the drain. From the painter, pulled from below. From Aldgar's story of the dead "coming up out of the flats".
- **Somebody was here lately and was afraid.** From the barred doors and the warm lantern.

**Consequences.** If the boat is lost, the only way off the mound is the maintenance embankment, which nobody in the party knows about. If the boat is kept, it is tied up at the steps, and the tide rises round it.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that breaks in through the west doors pulls down the pews and finds the footprints on the far side, leading to the same grate. A party that never sees the prints meets the same fact in Scene Three, when the water comes up through the floor and the dead with it. If Aldgar is lost on the steps, pulled under with the painter, his voice is among those that call through the drains later, and the party knows the cost of answering; his grumble about the grazing comes instead from the fowler's talk, or from Mildith, who heard it from the pilot who rowed her out.

## Scene Two: The Guest Cells

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Give the party the house's rules, each from more than one source, the first hint of who rules it, and, in the papers of the house, the first hint of what it is for.

> The cloister is dark and smells of the sea. A row of cell doors stands open along it, and in each cell the bedding is made up as if for a guest who has just stepped out. In the farthest, a pair of boots stands side by side at the foot of the cot.

**Situation.** Several cells hold travelers' possessions laid out neatly on the cots: a pack, a pair of boots, a coat, a purse with money in it. Nobody takes their boots and leaves. In the chapter room the house's drainage plans are pinned to a board, showing the drains of every room running down to the crypt, the sluice at the outfall of the drainage cut, and a passage from the vestry down to the bell-chamber above the crypt. The guests' book lies open on the lectern. Beside the passage door, freshly scratched into the plaster with a knife-point: _Answer not the brethren._ Above the chapel, in the bell-loft of the tower, a survivor is hiding.

**NPCs and threats.** Mildith Barnley is a young Novice of the [Silent Watch]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"}, sent to the house by the faith to find out why its returns had stopped, and alone on the mound for four days. She is exhausted and very calm, and she has drawn up the loft ladder. She whispers, by long habit and by present fear, and offers one rule and no more: "Answer them not." She has cut a tally into the loft's beam with her knife: four deep marks, one for each time the bell has rung, and a long row of short ones beside them. Asked, she says she cuts a short one each time the dead go down through the vestry floor. She does not know where they go. The drowned do not show themselves here. Through the drain-grates the party hears, now and then, a voice saying a name.

> [!TIP]
> Show the beam before Mildith says anything about it. Two rows of marks, four in one and many in the other, are a plain thing a player can count. Let the table decide what the longer row means.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ search the house room by room. Every room has a drain, and in every drain the water is a little higher than in the last.
- _Stealth:_ find Mildith without frightening her. Somebody who climbs to the loft quietly, or calls softly with a name she knows, is let up. A party that shouts is met with silence and a raised ladder.
- _Talk:_ win Mildith's trust. She knows the true rite of the Silent Judge, the one Leofwald changed, and knows that a damut over whom it is spoken goes still. She is afraid to speak it aloud, because whoever speaks in the house is heard below. Asked about the Keeper, she knows what the Silent Watch teaches of terebu: thirteen lives, a rising after each of the first twelve, and nothing after the last.
- _Cleverness with the papers:_ read the drainage plans and the guests' book. In the margin of the plans the house's own clerk has written that the gates must be opened at every ebb and shut before the flood, or the cut backs up and the lower grazing floods. The guests' book runs on for years after the last brother signed it, in many hands, and every entry after his is in the brethren's form, a name and "keepeth the house". Among the later entries one name comes back twelve times, Leofwald's, in one hand that is a little less steady each time. The newest is [Godric Stonyby]{.name data-type="being/creature"}'s, in a shaking hand, the ink still fresh, and his boots are the pair at the foot of the cot.
- _Cleverness with the rite:_ read the vestry rite-book. Its funeral rite has lines struck through and new lines written over them in a careful hand: where the old rite sends the dead to the god's gate, the new one bids them keep the house, in a call and an answer, "Who keepeth the house?" and "I keep it." The struck lines can still be read.
- _Giving something up:_ answer one of the voices on purpose, to see what it does. The bell rings below within the hour, and from then on the dead come for whoever answered.

**Clues and rewards.** The essential facts, and their sources:

- **The bell rings before each taking.** From Mildith's four deep marks. From the fowlers' saying, through Aldgar. From the party's own ears, from here on.
- **Answer not the brethren.** From the scratched warning. From Mildith. From the rite-book, whose altered rite is a call and an answer.
- **The dead come where the water comes.** From the footprints in Scene One. From the drainage plans, which show that the crypt and every drain in the house fill from the sea at high water and empty through the sluice at the ebb.
- **The true rite lays them down.** From the struck lines in the rite-book. From Mildith, once she trusts the party. From the lintel of the crypt gate, cut with the true rite's last line.
- **The way to the bell and the sluice.** From the drainage plans. From Mildith, who knows the house. From the sound of the bell itself, which comes up strongest through the vestry floor.

The guest cells hold what the drowned's last travelers carried: a little money, a good knife, a pilot's brass compass.

> [!WARNING]
> The plans' margin and the guests' book carry the reversal, and they are the easiest things in the house to describe too fully. Give the margin note as the house's old instruction to its brethren, and the book as a list of names. Do not add that the gates are still worked, or that the names are the drowned.

**Consequences.** The party knows the house's rules and that something rules it from below. Mildith, if befriended, comes with the party and will speak the true rite if she is protected while she does.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** If the party never finds Mildith, she comes to them, climbing down from the loft when the bell rings in Scene Three, because she cannot bear to be alone when it rings; the tally stays on the beam for a party that climbs up later. If the drainage plans are missed or lost, the sluice-book in the chapter room's chest holds the same plan and the same margin, and Mildith knows the way. If the guests' book is missed, Godric's own words at the window in Scene Three carry its meaning. If the rite-book is missed, the crypt gate's lintel holds the line that matters, and the party passes under it in Scene Four.

## Scene Three: The Rising Water

**Purpose.** The horror draws close. Show the dead coming, make the house shrink round the party, close the way back, and, at the turn of the tide, show the party what the drowned do.

> Night. The wind is behind the tide, and the tide is a spring tide. Somewhere beneath your feet the bell rings, and before it has stopped, a thin brown film of water is spreading across the chapel floor from the joints between the flagstones.

**Situation.** Within minutes the water is ankle-deep. At a window of the cloister a man is standing outside in the dark, his face just beyond the light, asking calmly to be let in. It is Godric, the traveler whose boots stand in the farthest cell. He knows the party's names if he has heard them spoken. He says the tide is coming and he is cold, and that he must be let in before the tide comes. He says it reasonably, again and again. Then he leans forward and presses his face to the glass, and the party sees that something in the water has eaten his eyes. His hands on the sill are raw at the palm. Beneath the gallery, in the flooded nave, more figures stand up out of the water, very still, faces turned upward.

**NPCs and threats.** Godric is newly drowned and raised, and confused enough to believe he is still alive, until he is not. The **drowned congregation** fill the nave as the water fills it, a dozen and then more, in fowlers' smocks and brethren's black, with the pilot's son among them if the party is looking for him. They cannot come onto dry stone. They wait, and the water rises. The gallery and the bell-loft are dry, for now.

> [!TIP]
> Play Godric as a man anxious to be somewhere on time. He is not hungry and not cruel; he is late, and the tide is coming. That is the frightening reading tonight, and the true one later.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight on the gallery stairs as the water climbs them. The drowned are slow, and a body dragged out of the water falls still, but there are more of them than the party has strength for.
- _Stealth:_ go up and wait. The water rises a step at a time and reaches the gallery floor before the turn. Waiting is not a refuge, only a delay, but it puts the party in the tower when the tide turns.
- _Talk:_ Godric pleads. A party member who answers him, or who calls to the pilot's son by name, is counted, and the bell rings again for that one.
- _Cleverness:_ use the drains and the dry stone. A floor or stair kept dry is a wall the dead cannot cross; a door packed with cloth and lime holds the water back for a time; the bell-loft ladder is dry.
- _Giving something up:_ throw the dead something to drown in their place. Godric's own pack, a cloak, a body, anything thrown into the nave is seized and dragged down. It buys minutes.

**At the turn.** In the small hours the tide stands, and then turns. The drowned in the nave stop as one, turn away from the gallery, and file down through the vestry door into the dark, unhurried, Godric among them. A party in the bell-loft sees Mildith cut another short mark in the beam. A lantern lowered down the bell-shaft shows them at the far end of the crypt, hands on an iron wheel, and the gates below the mound groan open. Over the next hour the water drains out of the house, room by room, down the drains it came up.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must establish that the drowned leave their prey at the turn, and that the house empties only after they have gone down. Whatever else happens tonight, describe the drowned turning away and the water going out behind them.

**Clues and rewards.** The water rule, now proved. The bell, rung at each taking, heard plainly from the vestry floor, which points the way down. Godric's last coherent words, said over his shoulder as he goes down: "The Keeper saith we keep the house. He is below with the bell."

**Consequences.** The boat, if still moored, is gone, pulled under at the steps. The way down, through the vestry passage, is ankle-deep at the flood and clear at the ebb. If the party goes down before the turn, the turn comes to it in the crypt.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that went straight down to the crypt in Scene Two meets these signs there: the bell rings as they descend, the water rises behind them on the stair, and Godric stands in the passage asking to be let past, because the tide is coming. If someone has answered the dead, the bell rings for them, and the drowned come for that person wherever the water reaches; the only escape is downward, to the source. A party that tries to leave by the embankment finds the landward door of the mound under water and the drowned standing in it until the turn, when they leave it and go below, and the door is open to the bank.

## Scene Four: The Flooded Crypt

**Purpose.** Confrontation and revelation. Show the Keeper, the bell and the sluice, and let the party use what it learned, knowing now what each act costs.

> The stair ends in a narrow stone room where a great bell hangs from an oak beam over a black shaft. Its rope runs down into the water below. Beyond an iron gate wrought as a black gate wound with bare vines, a long vault opens, half full of sea-water, and in the middle of it a man in black stands waist-deep, holding the rope.

**Situation.** The bell-chamber's shaft drops into the crypt. The iron gate's lintel is cut with a line of the true rite. The crypt is lined with niches of the dead, most of them empty. Beside the gate, above the old flood line, twelve niches in a row are new-made and closed to the shoulder with mortar, the oldest gray and the newest still pale. Each holds one body in a Threshold Keeper's black. They are of one height and one build, and the later ones are the more ruined. At the far end, at the outfall of the drainage cut, stands the iron gear of the sluice. Its wheel is rusted on the rim and bright on the handles, and the gear is greased with tallow scraped from the chapel's candles. In the middle of the water stands Keeper Leofwald.

**NPCs and threats.** Keeper Leofwald is grave, gentle and patient. He speaks the older speech of the house: "Thou art come to keep the house with us. Fear not. The water is cold but a little while." He rings the bell, and the drowned rise from the water and come. He cannot be talked out of his work, but he can be talked to, and while he is talking he is not ringing. Close to, his face does not quite hold: the courtesy is a garment, and it slips. The **drowned congregation** come up from the water at the bell, drag whoever they reach into the deep part of the crypt, and hold them under, until the turn.

> [!TIP]
> Play Leofwald as a man who cannot afford to lose. He is on his last life and knows it, so he never closes to strike: he keeps the drowned and the deep water between himself and the party, gives ground when a fight turns, and talks the moment he is threatened. A party that has counted his lives, from the book or the niches or Aldgar's telling, holds a lever. Shown the count and threatened, he bargains, and gives more than he would to a party he thought he could outlast. Let the threat work, and do not let him gamble his last life on a fight he might lose.

> [!TIP]
> Once the party has seen the turn, let Leofwald make his case, and make it true. He tells them what the Levels were in the bad year, that the drowned have opened the gates at every ebb since, that the commission has never paid a living hand, and that the grazing is dry tonight because of the keepers in his crypt. He asks who will turn the wheel if they break his house. Every fact he gives can be checked against the wheel, the plans and the book.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ destroy Leofwald. He is a tereb and hard to put down, and in the water his drowned stand round him; he gives ground rather than trade blows. A party that drags him onto dry stone, or fights him after the crypt is drained, has the better of it. Destroyed now, he does not rise again. Without him the drowned stand idle, and nothing drives them to the wheel again.
- _Break the bell:_ cut the bell-rope or bring down the oak beam that holds the bell. The bell drops down the shaft into the crypt and is silent under the water. The drowned, undriven, stop where they stand and slowly sink. Leofwald is left alone, and is only a dead man in the water. Nothing goes down to the wheel at the next ebb.
- _Reach the sluice:_ fight or wade to the sluice-gear at the far end. At the ebb, winding the gates open drains the crypt to the outfall over a long count; at the flood it lets more water in. A party at the wheel at the turn finds the drowned coming to it too, and they work beside the living without harming them.
- _Talk:_ speak the true rite. Mildith, or anyone who has the struck lines from the rite-book or the lintel, speaks the rite as it was before Leofwald changed it. Each damut it is spoken over goes still. Spoken over Leofwald with his name, it unmakes him, and on his last life that is the end of him.
- _Cleverness:_ the altered rite's call and answer. Leofwald asks, "Who keepeth the house?" Someone who answers with the true rite's words instead of "I keep it" turns the call against him.
- _Bargain:_ treat with Leofwald. He keeps his house for the gates, and will hear a party that offers the gates another way: living hands paid from the endowment chest, or a promise to work the wheel until they come. Pressed, he agrees to keep the house with the sea's own drowned and take no more of the living, though those keepers wear out fast and the gates are worked worse for it. A party that shows him it knows he is on his last life presses hardest, and he yields soonest to it.
- _Giving something up:_ give Leofwald what he wants, a brother, for a time. Somebody who goes into the water and lets him take their hand holds his attention while the others work, at real risk of drowning.

> [!WARNING]
> At the turn, the drowned let go. Any of the party held under is released, and the congregation turns its back and wades to the wheel. A party that strikes them then strikes workers with their hands full. Describe it before the party chooses, so that what it does next is done knowing.

> [!WARNING]
> The bell, the rite and the kill each close the gates, and the rite and the kill close them for good, because Leofwald does not rise again. A party that ends the house before the reveal lands still has the remedy, if it moves fast: the chest, the wheel, and a fortnight to the spring tide. A party that ends it after the reveal has chosen, and the Aftermath follows from what it knew.

**Clues and rewards.** The sluice and the bell explained by the Keeper who rings it, and borne out by the wheel. The twelve niches by the gate, which count his deaths beside the twelve signatures in the guests' book. The bell is silver-bronze, worth a great deal and too heavy to carry.

**Consequences.** If Leofwald is destroyed or unmade, he does not rise again, the drowned go still and stay still, the house is quiet, and the gates stand as they were left. If only the bell is silenced, the dead stand idle in the water and Leofwald waits; a bell can be hung again. If the crypt is drained and he survives, he waits in the mud at the bottom, and he speaks the rite over each new body the sea brings in. If the party bargained, the bell is silent while the bargain holds, and the drowned go down to the wheel at the next ebb as they always have.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes down unprepared, with neither the rite nor the plans, still has the bell-shaft above: the bell-loft ladder runs down the shaft to the beam, and cutting the rope from above is the quickest way to silence the house. A party that flees the crypt waits out the tide in the bell-loft and meets the turn there (see Scene Three). In the dry hours of the ebb, the treasure and the way out can both be reached.

## Scene Five: The Drained Chamber

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Pay out the treasure, resolve the house, and send the party back toward the living with the Levels' fortnight in its hands.

> At the ebb the vault lies open: wet black stone, the drowned standing at the far end with their hands on the wheel, or lying where the water left them. Through the outfall the gray of dawn shows across the flats, and the cut runs out under it to the sea.

**Situation.** In the niches above the old flood line, sealed with lead and wax, lie the house's treasures. The crypt has drained only if somebody opened the gates: the drowned, the party, or both. If nobody did, the vault stands half full at dawn, and the cut outside is brim-full and creeping over the lowest ditches. Either way, the twelve mortared niches beside the crypt gate stand above the flood line, plain in the lantern-light. The landward door of the mound opens on the maintenance embankment, a long overgrown bank that runs back toward inhabited country, with the lower grazing beside it.

**NPCs and threats.** Mildith, if she lives, wants to speak the rite over every body in the crypt, which takes until the next flood. Once she has seen the wheel, she asks the party what will turn it if she does. Aldgar, if he lives, wants his son's body taken home, and his steading is on the lower grazing.

**Player choices.**

- _Take everything:_ the silver vessels, the chest and the reliquary are heavy, and the embankment is long and broken in places. The tide comes back in hours.
- _Keep faith:_ return the reliquary to its family, the chest to the commission, the vessels to the faith. Each has a claimant who pays for its return. The chest, carried to the commission with what the party saw, is the remedy: its deeds settle what the commission owes the house, and its coin pays living hands at the wheel.
- _Stay:_ help Mildith lay the dead to rest. The house is quiet afterward, and the bell rings for nobody, and the gates are left to whoever comes for them.
- _Work the gates:_ keep the wheel at every ebb until living hands arrive. It is two strong backs' work at each turn of the tide, and the tide turns twice a day.
- _Bargain:_ the commission and the faith both want the endowment chest and both claim it.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **house's silver vessels**, the vessels of its rites, in a sealed niche.
- The **endowment chest** of the commission of sewers, holding the deeds of the endowment, the rolls of its dispute and a quantity of coin from the years it was paid.
- The **reliquary** of Lusinia's healers, gold and set with stones, sealed in a niche above the old flood line. Its family holds that a sick person who touches it in faith mends faster, and it is plainly worth more than the rest together.
- From the guest cells, the travelers' purses and the pilot's compass.

> [!TIP]
> Let the embankment show the stakes in daylight: the outfall, the cut and the lower grazing laid out side by side, with the steadings on it. Whatever the party chose below, it walks home past the ground its choice decides.

**Consequences.** The party leaves along the embankment with the morning, toward the first dry grazing and the smoke of a steading.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that leaves at the ebb without the treasure can return at the next ebb by the embankment, which it now knows. If Leofwald still stands, the dead are still in the crypt, and the bell still rings when a keeper wears out; the reliquary's niche is above the flood line and can be reached by the bell-shaft at any tide.

## Aftermath

- **If the chest reached the commission and the gates have living hands,** the commission's long dispute ends in a judgment nobody likes, the sluice is worked by paid men at every ebb, and Mildith or the faith can lay the drowned down with nothing lost. The faith of the Silent Judge sends a Threshold Keeper to the mound, and the Levels hear the bell again only when one of their drowned is found.
- **If the party bargained with Leofwald,** the bell is silent while the bargain holds, the grazing stays dry, and the house keeps its sea-dead at the wheel. The bargain lasts as long as the sea brings in enough of them.
- **If the party ended Leofwald or silenced the bell and left the gates unworked,** the cut backs up within a few ebbs, and the next spring tide comes in through the gates and over the lower grazing, as it did in the bad year and after each of his deaths. If the party ended Leofwald, he does not rise this time, and the grazing stays under until living hands work the gates. Steadings are lost, cattle and people with them, and the commission's clerks blame the house. Aldgar's steading is among them if he has one.
- **If only the bell is silenced,** the fowlers stop hearing it and think the house is clear. It is not, and the next person to hang a bell in the shaft finds out.
- **If the party fled,** the bell goes on ringing, the grazing stays dry, and the Levels lose a few more each year to the house that keeps them.
