The tide cuts the party off at [[place-threnhallow|Threnhallow]], an abandoned house of the Silent Judge on the seaward edge of the [[place-greylevels|Greylevels]]. A bell rings under the water before each disappearance. The dead of the house, the Levels' drowned, rise with the tide and come wherever the water comes, and the Threshold Keeper who taught them to rise commands them from the flooded crypt. The party has a tide's worth of time to learn how the house works before the water reaches the gallery.

## Overview

Threnhallow was built to give the Levels' drowned their rites. When the house could not pay for labour on its sluice-gates, **Keeper Leofwald**, its Threshold Keeper, altered the rite of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] so that the drowned in his crypt rose to work the gates. The [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]] came through the opening he made. His novices raised him with his own rite when he died, and he is a [[being-tereb|tereb]] now, a dead man who wears his former self: courteous, grave, very nearly the Keeper he was. His congregation is the house's drowned, [[being-damut|damutu]] who go where they are driven, and the bell is what drives them.

The rule of the house is the water. The drowned are bound to the house's water by the altered rite, and they come only where the water comes: up the drains, through the flagstones at high tide, along the wet stone. Out of the water they cannot walk, and a body dragged clear of it falls still. Leofwald rings the bell below before he sends them for someone, and the one they take is drowned in the crypt and brought back to keep the house. The drowned call through the drains and at the doors in the voices of the brethren and of those taken before. A living soul who answers is counted among the keepers, and the next bell is rung for them.

The party has three ways to break the house: lay the dead to rest with the true rite, silence the bell that drives them, or drain the crypt at the ebb by the sluice and strand them. What Leofwald is beneath the courtesy stays partly unexplained.

## Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The pilot stops rowing. The causeway you were following across the flats is gone, the last stones of it sinking out of sight under brown water that was not there a quarter-hour ago, and the tide is still coming in, fast and silent across the mud. Ahead, a stone house stands above the reeds on its mound, roofless at one end, no light in it. The pilot does not look at it. Beneath the boat, deep in the water, a bell rings once. Something knocks against the underside of the hull, twice, as if asking to be let in.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each leads to Threnhallow on a rising tide.

- **The thief.** The commission of sewers' endowment chest sits on the mound, unclaimed, and a Greywater lawyer wants the rolls inside it for a lawsuit. He says nothing of the silver vessels the house held, which the thief has heard about elsewhere.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A Levels family hires an escort to bring home a jewelled reliquary of [[affiliation-lusinia|Lúsinía]]'s healers that it left in the house's keeping. The pilot they hire insists on going out by day and coming back before the turn, and the tide turns early.
- **The leader.** The pilot **Aldgar Wraymon**'s son rowed two travellers out to the mound and did not come back. Aldgar heard the bell. He asks the leader to come with him, and he is the one at the oars in the opening.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is crossing the Levels on the causeway at low water, misjudges the tide, and reaches the mound with the water at the wanderer's knees. The pilot in the opening is a fowler who sees the wanderer and rows over, against his own judgement.

## Scene One: The Steps

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Put the party on the mound with the way back gone, and show the first sign that the house's danger comes with the water.

**Situation.** The boat grounds at the landing steps cut into the mound. As the party climbs out, the painter, the boat's mooring line, goes taut and pulls, hard, toward deep water. If nobody holds it, the boat goes out into the channel and does not come back. The chapel's west doors are shut. Through the gap between them the party sees that they are barred on the inside with pews stacked to the lintel. Somebody inside was keeping something out. A side door into the cloister stands open. Inside, on the dry flagstones of the cloister walk, a line of wet bare footprints leads away from the barred doors and stops at an iron drain-grate in the floor. An overturned lantern lies beside the grate, its glass cracked. The wick is still warm.

**NPCs and threats.** **Aldgar Wraymon**, or whichever pilot or fowler brought the party, is tight-lipped and wants to leave at once; with the boat gone he cannot. He knows the house's story as the Levels tell it and believes every word. Whatever pulled the painter is not seen. A hand on the line feels it pull in slow, patient tugs.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ haul the boat back in against the pull. A strong group wins it, and a pale arm lets go of the keel at the last moment and sinks out of sight. The boat is saved, and the party has seen a drowned man.
- _Stealth:_ go round the mound by the reeds to find another way in. The reed-margin is soft and sucking, and every channel the party crosses is deeper than it looks. They find the vestry window, small and unbarred, and the footprints come to meet them in the corridor beyond.
- _Talk:_ get Aldgar to say what he knows. He says the bell rings before somebody is taken, that the brethren were good men before, and that nobody from the Levels comes out here after dark.
- _Cleverness:_ read the footprints. They come up out of the drain, cross the floor to the barred doors, and go back down. The floor between is bone dry, and the prints are wet only where they are.
- _Giving something up:_ let the boat go and keep everyone together on the steps. The pull stops the moment the line is dropped.

**Clues and rewards.**

- **The water is their road.** From the footprints, which come and go by the drain. From the painter, pulled from below. From Aldgar's story of the dead "coming up out of the flats".
- **Somebody was here lately and was afraid.** From the barred doors and the warm lantern.

**Consequences.** If the boat is lost, the only way off the mound is the maintenance embankment, which nobody in the party knows about. If the boat is kept, it is tied up at the steps, and the tide rises round it.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that breaks in through the west doors pulls down the pews and finds the footprints on the far side, leading to the same grate. A party that never sees the prints meets the same fact in Scene Three, when the water comes up through the floor and the dead with it. If Aldgar is lost on the steps, pulled under with the painter, his voice is among those that call through the drains later, and the party knows the cost of answering.

## Scene Two: The Guest Cells

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Give the party the house's rules, each from more than one source, and the first hint of who rules it.

**Situation.** The cloister holds a row of guest cells. Several hold travellers' possessions laid out neatly on the cots: a pack, a pair of boots, a coat, a purse with money in it. Nobody takes their boots and leaves. In the chapter room the house's drainage plans are pinned to a board, showing the sluice, the drainage cut and a passage from the vestry down to the bell-chamber above the crypt. Beside the passage door, freshly scratched into the plaster with a knife-point: _Answer not the brethren._ Above the chapel, in the bell-loft of the tower, a survivor is hiding.

**NPCs and threats.** **Mildith Barnley** is a young Novice of the Silent Watch, sent to the house by the faith to find out why its returns had stopped, and alone on the mound for four days. She is exhausted and very calm, and she has drawn up the loft ladder. She speaks in a whisper, by long habit and by present fear: "The bell ringeth below ere any is taken. I have heard it four times, and four are gone. They call thee by the voices of them that went before. Answer them not, for whoso answereth is the next they ring for." The drowned do not show themselves here. Through the drain-grates the party hears, now and then, a voice saying a name.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ search the house room by room. Every room has a drain, and in every drain the water is a little higher than in the last.
- _Stealth:_ find Mildith without frightening her. Somebody who climbs to the loft quietly, or calls softly with a name she knows, is let up. A party that shouts is met with silence and a raised ladder.
- _Talk:_ win Mildith's trust. She knows the true rite of the Silent Judge, the one Leofwald changed, and knows that a damut over whom it is spoken goes still. She is afraid to speak it aloud, because whoever speaks in the house is heard below.
- _Cleverness:_ read the vestry rite-book. Its funeral rite has lines struck through and new lines written over them in a careful hand: where the old rite sends the dead to the god's gate, the new one bids them keep the house. The struck lines can still be read.
- _Giving something up:_ answer one of the voices on purpose, to see what it does. The bell rings below within the hour, and from then on the dead come for whoever answered.

**Clues and rewards.** The essential facts, and their sources:

- **The bell rings before each taking.** From Mildith. From the fowlers' saying, through Aldgar. From the party's own ears, from here on.
- **Answer not the brethren.** From the scratched warning. From Mildith. From the rite-book, whose altered rite is a call and an answer: "Who keepeth the house?" and "I keep it."
- **The dead come where the water comes.** From Mildith, who has watched them from the loft and seen them stop at the edge of dry stone. From the footprints in Scene One. From the drainage plans, which show that the crypt and every drain in the house fill from the sea at high water and empty at the ebb through the sluice.
- **The true rite lays them down.** From Mildith. From the struck lines in the rite-book. From the lintel of the crypt gate, cut with the true rite's last line.
- **The way to the bell and the sluice.** From the drainage plans. From Mildith, who knows the house. From the sound of the bell itself, which comes up strongest through the vestry floor.

The guest cells hold what the drowned's last travellers carried: a little money, a good knife, a pilot's brass compass.

**Consequences.** The party knows the house's rules and that Leofwald is below. Mildith, if befriended, comes with the party and will speak the true rite if she is protected while she does.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** If the party never finds Mildith, she comes to them, climbing down from the loft when the bell rings in Scene Three, because she cannot bear to be alone when it rings. If the drainage plans are missed or lost, the sluice-book in the chapter room's chest holds the same plan, and Mildith knows the way. If the rite-book is missed, the crypt gate's lintel holds the line that matters, and the party passes under it in Scene Four.

## Scene Three: The Rising Water

**Purpose.** The horror draws close. Show the dead coming, make the house shrink round the party, and close the way back.

**Situation.** Night. The tide is a spring tide, and the wind is behind it. The bell rings below. Within minutes, water begins to rise through the joints of the chapel's flagstones, a thin brown film across the floor, then ankle-deep. At a window of the cloister a man is standing outside in the dark, his face just beyond the light, asking calmly to be let in. It is **Godric Stonyby**, one of the travellers whose boots stand in a guest cell. He knows the party's names if he has heard them spoken. He says the tide is coming and he is cold. He says it reasonably, again and again. Then he leans forward and presses his face to the glass, and the party sees that something in the water has eaten his eyes. Beneath the gallery, in the flooded nave, more figures stand up out of the water, very still, faces turned upward.

**NPCs and threats.** **Godric** is newly drowned and raised, and confused enough to believe he is still alive, until he is not. The **drowned congregation** fill the nave as the water fills it, a dozen and then more, in fowlers' smocks and brethren's black, with the pilot's son among them if the party is looking for him. They cannot come onto dry stone. They wait, and the water rises. The gallery and the bell-loft are dry, for now.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight on the gallery stairs as the water climbs them. The drowned are slow, and a body dragged out of the water falls still, but there are more of them than the party has strength for.
- _Stealth:_ go up and wait. The water rises a step at a time and reaches the gallery floor before the turn. Waiting is not a refuge, only a delay.
- _Talk:_ Godric pleads. A party member who answers him, or who calls to the pilot's son by name, is counted, and the bell rings again for that one.
- _Cleverness:_ use the drains and the dry stone. A floor or stair kept dry is a wall the dead cannot cross; a door packed with cloth and lime holds the water back for a time; the bell-loft ladder is dry.
- _Giving something up:_ throw the dead something to drown in their place. Godric's own pack, a cloak, a body, anything thrown into the nave is seized and dragged down. It buys minutes.

**Clues and rewards.** The water rule, now proved. The bell, rung at each taking, heard plainly from the vestry floor, which points the way down. Godric's last coherent words: "The Keeper saith we keep the house. He is below with the bell."

**Consequences.** The boat, if still moored, is gone, pulled under at the steps. The tide will not turn for hours, and the water is still climbing. The only way to stop it is below. The way down, through the vestry passage, is already ankle-deep.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that went straight down to the crypt in Scene Two meets these signs there: the bell rings as they descend, the water rises behind them on the stair, and Godric stands in the passage asking to be let past. If someone has answered the dead, the bell rings for them, and the drowned come for that person wherever the water reaches; the only escape is downward, to the source. A party that tries to leave by the embankment finds the landward door of the mound under water and the drowned standing in it.

## Scene Four: The Flooded Crypt

**Purpose.** Confrontation and revelation. Show the Keeper, the bell and the sluice, and let the party use what it learned.

**Situation.** The vestry passage leads down a wet stair to the bell-chamber, a narrow stone room where the house's bell hangs from an oak beam over a shaft that drops into the crypt below. The bell-rope hangs down the shaft into dark water. Beyond the chamber an iron gate, wrought as a black gate wound with bare vines, opens on the crypt itself, a long vault half-flooded with sea-water, lined with niches of the dead, most of them empty. At the far end, at the outfall of the drainage cut, stands the iron gear of the sluice, its wheel rusted but whole. In the middle of the water, waist-deep, stands **Keeper Leofwald**, in his black, holding the bell-rope.

**NPCs and threats.** **Keeper Leofwald** is grave, gentle and patient. He speaks the older speech of the house: "Thou art come to keep the house with us. Fear not. The water is cold but a little while." He believes he saved the Levels, and he believes he is saving them still. He rings the bell, and the drowned rise from the water and come. He cannot be talked out of his work, but he can be talked to, and while he is talking he is not ringing. Close to, his face does not quite hold: the courtesy is a garment, and it slips. The **drowned congregation** come up from the water at the bell, drag whoever they reach into the deep part of the crypt, and hold them under.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ destroy Leofwald. He is a tereb and hard to put down, and in the water his drowned stand round him. A party that drags him onto dry stone, or fights him after the crypt is drained, has the better of it.
- _Break the bell:_ cut the bell-rope or bring down the oak beam that holds the bell. The bell drops down the shaft into the crypt and is silent under the water. The drowned, undriven, stop where they stand and slowly sink. Leofwald is left alone, and is only a dead man in the water.
- _Reach the sluice:_ fight or wade to the sluice-gear at the far end and wind the gates open. At the ebb this drains the crypt to the outfall over a long count; at the flood it does nothing but let more water in. The drainage plans or the sluice-book give the timing, and the party needs the tide to have turned. As the water falls, the drowned fall still where it leaves them.
- _Talk:_ speak the true rite. Mildith, or anyone who has the struck lines from the rite-book or the lintel, speaks the rite as it was before Leofwald changed it. Each damut it is spoken over goes still. Spoken over Leofwald with the right name, it unmakes him.
- _Cleverness:_ the altered rite's call and answer. Leofwald asks, "Who keepeth the house?" Someone who answers with the true rite's words instead of "I keep it" turns the call against him.
- _Giving something up:_ give Leofwald what he wants, a brother, for a time. Somebody who goes into the water and lets him take their hand holds his attention while the others work, at real risk of drowning.

**Clues and rewards.** Leofwald, if spoken with, tells his story in his own words, and the party understands the bell and the water at last. The bell is silver-bronze, worth a great deal and too heavy to carry.

**Consequences.** If Leofwald is destroyed or unmade, the drowned go still and stay still, and the house is quiet. If only the bell is silenced, the dead stand idle in the water and Leofwald waits; a bell can be hung again. If the crypt is drained and he survives, he waits in the mud at the bottom, and he speaks the rite over each new body the sea brings in.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes down unprepared, with neither the rite nor the plans, still has the bell-shaft above: the bell-loft ladder runs down the shaft to the beam, and cutting the rope from above is the quickest way to silence the house. A party that flees the crypt waits out the tide in the bell-loft; at the ebb the water drains from the house, the dead fall still wherever the water left them, and Leofwald stays below until the next flood. In the dry hours between, the treasure and the way out can both be reached.

## Scene Five: The Drained Chamber

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Pay out the treasure, resolve the house, and send the party back toward the living.

**Situation.** With the crypt drained, or at the ebb, the vault lies open: wet black stone, the bodies of the drowned lying where the water left them, and in the niches above the old flood line, sealed with lead and wax, the house's treasures. Through the outfall the grey of dawn shows across the flats. The landward door of the mound opens on the maintenance embankment, a long overgrown bank that runs back toward inhabited country.

**NPCs and threats.** Mildith, if she lives, wants to speak the rite over every body in the crypt, which takes until the next flood. Aldgar, if he lives, wants his son's body taken home.

**Player choices.**

- _Take everything:_ the silver vessels, the chest and the reliquary are heavy, and the embankment is long and broken in places. The tide comes back in hours.
- _Keep faith:_ return the reliquary to its family, the chest to the commission, the vessels to the faith. Each has a claimant who pays for its return.
- _Stay:_ help Mildith lay the dead to rest. The house is quiet afterward, and the bell rings for nobody.
- _Bargain:_ the commission and the faith both want the endowment chest and both claim it.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **house's silver vessels**, the vessels of its rites, in a sealed niche.
- The **endowment chest** of the commission of sewers, holding rolls, deeds and a quantity of coin from the years it was paid.
- The **reliquary** of Lúsinía's healers, gold and set with stones, sealed in a niche above the old flood line. Its family holds that a sick person who touches it in faith mends faster, and it is plainly worth more than the rest together.
- From the guest cells, the travellers' purses and the pilot's compass.

**Consequences.** The party leaves along the embankment with the morning, toward the first dry grazing and the smoke of a steading.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that leaves at the ebb without the treasure can return at the next ebb by the embankment, which it now knows. If Leofwald still stands, the dead are still in the crypt, and the bell still rings at the flood; the reliquary's niche is above the flood line and can be reached by the bell-shaft at any tide.

## Aftermath

- **If Leofwald is unmade and the dead laid down,** the faith of the Silent Judge sends a Threshold Keeper to the mound, and the Levels hear the bell again only when one of their drowned is found.
- **If only the bell is silenced,** the fowlers stop hearing it and think the house is clear. It is not, and the next person to hang a bell in the shaft finds out.
- **If the endowment chest goes to the commission,** its long dispute ends in a judgement nobody likes, and the sluice is worked again.
- **If the party fled,** the bell goes on ringing, and the Levels lose a few more each spring tide.
