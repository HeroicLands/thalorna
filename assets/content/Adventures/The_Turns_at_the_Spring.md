## Overview {#overview}

The [[affiliation-ekunda|Ékunda Tribe]] keep a spring in the [[place-sthrnsvnhs|Southern Savannahs]] that has never dried, and every clan of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]] waters its cattle there in a bad year. This dry season the [[affiliation-ruwozi|Ruwozi]] and the [[affiliation-kolembozi|Kolembozi]] are at war over the rivers. Each clan has asked the [[being-springkeeper|Keeper of the Spring]] to turn the other's herders away, and the elders have refused both. They keep the two clans to separate watering days, and the Keeper closes the spring to anyone who sheds blood at it.

The [[affiliation-greatlubemo|Great Lubemo]] gathers at [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]] this year, and the [[being-ekundadelegat|Ékunda Delegate]] carries the elders' answer to it: the open water, and nothing more, since the delegate may bind the tribe only with the elders' agreement. Both clans' claims on the spring must be entered into the record of the Lubemo, and the [[affiliation-lobwezi|Lobwezi]] griots keep that record. [[being-bonzimbelbwz|Bonzimbe of the Lobwezi]], the blind senior griot of the Long Pact, means to hear the elders' answer at the spring before the sitting, and he cannot travel without help.

The party enters as the griot's escort, as a witness for the Lobwezi paramount, or as guests of the spring. It reaches [[place-ekundavlg|Ékunda Village]] in the week a Ruwozi household and a Kolembozi household come to water there.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The separate days.** The elders keep the turns. The Ruwozi water on their day and the Kolembozi on theirs, travelers and Sengala traders have their own hours, and the night is nobody's so the pools can fill. The tribe's peace holds inside the village because both clans need the water more than they need the quarrel.

**The brother.** The Ruwozi household that comes for its day is the household of the herder killed at a Kolembozi landing, the killing that turned the feud into a war. His younger brother, the [[being-ruwozibrthr|Brother of the Slain Herder]], drives the herd. He has heard that a Kolembozi household waters on the day after his, and he means to stay past his turn and meet it at the pools. To him every Kolembozi household is the one that killed his brother.

**The Kolembozi household.** It comes from a landing below the reach where the killing happened, a landing the turned channel left dry. Its cattle are at Ékunda because its own water is gone, and its people believe, as their clan does, that another hand cut the channel. They had nothing to do with the killing.

**The rule of the spring.** The Keeper closes the spring to anyone who sheds blood at it. The elders have not said whether that means the one who struck, his household or his clan. Both clans' camps at Heto Lubemo want it read as widely as possible if the other side sheds the blood, because a clan shut out of Ékunda in a bad year has lost its last water. Blood at the spring this week forces the elders to answer, and their answer goes to the Lubemo as the tribe's brief.

**The griot.** Bonzimbe wants the elders' answer entered as the Ékunda's claim before the sitting, in his own words and in front of a witness from outside the clans, so that neither warring clan can say the Lobwezi took it down in the other's favor. He carries the terms of the Pact, the genealogies of the five paramounts and the judgments of twenty-three Lubemos, and his memory is the only complete copy. Three apprentices, each a [[being-griotappren|Griot's Apprentice]], are learning it, and none has the whole. He is old and blind, and the country between the clans is at war; the escort is not a courtesy.

**The chieftain.** [[being-ndowelaeknd|Ndowela nu Ékunda]] wants the spring kept for every clan and the tribe's neutrality seen as a service. The elders run the village more than she does, and she has no way to enforce the spring's peace except the clans' own need of it. She wants an outsider on watch beside the [[being-tradewarden|Warden of the Trading Ground]] through the dry week.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The spring is the Pact's third term made into a place: the grass, the rivers and the rains are shared. The scenario asks whether the Ékunda can keep it shared while two of the five are at war. The party's work is to keep the week bloodless long enough for the elders to answer that question themselves.

> [!WARNING]
> The brother is grieving and young, and he is not a villain. A party that disarms him in front of his herders shames his household, and his lineage answers for that at Heto Lubemo. A party that lets him reach the pools at first light gets blood at the spring, and the elders then answer under pressure from both camps.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The escort or soldier.** Bonzimbe needs a party to bring him and two apprentices to the spring and then to the enclosure, without saying whose escort they are.
- **The record-keeper.** An apprentice of [[being-bonzimbelbwz|Bonzimbe of the Lobwezi]] cannot recall the settlement of a feud between two reaches, and Bonzimbe wants it recovered from a Kolembozi griot who keeps a second account.
- **The guest or wanderer.** Ndowela wants an outsider on watch at the spring beside the Warden of the Trading Ground in the week both clans come to water.
- **The courier.** The Ékunda delegate needs a trusted traveler to carry the elders' brief to Heto Lubemo and bring back the place the Lubemo hears it in the order of business.

## The Opening {#opening}

[[place-lombesavlg|Lombesa]], the Lobwezi paramount's seat, under the great baobab at the village's center. An old man with clouded eyes sits against the trunk with three young people around him, and one of them is reciting a line of descent under her breath and stumbling. He stops her with a hand.

> "Again, from the grandmother," Bonzimbe says. Then, to the party, without turning his head: "You are the ones who walk. Good. I am the one who remembers, and I cannot do both."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Road with the Griot

**Purpose.** Meet Bonzimbe and the weight he carries.

**Situation.** The road from Lombesa toward the spring through dry grass, with a blind old man, two of his apprentices and whatever the country holds in a war year: herders driving cattle away from the rivers, a Ruwozi regiment's runners carrying word, and nobody who would harm a griot on purpose.

**NPCs and threats.** Bonzimbe, gentle, unhurried and amused, who answers a hard question with a story; the two apprentices, each of whom holds part of what he holds; the dry country itself, where a missed water is a danger to an old man.

**Player choices.** Travel slowly and safely, travel quickly, or ask Bonzimbe what the Pact says about the spring.

**Clues and rewards.** Bonzimbe recites the five terms of the Pact as [[lore-swearbaobab|The Swearing Under the Baobab]] keeps them, and says that none of the judgments he carries is a judgment on a war between two of the five.

**Consequences.** A party that brings him to the spring rested has a griot who speaks for it to every clan. A party that hurries him arrives with an old man who needs a day before he can sit with the elders.

**If bypassed.** The Lobwezi bring Bonzimbe to the spring themselves, a day later.

> [!TIP]
> Bonzimbe tests the apprentices on the road, aloud. Let the party hear one of them falter on a genealogy and Bonzimbe correct her without impatience. The party should understand, without being told, what is lost if he dies on the way.

### Scene Two: The Turns

**Purpose.** Arrive at the spring and meet both households.

**Situation.** Ékunda Village, at the spring that comes out of the rock and runs down into three cut pools and then into the reeds. The Ruwozi household has its day. The Kolembozi household waits outside the village for its own.

**NPCs and threats.** Ndowela, warm, shrewd and tireless; the Warden of the Trading Ground; the Keeper of the Spring; the brother and his herders, each herder a [[being-younglinrep|Young Lineage Representative]]; the [[being-kolhhldhead|Kolembozi Household Head]] and her people; [[being-sengalatrdr|Sengala Traders]] with bundles of rainforest goods, who see everything and say nothing.

**Player choices.** Take the watch beside the Warden, eat with the Ruwozi, cross to the Kolembozi camp, or stay with Bonzimbe.

**Clues and rewards.** The brother's herders let slip that he does not mean to leave at the end of his day. The Kolembozi household's head says where her household's landing is and that its water is gone.

**Consequences.** A party that eats with both households in one day is watched by both.

**If bypassed.** The party arrives as the Ruwozi day ends, with the brother still at the pools.

### Scene Three: The Night at the Spring

**Purpose.** The danger, and the story the spring tells.

**Situation.** Night at Ékunda. A water-warden tells [[lore-keeperspring|The Keeper of the Spring]] by the pools to whoever has watered there that day, as the Ékunda do. The night is nobody's turn. The brother is not in his household's camp.

**NPCs and threats.** The [[being-waterwarden|Water-Warden of the Spring]]; the brother, waiting in the reeds below the third pool; the Kolembozi household, who come to the pools at first light.

**Player choices.** Find the brother before dawn, warn the Kolembozi household, wake the Keeper, or put themselves between the reeds and the pools.

**Clues and rewards.** The story ends on a question and leaves it to the fire. A party that listens has heard the Ékunda's own account of what the spring is for.

**Consequences.** If the party stops the brother without shaming him, his household leaves at dawn with its honor, and the Ruwozi camp at Heto Lubemo hears that strangers kept the peace of the spring. If blood is shed at the pools, the Keeper closes the spring to the one who shed it, and the elders must say how far the closing reaches.

**If bypassed.** The brother meets the Kolembozi household at the pools at first light.

> [!TIP]
> The brother tells the party the truth if they ask the right way: he wants the man who killed his brother. Let a party that knows the Kolembozi household comes from a landing the channel left dry use it. Learning that these people lost their water too does not end his grief, and it gives him a reason to go home.

### Scene Four: The Elders' Answer

**Purpose.** The tribe decides its brief.

**Situation.** The elders sit in the shade by the spring with Ndowela, and Bonzimbe sits with them as the Lobwezi griot who enters their answer.

**NPCs and threats.** The elders, each an [[being-ekundaelder|Ékunda Elder]], who run the village; Ndowela, who wants a brief the tribe can keep; Bonzimbe, who asks questions and rules nothing; messengers from both clans' camps at Heto Lubemo, each a Young Lineage Representative, who arrive to press their claims.

**Player choices.** Speak as witnesses to the night, stay silent, or carry a clan's message to the elders.

**Clues and rewards.** The elders' answer, entered by Bonzimbe with the party as witnesses: the open water for every clan, the spring closed to whoever sheds blood at it and to nobody else. If blood was shed, the answer says what the closing reaches.

**Consequences.** A brief that keeps the spring open to both clans gives the Lubemo a place where the war has already been held to the Pact's third term. A brief that closes the spring to a clan gives that clan a grievance against the Ékunda as well.

**If bypassed.** The elders answer without witnesses, and each warring clan says the Lobwezi heard it wrongly.

### Scene Five: To the Wall

**Purpose.** The climax: the brief and the record reach the Lubemo.

**Situation.** The road to Heto Lubemo, the delegations' camps around the dry-stone enclosure, and the [[being-hetoguardian|Guardian of Heto Lubemo]] at the wall, who asks each arrival whose guest it is.

**NPCs and threats.** Bonzimbe; the Ékunda delegate; young lineage representatives of both warring clans, each a Young Lineage Representative, who know by now what happened at the spring.

**Player choices.** Enter as the griot's guests, as the delegate's, or not at all.

**Clues and rewards.** The claims on the spring are entered into the record of the Lubemo in Bonzimbe's words, and the party's names are entered as witnesses.

**Consequences.** The Ékunda delegate speaks for the open water at the sitting. When the war is heard as the first business, the paramounts hear that the spring has been held shared through the worst week of the dry season.

**If bypassed.** Bonzimbe reaches the enclosure with the Lobwezi's own help, late, and the claims are entered without the elders' answer.

> [!NOTE]
> The war heard at this sitting, and the channel whose turning began it, are the subject of [[scenario-cnvnrspkslst|The Convener Speaks Last]]. The two scenarios share their Great Lubemo and can be run together, with this one first.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The brother waits in the reeds and meets the Kolembozi household at the pools at first light. A herder of the dry landing is wounded or killed. The Keeper closes the spring to the brother and his household, and both clans' messengers press the elders to close it to the other's clan. The elders refuse and refuse to say more. Bonzimbe, arriving a day late, enters a brief that answers nothing, and at Heto Lubemo the Kolembozi say a Ruwozi has shed blood at the one water the Pact still shares.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The spring stays open, because the clans need it, and the elders' answer stands as the Ékunda's brief for the next four years. If the week passed without blood, the Ékunda delegate has a claim no paramount can object to, and Ndowela's tribe is seen as the service she wants it seen as. Bonzimbe reaches the enclosure whole, and the Pact's record with him. The party is known to the Lobwezi as people who carried the memory, and to two households of two warring clans as the strangers who stood at the water.
