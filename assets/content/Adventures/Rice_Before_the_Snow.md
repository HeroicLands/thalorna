## Overview {#overview}

[[place-zenjai|Zenjai]] is the cold north of the great island, and the winter before this one it came through on half rations. [[being-zenjaitsuiwo|Zenjai Tsuiwo]], who rules it from [[place-buiwu|Buiwu]], knows the cost of that winter village by village. He raises the northern levy at four men to the hundred bales, 1,520 in all, and he keeps no fleet. He wants the granaries of the north full before the first snow, and that matters more to him than who holds the office of Teng Ja.

The office stands vacant, and the balance of the Nine rests on two houses. If the [[affiliation-zenjaidmn|Zenjai Domain]] or [[affiliation-wojaidmn|Wojai Domain]] joins either bloc, the balance ends. [[being-henzoshuiwo|Shuiwojai Henzo]] of the [[affiliation-shuiwojaidmn|Shuiwojai Domain]] and [[being-reweihojujai|Hojujai Rewei]], regent of the [[affiliation-hojujaidmn|Hojujai Domain]], have each named a price to Zenjai, and each price is in rice. Tsuiwo means to sell his choice for grain, not for titles.

The party enters as hands hired by either bloc to carry rice to Buiwu, as counters Tsuiwo himself sends to learn what the bidders hold, or as the [Maiju]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}'s guests at the stable-yard when a roll comes up short.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The bargain.** Tsuiwo commits his levy to whichever bloc delivers grain to Buiwu before the snow. A bloc that delivers owns him, and he has not decided how to prevent it. A party hired by either side to carry the rice can tip the realm, and he knows it.

**The bids.** Both blocs are bidding in rice they may not have. Shuiwojai holds a great deal of silver and an assessment of 78,000 bales, and its granary at [[place-hawu|Hawu]] is the lord's four parts in ten. Hojujai's granary above [[place-yanwu|Yanwu]] is smaller, and its regent has not shown her own steward, a [[being-jnchsteward|Jenchai House Steward]], the figure she named. Neither granary holds what its bloc promised. The referee decides how short each falls, and the table finds out by going to see. A granary may hold the rice on paper only, or hold part of it, or hold it where it cannot be moved before the snow. A bloc can still put a smaller load into Buiwu by buying on the quay or stripping a fief of its stores, and each of those costs it silver or a lord's goodwill.

**The horses.** The Maiju keep the horse pastures and mount Tsuiwo's officers, and every rider answers to a roll he can recite. A [[being-maijuofficer|Maiju Horse Officer]] has reported mounts missing from the levy. Tsuiwo wants them found before the bidders learn how few he can field, and the missing horses may be strayed, sold or taken. The referee decides.

**The two lords.** Tsuiwo is stern and practical, and he treats a promise as a debt not yet paid. [[being-wojaidoja|Wojai Doja]], the other lord whose choice ends the balance, answers his own bidders with a patience Tsuiwo lacks, and Tsuiwo counts Doja a rival and has no patience for the delay, and a party that mentions him hears it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scenario is a race against the first snow. Nobody lies about wanting the north's spears, and nobody has told the whole truth about the rice. Play the count and the carrying as two halves of one choice.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The real rice.** Tsuiwo commits his levy to whoever delivers grain to Buiwu before the snow, and a party hired by either bloc to carry it can tip the realm.
- **The empty granaries.** He sends a party to count what Shuiwojai and Hojujai actually hold, because both bids may be in rice that does not exist.
- **The horse roll.** A Maiju officer reports mounts missing from the levy, and Tsuiwo wants them found before the bidders learn how few he can field.
- **The regent's steward.** Rewei's steward at Yanwu has not been shown the figure she named, and he asks a party to learn what it is before the granary is asked to meet it.

## The Opening {#opening}

Buiwu in the first week of the cold, with a skin of frost on the hall's terrace. Two stewards in two houses' colors stand at opposite ends of it with their hands in their sleeves, and in the yard below them a man with a notched tally is counting horses with his lips moving.

> "The north does not eat promises," says Tsuiwo from his seat, without looking at either of them. "Which of you is first?"

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Hall at Buiwu

**Purpose.** Hear both bids, and learn what the north needs.

**Situation.** The lord's hall on a terrace above the streets of Buiwu, a town of 2,800 people on the lower slope of Zenjai. Snow lies on its roofs from the first frost to the thaw, and the granary below the hall feeds it in winter. Tsuiwo sits with his hands on his knees in fur-lined lacquered armor.

**NPCs and threats.** Tsuiwo, blunt and short of speech, who lost the lobe of an ear to frost. The stewards of the Strait and of the loyalists, who each have a price in rice and a deadline. The town, which waits with the hall.

**Player choices.** Take a bloc's commission to carry rice, take Tsuiwo's to count what the blocs hold, ask what the half-ration winter cost, or ask what he would sell for something other than grain.

**Clues and rewards.** Tsuiwo names the number: grain in his granaries before the snow, and he will stand behind the one who gives it. A party that asks what he would sell for anything else hears that there is nothing else.

**Consequences.** The party leaves with a commission from one side, or from Tsuiwo, and the other bloc's steward knows.

**If bypassed.** Tsuiwo sends his own men south to count, and the party hears of the result at the first inn on the shoulder road.

### Scene Two: The Stable-Yard

**Purpose.** Learn how many riders the north can really mount.

**Situation.** The stable-yard below the hall, where the Maiju enter the horses that come down from the high pastures. A Maiju officer has found a short count on the horse roll, and the roll is the one document in Buiwu Tsuiwo does not want a bidder to read.

**NPCs and threats.** The Maiju officer, who has not slept. The grooms, who say little. The two stewards, who are looking for a reason to be in the yard.

**Player choices.** Count the horses against the roll, ask the grooms where the missing mounts went, follow the carters who left for the south last week, or tell Tsuiwo what the roll says.

**Clues and rewards.** The referee chooses where the horses went. A trail of frozen dung leads to the shoulder road in any case, and the carters took charcoal and horses together.

**Consequences.** The number of mounts the north can field is a fact one bloc may learn first. The bidders' prices follow it.

**If bypassed.** The Maiju officer reports the shortfall to Tsuiwo and the stewards hear of it in the hall.

### Scene Three: The Granary at Hawu

**Purpose.** Count what Shuiwojai holds.

**Situation.** Hawu, the strait-coast castle town of Shuiwojai. Behind the quays stands the granary where the lord's four parts in ten are delivered, and the hall where Henzo's retainers sit by rank. The silver is plain to see; the rice is behind a door.

**NPCs and threats.** Henzo, hearty and calculating, who speaks to envoys as a creditor and who tells the party the rice is in the granary. His retainers, who open the door only when he says to.

**Player choices.** Ask Henzo to open the granary, count the carts that leave it, ask the shipwrights about hulls bound for the north, or buy rice on the quay and see what it costs.

**Clues and rewards.** The count falls short of Henzo's word, by the amount the referee has chosen. A party that wants the number gets it by a count of the sacks, the carts or the barges and not by a lord's word, and the quay price of rice shows what making up the shortfall would cost.

**Consequences.** Henzo's granary cannot meet his promise. What he sends reaches Buiwu short and on time, short and late, or not at all, and the party carries the number back to Tsuiwo or to a bloc.

**If bypassed.** Henzo's steward reports the granary full and Tsuiwo does not believe him.

### Scene Four: The Granary Above Yanwu

**Purpose.** Count what Hojujai holds, and see what the regent has not told her own household.

**Situation.** Yanwu, the bathing town on the slope of [[place-hojai|Hojai]], where the steam of the springs lies over the streets and the mountain rumbles in the night. Rewei holds the keys of the castle and its granary for her son, and the rice of the ash-soil terraces of [Fanu]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} and [Weinu]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} comes in by the mountain road.

**NPCs and threats.** Rewei, grave, exact and stubborn, who holds the house by being seen to hold it. Her steward, who has not been shown the figure. The Shuiwojai envoy in the town, a [[being-jnchagent|Jenchai House Agent]], who sends word to Hawu after every tremor.

**Player choices.** Ask Rewei for the figure she named, ask the steward what the granary holds, ask a [[being-smtshrntnch|Summit Shrine Tanchei]] of Hojai what the mountain says about the terraces, or watch the envoy.

**Clues and rewards.** The granary holds less than the regent named, by the amount the referee has chosen, and her steward finds the gap before she admits it. A tremor in the night may close the mountain road and strand the carts.

**Consequences.** What the loyalists can send reaches Buiwu, short of the promise, or it does not. The Strait learns of it from its envoy whether or not the party tells him.

**If bypassed.** Rewei sends a rider to Tsuiwo with her figure and he weighs it against the one he heard from Hawu.

### Scene Five: The Shoulder Roads

**Purpose.** Carry rice to Buiwu before the snow, or watch it fail to arrive.

**Situation.** The shoulder roads between the south and Buiwu, which close with the first snow. Carters bring charcoal and horses down them and rice up. The cold is real and the first snow is a day off.

**NPCs and threats.** The carters, who take the road because they have no other. The weather. A rival party of the other bloc's men, each a [[being-tsaihwosldr|Tsaihwo Soldier]], if the referee chooses, carrying the other rice.

**Player choices.** Race the other party, share the road, drop part of the load to get through, or turn back with the rice and tell Tsuiwo it cannot arrive.

**Clues and rewards.** A load that reaches Buiwu in time eases the north and earns Tsuiwo's notice, though it is less than was promised. A load that does not is a promise, and Tsuiwo treats a promise as a debt not yet paid.

**Consequences.** Tsuiwo names the bloc whose load did most for his province, or names none. The balance holds or it ends.

**If bypassed.** The snow closes the road. Whichever bloc has rice in Buiwu by that morning has Tsuiwo, and nobody else does.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Both blocs send rice, and neither sends enough. The first snow closes the shoulder roads with the granaries of the north half empty, and Tsuiwo's levy sits in its villages and eats what the villages have. The north comes through a second winter on half rations. A lord who has been promised grain twice and given it once sells his spears to the bloc that comes closest, and the one that fell short has lost the Independents' balance for a year.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The Strait's load arrived.** Short of what Henzo promised, it is the larger of the two, and Zenjai joins Shuiwojai and Rinjai. The Strait bloc outweighs the loyalists. The loyalists' claim rests on a boy and a mountain that has begun to shake.
- **The loyalists' load arrived.** Short of what Rewei promised, it is the larger of the two. Zenjai joins Hojujai and Muwojai, and Henzo's silver has bought the court a debt and nothing else. Henzo remembers who carried the rice.
- **Neither load arrived.** Tsuiwo's spears stay in the north. Both blocs have lost a season and Wojai remains the one house that can end the balance.
- **The horse roll read.** A bloc knows how few riders the north can mount, and its next price is lower.
