## Overview {#overview}

A winter storm turns an old block in [[place-oldmole|The Old Mole]] and exposes a carved face. [[being-ralthannrulth|Ralthann Rultharuk]], the master mason, wants the gap wedged before another storm. [[being-marvindnalth|Marvinda Naltharuk]], [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|The Order]]’s factor, offers to pay for time to take copies. [[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]] has granted neither request because the Council forbids moving the old stones.

The party must make the copying and repair practicable or choose which work to put first. The carving’s meaning remains unknown. Access to the carving depends on keeping a working harbor safe.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The block shifts under the storm’s force. Ralthann’s concern is sound, and a delay exposes the upper work to another heavy sea. Marvinda cannot read the carving and wants a faithful record before it is covered. A rubbing or drawing preserves evidence; removing a carved fragment destroys its context and violates the standing order.

[[being-krildtvlgr|Krildthira Tvulgaruk]] keeps the closed ledge and the chain tower under the Harbormaster’s authority. [[being-flarnulthd|Flarnthann Ulthidrok]] knows the quay’s ropes and berths and can work the lifting lines with his dockers if paid. [[being-snurvdrskr|Snurvinda Druskaruk]] can give the weather and tide observations from the Lantern; she cannot promise the sea will hold for a scholar.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Marvinda hires a party to take copies at the next low spring tide before the mason covers the face.
- Ralthann needs hands and a safe line below the block so the wedge can be set without losing a worker.
- Hrundselda asks outsiders to inspect both proposals and bring her a timetable she can actually authorize.

## The Opening {#opening}

> A wet line darkens the old stone above your head. Below it a row of cut marks runs toward the water. A mason lays a wedge on the quay; beside him a factor unrolls clean cloth. At the stair the watch holds the way shut.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### The two petitions

Ralthann and Marvinda meet at the root of the Mole. Each can describe the work and what a delay costs. The mason needs to secure the settled block and rebuild the upper courses; the factor needs access to the newly exposed underside. Neither needs the party to pretend the Council’s order does not exist.

**Choices and evidence.** Inspect the damaged upper work from the quay, compare the petitions, and ask what portion of the copying can be completed before the first wedge is driven. A proposal that simply says “wait until finished” leaves the harbor’s risk unbounded. A proposal naming a low-water interval gives Hrundselda something to decide.

**If bypassed.** The party can attempt the night ledge, but meets the same tide with no lawful work party above it. The watch treats that as unauthorized entry, not evidence of scholarly merit.

### Plan the descent

At the Lantern, Snurvinda names the tide’s turn and the signs of the incoming weather. At the inner quay, Flarnthann shows which bollards can hold the lines without obstructing a berthed hull. Ralthann points out the upper stones that must not carry a person’s weight.

**Choices and clues.** Arrange a line from sound work, keep a watcher above and decide which signal orders a retreat. Prepare duplicate copying materials so a dropped sheet does not require beginning again. None of this deciphers the writing. It makes it possible to preserve it and return.

**If bypassed.** The wet line, the sea at the head and the settling upper courses remain visible warnings. Describe the consequences before a player commits weight to loose stone.

### Obtain a particular leave

Krildthira needs an instruction from Hrundselda to open the night ledge. Hrundselda can permit inspection and copying that moves no old stone, and can order repairs to the city’s upper work. Whether wedging the settled old block breaches the Council’s prohibition remains a matter she must answer to the Council. She can call a sitting rather than claim a power she does not have.

**Choices and consequences.** Seek a limited copying interval, carry the issue to a Councilor for a sitting, or help Ralthann work without leave. The last choice may protect the harbor and still bring a charge for moving an old block. The party knows the conflict before acting.

**If leave is refused.** A daylight inspection at low water can still show what is at stake. A refused party may withdraw, appeal or trespass; no invisible wall forces the covert route.

### The exposed face

At low water the ledge reaches the turned face. The outer swell breaks below it, and spray comes over the lettering before the tide itself reaches the workers. Ralthann can work above while a copy is made below, provided both crews agree when the line must be cleared. A wedge set without that signal can crush the copying frame or strand its holder.

**Choices and evidence.** Make a broad record of the entire face before refining individual marks. Take a second impression or drawing from another angle to distinguish a cut stroke from a crack. If water rises early, save the people and the first copy; an incomplete honest record is still usable. Cutting out a piece is slower, breaches the order and risks the block’s stability.

> [!TIP]
> Put the tide in visible changes: a submerged foothold, a wet edge of cloth, spray reaching the worker’s hand. Give the party a chance to shorten its work or retreat before a wave makes the decision.

**If the party falls.** The safety line and the watcher determine whether companions can haul the worker in. Without them, rescue must come from a boat or the lower stair while the current carries the person toward the harbor mouth. A rescue can cost the copy without ending the adventure.

### Hand over a record, then repair

Marvinda receives a copy with the position and orientation of its marks recorded. Ralthann shows what remains to be braced and rebuilt. Hrundselda wants witnesses to what was moved, if anything, and how long the works were delayed. The party can give each an accurate account or conceal an unauthorized cut.

**Rewards and choices.** Marvinda pays for a legible record. Ralthann pays for safe work. A copy left with the city keeps the evidence available even if the Order’s copy travels away. The party can recommend further study, but cannot answer the mole’s origin from marks nobody can read.

**If the face is covered first.** The harbor can still be made safe. The party records the work and what could be seen; Marvinda’s full commission remains unfulfilled. An honest partial payment or a refused fee follows the agreement made at the start.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Ralthann presses to wedge the block, Marvinda presses for time and the watch keeps the ledge closed. Another storm enlarges the repair unless the city authorizes work. The carving can be covered before a full copy exists.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A coordinated job preserves the carving’s record and the harbor’s protection, leaving interpretation for scholars. A covert job may achieve the same physical result and leave the party answerable for trespass or damage. A removed fragment is a disputed object whose original position must be reconstructed from the party’s records.
