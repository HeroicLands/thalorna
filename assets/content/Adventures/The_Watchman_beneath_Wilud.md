The dunes of the Khoshiqor have uncovered a street of [[place-wilud|Vülüd]], and at its end a counting-house tower of the [[lore-towercities|Tower League]] stands open for the first time in two thousand years. An Uruvol guide brings the party to it a day behind a rival crew. A sandstorm drives the party inside, where it finds the rival crew's work half done, one of them crushed flat with nothing on top of him, and a stone watchman at the foot of the stair that is never quite where the party left it.

## Overview

The adventure runs over one storm, from noon to the next dawn. Its clock is the sand: from the moment the storm breaks, sand pours through the doorways and windows of the ground floor, the shaded entrance fills by nightfall, and the lower passages fill after it, until the only ways out are the service door, which the watchman is blocking, and the roof, which is in the storm.

Vülüd was abandoned, not massacred, and nothing in [[place-bolidqiso|Bolidqiiso]] is a ghost. The danger is the **watchman**, a [[lore-golemcrtr|construct]] of the league that still keeps the house by orders nobody can read. It cannot be frightened or bargained with. It can be understood, and every scene below hands the party a piece of how it works. Every scene gives several ways through and says how play carries on if the party skips, fails or cuts short what the scene offers.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Tāntsou collector paid Uruvolun for the news of the street and is a day behind; he has paid the party to go ahead of him and bring out the seals before anyone else does.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** A Byzarian factor bought the same news, and his own crew went in ahead. He hires the party to follow, find out why his crew has not come back, and bring out what they found.
- **The leader.** The party's company needs money, and Uruvolun sells it the news cheap because she has already sold it twice. She guides whoever arrives first, and the party is first of the paying buyers, but not the first into the street.
- **The lone wanderer.** One of the rival crew is the wanderer's kin, or owes the wanderer money, and went in two days ago without saying goodbye.

## The Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> Uruvolun brought you to the street at noon. It is a trench cut in a slope of pale dune, with roofless houses either side, walls whole to head height, and at its end a square brick tower four storeys high. A camel lies dead at the trench mouth, still hobbled, half covered already. The tracks of the people who rode it go down into the street and do not come back up.
>
> The wind turns while you descend. Sand pours over the walls behind you in long smoking falls, and the sky to the south goes the color of a bruise. Uruvolun points to the tower's doorway. It is your only shelter for a day's ride.
>
> Beside the doorway, half buried, stands a stone watchman, man-high, its hands held out in front of it. Its face is worn smooth. Its hands are polished bright.
>
> Its head is turned toward the fresh footprints going in.

## Scene One: The Shaded Door

**Purpose.** Entrance and guardian. Force the party inside, introduce the watchman and its first wrongness, and close the door behind them.

**Situation.** The storm arrives within the hour. The party goes in past the watchman at the doorway. The shaded entrance is a long vaulted hall, the only cool in the street, and in its hollow walls [[being-bnscrpn|bone scorpions]] wait in the shade. At the far end of the hall the inner stair goes up. When the party reaches it, with the scorpions behind it, the watchman is standing at the stair foot, facing the doorway, hands out, palms up. Back at the doorway, where it stood, there is a hollow in the drift the shape of its legs, and a line of square, deep footprints runs up the hall past the scorpions, which did not stir.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Uruvolun** is small and quick, with a face burned nearly black and a cord of tokens round her neck. She keeps the whole bargain: the party arrives, or she stays with it until it cannot. She knows the Khoshiqor, and she has been in one counting-house before. She tells the party that league buildings have doors their guardians will not pass, and she does not know which doors those are in this one.
- **The bone scorpions**, two or three, lie in the cool along the walls. Their dry rattle carries under the wind.
- **The watchman** does not move while anyone is looking at it. It walked up the hall while every eye in the party was on the scorpions.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ clear the scorpions from the hall. They fight until they cannot move.
- _Stealth:_ keep to the sunlit strip along the doorway side and step round their shade; Uruvolun knows the custom of watering on the sunlit side and leads.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ climb the outer wall to a first-floor window and come in above the hall, avoiding both scorpions and stair; this puts the party on the counting floor, with the watchman beneath it.
- _Giving something up:_ turn a pack camel loose into the doorway shade as bait, and pass while the scorpions take it.
- _Parley:_ none with the watchman. A party that speaks to it hears its own voice come back off the vault.

To pass the watchman on the stair, the party must squeeze between it and the wall, under its outstretched hands. It does not move.

**Clues and rewards.** Its hands are polished while everything else about it is weathered, and the polish is deepest in the palms, where signs are cut. Its head faces the footprints the rival crew left on the stair. Uruvolun's league-lore. A shred of a Byzarian traveling cloak caught on its fingers.

**Consequences.** Once the party is up the stair, the storm closes the street behind it. Sand pours through the doorway, and the shaded entrance begins to fill.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes in by the window meets the watchman later, from above, at the head of the stair it has climbed in the dark. A party that tries to wait the storm out in the street finds the trench filling to the knee within the hour and the walls beginning to slough; Uruvolun refuses to let her charge die in a trench and leads them in. A party that flees back up the dune loses the camels to the storm and is driven down to the doorway by the wall of sand.

## Scene Two: The Counting Floor

**Purpose.** Discovery. The rival crew's fate, the house's plan, and the first look at how the watchman kills.

**Situation.** The counting floor is a long room of stone benches and bronze balances, its pans still hanging, with broken seals scattered on the floor where the league's clerks broke them when an account was closed. Drag marks cross the dust from the stair to the far door, which opens on the weighing hall. On the end wall, scratched into the plaster in a clerk's hand, is an engineer's diagram of the house. Beneath a fallen table lies a treasure hunter of the rival crew, a Byzarian with a factor's brand on his wrist. He is crushed flat. The table lies across him, but the table is light, and his chest carries two marks the size of a man's palms, pressed so deep that the cloth has gone into the flesh.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The dead hunter**, who carries a broken seal in one hand, a crowbar in the other, and a list of what his factor wants.
- **The watchman**, unseen in this scene. Its tracks are: square, deep footprints in the dust, coming from the stair, going to the dead man and back. The party passed it at the stair foot not an hour ago.

**Player choices.**

- _Cleverness with the place:_ read the diagram. It shows the strongroom behind the weighing hall, the service door out to the court, the vaults under the weighing hall floor and the shafts the counterweights drop into. It does not show the lamp-stair, which belonged to the light and not to the house.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ read the wall panels by lamplight. One shows a merchant holding up a seal to a stone figure that lowers its hands. Another shows a figure laid in a balance-pan while the stone figure stands by.
- _Force:_ go straight through to the weighing hall, following the drag marks.
- _Stealth:_ sweep the party's own footprints out of the dust behind them, or walk only in the rival crew's tracks, on the chance that the thing reads tracks. It does.
- _Giving something up:_ leave the dead man's pack, which is heavy with brass weights, at the stair foot as a decoy, and see whether it is moved.

**Clues and rewards.** Its palms killed the hunter. It reads footprints. A whole seal turns it aside, and a broken one does not. The strongroom and the service door exist and lie past the weighing hall. The broken seals, though worthless, fit the panel. A handful of the bronze weights are the league's own, worth a collector's price.

**Consequences.** By now the shaded entrance is knee-deep in sand. When the party next looks down the stair, the watchman is no longer at its foot.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reads the diagram learns the house from Uruvolun, who has seen one other counting-house, or from the second looter in Scene Three, or from the weighing hall's own cracked floor and hanging chains in Scene Four. A party that never finds the dead man finds the drag marks again in the weighing hall, leading to the pan of the great balance, where his partner's shoes lie.

## Scene Three: The Moving Watchman

**Purpose.** The setback. The watchman is active, it is between the party and the way out, and the house is being closed.

**Situation.** Each time the party returns to a room it has left, the watchman is somewhere else: at the foot of the stair, then in the doorway of the counting floor, then at the head of the stair, facing whichever way the party's tracks lead. It is never seen to move. In a storage niche off the counting floor, behind a stone block that was not there before, a second looter is pressed into a space too small for him, alive, breathing in short shallow gasps because his chest cannot rise. Then, through the walls, the party hears stone scraping on stone from the far side of the weighing hall: the guardian is setting blocks across the service door.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Kesükenöd**, the second looter, a Sovidur hired hand of the rival crew. He is a broad man with a broken rib and a whole seal in his fist, which he pried from the strongroom's door-frame. While he held it up, the watchman passed him by and took his partner; when he dropped it to drag his partner free, it filed him in the niche. He has been there since yesterday. He saw it carry his partner to the balance and lay him in the pan. He saw it stop at the lamp-stair door and turn back.
- **The watchman**, closing the house.
- **The sand**, now waist-deep in the entrance hall, closing the front door.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ shift the block with bars and backs to free Kesükenöd; it takes time, and the watchman moves whenever nobody is watching it.
- _Stealth:_ keep one person's eyes on the watchman at all times, so that it cannot move, and work round it in relays while the others free the looter.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ lay false tracks across the counting floor to draw the watchman away from the weighing hall door; or leave a trail into the lamp-stair, which it will not enter.
- _Parley:_ get Kesükenöd's account. He talks freely once he can breathe.
- _Giving something up:_ leave Kesükenöd in the niche and go for the strongroom. He calls after the party as long as he has the breath.

**Clues and rewards.** Kesükenöd's whole seal, if the party frees him and he gives it, or if it is taken. His account of the seal, the balance, the palms and the lamp-stair. Uruvolun, hearing the counterweight chains rattle in their shafts through the wall, says that a league house that lifts goods on counterweights drops them too.

**Consequences.** The house is closed. From now on the watchman moves in plain sight, and it comes for whoever is inside without a seal. The front entrance is full of sand to the vault, and the only ways out are the service door past the weighing hall, the strongroom's goods-chute to the court, and the roof by the lamp-stair, which is in the storm and full of numaq at dusk.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never doubles back and never sees the watchman change its place meets it standing in a doorway behind them, between the party and the stair, facing their tracks. A party that never finds the niche hears Kesükenöd's breathing through the wall of the counting floor; if it still does not look, he is in the pan of the great balance in Scene Four, alive and unable to move. A party that hurries ahead to the service door before the blocks are set reaches it as the last block goes into place, with the watchman on the far side of it.

## Scene Four: The Weighing Hall

**Purpose.** Confrontation and revelation. The watchman hunts, and the hall gives the party the means to beat it.

**Situation.** The weighing hall is two storeys high, lit by slits under the roof through which the storm shows as a brown roar. A great beam-balance hangs from the roof, its two pans as wide as a cart and its pivot a block of bronze. Its counterweights hang in square shafts at the hall's sides on chains wound round a drum. The floor is flagged over vaults, and a crack runs across it from wall to wall where a flag has sunk. Sand is coming up through the crack from the flooded vaults below. Panels on the walls show the merchant with the seal, the figure in the pan, and the service door blocked against blown sand. The strongroom door is at the far end, and the service door beyond it is blocked. The watchman stands between them, and it is coming.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The watchman** walks straight at whoever is nearest and has no seal. It is slow, and it does not stop. It closes its palms on what it catches, or lays it in the pan. It cannot be hurt by a blade worth the name, cannot be frightened and cannot be argued with. It turns aside from a whole seal held up to it. It follows tracks across the dust. It stops at the threshold of the lamp-stair.
- **The sand**, rising through the crack, so that every minute in the hall is a minute of the lower floor filling.

**Player choices.**

- _Cleverness with the place:_ the damaged floor. Lure it onto the cracked flags, along a trail of footprints, and break them with a bar or a dropped weight; it falls into the sand-filled vault and is buried to the chest.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ the counterweights. Knock the pin from the drum and drop a counterweight down its shaft onto the watchman at the shaft foot, or lure it into the great pan and drop the counterweight, which hauls the pan to the roof with the watchman in it.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ the binding. Get under its hands, which it opens only to close them on someone, and break the signs in its palms with a chisel or a crowbar. A construct whose signs are broken stops, and this one stops with its hands still open.
- _Parley, in its own fashion:_ hold up a whole seal, and walk past it to the strongroom; it turns aside for the one who holds the seal and comes on for everyone else.
- _Force:_ fight it with crowbars and hammers. It is stone, and a party that does this is buying time, not winning.
- _Stealth:_ split, and keep one person in plain sight drawing it round the hall along fresh tracks while the others open the strongroom and the chute.
- _Giving something up:_ leave someone in its path, or offer it the dead hunter's body, which it takes to the pan and lays down; that gives the party the length of its walk.

**Clues and rewards.** The panels make the rules plain: the seal is a pass, the pan is where an intruder is weighed, and the closed door means the storm. They do not say what an intruder is weighed against.

**Consequences.** Buried, hauled aloft or stopped, the watchman no longer stands between the party and the strongroom. Buried or hauled, it is still bound, and a party that comes back to Bolidqiiso finds it working its way free. Stopped, it stands in the weighing hall with its hands open, a statue worth a great deal to a buyer who can lift it.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that comes into the hall unprepared can survive it by running for the lamp-stair, which it will not enter, and climbing to the roof and the storm; from there a rope down the tower's lee face reaches the court. A party that reaches the strongroom with the watchman still walking can bar the strongroom door behind it, which holds for as long as it takes the watchman to turn back to its blocks, and leave by the goods-chute. The treasure is in the strongroom either way.

## Scene Five: The Strongroom and the Court

**Purpose.** The treasure, and a way out into shelter.

**Situation.** The strongroom is small, dry and windowless. Its shelves hold engraved trade seals of the league in neat rows, each whole, and crates of tower-lamp glass packed in straw that crumbles at a touch. Under the flags, which ring hollow, is the league's silver in ingots stamped with the same mark as the seals. In the corner a goods-chute slopes down to a hatch in the court wall. Beyond the blocked service door is the court, its high walls keeping the dunes out, where the storm goes overhead and does not come down.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Uruvolun**, who keeps the whole bargain, and wants her fee.
- **Kesükenöd**, if he lives, who wants a share for the seal and for the account.
- **The rival buyer**, whose men may still be on the far side of the dune, or the party's own employer, a day behind.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ lift every flag and take every ingot. The silver is heavy, and the camels are lost or dead; a party that takes it all buries most of it in the court and comes back for it before the next sand-front.
- _Stealth:_ take the seals and the glass, which are light, and leave the silver for a second trip.
- _Cleverness with the place:_ clear the blocks from the service door from the court side, where the watchman cannot follow, and use the strongroom as the party's base until the storm ends.
- _Parley:_ settle with Uruvolun and Kesükenöd before leaving the strongroom, in front of the others.
- _Giving something up:_ leave a whole seal on the watchman's palm if it still stands, and walk out past it.

**Clues and rewards.** Silver ingots of the league, worth a fortune at [[place-qisomrktcmp|Qiiso]] and more at the coast. Engraved seals, each worth a collector's price, and the more so to a buyer who knows what one does to a watchman. Tower-lamp glass, the clearest anyone in the market has seen, valuable beyond its weight to a collector. A seal kept by the party turns aside any watchman of the league it meets again.

**Consequences.** The storm blows out by dawn. The street is half buried again, and the next sand-front closes it within a few years. Uruvolun sells the news of the watchman to both buyers, and the price of a whole seal at Qiiso doubles within a season.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reached the strongroom still reaches the court by the roof, and can come back for the treasure after the storm, when the watchman, if it still walks, has gone back to its stair and stands with its hands out and its head turned toward the door. A party trapped inside at dawn finds the storm over and the light through the roof-slits strong enough to see the lamp-stair door, which Uruvolun has been holding open.

## Aftermath

- **If the watchman is stopped.** The house is open. Buyers send crews; Uruvolun sells the street to each.
- **If it is buried or hauled up.** It is still bound and still the house's, and it works its way free within a season.
- **If the party has a seal.** Word goes round the market that a seal is a pass, and somebody tests it on a watchman at another counting-house in another street.
- **If Kesükenöd lives.** He owes the party a life, and he knows where the rival factor keeps his silver, which he sells to the party at a fair price.
- **If the party left the rival crew to the watchman.** The rival factor learns of it from Uruvolun, who sells that too.
