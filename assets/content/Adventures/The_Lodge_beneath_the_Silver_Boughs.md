An envoy carrying a sealed casket to [[place-eshalshldg|Eshálosha Lodge]] has gone missing on the road beside the [[place-hntrsrng|Hunter's Ring]], and the casket with him. Thieves pried the silver from the ring's boundary stones on their way through, and the stones they overturned have let the ring's hunter out of its bounds. A Warden takes the party in to find the envoy. The wood closes behind them, and the hunter takes the one who is alone.

## Overview

The Hunter's Ring is a ring of carved boundary stones on the outer margin of the [[place-silvrfrsts|Silver Forests]], holding the ground of a fae hunter the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] call _súldinno_. Inside the stones it may take whatever walks there; outside them it may take nothing. It takes one at a time, and always the one who is alone. It hangs what it takes in woven branches in a moonlit clearing at the ring's heart, and it walks in the shapes and calls in the voices of those it has taken.

The envoy **Sédrik Véldarin** was travelling the road under the escort of the Warden **Ástheren**, carrying a silver casket for the great council at Eshálosha. A band of lowland poachers under **Bélrik Óthral** robbed them on the road at dusk, took the casket, and fled uphill into the ring, prying the silver bands off the stones as they went. With four stones down, the hunter's ground has no edge. It took Ástheren first, then came back wearing him and led the envoy away. It has since taken most of the thieves. One poacher, **Móren Hálren**, is holed up in the old keepers' lodge with a wound in his side, and has not slept for two nights.

The Warden **Kírnala** of the Tírindi, Ástheren's partner on the road, has come to find them. She has sense enough to know that a Warden alone in the ring is prey, and the party is her company.

The supernatural stays partly unexplained. Nobody knows who set the stones, or why the hunter was given its ground, and the party does not need to.

## Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The Warden stops beside a silver-barked tree and lays her palm flat on its trunk. You watched her cut a blaze into that bark an hour ago, a quick downward stroke at shoulder height; the wood of it is still wet. You walked away from it. It is ahead of you again. Across the path, among the trunks, stands a long timber lodge with a stone chimney and no smoke. As you look toward it, a shutter closes from the inside.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each leads to the lodge with Kírnala.

- **The thief.** A fence in [[place-beravel|Béravel]] has paid poachers for silver bands of an old pattern and wants the rest of their haul, said to include a jewelled casket. He sends the thief to meet them on the envoys' road, and a Warden meets the thief first and offers a choice of company.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The royal house hires a few blades to support the Warden escort, since an envoy is missing and the crown wants its own witnesses. Kírnala takes them, and says plainly that they go in as her company or not at all.
- **The leader.** The envoy is the leader's kinsman, friend or rival, and the casket carries the crown's word to the Lodge. The leader goes to the Wardens' post on the road and asks, which is the only way a lowlander enters that country.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is on the road at dusk when a Warden steps out of the trees, looks at the wanderer for a long moment, and asks for company for one night. She does not say why one companion is better than none.

## Scene One: The Lodge

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Set the practical task, find the envoy and the casket, and show that something has already walked here wearing a familiar face.

**Situation.** The lodge stands at the ring's lower edge, long, low and old, with carved posts at the door. Its shutters are closed and barred. A thin line of light shows under the door. Inside, **Móren Hálren** sits by a banked fire with a boar-spear across his knees and a cloth pressed to his side. By the hearth hangs a Warden's grey-green cloak, pierced through the back by a single narrow hole and stiff with blood round it. Kírnala knows it at once: it is Ástheren's.

**NPCs and threats.** **Móren** is a lowland poacher in his forties, unshaven, feverish and more frightened than he has been in his life. He refuses to open to the Warden. Through the door he says that her companion has already been here: Ástheren came at dusk two nights ago, sat at this hearth, and the envoy went out into the trees with him. Móren saw Ástheren's face clearly in the firelight. He does not believe a Warden is at the door, and he does not want to find out what is. Kírnala is quiet and controlled. She does not know what to make of his story and does not like it.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ break the door or a shutter. Móren puts the spear through the first figure in the gap and then collapses. The lodge is open, and so is the way in for anything else.
- _Stealth:_ find another way in: the smoke-hole in the roof, the wood-store at the back, a loose plank under the eaves. Móren is watching the door and does not see the second entrance until it is used.
- _Talk:_ prove to Móren that they are what they seem. He asks for something only a living stranger would know or do: to name the place they slept last, to show a fresh cut that bleeds, to come to the door all together. A party that thinks to send one person alone to the door gets nothing; one that comes all together is let in.
- _Cleverness:_ Kírnala, prompted, asks Móren a question only Ástheren could answer, about the patrol they walked together in spring. Móren cannot answer it, but he heard the other Ástheren talk at the hearth, and what he repeats is wrong in the way that makes Kírnala go pale.
- _Giving something up:_ leave weapons at the door, as the poacher demands. He opens. The party is inside, unarmed for the moment, and the weapons are outside in the dark.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **cloak**, pierced through the back by something long and narrow, and the plain fact that Ástheren was dead or taken before "he" came to the hearth.
- **Móren's story:** the thieves broke the stones for the silver, one man was lost each night after, and each was lost when he stepped away from the others. He says it without knowing it matters.
- The thieves' trail: a pry-bar on the lodge step, crusted with moss and stone dust.

**Consequences.** Móren, if he is let live and treated decently, is a guide of a sort: he knows where the thieves went in. If he is killed or driven off, the party has lost the one witness who saw the hunter at the hearth. Either way, Kírnala now knows her partner is dead, and she does not leave.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that goes past the lodge into the trees finds that the path brings it back to the lodge within the hour, from the uphill side. A party that kills Móren finds his body gone from the floor by morning and the door still barred from inside; his voice calls to them in Scene Three all the same. The cloak can be found in any case: if the party never enters, Kírnala finds it at dawn, hung on a branch above the path where she will see it.

## Scene Two: The Journal and the Carvings

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Give the party the means to understand the hunter, from more than one source, while the source itself stays out of sight.

**Situation.** The lodge's inside walls are carved floor to rafter with the ring: a long loop of standing stones, each cut with its mark, a stream through the fold, and at the centre a great tree with small figures hanging in its branches. The poachers' gear lies about the room: bedrolls, a cookpot, a sack of silver bands pried from stone. Under one bed is a leather satchel holding the envoy's journal. Outside, along the ring's edge uphill, the party can find the stones themselves, four of them overturned in a line where the thieves passed.

**NPCs and threats.** Nothing shows itself. The party, walking out to the stones in daylight or by lantern, hears footsteps in the leaf-litter keeping pace with them just out of sight. When they stop, the footsteps stop. When one of them walks a few paces apart to look at a stone, the steps move with that one.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ go out after the footsteps in strength. The steps withdraw ahead of a group and follow anyone who falls behind. Nothing is caught.
- _Stealth:_ watch from cover by the overturned stones. A watcher who keeps very still sees, briefly, a tall shape between two trunks that is not where the eye leaves it.
- _Talk:_ press Móren for everything. He tells of the silver bands, the order the men were lost in, and a thing he saw on the second night: a man who ran to a standing stone and stood against it, while something waited in the dark three paces off and did not come closer. The man left the stone at dawn and was taken that night.
- _Cleverness:_ read the carvings and the journal together. The carvings show the mark-stones and their places; the journal shows which ones the thieves broke.
- _Giving something up:_ Kírnala knows parts of the Wardens' song of the ring. She sings them only if the party gives her something in kind: the truth about why each of them is here.

**Clues and rewards.** Each of the essential facts can be had more than one way.

- **The hunter takes the one who is alone.** From the journal, where the envoy notes that his two servants were each lost when they stepped from the fire. From Móren's account. From the footsteps, which follow whoever walks apart. From Kírnala's song: "nothing walks alone in the ring."
- **A standing stone is a refuge, and the stones are its bound.** From Móren's story of the man at the stone. From the carvings, which show the hanging tree inside the line and nothing outside it. From Kírnala's song: "nothing runs from it but toward a standing stone."
- **Four stones are down, and where.** From the journal's last entries. From the carvings, compared with the line in the wood. From the pry-marks and the silver in the sack, which match the stones' heads band for band.
- **It wears the taken.** From the cloak and Móren's story. From the journal's last line, in the envoy's hand: "Ástheren has come back for me. He is smiling."

The envoy's journal also records the footsteps that followed the escort for two days on the road, always stopping when the party stopped. The final pages are written in the dark and slant across the lines.

The sack of silver bands is what was pried from the stones. Without it a set stone has no band, and the carvings show every stone banded.

**Consequences.** The party knows enough to plan. The hunter knows the party is in its ground: from here on the footsteps are always there at the edge of hearing, and the party can stop to listen and hear them stop.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reads the journal or the carvings still meets the facts later: the envoy, alive in Scene Four, says the same things in a whisper; the standing stones in the wood carry the same marks as the lodge walls; and the hunter's own conduct in Scene Three teaches the rule about the one alone at a cost. If the sack of silver is left behind, Kírnala goes back for it, or the party finds a few bands dropped on the path where a thief fell.

## Scene Three: The Barred Room

**Purpose.** The horror draws close. Show that the hunter is active, that a locked room is no refuge, and that the way out is gone.

**Situation.** Night comes down. Móren, fevered, asks to be shut in the lodge's back room, which has one window, barred with oak, and a door with a bar on the inside. He wants walls between himself and the trees. The party settles in the main room. Some time after midnight, the back room goes quiet. The bar is still across the door. The window bars are intact. Móren is gone. A moment later his voice calls from above the roof, from somewhere high in the trees, asking them to come out and help him down.

At first light, Kírnala walks the party down to the envoys' road, intending to send for more Wardens. The road is not there. Every track out of the lodge bends uphill within a few hundred paces. Her blazes from the day before turn up ahead of her, one after another, all pointing inward.

**NPCs and threats.** The **hunter**, still not seen plainly: a tall, narrow shape glimpsed between trunks, too quiet for its size, gone when looked at. It calls in Móren's voice and in Ástheren's. If anyone is alone, even for a moment, even behind a closed door, it is there. Kírnala is the likeliest next victim: she is the one who walks ahead, scouting, because that is a Warden's habit.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ go out after the voice. The hunter lets the party find Móren's boots at the foot of a tree and nothing else, and takes whoever went round the far side of the trunk.
- _Stealth:_ stay inside, all together, back to back, and wait out the night. The hunter circles the lodge until dawn, calling. Nobody is taken. The morning brings the folded paths.
- _Talk:_ answer the voice and keep it talking. The hunter speaks Móren's words, not its own, and repeats what Móren said in the lodge, word for word, in the wrong order.
- _Cleverness:_ stand someone against a standing stone as a test, with the others watching from a group. The hunter comes to three paces off and stops. Now the party has seen the rule work.
- _Giving something up:_ leave a light or a pack alone in a clearing as bait, and watch. The hunter takes it up into the branches, and in the moment of taking it can be seen whole: tall, many-jointed, dressed in cloaks and gear not its own.

**Clues and rewards.** The rule of the one alone, proved at a price if it was not learned in Scene Two. The folded paths, which tell Kírnala that the stones must be set right before anyone leaves. The direction of the clearing, which every path now leads to.

**Consequences.** Withdrawal is impossible while the stones are down. Kírnala says so plainly: nobody leaves the ring until it is whole. If she is taken, the party loses its guide and her songs, and gains a new voice in the dark that knows their names.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that leaves the lodge before night is overtaken by the same events in the open: one of them, or Kírnala, walks a little ahead and is gone, and the voice calls from the canopy. A party that refuses to go inward finds the folded paths make the choice for it: every way leads to the clearing by moonrise. If Kírnala is taken and the party has no songs, the carvings on the standing stones in the wood still show the line, and the envoy in Scene Four knows the rest.

## Scene Four: The Clearing

**Purpose.** Confrontation and revelation. Show the source, make the earlier clues intelligible, and let the party use what it learned.

**Situation.** At the heart of the ring the trees open on a round clearing, silver under the moon. In the middle stands a vast tree whose lower branches are woven together into hanging cradles of bough and bark. In the cradles hang the taken: the envoy Sédrik, alive and silent; two of the thieves, one alive; Móren, if he was taken, alive; Ástheren, dead. Others hang higher, older, dry. The roots below are crowded with bones and with gear rotted to scraps. The casket hangs in a cradle with Bélrik Óthral's pack, three branches up. The hunter is here, among the trunks at the clearing's edge, moving when the party moves.

**NPCs and threats.** The **hunter** at last, seen whole: very tall, long-limbed and thin, its joints bending where no joint should be, wearing Ástheren's leather and a thief's hood over a face that cannot be held in the eye. It moves between the trunks without sound and comes out of the dark at whoever is apart. It does not take what stands within arm's reach of an upright mark-stone, and it does not cross a line of stones that is whole. It is hard to wound and harder to catch; blades find it, but it is gone before a second blow. Wounding drives it off for a time; nothing the party carries is known to kill it, and the Wardens do not want it killed.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ stand in a ring of their own, shoulder to shoulder, and fight it off while others cut down the captives. The hunter circles and strikes at whoever leaves the ring, and every captive cut down is another body to guard.
- _Restore the stones:_ take the silver bands back to the four overturned stones, set each upright and banded, while the rest hold off the hunter. The work needs the line from the carvings or the journal and the silver from the sack. Each stone set closes part of the ground. When the fourth stone stands, the hunter's ground is the fold inside the ring again, and anyone inside it is still within the hunt.
- _Lure it back:_ the hunter takes the one alone. One volunteer walks apart, inside the line, as bait, while the others set the last stone behind the hunter as it follows. The volunteer must then reach a standing stone before the hunter reaches the volunteer.
- _Cleverness:_ draw the hunter after a voice of its own: a captive's cloak on a pole, a lantern carried apart, a call. It follows its prey, and a lure buys the moments the stone-setters need.
- _Talk:_ the hunter does not bargain. It answers in borrowed voices and says only what its prey has said. Kírnala, if she lives, can sing the ring's song to it, and while she sings it does not move against her; she cannot both sing and work.
- _Giving something up:_ leave someone, or something, inside the ring when the last stone is set. A thief left hanging, or the casket itself, is the price of a quick ending. Kírnala does not forgive leaving a living person.

**Clues and rewards.** The envoy, cut down and alive, tells what he saw in a whisper: Ástheren's face, the long walk, the cradle, the hunter's habit of stopping when he stopped. The cradles hold the casket, still sealed; Bélrik's pack; and the gear of the hunter's earlier prey, much of it rotten and some of it not. The rule of the stones is now plain to anyone who sees the hunter stop at a standing one.

**Consequences.** Captives brought out before the last stone is set are free. Anyone still inside the line when it closes is the hunter's, and only an upright stone will keep them until dawn. If the ring is closed, the paths unfold by morning. If it is not, the hunter's ground keeps spreading along the broken line.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that arrives unprepared, without the line or the silver, can still survive: an upright stone at the clearing's edge, the fifth from the gap, gives a refuge for those who reach it, and the hunter goes still among the roots at moonset until the next night. In the dark between moonset and dawn the party can cut down the captives, take the casket, and walk out along the line of standing stones, which the hunter's ground does not reach while the moon is down. The stones stay broken, and the Wardens must set them later. A party that fled the clearing can come back the next night by the same folded paths, with what it lacked.

## Scene Five: Before Dawn

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Resolve the casket and the envoy, pay out the reward, and leave the ring as it was, or not.

**Situation.** The moon sets. The clearing is dark. If the stones stand, the paths are straight again by first light, and the envoys' road lies where it always lay, a short walk downhill. Kírnala, if she lives, leads the party out before dawn by the outside of the stones, a hand on each stone as she passes.

**NPCs and threats.** No threat but what was left undone. If the ring is still open, the folded paths hold until dawn, and the party must walk the line of standing stones from refuge to refuge to reach the road. Sédrik is weak and needs carrying.

**Player choices.**

- _Keep faith:_ give the casket to Kírnala unopened. She returns it to the envoy, and the envoy carries it to Eshálosha.
- _Open the casket:_ it holds the crown's word to the great council, a folded letter under seal, and a cloak-clasp of Áelendan silver work. The clasp is meant for the party's reward. Opening the casket is a breach that Kírnala reports.
- _Keep the silver:_ the bands from the stones belong to the stones. A party that kept some back has left a stone unbanded, and Kírnala knows which.
- _Bargain:_ Bélrik's pack is the thieves' haul from before the road: gemstones from a lowland strongroom and coin-silver. Kírnala lets the party keep it, since it is no part of the wood.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **casket**, recovered intact, if the party kept it so.
- **Bélrik's pack:** a pouch of cut gemstones and a weight of coin-silver stolen in the lowland, which Kírnala permits the party to keep.
- The **clasp** the Lodge sends for the party's service: a cloak-clasp of Áelendan silver cut with the mark of the ring's stones, a closed line with one gap. A Warden who sees it passes its wearer through Warden ground with a word. The Wardens' song says a thing of the wood that keeps the marks passes such a wearer by, and does not say more.
- The gear of the hunter's older prey, from the roots, if the party searched: an old knife of good steel, a rotted pack with a lowland coin-purse, a horn.

**Consequences.** The envoy reaches Eshálosha. The party is known to the Wardens of that stretch of road, for good or ill.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that fled the ring without the casket hears within the season that the Wardens set the stones and recovered it from the roots. The reward goes to whoever brought out the envoy. A party that left the ring open is not welcome on the road again.

## Aftermath

- **If the stones were set and the captives brought out,** the ring is whole, the paths are straight, and the Wardens owe the party. Móren, if he lived, does not come back to the lodge.
- **If the stones were set with someone still inside,** that person hangs in the clearing until the hunter tires of them, and the Wardens sing them into the song.
- **If the ring was left open,** the hunter's ground runs farther along the broken line each night, and the envoys' road is closed until the Wardens find enough company to set the stones.
- **If the casket was opened,** the crown's word to the Lodge reaches the council broken, and the crown hears from the Lodge about it.
