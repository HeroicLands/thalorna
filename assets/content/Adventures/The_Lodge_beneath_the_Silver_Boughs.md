An envoy carrying a sealed casket to [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] has gone missing on the road beside the [[place-hntrsrng|Hunter's Ring]], and the casket with him. Thieves pried the silver from the ring's boundary stones on their way through, and the stones they overturned have let the ring's hunter out of its bounds. A Warden takes the party in to find the envoy. The wood closes behind them, and the hunter takes the one who is alone. The one survivor in the keepers' lodge tells the party the hunter took the envoy's Warden first, and the hunter, which can only say what its prey said, tells the party something else.

## Overview

The Hunter's Ring is a ring of carved boundary stones on the outer margin of the [[place-silvrfrsts|Silver Forests]], holding the ground of a fae hunter the [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]] call _súldinno_. Inside the stones it may take whatever walks there; outside them it may take nothing. It takes one at a time, and always the one who is alone. It hangs what it takes in woven branches in a moonlit clearing at the ring's heart, and it walks in the shapes and calls in the voices of those it has taken.

The envoy **Devrik Véldarin** was traveling the road under the escort of the Warden **Ástheren**, carrying a silver casket for the great council at Ésalosa. A band of lowland poachers under **Vendrik Othral** robbed them on the road at dusk, took the casket, and fled uphill into the ring, prying the silver bands off the stones as they went. With four stones down, the hunter's ground has no edge. It has taken most of the thieves, and the envoy, and Ástheren is dead. One poacher, **Norven Halren**, is holed up in the old keepers' lodge with a wound in his side, and has not slept for two nights.

The Warden **Sírgana** of the Tírindi, Ástheren's partner on the road, has come to find them. She has sense enough to know that a Warden alone in the ring is prey, and the party is her company.

The supernatural stays partly unexplained. Nobody knows who set the stones, or why the hunter was given its ground, and the party does not need to.

The party learns how the hunter works from several sources, and each fact can be had more than one way:

## What Is Really Happening

The full truth of the ring—what the hunter is, what the Wardens' last verses say, and what happened at the lodge door—is in [[place-hntrsrng#truth|the truth of the Hunter's Ring]]. In short: Ástheren came to the lodge alive, at dusk two days before the party arrives, and Norven, who had already seen the hunter walk back in a dead thief's shape, put his boar-spear through the Warden's back. He dragged the body out and barred the door, and the hunter took what lay alone outside. Norven's story, that the thing at his hearth was already dead, covers the cloak hanging by his fire. The hunter can only repeat what its prey said, and Ástheren's last words were said to Norven.

This section covers what that truth does at the table: where the party can find it, how it lands on each route, and what the party wants once it knows.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Norven's account in Scene One is a lie wrapped round true facts. Everything he says about the stones, the silver and the lost men is so, and only the hearth is invented. The party must be able to find each true fact without him, so that the lie is the only thing that rests on his word alone.

### How the reveal lands

The reveal belongs in Scene Three, and it comes from the hunter's own rule. The hunter calls from the trees in Ástheren's voice and says what Ástheren last said. It calls the same words wherever the party is.

- **In the lodge.** The voice calls from the canopy above the barred back room where Norven lies, and Norven, behind the bar, starts to shout back at it.
- **In the open.** The voice calls from the branches over the party's heads, in the dark between it and the lodge, and whoever has Norven beside them feels him go rigid.
- **If Norven is already taken.** Ástheren's voice calls, and Norven's own voice answers it from higher in the branches with what he said at the door: "Stay off me. I watched it take Torlan, and Torlan came back."
- **If the party killed Norven in Scene One.** The same exchange of voices, from the trees above the lodge, and the party knows whom it has killed.

> [!TIP]
> Land the reveal on the voice and then stop talking. Say the two sentences in Ástheren's voice, the same way each time it calls, and let the players put the spear, the cloak and the sanded floor together for themselves. Sírgana's face does the rest.

> [!TIP]
> Reward a party that guesses early. A party that suspects Norven in Scene One or Two and puts the cloak, the scraped shaft and the journal to him gets the truth from him in a rush, weeping, and the voice in Scene Three confirms it. That party chooses what to do with him before the hunter or Sírgana chooses for it.

> [!WARNING]
> Never let the hunter say anything Ástheren did not say. It has no words of its own. If it answers a question or names someone Ástheren did not name, the party has no reason to trust the voice, and the reveal goes with it.

### What the party wants afterward

Once the reveal lands, the party is no longer only finding an envoy. Sírgana wants Norven left alone in the ring, which is the Wardens' justice and the one thing the party has learned nobody survives. The party has to keep together a company in which its guide wants its witness dead, and Norven is the only person who knows where the thieves went in. In Scene Four, "leave someone inside when the last stone is set" turns over: Sírgana asks for exactly that, and it is Norven she means.

> [!TIP]
> Play Sírgana's demand as quiet and certain. She does not threaten the party, and she does not walk off alone, because a Warden alone in the ring is prey. She says what the law of the ring is and waits for the party to give her Norven.

## Hooks

Pick the entry that suits the hero. Each leads to the lodge with Sírgana.

- **The thief.** A fence in [[place-beravel|Péravel]] has paid poachers for silver bands of an old pattern and wants the rest of their haul, said to include a jeweled casket. He sends the thief to meet them on the envoys' road, and a Warden meets the thief first and offers a choice of company.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The royal house hires a few blades to support the Warden escort, since an envoy is missing and the crown wants its own witnesses. Sírgana takes them, and says plainly that they go in as her company or not at all.
- **The leader.** The envoy is the leader's kinsman, friend or rival, and the casket carries the crown's word to the Lodge. The leader goes to the Wardens' post on the road and asks, which is the only way a lowlander enters that country.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is on the road at dusk when a Warden steps out of the trees, looks at the wanderer for a long moment, and asks for company for one night. She does not say why one companion is better than none.

## Opening

Read or paraphrase:

> The Warden stops beside a silver-barked tree and lays her palm flat on its trunk. You watched her cut a blaze into that bark an hour ago, a quick downward stroke at shoulder height; the wood of it is still wet. You walked away from it. It is ahead of you again. Across the path, among the trunks, stands a long timber lodge with a stone chimney and no smoke. As you look toward it, a shutter closes from the inside.

> [!TIP]
> Let the blaze sit for a breath before anyone speaks. The opening's job is the first cold certainty that the wood is wrong, and the closing shutter is the first thing the party walks toward.

## Scene One: The Lodge

**Purpose.** Arrival and first unease. Set the practical task, find the envoy and the casket, show that something has already walked here wearing a familiar face, and put the cloak, the spear and the floor in front of the party.

> The lodge is long and low and old, with carved posts at the door and moss on the shingles. Every shutter is barred. A thin line of firelight shows under the door, and when the Warden calls out in her own tongue, the light under the door goes dark as someone stands against it.

**Situation.** The lodge stands at the ring's lower edge. Inside, **Norven Halren** sits by a banked fire with a boar-spear across his knees and a cloth pressed to his side. The spear's head is bound on with fresh sinew, and the shaft below it is scraped pale. The floorboards between the hearth and the door are sanded white. By the hearth hangs a Warden's gray-green cloak, pierced through the back by a single narrow hole and stiff with blood round it. Sírgana knows it at once: it is Ástheren's.

**NPCs and threats.** **Norven** is a lowland poacher in his forties, unshaven, feverish and more frightened than he has been in his life. He refuses to open to the Warden. Through the door he says that her companion has already been here: Ástheren came to the door at dusk two nights ago, already dead, sat at this hearth, and went out again leaving his cloak, and last night he came back with the envoy behind him and led the envoy into the trees. He does not believe a Warden is at the door, and he does not want to find out what is. Sírgana is quiet and controlled. She does not know what to make of his story and does not like it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Three things must be in the room before the party leaves it: the cloak with its single narrow hole, the fresh-bound head and scraped shaft of the spear, and the sanded floor. Describe them among the bedrolls and the cookpot, as plain facts of the place, and move on.

> [!TIP]
> Play Norven as a man the party wants to save. He is wounded, feverish and terrified, and every fear he names is real. The only thing he will not do is look at the cloak.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not let Norven react to the clues. If a player picks up the spear or asks about the floor, Norven says the plain thing (a head broke on a stag's shoulder; he bled where he lay), and says it tiredly. A party that distrusts him should do so on the evidence, never on the GM's tone of voice.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ break the door or a shutter. Norven puts the spear through the first figure in the gap and then collapses. The lodge is open, and so is the way in for anything else.
- _Stealth:_ find another way in: the smoke-hole in the roof, the wood-store at the back, a loose plank under the eaves. Norven is watching the door and does not see the second entrance until it is used.
- _Talk:_ prove to Norven that they are what they seem. He asks for something only a living stranger would know or do: to name the place they slept last, to show a fresh cut that bleeds, to come to the door all together. A party that thinks to send one person alone to the door gets nothing; one that comes all together is let in.
- _Cleverness:_ Sírgana, prompted, asks Norven a question only Ástheren could answer, about the patrol they walked together in spring. Norven says he cannot answer it, but that the thing at the hearth talked of that patrol, and he repeats what it said: that it walked the spring patrol with Sírgana of the Tírindi, and that the spring melt at the ford took her bow out of her hands. Sírgana goes pale. It is true, and nobody but Ástheren knew it.
- _Giving something up:_ leave weapons at the door, as the poacher demands. He opens. The party is inside, unarmed for the moment, and the weapons are outside in the dark.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **cloak**, pierced through the back by something long and narrow, and the plain fact that Ástheren is dead.
- The **spear**, the **floor** and the **spring patrol**, each of which reads as Norven says it does.
- **Norven's story:** the thieves broke the stones for the silver, one man was lost each night after, and each was lost when he stepped away from the others. The first was **Torlan**, who walked back to the lodge the next dusk in his own cloak, calling Norven by name. All of it is true.
- The thieves' trail: a pry-bar on the lodge step, crusted with moss and stone dust.

**Consequences.** Norven, if he is let live and treated decently, is a guide of a sort: he knows where the thieves went in. If he is killed or driven off, the party has lost the one man who knows what happened at the hearth. Either way, Sírgana now knows her partner is dead, and she does not leave.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that goes past the lodge into the trees finds that the path brings it back to the lodge within the hour, from the uphill side. A party that kills Norven finds his body gone from the floor by morning and the door still barred from inside; his voice calls in Scene Three all the same. If the party never enters, Norven opens the door a crack at dawn and throws the cloak out onto the step, where Sírgana finds it, and the scraped spear is in his hands as he does it.

## Scene Two: The Journal and the Carvings

**Purpose.** Evidence of something wrong. Give the party the means to understand the hunter, from more than one source, while the source itself stays out of sight, and put the envoy's own dates into the party's hands.

> In daylight the inside of the lodge shows its walls: carved floor to rafter, every stone of the ring in its place, a stream through the fold, and at the center a great tree with small figures hanging in its branches. Somebody has scratched four of the carved stones through with a knife point.

**Situation.** The poachers' gear lies about the room: bedrolls, a cookpot, a sack of silver bands pried from stone. Under one bed is a leather satchel holding the envoy's journal. Outside, along the ring's edge uphill, the party can find the stones themselves, four of them overturned in a line where the thieves passed. Beside the nearest upright stone the moss is trodden down in a tight circle a man's arm across, and a single set of boot-prints leaves it uphill.

**NPCs and threats.** Nothing shows itself. The party, walking out to the stones in daylight or by lantern, hears footsteps in the leaf-litter keeping pace with them just out of sight. When they stop, the footsteps stop. When one of them walks a few paces apart to look at a stone, the steps move with that one.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ go out after the footsteps in strength. The steps withdraw ahead of a group and follow anyone who falls behind. Nothing is caught.
- _Stealth:_ watch from cover by the overturned stones. A watcher who keeps very still sees, briefly, a tall shape between two trunks that is not where the eye leaves it.
- _Talk:_ ask Norven how he came by the satchel. He says the envoy dropped it on the step when the thing led him away, and that he pulled it in at dawn. That is true.
- _Cleverness:_ read the carvings and the journal together. The carvings show the mark-stones and their places; the journal shows which ones the thieves broke.
- _Giving something up:_ Sírgana knows parts of the Wardens' song of the ring. She sings them only if the party gives her something in kind: the truth about why each of them is here.

**Clues and rewards.** Each of the essential facts can be had more than one way.

- **The hunter takes the one who is alone.** From the journal, where the envoy notes that his two servants were each lost when they stepped from the fire. From the footsteps, which follow whoever walks apart. From Sírgana's song: "nothing walks alone in the ring."
- **A standing stone is a refuge, and the stones are its bound.** From the trodden circle at the upright stone, where one man stood a whole night within arm's reach of it and walked away at dawn. From the carvings, which show the hanging tree inside the line and nothing outside it. From Sírgana's song: "nothing runs from it but toward a standing stone."
- **Four stones are down, and where.** From the journal's entries. From the carvings, compared with the line in the wood. From the pry-marks and the silver in the sack, which match the stones' heads band for band.
- **It wears the taken.** From the journal's last line, in the envoy's hand: "Ástheren has come back for me. He is smiling." From Norven's account of Torlan.
- **It says only what its prey said.** From Sírgana's song, which she sings in a whisper: "it has no tongue but the tongues it took."

The envoy's journal also records the footsteps that followed the escort for two days on the road, always stopping when the party stopped. Its entries after the robbery are dated by nights and written beside a fire at a standing stone. The second-last entry reads: "At dusk Ástheren went down alone to the lodge, to bring the poachers to account and take back the casket. He says a Warden is not prey at a hearth with men at it." The last, a night later, is written in the dark and slants across the lines: "Ástheren has come back for me. He is smiling."

The sack of silver bands is what was pried from the stones. Without it a set stone has no band, and the carvings show every stone banded.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The journal's last two entries must reach the party: Ástheren walking down alone, alive, and coming back smiling a night later. Set them beside Norven's "two nights ago" and say nothing about the difference. A player who counts the nights has the reversal before the voice gives it.

> [!TIP]
> Have Sírgana read the entry about Ástheren going down alone, and let her be proud of him for it. Her pride is the first reading, and it makes the second land harder.

**Consequences.** The party knows enough to plan. The hunter knows the party is in its ground: from here on the footsteps are always there at the edge of hearing, and the party can stop to listen and hear them stop.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never reads the journal or the carvings still meets the facts later: the envoy, alive in Scene Four, says the same things in a whisper; the standing stones in the wood carry the same marks as the lodge walls; and the hunter's own conduct in Scene Three teaches the rule about the one alone at a cost. If the sack of silver is left behind, Sírgana goes back for it, or the party finds a few bands dropped on the path where a thief fell. If the journal is never opened, Sírgana finds it in the satchel in Scene Three, by the light of the fire, and reads the last two entries aloud.

## Scene Three: The Voice in the Trees

**Purpose.** The horror draws close, and the reversal lands. Show that the hunter is active, that a locked room is no refuge and that the way out is gone, and let the hunter tell the party what happened at the lodge door.

> Night comes down on the lodge. The fire settles. Some time before midnight a voice calls from the canopy, high above the roof, clear and unhurried, in the accent of the Áelendan: "Put up the spear. I am Ástheren, of the Wardens, and I am living." A silence. Then, lower and closer: "I was living."

**Situation.** At nightfall Norven, fevered, asks to be shut in the lodge's back room, which has one window, barred with oak, and a door with a bar on the inside. He wants walls between himself and the trees. The voice calls before midnight, and again each time the fire is fed, in the same words. Behind the barred door Norven begins to shout back at it. If the party leaves him barred in alone, the back room goes quiet some time after midnight. The bar is still across the door. The window bars are intact. Norven is gone, and a moment later his voice answers Ástheren's from high in the trees: "Stay off me. I watched it take Torlan, and Torlan came back."

At first light, Sírgana walks the party down to the envoys' road, intending to send for more Wardens. The road is not there. Every track out of the lodge bends uphill within a few hundred paces. Her blazes from the day before turn up ahead of her, one after another, all pointing inward.

**NPCs and threats.** The **hunter**, still not seen plainly: a tall, narrow shape glimpsed between trunks, too quiet for its size, gone when looked at. It calls in Ástheren's voice, and in Norven's once it has him. If anyone is alone, even for a moment, even behind a closed door, it is there. Sírgana is the likeliest next victim after Norven: she is the one who walks ahead, scouting, because that is a Warden's habit.

**Sírgana, after the voice.** Sírgana knows the hunter has no words of its own, and she knows what a man is told to put up. She goes to the barred door, or to Norven's side, and asks him one question: whether Ástheren was living when he came to the hearth. Whatever Norven says, she then tells the party what the Wardens' law of the ring is, and asks that Norven be left alone in the ring before the stones are set. If Norven is already taken, she asks that he be left hanging.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scene must put Ástheren's two sentences in the party's ears, word for word. Everything after it, Sírgana's demand and the choice in Scene Four, rests on the party having heard what the Warden said at the door.

> [!WARNING]
> Do not let Sírgana accuse Norven before the voice calls. She suspects nothing until she hears Ástheren's words, and a Warden who turns on the poacher early hands the party the reversal without the clues that make it fair.

> [!TIP]
> If Norven is with the party, let him confess only when cornered, and in pieces: Torlan at the door the dusk before, a second shape coming out of the dusk, the man turning his back to set the bar. He does not ask forgiveness. He asks the party not to leave him alone.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ go out after the voice. The hunter lets the party find Norven's boots at the foot of a tree, if it has him, and nothing else, and takes whoever went round the far side of the trunk.
- _Stealth:_ stay inside, all together, back to back, and wait out the night, with Norven among them. The hunter circles the lodge until dawn, calling Ástheren's words. Nobody is taken. The morning brings the folded paths.
- _Talk:_ answer the voice and keep it talking. The hunter answers only with Ástheren's two sentences, and with the hearth-talk of the spring patrol, in the same order each time; it never says anything new.
- _Cleverness:_ stand someone against a standing stone as a test, with the others watching from a group. The hunter comes to three paces off and stops. Now the party has seen the rule work.
- _Giving something up:_ give Norven to Sírgana now: put him out of the lodge door alone and bar it. He is gone before the party can change its mind, and his voice answers Ástheren's from the trees until dawn. Sírgana thanks the party and does not speak of it again.

**Clues and rewards.** The rule of the one alone, proved at a price if it was not learned in Scene Two. The rule of the voice, proved by its repetition. The truth of the lodge door. The folded paths, which tell Sírgana that the stones must be set right before anyone leaves. The direction of the clearing, which every path now leads to.

**Consequences.** Withdrawal is impossible while the stones are down. Sírgana says so plainly: nobody leaves the ring until it is whole. If she is taken, the party loses its guide and her songs, and gains a new voice in the dark that knows their names; the demand for Norven dies with her. If Norven is still with the party, it now guards a man its guide wants dead.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that leaves the lodge before night is overtaken by the same events in the open: the voice calls from the canopy over the party's camp, and one of them, or Sírgana, walks a little ahead and is gone. A party that refuses to go inward finds the folded paths make the choice for it: every way leads to the clearing by moonrise. If Sírgana is taken and the party has no songs, the carvings on the standing stones in the wood still show the line, and the envoy in Scene Four knows the rest.

## Scene Four: The Clearing

**Purpose.** Confrontation. Show the source, make the earlier clues intelligible, let the party use what it learned, and make it choose what happens to Norven.

> At the heart of the ring the trees open on a round clearing, silver under the moon. In the middle stands a vast tree whose lower branches are woven together into cradles of bough and bark, and things hang in them. One of them turns slowly as you look, and it is a man, and his eyes are open.

**Situation.** In the cradles hang the taken: the envoy Devrik, alive and silent; two of the thieves, one alive; Norven, if he was taken, alive; Ástheren, dead, with a spear-head broken off in his back. Others hang higher, older, dry. The roots below are crowded with bones and with gear rotted to scraps. The casket hangs in a cradle with Vendrik Othral's pack, three branches up. The hunter is here, among the trunks at the clearing's edge, moving when the party moves.

**NPCs and threats.** The **hunter** at last, seen whole: very tall, long-limbed and thin, its joints bending where no joint should be, wearing Ástheren's leather and a thief's hood over a face that cannot be held in the eye. It moves between the trunks without sound and comes out of the dark at whoever is apart. It does not take what stands within arm's reach of an upright mark-stone, and it does not cross a line of stones that is whole. It is hard to wound and harder to catch; blades find it, but it is gone before a second blow. Wounding drives it off for a time; nothing the party carries is known to kill it, and the Wardens do not want it killed. **Sírgana**, if she lives, cuts Ástheren down first, and when she finds the spear-head she holds it up to Norven's spear, or to Norven in his cradle, without a word.

> [!TIP]
> Describe the spear-head plainly as iron with a broken socket, and let the party match it to the shaft it has seen. By this point the party knows; the head is the proof it can carry out of the wood.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ stand in a ring of their own, shoulder to shoulder, and fight it off while others cut down the captives. The hunter circles and strikes at whoever leaves the ring, and every captive cut down is another body to guard.
- _Restore the stones:_ take the silver bands back to the four overturned stones, set each upright and banded, while the rest hold off the hunter. The work needs the line from the carvings or the journal and the silver from the sack. Each stone set closes part of the ground. When the fourth stone stands, the hunter's ground is the fold inside the ring again, and anyone inside it is still within the hunt.
- _Lure it back:_ the hunter takes the one alone. One volunteer walks apart, inside the line, as bait, while the others set the last stone behind the hunter as it follows. The volunteer must then reach a standing stone before the hunter reaches the volunteer.
- _Cleverness:_ draw the hunter after a voice of its own: a captive's cloak on a pole, a lantern carried apart, a call. It follows its prey, and a lure buys the moments the stone-setters need.
- _Talk:_ the hunter does not bargain. It answers in borrowed voices and says only what its prey has said. Sírgana, if she lives, can sing the ring's song to it, and while she sings it does not move against her; she cannot both sing and work.
- _Giving something up:_ leave someone, or something, inside the ring when the last stone is set. Sírgana asks for Norven. If he is in the party's company, she asks that he be walked into the clearing and left there while the last stone goes in; if he hangs in a cradle, she asks that he be left in it. A thief left hanging, or the casket itself, is also a price the ring takes, and Sírgana does not forgive leaving any other living person.

> [!WARNING]
> Sírgana's demand closes a route whichever way the party answers it. If the party refuses her, she still holds the line and sings, but she does not help bring Norven out, and she reports the refusal to the Wardens. If the party agrees, Norven is gone for good when the last stone stands. Make the stakes plain before the last stone is set, never after.

**Clues and rewards.** The envoy, cut down and alive, tells what he saw in a whisper: Ástheren's face, smiling, at his fire; the walk down to the lodge behind it; the voice calling through the lodge door, and nobody opening it; the cradle; the hunter's habit of stopping when he stopped. The cradles hold the casket, still sealed; Vendrik's pack; and the gear of the hunter's earlier prey, much of it rotten and some of it not. The rule of the stones is now plain to anyone who sees the hunter stop at a standing one.

**Consequences.** Captives brought out before the last stone is set are free. Anyone still inside the line when it closes is the hunter's, and only an upright stone will keep them until dawn. If the ring is closed, the paths unfold by morning. If it is not, the hunter's ground keeps spreading along the broken line.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that arrives unprepared, without the line or the silver, can still survive: an upright stone at the clearing's edge, the fifth from the gap, gives a refuge for those who reach it, and the hunter goes still among the roots at moonset until the next night. In the dark between moonset and dawn the party can cut down the captives, take the casket, and walk out along the line of standing stones, which the hunter's ground does not reach while the moon is down. The stones stay broken, and the Wardens must set them later. A party that fled the clearing can come back the next night by the same folded paths, with what it lacked.

## Scene Five: Before Dawn

**Purpose.** Escape and aftermath. Resolve the casket, the envoy and Norven, pay out the reward, and leave the ring as it was, or not.

> The moon goes down behind the silver trunks, and the clearing is dark. Somewhere downhill, faint and steady, water is running over stone where you heard none before.

**Situation.** If the stones stand, the paths are straight again by first light, and the envoys' road lies where it always lay, a short walk downhill. Sírgana, if she lives, leads the party out before dawn by the outside of the stones, a hand on each stone as she passes.

**NPCs and threats.** No threat but what was left undone. If the ring is still open, the folded paths hold until dawn, and the party must walk the line of standing stones from refuge to refuge to reach the road. Devrik is weak and needs carrying. If Norven is with the party, Sírgana walks behind him the whole way down.

**Player choices.**

- _Keep faith:_ give the casket to Sírgana unopened. She returns it to the envoy, and the envoy carries it to Ésalosa.
- _Open the casket:_ it holds the crown's word to the great council, a folded letter under seal, and a cloak-clasp of Áelendan silver work. The clasp is meant for the party's reward. Opening the casket is a breach that Sírgana reports.
- _Keep the silver:_ the bands from the stones belong to the stones. A party that kept some back has left a stone unbanded, and Sírgana knows which.
- _Bargain:_ Vendrik's pack is the thieves' haul from before the road: gemstones from a lowland strongroom and coin-silver. Sírgana lets the party keep it, since it is no part of the wood.
- _Decide Norven:_ hand him to Sírgana at the road, let him slip away into the lowland before the Wardens' post, or take him on to the crown's people at Ésalosa and argue for him.

**Clues and rewards.**

- The **casket**, recovered intact, if the party kept it so.
- **Vendrik's pack:** a pouch of cut gemstones and a weight of coin-silver stolen in the lowland, which Sírgana permits the party to keep.
- The **clasp** the Lodge sends for the party's service: a cloak-clasp of Áelendan silver cut with the mark of the ring's stones, a closed line with one gap. A Warden who sees it passes its wearer through Warden ground with a word. The Wardens' song says a thing of the wood that keeps the marks passes such a wearer by, and does not say more.
- The gear of the hunter's older prey, from the roots, if the party searched: an old knife of good steel, a rotted pack with a lowland coin-purse, a horn.

**Consequences.** The envoy reaches Ésalosa. The party is known to the Wardens of that stretch of road, for good or ill.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that fled the ring without the casket hears within the season that the Wardens set the stones and recovered it from the roots. The reward goes to whoever brought out the envoy. A party that left the ring open is not welcome on the road again.

## Aftermath

- **If the stones were set and the captives brought out,** the ring is whole, the paths are straight, and the Wardens owe the party.
- **If Norven was left in the ring,** he hangs in the clearing until the hunter tires of him, and on still dusks the charcoal-burners of Sóurimel hear two voices in the trees above the road, one asking for the spear to be put up and one refusing. The Wardens count the matter closed, and the party has the thanks of Ástheren's kin.
- **If Norven came out alive and was handed to the Wardens,** the Elder Wardens of the territory hear Sírgana and the party, and at the next dusk Norven is walked into the ring alone. The crown's magistrates do not interfere, since the killing was done on Warden ground.
- **If Norven came out alive and the party let him go,** he does not come back to the lodge, and the Wardens of that road want the party's account of why. Sírgana gives hers first.
- **If Norven came out alive and the party argued for him at Ésalosa,** the council hears that he killed in terror of a thing his own band let loose, and the Wardens hear the party speak for the man who killed one of theirs. Neither forgets it.
- **If the stones were set with someone else still inside,** that person hangs in the clearing until the hunter tires of them, and the Wardens sing them into the song.
- **If the ring was left open,** the hunter's ground runs farther along the broken line each night, and the envoys' road is closed until the Wardens find enough company to set the stones.
- **If the casket was opened,** the crown's word to the Lodge reaches the council broken, and the crown hears from the Lodge about it.
