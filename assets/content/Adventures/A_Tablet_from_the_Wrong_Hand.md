## Overview {#overview}

[[place-wongsen|Wongsen]] is the port of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]] and the seat of its Prince, [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], the strongest of the four Princes of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] and the one who names the price of the rice the other provinces buy. Its foreign merchants live in [[place-wongsenfrgnqrtr|The Foreign Quarter of Wongsen]], walled, watched and taxed. A party of the embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] comes to the quarter on an errand of the embassy's that needs the coast, carrying whatever classification the embassy holds by then. It is a party the law can see, in a place where most foreigners are seen only by the Prince's Warden of the Foreign Quarter.

A foreign merchant, entered in the registers as [[being-frgnmrchntwng|First Son of the Merchant of No Clan, from Beyond the Border]], finds that out at the gate. His trading classification, the _Shwonong Dou Chou_ that gives him the standing of a [[lore-shuonong|Shwonong]] for the length of his business, runs out at sunset on the fourth day, and his goods are still in a warehouse of the quarter. On the morning after, he is a foreigner with no classification: he cannot own property, sign a contract or seek the law's protection, and his goods stay in the warehouse when he cannot. He needs a buyer, an extension or a way to move the goods before the Warden's officers come for his tablet, and he needs it from people the law can see.

The party can learn why the merchant cannot sell in time, find a buyer or an extension, and meet the offer made behind the warehouses: a tablet that no bureau issued. The tablet is either a forgery or a trap, and forging a tablet is punished by execution in public. [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], has taken the embassy up, and an embassy servant caught with a forged tablet in Rinzho's port is a disgrace to him that costs Rinzho nothing.

**The tablet.** A person who travels beyond the district where he is known by face carries a _Zhau Mou_, a classification tablet: a plaque of hardwood inscribed with the bearer's caste or standing, his household and the authority that issued it, with the issuing authority's seal in lacquer on the reverse. A merchant holding a _Shwonong Dou Chou_ carries a tablet good for the length of his business. At the gates of the quarter a foreigner going in or out shows it, with the seal of the bureau that issued it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The merchant.** The merchant's classification was issued for the length of his business, and the business has taken longer than the tablet allows. His goods sit in the quarter's warehouses, documented and taxed, for the Shwonong factors who buy them. The Shwonong factor [[being-shwnngfctrwng|First Daughter of the Grain-Factor of the Clan of Scales at the Open Door, near Wongsen]] knows the date on his tablet as well as the Warden's officers do, and every day that passes lowers what she offers, because after sunset on the fourth day the goods belong to a man the law does not see. An extension is a bureau's to give, through the commercial administration that issued the first classification. The merchant has asked, and the answer has not come.

**The offer.** The sailors of the quarter say that a tablet can be bought behind the warehouses that no bureau issued. On the third day the merchant, desperate, or the seller [[being-tbltsllrwng|Ninth Bondman of the Rope-Maker of the Clan of Tar on the Slipway, near Wongsen]], who has watched the party, makes the offer, and the tablet carries a seal. Whether it is a forgery or a trap is the Game Master's choice, and each has someone who wants it found.

- **A forgery.** The seller is a member of the Wongsen cell of [[affiliation-haluozhao|the Black Lotus Society]], which trades in forged tablets, and the cell's Handler is [[being-fnrlshpkprwng|Third Daughter of the Spirit-Paper Seller of the Clan of Paper Boats on the Tide, near Wongsen]], whose funeral-goods shop stands at the mouth of the court. The tablet is good enough to pass the gate for a day. A buyer who is found with it is a forger, whoever sold it and whatever was paid.
- **A trap.** The seller is the Warden's man. The tablet is an object the Warden's officers made so that they could see who buys one, and the officers watch the warehouses' rear for the buyer. A buyer who is found with it is a forger all the same.

The party's tablets are the best classification in the quarter, and a tablet that the party takes to the gate and fails is read in public by the Warden's officers.

**The Warden.** The Warden of the Foreign Quarter, [[being-wrdnfrgnqrtr|Second Son of the Tower Captain of the Clan of Banners Above the Harbor, near Wongsen]], is a [[lore-wohou|Wohou]] officer of the Prince's who keeps the walls and the gates, taxes and watches the merchants. His officers read each tablet's date and seal and the reverse of the plaque. They keep the quarter as the Prince requires, and whether the Warden has set the trap is the Game Master's.

**The Prince's interest.** Rinzho is the strongest of the three Princes who want the embassy turned against Chengzho. He needs no order to be given. The quarter is his, and a servant of the embassy shamed at the gate of his own quarter serves him whether the Warden meant it or not.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scene the party must reach is the gate at sunset on Day Four with the merchant's goods sold or moved, his classification extended, or the party standing clear of the tablet. None of the three needs a lie.

> [!WARNING]
> The setting fixes only that the Black Lotus trades in forged tablets, that the Warden's officers watch the quarter and that forgery is punished by execution in public. Who sold the tablet is the Game Master's. Do not tell the table it is a forgery or a trap until the table has found out.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- **The merchant.** Which kingdom he comes from and what he trades. The setting says only that he is a foreigner who trades at the port and that his goods are in a warehouse of the quarter.
- **The party's errand.** Why the embassy has sent a party to the coast. The setting fixes that the party carries the embassy's classification and that the port belongs to the Prince who wants the embassy turned.
- **The forgery or the trap.** Who sold the tablet, who made it and whether the Warden knows of it. The scenario fixes that either answer ends a buyer's life if the buyer is found.
- **The factor's price.** What the Shwonong house offers on each day, and how far it falls. The scenario holds that the offer falls each day and that a buyer wants the goods only while the classification stands.
- **The extension.** Whether a bureau answers in time, and whether the embassy's classification is a reason for it to. The scenario holds that the Warden's office takes the request and that the commercial administration answers it, and leaves the answer to the Game Master.
- **The Warden.** How closely he watches the party, and what he says at the gate on the fourth day. He keeps the walls and the gates, and he is the Prince's officer.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The courtier: the merchant's classification.** A foreign merchant asks the party, as people the law can see, to help him past the date on his tablet.
- **The scholar: the date on the tablet.** The party wants to learn how the quarter classifies and tallies its merchants, and the merchant's tablet is the open case.
- **The warrior: the walls and the gates.** The party walks the quarter's walls and gates to see how they are kept, and the merchant's warehouse is on the way.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The gate of the quarter is a tall archway in a plain wall, and a line of foreigners waits in front of it in the morning heat. Each one holds out a hardwood tablet in both hands, seal upward, to one of the Warden's officers, who reads it, turns it over, reads the lacquer on the back and hands it back.
>
> When the party's tablets have been read and returned, a man steps out of the line behind. He holds his own tablet at his side, and he does not hold it out. "You are of the embassy," he says. "I would like to put a question to people the officers know by their seal."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Gate

**Purpose.** Meet the merchant, and learn that the party is seen.

**Situation.** Day One, morning, at the gate of the quarter. The party's tablets are read, and the merchant steps out of the line.

**NPCs and threats.** The officers of the Warden, [[being-wrdnfrgnqrtr|Second Son of the Tower Captain of the Clan of Banners Above the Harbor, near Wongsen]], who read each tablet and turn it over; the merchant, [[being-frgnmrchntwng|First Son of the Merchant of No Clan, from Beyond the Border]], whose classification ends at sunset on Day Four; the other foreigners in the line.

**Player choices.** Answer the merchant, send him to the embassy's office, walk on, or ask the officers what the date on his tablet is.

**Clues and rewards.** The officers read each tablet's date as they read its seal, and the merchant's ends at sunset on Day Four. The party's tablets pass without a second reading, and the line sees that they do.

**Consequences.** A party that walks on is found by the merchant again in the warehouse court on Day Two. A party that answers in the open is a party the officers know by name before the afternoon.

**If bypassed.** The merchant waits in the court behind the warehouses until the party comes through.

> [!TIP]
> Play the officers as correct and courteous. They have done nothing wrong, and a party that treats them as enemies has insulted the Prince in his own quarter.

### Scene Two: The Warehouse

**Purpose.** See what is at stake, and what the date takes.

**Situation.** Day Two, morning. The merchant's goods stand in a warehouse of the quarter, documented and taxed, with the Warden's officers walking the court.

**NPCs and threats.** The merchant; the Warden's officers, who count the goods against the tally and do not answer questions about the date.

**Player choices.** Count the goods against the tally, read the tax marks, ask the merchant what he owes and what he is owed, or ask how the goods leave the quarter.

**Clues and rewards.** The goods leave the quarter only on the merchant's tablet, and the officers read the tablet before each load. The goods are worth more than the merchant can carry out alone before Day Four ends, and he owes tax on them that a factor's price must cover.

**Consequences.** A party that counts the goods learns what the merchant must be paid to be whole. A party that reads the tally learns that, after sunset on Day Four, the goods are written up as held for no owner.

**If bypassed.** The merchant tells the party what the tally would have shown, and the party sees the goods from the door.

### Scene Three: The Factor

**Purpose.** Find the lawful answers, and how little time they leave.

**Situation.** Day Two, afternoon, in the receiving hall of a Shwonong house whose factors buy the quarter's goods. The factor has read the date on the merchant's tablet already.

**NPCs and threats.** The factor, [[being-shwnngfctrwng|First Daughter of the Grain-Factor of the Clan of Scales at the Open Door, near Wongsen]], who is courteous and exact and offers a price; the Warden's officers, who take a request for an extension and give a receipt for it.

**Player choices.** Take the price, bargain, ask for an extension through the Warden's office, or look for a second buyer.

**Clues and rewards.** The factor's price is below what the merchant owes, and it drops each day. The Warden's office takes the request and passes it to the commercial administration that issued the classification, which can answer in a day or in a year.

**Consequences.** A sale at the factor's price pays the tax and leaves the merchant with a loss. An extension requested today may come before sunset on Day Four or after it, and the party can ask for one and bargain for a sale at once.

**If bypassed.** The merchant goes to the factor alone, accepts the first price and is poorer for it.

### Scene Four: Behind the Warehouses

**Purpose.** The offer, and the choice.

**Situation.** Day Three, dusk, in the narrow court behind the warehouses where the sailors say a tablet can be bought. The seller, [[being-tbltsllrwng|Ninth Bondman of the Rope-Maker of the Clan of Tar on the Slipway, near Wongsen]], holds the tablet in a cloth. At the mouth of the court a funeral-goods shop keeps its lamp lit.

**NPCs and threats.** The seller, who names a price and asks no names; the Warden's watchers, who may be in the court or may not; the merchant, who has come with the party or comes after it; the shopkeeper, [[being-fnrlshpkprwng|Third Daughter of the Spirit-Paper Seller of the Clan of Paper Boats on the Tide, near Wongsen]], who stands in her doorway and greets the sailors who pass by name.

**Player choices.** Look at the tablet, buy it, refuse, walk away, ask the sailors or the shopkeeper who sells here, or tell the Warden. The seller does not argue.

**Clues and rewards.** The tablet carries a seal, and the lacquer on the back is fresh and well laid. Whether the seal is a forged bureau's or a trap's, the seller names the same price and says the same words: it is good for a day. The sailors name nobody, and the shopkeeper asks the party after its dead.

**Consequences.** Anyone who takes the tablet in hand is seen. A buyer is a forger in the eyes of the law. A party that tells the Warden is a party that knew of the sale. A party that walks away is seen walking away.

**If bypassed.** The merchant goes behind the warehouses alone on Day Four and buys it.

### Scene Five: The Gate at Sunset

**Purpose.** The climax: the classification runs out.

**Situation.** Day Four, at sunset, at the gate of the quarter. The merchant stands before the Warden's officers with his tablet in his hands.

**NPCs and threats.** The officers; the Warden, [[being-wrdnfrgnqrtr|Second Son of the Tower Captain of the Clan of Banners Above the Harbor, near Wongsen]], if the Game Master brings him to the gate; the merchant; the party; anyone with a tablet no bureau issued.

**Player choices.** Present an extension, produce a sale, hand over a tablet that is not the merchant's, or stand clear.

**Clues and rewards.** The officers read the date, then the seal, then the reverse. An extension that carries the bureau's seal is read and accepted. A sale's receipt is read and the goods leave the quarter by it. A tablet no bureau issued is read, and whether it passes is the Game Master's.

**Consequences.** The merchant who has an extension or a sale keeps his standing or his goods. The merchant who has neither is unclassified in the morning. A forged tablet that is read and stops the officers ends the one who carries it, in public.

**If bypassed.** The sun goes down, the officers take the tablet, and the goods are written up as held for no owner.

## Endings {#endings}

At sunset on Day Four:

- **Saved by the law.** An extension from the bureau, or a sale at a price the merchant can bear, has reached the gate. The merchant keeps his standing or his goods. The party's tablets were read in public and passed, and the embassy's party has helped a foreigner without a forged seal.
- **Saved by the tablet.** The forged tablet passes the gate for a day. The merchant leaves with his goods, and whoever sold or bought the tablet knows it. If the tablet is a trap, the Warden has the buyer's name, and the buyer is a forger when the Warden chooses.
- **Found with it.** A tablet no bureau issued is read at the gate and stops the officers. Whoever carries it is a forger and is put to death in public. If the forger is of the embassy, the court's first account of the embassy's people in Wongsen is of a forger, and [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]] carries the shame.
- **Lost.** Nothing reaches the gate. The merchant is unclassified in the morning, and his goods stay in the warehouse with no owner in law. The merchant's loss is the party's to carry.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The merchant waits in the court behind the warehouses until Day Three and buys the tablet alone on Day Four. Whether it passes at the gate is the Game Master's, as it is for the party. If the officers read it and stop, he is a forger and is put to death in public. If it passes, he leaves with a tablet that is good for a day. Either way his goods stay in the warehouse with no owner in law.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Whatever the ending, carry three things forward: which of the factors and officers know the party by name, what the party owes or is owed by the merchant, and what [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]] now has in hand against the embassy. A party that saved the merchant by the law has a merchant who is in its debt in a port where the embassy has no friends. A party that was seen at the warehouses has been seen by the Warden's officers, and Rinzho knows the embassy's people by what they did in his quarter.
