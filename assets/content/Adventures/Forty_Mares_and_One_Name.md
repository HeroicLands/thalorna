## Overview {#overview}

Forty brood mares leave the southern highland grazing in a Durrask raid during the summer of 719. The herdsmen who pursue kill [[being-kadrum|Kadrum]]’s brother at a ford. Now [[place-stonyford|Stonyford]] needs its breeding stock, the levy draws away the riders, and Kadrum offers horses for the killers’ names. The party can recover property, carry testimony or sell a name; those are different acts with different victims.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The theft and the pursuit both happened. The count of [[affiliation-durrask|The Durrask]] holds deaths owed by a lowland realm, and a death comes off it only when another death is set against it. Coin or returned horses can buy a separate bargain. They cannot erase an entry by themselves.

Kadrum also takes silver from an Aldorathi shore baron to spare that baron’s manors. That secret makes his public insistence on the count vulnerable, but the party does not discover the patron’s unnamed identity merely by asking for the mares. The pattern of selective treatment offers evidence of the bargain even while the patron remains unidentified.

[[being-alfmundkrtn|Alfmund Kaerton]], a great breeder and one of the year’s tavoi, commands the levy at [[place-kaermouth|Kaermouth]]. His letters speak for his breeding interests, but he cannot simply cancel the Senate’s levy or leave his northern command. Use [[being-courser|Courser]] for riding horses when an animal actor is needed. The herdsmen and stead households are the communities making claims; Kadrum and Alfmund are their principal negotiating faces here.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Alfmund’s breeders pay for the recovery of their mares while their riders serve in the northern levy.
- Kadrum offers a party horses for the names of the men who killed his brother.
- A party crossing the Highlands is asked to carry the pursuit’s witnesses to a meeting in the Thornwold, under a promise that covers testimony but does not settle vengeance.

## The Opening {#opening}

At Stonyford’s pens, a breeder points out the empty spaces between the remaining stock while a levy roll lists the riders serving in the north. A messenger from the Thornwold offers a different payment: horses, in exchange for a list of names. The party can hear both commissions before choosing one.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### A contract beside the pens

A letter from Alfmund authorizes payment for animals actually returned, not for a claimed victory over the Durrask. The brands, scars and foaling records identify the stock.

**Choices and evidence.** Take copies of those records, bring an owner as witness, or accept the messenger’s bargain instead. A rider still under levy cannot leave his post to help without leave. Agree whether the party promises animals, negotiations, or a raid. Each is a different undertaking.

**If bypassed.** The party can approach Kadrum first, but must still establish ownership before selling recovered mares in Stonyford’s fair-court.

### The ford’s witnesses

The pursuit’s surviving herdsmen describe the ford and their weapons. They acknowledge fighting the raiders and killing a man. They do not offer themselves to Kadrum. Compare the accounts in one sitting: where each rider stands, which bank the raiders use, and who sees the fatal blow. A confused account is not proof of deliberate deceit.

**Choices and consequences.** Carry signed testimony, carry the witnesses themselves under explicit safe-conduct, or sell an untested name. The witnesses will describe the pursuit to an intermediary without agreeing to be surrendered. The party can preserve the account while withholding names, which leaves Kadrum dissatisfied and keeps the riders alive for further negotiation.

**If bypassed.** Kadrum asks how the party knows its accusation is true. A name bought from tavern rumor is a weapon he can use, but the party has no evidence to defend when the wrong household is threatened.

### What the Grey Heads count

At a Durrask stead in [[place-thornwold|The Thornwold]], Kadrum hears the report in the presence of those who keep his people’s count. This is a meeting during his present term, not a second autumn swearing. He names his brother’s death and asks for the name that answers it.

**Choices and evidence.** Have the count recited, ask what a safe-conduct covers, or negotiate the horses apart from the death. The count’s keepers do not accept money as a death. Kadrum can release mares for payment, prisoners or a useful service without claiming the entry paid, if the bargain is made openly. He can also refuse.

> [!WARNING]
> A neat settlement of the horse theft does not end the vengeance. Say which promise is being made before the players pay. A living person is not an interchangeable coin in the count.

**If bypassed.** A party seeking only the mares can scout the herd and attempt a recovery. It leaves the brother’s death unresolved and gives Kadrum a fresh practical reason to pursue it.

### Taking horses through a watched country

The surviving mares are kept on Durrask grazing in the oak folds. The party’s copied records identify which animals belong to the stolen stock. A negotiated recovery gives time to sort the herd. A theft must be accomplished while its keepers are elsewhere, and a chase risks mares and foals on rough ground.

**Choices and consequences.** Recover a smaller sound group, risk the whole herd, or accept a delayed delivery witnessed by both communities. Armed pursuit in the folds favors the Durrask. Open grazing favors riders who know the animals. Neither side gains a new army because the party draws a sword.

The route also shows the uneven pattern of raids: one Aldorathi estate’s stock travels with less interference than its neighbors’ stock. That is a question to ask, not proof of the paying baron’s name. Kadrum can be pressed about taking a lowlander’s silver while demanding the count; he denies any obligation he has not admitted before witnesses.

**If the party fails.** The mares remain available for a second bargain, but stolen animals lost during a reckless chase cannot be returned by a successful speech. The breeders pay for what comes home.

### The name carried home

At the pens the party accounts for the recovered mares and the promises it makes. At the stead Kadrum receives whatever testimony it undertakes to carry. These reports can be made separately, but neither employer is entitled to a false report.

**Choices and rewards.** A party can return the mares without giving a name; take Kadrum’s horses and deliver the witnessed account; disclose his selective protection to the count’s keepers; or hide one bargain from the other side. The fair-court can settle ownership and payment. It cannot declare a Durrask death avenged. The Grey Heads can question Kadrum’s fitness for another term; they cannot make the levy disappear.

**If bypassed.** The employers act on the missing report. Alfmund treats unrecovered animals as a loss; Kadrum searches for the riders by other means. A party holding witnesses’ names still chooses whether to release them later.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The levy keeps the southern grazing thinly watched. Kadrum seeks the killers and raids again. The breeders answer with another pursuit, and another death can enter the count.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- Returned mares preserve a breeding season and pay the party without ending the feud.
- A named killer becomes a particular target. Follow what the party actually reveals, including whether it warns the household.
- A bargain witnessed by the Grey Heads limits what Kadrum can deny, but binds only the acts it names.
- Evidence of the baron’s silver threatens Kadrum’s next swearing. An unsupported accusation may instead strengthen his claim that outsiders lie about the steads.
