The rains come ashore off [[place-meghrsmdra|the Southern Sea]] in a few days and close the coast for four months. When they stop, the season opens on both of [[place-vedyarargn|Vedyara Region]]'s coasts at once, and every cargo is loaded, carried and sold inside that window. A hull that misses it winters where it lies at the owner's expense. This frame is one sailing season out of [[place-chandrmukha|the Moon-Mouth]], the white city's port at the bar, and [[place-nilatir|Nilatir]], the cloth port of the east. The galley hire is unsettled, the fleet's commander is unconfirmed, a deep-water hull out of Nilatir stands half built in the yard, and the long passage west is priced in Khelathi hands.

## The Situation and the Stakes

**The two calendars.** The coast plants when the rain starts and sails when it stops, and the interior plants when the rivers rise. The first sailing is given by the hour, and the hour depends on the year. The **Ganoka** gives it by [[affiliation-ganokashalda|the Reckoners' College]]'s computed year, and the **Purohata** holds it by the Surta temple's sighting, a day later. The pilots of [[affiliation-nshrnchmk|the Pilots' Shrena of Chendramukhva]] take hulls over the bar in the order of their queue, and the master who is first over the bar sells first.

**One fleet.** [[affiliation-chendrapur|Chendrapur]] has the galleys and [[affiliation-vyalendra2|Vyalendra City-State]] has the cargo. Vyalendra keeps no war-fleet and no deep-water hulls, and the hire of Chendrapur's galleys is argued out before every sailing season and settled late, which suits Chendrapur. This season Vyalendra's **Dutsa** has offered the Nau-senapavi a bargain: Vyalendra's Triyacharya speaks for his confirmation before the temples of the coast, and in return the hire is fixed for three years at this season's rate. No fixed hire has been agreed between the two cities in living memory.

**The commander.** [[being-vjrtnsrntk|Vajratan Sirnataka]], born an Outcaste on the beach at Chendramukhva, has commanded the galleys for twenty years and the fleet for three. The temples have neither confirmed his appointment nor refused it. The [Nine Houses]{.name data-type="affiliation"} have three candidates of their own.

**The Khelathi passage.** The long voyage west to the delta of [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] is the most valuable passage any Vedyari ship makes: spice, gems, ivory and fine cotton out, grain, linen, papyrus and gold back. What reaches the Vilérian Sea from Vedyara goes through Khelathi hands and at Khelathi rates, and the Vedyari houses have never found a way around it. The Khelathi factors keep a walled street at Chendramukhva with their own well and their own weights.

**The first hull of Nilatir.** [[being-priturajasam|Prituraja Samudraya]] owns two coasters outright and has put down the first payment on a deep-water hull in the west-side yards, the first of her size owned in Nilatir. She would carry cloth to the Khelathi ports without Chendrapur's galleys. Her yard bill is drawn on a Collective letter against both coasters.

**The eastern sea.** [[place-tantsouregin|Tantsou]] junks come round the eastern capes late in the season and lie outside the bar rather than take a pilot. Out in [[place-surtaislnds|the Surta-samedra Islands]] a dozen Vedyari merchant colonies answer to island kings, speak a Vedyari a northern scholar cannot follow, and have never been sent an official from the mainland.

The stakes are the price of a season's cloth, who commands the only war-fleet in Vedyara, and whether a Vedyari hull ever sells at the delta on its own account.

## Who Moves It

[[being-vjrtnsrntk|Vajratan Sirnataka]], Nau-senapavi of Chendrapur

[[being-priturajasam|Prituraja Samudraya]], master and owner at Nilatir

[[being-senpvvkrmd|Senapavi Vikramada]], bosun

[[being-darmejadar|Darmeja Daranir]], master of the Crimson Sail

## Ways In

1. **On the deck.** The party signs on at the quay, where nobody asks to see a wrist. Darmeja wants to know which hour the Maharava declares before the pilots' queue is drawn, and pays well for it.
2. **The hull.** Prituraja asks the party to put the new hull's mastership to Senapavi before the season opens, or to find her keel timber, three months late from the forest and said to have been sold on the river to another buyer.
3. **The confirmation.** Vajratan asks the party to learn which of the Nine Houses' candidates the Purohata favors, and what Vyalendra's offer is worth to the temples who would have to hear it.
4. **The dues.** A master has sailed from Naughat without paying the anchorage at all, and the Sarveda Mouth's sabhra asks the party to collect at his next port before the bar closes for the rains.

## If the Party Does Nothing

- The hire is settled late, as it is every year, at Chendrapur's price. Vyalendra's offer lapses, and its cloth goes west in the galleys on the old terms.
- Vajratan goes another season unconfirmed, and one of the Nine Houses' candidates is given a galley of his own to command.
- The Collective's house at the bar hears that Prituraja's coasters are uninsured and calls in the yard letter early. The half-built hull is sold in the yard, and Nilatir still has no deep-water hull of its own.
- The first hull over the bar sails by whichever year the Maharava declares, and the masters who reckoned by the other are a day late in every port of the passage.

## The Secret

The Khelathi factors' street pays one of the Nine Houses a share of every bale it buys at the bar. The arrangement is old, unwritten and the whole reason that house has prospered for three generations. A Vedyari hull that sells cloth at the delta on its own account would end it, and so would a fleet commanded by a man who has fought the galleys through two seasons on the Khelathi passage and owes the house nothing.

That house started the rumor that Prituraja's coasters are uninsured. It is the house with a candidate for the fleet and the house that asked the Purohata to have wrists read at the quay. A party that traces any one of the three to it has a lever on all three, and on the street of the factors who pay for them.
