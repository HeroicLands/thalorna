The wind has uncovered [[place-mezhvadez|Mezhvadez]], the Dry Stair. Mezhvadez is a vault the [[lore-dunhariclt|Dunhari]] do not claim, built by a people whose king's name is chiseled from every wall, and the woman who kept his household lies at its center. The party must reach her, learn what she keeps, and carry out what they can.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A tagemader of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], a polished man named [Ghezrun]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, pays well for the king's signet and says he does not care how it is taken.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** A merchant's caravan is short a guard. The caravan-master wants an escort to the dune that covers the stair, and his two foreign clients carry a map.
- **The leader.** A bahar of [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|the Warrior's Circle]] hears that something has been walking from the Dry Stair toward the southern pastures. The Circle gathers against an enemy none of the tribes could face alone, and the leader must see whether this is one.
- **The wanderer.** A desert mystic returning from a vigil speaks of a voice beneath the sand, calling in the speech of her mother. He asks the party to make it stop.

## Scene One: The Sealed Gate

**Purpose.** The entrance and the guardians: show the vault as old, foreign and watched.

**Situation.** The stair is open at the dune's flank. Wind-polished steps run down into shadow between rows of veiled women carved in the pale stone, and the lowest landing is sealed by a slab of the same stone. A drift of fine sand lies over the threshold, unmarked by any foot.

**NPCs and threats.** The vault's first guardians are the **sand-veils**, strips of grey cloth that hang in the gate-arch and drift upright when a living thing draws near. They are thin and quiet, and they move toward warmth. Where one lands on bare skin it clings, and a body wrapped in them is slowly emptied of breath and water. They fear fire and the glare of a mirror held to the light. A **guide**, [Zirvash]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a lean man who has seen the stair open twice and been below once, waits at the top with the caravan's camels. He will not go down.

**Player choices.**

- _Fire._ Torches clear the veils. It is noisy and shows the party's strength to anything watching.
- _Mirrors and polished shields._ A party that thinks about the sun's angle burns them back without a flame.
- _Stealth._ The veils drift toward warmth and not toward sight. A party cooled by wet cloth passes unremarked.
- _Parley._ Zirvash can be persuaded to tell what he saw below, if the party eats salt with him first.

**Clues and rewards.** The slab bears a line of inscription: the keeper's name has been scratched out, but her title remains, and so does a rule that the party can read from the carving. Visitors may enter if they carry no water in a vessel of their own. Zirvash's account tells of the dust-sworn.

**Consequences.** Fire draws the vault's attention. The party's descent is watched from below.

**If bypassed.** A party that finds the old air-flue and drops through it into the second chamber misses the sealed gate and the veils, but arrives into the middle of the second scene.

## Scene Two: The Hall of Questions

**Purpose.** A puzzle and roleplay encounter in the vault's inner court.

**Situation.** The stair opens into a long hall whose pillars carry a continuous band of figures. In the hall's center stands a basin of dry stone with three jars beside it: one of salt, one of ash, one of clean sand. The air hums faintly, as if a voice is speaking at the edge of hearing, and the whispers are in [[skill-dunharlng|Dunhari]].

**NPCs and threats.** The hall is guarded by **whisper-moths**, large grey insects with pale, human-shaped markings on their wings. They settle on a visitor's shoulder and speak in the voice of the listener's own dead, answering questions and offering to help. They are honest in nothing. They lead travelers off the safe path with the voices of friends. A single bright flame makes them dissolve.

**Player choices.**

- _Salt._ The jar of salt is the proper offering in this hall: set a pinch on the basin and the hall opens the passage on the far side.
- _Ash._ It brings the dust-sworn from their alcoves, awake and angry.
- _Sand._ It blocks the passage for a day.
- _Listen to the moths._ A voice may tell the truth by accident, and a clever party can learn from what the moths want to hide.
- _Force._ Smashing the basin starts a collapse.

**Clues and rewards.** The band on the pillars tells of a king who made a bargain to hold death at arm's length, and of the keeper who sealed him in so that the bargain would not outlive him. The moths' false voices say the keeper still walks.

**Consequences.** The wrong jar costs the party the hall. A party that listens too closely to the moths comes away with a mouthful of a dead friend's laughter.

**If bypassed.** A party that dispatches the moths with fire reaches the far passage without understanding the basin, and meets the dust-sworn awake.

## Scene Three: The Alcoves of the Household

**Purpose.** A trick and setback: the vault turns the party's greed against it.

**Situation.** Beyond the hall lie the household's alcoves, row on row, each with a sealed slab and a jar of grave-goods on a shelf beside it. Heaped on the shelves are silver bangles, lapis beads, amber, folded cloth that has kept its color through the centuries. Much of it is worth a camel.

**NPCs and threats.** The dust-sworn rise in order. Each is a dry, light-boned figure in a veil of stiff silk, with a face that is only skin on bone. They are quick and quiet and carry curved knives of green bronze. They do not strike a party that touches nothing. They wake in numbers for each piece of grave-goods lifted. Their leader, the [Warden of Jars]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, is taller than the others and wears a necklace of dried keys. It speaks, once, in a dry whisper: _put it back._

**Player choices.**

- _Take nothing._ The dust-sworn stay on their slabs and the party passes unmolested.
- _Take one piece and replace it._ The dust-sworn return to rest if the thing is set in its alcove and a pinch of salt laid beside it.
- _Fight._ Dust-sworn are brittle but there are many.
- _Bargain._ The Warden of Jars can be asked for a gift. It names a price: a word spoken aloud in the household's tongue.

**Clues and rewards.** A modest portion of the grave-goods can be taken honorably, with the Warden's leave: a handful of bangles, a lapis bead, a folded cloth. The Warden's keys open the last door.

**Consequences.** Anything taken without leave is followed home by a dry whisper.

**If bypassed.** A party that crosses the alcoves by the roof gallery avoids the dust-sworn and meets the keeper unprepared.

## Scene Four: The Keeper

**Purpose.** The climax: the keeper at the door she was set to guard.

**Situation.** The innermost chamber holds a dais, a stone bed, and a woman lying on it with her hands crossed on her chest. She is whole and brown and dry as a cut date. Behind her, set in the wall, is a door of black stone with no handle. It is cold to look at, and the air in front of it does not move.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Keeper** wakes when the party arrives. She is tall, with a face of great composure, wrapped in the veils of her office and hung with keys of a dozen metals. She is bound to [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] as the dead of the vault are, and she was bound willingly, to hold a door that her king's bargain had opened and that nothing else could close. She is courteous and implacable. She wants nothing taken from the chamber and nothing let through. She fights with the door's cold: a touch that stills the hand, a word that sets the lungs to dust. She does not hunt. She is not wrong.

**Player choices.**

- _Fight her._ She is strong, and the door grows colder as the fight goes on.
- _Parley._ She speaks Dunhari, archaic and slow. She will explain the door to a guest who eats the salt she offers from a jar at her feet. Salt is the one law she shares with the living.
- _Steal._ The signet lies in her hand, and taking it wakes the cold.
- _Open the door._ A key is missing, and the only way to find it is to ask.

**Clues and rewards.** The king bargained with a thing outside creation for time. The thing asked for the household, one by one. The keeper agreed to be the last. The door is the way the Shadow comes to collect, and the keeper's will is what holds it. The signet in her hand is the king's seal, and the seal is the door's lock.

**Consequences.** Kill her and the door opens. Take the signet and she wakes, enraged. Leave her and the door stays shut.

**If bypassed.** A party that comes by the air-flue arrives behind the dais, and the Keeper speaks first.

## Scene Five: What the Door Keeps

**Purpose.** The twist and reward.

**Situation.** Whatever the party chooses in the chamber, the stair above them shudders. The dune is closing. The way out is a race against sand.

**NPCs and threats.** If the door has been opened, the [Hollow King]{.name data-type="being/creature"} steps through: a tall figure in an unmarked robe with no face, only the suggestion of where a face should be. He is the king whose name is struck, not as a man, but as a place the Shadow wears. He asks the party for a name and a debt, and he is polite. If the door has been left shut, the Keeper herself speaks the last words of her office and the vault collapses gently over her, closing itself.

**Player choices.**

- _Seal it again._ A party that brings the signet back to the door and speaks the Keeper's name closes it, at the price of the Keeper's last rest.
- _Run._ The party can outrun the dune, but whoever has the signet is followed.
- _Bargain._ The Hollow King can be bargained with. The bargain is a debt that follows the bearer's line.
- _Carry the Keeper out._ She asks to be laid in the open desert, with a fire kindled where she is set down, as the Dunhari do. This is the first time in an age that anyone has offered.

**Clues and rewards.** The Keeper's reward, if she is given a Dunhari burial, is a gift of the keys: a ring of green bronze that will open any lock made by the household's smiths, and a name for the vault's builders that a poet will carry. The signet is a lure and a ruin.

**Consequences.** What leaves Mezhvadez in the party's packs is a debt, a seal, a blessing or a warning, depending on what they did below.

**If bypassed.** The vault closes. The Dry Stair is gone with the next wind.

## Aftermath

- **The door sealed.** The southern pastures are quiet and the poets add a stanza to the recitation. A _tagemader_ in [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]] stops paying anyone to dig.
- **The Keeper laid to rest in the open.** Zirvash and the guides say her fire was seen across the dune sea. The tribes' account of what came before the Dunhara gets a new line.
- **The door opened.** The Hollow King walks the southern pastures and the dead begin to stand. The Warrior's Circle meets in an extra gathering.
- **The signet in Amradad.** The tagemader has the proof he wanted and a debt he does not know about.
