## Overview {#overview}

Every autumn the war-bands of the forest cross the edge below [[place-sdrvdfrt|Sedrovod Fort]], and every autumn some of the people they take come home by the ford. The ransom brokers of [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]] carry a family's coin down the river road to Yaravesh, the ford town where ransoms are counted out and captives handed back, and they bring the captive home if the price is paid. A captive nobody ransoms goes down Vristwald's rivers before the ice, to buyers who sell nobody back.

This autumn a war-band came out of the trees in the last hour of the night and took the man on the watch of the village beneath Sedrovod Fort from the path below the bell hill. The village has emptied its chests to raise his price and asked [[being-tshvnsvdch|Teshvan Sovidich]] to carry it. Teshvan knows the go-betweens at the ford, how a price is named and how it is lowered. This time the price is not the one the village expected.

The party enters as riders Teshvan hires because the road is riskier than he admits, as witnesses the village sends with its coin to see it counted, or as strangers whom [[being-vrklrvgrch|Ovrik Lervogorich]], captain of the forts, asks to keep the whole matter out of the upper hall.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The watch.** The captive is a villager, not a warden. The roll the prince sees each quarter names a paid warden at the post the captive stood, and that warden is not in the barracks. Ovrik pays the absent wardens' wages to the bell-ringers, cooks and village watches beneath the forts, because the port's silver comes late and the lowland grain later, and the captive was earning one of those wages in a warden's coat. The war-band took the coat for what it looked like.

**The price.** At the ford a war-band names a family's price for a villager and a prince's price for a warden, since a prince pays for his own men and can afford to. The go-between has named the second. The village's coin covers the first and no more.

**The broker's tenth.** A tenth of every family's coin that Teshvan carries never reaches the ford. In an ordinary year he talks the go-between down by more than he keeps, the captive comes home, and no family counts what was sent against what was paid. This year the go-between does not come down, so the purse he opens at the ford is the village's coin less his tenth, and it falls well short of a warden's price.

**The captain.** Ovrik cannot ask [[being-krdgrbrvgrch|Krodogor Brevogorich]] to ransom the captive as a warden, because the prince would ask which of his paid men was taken, and the name on the roll he reads is not the captive's. Ovrik cannot let the go-between learn that the captive is a villager either, because that tells the war-band that the forest posts are held by villagers in borrowed coats. He wants the ransom paid quietly, with coin that comes from nowhere, and he has none.

**The ford.** The go-between is a woman of a forest landing, where the captive trade is kept by the women, and she keeps the custom of the ford. A price named there is not raised, a captive handed over there is not taken again on the road home, and the word given at the ford is kept at the ford. She takes coin and refuses promises. If the price is not paid before the first ice, the captive goes down the river with the season's last boat.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The truth that lowers the price is the truth that the war-band can use against the forts in the next raid. Nobody in this scenario is a villain: the captain feeds his villages with stolen wages, the broker takes a living from grief, and the go-between sells what she was given to sell. The referee decides whether the go-between ever lowers her price, and the party's choices decide who learns why.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The warrior: coin for the ford.** Teshvan wants riders beside him on the river road to Yaravesh and back, at a fee from his own purse.
- **The trader: the village's witnesses.** The headwoman of the village beneath Sedrovod Fort wants strangers to watch the coin counted out at the ford, since the village has never seen a ransom paid.
- **The courtier: the captain's quiet.** Ovrik wants the ransom settled before the quarter's rolls go up to the prince, and he asks a party with no stake in the forts to see to it.
- **The woodsman: a track that stops.** [[being-skgrdbrshv|Sokogor Dobrashov]] has read the war-band's trail from the bell hill, and he wants someone to tell the warden what it shows before the warden guesses.

## The Opening {#opening}

The yard of the headwoman's house beneath Sedrovod Fort, in the gray hour before the bell. A plank table holds the village's coin in small piles, a woman's ring, a pair of silver buckles and a cup that came from somebody's grandmother. A thin man in a gray mustache counts it twice, writes the sum in a clerk's hand and ties the purse to his belt.

> "Is it enough?" asks the headwoman. He knots the cord before he answers. "It is what you have," Teshvan says, "and I carry what you have."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Village beneath the Fort

**Purpose.** Learn who was taken, how, and what the village has raised.

**Situation.** The village lies under the stone wall of Sedrovod Fort on the forest road, with its bell and signal fire on the hill. The war-band crossed the edge at the end of the night, took the man on the path below the hill before he reached the bell and went back into the trees without touching a house. The captive's mother has given her cow's price to the purse, and the headwoman has written every household's share on a tally-stick in front of the village.

**NPCs and threats.** The headwoman, who is exact and frightened. Teshvan, who counts the coin, does not offer comfort and asks only whether the village wants him to ride. The warden of the fort, who is tired and says that the man was on the watch. Sokogor, a rider of the [[affiliation-hsttrvl|Tarvál Host]] lent to the fort as a tracker, who has walked the trail at first light.

**Player choices.** Take Teshvan's commission, take the village's, read the trail with Sokogor, or ask the warden why a villager was wearing the fort's coat.

**Clues and rewards.** The tally-stick gives the village's full sum, and a party that copies it holds the only figure that can be set against the purse at the ford. Sokogor shows that the war-band lay in the trees above the path for several nights before it moved, and walked straight to the post and nowhere else: it had watched the post, and knew it was held by one man. The warden answers the question about the coat by saying that the village sends a man to the watch in turn, which is true and is not an answer.

**Consequences.** The party leaves with Teshvan, with the village's trust, or with both, and the warden knows what it asked.

**If bypassed.** Teshvan rides alone with the purse, and the village's sum stays on a stick in the headwoman's house.

### Scene Two: The River Road

**Purpose.** The road between the fort and the ford, and why it is riskier than Teshvan says.

**Situation.** Two days down the river road from the forest edge to the ford, past the posts of the paid wardens, who know Teshvan's face and let him through at any hour, since it is in no warden's interest to stop a ransom. Beyond the last post the road runs between the river and the trees.

**NPCs and threats.** Teshvan, who is soft-spoken and exact, and who will tell the party how a ransom is carried and nothing about how it is paid. A second band from the forest, not the one that holds the captive, has heard that a broker rides with a purse and waits at a ferry where the road crosses a side-stream. The peace of the ford holds at the ford and not on the road.

**Player choices.** Fight at the ferry, ford the stream higher up, let Teshvan talk, or send for a warden's escort and let the posts know the purse is passing.

**Clues and rewards.** Teshvan rides this road six times in a quiet year and twenty in a bad one, and he knows the ferry: the band's leader greets him by name, because he has paid at it before and has never told a family so.

**Consequences.** The purse reaches the ford whole, short, or not at all, and Teshvan owes the party a debt he does not acknowledge.

**If bypassed.** Teshvan pays the ferry's price out of the purse, and the ford sees even less of the village's coin.

### Scene Three: The Ford at Yaravesh

**Purpose.** The count, the price and the reason for it.

**Situation.** Yaravesh is a small town of the prince's at the ford, and the counting is done on the gravel bank below it, in sight of both shores. The go-between waits on the far side with the captive beside her, his hands untied, in the fort's coat. She names the price once. It is a warden's price.

**NPCs and threats.** The go-between, a broad, gray-haired woman of a forest landing with a set of scales, who keeps the custom of the ford and is not in a hurry. Two riders of the war-band behind her. Teshvan, who opens the purse, counts it out on a cloth and begins to lower the price as he has lowered every price before.

**Player choices.** Let Teshvan bargain, set the village's tally beside his count, tell the go-between that the captive is a villager, ask her who told the war-band which post to take, or ask for time before the ice.

**Clues and rewards.** Set against the village's tally-stick, the purse is short by a tenth, and Teshvan does not deny it once it is counted in front of him. The go-between asks a warden's price because the man wore the coat and stood a warden's post. She lowers it to a family's price only if someone convinces her that he is a villager. Asked who chose the post, she says that the war-band watched it for three nights and saw one man on it each night. She gives time until the first ice, and not a day past it.

**Consequences.** The party knows the price, the reason for it and the broker's tenth. The go-between knows whatever the party has told her about the forts.

**If bypassed.** Teshvan counts out the purse, fails to lower the price, and rides back with the coin and the news that a warden's ransom is wanted.

### Scene Four: The Captain's Room

**Purpose.** The coin that comes from nowhere, and what it costs.

**Situation.** The fort at Brevavesh, a square of gray stone with a tower at the river corner, where Ovrik keeps the rolls and the pay owed the wardens. Above him, in the upper hall, the prince sees the rolls each quarter when the port's silver arrives, and the quarter's day is close.

**NPCs and threats.** Ovrik, gruff and tireless, who keeps every man's debts in his head. He listens, and then asks the party not to go upstairs. The prince, blunt and tired and loyal to his men, who pays a warden's ransom without argument if he is told a warden was taken, and asks the warden's name.

**Player choices.** Ask Ovrik for the difference, take the matter to the prince, raise the coin elsewhere, or make Teshvan give back his tenth.

**Clues and rewards.** Ovrik has no silver to give: he has already given it away beneath the forts. He offers a promise of the next quarter's coin to whoever lends the difference now. A party that reaches the prince learns that he pays for his men and reads his rolls, and that a warden's name on the roll is the first thing he asks for.

**Consequences.** The prince learns of the second roll, or Ovrik owes the party, or the party has paid the difference itself.

**If bypassed.** Ovrik waits for the quarter's silver, and the quarter's silver comes late.

### Scene Five: The Last Boat

**Purpose.** The ice, the river and the end of the ford's season.

**Situation.** The first skin of ice on the shallows at Yaravesh. The war-band's last boat of the season lies at the far bank, loading the captives nobody has ransomed for the journey down Vristwald's rivers. The captive is among them.

**NPCs and threats.** The go-between, who keeps her word to the day. The war-band's riders. The river, which is cold enough to kill a swimmer in the time it takes to cross.

**Player choices.** Pay at the bank, follow the boat down the river and bargain at its landing, or try to take the captive back by force.

**Clues and rewards.** A price paid on the bank is taken as gladly as a price paid at the ford. A captive taken back by force at the ford ends the ford's custom for this war-band, and every family with a captive in its hands pays for that next autumn.

**Consequences.** The captive comes home, goes down the river, or comes home at a price the ford remembers.

**If bypassed.** The boat leaves on the morning of the first ice, and the ford closes for the winter.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Teshvan rides to the ford with the purse, fails to bring the go-between down to a family's price and rides back. The village learns that a warden's price is wanted and cannot raise it. Ovrik says nothing upstairs. The captive goes down the river on the last boat, the village sends another man to the watch in turn, and next autumn the war-band crosses the edge at the same post.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **Home at a family's price.** The captive is back under the bell hill, and the war-band knows that the forest posts are held by villagers in borrowed coats. The next autumn's raids find the thin posts first.
- **Home at a warden's price.** Someone paid the difference: the party, the prince or Teshvan's own purse. If it was the prince, he has read the second roll, and the captain of the forts answers for it.
- **Down the river.** The village has lost a man. The purse Teshvan carried home holds the village's coin less his tenth, and it does not ask him to ride for it again.
- **The tenth counted.** Every family that has ever sent coin with Teshvan begins to count. He leaves the river road before the war-bands learn his face, or stays and is made to pay it back.
