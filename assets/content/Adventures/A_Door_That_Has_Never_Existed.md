The embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] has come east to ask the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] for direct relations, and in Tantsou a foreigner reaches the Emperor only through the Speaker's whisper. The request asks for a door that has never existed. This frame is the eastern leg of the embassy's campaign. It runs from the day the embassy comes down from [[place-jnanadvar|the Scholars' Door]] to the frontier way-station of [[place-kwisen|Kwisen]], through its filing and its hearing at [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], to the season the court spends deciding what the hearing means. One Prince has taken the embassy up, the other three mean to turn it against him, and every page the embassy is entered on, from the station's register to the Speaker's roll, is a move in their game.

## The Situation and the Stakes {#the-situation}

**The request.** The [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Dúnavarre Synod]] commissioned the embassy, and the Kingdom wants direct relations with the Empire, ending its dependence on the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] as intermediary. Only the Emperor's attendants and the Princes speak to the Emperor. Everyone else, a minister, a general or an ambassador, speaks to the Speaker, who hears the question in the gate-court before the enclave wall, whispers it to the Emperor and speaks the answer aloud, and the answer he gives is the answer on the record. The court therefore reads a request for direct relations as a breach of the Speaker's protocol. Granting it would shift power among the Princes, so the court's game decides it, and not the Emperor.

**The sponsor.** [[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], is younger than the other three and rising fast, a reformer with the young Emperor's ear. He takes the embassy up first and openly, for the foreign weight it adds to his hold on that ear, and he wants the embassy seen with him and never without him.

**The three.** The other three Princes each read the embassy as a move against them, and each works to turn it against its sponsor. [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-wongsenprv|Wongsen Province]], is the strongest and the most feared, and the throne's granaries buy his rice at the price he names. [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-mwonouprv|Mwonou Province]], and [[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-kwozhengprv|Kwozheng Province]], are bitter rivals of each other, and each takes the other's loss as his own gain, so neither moves against Chengzho without watching the other. Their contest is [[lore-weizhairui|The Jade Fan Dance]] (_Wei Zhai Rwi_), in which a rumor, a shaming or a misfiled petition does as much harm as an army.

**The classification.** A foreigner in Tantsou is unclassified until a bureau says otherwise. Without classification he cannot own property, sign a contract or seek the law's protection; a crime against him is no crime under the law, and a crime by him is a breach of the order, punished fast. The highest classification is the Emperor's own decree, kept for dignitaries, ambassadors and those who have done the empire an extraordinary service, and a tablet under the Emperor's seal is in practice a safe-conduct anywhere in the empire. The Caste-Registrar, [[being-cstrgstrtnts|Third Son of the Archivist of the Clan of Lanterns Along the Bank]], keeps the rolls on which every caste is entered and is the young Emperor's chief counselor. Chengzho wants the embassy classified high and quickly, and the other three Princes want the classification delayed.

**The new reign.** The Emperor, [[being-tengrauemp|Tengrau]], is young and newly crowned, and he is still finding the reach of his own power. The Retired Emperor, [[being-yanzhortrd|Yanzho]], lives in the background of the court. Each reign is its own era, the chancellery on the two shores is cross-referencing every filing of the old era to the new, and a filing that waits in that work can wait a year.

The stakes are whether the embassy is heard at all, whether the Kingdom of Dúnavarre wins its direct relations or stays dependent on Viléria, and how much of the young Emperor's ear Chengzho keeps.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The frame prescribes no answer to the request and no winner among the Princes. The Emperor is never met. What the party meets are the people who carry paper toward him and the households that want the paper lost: play each move of the game as a matter of forms, by people who decline without saying no.

## Who Moves It {#who-moves-it}

[[being-arancelavren|Aran Kélavren]], the ambassador
: An experienced mage and diplomat whose recent promotion draws accusations of favoritism. He wants a conspicuous success.

[[being-toradhtaravren|Dame Torad Taravren]], captain of the Order of the Just Blade
: A captain of the [[affiliation-justbladeordr|Order of the Just Blade]], who gives the ambassador military counsel and holds the embassy's compound for him in Zhowen.

[[being-marricharavrel|Marrik Háravrel]], Sergeant-at-Arms
: He trains the embassy's guards, and the ambassador takes his advice.

[[being-loristaravel|Loris Taravel]], the translator
: He made the embassy's Tantsou fair copy on the road, and he claims a translator's place wherever the embassy is heard.

[[being-arrenthabavel|Sir Arren Thábavel]], the ambassador's guard
: The champion of the [[lore-kelivrentrny|Royal Kelivren Tournament of Chivalry]], who stands a half-step behind the ambassador in every hall.

[[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], Prince of Doulwen
: Quick, open in his sponsorship and impatient with delay. He wants reform, and the young Emperor's ear held long enough to make it, and the embassy is part of how he holds it.

[[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], Prince of Wongsen
: Patient and sure of his price, he lets the other Princes come to him. His water-gifted [[lore-wanduo|Wandwo]] bring rain to the delta while the rain fails elsewhere, and the embassy's business in his port is business in his house.

[[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]], Prince of Mwonou
: Proud and slow to forgive, he holds the famine province the embassy's road runs through, buys rice at Rinzho's price and works to turn the embassy against Chengzho.

[[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]], Prince of Kwozheng
: Hard and unforgiving, he holds the north against the steppe riders, keeps account of every slight to his house and works to turn the embassy against Chengzho.

[[being-cstrgstrtnts|Third Son of the Archivist of the Clan of Lanterns Along the Bank]], the Caste-Registrar
: Exact and unhurried, he wants the rolls complete and correct. An officer of each Prince waits in his anteroom more often than the business of the rolls requires, and the embassy's classification is among the business they bring.

[[being-gvrnrzhowen|Eldest Son of the Gate Captain of the Clan of the Heron on the Wall]], the Governor of Zhowen
: He holds the capital's two shores for the throne. His officers enter the day's list at each bridge foot and hold the rice barges from Wongsen at the canal locks until their seals are checked.

[[being-kwisenregdtr|Second Daughter of the Registrar of the Clan of Moon Rises Over Still Water]], at Kwisen
: Courteous, exact and without deference to a foreigner, she receives every newcomer down the pass and opens a fresh page for each in the station's register.

The bodies
: [[affiliation-hvarnguides|The Hvarn]], who guide travelers over the pass and go no farther than the station; the clerks of the chancellery, one of whom is a [[being-chnclryclrk|Chancellery Clerk of Zhowen]], who receive papers, carry them between the halls and are for sale; the officers at each bridge foot, such as the [[being-brdgofcrzhwn|Bridge Officer of Zhowen]], who keep the day's list; the Speaker, heard only by those the day's list names, whom this frame never puts before the party; the Warden of the Foreign Quarter of Wongsen, Rinzho's officer, who keeps its walls and gates and watches the merchants inside them; and [[affiliation-haluozhao|the Black Lotus Society]], which trades in forged tablets.

## The Arcs

The frame follows the embassy from the pass to the court's verdict, and each arc turns one strand of the game.

1. **The door.** The leg as this frame runs it brings the embassy down by the Scholars' Door to Kwisen, where the Hvarn turn back, the escort meets it at the col and not a step sooner, and the registrar's daughter opens the first page the empire holds on it. The Kwisen adventure, set at [[place-kwisen|Kwisen]], runs the station's two hooks: weather shuts the col with the embassy still above the place where the escort waits, and the registrar's daughter finds a party with no caste to answer with and wants an account of it that her register can hold. Below the station the road runs down into [[place-mwonouplnd|The Mwonou Uplands]], Hozho's terrace country, where the southern famine has its heart.
2. **The filing.** In Zhowen the embassy lodges in the compound the chancellery assigns it and keeps to it until it is summoned. [[scenario-flngnwrgn|A Filing in the New Reign]] runs its papers: two clerks come to the compound gate for them, each for a different hall, the roll for the next hearing closes at sunset on the fourth day, and the hearing itself is held at the bridge foot and the gate-court.
3. **The port.** [[place-wongsen|Wongsen]] is Rinzho's seat and port, and its foreign merchants live in [[place-wongsenfrgnqrtr|The Foreign Quarter of Wongsen]], walled, watched and taxed. The Wongsen adventure, set in the quarter, runs its two hooks: a foreign merchant's trading classification is about to run out with his goods still in a warehouse, and a tablet bought from the wrong person in the quarter is either a forgery or a trap. A party of the embassy in Wongsen is in the port of the strongest of the three Princes who want the embassy turned, and it carries whatever classification the embassy holds by then.
4. **The game.** Through every arc the Princes' households move. Chengzho's officers want the embassy's papers in the right hall and its people classified high. The other three households pay for a misfiled paper, a delayed classification or a shaming at the bridge foot. Rinzho's rice barges wait at a canal lock above Zhowen on a question of seals, and the price of rice moves on both shores while they wait.

The door comes before the filing, because the embassy reaches Zhowen by the road down from Kwisen. The port runs whenever the embassy has a classification to travel on and a reason to send someone to the coast, before the hearing or after it. Each arc moves the game whether or not the party finishes it.

## Ways In {#ways-in}

1. **With the embassy.** The party serves the embassy as guards, scouts, clerks or trusted agents, as in [[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dúnavarre to Tantsou]], and comes over the pass with it.
2. **For the sponsor.** Chengzho's officers want the embassy's papers filed in the right office, its people classified quickly, and no embassy servant carrying a message to another Prince's officers.
3. **For the other three.** Rinzho's factor wants his barges released from the lock above Zhowen, Hozho wants to learn what Rinzho will take for rice besides cash, and Tuizho wants to know what Hozho has paid. A party in one household's pay is a party the other households watch.
4. **From the empire itself.** A party of Tantsou meets the embassy from the other side: a clerk of the chancellery sent to its gate, a Tanchei registrar rotated to a district on its road, or a grain merchant's clerk at the canal lock above Zhowen whose barges wait while the price rises.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- At Kwisen the station houses the embassy as long as the weather requires, and the embassy goes down the valley on whatever account of it the registrar's daughter writes.
- In Zhowen the embassy's papers go to the hall that cross-references the old era, the roll for the next hearing closes without them, and nobody crosses the bridge.
- The embassy keeps to its compound while the cross-referencing runs, perhaps for a year, and its classification waits as the three Princes want it to.
- The other households tell the court that Chengzho sponsored foreigners who could not file a paper.
- In Wongsen the merchant's classification runs out, and his goods stay in the warehouse when he cannot.

## The Secret {#the-secret}

**The translator.** Loris Taravel is an agent of the Vilérian Empire, paid for correspondence, military information and opportunities to spoil the negotiations. He wants the embassy dependent on Viléria, and a year's wait in Zhowen is a long argument for Viléria as intermediary. He has learned that Marrik Háravrel is the embassy's Spymaster, an office Marrik confides only to Aran, and he feeds Marrik false intelligence, so advice Marrik gives the ambassador can be Loris's at second hand. In Zhowen the fair copy Loris made is dated in the old reign, and the date alone sends the embassy's papers to the wrong hall.

**The households.** A household of one of the three Princes pays a clerk of the chancellery to collect the embassy's papers for the hall of the old era. Loris has never dealt with that household; the two serve one end without knowing each other.

This frame does not say whether the court grants direct relations, whether the Emperor's decree classifies the embassy, or which Prince gains by the outcome. Nor does it say whether Rinzho's sorcerers are behind the drought, which nobody has proven. The referee decides, and may let the party's actions decide.
