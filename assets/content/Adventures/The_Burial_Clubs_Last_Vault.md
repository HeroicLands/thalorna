## The Unwritten Vault

In the dry margin west of the river lies a rock-cut common tomb that no temple roll mentions. Its dead were walled in unspoken, and a struck priest of the Houses now raises them at dusk and sets them to hauling stone. The party goes in for loot, for a debt, for a refuge or for a widow's sake, and finds out why a place unwritten is a dangerous place. The adventure runs in five scenes, each of which can be met with force, stealth, guile or parley, and each of which states how play carries on if the party skips it.

Read [[place-clublstvlt|The Burial Club's Last Vault]] first. The adventure assumes the [[lore-khelathiclt|Khelathi]] ledger-ethic: an open entry follows a person into the [[place-zulaten|Zulaten]], a person's real protection is whoever will stand for them, and the poor fear nothing so much as dying unspoken.

### The Truth Behind the Vault

**What happened.** The quarrymen of Zel-Qedlu paid a club that bought each of them the third grade of burial. The club's master, **Wazlegir**, spent the club's last purse on a gifted boy of the village, **Qeltzin**, sending him to the temple school and from there into [[affiliation-garqeltnu|Gar-Qelt'nu]], so that the club would one day have a lector of its own and none of its dead would lie unspoken. Qeltzin rose to the rank of Lekhau. He was struck from the House, for reasons the House does not give and keeps no record of, before he could return. The club failed, a fever came, and its dead were walled up with no rite said.

**What Qeltzin believes.** Qeltzin came home to a hill of unspoken dead and could not speak the rite, because a struck man's words carry no House authority. He has convinced himself of a grievance: the club's last purse paid for his schooling, so the club's own accounts are not closed, and the dead owe the labor. He raises them at dusk and cuts a tomb for himself, because a man struck from the House's roll has no Name on any record and fears the Weighing. His working reads: _rise at dusk and work until the entry is closed._

**What is true.** The roll in the sealed chamber shows that the schooling was a prepaid fee. Every account in the club is closed, the dead owe nothing, and the only open entry in the whole roll is Qeltzin's own promise to come home. His working is looser than he knows. Because the Names of the dead were never set, the Shadow fills the gaps where Names should be, and every season the Unwrapped are less themselves. The vault is on its way to becoming a _zelalgit_, a thin blood-field, the Devourer's place.

### Hooks

- **The thief.** A drover sells the rumor that a poor club's savings lie in the sealed chamber at the back of its vault and that nobody has ever robbed it. The party takes the caravan road west to find out why.
- **The soldier or the deserter.** A soldier in flight from the Meglay is told the margin is empty. A mercenary hired to escort a temple's captain finds that three watch-priests walked west and did not return. Either way the one place with a roof and a door is the vault.
- **The leader.** A herder-chieftain of the western margin holds grazing under arrangement with the Halzi'a, and three of his men have gone to the red hills in a season. He offers pasture-rights in exchange for the matter being closed, and he cannot pay in anything else.
- **The wanderer.** A widow in a river town holds her husband's dues receipt in a clay jar and has been refused the rite at two temples, because the club's roll is not in the temples' hands. She asks to be taken to his niche.

### Scene One: The Haul-Way and the Walled Door

**Purpose.** To show that the dead are working, to teach the rule of dusk and dawn, and to make the door a problem the party solves rather than bludgeons.

**Situation.** A dressed ramp climbs from the quarry floor to the vault mouth, wide enough for a sledge. The door is walled up with fresh mortar over old, and three marks are cut deep in the jamb: the body-form of Gar-Qelt'nu, the waking-form of [[affiliation-garithnetau|Gar-Ithnetáu]] and the seal of [[affiliation-garqarquzu|Gar-Qar'kuzu]]. The seal refuses, and does not bar: a party can take the wall apart around it, and the break shows forever. At dusk the walling opens inward like a gate and the gang comes out to haul.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The Unwrapped.** Quarrymen dead for years, still in the cheap brown linen of the third grade, rotted at the hem. Their hands are the same hands, scarred and blunt, and their faces are slack and turned a little aside. They haul in step to a work-song nobody sings, and their lips move on the second line. They do not run, do not tire and do not pursue past the foot of the ramp. They strike whoever breaks the line of work, and they ignore whoever works with them. A gang of six moves at a time.
- **The cornerstone.** A block at the ramp's head carries a half-cut sign in the sacred hand, repeated down the face, and is the cornerstone of the thing being built below.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ Fighting the gang at night means fighting six at once with more behind them, and noise down the gallery wakes the rest. By day they stand idle where the dawn left them and can be broken one at a time, but then the door is shut.
- _Stealth._ By day the party can slip between the standing dead and take the wall apart. By night it can lie against the quarry wall until the gang has passed and enter behind it.
- _Guile._ A character who takes a corner of a sledge and hauls in step is let by. The working says _strike whoever breaks the work_, and a hauler does not break it.
- _Parley._ A character who sings the club's work-song, or knows someone who does, makes the line falter at the second verse for the length of a breath. That is not control, but it shows the dead remember.

**Clues and rewards.** The sign on the cornerstone is a Name repeated, and it is not the name of anyone in the village. The sledge-ropes are tied with a quarrymen's knot, and a hauler's wrist-cord carries a small copper token stamped with the club's mark. A character who takes a token has a proof of membership for Scene Two.

**Consequences.** Breaking the walled door announces the party's presence to anyone below by morning. A destroyed gang is counted: Qeltzin reckons his stone each dusk and knows how many blocks have not come down.

**If bypassed.** An earlier robber cut a shaft into the hill's back, a squeeze that opens into the middle of the gallery. The party starts Scene Three without having met Scene Two, and the dues-hall can be reached later by going back along the gallery.

### Scene Two: The Dues-Hall

**Purpose.** To make the vault a place of people and not merely a place of monsters, and to give the party the key to the roll chamber if they take it.

**Situation.** The first chamber inside the door is the club's dues-hall, a long bench, a table and shelves of clay tally-jars in rows, each stamped with a member's mark. A figure in wrapped linen sits upright at the table with a tally-stick across his knees. When anyone enters it says, in a dry level voice, the opening of the club's oldest formula: _Whose are you? Who stands for you?_

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Wazlegir.** The club's master, dead with his accounts closed and his wits partly intact, because no working could bind a man who had entered his own Name in the roll. He is one of the few things in the vault not entirely Qeltzin's. He keeps the hall as he kept it in life and remembers his office better than his face. His jaw is bound with a linen strip, and his speech is slow and exact. He wants dues from every visitor, a copper gezan a head, or a member's word. He does not fight unless the hall is robbed, and then he fights as a man whose purse is being taken: without art, with no sense of mercy, with total persistence.
- **The tally.** Wazlegir's stick is notched and the shelves are full. A character who reads the jars finds one stamped _Qeltzin_ with the seal broken.

**Player choices.**

- _Pay._ A copper gezan a head takes the party past Wazlegir with nothing else asked.
- _Sponsor a name._ A character with a club token, or a living member, can stand for a stranger. Wazlegir enters the stranger's true name on a new jar. The seal on the roll chamber then recognizes that name. The cost is real: a name written in a dead man's ledger is a name the vault knows.
- _Parley._ He will tell what he knows of the Debtor, as he calls Qeltzin, and of the roll. He says the Debtor has never passed the dues-hall and comes round it by the quarry. He says the roll is behind the seal and that only the club's own may open it. He does not know the dead are being worked.
- _Guile._ A false name is a false entry. Wazlegir sees through it with the exactness of a clerk, and the penalty in the club's formula is a fine in copper, then exclusion, then the stick.
- _Force._ Fighting him makes noise that carries down the gallery. Destroying him closes this scene and leaves the roll chamber closed to anyone not already a member.

**Clues and rewards.** The broken jar stamped _Qeltzin_, and Wazlegir's account of the Debtor: _He owes the club. We paid. We were very proud. He did not come._ A sponsored name is admitted to the roll chamber. Wazlegir's tally-stick is a half-token, matched by the half in the roll chamber.

**Consequences.** A party that pays and passes without learning anything has no idea what the working's condition is and meets Scene Four blind. A party that fights loses its easiest route to the chamber.

**If bypassed.** The roll chamber's seal can be broken by force at a cost: the break is loud, and the stone behind it carries the Unwrapped's attention for the rest of the night.

### Scene Three: The Gallery of Niches

**Purpose.** To spring a trap that costs the party time and options, so that dusk finds it inside.

**Situation.** The gallery runs deep into the hill, a niche to each wall-span, and by day it is silent. The Unwrapped stand in rows between the niches, still, with their hands at their sides. The floor is the haul-way, rutted by sledge-runners. Mid-gallery, a block of fine-dressed stone sits across the way as if set down mid-journey. The block is the plug of the quarry's old sand-sluice, and moving it brings the sluice down.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The standing dead.** Between the niches are dozens of the Unwrapped, motionless in daylight, who wake when the light at the door fails. A character who brushes one finds the linen dry and light and the flesh beneath it like leather.
- **The sand-slip.** When the plug moves, a slope of fine red sand pours from the sluice-mouth and fills the gallery behind the party to chest height, then to the roof. It does not move quickly. It moves faster than a man can dig through.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ Digging back through the slip takes hours and carries the party past dusk.
- _Stealth._ A party that does not touch the plug passes it by squeezing along the niche-wall. The block was set in the way to be shifted, and a cautious character notices that.
- _Guile._ The quarrymen cut their sluices with a drain beneath: the _qatlun_, a hidden channel, which carries the water of the winter rains out under the hill. A character who knows quarry-work, or reads the marks on the niche-walls that show the drain's line, finds the lid and a crawl-way beneath.
- _Parley._ There is no one to speak to by day. After dusk the rows wake and walk.

**Clues and rewards.** The drain runs under the gallery and comes up behind the great hall of Scene Four. On the way a character finds the dues-box of a small family: a few silver qelu and a child's amulet. The niche-walls carry a quarrymen's inscription in the common hand: the names of the members, cut as a row, with a gap at the end where the last names should go.

**Consequences.** Anyone left on the far side of the slip is cut off from the entrance. If dusk comes with the party in the gallery, the rows wake and the party is in the way of the work.

**If bypassed.** A party that came by the robbers' shaft or the drain skips the plug and reaches the great hall directly.

### Scene Four: The Cut Hall and the Sealed Chamber

**Purpose.** The confrontation with Qeltzin, who is working at the one door the Unwrapped cannot open.

**Situation.** The gallery opens into a great cut hall, a quarry gallery turned into a tomb-in-the-making. Dressed blocks are stacked in courses to the roof, each face cut with the same Name in the sacred hand, row on row on row. At the back of the hall is a door of plain stone with a seal cut in the jamb, and in front of it stands a thin man in the remains of a Lekhau's robe, with a mallet in one hand and a reed pen in a sling at his hip. He is cutting a sign, and does not turn round.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Qeltzin.** A struck Lekhau of Gar-Qelt'nu, grey with lime-dust, thin in the way of a man who has not eaten for the work. His hands are black to the wrist, and a close look shows the black is not dirt and does not rub off. He speaks the formal diction of the House, precise, tired, and slightly wrong in a way that is hard to name: the voice comes out a half-beat behind his lips. He holds the Unwrapped by speech and by a knotted cord at his belt, and they stop when he stops. He can seal a threshold against the party, bind a tongue to silence and strike a name from a person's hearing, so that those who give their names freely to him find that they are not remembered by anyone for an hour. He is not a coward and not a fool. He is a man who has been right about everything except one thing.
- **The hall's Unwrapped.** Twenty or thirty, hauling and setting, who turn on his word and fight as a block, without hurry.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ Strike Qeltzin down and the dead stop where they stand, except that the last instruction he gave is carried out: the sign he was cutting is finished by the nearest hand, wrongly, and the hall draws breath. The stone listens. This is the cheap victory and the costly one.
- _Silence him._ Bind his tongue and the Unwrapped stand idle but do not lie down. The working's condition still holds.
- _Stealth._ Bypass him through the drain and reach the sealed chamber door from the side. He knows the hall's every sound.
- _Guile._ Offer to open the chamber for him. A member of the club (a sponsored name, a token, a living widow) is the only one the seal admits. He will bargain, and he will lie about what he is looking for.
- _Parley._ He wants to be named. He says so within three exchanges: _I have no Name on any record. I am struck. Unwritten. Do you know what that costs at the Weighing?_ A character who answers as a person and not as an enemy opens the door to Scene Five without spending a blow.

**Clues and rewards.** The sign repeated on every block is Qeltzin's own name, and one stroke in each repetition is not his hand. The Shadow has been writing in the gaps. Qeltzin's tools, if taken, are a House set of cutters stamped with Gar-Qelt'nu's mark, which is worth a great deal to any House that wants to know where its struck man went.

**Consequences.** If the sign is finished wrongly, the hall becomes a _zelalgit_ that season, and the Unwrapped stay on their feet at dawn. If Qeltzin lives to open the chamber, he reads the roll.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes through the drain to the chamber door unnoticed can open the chamber with a member's name or break the seal. Qeltzin, working a dozen paces off, does not turn until the seal breaks.

### Scene Five: The Roll

**Purpose.** To reward the party, to reveal the vault's truth and to leave the ending open to decision.

**Situation.** Behind the seal is a small dry chamber, shelved to the roof with clay jars, a tablet to each member. The air is still and cool. On a stand in the middle lies the club's roll in its open form, a long tablet in the common hand, and beside it a flat chest.

**NPCs and threats.** If Qeltzin has been admitted he stands at the stand with his hands over the tablet, afraid to read it. If he has been killed or bound, the chamber is quiet. The Unwrapped hold still at the threshold, as they do for a member entering, and then wake again unless the roll is read.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the roll aloud._ The first column holds the members and the sum each paid. Every entry has a closing stroke. The last entry is not a member's. It records the sum paid for the education of a boy of the village _to be the club's lector_, with a closing stroke already made, and under it Wazlegir's note: _Paid in full. Open entry: the boy's coming home._ The working's condition is _until the entry is closed_, and a character who closes Qeltzin's entry by saying _he is home_ ends it. The Unwrapped lie down.
- _Take the roll._ A temple of Weth'ur or a House that wants to prove a lector was schooled at a club's expense pays for it.
- _Take the chest._ A few silver qelu and a mass of copper tokens, a lector's contract, and the club's amulet-mold.
- _Speak the rite._ A priest of [[lore-wethurdty|Weth'ur]] or a Lekhau of Gar-Ithnetáu who reads the Names and says the Westward words puts the dead to their rest. Without one, the Unwrapped lie still and the vault remains unspoken.

**Clues and rewards.** The roll itself. The chest. If Qeltzin lives, a confession: he was struck for speaking a pauper's rite for nothing, as the club had hoped he would for them. A character who can show a House that evidence may reopen his entry. Treasure is modest: a few silver qelu, a handful of copper, the House cutters and a lector's contract worth more to a scribe than to a thief.

**Consequences.** If the roll is read and the rite said, the vault is stilled and the _zelalgit_ does not form. If the roll is read and no rite is said, the vault is still but unspoken, and the Shadow lingers in the gaps. If the roll is not read, the working continues.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves without the roll leaves the working unbroken. The widow in the river town still has her receipt, and a new crew comes with the next hook.

### Aftermath

- **The roll read and the rite said.** The dead lie down at dusk and stay down. The caravan road is quiet. The watch-priests of [[place-khelzuret|Khelzuret]] enter Zel-Qedlu on their roll and send keepers. Qeltzin, if he lives, goes to the House with the roll in his hands and may be taken back or struck again. The widow is given her husband's niche and his name, and has the rite said.
- **The roll read and no rite said.** The Unwrapped lie still, and the vault is quiet in daylight. The thin field forms over a year. Another party is needed before the next fever season, with a priest.
- **Qeltzin dead and the sign finished.** The hall draws breath. The Unwrapped stay on their feet at dawn. They walk west, then to the canal heads. The Meglay burn the hill, and the margin is closed to herders.
- **The party bypassed the vault.** The working continues. Stone goes into a tomb that is no one's. The caravan road shifts east, and the drovers add half a day to their journey.
- **The House learns where its struck man went.** [[affiliation-garqeltnu|Gar-Qelt'nu]] and Gar-Ithnetáu each claim the right to deal with him, and each claims the other should answer for the dead.
