## Overview {#overview}

The crown of [[affiliation-kngdmglvrn|Galvaren]] buys its salt from the pans of [[place-threskon|Threskon]] and sells it to the curing houses of [[place-durvolka|Durvolka]] at its own price, and for the second year running the price has risen. The curing-house mistresses of the Strand have begun to buy from a ship that anchors outside the bay at night. The Warden of the Strand wants the ship named. The crown's buyers have complained to the council of [[affiliation-frctyvlthr|Valthari]] that a Valthari ship is selling past them, and the Reeve of Threskon, [[being-oltremobraz|Oltremo Brazis]], has been told to find out whose. Meanwhile the Masters' Hall elects in the autumn, and somebody is paying the herring crews to vote.

The party enters on any of three errands: the king's, the mistresses', or the Reeve's. [[being-brazemabetsu|Brazema Betsuris]], who commands the king's six galleys, thinks she knows the hull.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The ship.** The night ship sails from Threskon and carries the salt of the Reeve's own house. The Reeve asks the masters of the town's salt ships the council's question in public and keeps his own house's master out of his questions. The ship sells to the mistresses at a price under the crown's, and the mistresses pay in silver.

**The silver.** The house does not spend its profit on more salt. A factor of the house in Durvolka pays a shipmaster, [[being-tsalaornet|Tsala Ornetis]], who lost two boats on the Far Grounds this year and stands against the king's candidate for one of Durvolka's three seats at the Long Table. Her paymasters give the herring crews silver on the eve of the Masters' Hall and no more. She does not know where it comes from, and the crews do not ask.

**The threat.** Somebody else is threatening the crews who take the silver. It is the king's candidate's brother-in-law, who runs the Strand's net lofts and is afraid of losing the king's custom. He has hired two men to follow a crew home and leave a net cut. He does not know what the silver is, only that it is buying votes.

**What the house wants.** The Reeve's house wants a Long Table whose harbor side can force the crown to stop buying and selling the kingdom's salt, so that Valthari sells to the curing houses directly. A Tsala Ornetis on the Table, with [[being-korvaththal|Korvath Thaluris]] of Ulhera shouting beside her, would be most of the harbor side. If the crown loses the salt, it loses the revenue that pays for its galleys. [[being-ganothervo|Ganoth Ervothandis]] would then need silver from somewhere, and Lyenrik has the richest purse in Tarvénia. The house has not planned the Lyenrik half. It will take the benefit.

**The king.** Ganoth is a careful man with the crown's money, and he does not yet know the crown's salt is the target. He sees pirates on the salt route, riots on the Strand and a harbor side that calls him a countinghouse king. He wants the ship named before the Table sits.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The ship is a smuggler. Nobody on it is a villain. The Reeve is doing a house's work and no more. The scenario ends when the ship is named, sunk, bought or warned, and the votes are cast.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The leader.** Ganoth, through his Keeper of the Seal, asks a party to name the night ship before the Table sits.
- **The mercenary.** Brazema Betsuris asks a party to row out with her on a night the fog is down.
- **The thief.** The mistresses of the Strand pay a party to see that the Warden of the Strand does not find the ship.
- **The wanderer.** A herring crew's cut net sends its master to ask a party who is following him home.

## The Opening {#opening}

The Strand at the turn of the tide. The fog is in, and the boats go out by bell and by shout. A curing-house mistress comes down to the fish market under the long slate roof with her sleeves rolled.

> "The crown's salt is a quarter dearer than last spring," she says, "and the crown's salt is not the salt in my cellar."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Strand

**Purpose.** Show the price and the cellar.

**Situation.** The fish market bench, the curing houses where the cod is split and stacked, the net lofts and the cellars where the large spiders of the coast kill the rats.

**NPCs and threats.** The Warden of the Strand, who hears quarrels at his bench at dawn. The curing-house mistresses, who say nothing to him. The king's Keeper of the Salt, the most resented man on the coast, who sits in the salt-house at the Lyenrik Gate.

**Player choices.** Take the king's errand, take the mistresses', or ask what the crown's salt costs a cask of herring.

**Clues and rewards.** The salt in the cellars is whiter than the crown's and comes in casks stamped with a Valthari house's mark, scraped off. A mistress says the ship's boat comes in under the gorse of the headland.

**Consequences.** The party knows what to look for, and so do the Warden's men.

**If bypassed.** Brazema Betsuris puts her own oars in the water.

### Scene Two: The Masters' Hall

**Purpose.** The vote, the silver and the threat.

**Situation.** The Masters' Hall on Masters' Row in the days before the autumn election. The masters of the harbor meet to choose three of their number for the Long Table.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-tsalaornet|Tsala Ornetis]], loud and sure of her case. [[being-korvaththal|Korvath Thaluris]] of Ulhera, who has come up the coast to speak against the crown's price and is the first voice of the harbor side. Herring crews, some paid and some frightened. The two men who follow crews home.

**Player choices.** Watch the crews, follow the silver, protect a crew, or tell the king's candidate's brother-in-law what the silver is.

**Clues and rewards.** The silver is Valthari coin, minted in the city's name. A crew master shows a cut net and does not say who he thinks did it.

**Consequences.** If the party protects the crews, Tsala Ornetis is elected by a narrow vote. If it exposes the silver, her seat is in doubt and so is the party's welcome on the Strand.

**If bypassed.** The vote goes the way the silver does.

### Scene Three: The Bay at Night

**Purpose.** The ship itself.

**Situation.** Outside the bay, in fog. [[being-brazemabetsu|Brazema Betsuris]] in a fishing boat with muffled oars, and a ship at anchor with no lamp.

**NPCs and threats.** The night ship's master and crew, who will sell, run or fight depending on the party's choice. A curing house's boat alongside. Brazema, who recognizes the hull and says nothing.

**Player choices.** Board the ship, follow the boat to the headland, ring the harbor bell, or let it be.

**Clues and rewards.** The cargo is white salt, not crown salt. The papers name a house of Valthari. A crew member says the master takes his orders from the Reeve's factor.

**Consequences.** If the ship is taken, the crown has its smuggler and the house has a reason to fight. If it is warned, the sale happens at a safer anchorage.

**If bypassed.** The ship sells and sails with the dawn tide.

### Scene Four: The Lagoon

**Purpose.** The Reeve, and what the council knows.

**Situation.** [[place-threskon|Threskon]]. The salt sheds, the pans in squares of gray and white, the channel across the dunes, the temple of Pozeras.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-oltremobraz|Oltremo Brazis]], Reeve of Threskon, patient and courteous, who asks the party the council's question again. The masters of the salt ships, who say they sell only at the crown's quay. The house's own master, who is not in town.

**Player choices.** Name the house to the Reeve, bring the name to the council, keep it, or sell the name to the crown's buyers.

**Clues and rewards.** The house's own sheds hold more salt than the pans could make. The Reeve says, if pressed, that the crown's price is a thing the council could take up.

**Consequences.** If the name reaches the Konsezarath, the council must choose between a house and the charter's good name. If it reaches the king, Ganoth has an enemy he can name.

**If bypassed.** The council hears the Reeve's account and no one else's.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The mistresses go on buying. The Table sits, Tsala Ornetis takes her seat beside Korvath Thaluris, and the harbor side demands galleys south and a lower price for salt in one motion. Ganoth refuses the galleys and delays the price, and the Strand riots in the winter.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The crown of Galvaren is poorer than it was, and the king is looking for silver. A party that named the ship has made the Reeve's house an enemy and has Ganoth's thanks. One that left it alone has watched a harbor side take the Table. One that exposed the silver has put a shipmaster's seat in doubt and given the Strand a grievance against the crown.
