## Overview {#overview}

The snow is going off the saddle of [[place-grumagando|Grumagando]], and the [[place-westernmrch|Western March]] is waiting at [[place-kemthoza|Kemthoza]] for the season of [[lore-summerwar|The Summer War]]. The barons will meet in the fort's hall when [[being-kaldrokanar|Kaldro Kanarris]] says the saddle is open, swear at the oath stone in the yard and go down into [[place-talothe|the Talote]], and the first host over sets the terms of the season.

Before the hosts go, the bough-carriers go. At the thaw [[being-bedarrekulm|Bedarrek Ulmis]] went down the pass under the green bough with the ransom for a boy of Kaldro's valley, as he has gone down every spring for twenty years. He should have been back days ago. The boy's mother waits at Kemthoza's gate. Meanwhile one baron, [[being-tharozozurg|Tharozo Zurgaregi]], has hired a coast company, [[affiliation-kraveldo|Kraveldo]], and sent none of his men up to the yard. Above the March, the Áelendan clan of [[place-mirgonlodge|Mirgon Lodge]] has found the tracks of men on its high pasture below its hallowed grove.

The party enters on the mother's errand, the baron's or the clan's, or on [[being-srdlvnthrvkknghtwstrnmrchs|Sir Dalven Thorvek]]'s old wish to see the dead below the pass received.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The count.** Tharozo's valley came home short two seasons running and wintered hungry. He asked Bedarrek, his own valley's man, to count the remounts in the muster's yard at [[place-durvalis|Durvalis]] while he waited there for the boy. Bedarrek cut the count as notches in the stick of his bough. The herald who receives the bough-carriers at the gate of Durvalis, [[being-arislkrsvr|Arisla Korisvar]], saw the notches when he laid the bough down. She took him and the ransom silver into the keeping of the [Winter Assize]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} rather than hand him to the marshal's officers, who want him hanged as a spy. The Assize sits until the thaw opens the road, and it has his case before it at its last sittings.

**What the bough is.** The green bough is a fir bough cut fresh and carried in the hand. Under it a carrier goes up or down the war pass with ransom silver and comes back with the ransomed, and both sides let him pass, because both sides count the men it brings home. Nothing but custom holds it. If the Assize rules that the March used the bough to spy, the Talote will refuse it for the season, and every man held for ransom on either side stays held. The boy of Kaldro's valley is held by the border family of [[being-ryldnbrnwyld|Rilden Branvilde]], who will sell him down the march if the silver is not his by the time the muster rides.

**The crossing.** Tharozo means to go over before the barons meet, and not by the war pass. North of Grumagando the shepherds' way climbs from his valley past the Mirindi's boundary cairn above Mirgon Lodge, crosses the clan's high pasture under its hallowed grove, goes over the ridge and comes down on the Talote side above the [[place-bldfldmalnebris|Blood-field of Malnebris]]. His men have walked it twice since the thaw. At the thaw the Talote's muster rides up from Durvalis and camps in the meadows below the field, where the valley opens, to hold the foot of the pass. A host coming down the shepherds' way falls on that camp from above, behind anyone watching Grumagando, and Tharozo means to drive off its horses before it is whole.

**The company.** [[being-pramizamozk|Prámiza Mozkarreta]]'s contract with Tharozo binds Kraveldo to go down the war pass with his host in the season and come home with it. It names Grumagando. Tharozo has not told her the road he means to take. A company that keeps every word of a contract and nothing it does not say is free of a road the contract does not name, and Prámiza knows it the moment she hears the road.

**The clan.** The Mirindi hold the high pasture and the grove under the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"}. Their Warden, [[being-tergadanmiri|Térgadan Mirindi]], already wants a herder of Kaldro's valley who drove a flock into the grove last autumn. The elder [[being-randiramiri|Rándira Mirindi]] has held the young men back from the cairn once. A host on the shepherds' way at night is a second violation in one year, and the young men will meet it at the cairn. That is the beginning of a mountain-war, which the crown of [[affiliation-kingdmtrvn|Tarvénia]] settles through Élavendri hands and makes the March pay for.

**The dead.** No rite of settlement has been said over Malnebris, because it would have to be said in the campaign season on ground both crowns claim, and neither crown will give the other's priests safe conduct. The bough is not a crown's grant. [[being-tordimakalv|Tordima Kalvezis]], the Threshold Keeper of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]] at Durvalis, has wanted the rite said for years. A priest walking to the field beside a bough-carrier, with the silver of a ransom in his satchel, is under the bough's custom and under no crown's.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Nobody here is a villain. Tharozo is a hungry baron who has stopped believing the custom of the season is for men like him. Bedarrek did what his lord asked. The scenario turns on whether the bough survives the spring, and on what the March does with a baron who breaks two customs at once.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The leader.** Kaldro Kanarris asks a party to ride to the other valleys and learn which baron means to cheat the custom, because two of them want the first crossing and one has stopped coming to the yard.
- **The wanderer.** The mother of the boy whose ransom Bedarrek carried asks a party to go down the pass under a bough and find out whether he reached Durvalis.
- **The mercenary.** Prámiza Mozkarreta hires a party to ride the March's valleys and tell her what her baron's neighbors think of her contract.
- **The pious.** Sir Dalven Thorvek asks a party to find a way to say the rites for the dead below the pass, and to start by asking who on the far side would say them.
- **The outsider.** Térgadan Mirindi asks a party to learn whose men have been on the clan's pasture below the grove.

## The Opening {#opening}

Kemthoza's yard in the last days of the snow. Men of three valleys are camped against the walls with their mules, the wall-walk watch calls the snow line on the saddle at every change, and a woman of Kaldro's valley sits on the mounting block by the gate with her hands in her lap.

> "He goes down first every year," she says. "He brings them home in six days. This is the ninth."

> [!TIP]
> Let the party see the yard before anyone asks them for anything: the banners by valley, the oath stone, the stack of fir boughs cut for the carriers, the gap along the north wall where Tharozo's valley camps every other spring.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Yard at Kemthoza

**Purpose.** The season, the custom and the missing carrier.

**Situation.** Kemthoza's yard and hall in the days before the barons meet. The watch on the wall-walk, the hosts waiting below, the boy's mother at the gate.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-kaldrokanar|Kaldro Kanarris]], curt and watching the saddle. [[being-srdlvnthrvkknghtwstrnmrchs|Sir Dalven Thorvek]], come up for the season. The boy's mother. Men of the waiting valleys, who say aloud that Tharozo's valley has sent nobody up and that a coast company has marched into it.

> [!NOTE]
> The men of the March's hosts are run as [[being-elsrdrgn|Élsaro Durgana]], a stock sellsword.

**Player choices.** Take Kaldro's errand to the other valleys, take the mother's errand down the pass, or listen to the yard.

**Clues and rewards.** Kaldro names the day the barons will meet: the sixth from now, if the weather holds. A muleteer says Tharozo's steward bought fodder for more mules than the valley owns. Sir Dalven says the bough has never failed in his lifetime, and that a carrier who does not come back is either dead on the pass or held. Kaldro will give a party a fresh bough to go down under, if it carries the boy's ransom question with it and nothing else.

**Consequences.** A party that asks about Tharozo openly in the yard has the question carried up his valley before it arrives.

**If bypassed.** Kaldro sends his own man to Tharozo's valley, who is told the baron is ill.

### Scene Two: The Upper Valley

**Purpose.** The baron, the company and the contract.

**Situation.** Tharozo's tower over the one village of the high valley north of Kemthoza's, half a day's ride away. Kraveldo is camped in the meadow below the tower, a hundred and twenty crossbowmen with their pavises stacked and the crow painted on them. A pale track climbs the slope north of the valley's head toward the clans' ground.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-tharozozurg|Tharozo Zurgaregi]], loud, generous with his wine and offended by any question about the season. [[being-pramizamozk|Prámiza Mozkarreta]], amused by the March and counting her men every morning. Her clerk, who reads the contract aloud to anyone the captain tells him to. The valley's shepherds, who know where the baron's men have been walking.

> [!NOTE]
> Kraveldo's crossbowmen are run as [[being-thesidlvnr|Thésira Delvanor]], a stock sellsword. Tharozo's own men are run as [[being-elsrdrgn|Élsaro Durgana]].

**Player choices.** Accept Tharozo's offer of a share of the season, ask the clerk to read the contract, follow the shepherds' track, or tell Prámiza what the track is.

**Clues and rewards.** The contract says the company goes "down Grumagando with the baron's host in the season, and home with it". A shepherd boy says the baron's men went up past the Mirindi's cairn twice and came back over the ridge. The fodder is stacked at the head of the valley, not at the road to Kemthoza.

**Consequences.** A party that shows Prámiza the road has the company's choice in its hands. She reads the clause twice and says that her company is free of any road the contract does not name. Then she asks what the baron will pay to name it. Tharozo, told that she knows, can match the price, go without her, or give up the road.

**If bypassed.** Prámiza learns the road on the night Tharozo marches, and her company marches with him because it is already paid to the day.

### Scene Three: The Cairn above Mirgon Lodge

**Purpose.** The clan's ground and the price of crossing it.

**Situation.** The boundary cairn on the herders' path above Mirgon Lodge, a day's climb above Kemthoza's valley and a long morning from the head of Tharozo's. The lodge below in its fold of fir, the high pasture above, the stand of old pines in the corrie under the ridge, and the tracks below the grove where the snow is still lying.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-tergadanmiri|Térgadan Mirindi]], the clan's Warden, who gives a warning once. [[being-randiramiri|Rándira Mirindi]], who has held the young men back and will not do it twice. The clan's young men, at the cairn with spears and dogs.

> [!NOTE]
> The clan's young men are run as [[being-chlbrhydyn|Chelebe Riddin]], a stock hunter. A fight at the cairn is one a seasoned party can win, and winning it starts the mountain-war the clan is trying to avoid.

> [!WARNING]
> A party that walks past the cairn uncalled has done what Tharozo's men did. Térgadan turns it back once, and Rándira's patience ends with the second.

**Player choices.** Call at the cairn and wait, tell the clan whose tracks they are, carry the clan's word to Kaldro, ask the Wardens to stand at the cairn on the night of the crossing, or offer to bring Kaldro's herder up to answer.

**Clues and rewards.** Térgadan can read the tracks: twenty men or so, twice, going over the ridge and coming back. He knows where the shepherds' way comes down on the far side, above the field. If the party brings Kaldro's herder up to answer at the cairn, the clan has one quarrel with the valleys instead of two, and Rándira will hold the young men back until the barons have spoken.

**Consequences.** If the clan stands at the cairn on the night of the crossing, Tharozo must fight through it, turn back, or go round by a way that adds a day and brings his host down after the muster is whole. If the party tells Kaldro what the clan has seen, the other barons can stop Tharozo themselves.

**If bypassed.** The young men are at the cairn on the night Tharozo marches, and they meet him there.

### Scene Four: Under the Bough

**Purpose.** The war pass in the last of the snow, and the custom that carries a party down it.

**Situation.** Grumagando from Kemthoza's gate to the meadows below the field: the switchbacks, the saddle with its cairns and old snow, the steep descent, and the narrow road between the ridge and the stream at Malnebris. The party carries a fresh fir bough in the hand.

**NPCs and threats.** Riders of the Talote's border families, who meet anyone on the road. A Talote picket at the foot of the field. The field itself on a still night.

> [!NOTE]
> The border riders are run as [[being-dornsyndlr|Doren Sindalre]], a stock sellsword, and the picket as [[being-brygrlyskl|Brigar Liskal]], a stock guard.

> [!TIP]
> On the first still night below the saddle, [[place-bldfldmalnebris|Malnebris]] is loud: horses, orders in two languages, a shield-line giving way, lights moving on the road. Nothing is there in the morning. The riders who meet the party at dawn say the loud night was a warning, and ask whether the March is coming down early.

**Player choices.** Keep to the bough's custom and answer the riders' questions, ride on past the picket, or go round it off the road.

**Clues and rewards.** The riders honor the bough, as they always have, but they are colder than they were last year, and one says that the last carrier is "with the Assize, for what his stick said". The picket at the foot of the field has orders from the marshal's officers to send any carrier to Durvalis under guard.

**Consequences.** A party that keeps to the custom arrives at Durvalis under the bough. One that goes round the picket arrives as spies.

**If bypassed.** The party learns at Durvalis that it has been watched since the saddle.

### Scene Five: The Last Sitting of the Assize

**Purpose.** The bough on trial, and the people who could save it.

**Situation.** Durvalis on the last days of the Winter Assize. The town is filling for the muster, the garrison is busy, and the Assize sits in the great room over the gate to hear what the winter had no time for. Bedarrek Ulmis is held in a room off the gate yard with the ransom silver sealed in a chest beside him.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-arislkrsvr|Arisla Korisvar]], the herald who took Bedarrek into the Assize's keeping, who wants the bough's custom kept and her own judgment vindicated. The marshal's officer, who wants a hanging at the gate before the muster rides. [[being-ryldnbrnwyld|Rilden Branvilde]], head of the border family that holds Kaldro's boy, who wants his silver and suspects everyone. [[being-tordimakalv|Tordima Kalvezis]], the Threshold Keeper, who is in the town for the Assize's dead and asks every Tarvenan she meets for the names of last season's dead. Bedarrek, ashamed, who will not name who asked him.

> [!NOTE]
> The marshal's officer and the gate guards are run as [[being-brygrlyskl|Brigar Liskal]]. Rilden's riders are run as [[being-dornsyndlr|Doren Sindalre]].

**Player choices.** Argue Bedarrek's case before the Assize, pay Rilden from the sealed silver or their own, tell the Assize who asked Bedarrek to count, warn the muster that a host may come down the shepherds' way, or bring Tordima to the field under the bough.

**Clues and rewards.** Arisla's case is that the bough is the herald's business and not the marshal's, and that a carrier who abuses it has broken his own word, not the bough's. If the Assize hears that the count was one baron's doing and not the March's, it can rule that way: the man is punished, the bough stands. Rilden will release the boy to the party for the sealed silver if the Assize releases it. Tordima will walk to Malnebris beside a bough-carrier with a ransom in his satchel, and she says so plainly.

**Consequences.** If the party names Tharozo, the bough survives the spring and Bedarrek goes home to his valley, which will not thank him. The Talote also learns that a March host may come down above the field, and the muster moves before Tharozo arrives. If the party pays Rilden and takes Bedarrek out of Durvalis by night, the bough is refused for the season and the party is hunted to the foot of the field.

**If bypassed.** The Assize rises without a ruling, the marshal's officers take Bedarrek, and the bough is refused.

> [!WARNING]
> Naming Tharozo to the Talote warns an enemy of a Tarvenan baron's crossing, and the March counts that as treachery, whoever does it. A party that tells Kaldro first and the Talote nothing can save the bough only by arguing that the count was Bedarrek's own folly. Bedarrek does not deny it. Arisla does not accept it without a name, and the Assize follows her.

### Scene Six: The Rite on the Road

**Purpose.** The dead below the pass, if the party wants them received.

**Situation.** The road at Malnebris, between the ridge and the stream, at dusk. Tordima Kalvezis in a plain hood, a bough-carrier beside her with the boy's ransom or the boy himself, the Talote's muster camped in the meadows below, and the March's watchers on the saddle above.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-tordimakalv|Tordima Kalvezis]]. Border riders of both sides, who come to watch and stay out of bowshot. Whatever comes down the shepherds' way that night, if Tharozo has not been stopped.

**Player choices.** Stand with Tordima while she says the rite, keep the watchers back, or send word up the saddle that the March may come and witness.

**Clues and rewards.** Tordima says the rite as she would say it at a graveside, at length, naming the dead whose names she has, and placing all of them in the god's keeping. The field is quiet for the night she says it, as a field is while a rite is said over it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scenario does not decide whether the settlement takes, and the land's people will not know for a season. Sélmoros's priests say the rite works perhaps half the time. The border families will argue for years about whether the field is quieter, and nobody can count a noise.

**Consequences.** If the rite is said under the bough, a priest has stood on Malnebris in the season with both sides watching, and neither crown granted it. Both crowns will hear of it.

**If bypassed.** The field stays as it is, and the March sends its men past it in the season.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The Assize rises without a ruling. The marshal's officers hang Bedarrek at the gate of Durvalis, the Talote refuses the green bough for the season, and Rilden Branvilde sells Kaldro's boy down the march. On the fifth night Tharozo takes his host and Kraveldo up the shepherds' way. The Mirindi's young men meet them at the cairn in the dark, and men of both sides die on the clan's pasture under the grove. Tharozo goes over the ridge anyway, comes down above the field and drives off part of the muster's horses at dawn. His valley eats through the next winter.

The barons meet at Kemthoza to find the season already begun on terms none of them set. A mountain-war runs on the slopes above the March through the summer, and the crown settles it before the snow through Élavendri intermediaries, at the March's cost. Below the pass the Talote's border families take no ransom and give none, and every man captured on either side that season is held, sold or killed.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that saved the bough has done the March a service that every family on both sides will remember, and has made an enemy of a baron and a herald's gratitude. One that named Tharozo to the Talote is welcome at Durvalis and suspected at Kemthoza. One that turned Kraveldo has Prámiza Mozkarreta's respect, which is worth a contract later, and Tharozo's lasting grudge. One that brought Kaldro's herder up to the cairn has the Mirindi's word at the cairn, and a way onto the clan's ground that no baron has.

If Tordima said the rite under the bough, the crowns of Tarvénia and Provénzia both hear that a priest stood on Malnebris in the season without either one's leave. The crown of [[affiliation-kngdmprvnz|Provénzia]] will ask its marshal how, and the High King will be asked by the barons whether the bough is now a road for priests.
