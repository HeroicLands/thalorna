## Overview {#overview}

In the hiring yards behind the quays of [[place-valthari|Valthari]], the crossbowmen of a high valley of [[affiliation-kngdmstrth|Asturath]] have waited out a month of the seamen's strike. Their captain, [[being-kanarrektsuk|Kanarrek Tsukurreta]], signed them to a Taruzelian buyer at the stone in their own valley, and the contract names a ship, a quay and a day. The ship lies at anchor in the roads because no crew will sign on until the owners raise the share. [[being-hiltherbetsu|Hilther Betsuris]] begs among the company's tents and knows the day by heart. Every tide past it takes a tenth of the pay.

The valley is the one the High King holds in his own right, and the company's pay is most of what the valley has. Kanarrek wants a ship, any ship, and he wants it before the day. Two others want something of the party in the same week: [[being-dalenavlrn|Dálena Valaron]], whom the owners' council has asked to sail and break the refusal, and the seamen, who want to know which way she will sail.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The ship and the note.** The ship at anchor belongs to a house on the owners' council. The same house holds the note on the family ship of Dálena's father, and the same house, through a factor in the Taruzeli Quarter, bought the paper the Garrundeta valley's steward has borrowed against for years. The steward is an honest man who raised silver against the company's coming pay and told the old king nothing, because the old king does not ask. If the day passes, the pay is forfeit and the note falls due. The house will then hold a claim on the valley of the High King of Tarvénia.

**The strike's use.** The house did not call the strike. It has found that the strike is useful and does not hurry to end it. It has no wish to break a High King's valley. It wishes to hold, when the hearth goes out, a debt that every mountain lord will see as Valthari's hand on the crown's own house, and to spend that debt on the charter.

**The ruling.** The Arbitrators at [[place-charterhall|the Charter Hall]] must decide whether the seamen's refusal excuses a house that could not sail. If it does, the same ruling excuses the company's late embarkation under its contract's own clause on causes beyond the master's control, and the forfeit falls away. [[being-hlrksbrnthr|Hálrikos Bránthira]] knows the older rolls hold a ruling on a pilots' refusal that decides the point. The Arbitrators have not asked him because the bench is split on whether to say so.

**The pilot.** Dálena has already told someone what she will do, and Hilther has heard it from the cookshop. She will not break the refusal, because the seamen are her crew's kin. She also cannot refuse the house that holds her father's note, and she has not yet found the third course. A party that gives her one is a party she will sail for.

**The share.** The council could settle the share in a single sitting. The First of the Council, [[being-orthemaiken|Orthema Ikentsis]], would like the strike over, because her house's wine and oil lie in the idle warehouses, and she cannot say so without seeming to serve her own trade. She does not know that one of her colleagues is content to wait.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Nobody in Valthari means to harm the High King. The house means only to hold a debt, and the damage lies in what the mountain lords make of it. Play the house as reasonable and patient, and let the table decide what the news does at Lezkora.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The mercenary.** Kanarrek hires the party to find the company a hull and a crew before the day, with a third of the pay to the party and the rest to the valley.
- **The wanderer.** Hilther asks a party to learn which way Dálena will sail and to bring the seamen the answer, so that nobody burns a boat in the lagoon.
- **The leader.** Ilthera Hiltheris asks a party to carry a case to Hálrikos Bránthira and bring back his answer in his own hand, which is the only form the bench trusts.
- **The thief.** The factor's ledger in the Taruzeli Quarter names the paper the steward borrowed against, and a rival house pays a party to learn what is written in it.

## The Opening {#opening}

The hiring yards at dusk. A hundred and more crossbowmen sit on their chests among cookfires, and no one has sung for a week.

> Hilther rattles his cup at the party's feet and speaks softly. "Three tides gone since the ship should have sailed," he says. "The captain does not count them aloud. Ask the boys."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Hiring Yards

**Purpose.** Show what an idle quay costs a mountain valley.

**Situation.** The yards behind the customs house. The company's chests, its cloaks in the valley's color, a stack of crossbow staves from [[place-ardalis|Ardalis]] and a captain who reads his contract each evening to men who know it by heart.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-kanarrektsuk|Kanarrek Tsukurreta]], steady and short of sleep. Hilther Betsuris, who recites the rolls of the dead for a coin. Agents of two other houses who walk the yards offering a passage on terms that would cost the company its contract.

> [!NOTE]
> The company's crossbowmen are run as [[being-thesidlvnr|Thésira Delvanor]], a stock sellsword. The houses' agents, the Garrundeta valley's steward and the factor in the Taruzeli Quarter are run as [[being-valarnlvnr|Valaren Álvanar]], a stock clerk.

**Player choices.** Take the captain's errand, press Hilther for what he has heard, or read the contract's clause on causes beyond the master's control.

**Clues and rewards.** The contract's last page names the house that holds the ship. A man in the Garrundeta colors will say, if asked, that the steward went to the Taruzeli Quarter in the spring.

**Consequences.** The party knows the ship's owner, and the owner knows someone is asking.

**If bypassed.** Hilther brings the same facts to whoever is nearest, for a coin.

### Scene Two: The Charter Hall

**Purpose.** The law of the strike, and the only precedent that might save the company.

**Situation.** The Arbitrators' bench, crowded with cargoes that have not sailed. Houses sue one another for late delivery, and every house waits to learn which way the bench rules.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-iltherahilth|Ilthera Hiltheris]], composed and weary. [[being-hlrksbrnthr|Hálrikos Bránthira]], who answers a question with the exact page. Advocates in the arcade who sell opinions to both sides.

> [!NOTE]
> The advocates are run as [[being-valarnlvnr|Valaren Álvanar]].

**Player choices.** Carry the case to Hálrikos, press the bench to rule, or buy the precedent from an advocate.

**Clues and rewards.** Hálrikos names the roll and the page of the pilots' refusal, in his own hand. A ruling for the seamen frees every house that could not sail, the company's clause included.

**Consequences.** If the ruling stands, the forfeit falls away and the note stays quiet. The house that wanted the debt learns who carried the page.

**If bypassed.** The bench splits and rules nothing before the day.

### Scene Three: The Idle Slips

**Purpose.** Find the house, and the one man who wants the strike to end.

**Situation.** The shipyards. Half the slips stand empty. The shipwrights' Guild Master, [[being-ornethothol|Ornetho Tholmaris]], presses the houses and the seamen to settle, and his journeymen are losing a season.

**NPCs and threats.** The Guild Master, who knows which slip has been quiet and which has not. [[being-orthemaiken|Orthema Ikentsis]], who comes down to the yards before the council sits and asks what a stranger has learned. A house's yard foreman who will not answer questions about the ship at anchor.

> [!NOTE]
> The yard foreman is run as [[being-jolrenlvnr|Holren Álvanar]], a stock mason.

**Player choices.** Tell the First what the party knows, keep it, or bring it to the seamen.

**Clues and rewards.** The slip of the house that holds the note has had no timber since the strike began. Orthema will offer the party the council's word that she will move the share if it can show her the strike is being lengthened.

**Consequences.** The First moves in the square if she is given proof. She moves slowly and not at all if she is given only a name.

**If bypassed.** The council sits and does not move the share.

### Scene Four: The Bar

**Purpose.** The pilot's choice and the tide.

**Situation.** [[being-dalenavlrn|Dálena Valaron]]'s ship at the foot of a dark quay, her crew loyal and idle. The tide serves in the early morning and the bar is quiet.

**NPCs and threats.** Dálena, who has no third course. Her crew. Two seamen of the strike who have come to learn which way she sails and who have brought friends.

> [!NOTE]
> Dálena's crew and the seamen of the strike are run as [[being-tavirdlvnr|Taviro Delvanor]], a stock seaman.

**Player choices.** Take the company aboard Dálena's ship under a new contract, argue that she sail for the seamen, or find the seamen a way to let her go.

**Clues and rewards.** Dálena's ship holds the company if the cargo is landed. The seamen will let a ship carry mountain crossbows if it is not an owner's.

**Consequences.** A company carried on her ship embarks inside the day. Dálena's father's note falls to the house that holds it, and she knows it.

**If bypassed.** The day passes. The company stays on the quay, the pay is forfeit, and the note falls due.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The day passes. The Taruzelian buyer takes his pay back, Kanarrek's company goes home over the passes with nothing, and the steward writes to the Garrundeta valley that the note is called. The strike ends a fortnight later when the council moves the share, and the house that held the debt holds a High King's valley.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The news goes up the plateau road before the next election, and the mountain lords hear it as Valthari's hand on the High King's house. A party that saved the company has made an enemy of the house that held the ship and a friend of Dálena Valaron. One that proved the strike was lengthened holds a fact the First of the Council can use, and the owners' council will want it back.
