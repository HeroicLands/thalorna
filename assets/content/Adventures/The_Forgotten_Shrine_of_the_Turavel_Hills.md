## Overview {#overview}

The [[place-thuravelhills|Túravel Hills]] roll across the south of the kingdom, low, wooded and mapped with a vagueness unusual in so well-surveyed a realm. They were a country of [[affiliation-theoldway|the Old Way]] before the Twelve were worshipped here, and they were never so much converted as left alone. Somewhere in them stands the [[place-shrinethuravelhills|Shrine of Túravel Hills]], raised for [[affiliation-firstgods|the Old Gods]], forgotten by official history, remembered in the ballads of the hill villages and held still by fae spirits who can be addressed by anyone who knows the older forms.

This summer more scholars, antiquarians and agents of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] have outfitted at [[place-palvothe|Palvote]] for the hills than in any year the innkeepers remember. A hill guide who took a party up in spring has not come down, and his family has not asked the town for help. They have asked [[being-cardnchrns|Kareden Charnis]], the old priest of [[affiliation-zunelas|Zunelas]] who keeps the hill families' observances that the summer families never see.

The party enters at Kareden's request, in the Order's hire, or in the pay of a young lord who wants a path into the hills.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The guide.** The missing guide knew the older forms, as his family has known them for generations, and in spring he took [[being-slmrgrdls|Solmere Gredalis]], a scholar of the Order from Aurelane, and two servants up into the hills, following the ballads as directions. They found the shrine's approach. The guide spoke the forms at its edge and was answered.

**The breach.** Solmere would not wait at the edge as the guide told him. He went past it with his notebook open, writing down what he saw. The guardians turned the strangers out of the hills, lost, and they came down a week later with nothing. They kept the guide. He brought strangers to the shrine, and by the older forms the one who brings answers for what they do. He is alive, in the shrine's dark, as his family dreamed him.

**The scholar.** Solmere came down without the guide and said nothing in Palvote except that he had lost his way. He has stayed in the town all summer, and at night he goes to the spring above it and writes down the prayers Kareden's people say there, so that he can speak the forms himself and go back. He does not understand that the forms are not a password.

**The young lord.** A younger son of a house of Atavel, short of land and long on ambition, has sent his men into the hill villages to ask about old observances, hoping the shrine holds something that will make his fortune. His men have learned of Solmere's notebook and mean to take it.

**The way back.** The guardians give back the guide to someone who comes to the shrine's edge with the forms properly spoken and the strangers' writing in hand to be given up. Kareden knows the forms and is too old to walk the hills. The guide's family knows the way and will not take a stranger up again.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The shrine holds no prize for the party. The scenario ends when the guide comes home or is given up for lost, and when somebody decides what happens to Solmere's notebook.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The faith.** Kareden Charnis asks a party to go up to the place his dream shows him and to tell nobody in the summer town why.
- **The scholar.** Solmere Gredalis hires a party to guard him on a second journey into the hills, and says the first went wrong because of bad weather.
- **The mercenary.** A young lord's steward pays a party well to bring him a scholar's notebook, by purchase or otherwise.
- **The wanderer.** A party on the Palvote road is stopped by a shepherd boy who offers, for silver, a path into the hills that is not written down.

## The Opening {#opening}

The spring above Palvote at midnight, under the ridge. An old man in a priest's cloak sits on a stone with his eyes closed while a hill woman leaves a cake on the rock. Below them in the dark, someone is writing by a shuttered lantern.

> "He comes every night," the old priest says, without opening his eyes. "He writes down what she says. He thinks it is the words that matter."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Summer Town

**Purpose.** Show the two lives of Palvote and the crowd outfitting for the hills.

**Situation.** [[place-palvothe|Palvote]] in high summer: villas on the ridge, the inns full of antiquarians, the small temple of Zunelas below the ridge where the summer families have their dreams read.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-cardnchrns|Kareden Charnis]], frail and sharp. The guide's family, who will not speak to a stranger without Kareden's word. The young lord's men, asking in the taverns. Solmere, polite and evasive.

**Player choices.** Hear Kareden's dream, talk to the guide's family, question Solmere, or watch the young lord's men.

**Clues and rewards.** The dream shows the guide alive in a dark place with a carved threshold. The family says the guide always waited at the edge, and that he would never have let a stranger past it.

**Consequences.** The young lord's men notice the party's interest in Solmere.

**If bypassed.** The party learns of the guide only when the young lord's men take Solmere's notebook in the night.

### Scene Two: The Scholar's Notebook

**Purpose.** What Solmere did.

**Situation.** Solmere's room at the best inn, or the spring at night. His notebook holds the ballads, a rough map, and pages written past the shrine's edge.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-slmrgrdls|Solmere Gredalis]], who is ashamed and will not admit it. The young lord's men, who come for the notebook the same night as the party.

**Player choices.** Persuade Solmere to tell the truth, take the notebook, protect him from the lord's men, or let them have it.

**Clues and rewards.** The pages written past the edge describe a court and a threshold and stop in the middle of a line. Solmere, if he confesses, says the guide shouted at him to come back.

**Consequences.** Whoever holds the notebook holds the strangers' writing the guardians want given up.

**If bypassed.** The lord's men carry the notebook up into the hills to look for the shrine themselves, and the guardians turn them out lost too.

### Scene Three: The Paths Not Written Down

**Purpose.** The journey into the hills.

**Situation.** The [[place-thuravelhills|Túravel Hills]]: good pasture and fair timber, sheep paths that fork without reason, a mist that comes up the valleys in the afternoon. Every chart disagrees with the last.

**NPCs and threats.** The hills themselves, which turn a party that walks them without the forms. A hill family on the high pasture who will not help without Kareden's token. The young lord's men, if they still have the notebook.

**Player choices.** Follow the ballads, follow the notebook's map, bring a token from Kareden, or hire the shepherd boy.

**Clues and rewards.** With Kareden's token the hill family sets the party on the way. With the notebook's map alone, the party walks in circles for a day.

**Consequences.** A party that walks the hills in good faith reaches the edge. A party that tries to force its way is turned out on the Palvote road at dawn.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the shrine only with someone who knows the way.

### Scene Four: The Edge

**Purpose.** The guardians, and the bargain.

**Situation.** The shrine's approach at dusk: a hidden valley, weathered stones, a carved threshold, and beyond it a dark that the eye does not go into. The guardians are there, felt more than seen.

**NPCs and threats.** The fae spirits, unrelenting, bound to a duty that no longer has anyone to report to. They do not fight. They take.

**Player choices.** Speak the forms Kareden taught, give up the notebook, try to cross the threshold, or argue.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that speaks the forms properly and gives up the notebook at the edge sees the guide walk out of the dark, thin and blinking. A party that crosses the threshold loses one of its own to the dark in his place.

**Consequences.** The guide comes home, and the notebook is gone for good.

**If bypassed.** The guide stays in the dark, and his family keeps his place at the hearth.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Solmere goes back up at the end of summer with the prayers written out and the young lord's men behind him. He speaks the words at the edge and is not answered, because he is a stranger and the words are not the forms. The lord's men cross the threshold, and one of them does not come back. The hill families close their paths to every stranger, and the summer town loses its guides.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that brought the guide home has the hill families' trust, which they give rarely and do not give twice. Kareden Charnis, who sent it, has broken his own rule of telling nobody in the summer town. Solmere Gredalis goes back to Aurelane with nothing to show and a reason to say little, and the Order hears from him what he chooses to tell. A party that kept the notebook instead has the only written account of the shrine's court, and everyone who wants the shrine wants it.
