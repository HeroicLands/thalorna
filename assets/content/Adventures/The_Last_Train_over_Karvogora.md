## Overview {#overview}

From late summer until the snow shuts them, the passes of [[place-krvgr|Karvogora]] carry the Velanthian harvest south and bring ore and bar iron north, and every wagon that climbs them changes its team at [[place-tvskvsh|Tveskavesh]]. The riders of the [[affiliation-hstsvrt|Sevret Host]] patrol the approaches and ride escort, and they are paid at harvest. This year the rain came late to the eastern fields, the harvest is short, and the pay has not come.

The last trains of the season are loading in the wagon-sheds of Tveskavesh. At the upper pass the riders who have been owed longest have begun to take a toll of their own from every wagon, and the customs officers of the [[place-northrnmrch|Northern March]] on the far side are waiting to see what is left to tax. [[being-tshnmvrkv|Teshena Mevrekov]], the rain-wife of the foothills, has named the week the pass will close. A train that has not crossed by then does not cross until spring, and a grain town that cannot sell south sells to the port at the port's price.

The party enters as escort for a lowland wagon master who needs to cross before the snow, as messengers the Host sends with its count of arrears or its pledge for an advance of grain, or as travelers lodging at the [[place-frrygrnry|Ferry Granary]] on the night the prince's keeper and the ataman both arrive there wanting something its starbrat will not give.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The pay.** [[being-zhtvddbrvdch|Zhitovod Dobrovodich]], prince of [[place-zhtvsh|Zhitavesh]], has pledged a third of the autumn tithe to a grain-town house against the silver for his Hosts' pay. The late rain has left the tithe short, so he owes grain he does not hold, and he has told neither his keeper of the granary nor the Hosts. The harvest pay is late because it is already spent. He hopes to sell grain at Tveskavesh that the port houses do not count, and make up the difference before anyone asks.

**The advance.** [[being-svdnvlktch|Sovidan Volketich]], ataman of the Sevret Host, has asked the Ferry Granary to advance grain against the pay due at harvest, so that his riders can eat while they escort the last trains. The house's barn is nearly empty. It lent most of its reserve to the [[affiliation-mrchnthsrvr|Merchant Houses of the Rivers]] against the next harvest, and it has already advanced grain to the Sevret Host on the same terms. Its starbrat refuses the ataman on his bare word because there is little to give, and refuses [[being-dvkrdvdch|Duvek Radovodich]], the prince's keeper, a count because a count would show it. She has sent for [[being-rdrbskvnch|Radora Boskvanich]], the starbrat of the great house at Zhitavesh, to hear both men.

**The toll at the upper pass.** The riders at the upper post take a share of every wagon's load in place of the pay they are owed. [[being-kdvntshgrch|Kodvan Teshogorich]], the Host's Warden of the Passes, keeps the roster and the signals of the posts, and he writes every share the riders take on the roster as a sum against the prince's arrears. Sovidan has given no order to collect and no order to stop, because he cannot pay his riders to stop. If the toll grows, the drovers turn back, and a pass that stops carrying wagons stops the market at Tveskavesh within a week.

**The monks' wagons.** By an old grant the monastic wagons pass the south gate of Tveskavesh without toll. [[being-tvsknsdrgrch|Tveskana Sedrogorich]], the reeve of the pass market, keeps a private count of them, and it grows every year past what the monasteries admit to holding. [[being-mvrshzhtgrch|Mevrash Zhitogorich]], who counts the southbound wagons for a port house, leaves the monastic wagons off his list because a carter in the lane behind the south gate pays him a silver a month to look the other way. The referee chooses whose grain rides under the monks' grant this season: a house selling some of its reserve without its starbrat's knowledge, a grain-town house passing the gate untolled, or the lowland prince's own grain going south uncounted under the monks' name.

**The far side.** Marauders come down from the mining country beyond the range, and [[affiliation-blackflame|The Black Flame]] is strong there. It holds nothing inside Velanthia: the danger belongs to the passes. A rider of the Host has found a signal post burned on the northern side, and the referee decides whether the marauders burned it to blind the patrols before the last trains, or a rider burned it himself to leave the post.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Every party on the road waits for another to move first: the prince for the harvest, the Host for the prince, the house for a word it can trust, and the March for whatever the Host leaves on the wagons. The weather does not wait. The referee keeps Teshena's week, and the party's choices decide who is paid and how many wagons cross before it ends.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The warrior: escort before the snow.** A lowland wagon master with the last grain of a grain-town house needs riders over the pass, since the Host's roster is thin until the pay comes.
- **The trader: the two tolls.** The same wagon master needs a go-between to settle with the March's customs officers and the Sevret riders what each will take from his wagons, since each waits for the other to name a figure.
- **The courtier: the late pay.** Kodvan wants a party to carry his count of the Host's arrears, with every toll the riders have taken written against it, to the prince's hall at Zhitavesh.
- **The cleric: the advance.** Sovidan wants a go-between to carry his pledge to the starbrat of the great house before the last wagon trains go up.
- **The scholar: the count.** Duvek wants a witness he can trust at the Ferry Granary when he asks again to see the barn.

## The Opening {#opening}

The south gate of Tveskavesh at first light, in a smell of axle-grease and frost. A line of wagons faces the pass road, each with a heavy team rented from the stables, and the reeve stands at the gate with her book open. A wagon with the sheaf of Lizelas painted on its tailboard rolls through without stopping. On a bench by the gate a young man with a tally-board writes down the wagon behind it and not that one.

> "That's six," says the reeve, to no one in particular, and makes a mark in the back of her book. Above the town a thread of smoke goes up from the upper post and stops halfway, as if someone had thought better of it.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The South Gate

**Purpose.** Meet the market, its book and the wagons nobody counts.

**Situation.** Tveskavesh lives by the change of teams. Its main street is a long yard of wagon-sheds, harness shops and stables, and every wagon in it faces the pass or the lowlands. The customary law of the market is simple: a bargain struck in the square binds until the wagon has crossed the south gate.

**NPCs and threats.** Tveskana, brisk and unimpressed by rank, who tolls every wagon but the monks' and remembers every one. Mevrash, pleasant and curious, who pays a fair price for a list of wagons and leaves one kind off his own. The wagon master, who has a last load of grain, a heavy team, and a bargain struck in the square with a buyer in the March that ends when his wagons pass the gate.

**Player choices.** Take the wagon master's commission, sell Mevrash a list, ask the reeve why she marks the monks' wagons, or follow a monastic wagon to its yard.

**Clues and rewards.** The reeve's private count shows more monastic wagons this season than any year before. The monastic wagon's yard is the carter's in the lane behind the south gate, and the carter pays Mevrash at the end of each month. A party that sees the money change hands holds something both the reeve and the port house would pay to know.

**Consequences.** The party leaves for the pass or the granary with a commission, and someone in the square knows what it saw.

**If bypassed.** The wagon master hires the first escort he can find, and the monks' wagons go through uncounted.

### Scene Two: The Ferry Granary

**Purpose.** The advance, the count and the empty barn.

**Situation.** The house stands on the river that the grain road crosses a day's ride below the range, with a barn of dressed stone, a wharf for the ferry, a chapel of the [[affiliation-lizelas|Faith of Lizelas]] and a guest barn. The ferry runs from first light until the bell at dusk. A guest eats at the monks' long table, keeps the hours, and works for a bowl. On this night Duvek arrives from the lowlands for a count, Sovidan rides down from the foothills for an advance, and Radora comes from Zhitavesh to hear both.

**NPCs and threats.** The starbrat of the house, who gives neither man what he asks on his word. Duvek, anxious and exact, who has been refused twice and is not a bold man. Sovidan, young, quick and impatient with princes, who keeps his word to a drover as he keeps it to a clan head. Radora, mild and immovable, who answers every question with a question.

**Player choices.** Witness for Duvek, carry Sovidan's pledge, ask the granary-keeper for a bowl's work in the barn, or offer Radora a sealed count.

**Clues and rewards.** Radora admits an outsider to count the barn only if the count is sealed and given to her alone. A party that counts it learns that the barn is nearly empty and that its grain stands in the merchants' warehouses, owed to the house against the next harvest. With that sealed count Radora can ask the merchant houses for the house's grain before the snow, and the Host can be fed from it.

**Consequences.** Duvek has a figure, or Radora has one, or nobody does. Sovidan rides back to the foothills with grain, with a promise, or with nothing.

**If bypassed.** The starbrat refuses both men, Radora sends them home with questions, and the Host's riders eat at the upper pass from the wagons.

### Scene Three: The Muster Town

**Purpose.** The Host's hall, the roster and the warning.

**Situation.** [[place-tvsknv|Tveskanov]], the Sevret Host's muster town close under the range. In the ataman's hall Kodvan keeps the roster of every pass post and the signals between them. On the edge of the town Teshena reads the sky for the foothill farms and the Host's riders, and charges no rider for a warning.

**NPCs and threats.** Kodvan, slow-spoken and dry, who writes everything on the roster before he speaks of it, and whose clan of riders would follow him if the ataman asked them to. Teshena, spare, cheerful and certain, whose knot against the first frost has come undone. The keeper of the muster, who holds the banner and the roll.

**Player choices.** Read the roster, carry Kodvan's count to the prince, carry Teshena's sorrel to the upper post, or ask which post burned.

**Clues and rewards.** The roster shows every share the riders at the upper post have taken, set against the arrears, so the Host claims that the prince owes less each day and that the toll is lawful. Teshena gives the week the pass closes, and the party knows how long the last train has. Kodvan names the rider who found the burned post, and the day.

**Consequences.** The party carries a count, a warning or a handful of sorrel up the pass road.

**If bypassed.** Kodvan sends his own rider to the hall at Zhitavesh, and the prince reads the count in the week the pass closes.

### Scene Four: The Upper Pass

**Purpose.** The riders' toll, the March's customs and the burned post.

**Situation.** The grain road climbs from the town to the lowest pass in a long pale line against the range, past the Host's posts, to the crest and the March's customs post beyond it. Snow lies in the gullies already. The riders at the upper post have a rope across the road.

**NPCs and threats.** The riders of the upper post, who are owed the longest and say so. The March's customs officers on the far slope, who tax what the riders leave. Marauders from the mining country, who know that the signal post on the northern side is dark.

**Player choices.** Pay the riders, refuse them, settle with both tolls at once, go round by the lesser drove road farther east, or ride to the burned post first.

**Clues and rewards.** The riders take less from a wagon master who carries the Host's count or Radora's word that grain is coming. The March's officers settle for a figure once the riders have named theirs. The burned post shows whether marauders or a rider fired it, as the referee has chosen. A party that has guessed right reaches the marauders before they reach the last train.

**Consequences.** The train crosses whole, short a share, or not at all, and the riders know who spoke for them.

**If bypassed.** The riders take their share, the March takes its toll from what is left, and the marauders find the last train on the far slope.

### Scene Five: The Snow

**Purpose.** The week Teshena named, and who is paid at the end of it.

**Situation.** The pass road in the first heavy snow. The last wagons on the slope either reach the crest or turn back, and the Host's riders hold the posts or ride down to the foothills for the winter.

**NPCs and threats.** The weather. Sovidan, who rides to the upper post himself to see what his riders have done. Tveskana, who sends a rider up the road with her book to count the wagons that come back.

**Player choices.** Hold the last train on the slope for one more day, turn it back, or bring the riders down with whatever pay has come.

**Clues and rewards.** A Host that is fed from the house's grain, or paid at the prince's hall on Kodvan's count, comes down from the posts with its riders' word kept. A Host that took its own toll comes down owing a prince its quarrel.

**Consequences.** The market at Tveskavesh ends its season with its trade, or with a closed road and a winter of argument.

**If bypassed.** The snow closes the pass with a train still on it, and the wagon master winters on the March side with his grain.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The riders at the upper post take their share of every wagon until the drovers stop climbing, and the market at Tveskavesh closes a week before the snow. Duvek gets no count, Sovidan gets no advance, and the prince reads the Host's count of arrears with a short tithe and a pledged third in front of him. The grain towns sell what they could not take south to the port houses at the port's price. When the Hosts next gather, the Sevret Host comes to the ring with a toll it has already taken and a price it has not yet named.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The Host fed.** Radora's sealed count draws the house's grain back from the merchants' warehouses, the riders eat, and the toll at the upper pass stops. The merchant houses know that a granary house called in its loan, and they ask which one.
- **The Host's toll stands.** Kodvan's roster is the Host's claim against the prince, and the prince owes less on paper and more in temper.
- **The barn known.** If Duvek's figure reaches the prince, he knows the Ferry Granary's reserve is gone, and every house of the network is asked what its own barn holds.
- **The monks' wagons known.** If the carter's arrangement reaches the reeve or the port house, the grant that lets the monks' wagons through the gate untolled is questioned for the first time in living memory.
