## Overview {#overview}

The Khelathi flood has failed two years running, and the grain of Bethua's irrigated strips is the one reliable surplus on the northern shore. The [[affiliation-councilhouses|Council of Houses]] presses the Metrissa to sell it to Bethua's Khelathi patron, the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]]. The [[affiliation-sacredcollege|Sacred College]] presses her to keep it in the cisterns against a dry year of Bethua's own. Every granary on the coast and at the channel-heads is counted twice, once for each pillar, and each pillar argues from its own count.

[[being-amithenakrvn|Amithena of House Kervanion]], Metrissa of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]], has not chosen. A Metrissa who sides wholly with one council is half a sovereign, and her delay is becoming a choice of its own. She wants one figure that neither pillar can disown: what the realm holds, what the interior needs in a dry year, and what is left to sell. She cannot ask a Grammatissa of either council to find it, so she asks strangers who belong to no House and no temple.

The party enters as that count. It walks the bonded warehouses of [[place-bethura|Bethura]] and the channel-head granaries of the interior, and it finds that the two counts disagree because some of the grain has already gone.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The two counts.** [[being-izethalzi|The Izet-Halzi]], the realm's treasurer, writes the Council's account from the receipts of the bonded warehouses on the _en-telekas_ at Bethura, where carted grain is entered, tolled and held. [[being-izetmu|The Izet-Mu]], who sits in the College by right of office, writes the College's from the gate-rolls of the [[affiliation-orderwaters|Order of the Waters]] and the tallies of the channel-head granaries, where the grain is counted as it leaves the strips. Both are honest. The channel-heads have sent north more grain than the bonded warehouses have received, and neither office has set its count beside the other's.

**The horse-yards.** The difference lies in the yards of [House Bera-Sehel]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the Great House of horse-breeders and army suppliers, behind the quay at Bethura. The House's carters unload its grain there and not at the bond, and the House's transports carry it out as fodder for the horses they ship east. Each sailing carries more fodder than its horses eat in a month. The transports sail at night outside the Fleet's convoys, because a convoy's berths and cargoes are on the Harbor-Mistress's list.

**The buyer.** The grain goes to [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]], the Gar-Au. She has drawn on the throne's household treasure to buy small lots from the private stores of the Trade-Houses, most of them from Bera-Sehel's, through the mediators of the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]], and has told her own bureaus nothing. The price is paid into the House's correspondent account at [[place-garanlaghet|Gar-Anlaghet]], the strongest of its ties to the Khelathi temples, and at Gar-Anlaghet the Overseer of the Sea-Gate, [[being-gakhotigzt|Gakhoti Gul'Zetu]], lands the lots by night and enters the ships as carrying ballast. The lots are small beside the sale the Council of Houses wants, and they are a sale all the same, made while the Metrissa weighs it.

**The House's matriarch.** [[being-beraofbrsehl|Bera of House Bera-Sehel]] sells what her own granaries hold, and in her own reckoning she breaks no law of the realm: the grain is her House's, and the horses the transports carry are real. What she evades is the toll at the bond, the count that both pillars argue from, and the Metrissa's choice. Her House's letters of credit have grown strong in a year when Viléria is squeezing the Houses' paper, and the Houses that watch each other's credit have noticed.

**The senior voice.** [[being-aseliakhlthmr|Aselia of House Khalath-Mara]], senior voice of the [[affiliation-councilmatriarchs|Council of Matriarchs]], has seen Bera-Sehel's account at Gar-Anlaghet swell and guesses why. She has said nothing in the Council of Houses, because a charge laid there against a Great House shakes every House's letters with the Khelathi temples at the moment Viléria presses them. She wants the matter brought to the Council of Matriarchs, which settles disputes between Houses and can discipline a member in private. Until it is settled she keeps her own House's lean to herself, because a call for the sale covers Bera-Sehel's lots and a call against it accuses them.

**The cisterns.** The Izet-Mu is right that the cisterns are not full enough to empty. The strips of the interior stand green because the Order's works water them, and a sale of the whole surplus leaves the qanat-fed farms nothing in a dry year. The College's case does not rest on any falsehood, and a party that goes looking for one finds none.

**The envoy.** A Khelathi envoy has asked for an audience about the grain. He is received with the marked deference Bethua shows every official of its patron, and he speaks of the throne's need. The Genzet'Palu's mediators sent him and not the bureaus. He has two errands: to learn whether the Metrissa means to sell, which decides whether the private lots go on, and to see that nobody at the Court learns of them first.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scenario turns on a count, and the count is honest at both ends. What the party carries back to the Metrissa is a figure and the reason for the gap in it. What she does with them is her choice, and every choice costs her one pillar's trust or the patron's.

> [!WARNING]
> The Gar-Au's purchases are a secret of the Khelathi throne as well as of a Bethuan House. A party that names the buyer in open court exposes Meqes XVI to her own bureaus. Let the trail reach the [[being-veilenvoy|Khelathi Envoy at the Court of the Veil]] before it reaches the throne, and let the party decide how far up it to say aloud.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar or scribe.** The Metrissa wants someone who can read a granary tally and a warehouse receipt, and who owes nothing to either the Izet-Halzi or the Izet-Mu, to set the two counts side by side.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** A carter who hauls barley north for the landholders of the interior is short of guards on the road to the coast and pays in coin and in gossip about where other carters unload.
- **The mariner.** [[being-nikhtirahdrths|Nikhtira Hadrithos]], a warrant-captain of [[affiliation-bethuanflt|The Bethuan Fleet]], wants hands who can tell her why horse transports put out from Bethura at night with no escort while she waits for a berth in the grain convoy.
- **The courtier.** A household officer of the Court of the Veil needs a witness at the Khelathi envoy's audience who can say afterward, to the Metrissa alone, whose man the envoy is.

## The Opening {#opening}

A cool room in the [[affiliation-courtveil|Court of the Veil]], high on the slope above the harbor, with the two halls of the pillars visible from its window. Amithena wears no veil here, only a plain priestess's robe. Two rolls lie open side by side on her table, one under the Izet-Halzi's seal and one under the Izet-Mu's, weighted at the corners with spindle-whorls.

> "Each of these tells me how much grain my realm has," she says. "They do not agree, and each of the women who wrote them is certain of her own. I want a third count, and I want it from someone neither of them can call a friend."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Bonded Warehouses

**Purpose.** Establish the Council's count and the first sign of a gap.

**Situation.** The _en-telekas_ at Bethura, where carts come down from the interior and their grain is weighed, tolled and entered into bond. Factors quote the grain at a firmer price each month, and foreign traders off the ships ask what the Houses mean to do with it.

**NPCs and threats.** The Izet-Halzi's clerks, each a [[being-treasclerk|Treasury Clerk]], who answer precisely and resent being checked; factors of the Trade-Houses, each a [[being-tradefactor|Trade-House Factor]] sure the sale is coming; a [[being-khelfactor|Khelathi Factor]] who asks the party, pleasantly, who has hired them.

**Player choices.** Read the bond receipts, count a warehouse by hand, or follow the carts that come down the coast road and do not turn toward the bond.

**Clues and rewards.** The receipts are in order. Carts marked with Bera-Sehel's seal come down the road with the rest, and none of them appears in the bond.

**Consequences.** Word that the Metrissa has set strangers to count reaches every Great House by evening.

**If bypassed.** The party learns of the carts from the carters in Scene Two.

### Scene Two: The Head Gate at Sehela

**Purpose.** Show what the College is defending, and give the second count.

**Situation.** [[place-sehela|Sehela]], a grain-village of the dry southern interior, where a tunnel-well brings water to the strips and the green ends at the last gate. The village's grain goes to its House's granary and north by cart. [[being-tazithakhlthn|Tázitha of House Khalithon]] holds the village, and the Order keeps a Hiora at the head gate who answers to the Order and not to the Dómissa's steward.

**NPCs and threats.** Tázitha, blunt and busy, who wants her tenants fed in a dry year and her House's share of any sale kept fair; the [[being-headgatehior|Hiora of the Head Gate]], exact about the gate-roll; the [[being-dmissasteward|Steward of the Dómissa]], who keeps the tenants' roll and quarrels with the Hiora over whose word a gate answers to; carters waiting on the road north.

**Player choices.** Walk the line with the Hiora, sit with the steward over the shares, or drink with the carters.

**Clues and rewards.** The gate-roll and the granary tallies, which agree with the Izet-Mu's figures. A carter who hauls for several Houses says that Bera-Sehel's loads go to the House's horse-yards behind the quay, "fodder, they say, though no horse eats like that."

**Consequences.** A party that helps settle the gate quarrel has Tázitha's trust and her voice in the Council of Houses as a landholder who wants a fair count.

**If bypassed.** The Izet-Mu's roll is taken on trust, and the party has no witness to the carts.

> [!TIP]
> Play Tázitha as the face of the dry-year argument. She is no friend of the College; she simply knows every tenant's strip by name, and she knows what a sale takes from them. A party that meets her understands why the Metrissa cannot simply sell.

### Scene Three: The Horse-Yards at Night

**Purpose.** Find the gap.

**Situation.** Bera-Sehel's yards behind the quay at Bethura, with stalls, a fodder store and a private landing. A transport loads after dark, horses first and then sacks into the hold.

**NPCs and threats.** The House's yard-women and its carters, each a [[being-housecarter|House Carter]]; the House's guard-women, each a [[being-berayardgrd|Guard-Woman of House Bera-Sehel]], trained in the [[skill-charissa|Kharessa]] and no one to cross in the dark; Nikhtira, who has watched the transports leave and is happy to point.

**Player choices.** Watch, count the sacks, talk their way in as buyers of horses, or follow a transport out with Nikhtira's galley.

**Clues and rewards.** The fodder in each hold is many times what its horses need for the crossing. A sack bears the mark of a channel-head granary and no mark of the bond. A yard-woman lets slip that the price is paid "at Gar-Anlaghet, where the House banks."

**Consequences.** A party seen at the yards has made an enemy of a Great House. Bera's factors find out who hired them within days.

**If bypassed.** Nikhtira brings the party a sailing-list she has kept from her own deck, which shows the same transports leaving and none returning with cargo.

> [!WARNING]
> Following a transport to sea takes the party to the Khelathi delta and into another land's trouble. The scenario does not need it. If the party insists, the transport makes for Gar-Anlaghet, its sacks go ashore at night, and the customs rolls enter the ship as carrying ballast.

### Scene Four: Aselia's Table

**Purpose.** The senior voice makes her offer.

**Situation.** The Trade-Houses' hall at Bethura, where the matriarchs of the Great Houses meet and their clerks keep the protocols of credit. Aselia asks the party to dine.

**NPCs and threats.** Aselia, who speaks rarely and pauses before she speaks; a clerk of the federation's common fund; the Khelathi envoy, who is also a guest and is placed at Aselia's right as Bethua places every official of its patron.

**Player choices.** Tell Aselia what they found, keep it for the Metrissa, or bargain with one for the other.

**Clues and rewards.** Aselia confirms, without naming the House, that one Great House's account at Gar-Anlaghet has grown beyond its trade. She offers to bring the matter to the Council of Matriarchs and see it settled there, quietly, if the party gives the Metrissa the figure without its cause.

**Consequences.** If the party agrees, the federation disciplines Bera-Sehel in private and the lots stop. The Metrissa decides on a true figure and never learns why the two counts disagreed. If the party refuses, Aselia does not oppose them, and Khalath-Mara declares for whichever side the Metrissa takes.

**If bypassed.** Aselia sends a letter to the party's lodging making the same offer, under her seal.

### Scene Five: Before the Veil

**Purpose.** The climax: the count delivered and the envoy received.

**Situation.** The Court of the Veil, where Amithena hears the party in private and then receives the Khelathi envoy in public, wearing [[miscgear-veilpurple|the Purple Veil]].

**NPCs and threats.** Amithena, composed and slow to commit; the envoy, deferential and well informed; the Izet-Halzi and the Izet-Mu, each waiting to learn whose count stands.

**Player choices.** Give the Metrissa the figure alone, the figure and the House, or the figure, the House and the buyer. A party can also warn her what the envoy is, or let her find out in the audience.

**Clues and rewards.** The Metrissa's favor, a letter of the Court's that opens doors anywhere in the realm, and a place on whatever side of the quarrel she takes.

**Consequences.** With the figure and the House, she has a fact both pillars must accept and a Great House she can hold to account. With the buyer as well, she knows the patron is already buying, which strengthens the Council's case for a sale and the College's fear of what a patron who buys the bread can ask. In either case the envoy learns from her face that the Court knows something.

**If bypassed.** The Metrissa receives the envoy without the count and promises him an answer after the Council's next session.

> [!TIP]
> Amithena never thanks the party in public and never says which council she favors in front of the envoy. Her reward comes later, by letter. A party that expects a ruling on the spot has misunderstood her, and she lets them.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The Metrissa goes on weighing the sale. The Council of Houses presses harder each session and the College answers each time with the cisterns. Bera-Sehel's transports go on sailing at night, and its letters go on growing stronger. When Amithena does choose, she decides on two counts that disagree without knowing why, and if the House's sales come out afterward, each pillar blames her: the Council for letting one House take the margin of a sale she had not allowed, and the College for letting the realm's bread go east while she weighed it.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Whatever the Metrissa decides, she decides on a figure both pillars must accept, and that is what she wanted from the party. A House brought to account before the Council of Matriarchs pays in standing and in credit, and the Houses remember who brought it there. The envoy reports to the Genzet'Palu that the Court of the Veil has learned more than it was meant to, and the Khelathi throne's private buying grows more careful or stops. The party is known in Bethura as people who can count and keep their mouths shut, which in that city is a trade.
