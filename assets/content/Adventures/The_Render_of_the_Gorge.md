## Overview {#overview}

At [[place-anlaghzetun|Anlagh-Zetun]], the granary capital of the upper river, [[being-zinurilsltr|Zinuri Les'Leretu]] reckons what each of [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|The Upper River Selatu]] owes the throne after every harvest and reports it to the Gar-Au. Two low floods have left his render rolls out of step with the river: the reckonings from the selatu run smaller than the flood explains, and he has the only complete account of who sent what. He does not argue a figure. He shows the roll.

This season the receipts of the capital's granaries, copied to him with the year's accounts, credit [[affiliation-selatyathlmt|The Selat of Yath-Lemet]] with several barge-loads of grain received at [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]. Yath-Lemet is the smallest of the upper selatu, a gorge where the valley pinches almost shut, and its town buys its own bread by barge. Its Halzi'a, [[being-mentothaakhel|Mentotha Akhelo'Naru]], sends the whole of a small render each year, late, with a clerk's note about the river. Zinuri does not sign a reckoning with a figure he cannot account for, and the reckoning is due.

The party enters as strangers Zinuri hires because no Halzi'a owns them, as hands the Halzi'a of the capital hires to count a granary that has given two tallies in one month, or as weighers the Overseer of the Sea-Gate sends to a quay whose stones run light.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The short renders.** Several upper selatu send less than the flood explains, and Zinuri's roll shows which and by how much. Every Halzi'a he names can hurt him, and he cannot hurt them back: he was born a Zelar, married into the titled house of Les'Leretu, and has no soldiers and no house of his own behind him. The scribes of the eighteen selatu send him warning before they send their Halzi'a their reports.

**The credit.** The grain credited to Yath-Lemet is real. It lies in the capital's granaries in sacks no upper-river village weaves.

**The false entries.** At the Gar-Au's private instruction, [[being-khelansisgth|Khelansi Sa'Gathau]], Halzi'a of [[affiliation-capitalselat|The Capital Selat]], books small lots of Bethuan grain as the render of Yath-Lemet, a selat that never sent it. He chose the gorge because its render is small and nobody reads it closely. The entries balance in the capital's books, and they do not survive an audit by Zinuri Les'Leretu.

> [!WARNING]
> Let the party reach the false entries and the ballast before the household treasure, because every figure that names the grain also names the Gar-Au.

**The ballast.** [[being-gakhotigzt|Gakhoti Gul'Zetu]], the Overseer of the Sea-Gate at [[place-garanlaghet|Gar-Anlaghet]], lands the lots duty-free at night. The customs rolls enter the ships as ballast. Gakhoti asks no questions, because the throne owes him a favor of this size and he wants the customs treasury he has built to be the throne's answer to a hungry year.

**The household treasure.** The grain is [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]]'s own purchase. She has drawn on the throne's household treasure to buy small lots from the private stores of the Bethuan Trade-Houses through the mediators of [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]], well short of the large sale that waits on the Metrissa's choice, and she has told the bureaus nothing. She has set a date by which she announces a failed flood or confesses the purchases, and the date is close. An honest reckoning that names the grain names her.

**The copy for Anlaghur.** Zinuri has already sent a full copy of his reckoning to the household of [[being-anlaghur|Anlaghur]], the Gar-Au's nephew, showing which Halzi'a held back how much. He tells himself it is a duty to the throne's heir, and he hopes for the nephew's favor if the succession is decided. Whatever Zinuri writes into this year's reckoning, a copy goes the same way.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Every figure in this scenario is somebody's honest answer to a different question. The scenario ends when Zinuri signs the reckoning and sends it, and what the party has learned decides what it says and who else reads it.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar.** Zinuri hires a party that no Halzi'a owns to learn where the grain credited to Yath-Lemet came from before he signs.
- **The leader.** Khelansi Sa'Gathau has a granary that gave two different tallies in one month, and he asks a party that belongs to no bureau to count it by hand.
- **The thief or the merchant.** A harbor-master at Gar-Anlaghet reports that the weights at one quay run light against the Overseer's standard, and Gakhoti asks a party to re-weigh them before the harbor-master does.
- **The wanderer.** Mentotha Akhelo'Naru hears that the capital credits his selat with a harvest it could not grow, and wants strangers to carry his own roll to the Overseer.

## The Opening {#opening}

The tally-bureau at Anlagh-Zetun in the morning, with the granary row white in the sun beyond its windows. Clerks sit cross-legged at their boards along the walls, and the scratch of reed on papyrus fills the room. Zinuri has two rolls open on his table, weighted with tally-stones, and he does not look up.

> "Yath-Lemet buys its bread," he says, and lays a finger on the capital's copy. "Here it has sent us a harvest."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Tally-Bureau

**Purpose.** Hire on, learn the reckoning, and see what the Overseer cannot do for himself.

**Situation.** Anlagh-Zetun is a city of 80,000 among the richest wheatland in the empire, and its skyline is a forest of granary-domes. The survey-courts and tally-bureaus are its busiest halls. The stores themselves stay under the Halzi'a of [[affiliation-selatnlghztn|The Selat of Anlagh-Zetun]], [[being-zahuraanlagh|Zahura Anlaghet'Zaru]], and a granary is opened only on his word. At the end of the granary row stands the bricked dome of [[place-eleventhgran|The Eleventh Granary]], where the tallies never balance.

**NPCs and threats.** Zinuri, meticulous, soft-spoken and stubborn. His clerks, who read every roll aloud twice. Zahura Anlaghet'Zaru, a land-proud soldier whose advancement at the capital has stalled, who wants his own selat shown full in the Overseer's reckoning and his granaries left shut. Zahura's wardens on the granary row.

**Player choices.** Take Zinuri's commission, ask Zahura to open the domes, walk the granary row, or watch who comes and goes at the bureau.

**Clues and rewards.** The capital's receipts give the days each lot was received and carry the seal of the capital's granaries. A steward on the row tells a new clerk not to look for the error in the eleventh, and a party that does wastes a night beside a dome that sweats in a lean year. A clerk leaves the bureau at dusk with a sealed roll under his arm and takes the road toward the nephew's household, not the river.

**Consequences.** The party has Zinuri's letter, which opens tally-bureaus and not granaries.

**If bypassed.** The party starts from the capital or the sea-gate and reaches Zinuri last, with the answer before the question.

### Scene Two: The Gorge

**Purpose.** Learn what Yath-Lemet actually sent.

**Situation.** [[place-yathlemet|Yath-Lemet]], about 6,000 people crowded between the cliff and the water, the smallest capital on the upper river. Its Halzi'a keeps his court beside the chief temple of the [[affiliation-chelsur|Faith of Chelsur]], whose god is the crocodile. A low river leaves the farmers and the beasts in the same few pools, and the farmers fetch water at dawn and keep watch. Every barge that passes the narrows pays the toll, and a barge-master who dodges it by night is fined the cargo.

**NPCs and threats.** Mentotha Akhelo'Naru, who holds a small court and owes a small render, and who knows that the Overseer lists his selat last in every reckoning. The temple's boatmen, who clear the narrows when a barge grounds. The crocodiles in the pools.

**Player choices.** Ask Mentotha for his roll, read the toll roll at the narrows, ask the bread-barges where their grain comes from, or hire a temple boatman to take the party downriver.

**Clues and rewards.** Mentotha's roll shows his whole render sent in one barge, a week late, with a clerk's note about the river. The toll roll at the narrows records every barge that passed this season, and no other grain barge under the selat's seal. Mentotha, shown the capital's credit, is alarmed: a gorge credited with a harvest it cannot grow is asked one day where the fields are.

**Consequences.** Mentotha gives the party a sealed copy of his roll for the Overseer, and his name is now on their errand.

**If bypassed.** The party never confirms the gorge's side, and Zinuri does not sign without it.

### Scene Three: The Capital's Granaries

**Purpose.** Find the credited grain, and the man who entered it.

**Situation.** The granaries that feed Galezkara between floods, under the Halzi'a of the Capital Selat, who also keeps the canals round the sacred island and the city guard of [[affiliation-meglai|The Meglai]]. One granary has given two tallies in a month. The lots credited to Yath-Lemet lie in it, entered on the days the receipts name.

**NPCs and threats.** Khelansi Sa'Gathau, precise, dry and courteous to scribes, who wants the capital's granaries to carry the city to a good flood and cannot hand the Gar-Au a figure she does not want. His granary-keepers. The Meglai at the gates.

**Player choices.** Count the granary by hand as Khelansi asks, open the credited sacks, ask the keepers who brought the lots in, or put the receipts in front of Khelansi.

**Clues and rewards.** The credited sacks are a weave no upper-river village makes, and the grain in them is not the gorge's. The keepers say the lots came upriver from the delta by night barge, not downriver. Khelansi, shown the receipts, says only that the entries balance, and asks the party to finish the count before they draw conclusions.

**Consequences.** Khelansi knows what the party is asking, and the Gar-Au's household knows by evening.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the sea-gate without knowing which grain to look for.

### Scene Four: The Sea-Gate

**Purpose.** Follow the grain to where it came ashore.

**Situation.** Gar-Anlaghet, the empire's principal sea-gate, a sprawl of quays, bonded warehouses, counting-houses and foreign quarters where the factors of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] keep permanent quarters. Four hands are in every purse on the quay: the selat, the customs-house, [[affiliation-garhalzi|Gar-Hálzi]] and the factors. The Customs-Scribes enter every bale in the rolls.

**NPCs and threats.** Gakhoti Gul'Zetu, the Overseer of the Sea-Gate, genial and unhurried, who counts the people he meets as carefully as the cargoes. The harbor-master and his light stones. Factors who tell the Overseer the truth because it costs them less. The waterfront at night.

**Player choices.** Re-weigh the quay's stones, read the customs rolls for the nights the receipts name, watch the quays after dark, or accept Gakhoti's invitation to attend a Halzi'a's feast as his household.

**Clues and rewards.** The customs rolls for the nights before each credited lot enter ships from the Bethuan shore and nothing in their holds but ballast. A party on the quays after dark sees a ship unloading into a night barge with no Customs-Scribe present. Gakhoti, asked, smiles and says that the throne's business is the throne's.

**Consequences.** Gakhoti makes the party an offer of his friendship, which in Gar-Anlaghet is worth more than silver, and he expects them to know what it costs.

**If bypassed.** The party has the grain and the entry and not where it came from, and Zinuri can strike the credit but not explain it.

### Scene Five: The Reckoning

**Purpose.** Decide what the Overseer writes, and who reads it.

**Situation.** The tally-bureau at Anlagh-Zetun on the day the reckoning is due. Zinuri's roll lies open with a space where the credit to Yath-Lemet stands. A courier waits to carry the reckoning to the throne, and a clerk of the nephew's household has business at the bureau the same day.

**NPCs and threats.** Zinuri, who wants to report what each selat truly owes, whoever is embarrassed by it. Zahura's steward, who wants to see the figure for Anlagh-Zetun before it leaves. A messenger of the Gar-Au's household, if the party has stirred the capital. The nephew's clerk.

**Player choices.**

- _Tell Zinuri everything._ He writes the credit as grain bought and landed, not rendered, and signs his name to it.
- _Tell Zinuri only that the gorge is clean._ He strikes the credit to Yath-Lemet and enters the lots as unaccounted, and the capital's books no longer balance.
- _Carry it to the Gar-Au first._ The party goes downriver to the island before the reckoning leaves, and the choice is hers.
- _Carry it to the nephew._ Anlaghur already knows who held back; with this he knows what the throne has done.

**Clues and rewards.** Zinuri pays in his rolls, which open every tally-bureau on the upper river to the party's name. Mentotha pays in the gorge's goodwill and a free passage at the narrows. The Gar-Au, Khelansi and Anlaghur each pay for silence or for the truth, and none of them pays the same.

**Consequences.** The reckoning reaches the throne, and its copy reaches the nephew's household.

**If bypassed.** Zinuri signs with the credit marked as unaccounted, in his own hand, and sends it.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Zinuri signs the reckoning with the credit to Yath-Lemet marked as unaccounted and sends it to the throne, and a copy goes to the nephew. The capital's bureaus ask Khelansi to explain the lots, and he asks for time. Mentotha is asked to account for a harvest his gorge could not grow, and he answers with his roll and a clerk's note about the river. The Halzi'a who held back render read their own names in the Overseer's figures, and one of them offers Zinuri a house and a Zabu's household for a smaller figure next year.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The reckoning names the grain.** The bureaus learn that grain came in at the sea-gate and was entered upriver, and the court asks the Overseer how he learned what he wrote.
- **The gorge cleared quietly.** The credit to Yath-Lemet is struck, Mentotha's name is clean, and the capital's books carry lots that nobody rendered. Khelansi owes the party a favor and fears them.
- **The Gar-Au told first.** The throne has the party's silence or their price, and the reckoning says what the island asks it to say. Zinuri knows that his roll has been edited, and he does not forget.
- **The nephew told.** Anlaghur holds what the party found at the sea-gate, and the succession has a new weight in it.
