## The Hearth Without Light

On the grazing lands east of [[place-zarhanis|Zarhánis]] stands a ruined temple estate that the clans avoid and the temple does not name. A fire burns beneath its granary that gives no light, and the cattle that stray near it come out as ash on four legs. The party goes in for gold, for a hire, for a captain's order or for a herd, and finds that the wrong done at Shuremis is older than the fire, and that the fire is only the wrong's answer.

Read [[place-shuremis|Shuremis]] first. The adventure assumes the creed of the [[lore-okharinclt|Okhárin]]: the Flame renews only what is given to it, a person put out of the Flame is a shurem, and the worst a family can say of a grazing right is that it was yielded once.

### The Truth Behind the Ash

**What happened.** In a famine year the temple of Zarhánis held the estate of Shuremis, with grain in its pits and a yard of bondsmen. Those who had declined the Flame, and taken up other gods or none, were gathered into the yard as shurem, and when the stores ran short the order came to seal the pens beneath the granary with the shurem inside. The Granary-Keeper's office of that year sealed them. One ordained priest of Zarhánis, sent to witness the closing, lit a three-wick lamp at the granary niche and went down with them. The estate was struck from the rolls the next season.

**What Talitho believes.** **Talitho**, once a Talithari Nkaring of Zarhánis and a reader of its registers, found the order in the temple's records. He said so aloud, in the moot-hall, and was put out of the Flame for saying it. He rode to the ruin. In the pens he found a banked hearth that had never gone cold, and the voices of the dead asked him to be given what they were denied. He kindled a fire from their ash, and he feeds it, and he believes it will be the shurem's hearth.

**What is true.** The ash has no descent from the First Ember and no one to give it back. Those who died in the pens died with no ashmark, no hearth and no name spoken. The Shadow fills the gaps, and the voice Talitho hears is the dead wearing the Shadow's mouth. The fire takes whatever it is fed and renews none of it. Talitho is cold to the bone and grey to the wrist and cannot tell it. The living shurem of the yard are the descendants of those sealed in the pens, and they are afraid of the fire and fed by it.

### Hooks

- **The thief.** A drover sells the story of the estate's tribute-chest, lost when Shuremis was struck from the rolls, and says the temple would pay quietly to have it returned.
- **The soldier.** A warrior of [[place-vuthraka|Vuthráka]] on a watch over a clan's cattle-ground reports lights in the pens and a herd that moves in step. His captain orders the watch kept, and gives him no more men.
- **The leader.** **Ndumbo**, a chieftain of a herding clan, has lost the best of his herd to the ground and fears what the moot will say of his clan's neglect. He offers grazing and a seat at his fire for the matter closed.
- **The wanderer.** A shaman of the [[affiliation-yenorzhrng|Yénor Zohiring]] has been told by a cairn to go to the ruin and told nothing else. She wants an escort through the cattle-ground and cannot say why.

### Scene One: The Cattle-Ground

**Purpose.** To show the wrongness of the place, to teach its daily rhythm and to put the cairn in the party's hands.

**Situation.** A wall of dry-laid stone runs across the rise. The gate cairn has its flame-mark chiselled out and the stone beneath is black. Beyond lies the cattle-ground, a field of trodden ash with the pens in a ring at the far side, and in it stand cattle the color of banked hearth-ash. In the morning they stand in the herders' way. By afternoon they drift back toward the granary. At night they face it in rows, and nothing in the field moves.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The ash-kine.** Cattle burnt without burning: horn, hide and hoof gone grey, and a dull red seam showing under the skin of the flank. They do not eat, and they make no sound. They walk in step and press into whatever stands in the way, and a struck one comes apart in a cloud of fine ash that blinds and scorches. A cow's weight is on a man when a dozen press.
- **The Cinder Bull.** The lead animal, bigger than the rest, with a three-wick lamp of black iron hung on a cord from the horns. Its wicks burn cold. The herd goes where the bull goes, and it charges at the sight of an open flame.
- **Zhonko.** A young herder who lost his father's cattle to the ground and watches from the wall at noon. He knows the herd's habits and will lead a party across for a share.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ A running fight with the ash-kine leaves a party blinded and scorched.
- _Stealth._ The herd is slow in the afternoon, when it drifts toward the granary, and nearly still at noon. A party that moves between the animals with its lamps covered and its breath held reaches the pens.
- _Guile._ Count the herd. Okhárin herders count what they own, and the ash-kine were the temple's herd. A character who counts them aloud in the herders' manner, one stroke to each, finds the beasts stand for the length of the count.
- _Parley._ Re-cut the flame-mark on the cairn with a chisel and set an ashmark on the stone. The cairn answers. A Keeper of a Cairn or a shaman does it best, and the herd goes still while the zohira's warning stands again at the gate.

**Clues and rewards.** The bull's lamp is a temple lamp, of the kind issued to a granary, and bears a Zarhánis steward's mark. Zhonko will say that a man with a cold lamp counts the herd at dusk. A re-cut cairn marks the way back as a refuge.

**Consequences.** A fight leaves the Cinder Bull aware of the party, and it comes to the granary door at dusk. A cairn repaired without proper rite is a cairn answered by the wrong voice. Zhonko, if hired, can be lost.

**If bypassed.** The old drovers' track runs along the wall's north face to a breach, and a party that uses it enters the bondsmen's yard from the rear and skips the cattle-ground.

### Scene Two: The Yard of the Cast-Out

**Purpose.** To give the shurem a face, to test the party's manners by the Okhárin standard and to offer an easier road into the granary.

**Situation.** The bondsmen's yard is a ring of sunken floors roofed with hides and reeds, a hearth at the center. About twenty people live there: shurem, descendants of those sealed in the pens, and the cast-out who joined them. They keep a hearth that was kindled from no temple fire. They have no ashmark at any passage, and they have buried their dead in the cattle-ground's ash.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Kwenda.** A woman of age, the yard's elder. She keeps the hearth, speaks for the yard and holds the soured-milk bowl that is the Okhárin test of a stranger. She is lean, direct and tired, and does not say what she thinks of Talitho. She fears him, and he feeds them.
- **The yard's people.** Frightened, proud and reserved. They give nothing and take nothing, except from those who stand by the soured milk.
- **Talitho's watchers.** Two or three of the yard's young men, who carry the fire's cold lamp from the pens at dusk. They are not dangerous alone. They run to Talitho the moment a stranger is proved hostile.

**Player choices.**

- _Parley._ Kwenda hands a stranger soured milk, cold from the shade, and the yard stands to watch whether it is drunk. A party that drinks and accepts the yard's seat at the fire is heard. A party that refuses is watched, and the yard sends word below.
- _Guile._ A false name or a feigned faith is met by a people with nothing to lose and long practice at hearing it. A feigned priest is a priest of the temple that did this, and is received as one.
- _Stealth._ The yard sleeps at midday. A party that crosses it without waking a hearth-keeper reaches the granary stair.
- _Force._ The yard has no warriors. Killing one of them closes every door in it and sends the young men to Talitho at a run.
- _Puzzle._ The granary's front door is a wall with three niches, each holding a lamp-bowl. The niches are lit in the order the culture reads the Flame, faith, then abundance, then war: Zarhánis, Kaljékor, Vuthráka. Lit in that order the door swings open. Lit in any other order it stays shut and the ash-kine are drawn to the wall.

**Clues and rewards.** Kwenda asks one thing: the ashmark that the yard's dead were denied. If the party promises to bring it, she leads them by the back stair, which skips the granary door's three niches. She also says that the man below is not as he was, and that the fire speaks in his voice more often than he does.

**Consequences.** A promise to Kwenda is a promise to a people the temple will not recognize. A party that breaks it is remembered by the yard for as long as it lasts.

**If bypassed.** A party that never speaks with the yard can light the three niches itself, or break through the granary wall at the cost of the cattle's attention. Either way it arrives at Scene Three without Kwenda's promise or her warning.

### Scene Three: The Granary Floor

**Purpose.** To strip the party of fire and give it a clue it can use against the one who took it.

**Situation.** The granary floor is a hall of stone ribs and pits. The pits are brimmed with fine ash, and the floor between them is a crust over deeper ash. Light from a doorway reaches a few paces and fails. A flame carried in leans toward the stair at the far end and gutters out. A lamp snuffed in the hall cannot be relit with a spark.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The ash.** The pits are a mass of fine grey ash, warm to the touch and not hot. A step on the crust above a pit breaks it, and the ash pulls at the leg like silt. It is not hungry. It is only deep.
- **Ash-kine in the dark.** A pair stand among the ribs facing the stair, still until the party passes, and then moving.
- **The niche lamp.** In the far wall is the granary-keeper's niche, a small shrine with a three-wick lamp. One wick burns with a small, true flame. It has burned there as long as anyone can say, and the fire below cannot take it.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ Fighting among the pits risks falling into one, and the kine come out of the dark in a pair.
- _Stealth._ Move in darkness along the ribs, where the crust is stone, and use a covered pyrestone's dull glow or the touch of a hand on the wall.
- _Guile._ Read the pits. A ridge of paler ash marks the old floor-line, and a character who reads the crust finds a safe path.
- _Parley._ Make an offering at the niche. A pinch of grain, a few drops of oil and the words of the offering, and a fresh wick lit from the old flame stays alight. A flame that is given is not taken.

**Clues and rewards.** The niche lamp is the clue to the climax: fire that is given freely renews and does not feed. Lit wicks from it keep burning in the hall below, where all other flame fails. The steward's mark on the niche matches the mark on the Cinder Bull's lamp.

**Consequences.** A party that loses its lamps here is blind in the climax. A party that finds the niche lamp has light that holds.

**If bypassed.** The back stair from the yard drops into the pens' vestibule below the granary, skipping the floor and the niche. The clue can be found later in the sealed pens.

### Scene Four: The Hungry Hearth

**Purpose.** The confrontation with Talitho and the fire, and the choice of what the fire is given.

**Situation.** Beneath the granary a vaulted pen holds the hearth, a ring of black stone with a fire in it that gives no light. The air at the ring is cold as winter. Around the walls the ash-kine stand in rows facing the center, and on the hearth's rim sits a tall man with a shaven head and a scrubbed forehead, with a three-wick lamp in his lap whose wicks burn cold.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Talitho.** A former priest of Zarhánis, grey to the elbow and cold to the touch, with the forehead of a man who has scraped his own ashmark off with a stone. He speaks in the cadence of a scholar of the temple and thinks in registers. He is terribly courteous, and he is dying. His touch turns warmth to ash, and fire brought near him leans and is lost. He does not want to fight. He wants the dead named.
- **The ash-kine at the walls.** Dozens, still until he speaks, and then moving in step.
- **The fire.** It does not move and has no shape. It takes what it is fed, and it answers in the voices of the dead, in a murmur, low and many.

**Player choices.**

- _Force._ Kill Talitho and the kine fall to ash, and the fire flares. It is fed on the man himself. This is the cheap victory and the costly one.
- _Stealth._ Cut the fire's feed, the cattle-trail from the field, and starve it. Talitho feeds it himself instead, from his own body, within a season.
- _Guile._ Persuade Talitho the fire is the Shadow's. He is a scholar and will listen to an argument, but not to one made by a stranger with a blade.
- _Parley._ Offer the fire what it was denied: a wick lit from the niche lamp, an ashmark set on the hearth's rim and the names of the dead, spoken aloud. This is the one thing it is not given by Talitho. A fire fed by gift renews, and the Shadow's grip on the gap slackens. Talitho must be a party to it, or restrained.

**Clues and rewards.** The names are in the sealed pens in Scene Five, and a party that arrives without them can still offer the flame and the ashmark, which quiets the fire for a night. The hearth's black stones are cut with the shurem's tallies and are worth a scholar's trip.

**Consequences.** A party that kills Talitho while the fire is lit leaves a hearth that burns without a keeper. A party that quiets the fire without the names leaves it asleep.

**If bypassed.** The sealed pens open behind the hearth, and a party that slips past the ring unseen reaches them. It leaves the fire burning and Talitho unchallenged.

### Scene Five: The Sealed Pens

**Purpose.** To reward the party, to reveal what the temple did and to leave the ending open to decision.

**Situation.** Behind the hearth a low door opens on a row of stone pens, narrow cells with iron rings in the wall, each full of bones laid close. The bones have no ashmark. In the last pen lies the body of a man in a priest's robe with a three-wick lamp on his chest, still wrapped in cloth, and a clay tablet at his side.

**NPCs and threats.** The dead are quiet. If the fire is lit they stir faintly, and the voices from the hearth are a pleading, not a threat. The Last Talithari's body lies at peace, as the only one among them who was given a rite, by himself.

**Player choices.**

- _Read the tablet._ It is the order that sealed the pens, in the Granary-Keeper's office of the year, with a steward's mark and a temple seal. A line below it, in a priest's hand, says that he went down by choice and that someone should come back.
- _Read the bones._ A Bone-Reader or shaman who handles the bones can name them. Their names are not in the temple's rolls and are in the bones' own hand.
- _Take the tablet._ A temple office that wants it buried pays for it, and another that wants the Granary-Keeper's office shamed pays more.
- _Take the chest._ A small chest under the priest's cot holds weight-pieces, a few silver gezan and a quantity of copper, and the steward's seal. The plunder is modest.

**Clues and rewards.** The tablet. The names. A chest of weight-pieces. A pyrestone as big as a fist, glassy black, that took the heat of the fire and keeps it. If Talitho is brought here alive, he reads the names aloud.

**Consequences.** The names read aloud over the ashmarked hearth end the fire's hunger. Without them, the shurem dead still wait, and the fire is a fire that wants.

**If bypassed.** A party that never reaches the pens leaves the wrong in its grave and the fire fed. The yard brings a second party within a season.

### Aftermath

- **The names read, the ashmark set.** The fire sinks to ordinary ash and is still. Talitho, if he lives, is cold for a season and then warm. The yard buries its dead at the cairn with proper rites, and Kwenda's people become a household of the grazing, outside the temple's roll. The clan's herd is its own again.
- **The names unread, the fire quieted.** The ash-kine stand still and the cattle-ground is quiet. At the Night of Ashes the dead walk, and the yard is afraid.
- **Talitho dead and the fire unfed.** The kine fall. The pens go cold. The dead stay unmarked, and the Shadow keeps its foothold in the gaps, a thin and quiet field at the edge of the grazing.
- **The tablet taken.** A temple office pays for it or buries it. Whichever does, [[place-zarhanis|Zarhánis]] learns that the order was found and who found it.
- **The party bypassed Shuremis.** The cattle-ground widens. A clan loses a valley, and the moot hears of it as neglect.
