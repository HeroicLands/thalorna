A hall-lord of the [Age of Halls]{.name data-type="lore/calendar"} sits in his high seat under a bare fell, with his dead war-band at the benches and the grave-ale owed to him still undrunk. A hidden servant of [[lore-nahilddty|Nahild]], the corpse-goddess, has set a shard of black glass in his ship to hold him there, and means to turn the oldest oath on the pass into a weapon. The party climbs to the howe to find out what the dead man wants and what it will cost to give it to him.

## Overview

Above the farms of [[place-horgsvangr|Horgsvangr]], the pass road runs under the fell of [[place-unthmylsfell|Unthmilsfell]]. Under the turf lies the ship of [Unthmil]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, who held the pass before there were kings. His heir died beside him, nobody drank the grave-ale, and in the north's reckoning he is a dead man still in his chair. That was a sorrow and not a danger until a **Drottmadr of [[affiliation-nalok|Nalok]]**, [Knirvthann]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, climbed the fell in the autumn and set a shard of black glass cut from the ice of [[place-dvilgvatn|Dvilgvatn]] into the ship's prow-post. The glass gives [[lore-theshadow|The Shadow]] a place to grip. The dead hall now wakes by night, the war-band stands at the toll-cairns on the pass road, and Knirvthann means to bind the hall-lord as a [[lore-nagengir|Nágengir]] and the war-band as [[lore-haugverdir|Hrúmverdir]] for a hidden hov.

The [[affiliation-ravenguard|Raven Guard]] of [[place-odholm|Odholm]] have heard of it and cannot spare the warriors.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A fence in the trading stead below Horgsvangr will pay for hack-silver of the old make, which the traders say lies in the ship of Unthmil. He does not mention the toll-stones. Taking goods from a howe is theft from the dead and the line together, and the thief knows it. The fence knows it too, and pays well enough to be forgiven.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** [Hrandinda]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a sworn warrior of the Raven Guard, needs strong arms to carry the baggage and hold the road behind her while she sets a ward on the howe. The Guard cannot send a company, and she has the pay of three men in her own purse.
- **The leader.** A hersvald of the district, whose last two trade caravans vanished on the pass road, wants a captain to clear it. He has the muster for the work and no stomach for the pass after dark.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer has a grandmother's song of an empty chair on a high pass, and the song's last line names the wanderer's own clan. A wanderer of the old odel-line of Unthmil is owed the cup, and is the only one who can drink it without having to be forgiven.

## Scene One: The Toll-Stones

**Purpose.** Show the pass road's dead, and give the party a reason to go through the howe and not around it.

**Situation.** Half a day below the fell the road narrows, and a line of waist-high cairns follows it up the slope. At dusk the cairns have men standing at them. They are grey with rime, their eyes are shut, and they wear mail of a make that nobody now remembers. A traveler who passes one hears a question in the old speech of the halls: _who holds the pass?_ The answer decides what happens next.

**NPCs and threats.** The war-band of Unthmil, the **Eidnár**, the oath-corpses. They are stiff, courteous and entirely without mercy toward a lie. The old men of the herders say a traveler who answers with an oath of his own is let through, and a traveler who gives an answer the dead cannot take is held at the cairn until morning and not seen again at the next stead. The Eidnár do not chase. They stand where they stood and wait.

**Player choices.** Force: cut a way through the line, which wakes the whole war-band and every cairn above. Stealth: climb the scree beside the road in the dark, where the dead do not look. Guile: answer with a false oath sworn on a true spear, which the Eidnár accept until they feel the lie in it. Parley: say that the pass is held by the dead man who holds it and that the party comes to relieve him, which is the answer they have waited for.

**Clues and rewards.** A herder at the last stead tells the party about the door-seat share at [[lore-vetrnaetr|Vetrnetr]] and that the dead have grown restless since the harvest. A rim of black glass shows on the nearest cairn, a chip fallen from something carried up the fell.

**Consequences.** A party that fights arrives at the howe with the war-band awake behind it. A party that swears truly is let through and is watched from the cairns as it climbs.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes up by a high shepherds' track reaches the fell at the north flank, where the gap in the turf is, and meets the toll-line from above on the way out.

## Scene Two: The Howe-Wake

**Purpose.** Learn what the dead man wants and what is owed to him.

**Situation.** The wake must be sat on the crown of the fell, awake, from dusk to dawn on Vetrnetr or on the ninth night of [[lore-jol|Jel]]. A velva's leave is customary and seldom given. [Hrumrún]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the seeress of Horgsvangr who knows the fell, listens to the party's reasons in the cold of the altar-field and gives leave to one of them, the one she judges can bear a question. She warns that the dead may ask a truthful answer in return.

**NPCs and threats.** Hrumrún is a gaunt old woman with a voice like a wind in a doorway. She will not go up the fell herself and tells the party plainly why: she has seen what the Shadow does to the ones who listen too long. At the wake, the thing in the howe speaks. Unthmil is courteous, confused and very tired. He does not know that he is dead. He asks his questions as a host asks a guest, and each one is a test: _has my seat been filled? who drank for me? who holds the pass?_

**Player choices.** Force: demand answers from the dead, which costs the seeker her warmth and her speech for a day. Stealth: listen without answering, which gives half the truth. Guile: answer as the herders do and flatter him, which holds him but binds him closer. Parley: tell him the truth gently, that he is dead, that his heir is dead and that nobody drank the cup.

**Clues and rewards.** The truthful answer to _who drank for me?_ is nobody. Asked what he is owed, Unthmil names it: the reckoning. What he owed was the oath to hold the pass until relieved, and what was owed to him was the cup and a successor to take the seat. The party learns that the grave-ale can be drunk by one of his odel-line, or by any person who will stand as the head of his hall, take his debts on themselves and swear his oath. It also learns that he cannot rest while the black glass holds him.

**Consequences.** What the dead tell a seeker is no witnessed record, and an answer taken from the wake must be borne out by living evidence before anyone acts on it. A wake worked against a named person is judged a grave wrong at the ting, and a party that names Knirvthann in the dark may be held to account for it. A party that lies at the wake binds the dead man to the lie.

**If bypassed.** A party that does not sit the wake can learn the same from the herders and from Hrumrún, though the truth is harder to credit second-hand. It meets Unthmil in the hall with less to say to him.

## Scene Three: The Gap and the Feast

**Purpose.** A trick of the hall, and the courtesy that governs it.

**Situation.** The only way into the ship is the gap in the north flank, where the dead were carried out. It is the way out, and the party enters by it. A section of turf lifts, planks lie behind it, and beyond them the hall is lit. The gap closes behind the party as the last of them steps through. Under the turf is a long hall that is also a ship, its keel under the floor and its benches lined with the dead. A feast is laid.

**NPCs and threats.** The Eidnár sit at the benches in rimed mail with their eyes shut, and their cups are full. A **steward of the hall**, an old retainer with a face like a boiled root, shows the party to the guest-benches. He treats them as guests, and he is bound by the old law to do so. Guest-right holds under the hall's roof as long as it is not broken. The black-glass shard in the prow-post hums.

**Player choices.** Force: attack the table, which breaks the guest-right and wakes the whole war-band to kill. Stealth: take nothing and slip through the shadows at the benches' end to the stern. Guile: eat and drink with the courtesy of a guest and take nothing from the howe; the ale of the dead binds a person who drinks all of a cup, and a careful guest takes a mouthful. Parley: tell the steward honestly why the party has come, and ask leave to approach the high seat.

**Clues and rewards.** Hack-silver lies heaped beside the stern chest as a challenge. The steward watches who looks at it. A person who asks leave to take a ring as a gift of the hall is given one. The cup of the grave-ale, an old horn bound in tarnished silver, stands empty in the stern chest, where it has stood since the day it was laid there for the heir.

**Consequences.** A party that eats and drinks fully is bound to the hall until the grave-ale is drunk. A party that breaks the guest-right has the whole war-band at its back.

**If bypassed.** A party that crosses the hall in a rush by the fighting-gangway through the benches reaches the stern with the war-band stirring behind it and no time to take the cup quietly.

## Scene Four: The High Seat

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the hall-lord, his captor and the oath.

**Situation.** At the stern, in the carved high seat, sits Unthmil, a tall grey man rimed to the beard, with a broken sword across his knees. Beside the seat stands Knirvthann with a bowl of black glass shards in his hands. He has come to bind the hall-lord. A rune-circle is scratched around the seat and lit with lamps burning grave-fat. When the party arrives, Knirvthann is in the middle of the rite.

**NPCs and threats.** Unthmil is the oath-bound lord of the pass, courteous to the last, and capable of killing a person who offers him an insult. Knirvthann is a Drottmadr of Nalok, a middle-aged man with soot-black fingernails and the quiet voice of a clerk, who serves the hidden hov at Dvilgvatn. He is not a fighter. He believes that the dead of this howe are owed rest and that Nahild will give it to them, and he is half right and wholly sure. The war-band wakes at his word.

**Player choices.** Force: kill Knirvthann before he finishes. The rite leaves Unthmil a nágengir if it completes and a broken corpse if it is interrupted by violence. Stealth: slip the shard from the prow-post while the Drottmadr's back is turned, which frees the hall-lord to rest and has the war-band on the party. Guile: persuade Knirvthann that his Dróttstjori will take his work for her own, which turns his fear against his orders. Parley: stand at the foot of the high seat, drink the grave-ale, and swear the hall-lord's oath. The party's odel-claimant, or any volunteer who is willing to answer for the dead man's debts, takes the horn.

**Clues and rewards.** The grave-ale is the only thing that ends this. The cup must be filled, the reckoning spoken aloud, and the heir must drink it standing at the foot of the high seat and swear the oath he means to keep as the hall's head. The person who does it is the hall's head, with the debts that go with it. Unthmil does not rise from the seat again once the cup is drunk.

**Consequences.** A party that drinks the cup lays the war-band to rest, and the Eidnár fall at the benches like cut grain. The drinker takes on the debts of a hall that broke up in the Age of Halls, and the claim to a pass that nobody holds. A party that breaks the shard without the cup frees the black glass but leaves the dead man unreceived and angry. A party that kills Knirvthann without the cup leaves a hall that still keeps the pass.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the high seat and flees with the hoard is chased by the war-band to the edge of the cairns, and the toll-stones close the road behind them.

## Scene Five: The Tally-Stick

**Purpose.** Pay the reward and reveal the larger design.

**Situation.** Knirvthann's satchel, or the stern chest if the party is quick, holds what he carried up the fell: a bone case of black glass shards, each cut to the same size, and a hazel tally-stick notched with rune-marks. The hoard of the ship lies behind the stern chest, where the war-band laid it.

**NPCs and threats.** If Knirvthann lives, he is the one asking for the tally-stick back, and the Nalok send others after it.

**Player choices.** Force: take the hoard and run. Stealth: read the tally by lamp-light and put it back. Guile: keep it and sell its contents to the Raven Guard. Parley: give it to Hrumrún and ask the velvur to read it.

**Clues and rewards.** The hoard is modest: arm-rings of hack-silver, a pattern-welded sword of old make, a drinking horn bound in silver and a few Vylarian Ardini from a trader long buried in the herders' ground. It is far less than the rumor said, and more than a thief could carry in a night. The tally-stick lists seven howes, each with a notch where a shard has been set. Beside the notches is a mark that stands for the black ice of Dvilgvatn.

**Consequences.** The shards are in other howes, and each of them holds a dead man whose cup was never drunk. A party that follows the tally will find that the Nalok work is wider than this fell.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves the stern at once hears of the tally from Hrumrún within a season, when a herder finds a bone case on the fell and brings it to her.

## Aftermath

- **If the cup was drunk.** The war-band falls and the toll-stones go quiet. The drinker is named among the district's old halls, and the herders send the door-seat share to the drinker's own table. The hold of the Nalok on the pass is broken, and the black glass in the prow-post goes dull.
- **If the shard was broken without the cup.** The Shadow loosens its grip and Unthmil subsides into the long restlessness of an unreceived dead man. The toll-stones stop their questions and begin to ask for something else.
- **If Knirvthann escaped.** He carries the tally to his Dróttstjori, and another howe is seeded within the year.
- **If the party failed.** Unthmil becomes a nágengir, the war-band becomes hrúmverdir, and the pass road is closed until the Raven Guard can find the warriors to open it.
