## Overview {#overview}

The embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] has reached [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], the capital of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]], to ask for direct relations with the throne. The chancellery has lodged it in a compound on one shore, and it keeps to the compound until it is summoned. To be summoned it must file its papers: the [[affiliation-dunavarsynod|Dúnavarre Synod]]'s commission and the Kingdom's request, in the Tantsou fair copy that its translator, [[being-loristaravel|Loris Taravel]], made on the road.

[[being-chengzhodlwn|Chengzho]], the Prince of [[affiliation-doulwenprv|Doulwen Province]], has taken the embassy up openly. The other three Princes each read it as a move against them, and each works to turn it against him. Each Emperor's reign is its own era, the chancellery's scholars are cross-referencing every filing of the old era to the new, and a filing that goes into that work can wait a year. The clerks of the chancellery are for sale.

The party serves the embassy, as in [[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dúnavarre to Tantsou]]. It can learn that the fair copy itself sends the papers to the wrong hall, put a copy of the new reign into the right hands before the roll closes, and bring the embassy to the bridge foot on the morning of its hearing with nobody unnamed beside it.

**The hearing.** A petitioner crosses a bridge on written permission and waits in the gate-court before the enclave wall, where the Speaker hears the question, whispers it to the Emperor and speaks the answer on the record.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The fair copy.** Loris dated the fair copy in the reign of the Retired Emperor, [[being-yanzhortrd|Yanzho]]. He knows the reign has changed: since the embassy entered the empire he has read Tantsou papers dated in the new reign of [[being-tengrauemp|Tengrau]], and among them is the chancellery's own lodging order, which [[being-elowyrnimavren|Elowir Nimavren]] entered in the embassy's register of correspondence on the day of arrival and passed to him to read. A paper dated in the old reign is a filing of the old era, and it goes to the hall that cross-references the old era to the new. Loris wants the embassy to need the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]], and a year's wait in Zhowen is a long argument for Viléria as intermediary. He has told [[being-marricharavrel|Marrik Háravrel]] that the chancellery's first clerk is the proper hand for the papers.

**The first clerk.** A household of one of the other three Princes has paid a clerk of the hall of the old era to collect the embassy's papers. The clerk's order is genuine, and the hall would keep a paper of the old reign whoever carried it there. The clerk knows the household by its seal and knows nothing more.

**The clerk of the roll.** Chengzho's officers pay a clerk of the hall that keeps the Speaker's roll and writes the permissions to cross; on the evening the roll closes the hall sends it to the Governor's officers, who enter its names on the day's list at the bridge foot. The roll for the next hearing closes at sunset on Day Four and takes papers of the new reign only. This clerk is no more honest than the first. For now the Prince's interest and the embassy's run together.

**The names.** The permission names the petitioner and two attendants. [[being-arancelavren|Aran Kélavren]] leaves the choice to [[being-toradhtaravren|Dame Torad Taravren]], who holds the compound for him. Loris claims a place as translator. [[being-arrenthabavel|Sir Arren Thábavel]] stands a half-step behind the ambassador in every hall, and he means to stand there in the gate-court whether the list names him or not.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scene the party must reach is sunset on Day Four with a fair copy of the new reign in the hands of the clerk of the roll. Without it the embassy is not heard.

> [!WARNING]
> Loris says he followed the forms Viléria's court uses for letters to the east, and nothing in the embassy shows otherwise. The register proves that he read the lodging order, not whom he serves. The two serve one end, but Loris has never dealt with the household that bought the first clerk.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- **The household.** Which household paid the first clerk: that of [[being-rinzhowngsn|Rinzho]], of [[being-hozhomwnou|Hozho]] or of [[being-tuizhokwzng|Tuizho]]. Nobody from the household comes to the compound.
- **The fees.** The first clerk's price, the higher sum that turns the clerk, and the clerk of the roll's fee for a new copy.
- **The compound and the hearing.** Where the compound stands, a short walk from its shore's administrative center and the bridge foot before it. The setting fixes only that permission to cross is written and that the officers at each bridge foot keep the day's list; the rest is the Game Master's. The scenario holds a hearing on Day Five, gives the Speaker's roll and the permissions to one hall of the chancellery, closes the roll at sunset the day before and takes papers of the new reign only, and has a permission name the petitioner and two attendants.
- **The ambassador.** Aran keeps to his rooms from Day One to Day Four and sends his word to the party through Torad; he comes out only for the walk to the bridge foot on Day Five. Elowir and the rest of the household stay in the background.
- **The Speaker's words.** The answer given in the gate-court is the Game Master's to word. The scenario decides only whether the embassy is heard.
- **Loris's orders.** Whether Viléria told him to date the copy in the old reign, or he chose it himself.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The courtier: the compound gate.** Dame Torad asks the party to receive the chancellery's clerks at the gate in the ambassador's name, and to let no paper leave without her word.
- **The scholar: a second reader.** The ambassador, through Torad, wants someone besides the translator to read the fair copy before it is filed.
- **The warrior: the walk to the bridge.** Marrik wants the party to plan the embassy's walk from the compound to the bridge foot on the morning of the hearing, and to learn the archers' rule before anyone sets out.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The compound is a square of gray brick around a paved court, with a gate on a street so straight that its far end is lost in haze. On the first morning someone knocks at the gate, once.
>
> A clerk in a dark blue robe stands in the street with a scribe's kit at the belt and a folded order held out in both hands, the seal upward. "The embassy's papers," the clerk says in careful Tantsou, and waits.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Compound Gate

**Purpose.** Meet the first clerk, and the first wrong door.

**Situation.** Day One, morning, at the gate. Dame Torad stands in the gateway; Marrik is beside her; Loris comes from the writing room with his portfolio.

**NPCs and threats.** The first clerk, a [[being-chnclryclrk|Chancellery Clerk of Zhowen]], who asks to see the papers to know which hall keeps them; Marrik, who says on Loris's word that this clerk is the proper hand; Loris, who offers the portfolio; Torad, who wants the party's opinion before anything leaves.

**Player choices.** Hand the papers over, show them without letting them go, read the clerk's order, or decline the way a Tantsou declines, without saying no: the papers are with the translator, a copy is being checked.

**Clues and rewards.** Shown the fair copy, the clerk reads the date in silence and says only that the clerk's hall, which cross-references the old era to the new, keeps every paper dated in the old reign. The order says the same, under that hall's seal.

**Consequences.** Papers handed over go into the hall of the old era that day, and only the first clerk, paid more than the household paid, brings them out again. A courteous refusal sends the clerk away; the clerk says the hall will send again on Day Three. Marrik must decide whether to correct the advice he has already given Aran.

**If bypassed.** Torad hands the papers over on Marrik's advice, and the clock runs as under If the Party Does Nothing.

> [!TIP]
> Play the clerk as courteous and entirely within the law. The order is real, and a party that calls the clerk a cheat at the gate has insulted the chancellery in the open street.

### Scene Two: The Translator's Table

**Purpose.** Find the fault in the embassy's own paper.

**Situation.** Day One, evening, in the embassy's writing room: Loris's portfolio, the fair copy, and Elowir's register of correspondence on the table where Elowir keeps it.

**NPCs and threats.** Loris, who is helpful and unhurried.

**Player choices.** Read the fair copy, read the register, ask Loris about the date, or take what they find to Marrik or Torad.

**Clues and rewards.** The copy is dated in the reign of the Retired Emperor. A character who reads [[skill-tengchouscript|Teng Chou]] sees it at once; asked directly, Loris reads the date aloud correctly. The register shows the chancellery's lodging order received on the day of arrival, dated in the new reign, and entered as passed to Loris to read the same day.

**Consequences.** Asked about the date, Loris says he followed the forms Viléria's court uses for letters to the east, and offers to correct it that night. A copy he corrects is correct; he does not mean to be caught twice. The register gives Marrik reason to doubt his informant, not proof of what Loris is.

**If bypassed.** The copy stays as it is, and the rule the clerk of the roll gives in Scene Three shows what that costs.

### Scene Three: The Prince's Letter

**Purpose.** Find the right door, and the deadline.

**Situation.** Day Two, midday. A second clerk comes up the open street to the gate with a letter under the seal of the Prince of Doulwen, commending the embassy to the hall of the roll. The clerk carries it seal outward.

**NPCs and threats.** The clerk of the roll, a Chancellery Clerk of Zhowen, who is brisk and wants the papers and a fee; Torad, who receives the letter.

**Player choices.** Give the clerk the papers, ask what the roll requires, buy a new copy, or ask about the first clerk.

**Clues and rewards.** The clerk says that the roll for the hearing on Day Five closes at sunset on Day Four and takes papers of the new reign only, and that the permission names the petitioner and two attendants, whose names the clerk must have with the papers. Asked about the first clerk, the clerk says only that somebody sent the first clerk.

**Consequences.** For a fee the clerk writes a fair copy in the new reign's forms from the old one and brings it back to be checked before sunset on Day Four. The roll does not take the old copy, and a party that hands it over as it is gets it back.

**If bypassed.** The clerk leaves the letter with Torad and comes back at sunset on Day Four for whatever the embassy has.

### Scene Four: The Second Order

**Purpose.** Answer the first clerk, and choose who crosses.

**Situation.** Day Three, at the gate and in the court. The first clerk returns with the hall's written claim to the papers of the old reign. The same day Torad must settle the two names.

**NPCs and threats.** The first clerk; Torad; Loris, who claims the translator's place; Arren, who claims the place behind the ambassador and resents any suggestion that Torad should have it.

**Player choices.** Show a copy of the new reign, delay the claim with courtesy, pay the first clerk more than the household did, or argue the names with Torad, Loris and Arren.

**Clues and rewards.** Outbid, the first clerk says that a household of another Prince paid for the papers, and describes its seal. Asked, or passed over, Arren says that he stands a half-step behind the ambassador in every hall and stands there in the gate-court, named or not.

**Consequences.** A copy of the new reign ends the claim, and the clerk goes without argument. With only the old copy the claim is lawful: the party can put the clerk off with courtesy until the roll closes, or pay the clerk to go. A party that names Arren, or persuades him or Torad that the list is the law, takes the danger out of Scene Five.

**If bypassed.** Torad names Loris and herself, and Arren is not named.

### Scene Five: The Bridge Foot

**Purpose.** The climax: the list, and the bridge.

**Situation.** Day Five, morning. The bridge foot before the administrative center: the [Jingdou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"} or the [Bwidou Bridge]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, whichever crosses from the compound's shore, running out across the water to the gate-court, the enclave wall behind it, and archers walking the top of the wall. The embassy comes on foot from the compound, Marrik's guard around it.

**NPCs and threats.** A [[being-brdgofcrzhwn|Bridge Officer of Zhowen]], who reads the permission against the day's list and names each person aloud; Torad and Marrik; Loris, if named; Arren. The ambassador, who crosses if the list names him, speaks to nobody but the officer.

**Player choices.** Present the permission, hold back whoever is not named, or try to have a name added. The officer adds no name: the list is the day's list, entered by the Governor's officers from the closed roll, and nobody at the bridge foot can change it.

**Clues and rewards.** A permission written on the roll matches the list, and the named cross to the gate-court and come back across with the Speaker's answer. The scene stays at the bridge foot: what passes in the gate-court reaches the party through those who come back across, whoever is named.

**Consequences.** An unnamed member who follows onto the bridge is shot dead by the archers on the wall, and nothing at the foot can fight them. Held back by force, Arren lives, shamed before Torad and the city, and does not forgive it.

**If bypassed.** If no one has warned Torad of what Arren means to do, he follows the ambassador onto the bridge.

## Endings {#endings}

On Day Five, by the end of the hearing:

- **Heard.** A copy of the new reign has reached the roll in time, the named have crossed, and nobody has stepped onto the bridge unbidden. The embassy's request is a filing of the new reign, and the Speaker's answer is on the record. Chengzho has shown the court that his embassy can be heard. The households of the other three Princes turn to other means.
- **Heard, with blood.** The papers have reached the roll, and an unnamed member of the embassy has died on the bridge. The court's first account of the embassy is of foreigners who could not keep to a list. Chengzho's sponsorship carries that shame, and the other households use it.
- **Not heard.** The papers are in the hall of the old era or have missed the roll. The embassy keeps to its compound while the cross-referencing runs, perhaps for a year. Chengzho has sponsored foreigners who could not file a paper, and the other households say so. Loris writes to Viléria that the embassy waits.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

On Day One Torad gives the papers to the first clerk on Marrik's advice, which came from Loris, and they go into the hall of the old era. On Day Two the clerk of the roll brings Chengzho's letter and finds nothing to take. The roll closes on Day Four without the embassy, no permission is written, and on Day Five nobody crosses and nobody is shot.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Whatever the ending, carry three things forward: where the papers are, who in the embassy knows what Loris wrote, and what Chengzho now owes or blames the embassy for. A party that outbid the first clerk knows which household moved against the embassy, and that household knows somebody paid more.
