## Overview {#overview}

A caravan of a Bethuan Great House, carts and pack-mules loaded with grain and cloth, has come the length of [[place-marchesroad|the Marches Road]] from the frontier posts, because the coast is shut or too closely watched for this House's cargo, and stands at [[place-hasembecamp|Hasembe Camp]] in the east of the [[place-sthmrchsrgn|South Marches]]. It means to hire Hasembe riders for the eastern stages, the last wells before the grazing selatu of [[place-aukhelathrgq|Au'Khelathu Region]]. The Hirer of Riders has none to give: the [[affiliation-hasembetribe|Hasembe Tribe]] has more caravans asking than riders this season.

A riding-party of [[affiliation-jcklsthmrchs|the Jackals of the South Marches]] has camped behind the caravan since the last western stage, and it offers the caravan's [[being-tradefactor|Trade-House Factor]] an escort east at its own price. [[being-khandoruhsmb|Khandoru of the Hasembe]] does not want the Jackals to be the company that gives it, because a caravan that learns to pay the Jackals learns to stop paying him. He offers the contract to the party.

The party can learn that the Jackals followed this caravan on purpose, which well ahead has failed, and where the riding-party is weakest.

### The clock

**The contract.** Khandoru's terms are that the caravan reaches the last Hasembe well whole and pays no company on the way. He pays half at the horse-lines and half when his lent riders come back and tell him it did. Water bought from a clan is not a company's toll.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The caravan.** The factor has paid the Jackals' toll at each western stage they hold. The factor would rather not pay them a second time, and cannot find Hasembe riders.

**The riding-party.** A [[being-jcklieutent|Lieutenant of the Jackals]], the speaker, rides out from the western stage she holds with two of the Houseless, each a [[being-houslessftr|Houseless Fighter]], and about a dozen hired marcher men, each a [[being-hiredmarchr|Hired Marcher Rider]]. The marcher men are Hasembe sons that the Hirer of Riders contracted to the Jackals in the spring, and their contract runs to the autumn drive, which is close. The tribe's other horsemen are home for the drive; these are held by their contract until it. The speaker names [[being-argyrirjckl|Argirira the Jackal]]'s price and does not move from it. The riding-party followed this caravan east on purpose. A Bethuan caravan that pays the Jackals on the eastern stages as well as the western ones tells every caravan behind it which company holds the road.

**The tired well.** The clan that waters at the third stage found its shaft giving a trickle and its cistern low, and took its herds east to share the last well with its kin some days before Day One. Word went west only as far as the clan at the second stage, so Khandoru does not know. The third stage is a broken tower, a wall-stump and a stair, with the shaft and the open cistern beside it. What is left in the cistern waters the riding-party's horses and the caravan's beasts once, and no more.

**The seam.** Two of the Jackals' marcher men are sons of that clan. They learn at the second stage, as the party can, that their fathers have left the well. They do not object to the riding-party drinking at an empty well. Selling their clan's water to a Bethuan caravan, said aloud in front of the others, is a different thing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scene the party must reach is the tired well with a choice of its own: water it carried, the clan's sons on its side, or the strength to take the road. Without one of these, the factor pays.

> [!WARNING]
> Nothing at the ruin shows that the Jackals fouled or blocked the well, because they did not: it failed. Whether drinking at a well without its clan's leave is a wrong is for the clan to say.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- **How many stages.** The road east of Hasembe Camp to the grazing selatu has as many stages as the table needs; the tired well is the next to last.
- **The factor's House and cargo.** Any Great House of [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|The Bethuan Trade-Houses]] that sends grain and cloth east by the road.
- **The speaker.** An exiled Bethuan woman of the Houseless, as the Lieutenant's note has her; her name and past are the Game Master's.
- **The prices.** Khandoru's fee, the speaker's price at the camp, and Argirira's toll for water at a well the Jackals hold. The Jackals' price makes the factor hesitate; it does not break the House.
- **The clans in person.** The clans at the wells are not met in play. The lent riders ask leave for the caravan, as its Hasembe escort, and carry the answers back. The Hirer of Riders is not met either: Khandoru makes this contract himself, in the Hirer's tent.
- **The tired well's clan.** Whether it makes a quarrel of the Jackals at its well once it hears, and what Khandoru does with that quarrel. The clans tolerate the Jackals because they are useful against the north.
- **The sons, if nobody speaks.** Whether they ride on with the Jackals or go home to their clan at the drive.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The warrior: a contract at the horse-lines.** Khandoru sets the Hirer's price for an escort and lends two riders who know the stages. A party hired here meets the caravan on Day One.
- **The trader: company from the frontier.** The party has come the whole road as the factor's hands and reaches Hasembe Camp with the carts on the evening before Day One. The factor wants it to take Khandoru's contract and his riders for the eastern stages.
- **The woodsman: passage east.** The party is bound for the selatu itself and finds no escort at the camp. Khandoru offers it the contract, and its second half waits at the camp until the party comes back west.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The carts stand in a line beside the walled well with their shafts down, the mules hobbled along the horse-lines among the small marcher horses. Somebody has spread the House's cloth bales under an awning to keep them out of the dust. A woman in a leather surcoat sits a horse beside the lead cart, a shortsword at her hip, talking down to the factor in Bethuan.
>
> A man in a felted cloak, a strip of red wool braided into his beard, comes along the horse-lines with a bowl of soured milk in his hands and does not look at the rider. He holds the bowl out to you. "The Hirer has no riders," he says. "I have a price. Drink first."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Horse-Lines at Hasembe Camp

**Purpose.** Set the contract, the price and the rival.

**Situation.** Hasembe Camp on Day One: tents, horse-lines and the walled well on the road. The caravan stands by the well. The riding-party camps on the camp's western edge and waters at the well by its marcher men's leave.

**NPCs and threats.** Khandoru, who names his price at once and holds it; the factor, who wants to go east tomorrow, would rather not pay the Jackals twice, and pays them if nobody else offers; the House's carters, each a [[being-housecarter|House Carter]]; the speaker and the Jackals' marcher men; two Hasembe horsemen, each a Hired Marcher Rider, whom Khandoru lends to the party.

**Player choices.** Drink and bargain with Khandoru, talk to the factor and the carters, or go to the Jackals' fire and hear their offer.

**Clues and rewards.** The carters say the same riders have camped a mile behind the train since the last western stage, and the factor's tallies show the Jackals' toll paid at each stage they hold. Asked who rides with the Jackals, Khandoru says most of them are Hasembe sons the Hirer contracted to the Jackals in the spring, until the drive, and that the tribe has their share of the pay.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the contract leaves at dawn with the caravan, and the riding-party follows.

**If bypassed.** The factor pays the speaker, and the clock runs as under If the Party Does Nothing.

> [!TIP]
> Play Khandoru as a man selling the one thing he has short. He is not ashamed that his tribe hires riders to the Jackals; he objects only to their selling the escort on his road.

### Scene Two: The Second Stage

**Purpose.** Learn what lies ahead, in time to act on it.

**Situation.** The second stage on Day Three: a Khelathi fort with its gatehouse fallen, the well in its courtyard and the clan's tents outside. The lent riders ask leave for the caravan; the riding-party's marcher men ask for their own. Both water at the same trough after dark.

**NPCs and threats.** The lent riders, who are loose-tongued when paid; the Jackals' marcher men, talking with their kin; the speaker, who keeps the Houseless apart.

**Player choices.** Ask the lent riders what the clan said, sit at the trough, buy water, or watch the Jackals' camp.

**Clues and rewards.** Asked or paid, the lent riders tell what the clan told them: the third stage's clan has taken its herds to the last well, its shaft tired. After the trough they say that two of the Jackals' marcher men are sons of that clan and that the news sat badly with them. The clan sells water by the skin, enough to carry the beasts through one dry stage, for a price the factor can pay. At dawn on Day Four the riding-party's tracks run east.

**Consequences.** A party that buys water passes the third stage owing the Jackals nothing. A party that watches the Jackals' camp sees them ride out in the night.

**If bypassed.** The caravan reaches the third stage on Day Four with nothing carried, and the party learns of the tired well from the speaker.

### Scene Three: The Tired Well

**Purpose.** The confrontation: water, the price, and the seam.

**Situation.** The third stage on Day Four: a wall-stump and a stair on a rise, the shaft and the open cistern below it, the water a hand's depth down. The riding-party sits on the ruin. Its horses have drunk, and what is left waters the caravan's beasts once.

**NPCs and threats.** The speaker, a [[being-jcklieutent|Lieutenant of the Jackals]], who names the price: the escort to the last well at the camp's price, and Argirira's toll for the water here; the two Houseless, who keep the stair; the marcher men, among them the clan's two sons.

**Player choices.** Pay, pass by on carried water, put it to the sons in front of the others that the Jackals are selling their clan's water to a Bethuan caravan, or take the water by force.

**Clues and rewards.** The shaft gives a trickle, and nothing is down it or across it. Paid or asked away from the speaker, a marcher man says that if the factor refuses, the riding-party takes the carts on the open stretch before the last well, the next day.

**Consequences.** Put to them plainly, the sons refuse to let the Jackals sell their fathers' water, tell the lent riders the caravan may drink as their clan would let it, and ride on that night to their fathers at the last well. The other marcher men do not hold a Hasembe well against a Hasembe contract for the Houseless, and go home for the drive. The speaker and the two Houseless, out of their line, ride west. A fight at the ruin against the whole riding-party is one a seasoned party wins at a cost. A party that passes on carried water owes nothing here, and the speaker lets it pass.

**If bypassed.** The factor pays on the evening of Day Four.

### Scene Four: The Last Hasembe Well

**Purpose.** The climax: the open stretch, and the end of the contract.

**Situation.** Day Five. The road leaves the ruin across open stony ground and reaches the last Hasembe well in the late afternoon, where two clans' herds crowd the trough and drink before any caravan.

**NPCs and threats.** If the factor refused the price and the riding-party is whole, the speaker, the Houseless and the marcher men come at the carts from the flank at midday to cut out the grain and drive it west. Caught strung out on the road, the caravan loses carts even if the party wins. A party that knows the raid is coming can close the carts up and choose its ground, and then it is a fight a seasoned party wins at a cost to itself and not to the carts.

**Player choices.** Fight, run the carts for the well, or meet the speaker on the road and pay there.

**Clues and rewards.** Arriving whole ends the contract. The lent riders ask leave and the clans water the caravan after their herds.

**Consequences.** If the sons rode ahead, their clan knows the Jackals sat on its well, and the lent riders carry its word to Khandoru.

**If bypassed.** The caravan reaches the well under whatever escort it has.

## Endings {#endings}

On Day Nine Khandoru hears his lent riders at the horse-lines.

- **Whole, and no company paid.** The caravan carried its water or the sons refused the Jackals their fathers' water, and either no raid came or the party met it ready and lost no cart. Khandoru pays the second half and wants the party for the next caravan. The factor asks the Hirer for Hasembe riders first next season. Argirira learns who stood on the eastern road.
- **Through the raid.** The caravan reached the well with carts lost. Khandoru pays for what arrived; how much is the Game Master's. The Jackals have shown the eastern road that a Hasembe escort can be cut.
- **The factor paid.** The caravan reached the well whole under the Jackals' escort. Khandoru keeps the second half. The factor goes to the Jackals at the frontier posts next season for the whole road, and the riding-party sells escort at the eastern wells for the rest of the dry months.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The factor pays the speaker on Day One and goes east under the Jackals at dawn. On Day Four the riding-party waters the caravan at the tired well for a second sum, and on Day Five it delivers the caravan whole to the last Hasembe well. Khandoru has lost a caravan to the Jackals on his own road.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that split the riding-party has shown Khandoru that a clan's grievance can take his tribe's sons back from the Jackals. What he does with that is his. The speaker remembers whoever turned her riders.
