## Overview {#overview}

The village of [[place-belsaren|Belsaren]] has given up the outlaws' cave, and [[being-diravekldrm|Dirave al Kaldremis]] knows it. He means to move his band, and he has not moved it yet. The party goes up the ridge trail to the cave in its rocky outcrop, through the band's traps and watchers, and into the cave where the deposed heir of Belsaren makes his stand. What the party finds on the bodies and in Dirave's chamber is the first evidence that the outlaws were never only outlaws.

This adventure follows [[scenario-bndtscrvlvl|The Bandits of Keravel Vale]], and it can be its last evening.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The band.** Dirave holds the cave with about a dozen men. The cave is a natural cavern in a rocky outcrop at the foot of a forested ridge south of the village, its mouth hidden by an overhang and heavy brush. Smoke from its fires rises faintly through gaps in the canopy. It has a second way out, a narrow back way through the rocks known only to the band.

**The lieutenants.** Three of Dirave's lieutenants joined him this spring. They are Black Flame soldiers lent by [[being-kaldinlnvl|Kaldin al Lénavel na Leravren]] to [[being-kilanilvrl|Kilan Ilovrel na Ilovren]] and placed in the band through Kilan's go-betweens. They pray before a raid and wear the Flame's emblem on a cord. They fight hard, and if the fight is lost they try to escape by the back way and carry word to Ilovren.

**What Dirave knows.** He knows that a friend among the vale's lords sends him supplies and word of the escorts, and he does not know which lord. He has received a second letter, from a writer who signs himself "a friend", warning him that his help is bait and offering proof of Kilan's lies at the grove near Ilovren at the new moon. He has not gone. The writer is [[being-darisenlrn|Darisen al Olrun]].

**What Dirave will do.** He talks before he fights, because he was raised a lord and wants to be heard as one. If the fight goes against him, he calls a truce and bargains: he names Kilan as the man who used him, and offers the name of an informant who knows Kilan's plans in exchange for his life. He means Darisen, though he knows him only by the letter's promise.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Dirave's speech is the center of this adventure. Let him make his case. Whether the party kills him, takes him to Tarlas or lets him go shapes everything after.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The lord's order.** [[being-tarlasplnvr|Tarlas]] wants Dirave's head on the manor gate before the Marshal hears the square burned.
- **The guide.** The goat herder who broke in the square offers to lead the party up the ridge trail, and asks it to spare the young men of the village who are up there.
- **The old lord's son.** [[being-sateretrvl|Satere Torvilis]], released from his bonds, asks the party to bring Dirave out alive.

## The Opening {#opening}

Dawn on the ridge trail. The path climbs through oak and thorn over exposed roots, and the party passes a discarded sack, a fresh boot print, a cold fire. Thin smoke rises through the canopy ahead, where the ground lifts into rock.

A bell jangles somewhere close: the party, or a deer, has found a tripwire.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Approach

**Purpose.** The band's outer defenses, and the choice between stealth and speed.

**Situation.** The forested slope below the outcrop. Tripwires strung across the path ring bells or bring down a rattle of stones. A pit ten feet deep lies under leaves and branches. A narrow ledge over a twenty-foot drop is the only way round a stretch of thorn, and a slip there is heard in the cave.

**NPCs and threats.** Two outlaws watching the path from a hide in the trees, who run for the cave if they see the party.

**Player choices.** Creep up and take the watchers quietly, deceive them, or rush the cave before they can raise the alarm.

**Clues and rewards.** The watchers, if taken, talk about the traps inside and grumble about the last ambush, where Dirave's plans got men killed. A frightened young watcher from the village offers to show the party the traps in exchange for mercy.

**Consequences.** A party that reaches the cave unseen meets its defenders before they have gathered.

**If bypassed.** The watchers warn the cave, and the defenders are at the barricades.

### Scene Two: The Mouth and the Passage

**Purpose.** Fight into the cave.

**Situation.** The entrance chamber is wide and low, behind crude barricades with a raised platform for archers. Beyond it a narrow winding passage runs inward, lined with barricades and a tripwire that tips a jar of burning oil into the passage. A wide chamber off the passage looks like a way out, with light falling from an opening above. It is a dead end, and the band's best men wait behind the rocks there.

**NPCs and threats.** Three outlaws at the mouth, who try to hold while the rest gather. Two swordsmen and an archer in the passage, who fall back as they fight. Archers and close fighters in the false exit, attacking from both sides.

**Player choices.** Storm the barricade, burn the defenders out, find the oil trap before it is sprung, or follow a fleeing outlaw into the false exit.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that sees footprints and loose earth at the false exit is not surprised by it. The defenders there have a stash of supplies, herbs and spare weapons, and a rough map of the cave.

**Consequences.** Every man who falls back carries word deeper in.

**If bypassed.** The party finds the back way through the rocks, from a prisoner or the village, and comes in behind the lieutenants.

### Scene Three: The Stores and the Lieutenants

**Purpose.** The evidence, and the men who do not belong.

**Situation.** A side chamber of stolen goods: food, tools, cloth and weapons. Beyond it the lieutenants' quarters guard the approach to Dirave's chamber.

**NPCs and threats.** The three lieutenants. One fights with a heavy axe, one with a bow that seeks out the party's weakest, and one with daggers and short sword, moving and striking from the flank. Each wears a small medallion bearing the Black Flame's emblem.

**Player choices.** Fight, take a lieutenant alive, or let one run for the back way and follow him.

**Clues and rewards.** A locked chest in the stores holds messages in a simple cipher about the escorts' orders, which only someone close to the lords could know, and a scrap marked with the Black Flame's sign. A lieutenant taken alive says nothing of his faith and asks for a priest of the Flame before he dies.

**Consequences.** A lieutenant who escapes reaches Ilovren by night, and Kilan knows the cave has fallen.

**If bypassed.** The lieutenants fall back to Dirave's chamber and fight beside him.

### Scene Four: Dirave's Chamber

**Purpose.** The outlaw's case, and the party's choice.

**Situation.** A large cavern with a natural chimney carrying the smoke away. A crude chair stands at the back, flanked by torches. The approaches are blocked with overturned tables, crates and rocks into narrow ways, and Dirave has rigged a rockfall he can bring down if the fight turns.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-diravekldrm|Dirave]] and his two best fighters. He stands with his hand on his sword and speaks before anyone draws.

> "I wasn't always living in the dirt. Belsaren was my family's. We cared for its people, and they prospered, until the Marshal called us traitors and gave it to a man who thinks the Tanevar are cattle. Now they call me a bandit. What would you do, if everything you were was stolen from you? Bow, like the Tanevar working his fields? Or take it back?"

He offers the party a place in what comes after. If the party asks about the attack on the square, his face hardens: the village forgot him and bowed to Tarlas, and if fear is the only way to remind it who its lord is, so be it. If the party says he is becoming what he hates, he says perhaps he is, and that it makes Tarlas no less a tyrant. If the party asks about his lieutenants' emblems, guilt flickers: he does not ask where his men have been or what gods they pray to, and this is about Belsaren and nothing else. If the party demands his surrender, he laughs at it and draws.

**Player choices.** Fight, talk him down, accept his bargain, or take him alive for the lord.

**Clues and rewards.** When the fight turns, Dirave calls a truce, bloodied: "You think this is about me? I was a tool. Kilan Ilovrel used me. He whispered promises of a free Belsaren, and what he wants has nothing to do with the Tanevar. Let me go, and I'll give you the name of the one who knows." If he dies, his last words are: "You've put out a spark. The fire's coming."

In his chamber the party finds the band's silver, goods from the raids, his journal and a letter. The journal says a friend among the lords sends him help, and mentions a second letter and the word "grove". The letter is on creased parchment, sealed with dark red wax and an unmarked seal, in a neat educated hand:

> Dirave,
>
> Your cause is just, but you have been blind to the true game. Those who claim to support you weave a far darker web than you imagine. What was sent your way was no charity. It was bait. They use you to break Belsaren, not for the Tanevar, but for a scheme you do not understand.
>
> If you wish to learn the truth, meet me in the grove near Ilovren on the night of the new moon. I can give you proof of Kilan's lies. Come alone, for your safety and mine.
>
> Yours in the shadows, a friend

The letter carries a cipher in the first letters of its lines and words, which a patient reader can draw out: Kilan's scheme includes a Black Flame shrine; he means to break Keravel and recover an artifact; and Darisen is Dirave's friend and ally now.

**Consequences.** A Dirave who lives and goes free gives the party Darisen's name and vanishes into the hills. A Dirave delivered to Tarlas is hanged at the crossroads, and the village does not forgive the party. A Dirave killed in the cave leaves the letter to speak for him.

**If bypassed.** Dirave escapes by the back way with his two fighters, and the party finds the chamber, the journal and the letter.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Dirave moves the band to the Blood Hills, where pursuers will not follow, and the raids go on from there. Tarlas puts the Tanevar families to the question one by one. The lieutenants report to Ilovren, and Kilan begins to tell the Marshal that Tarlas let the outlaws escape.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The band is broken, and the party holds a letter that names the lord of Ilovren. Tarlas pays the party, grows uneasy about the flame emblems and urges it to keep looking while he tightens his grip on the village. The party must decide what to do with any outlaws who surrendered and with the villagers who fed them, and whether to collect its reward and ride on or follow the letter to [[place-ilovren|Ilovren]] ([[scenario-webdeceit|The Web of Deceit]]).
