## Overview {#overview}

The Maul of Gashang is a spiked mace of blackened steel made by the priest-artificers of [[affiliation-blackflame|the Black Flame]] to show what the faith holds to be the cruel and destroying face of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]]. The Ashlord Hazarak Bloodhand carried it at [[lore-bloodhillsbtl|the Battle of the Blood Hills]] about 248 AF and fell with it. The Flame's zealots carried it to a cave shrine in [[place-bloodhills|the Blood Hills]] and set traps to keep it for a worthy successor.

The party enters the shrine, passes the blood gate, the fire pits and the altar, faces the guardian, and finds the maul on its pedestal. Then it must decide whether to take it, destroy it, or give it up, and to whom. This adventure follows [[scenario-firsbldhls|The Fires of the Blood Hills]] and ends the campaign.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The maul.** The head of the maul carries eight blades with jagged spikes. The vale's legend says each blade stands for a battle or a sacrifice offered to the Flame, that the maul was forged in the heart of a volcano, and that its spikes were cooled in the blood of enemies. The handle is wrapped in red leather that the legend says was flayed from priests of [[affiliation-sacredforge|the Sacred Forge]], inscribed with the Flame's prayers for violence and torment. In the shrine's ember-light the blades seem to glow when blood is on them.

**What it does.** The maul's power is craft as much as faith, and a careful party can tell which is which.

- **The fire.** The handle is hollow and holds a thick concoction the Flame's artificers call the Fire of Gashang, of naphtha and quicklime. When the maul is swung, the fluid leaks through small holes at the base of the blades and can be set alight. A struck foe is sprayed with it and burns for several heartbeats, and cloth or leather on him may catch. Water does not put it out.
- **The spikes.** The spikes are shaped to tear, and every serious wound the maul gives bleeds freely.
- **The Flame's favor.** A devotee of the Flame who kills with it feels no pain for a short while after, though his wounds go on worsening.
- **The dread.** The maul carries the Blood Hills' dread with it. Those near its wielder feel the line about to break, the Forge's faithful most of all.
- **The relic.** It is plainly a weapon of the Black Flame. A faithful of the Forge who wounds a foe with it may freeze in horror, and the worst of them may collapse.

**The shrine.** The zealots built the shrine in a natural cave and set four defenses, each meant to pass a devotee of the Flame and stop anyone else.

- **The blood gate.** A stone door carved with the Flame's signs opens only to the blood of a willing devotee. The blood need not be fresh, and the bodies of Kaldin's soldiers at the gate carry plenty.
- **The fire pits.** Narrow, crumbling paths cross pits of smoldering coals. A misstep is a fall into the embers and grievous burns. The Flame's prayers on the walls name the path, for anyone who knows them.
- **The altar.** An altar that looks like a place of worship bursts into flaming gas when anyone who does not know the Flame's rites approaches, filling the chamber with fire. The right prayer disarms it.
- **The guardian.** In the last great chamber, wreathed in shadow and flickering light, stands a towering flame-wreathed being, a devil to the Aureldian faiths, bound by the zealots' priests to keep the maul. It strikes with terrible strength and fire and is not hurt by ordinary weapons. It lets pass anyone who has come through the Flame's tests and shows a devotee's rites. It is held by the binding's commands: invoked with the right phrases of the Flame, it falters, and regains itself if the party does not act quickly.

**Who wants it.** [[being-kaldinlnvl|Kaldin al Lénavel na Leravren]] wants it in the faith's keeping. [[being-kilanilvrl|Kilan]] wanted it to take the vale. The Knights of the Forge want it out of the Flame's hands. [[being-darisenlrn|Darisen al Olrun]], a Limor of [[affiliation-selmoros|Sélmoros]], wants it laid in a Sélmoran crypt so that the dead it made can be given their rites, and the field with them.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The maul is a weapon, not a curse. Its whispers come from the dread of the field it was carried out of, and the party may take them for the Flame's voice. Let the table decide what it heard.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The race.** Kaldin's soldiers reached the gate before the party, and some of them are dead on its threshold.
- **The promise.** The party promised a Forge knight, a Flame keeper or a scholar what it would do with the maul, and the promises disagree.
- **The relic hunter.** A party that came to the vale for the legend alone has reached the shrine with no promise to keep.

## The Opening {#opening}

The stone door stands in the back of the cave mouth, carved with the Flame's signs and taller than a man. Two of Kaldin's soldiers lie dead before it, one with his hand cut open on the carving. The door has not moved.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Blood Gate

**Purpose.** The first test, and the first choice about the Flame.

**Situation.** The carved door, the dead soldiers and the smell of old incense. A groove in the carving runs to a basin.

**NPCs and threats.** No living guard. The door.

**Player choices.** Shed a devotee's blood, use the dead soldiers' blood, force the door, or turn back.

**Clues and rewards.** The basin and the groove show what the door wants. A party that has read Kilan's papers or bargained with the old keeper knows the blood need not be fresh.

**Consequences.** Forcing the door takes hours and is heard by anything inside.

**If bypassed.** The party finds a crack that a small person can squeeze through and the rest cannot.

### Scene Two: The Pits and the Altar

**Purpose.** The traps that test the Flame's knowledge.

**Situation.** Past the gate, passages cross pits of smoldering coals on narrow crumbling paths, in heat that makes breathing hard. Beyond them a chamber holds an altar, ringed with prayers carved in the stone.

**NPCs and threats.** The heat, the crumbling paths, and the altar's fire.

**Player choices.** Read the prayers for the path, test every stone, rope the party together, or rush the altar chamber.

**Clues and rewards.** The prayers on the walls, read with Kilan's copies or Darisen's help, name the safe path across the pits and the words that quiet the altar. Carvings near the inner chamber describe the guardian and warn that only a true servant of the Flame survives its wrath.

**Consequences.** A party that triggers the altar is burned and alerts the guardian.

**If bypassed.** A survivor of Kaldin's band, burned and trapped beyond the pits, shows the path in exchange for being carried out.

### Scene Three: The Guardian

**Purpose.** The great danger of the shrine.

**Situation.** A large chamber wreathed in shadow, lit by flickering ember-light. The guardian stands in its center and turns toward the party.

**NPCs and threats.** The bound guardian, towering and wreathed in flame, which attacks the unworthy with strength and fire and is not harmed by ordinary weapons.

**Player choices.** Fight it, invoke the Flame's phrases to make it falter, pass as devotees, or retreat.

**Clues and rewards.** The guardian hesitates when the Flame's phrases are spoken or its signs shown, because it is bound to obey them. A party that uses them can hold it for a short while and must act before it recovers. Breaking its binding, or banishing it, opens the last chamber.

**Consequences.** A party that passed as devotees has the guardian at its back on the way out.

**If bypassed.** The party cannot reach the maul without passing the guardian.

### Scene Four: The Maul

**Purpose.** The choice.

**Situation.** The last chamber, wreathed in shadow and ember-light. The maul rests on a stone pedestal ringed with inscriptions of its power and history, beside an altar stained with old blood. It draws the eye.

**NPCs and threats.** The maul itself. Anyone who touches it without the Flame's rites is burned by the fire in its handle, which the inscriptions call the Flame's curse. Visions and whispers promise power to whoever takes up its purpose.

**Player choices.** Take it, destroy it, give it to the Forge's knights at the vigil, carry it to Darisen for a Sélmoran crypt, or leave it for the next seeker.

**Clues and rewards.** The inscriptions tell the maul's history from its forging to Hazarak's fall. The pedestal hides the zealots' offerings: old coin, the Ashlord's rings, and a list of the Flame cells sworn to recover the maul.

**Consequences.** See Aftermath.

**If bypassed.** The maul stays on its pedestal, and Kaldin sends more men.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Kaldin's next band passes the gate with a devotee's blood and the guardian with a devotee's rites. The maul goes to Leravren and into the Flame's keeping. The Forge's knights hear of it at the next vigil and carry the grievance to the Marshal and the Council.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

**If the party takes the maul,** it has a terrible weapon and the enmity of the Forge and the Flame together. The Flame's cells on the pedestal's list come for it, the Forge's knights want it taken from the Flame's hands, and the Marshal wants to know why a Black Flame relic is riding through his vale.

**If the party destroys it,** the Fire of Gashang in the handle burns hot and long, and the Flame's faithful in the vale swear revenge. Kaldin makes the party his enemy for life. The dread of the Blood Hills does not lift.

**If the party gives it to the Forge,** the knights of the vigil carry it to the Forge's cathedral-forge as a trophy, the Forge's standing in the province rises, and Kaldin and the Flame in the vale make the knights and the party their enemies. The quarrel between the faiths in the vale grows sharper.

**If the party gives it to Darisen,** he lays it in a Sélmoran crypt and the Limori begin the rites the dead of the Blood Hills have never had. The field grows quieter over the years. Neither the Forge nor the Flame forgives the party, and both blame the other for the maul's loss.

Whatever it chose, the party has changed the vale: who holds [[place-belsaren|Belsaren]] and [[place-ilovren|Ilovren]], what the [[being-jarvelplnvr|Marshal]] owes it or holds against it, and which faith has a grievance it will carry to court.
