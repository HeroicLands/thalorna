## Hands That Never Stop Turning

A serpent-cult died in the deep forest and its priests did not. At the [[place-drwndzgrt|Drowned Ziggurat]], mummified hands turn bronze prayer-wheels without rest, and the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]] that keeps them turning has begun to want more than hands. The party must cross a flooded temple, survive its rising water, and decide what to do with the wheels. Victory here is partial at best: the wheels can be stopped, but the thing they hold shut does not stay shut.

### Hook

Offer the entry that suits the hero.

- **The thief.** A drunken river-factor in a northern port sells a map to a temple roofed in green stone. The roof is root and moss, but the bronze inside is real, and so is the jade at the heart of it.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** A Khelâthi salt-factor, who lost two salvage crews to the Ziggurat, hires guards for a third. His pay is good and his reasons are thin.
- **The leader.** The chieftain of a river-tribe has watched the drowned plain's fish die and its water turn black. The tribe's eldest says the serpent-patron is troubled. The chieftain needs someone to go upriver who is not of the tribe, because no one of the tribe can break the taboo.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer has a fever-dream of a bronze wheel turning, and has dreamed it for a month. A river-guide who knows the way owes the wanderer a life and offers to pay.

The place note is [[place-drwndzgrt|The Drowned Ziggurat]].

## Scene One: The Flooded Stair

**Purpose.** Establish dread, the sound of the wheels, and the cost of carelessness. Introduce the guardian.

**Situation.** The causeway of roots ends at the lowest terrace, which is knee-deep in black water. The stair rises from it into the dark. The creak of the wheels is loud here, and regular enough to count.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Old Coil.** A river-python as long as a dugout, half blind and starved, coiled around the stair-foot in a nest of drowned roots. It is the last living remnant of the cult's patron, and the Shadow has not touched it, only left it hungry. It strikes at movement in the water and does not pursue far. It wants food more than blood: a cut of meat thrown to it draws it off.
- **The salt-factor's second crew,** if the hook uses them: three ragged survivors camped on the causeway, afraid to go on and afraid to leave. They know the first terrace and will sell what they know.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ kill the python from the causeway with spears. It takes a wound badly and thrashes the stair into rubble.
- _Stealth:_ cross at the stair's edge on the roots, keeping out of the water.
- _Guile:_ draw Old Coil off with bait cast on the far side.
- _Parley:_ a Kubindo keeper or any elder of the river can recognise the python as the patron's kin and offer the old river-offerings. It lets them pass and does not follow.

**Clues and rewards.** Serpent-coils carved into the stair-risers show the way: the coils lead up. A drowned offering-bowl in the nest holds a handful of river-gold dust.

**Consequences.** Killing Old Coil is permanent, and the river-tribes read it as an insult to the patron. Feeding it earns a passing mark of favour among those tribes who hear.

**If bypassed.** If the party comes by water at the rear of the Ziggurat, they climb a fallen fig-root to the third terrace and skip the stair. The python is still there on the way out.

## Scene Two: The Hall of Wheels

**Purpose.** Show the horror in full, and give the party a puzzle that asks them to understand the cult before they can pass.

**Situation.** The third terrace is a gallery of carved serpent-coils with the prayer-wheels set in rows along both walls. Each wheel is cranked by a mummified forearm that emerges from a niche in the wall. Behind each niche, in the dark, a dried body sits. The hands turn in unison, one revolution to each long breath. The gallery's far end is a bronze door carved with a serpent biting its tail, and the door has no handle.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The Wheel-Keepers.** [[being-damut|Damutu]] of the lesser priests, driven by a binding that gives each body one task. They turn the wheels. They do not leave their niches, and they do not respond to the living unless the rhythm breaks. A wheel stopped by force sends the nearest Keeper out of its niche in a lurching rush, its free hand clawing, while the other hand keeps turning.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ cut the arms from the wheels. Each severed hand frees a Keeper.
- _Stealth:_ walk the gallery in step with the hands, never breaking the rhythm. A stopped breath is the only danger.
- _Guile:_ the door answers to the pattern of the wheels. Counting the turns, a party member notices that every ninth turn the hands pause for one beat, and that the door's serpent-ring ticks open on the pause.
- _Parley:_ an elder who speaks the old river-prayers can answer the wheels' bark-scrolls. The prayers on them ask the patron to stay.

**Clues and rewards.** The prayers on the scrolls are the cult's last plea, and they reveal that the wheels were meant to hold a spirit in the world. A careful reader learns that the Keepers' hands have not turned the same prayer for some time. The Shadow rewrote it.

**Consequences.** Breaking the rhythm brings Keepers in numbers. Stopping a single wheel alerts the sanctum above.

**If bypassed.** If the party flees the gallery, the door opens on its own as the wheels race, and the party is driven up the stair to Scene Three.

## Scene Three: The Rising Water

**Purpose.** A setback: the Ziggurat turns on the party, and a rival complicates the climb.

**Situation.** The stair above the gallery narrows into a shaft. As the party climbs, the sluices below open. The river pours into the lower terraces, and the water rises up the shaft behind them. The only way is up. A band is already in the shaft.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **Black-Oar.** A river-ferryman and tomb-robber who came in by the back channel with four crewmen and a crowbar. He is lean, scarred and practical, and he offers a plain bargain: share the sanctum's treasure or drown in the shaft together. He has a rope.
- **The rising water,** which fills the shaft in a short time. Anything heavy slows the climb.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ kill Black-Oar's crew for the rope and the shaft. They fight dirty.
- _Stealth:_ slip past the crew in the dark, or take the rope while they argue.
- _Guile:_ trade the rope for a promise of shares, then renegotiate at the top.
- _Parley:_ unite with Black-Oar. He knows the Ziggurat's back channel, which is a way out.

**Clues and rewards.** Black-Oar knows a way out through the river-door at the shaft's foot. He also knows that the sanctum holds a great wheel, and that his first crew died in front of it.

**Consequences.** If Black-Oar lives and is betrayed, he waits at the back channel with a knife. If he lives and is trusted, he is a useful man in the forest and a dangerous one in the north.

**If bypassed.** A party that avoids the shaft by an outer fig-root climb reaches the sanctum with no rival and no water, but also with no rope and no back channel.

## Scene Four: The Serpent's Bride

**Purpose.** The climax: the cult's last high priestess, and the great wheel.

**Situation.** The sanctum is a dry chamber under a vault of living root. The great wheel stands at its centre on a spindle as thick as a tree, turned by a single mummified hand the colour of old leather. On a throne of coiled bronze before it sits a woman in the green and gold robes of the cult. She is not mummified.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The Serpent's Bride.** A [[being-tereb|tereb]], the cult's last high priestess, who persists past death by deliberate ritual. She wears her former self like a garment, and the Shadow behind her looks out of her eyes. She is courteous, patient and persuasive. She wants the wheel turned until the patron wakes, and she believes it will wake as it was, whole and sane. It will not. Her tactics are to speak first, offer a place beside her, and strike only when refused. She drives the Keepers with a gesture. She cannot leave the sanctum, because her binding is anchored to the wheel.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ fight her and the Keepers. The Bride is hard to harm while she sits at the wheel.
- _Stealth:_ reach the wheel's spindle from behind the throne and jam it with a bronze wedge or a spear.
- _Guile:_ lie to her. Tell her the patron is awake in the river. Her attention turns to the water long enough to act.
- _Parley:_ listen. She offers truth in exchange for the party's promise to turn the wheel for her. The truth is real.

**Clues and rewards.** Stopping the great wheel ends the binding on the Keepers and the Bride together, and the sanctum begins to settle. The Bride's throne bears a jade serpent-signet.

**Consequences.** Stopping the wheel also stops whatever the wheels were holding shut. See Scene Five.

**If bypassed.** If the party flees the Ziggurat without confronting her, the wheels speed. The next season, the river-tribes find the drowned plain full of the Quiet Dead.

## Scene Five: What the Wheels Held

**Purpose.** Reward, revelation and a twist.

**Situation.** Behind the throne a shallow alcove holds the cult's treasure: a handful of river-gold, bronze ceremonial tools, three bark-books of prayers, and the jade serpent-signet. The floor of the alcove is carved with a map in relief: the river, the Ziggurat, and a circle.

**NPCs and threats.** A last Keeper sits in the alcove, mummified and still, with its hand on a small wheel. If the great wheel has been stopped, this one turns.

**Player choices.**

- _Force:_ take everything and run.
- _Stealth:_ copy the map and leave the treasure.
- _Guile:_ leave a false map for any who come after.
- _Parley:_ carry the bark-books to a river-tribe elder who may be able to read the cult's last words.

**Clues and rewards.** The treasure is modest: river-gold dust, bronze tools, and the jade signet, which a river-tribe will buy or kill for. The map shows the circle of [[place-slntclrng|the Silent Clearing]]. The prayers on the last bark-book say the cult's wheels were also meant to hold the Clearing shut.

**Consequences.** The twist: the Ziggurat's cult did not only seek to keep a patron alive. Its priests found the Clearing's wound and tried to bind it with prayer. They failed in the way the Shadow makes everything fail. Stopping the wheels frees the wound's pull.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves without reading the alcove learns of the Clearing from a river-tribe elder a season later.

## Aftermath

- **If the wheels are stopped,** the Keepers collapse, the Bride is gone, and the river-tribes reopen the drowned plain within a season. The Quiet Dead walk farther from the Clearing than they did.
- **If the wheels still turn,** the Bride's plan goes on, and the river-water darkens another stretch downstream.
- **If the party robs the Ziggurat and leaves,** salvage crews follow within a year, and the Bride's offer reaches another ear.
- **If Old Coil is dead,** the river-tribes remember who killed the patron's kin.
- **If the signet is sold in the north,** a Khelâthi scholar asks where it came from.
