Every twelve years a quarter of a million people stand on [[place-melkaground|the Melka Ground]], where [[place-mahanodi|the Mahanodi]] takes its greatest tributary. The first thirty days of the [Mahva-Melka]{.name data-type="lore/gathering"} are the religious observances, and the last ten are the business of the [[affiliation-janpdsvdyr|confederation]]. The next Mahva-Melka is eight years off. It is the last at which [Mahaprasoda of the Sarveda]{.name data-type="being/npc"} convenes, and the business of those ten days is already being done in letters, at temple doors and in copyists' rooms. This frame runs through the years before the gathering and ends on the ground.

## The Situation and the Stakes

**The succession.** The senior priest of [[place-sangema|Sangema]] is **Sabhapavi of the Melka**. He convenes the assembly, puts its questions and declares what it has agreed, and he holds no authority at all between assemblies. Mahaprasoda is about seventy and the most respected priestly authority in inland Vedyara. His successor is being negotiated now, and the negotiators are the other two members of [The Council of Three]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"}: the senior priests of [[place-surtatempl|the Surta temple]] and of [[place-bhrnytemple|the Mahajaya Temple at Bharanya]]. A joint word of the three carries near-universal weight. It works as an executive committee, and the priests deny that it is one. The next Sabhapavi decides whether they go on denying it.

**The dissolution.** [Shemapal Rajapali]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a Rajapuri scholar living under [[affiliation-chendrapur|Chendrapur]]'s patronage, writes to the kingdoms urging further dissolutions on the model of [[affiliation-rajeprjnpd|the Janpoda of the King's-Town]], which replaced a kingdom with a janpoda. The kingdoms call him a dangerous radical and have asked Chendrapur to expel him, and Chendrapur has declined. The Melka recognizes newly formed janpodas, so a dissolution ends on the ground, in the roll at Sangema.

**The gold outside the Compact.** [[affiliation-suvrgrjnpd|The Janpoda of the Gold Mountain]] never signed into [[affiliation-assmblycmpct|the Assembly of the Compact]], and its gold reaches the coast anyway. The Assembly wants a sixth seat and the gold behind it. [The Twenty-Eight]{.name data-type="affiliation"} panning-families want their cap kept. The janpoda's convening priest, Podmashri Kaneka-Hasta, is expected to be a major voice at the gathering.

**The day.** The Surta temple's sighting of the year fixes the Melka's date. [[affiliation-ganokashalda|The Reckoners' College]] computes the year instead, and the two methods have differed by a day for as long as anyone has checked. A day moves the opening of the thirty days, and a court states which method it follows.

**The roll.** The roll at Sangema is the confederation's standing argument. Which circuit a border janpoda belongs to has gone to litigation for generations. The descendants of [Tirnugram]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"}, burned in its own hall by the conch-door adventurer and mourned at the Melka of 320, have never accepted the division of its land.

The stakes are who speaks for the confederation, whether a kingdom can become a janpoda again, and where the subcontinent's gold is counted.

## Who Moves It

[Mahaprasoda of the Sarveda]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Sabhapavi of the Melka
: He convenes his last assembly and has named no successor. The office has no means of naming one.

The Council of Three
: The senior priests of Sangema, the Surta temple and Bharanya. They deny that they are an executive, and they are choosing the next head of the one body they deny governing.

[Podmashri Kaneka-Hasta]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, convening priest of Suvargeri
: She has to be seen to want both the sixth seat and the cap, and to bring her janpoda's gold to the ground on terms the Twenty-Eight accept.

[[being-jayarshapadm|Jayarsha Padmadiksha]], head of the Padmadiksha
: An old head of one of the Twenty-Eight. He means to name his daughter to the family's seat while he lives to defend it, and no woman has sat at that table.

[Shemapal Rajapali]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, scholar of the dissolution
: His letters lie on the correspondence shelf at [[place-rajavalilib|the Rajavali Library]], in [[place-shitakoshta|the Cold Chamber]], the driest room it has. Several kingdoms read them, and he is well received in some janpodas.

[[being-kalyanikakal|Kalyanika Kalira]] and [[being-madurvstrpl|Madurava Sutrapal]], heralds of Chendrapur
: Every claim of lineage brought to the ground, from a kingdom's heir to a dissolved crown's last line, rests on rolls these two read in opposite ways. Kalyanika's index finds an entry in an hour, and Madurava holds that a lineage read without the set form cannot be trusted.

The bodies
: The four river-circuits and their Circuit-Speakers, chosen for the gathering; each janpoda's **Dutsa**; the copyists of [[place-dirmapur|Dirmapur]]; the Ganoka-shalda; the kingdoms and the two cities, which hold their seats in their own right; and the bond-holders, who come to the assembly to be paid.

## Ways In

1. **The candidate.** A janpoda's Dutsa asks the party to learn, before the thirty days begin, which candidate the priests of the Surta temple and Bharanya favor. The Dutsa's warning to a foreigner applies to the party: the priests who settle the water at the end are the same priests who watched them at the bathing place at the beginning.
2. **The escort.** The Sarveda circuit's sabhra means its Circuit-Speaker to reach the ground for the first of the thirty days, and hires the party to bring him there through a river country full of interested parties.
3. **The roll.** Tirnugram's descendants are preparing a petition asking that Tirnugram be recognized as newly formed. It fails without proof of the old boundaries, and every copy burned in the hall. The party searches the roll at Sangema and the copyists' rooms at Dirmapur.
4. **The gold.** Suvargeri's last moon reads light against the Ganoka-shalda's constants, and Podmashri wants an examiner brought up from Chendrapur before the figure is read aloud at the rail. Whatever the examiner finds is argued at the Melka.

## If the Party Does Nothing

- The Council of Three agrees a successor privately and lets the ground acclaim him in the last ten days. Every janpoda that resents the arrangement has learned that the Council chooses, and says so at the next gathering.
- Shemapal's letters find one kingdom willing to listen. Its council does not dissolve, but it asks the Melka what recognition would require, and every other kingdom treats the question as a declaration.
- The sixth seat is refused again. Suvargeri's gold goes on reaching the coast through buyers at the edge of the territory, and the Assembly blames the Twenty-Eight.
- Tirnugram's petition is heard without its survey and fails, and the descendants go home to say the names of their families at the bricked gate.

## The Secret

Mahaprasoda is failing. His household at Sangema keeps it from the pilgrims, and the two other senior priests of the Council of Three know. He may not live to the gathering. If he dies before the thirty days open, nobody has the authority to convene it: the office acts only at a Melka, and only the Sabhapavi convenes one. The Council of Three would then have to name a convening priest itself, openly, and so be what it has always denied being.

The two priests have not agreed on a successor, and each is preparing for that day. The Surta temple's priest holds that a confederation convened by the temple that sights the year needs no further authority. Bharanya's holds that the Mahajaya temple feeds the south's pilgrims and the south will not come to a gathering convened from the north. Every party that wants something from the next Melka wants it settled before Mahaprasoda dies, and none of them has been told how little time they may have.
