### Summary of Provènzia

#### Geography

Provènzia is a region of diverse landscapes and climates, ranging from temperate plains to rugged mountains rich in minerals. Key geographical features include the Áelendan ranges, known for their deposits of precious metals and gemstones, and the fertile river valleys that are essential for agriculture and trade. The region also contains coastal settlements and riverine trade hubs that connect it to neighboring realms.

The kingdom’s strategic position as a crossroads of trade and its mineral wealth make it both an economic hub and a target for external aggression. The Áelendan ranges host hidden mining communities, while urban centers like [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnazdàl]] serve as the royal and commercial capital and [[place-aureliane|Aurèliane]] as the seat of law and learning.

#### Cultural and Political Dynamics

Provènzia's cultural landscape is marked by tension between the **Thânevar**, the old people of the Provenzal country, who emphasize traditional local governance and remember their petty kings, and the **Provenzians**, the city-dwelling heirs of the old Vylarian province. These groups clash over land, governance, and cultural identity. A centralized monarchy governs from the cities, but local traditions persist in rural areas, fueling resentment and periodic rebellions.

The kingdom's religious tolerance is another area of cultural complexity. While the Church of the Sacred Forge is officially favored and politically powerful, other faiths, including Brâzêmâs and Black Flame traditions, coexist uneasily. This pluralism stems from policies established by the first king, Ylârven Lédravren, to unify the estates that chose him. However, the growing dominance of the Forge faithful has caused friction, particularly in regions where traditional or minority religions hold sway.

#### Major Personalities

- **King Krâlen alrí Lédravren**: The reigning monarch of Provènzia, King Krâlen strives to maintain unity in a kingdom marked by cultural and political divisions. Known for his pragmatic leadership, he faces challenges from both ambitious nobles and external threats from Tarvénia.

- **Princess of [[place-gwefavel|Gwéfavel]]**: A powerful magnate whose clan's influence was diminished under the previous king. Resentful of past failures, she focuses on restoring her family’s prestige, leveraging her stronghold in Gwéfavel to exert significant regional influence.

- **Earl of [[place-degavel|Dégavel]] (Rútal alrí Pélanvri)**: A prominent and influential noble, the Earl of Dégavel is embroiled in political rivalries, particularly with the Count of [[place-athavel|Áthavel]]. His leadership in the southern regions of Provènzia gives him significant sway in both court politics and military strategy.

- **Jârvel al Pélanvri**: Brother to the Earl of Dégavel and a key military leader, Jârvel serves as the Élgâr of [[place-caerceravel|Céravel Castle]] and **Marshal of the Western Army** of Provènzia. Tasked with defending the kingdom’s easternmost frontier, his command is critical in managing both banditry and incursions from Tarvénia.

- **Count of Áthavel**: A politically embattled noble facing internal family disputes. His clashes with the Earl of Dégavel reflect broader struggles among the magnates, which weaken the kingdom’s cohesion during times of external threat.

- **Álegar alrí Zûravel**: Baron of [[place-zuravlenrgn|Zûravlen]] and [[place-kyvarel|Kývarel]], Álegar is a prominent supporter of the Order of the Burning Brand. His clan’s alignment with this religious faction places him at the center of significant political and spiritual influence.

- **Avaârl alrí Zylávren**: Earl of Hárivren and the most senior Black Flame noble in Provènzia, Avaârl commands great respect and power. As Álegar’s overlord, he exemplifies the integration of nobility with the religious hierarchy, further complicating Provènzia’s cultural and political landscape.

#### Historical Primer

Provènzia’s history is marked by cycles of conquest, rebellion, and consolidation. A Vylarian colony was planted at [[place-aureliane|Aurèliane]] under the Republic ([[lore-aurelianefound|The Founding of Aurèliane]]), and the legions made the country a province by breaking the Thânevar kings at [[lore-tiravlenbattle|Tiravlen]] in about 152 AF. The kingdom’s foundation lies in the province’s refusal of its governor, a little over a century before the present year of 720 AF, after a decade of Nordland raids (519–530 AF) had shown how little the empire’s governors could defend. The war was fought for a Charter of Rights and not for a crown, and the estates chose a king to hold the charter ([[lore-charterwar|the War of the Charter]]). The first kings pursued pragmatic policies of religious tolerance and power-sharing between the great houses and local leaders, which created a fragile unity, repeatedly tested over the centuries.

One of the most significant events in Provènzia’s history since then was the **War of the Princes (653–655 AF)**, also known as the **Céravian Rebellion** ([[lore-warprinces|The War of the Princes]]). This civil war erupted over succession disputes following the death of King Pâdran Lédravren, who left no clear heir. Competing claims from rival factions of the royal family and their noble allies plunged the kingdom into chaos. The Provenzian factions, which emphasized centralized rule and military strength, clashed with Thânevar factions advocating for a return to regional autonomy.

The war devastated large swathes of the kingdom. Regional economies collapsed, and the vale of Céravel became the battleground for opposing forces. The conflict ended with the accession of King Dâlenar Lédravren, a compromise candidate supported by both moderate Provenzian and Thânevar factions. However, the scars of the war linger, with noble families still nursing grudges and local governance weakened in many regions.

The _War of the Princes_ also highlighted the deep cultural divide between the Thânevar and Provenzian populations. The Thânevar resistance to Provenzian dominance crystallized during the Céravian Rebellion, as traditional leaders leveraged popular discontent to rally their followers. This divide remains a source of tension in modern Provènzia, particularly in rural regions where memories of the war are still vivid.

Provènzia’s subsequent history has been shaped by efforts to recover from the war’s devastation. While successive monarchs have worked to strengthen royal authority, the balance of power between the crown, nobles, and local leaders remains precarious. This legacy of division continues to influence both internal politics and the kingdom’s capacity to respond to external threats.

#### Major Current Conflicts

Provènzia faces a multi-layered crisis of internal and external conflicts, each feeding into the other and threatening the kingdom’s fragile stability. Internally, the noble families vie for influence, with long-standing rivalries between magnates like the Earl of Dégavel and the Count of Áthavel creating political gridlock. Local unrest in the towns, fueled by factional disputes and economic instability, has further eroded the monarchy's control. The cultural tension between the Thânevar and Provenzian populations continues to simmer, with rural Thânevari resisting central authority and perceived Provenzian dominance, occasionally erupting into open rebellion. Religious divides compound these issues, as the Church of the Sacred Forge’s growing power alienates other faiths, particularly in regions with strong Black Flame or Táranite traditions.

Externally, Provènzia remains under constant threat from Tarvénia, whose rulers exploit Provènzia’s internal weaknesses to press territorial claims. Border skirmishes are frequent, particularly in contested regions like the southeast, where Tarvénia forces test Provènzia’s defenses. The kingdom also faces the resurgence of banditry and piracy, disrupting trade and straining regional lords’ ability to maintain order. These external pressures exacerbate internal divisions, as magnates and military leaders, distracted by personal rivalries, struggle to present a united front against external aggression. The interplay of these conflicts creates a precarious balance that threatens to tip the kingdom into chaos.

#### Céravel, Tarvénia, and Léravren

[[place-ceravelvale|Céravel]] serves as a critical frontier region for Provènzia, encompassing several manorial estates and villages anchored by its castle town. Céravel is commanded by Jârvel al Pélanvri, who manages defenses against Tarvénia. The area is also troubled by banditry, which local lords, such as **Târlas al Pélanvri**, work to suppress. Its position near the southeastern border makes it a frequent flashpoint for conflict, with local lords balancing the demands of defense, governance, and trade.

[[place-leravren|Léravren]] lies to the northeast of Céravel and serves as a gateway to the mountains and forests vital for resources and trade. The region is also closely associated with the Black Flame Order, particularly through the Zûravel clan. **Kâldin al Lénavel**, lord of Léravren, is a key player in the unfolding political and religious conflicts. His connections to the Zûravel clan and the Black Flame faith deepen the complexity of both local governance and regional intrigue.

**Tarvénia** remains the greatest external threat to Provènzia, persistently challenging its southeastern borders. A larger and more unified neighbor, Tarvénia exploits Provènzia’s internal divisions and vulnerabilities. Its skirmishes along the contested territories exacerbate tensions, draining resources and testing the kingdom’s fragile unity.
