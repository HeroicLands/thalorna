## Overview {#overview}

The embassy of the [[affiliation-kingdmdnvr|Kingdom of Dúnavarre]] is crossing [[place-jnanadvar|the Scholars' Door]] on its way to ask the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Empire of Tantsou]] for direct relations. [[affiliation-hvarnguides|The Hvarn]] guide it over the pass, under the Reach-guide [[being-vrndzlbrn|Vurn Dzelbern]], and go no farther than the way-station of [[place-kwisen|Kwisen]]. The Tantsou frontier officers send an escort under [[being-kwisenescrt|Third Son of the Gate Warden of the Clan of Red Gate in Autumn]] to meet a party at the col and not a step sooner, and weather has shut the col with the embassy still above the place where the escort waits.

The party serves the embassy, as in [[scenario-embsydnvrtantsou|Embassy from Dúnavarre to Tantsou]]. It has to bring the embassy down to the escort, or wait for a thaw that the station does not hurry, and then it has to stand before the [[lore-tanchei|Tanchei]] who receives foreigners at Kwisen. [[being-kwisenregdtr|Second Daughter of the Registrar of the Clan of Moon Rises Over Still Water]] opens a fresh page in the station's register for every newcomer. Nobody in the embassy has a caste to answer her with, and she wants an account of the party that her register can hold before she lets it go down the valley.

This is the first of the three adventures in the campaign frame [[scenario-doornvrexst|A Door That Has Never Existed]]. The page the registrar's daughter writes is the first page the empire holds on the embassy, and the halls below read it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The weather.** It is weather. The frame leaves unproven whether anyone's sorcery is behind the drought in the south, and this adventure adds nothing to that. The Game Master sets how long the col stays shut and what the way down costs.

**The escort.** The frontier officers hold that the Vedyari shrenis are a tributary trade of no administrative consequence, and they keep an exact register of every Vedyari caravan all the same. Their orders are to meet a party at the col and not a step sooner. The captain keeps the order for an embassy exactly as he keeps it for a caravan, and no rank in the embassy changes it.

**The Hvarn.** The Hvarn who guide travelers over the pass go no farther than the station, where they are fed at a table of their own. A Hvarn Storm-Reader's refusal ends a day's travel, and a Reach-guide answers to the Hvarn council for whoever does not come back. Vurn knows the roll of his crossing, and the Hvarn will not say how many travelers turned back at the col this season. The Game Master decides why: the setting gives only that they will not.

**The register.** The station's register has been kept without a gap for four hundred years, and the party is never shown it. The registrar's daughter is courteous, exact and entirely without deference to a foreigner, and she does not hurry. She wants every traveler who comes down the pass on a page the register can hold, and a traveler the register cannot hold is a problem she cannot set aside. She holds the party at the station, fed and housed, until the page holds. Under [[lore-castelaws|the Caste Laws]] a foreigner is unclassified until a bureau says otherwise, and an ambassador's highest classification is the Emperor's own decree. The setting does not say whether she enters the embassy as one body awaiting the decree or classifies its members one at a time. The Game Master decides.

**The translator.** [[being-loristaravel|Loris Taravel]] is the only person at the table who reads both tongues, and he puts the embassy's answers into Tantsou for her. As the frame's secret has it, he is an agent of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vilérian Empire]] who wants the embassy dependent on Viléria and a year's wait at Zhowen is a long argument for that. Whether he words the account for the embassy or for Viléria is the Game Master's call, and the page carries his wording down the valley whichever he chooses.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The scene the party must reach is the registrar's daughter with an account she can write. Without it the embassy does not go down the valley, and an account she writes alone is written from what the soldiers, the Hvarn and the embassy's own words gave her.

> [!WARNING]
> Nothing at Kwisen shows the party what the page says. She writes it where it cannot be seen, and the party learns its wording only from what it has told her.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

- **The col and the thaw.** How long the weather holds the col, what waiting costs in supplies and patience, and what the descent to the escort costs. The Hvarn keep an office for recovering the dead from the gorges, and a crossing that goes wrong is a body they find.
- **The account.** The wording of what the register holds about the embassy, and whether the registrar's daughter enters the embassy as one body or its members one by one. The setting fixes that a bureau's classification is narrow and lasts the length of the business, and that the Emperor's decree alone classifies an ambassador.
- **The figure.** What the clerks say the party's coin is worth. The setting fixes only that the figure is named and not argued.
- **The count.** How many travelers turned back at the col this season, if the party has seen any, and whether Vurn learns who gave the count.
- **Loris's wording.** Whether he words the account faithfully, under What Is Really Happening.
- **Aran's patience.** [[being-arancelavren|Aran Kélavren]] wants a conspicuous success. Whether the first week in Tantsou, spent at a station on a stranger's bench, tests that is the Game Master's to play.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The courtier: the way down.** [[being-toradhtaravren|Dame Torad Taravren]] asks the party to bring the embassy to the place where the escort waits, with Vurn's leave, and to keep the ambassador from waiting on a thaw.
- **The scholar: the account.** The ambassador asks the party to help word an answer for the station's register, and to read a second copy of what Loris puts into Tantsou before it goes to the registrar's daughter.
- **The woodsman: the Hvarn table.** The registrar's daughter wants a party member who walked the mountain road with Vurn to tell her how many travelers turned back at the col this season, since the Hvarn will not.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> Cloud lies on the col, and Vurn, who has walked in front of the embassy for a month and a half, has stopped with his hand on the stone. He does not say the road is shut. He says that the Storm-Reader has spoken, and that the Hvarn go no farther than the station and no farther today than the weather allows.
>
> Below the cloud there is a post where an officer in lacquered scale and his soldiers meet a party at the col and not a step sooner. They have not climbed to meet you, and they do not.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Shut Col

**Purpose.** Meet the Tantsou frontier's first rule, that nothing comes up to meet a traveler.

**Situation.** The embassy's train stands above the place where the escort waits, with cloud on the col. Vurn has declined the day's travel. Dame Torad counts the train and the hours of light, and [[being-arancelavren|Aran Kélavren]] stands apart, wanting a conspicuous success and not a wait.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-vrndzlbrn|Vurn Dzelbern]], the [[affiliation-hvarnguides|Hvarn]] Reach-guide, who is quiet, patient and exact, checks every rope twice and speaks for the weather and the order of his crossing; Torad, who wants the embassy down; Aran.

**Player choices.** Wait for a thaw, ask Vurn to take the embassy down to the escort's post in the weather, or send a party member ahead to find the post. Work out what Vurn will and will not do.

**Clues and rewards.** Vurn says the escort meets a party at the col and not a step sooner, and that the Hvarn go no farther than the station. No party has been lost on his rope. A thaw does not come for being asked, and the station does not hurry it.

**Consequences.** Waiting costs what the Game Master sets. A descent in weather costs what the Game Master sets, and a Reach-guide's refusal is a day's travel ended. A party that goes without Vurn's leave goes without him.

**If bypassed.** The weather lifts at its own pace, the escort keeps its post, and the embassy comes down late with Vurn in front.

> [!TIP]
> The escort never comes up. Play every offer of coin or rank to bring the escort higher as an insult delivered in forms, with no refusal anyone says aloud.

### Scene Two: Where the Escort Starts

**Purpose.** Come under the empire's escort and learn what it lets a party say.

**Situation.** At the place where the escort waits, the captain and his soldiers in lacquered scale meet the party exactly there. They walk ahead of it, in silence, down a road of fitted stone, for two days.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-kwisenescrt|Third Son of the Gate Warden of the Clan of Red Gate in Autumn]], who speaks the forms and nothing more, and his soldiers; Torad, who walks beside the embassy and watches the road; Vurn, who comes down with the party as far as the station.

**Player choices.** Question the captain or his soldiers, keep silence with them, or use the road to prepare the account the registrar's daughter will ask for.

**Clues and rewards.** The escort keeps the forms for an embassy as for a caravan. A question put to the captain or a soldier is answered with a form, or with nothing. A party that uses the two days to settle who it serves, who sent it and what it comes to ask arrives at the station with an answer already in order.

**Consequences.** A party that arrives with an answer in order has something to put on the page. A party that arrives without one has an unfinished account, and the registrar's daughter finishes it.

**If bypassed.** The captain delivers the party to the station on the third evening without a word.

### Scene Three: The Fresh Page

**Purpose.** Meet the registrar's daughter, and learn what the register can hold.

**Situation.** The station rises out of the pines on the third evening: a walled court of gray brick, green-glazed tiles, red-lacquered gates, a bronze bell under the eaves. At the gate porters set the bales in chalked squares and step back, and the captain's soldiers check the wooden tablet on a cord at every belt. The Hvarn are led to a table of their own. The registrar's daughter comes to the edge of the veranda, in a scholar's blue robe with her hair pinned by a single plain comb, and bows precisely and not deeply.

**NPCs and threats.** The registrar's daughter, who gives her description and her caste and waits; Aran; Loris, who puts the embassy's words into Tantsou; Torad; the captain, who hands the party over at the gate and goes.

**Player choices.** Answer for the embassy as a body or for each member, name the Synod and the Kingdom, put forward Aran as the one whose rank the register must enter first, or let Loris answer. A direct refusal to answer is the mistake to avoid, because it embarrasses both parties.

**Clues and rewards.** When it is plain the party has no caste to answer with, she inclines her head, seats the party at the lowest end of a long bench, sets millet and pale tea before it, and opens a fresh page in a bound register the party is not allowed to see. She wants an account of who the party serves, who sent it and what it comes for, in forms the register can hold. A party carrying foreign coin is told by her what it is worth in the empire, and the figure is not negotiable.

**Consequences.** The page opens whatever the party says. What it holds depends on the party's account. An account the register cannot hold keeps the page open, and she does not send the party down the valley.

**If bypassed.** She writes the account alone from what the captain, Vurn and the embassy's own words gave her.

> [!TIP]
> Play her as courteous and without deference. She never raises her voice, and she never says the party is unclassified. She asks the next question.

### Scene Four: The Hvarn Table

**Purpose.** Give her what the Hvarn will not, and see what the register does with it.

**Situation.** The next day at the station. The Hvarn eat at their own table, fed and apart, Vurn among them. The registrar's daughter has learned that a party shared the Hvarn's road, and she wants it to tell her how many travelers turned back at the col this season.

**NPCs and threats.** The registrar's daughter; Vurn, who knows the roll and will not give the count; Loris, if the party speaks Tantsou through him.

**Player choices.** Give her the count the party saw, ask Vurn for his, decline the way a Tantsou declines, without saying no, or ask what the register does with it.

**Clues and rewards.** Vurn will not say. A party that gives her the count has given the register something it can hold, and she writes it beside the party's page. She does not say what the count is for.

**Consequences.** A count given is a count the Hvarn did not give, and whether they learn who gave it is the Game Master's call. A count withheld leaves her with Vurn's silence and the party's, and she writes the silence.

**If bypassed.** She goes to the Hvarn table herself and returns with Vurn's courtesy and no count.

### Scene Five: The Eastern Gate

**Purpose.** Leave on the account the register holds.

**Situation.** The weather lifts and the page is held. The registrar's daughter walks the classified party to the eastern gate and points down the valley with her closed fan. Below the station the road runs down into [[place-mwonouplnd|the Mwonou Uplands]], the terrace country of the Prince of Mwonou, where the southern famine has its heart.

**NPCs and threats.** The registrar's daughter; Aran, who goes down on whatever the page says; Loris; Torad.

**Player choices.** Ask what the page says, ask for a copy, ask what the tablets will carry, or go down in silence.

**Clues and rewards.** She does not say what the page says, and nobody at the station shows it. She tells the party: "Everything below us is written down. Every terrace has a lineage, every lineage has a page, and every page has a seal." A party that has kept its account in order learns at the gate that the page is the one the valley reads.

**Consequences.** The embassy goes down the valley carrying the account, and every hall that reads it reads the register's wording and not the embassy's. A party the register cannot hold does not reach the gate: she keeps it, fed and housed, and the page stays open.

**If bypassed.** The embassy goes down the valley on the account she writes alone.

## Endings {#endings}

- **Held.** The party gave an account in forms the register can hold, and she writes the page, walks the classified party to the eastern gate and sends it down the valley. The embassy enters the empire on a page that says what it said. [[scenario-flngnwrgn|A Filing in the New Reign]] is the next leg.
- **Held on her wording.** The account she writes is hers, assembled from the captain, Vurn and Loris's words. The embassy goes down, and the page the halls read says what she heard.
- **Kept.** The register cannot hold the party's account. She does not send it anywhere and keeps it at the station while the page stays open. Every day the embassy stays is a day it is not at Zhowen.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The station houses the embassy as long as the weather requires. The weather lifts at its own pace, the escort walks the embassy down, and the registrar's daughter opens a fresh page and writes it alone from what the captain, Vurn and the embassy's own words gave her. Loris puts the embassy's answers into Tantsou, and the page carries his wording. The embassy goes down the valley on that account, and nobody asks Vurn how many turned back at the col.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

Whatever the ending, carry three things forward: what the page says of the embassy and in whose words, whether the Hvarn's count was given, and what Aran owes the party for the days spent on the bench. The road down the valley comes to [[place-zhowen|Zhowen]], where the chancellery reads what Kwisen wrote.
