Every year the priests of [[affiliation-uqaa|Uqa'a]] read the flood at [[place-themeasure|The Measure]] on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], and at the crest they proclaim the number to the city and the empire. The levy follows the number, and so does the year of every selat. The Measure has read short at two Inundations, and the river is coming to its crest low again. This frame runs through the year of that low river. The throne, the bureaus, the priesthood and the houses of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] each have a number they want proclaimed, a render they want forgiven or a successor they want named, and the grain of [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Bethua]] and the horsemen of [[affiliation-okharis|Okharis]] are courted by all of them at once.

## The Situation and the Stakes {#the-situation}

**The river.** The flood has come low two years running, and the state granaries, the stores kept for exactly this, are drawn down. A full Measure means plenty and a heavy levy, a low one means dearth, and a failed one means famine. An honest low reading cuts every selat's render.

**The fiction.** The Khelathi hold that the river answers to the Gar-Au. Nobody believes [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]] is a god, everyone knows nobody believes it, and the fiction is kept because the river has kept its side of it. A failing flood strains the fiction's legitimacy, not anyone's belief. The empire's schoolrooms teach [[lore-akhstruckhs|the Struck House]] and [[lore-failfloods|the Failing Floods]] as the last time a throne and a short river met, when a Gar-Au had the Measure read by other hands to hide a failing flood, and the bureaus cite them in arguments about the present.

**The renders.** The court's trouble is with what comes in beyond that cut. A Halzi'a reports a smaller crop than the Measure explains, sends the render late, sends coin where grain was due, or lets a barge sit at his own toll. None has refused the throne in words. At [[place-anlaghzetun|Anlagh-Zetun]] the Overseer of the Granaries reckons the render of [[affiliation-upperrivrslt|The Upper River Selatu]], and his rolls show which Halzi'a hold back and by how much. The gap between what the flood should have rendered and what reaches the treasuries is the figure the central bureaus guard most closely.

**The succession.** Meqes XVI is 46 and in her twelfth year. Her nephew [[being-anlaghur|Anlaghur]], an Iru'palu at Anlagh-Zetun, stands near enough the succession that the court asks the question seriously, and [[being-gerragezeelan|Gerra Geze'el'Anlaghu]] presses the descent of [[affiliation-gezeelanlaghu|Geze'el'Anlaghu]], the house of three old Gar-Auu. Factions of the central bureaus and of the priesthood back different answers, and a failing flood turns a quarrel about the future into a quarrel about whether the present throne can hold. The mediators of the [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]] trade in what each faction offers, and their influence grows with every unresolved week.

**The shore.** Bethua draws its grain from aqueducts, qanats and cisterns, not from the river, and the surplus of its irrigated strips is the only dependable harvest on the northern shore. The [[affiliation-councilhouses|Council of Houses]] presses the Metrissa to sell it to the empire, and the [[affiliation-sacredcollege|Sacred College]] presses her to keep it in the cisterns. [[being-amithenakrvn|Amithena of House Kervanion]] has not chosen, and what Bethua decides sets the price of bread in the delta ports.

**The horsemen.** Okharis owes the empire nothing and trades with it freely, so the Khelathi houses ask it for horsemen, not tribute: riders for a general who needs a force he trusts, escorts for a temple that distrusts its garrison, an ally against a Halzi'a who holds back his render. The embassies wait at the temple-cities, and their gifts go on past the cities to the chieftains, where the terms are better than the cities' own. The Oracle has given no answer to the King's question this year, and [[being-takorozanethar|Tákoro Zanethar]] cannot act while she is silent.

The stakes are the number proclaimed at the crest, the render of every selat, the standing of the throne through a hungry year, and who is named heir before a quarrel over the chair becomes a quarrel over the reign.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The frame prescribes no heir, no number at the crest and no answer from Bethua or Okharis. The water on the wall at the Measure is what it is, and the referee decides how near the line it stands. What the party meets are the people who want a number, a render or a successor, each with a count of their own and a reason to keep it from someone.

## Who Moves It {#who-moves-it}

[[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]], Gar-Au of Au'Khelathu
: Forty-six, practical, exact and patient, who prefers a late answer to a wrong one. She wants a good flood and a settled succession, in that order. She knows the render of every selat and reads a short crop in a late barge, she weighs omens too heavily, and she has answered no Halzi'a in writing.

[[being-anlaghur|Anlaghur]], the Gar-Au's nephew
: Forty-three, an Iru'palu of Anlagh-Zetun, whose blood places him near the succession and whose temperament places him some way from it. He dines the Halzi'a of the upper river as a creditor dines debtors.

[[being-gerragezeelan|Gerra Geze'el'Anlaghu]], Halzi'a of Lenen-Nezut
: Forty-one, head of Geze'el'Anlaghu at [[place-lenennezut|Lenen-Nezut]], the faded old capital of that line, who presses a claim on the strength of the descent. His supporters outnumber his real prospects by some distance.

[[being-khelansisgth|Khelansi Sa'Gathau]], Halzi'a of the Capital Selat
: Fifty-one, a senior officer of the Gar-Au's court who sets the capital's ration by the Measure's number. He wants the capital's granaries to carry [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]] to a good flood, so that nobody in the city goes hungry on his reckoning.

[[being-zinurilsltr|Zinuri Les'Leretu]], Overseer of the Granaries of the Upper River Selatu
: Forty-four, who reckons the render of the eighteen upper selatu at Anlagh-Zetun and reports to the throne what each owes, whoever is embarrassed by it. He has no soldiers and no house of his own behind him, and he does not sign a figure he cannot account for.

[[being-gakhotigzt|Gakhoti Gul'Zetu]], Overseer of the Sea-Gate
: Fifty, who collects the Gar-Au's customs at [[place-garanlaghet|Gar-Anlaghet]] for the ten delta selatu. He wants the customs treasury he has built to be the throne's answer to a hungry year.

[[being-mentothaakhel|Mentotha Akhelo'Naru]], Halzi'a of Yath-Lemet
: Holder of the smallest of the upper selatu, a gorge whose town buys its own bread by barge, who sends the whole of a small render each year, late, with a clerk's note about the river.

The Measure
: The [[being-thazlekhau|Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a]], who proclaims the number at the [[place-greattempluqaa|Great Temple of Uqa'a]]; the old [[being-msrreader|Reader of the Measure]], who goes down the stair each day of the Inundation; the [[being-msrkeeper|Keeper of the Measure]]; and his [[being-msrsenapprn|Senior Apprentice]], who carries the Reader's tally-board on a morning the Reader cannot go down.

[[being-amithenakrvn|Amithena of House Kervanion]], Metrissa of Bethua
: Fifty-one, Queen and High Priestess at [[place-bethura|Bethura]], who wants the two pillars in balance and a decision that neither can disown. Her delay is becoming a choice of its own.

[[being-beraofbrsehl|Bera of House Bera-Sehel]] and [[being-aseliakhlthmr|Aselia of House Khalath-Mara]]
: The matriarch of the Great House of horse-breeders and army suppliers, whose letters of credit have grown strong this year, and the senior voice of the [[affiliation-councilmatriarchs|Council of Matriarchs]], who has watched that credit grow and keeps her own House's lean to herself.

[[being-izetmu|The Izet-Mu]]
: Chief engineer-priestess of the [[affiliation-orderwaters|Order of the Waters]], who writes the Sacred College's count of the grain and holds that the cisterns are not full enough to empty.

[[being-takorozanethar|Tákoro Zanethar]], King of Okharis
: Forty, Keeper of the Three Flames at [[place-zarhanis|The First Flame]], who holds the three temple-cities and the tribes together by ceremony more than by command. He wants the Oracle's answer to come before he must act, and the Three-Flame Settlement held until it does.

[[being-ewuratebara|Éwura Tébara]], the Oracle of Okharis
: Fifty-one, who wants the answer to come as the Flame gives it and will not hurry it for the King, the council or the Warden.

[[being-ngaluromara|Ngalu Rómara]] and [[being-omaresonkara|Omare Sónkara]]
: The Captain of the Host, who waits for a summons the King cannot give, and the Warden of the Flame at [[place-vuthraka|The Wild Flame]], who asks more riders for the frontier and reads a late draw as an insult to the Flame.

[[being-khelathienvy|The Khelathi Envoy]]
: A courteous, patient envoy of a Khelathi house that needs horsemen and will not say which, who has waited long enough in the guest-houses of Zarhanis.

The bodies
: The Genzet'Palu and its mediators; the priesthood of Uqa'a, which reads and proclaims the number; the Halzi'a of the upper river who hold back render; the Council of Houses and the Sacred College, each with its own count of Bethua's grain; the [[affiliation-bthntrdhss|Bethuan Trade-Houses]], whose private stores are for sale; the temple-cities of Okharis and the chieftains of its village tribes, the [[affiliation-magaratrb|Magara Tribe]] first among them.

## The Arcs

The frame follows one low river from the crest through the reckoning of the render, and each arc turns one strand of it.

1. **The number.** The count is read on the stair at the Measure and walked across Yath-Telgu to the Great Temple, and the number the priests proclaim is the number that arrives. A number proclaimed at dawn goes up and down the river on the first boats. [[scenario-nightbfrrdng|The Night Before the Reading]] runs the last night before the crest, when the Gar-Au asks strangers to stand at the gauge and her nephew gives a dinner in the city.
2. **The render.** At Anlagh-Zetun the Overseer's rolls are out of step with the river, and the capital's granaries credit [[place-yathlemet|Yath-Lemet]] with grain the gorge could not grow. [[scenario-rndrgorge|The Render of the Gorge]] runs the reckoning the Overseer will not sign until he can account for every figure in it.
3. **The grain.** The surplus of Bethua's strips is counted twice, once for each pillar, and the two counts disagree. [[scenario-hrvstcntdtwc|A Harvest Counted Twice]] runs the count the Metrissa asks of strangers who belong to no House and no temple. On the Order's round in the dry south, [[scenario-grnpstthln|The Green Past the Line]] runs a failed tunnel-well that the Izet-Mu will not send a gang into until she knows why it fell.
4. **The horsemen.** The Khelathi embassies wait at Zarhanis for an audience the King cannot grant, and the draw of riders comes in late and short at Vuthraka. [[scenario-gftmgrflds|A Gift for the Magara Folds]] runs one envoy's ride to the Magara chieftain's fire, and what his scribe writes when the gift is taken.
5. **The marches.** Bethua cannot press the clans of the [[place-sthmrchsrgn|South Marches]] while the grain question is open, and their tribute is late. [[scenario-trbtftrdrv|The Tribute After the Drive]] runs a clan's claim that Bethuan riders have already taken its share.

The arcs run in any order, and each moves the year whether or not the party finishes it. Several arcs turn on two counts that disagree, each honest at its own end. Set side by side, they show more than either keeper knows.

## Elsewhere in the Land

These scenarios run beside the frame and do not move it. They suit a party that has made enemies at court, needs coin between arcs, or travels to the land's edges on the frame's business.

- In the dry margin west of the river, [[scenario-clublstvltadv|The Unwritten Vault]] runs a rock-cut common tomb that no temple roll mentions, where the dead rise at dusk to haul stone.
- In the desert west of the river, [[scenario-sldmouthsadv|The House of Sealed Mouths]] runs a temple scribe's errand up a canal that is going dry, to a funerary estate whose servants meet the boat with their lips sewn shut.
- On the grazing lands east of Zarhanis, [[scenario-shuremisadv|The Hearth Without Light]] runs a ruined temple estate that the clans avoid and the temple does not name.
- On the eastern stages of [[place-marchesroad|the Marches Road]], [[scenario-nxtwllst|The Next Well East]] runs a Great House's caravan at [[place-hasembecamp|Hasembe Camp]] that needs riders before the Jackals sell it an escort.
- In the [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|Central Rainforests]], [[scenario-whlsdrwnzgrt|Hands That Never Stop Turning]], [[scenario-hshcrclslnt|The Hush at the Heart of the Wood]] and [[scenario-trbtlpdkng|The Unseen King of the Spotted Court]] run the drowned temple, the silent circle and the sorcerer-leader of the forest.
- In the [[place-sthrnsvnhs|Southern Savannahs]], the [[affiliation-greatlubemo|Great Lubemo]] gathers at [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]] in a year of war between two clans. [[scenario-trnsatsprng|The Turns at the Spring]] and [[scenario-cnvnrspkslst|The Convener Speaks Last]] run the dry season before the sitting and the sitting itself.

## Ways In {#ways-in}

1. **For the throne.** The Gar-Au summons a party from outside the bureaus to stand at the Measure through the night before the reading, to learn which selat's reckoning is wrong, or to tell her who sits at her nephew's table ([[scenario-nightbfrrdng|The Night Before the Reading]]).
2. **For the bureaus.** Khelansi Sa'Gathau wants a granary that gave two tallies in one month counted by hand. Zinuri Les'Leretu wants strangers no Halzi'a owns to learn where an upper selat's credited grain came from. Gakhoti Gul'Zetu wants the weights at a light quay re-weighed before his harbor-master does it ([[scenario-rndrgorge|The Render of the Gorge]]).
3. **For the nephew.** Anlaghur invites the party to dine on the night before the reading, and the guests seated nearest him are the Halzi'a who held back render ([[scenario-nightbfrrdng|The Night Before the Reading]]).
4. **For the shore.** The Metrissa wants one figure for the realm's grain that neither pillar can disown, a witness at the envoy's audience, or a party to ride to the Hégissa about the late tribute of the Marches ([[scenario-hrvstcntdtwc|A Harvest Counted Twice]], [[scenario-trbtftrdrv|The Tribute After the Drive]]).
5. **For the horsemen.** The King sends an honest guide with a Khelathi envoy to a chieftain's fire, the Captain of the Host wants eyes at that fire, and the Magara chieftain wants outsiders to read the envoy's terms ([[scenario-gftmgrflds|A Gift for the Magara Folds]]).

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- At the crest the Senior Apprentice carries the count across the island in the Reader's place, and the Thaz'Lekhau proclaims a failed Measure. Every Halzi'a who held back render has the flood to answer for it, Gerra's partisans call the failed flood the throne's third judgment, and the Gar-Au announces famine and opens the throne's own stores. The Keeper reports his own reading before the day is out, and within the month the city knows that the number proclaimed and the water on the wall disagree.
- Zinuri signs his reckoning with the credit to Yath-Lemet marked as unaccounted. The bureaus ask Khelansi to explain the credit, and he asks for time.
- The Metrissa does not choose, and the two counts of Bethua's grain go on disagreeing. The tribute of the Marches stays late.
- [[being-omilotarombu|Omilo Tárombu]] takes the envoy's gift and commits to nothing, and the scribe writes. At the next moot the cities name the Magara as a tribe that sells its riders, the Magara name the cities as a crown that counts a gift twice, and the King cannot act while the Oracle is silent.
- The question of the succession stays open, and the Genzet'Palu's mediators carry every faction's offer to the others.

## The Secret {#the-secret}

**The throne's grain.** Meqes has drawn on the throne's household treasure to buy small lots of grain from the private stores of the Bethuan Trade-Houses, most of them from the yards of House Bera-Sehel, through the mediators of the Genzet'Palu, and has told the bureaus nothing. The lots are well short of the large sale that waits on the Metrissa's choice. Gakhoti Gul'Zetu lands them duty-free at night at Gar-Anlaghet and enters the ships as ballast, and at the Gar-Au's private instruction Khelansi Sa'Gathau books some of them as the render of Yath-Lemet, a selat that never sent it. She has set a date by which she announces a failed flood or confesses the purchases, and she has tied it to this year's proclamation. A failed Measure lets her bring the grain out as the throne's provision. A low one leaves her the confession.

**The paid count.** The Senior Apprentice of the Measure has been paid to carry a count one cubit lower than the water stands, by the steward of an upper-river Halzi'a whose render has come in short. A failed Measure excuses every short render in the empire and lays the dearth at the throne's door. The water itself stands above the line of a failed flood: dearth, a lighter levy and a hard year, but no famine.

**The nephew's roll.** Zinuri Les'Leretu sends a full copy of every reckoning to Anlaghur's household, in hope of the heir's favor if the succession is decided. A party that reads Anlaghur's copy can name the selat whose silver bought the apprentice.

The Gar-Au's purchases are the one secret that touches every arc: the count at the Measure, the credit to Yath-Lemet, the ballast at the sea-gate and the missing grain in Bethua's two counts all lead back to her. This frame does not say who is named heir, or whether anyone is. The referee decides, and may let the party's actions decide.
