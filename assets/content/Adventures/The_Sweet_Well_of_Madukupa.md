In a drought year, [[place-madukupa|Madukupa]] is the only place in the foothills with water, and its Samonta gives it free. The party must find out what the water costs, who has been paying, and whether the fort can be saved from its own well.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A road-keeper of [[affiliation-adanshreni|the Adanshreni]] pays well for the Samonta's ledgers. The shrena has never been able to stop a wagon near the fort, and the Margapala who licenses that stretch wants to know why before his charter is renewed.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** A caravan-master is short two guards for the dry stretch past the fort, and says it quietly: _drink only from your own skins._
- **The leader.** A woman of the herder village below the fort asks the chief of a company or a household to find her son, sold into the Samonta's household three dry seasons ago. She was told he left.
- **The wanderer.** A Jvelita of the [[affiliation-agmipantra|Agmi-pantra]] arrives at the fort bearing the Samonta's own letter, asking for a purgation. The Jvelita has not been told what must be purged, and asks a traveler to stand beside him at the gate.

## Scene One: The Cup at the Gate

**Purpose.** The entrance and the guardian: show a courtesy that cannot easily be refused.

**Situation.** The track climbs to a gatehouse of white stone as the sun sets. The gate is open. A servant stands in it with a cup and a jar, and behind him the courtyard is lit and full of the smell of food. Everyone who comes in at this hour is a guest, and a guest is fed before he is questioned.

**NPCs and threats.** The **gate-ward** is [Kavrapal]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a stout Senaja who served in the Maharava's host and now keeps the gate of the Samonta's household. He is kind and bluff and has the habit of drinking from the jar himself, before and after every guest. The cup he offers is the first threat. The sweet water is cold and deeply restful, and a few swallows start the long dream. Kavrapal is not wicked; he has seen the sleepers walk, and he has talked himself into believing it is a rite. Behind him, [Nili]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a bonded cup-bearer no older than fifteen, stands with her wrist wrapped in cloth and her bond-token on a cord. She does not meet any guest's eye.

**Player choices.**

- _Drink._ A single swallow gives the dream a hold. A bowl gives it the night.
- _Pour a libation._ Spilling the first of the cup to the ground, with a word to the pass-gods, is a courtesy as old as the road. Kavrapal accepts it, a little puzzled.
- _Bring their own._ A guest with their own skin may drink from it. It is no insult, and the household remembers it.
- _Claim a station._ A Retuja, an ascetic or a bearer of a letter from [[affiliation-mrchntclctvvdyr|the Merchant Collective of Vedyara]] may be taken in without the cup. The household bows.
- _Climb the wall._ The curtain is plain stone. A stealthy party enters over the north angle, and sleeps in the horse-lines.

**Clues and rewards.** Every servant wears a token stamped with a ring and a line, the well and its stair. None of them wears gray hair. Nili's cord is knotted, and the knot is a count of dry seasons.

**Consequences.** A guest who drinks is among the Samonta's sleepers by morning unless a companion wakes him. A guest who refuses is marked, and the household watches him.

**If bypassed.** A party that enters over the wall lands in the horse-lines in the middle of the feast and meets the Samonta unannounced.

## Scene Two: The Feast and the Ledger

**Purpose.** A roleplay and a puzzle: learn what the fort is and who knows it.

**Situation.** The hall is long and warm, and the feast is generous. The Samonta sits at the high table with his household, and his guests are placed by rank. The servants move quietly and the talk is of horses and weather. A bound ledger lies on the steward's table at the far end of the hall.

**NPCs and threats.** [Jayabira Chorbhavishana]{.name data-type="being/npc"} is the Samonta, a big grey man whose courtesy is all that holds him upright. He speaks well and laughs well, and he has not slept properly in a year. His wife, [Sundeli]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the mistress of the house, watches every guest as one watches a debt. Their son [Anandabira]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a boy of sixteen, does not drink the water and does not say why. The household priest, Purohata [Jnanadasa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is a quiet man in grey who asks the party their shrines and listens to the answers. The steward, [Olgupal]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an Outcaste whose struck-through wrist-line is bound in cloth, keeps the ledger. He was never asked to the high table, and he is the only person in the household who has counted.

**Player choices.**

- _Charm the high table._ The Samonta wants a story, and a good one earns a private word with him on the wall at night.
- _Read the wrists._ The servants' bond-tokens and wrist-wraps tell the ledger's story. A bonded servant's token is stamped with the year of the bond, and none is more than three dry seasons old.
- _Read the ledger._ Olgupal will show it to someone who sits with him and eats from his dish, the way the Outcaste are never asked to eat. The ledger records every bonded servant bought and every one _sent down_, with the second column running ahead of the first.
- _Question the priest._ The Purohata says the stair is a rite of the household. He says it carefully.
- _Be quiet._ A party that eats and watches sees the servants refill the Samonta's cup from a different jar than the guests'.

**Clues and rewards.** The fort sends down one a season, always a servant bought in the dry months, never a member of the household. The Samonta's grandfather wrote an oath into the first page of the ledger, a _kortu_, naming a debt to the well. The Samonta is a man trying to break it.

**Consequences.** A party that reads the ledger is trusted by Olgupal and watched by Sundeli. A party that confronts the Samonta openly loses the household's goodwill and hears nothing more.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the feast and goes straight for the well finds the courtyard empty and the stair open, with a servant at the curb who is not sure why she is there.

## Scene Three: The Walking Hour

**Purpose.** A setback: the water takes some of the party.

**Situation.** Deep in the night, the courtyard fills with soft footsteps. Those who drank at the gate, and the sleepers among the household, are walking out into the dark with their eyes open and their faces smooth, in single file, toward the well. The Samonta is watching from the wall, and he does not move.

**NPCs and threats.** The walking are the household's bound servants, a dozen men and women and a handful of the party if any drank. They do not hurry. They descend the stair one by one, and the cold at the curb is like a hand on the neck. The **gate-ward** Kavrapal is among them, still asleep in his own hall. Anyone who wakes a walker finds the walker waking terrified and aware, the stair beneath them. The fort has its own watch, and the watch does not interfere.

**Player choices.**

- _Wake them._ A shout does nothing. Salt, ash, cold water from a skin and a struck blow all do. Each walker woken must be held, or they will gho back.
- _Follow._ A sleepwalking party member may be shadowed down the stair, and the follower sees where the others gho.
- _Block the curb._ A line of the bold at the curb stops the walkers physically. The cold at the shaft's mouth drains them.
- _Confront the Samonta._ He is on the wall and says nothing for a long minute. Then he asks for help.
- _Let it happen._ A party that does nothing finds the household reduced by one, and its guest or guide among them.

**Clues and rewards.** The sleepers walk with their eyes open to the dead end of a shaft where nobody has ever found a floor. Olgupal's ledger names the chamber at the bottom: _the lower room_. A walker who is woken tells the party what they saw: a woman made of water, smiling, in a room of sleepers.

**Consequences.** Every sleeper not woken goes down. The Samonta's confession, if the party earns it, is a plea.

**If bypassed.** A party that is away from the courtyard during the hour comes back to find the household one short and the stair still warm.

## Scene Four: The Lower Room

**Purpose.** The climax: the thing under the fort.

**Situation.** The shaft descends past the water-line into a low cavern the grandfather found when he cut the well. The floor is a pool of black, still water, and around it, laid in rows on ledges of limestone, are the sleepers. They breathe. Some of them have been here since the grandfather's day. Their faces are smooth and young, or they were young once. At the pool's center, something stirs.

**NPCs and threats.** The [Well-Mother]{.name data-type="being/creature"} is a woman made of water, tall and smiling, with long unbound hair that falls into the pool and does not end. She is not a god and not an animal. She is older than the stone, and she is a thing of [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] that has learned the shape of those she has taken, and wears a woman's. She speaks in the voices of the sleepers: their mothers, their children, their old friends. She is kind. She says she is lonely and means it, and she holds the water of the whole seam, which lies under the fort and the three nearest wells. She fights by dream, with sleep and with the stair, and when pressed she fights with the cold.

**Player choices.**

- _Fight._ The Well-Mother cannot be cut. Fire drives her back from the pool's edge, and the cold comes up the shaft behind it.
- _Bargain._ The oath names the Samonta's line, not the household, and a new oath with a lighter price can be sworn if the party offers it: one night's dream each season, freely given, from the Samonta's own head.
- _Purge._ A Jvelita can burn the cold out of the seam with his ember, at the cost of the ember and the water.
- _Pay the debt._ The Samonta may descend himself. The oath is satisfied by the line's own blood and not by servants. He was always willing, and was always afraid.
- _Ask her name._ She has one, and has not heard it in an age. A party that finds it in the ledger's first page and speaks it gives her a reason to listen.

**Clues and rewards.** The Well-Mother wants to be known. The grandfather swore to her and then never spoke to her again; the line has kept the oath and never the thing it was made with. She will release the sleepers to someone who talks to her.

**Consequences.** Kill her and the seam fails, and the fort and three other wells gho dry. Bargain, and the fort keeps its water at a price it can afford. Leave her, and the sleepers gho on.

**If bypassed.** A party that stops at the stair's foot and does not enter the room finds the water on the stair rising behind it.

## Scene Five: The Sleepers Wake

**Purpose.** The twist and the reward.

**Situation.** Whatever the party chooses, the sleepers on the ledges stir. They are alive. The bonded servants sent down in the grandfather's day, and the strangers without kin, have slept in the lower room for decades, tended in their sleep and unaged. If the Well-Mother releases them, they wake as they were, and their world is decades gone.

**NPCs and threats.** The sleepers wake confused, frightened and hungry. Among them is the herder woman's son from the hook, who stands up on the ledge and asks what year it is. Beside him is a man in the dress of a generation ago, a servant of the grandfather's, who knows the oath's text and says it aloud. There is no villain in the room. The Samonta, if he lives, kneels in the water and weeps.

**Player choices.**

- _Lead them out._ The stair is long, and the sleepers are weak.
- _Take the cache._ The grandfather left a chest on the lowest ledge, with a purse of chendra, a string of tomra and a bundle of cloth that has kept its colour.
- _Tell the council._ The three wells that share the seam belong to Samantas of the Maharava's martial council, who have their own reasons to keep the secret. The party's choice of what to tell them shapes the march.
- _Keep it quiet._ A pledge of silence to the Samonta, in return for the fort's protection, buys the party a favor in the foothills.

**Clues and rewards.** The cache is modest, a purse of silver and some cloth. The sleepers are the true reward: a household's worth of living people, most of them thought dead, and a mother who finds her son.

**Consequences.** The household breaks apart or knits together depending on how the Samonta is treated. The next dry season tests the new terms.

**If bypassed.** The Well-Mother lets the sleepers gho herself, a few at a time, over the next season, and the fort fills with strangers who do not know the year.

## Aftermath

- **The bargain broken.** The seam fails. Three wells in the foothills gho dry in the next drought, and the march road's herders move south. The Maharava's council quarrels.
- **A new oath.** Madukupa keeps its water. The Samonta sleeps for the first time in a year, and his heir is told the truth.
- **The Samonta in the lower room.** His son holds the fort, and the household takes a new name and a new rite.
- **The sleepers freed.** The herder village below fills with returned dead who are not dead. The temple at [[place-ashvapada|Ashvapada]] has to rule on whether they are the same people.
- **The ledger in the shrena's hands.** The Margapala renews the charter on the stretch of road and says nothing.
