The [[place-twrlghtnmrkd|Tower of the Lightning-Marked]] stands on the salt of the [Pale Floor]{.name data-type="place/region"}, and the lightning finds it every night the sky offers. Its last master, [Vahrun Marzuvan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, is said never to have died. The party must cross the Floor, climb the tower and learn what walks in the storm above it, and then decide what is owed to one of the [[lore-dunhariclt|Dunhari]] who has stopped being one.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A fence in [[place-qarvelakhend|Qarvelakhend]] pays well for the master's iron crown and asks no questions. A rival went south before him and did not come back.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** The Amradi _ghedvader_ at Qarvelakhend posts the party to the [Kinless Watch]{.name data-type="place/structure"} on the edge of the Floor. The garrison is hired with coin, owes the party no water, and keeps a cistern nobody is allowed to see.
- **The leader.** A chieftain, or a member of [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|the Warrior's Circle]], hears that something in the storm is calling Dunhari to the Floor. The [Old Promise]{.name data-type="lore/custom"} forbids raising a blade against a Dunhari while an outside enemy walks the land, and nobody can say whether the master is still a Dunhari.
- **The wanderer.** A traveler is found on the salt with glass rooted in his hands. He mutters verses in a voice-bearer's cadence that no living voice-bearer teaches.

## Scene One: The Toll and the Glass

**Purpose.** Establish the Pale Floor as hostile, the toll as law, and the tower as a thing [[affiliation-strmcltsbhrms|the storm cults]] will not discuss.

**Situation.** The party reaches the northern tent at dusk. A caravan waits to cross and the toll-keepers have named a day three nights hence. The sky is clear, and out on the white flat the lightning has already begun to strike, a thin forked line a long ride away.

**NPCs and threats.** Two apprentices of [The Lightning-Marked]{.name data-type="affiliation"} (_hamelogar_) keep the tent: [Dahrin]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a tall, hollow-eyed young man who has not slept properly since he came to the Floor, and [Sarvesh]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an older apprentice with a scar across the lip who does the talking. They are polite, firm and afraid. Beyond the tent, the flat is walked at night by **glass-wights**, men and women of the caravans that crossed on the wrong day. A glass-wight is a figure of salt-white skin and green-grey glass, lit from inside by a flickering light. It moves in a stiff, patient march toward any traveler out of the tent's lamplight and does not speak. Its touch burns. It fears nothing except water poured onto its feet, which cracks the glass and slows it.

**Player choices.**

- _Pay and wait._ One load of salt in twenty and three days of rest. Safe, and it gives time to talk to the apprentices.
- _Cross early._ The party bribes a guide or waits for the caravan's cover. This brings the glass-wights.
- _Stealth._ Go at night between bolts. The wights go where the lightning leaves its mark, and a quiet party can slip between strikes.
- _Parley._ Sarvesh will say what the toll is for if he is shown salt shared in the proper way, bread broken and a pinch pressed into the torn edge.

**Clues and rewards.** The apprentices know that the lightning strikes more than the iron spike. It has begun to strike the same ring of flat around the mound, and the glass-wights mark that ring. The toll-keepers also hold a leather pouch of the lightning-marked's talismans, glass beads that will glow when a glass-wight is near.

**Consequences.** Paid and welcomed, the party gains the toll-keepers' goodwill and a name for the safe day. Seen to cross unpaid, they are branded to the storm cults as thieves of the god's road.

**If bypassed.** A party that crosses the flat by another route reaches the mound at night and finds the glass-wights standing in a ring around its foot, still, facing inward.

## Scene Two: The Hall of Sitting

**Purpose.** A roleplay and puzzle scene that makes the party learn what the cults know and what they are hiding.

**Situation.** The door at the foot of the tower is a slab of fused glass with an iron lintel. It does not open to force. In the vestibule beyond, the walls carry verses cut into the stone in the _voice-bearer's_ cadence, and a half-stair of iron leads up to the hall where the lineages sit. On the stair, waiting, is [Mehrava]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the senior elder of the Lightning-Marked, who has come alone from her camp and will not say how long she has been here.

**NPCs and threats.** Mehrava is a thin, upright woman in indigo, with a forked mark across her left cheek and the stillness of someone who listens to the sky. She tells no lie and softens no reading. She will not help the party kill the master, because she cannot say that the master is dead. She also has no wish to see him freed. She asks what the party wants, and weighs the answer.

**Player choices.**

- _Recitation._ The door opens to the first four lines of the lament the voice-bearers keep for the dead. A party member who can recite them, or who has a poet or voice-bearer along, opens it.
- _Force._ A thick glass door and a lightning-charged iron lintel: a hard, loud, dangerous way, and one that wakes the stair.
- _Parley with Mehrava._ She can be persuaded to speak the lament if the party tells her what they intend. She judges truth with ease.
- _Guile._ Her reading of the sky is exact. A lie about the storm is the quickest way to lose her.

**Clues and rewards.** The verses on the wall are a roster of the lineage's initiates, and the last ten names have been struck through. Mehrava names the cult-master's crime in plain words: he called the lightning not to read it but to hold it, and the god has not answered since. She gives the party the _marked stone_ of her lineage, a flat pebble scored with a fork. It is a token the storm will not strike.

**Consequences.** If the party lies, Mehrava withdraws and the door remains shut until another way is found. If they win her trust, she warns them that the stair will test them and that the master is not alone.

**If bypassed.** A party that forces the door finds the hall empty and Mehrava gone. The iron stair above is already humming.

## Scene Three: The Singing Stair

**Purpose.** A trick and setback that turns the tower against the party and tempts them with the crown.

**Situation.** The stair winds up through the dark, bound with iron rungs, and at each turn the lightning outside strikes the tower and the whole stair sings. On the landing of the sealed room the door stands open. Inside, on a stool in a pool of faint light, sits an iron crown.

**NPCs and threats.** The crown is a lure. The master forged it to draw the bolts off himself, and it is wired to the iron of the stair through a seam in the floor. Lifting it, or moving the stool, sends a bolt down every rung at once. The stair also holds a **struck man**: [Dorvan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a young apprentice bound to the wall by fused glass, still alive and half-blind, begging for water. The glass-wights from the flat press up the stair behind the party and close the way down.

**Player choices.**

- _Free the struck man._ Water, a knife and patience cut him loose. His gratitude is real and so is his terror of the gallery.
- _Take the crown._ The lure is a poor imitation, thin iron with fused glass for gems. Lifting it marks the bearer to the storm.
- _Climb on._ The stair is safe at the rhythm of the thunder if someone counts, and a quick wit and a sure foot outrun a bolt.
- _Look for another way._ A narrow flue in the sealed room's wall runs to the gallery, and a thief finds it.

**Clues and rewards.** Dorvan says the master did not climb to the gallery in triumph. He went up to hold a door shut, and a voice answered him from the storm that was not the god's. He also says the master's true crown went with him, because the master has never taken it off.

**Consequences.** Every bolt that strikes while the party is on the stair leaves a burn on whoever stood nearest the rungs. The party loses the way down.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the room reaches the gallery by the upper flight, with the glass-wights behind them.

## Scene Four: The Standing Storm

**Purpose.** The climax. The party meets what the master became.

**Situation.** The gallery under the iron spike is open to the sky. A single cloud hangs directly overhead with an edge as sharp as a blade, and under it, at the foot of the spike, a man stands. The air smells of burning metal. The lightning strikes the man over and over and he does not fall.

**NPCs and threats.** Vahrun Marzuvan stands wrapped in a column of storm: tall, bare-armed, his beard and hair fused into iron and glass, the real crown a ring of dull black metal burned into his brow. He speaks in two voices at once, his own and a vast flat one that is not [[lore-bahramisdty|Báhramis]]. [[lore-theshadow|The Shadow]] is in the storm, the chaos from outside creation that crept in at a crack the master made when he tried to bind what he held to be the god's lightning to his will. He did not go into the storm to escape death. He went to hold the crack shut, and he cannot let go. He fights by calling bolts, by hurling fused glass like spears, and by speaking the party's names, which the storm repeats until the one named forgets where to stand.

**Player choices.**

- _Fight._ He is hard to hurt and the bolts are deadly. Fighting from behind the iron of the stair spreads the current.
- _Cut him from the spike._ The iron spike is his anchor. Snapping the chain of rungs that feeds it weakens him for a few breaths.
- _Parley._ He knows the party's names and answers questions asked as a guest asks, with salt, although the salt burns in the storm.
- _Use Mehrava's marked stone._ If the party has it, the bearer is steadied against the storm's calling.

**Clues and rewards.** In the storm's lulls the master speaks as a poet. He recites the lines of his own lineage, the names of the apprentices he lost and one sentence he cannot finish. A party that speaks the voice-bearers' lament over him in full makes him pause, and in the pause the crack can be seen: a seam of absence in the cloud.

**Consequences.** If the party strikes him down, the crack widens and the storm that follows them down the stair belongs to nothing living. If they bind him with the lament, the crack holds as long as the tower stands.

**If bypassed.** A party that refuses the gallery meets the master at the foot of the stair on the descent, and the fight comes to them in the worse place.

## Scene Five: The Promise and the Crown

**Purpose.** The reward, the twist and the decision.

**Situation.** The storm ebbs. The master kneels, a thin old man with a burned face. The crown on his brow is cold, and he offers it to the party.

**NPCs and threats.** Mehrava has climbed the stair behind them. She tells them what they do not want to hear. Vahrun is a Dunhari, the Shadow in the storm is an enemy from outside, and the Old Promise forbids any Dunhari blade raised against another while such an enemy walks Dunhari land. The blow that kills him is the blow the Promise forbids. The crack, however, will not close unless someone stands in it.

**Player choices.**

- _Kill him._ The Promise is broken in the Circle's eyes, the crack opens, and the Shadow walks the Floor.
- _Leave him._ He holds the crack for as long as he lasts, and the tower sings on.
- _Take the crown._ A storm-walker, or anyone the storm will accept, can wear it and stand in his place. The cost is the wearer's own, and Mehrava says plainly what it is.
- _Carry his case to the [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]]._ Mehrava will ask the [Council of Elders]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} to name him not a master but a warden of the crack, and the Circle to rule that he is held to the Promise and not outside it.

**Clues and rewards.** The crown is of iron said by the lineage to be tempered by the god's own fire, and in the right hands it steadies a storm-walker against any storm. In the sealed room beneath the stair lie the lineage's rolls of initiates and a modest store of salt in glass jars. A party that has paid the toll is not asked to pay it again.

**Consequences.** What the party chooses becomes what the Circle and the cults say of them.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves without learning what the master held finds the storm back above the Floor within a season, and a second figure standing in the gallery.

## Aftermath

- **If the master is bound.** The Floor is safe to cross, the toll falls, and the Lightning-Marked lineage grows in standing. Mehrava's readings are taken more seriously by the Council.
- **If the master is killed.** The storm leaves the tower and goes into the open desert. Caravans lose camels to lightning that does not strike.
- **If the crown is taken to the Stone of Ranaz.** The Council argues for a season, and the Circle is asked whether the Old Promise binds against a Dunhari who is a door.
- **If the Kinless Watch learns what was done.** They report to [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Amradad]], and the Kingdom's interest in the Floor deepens.
