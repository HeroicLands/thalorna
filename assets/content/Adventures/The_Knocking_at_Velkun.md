An oath is knocking at a cedar door in the dark, and a town of four thousand has learned not to answer. The party must find out what the voice wants, what it was before it wanted it, and whether the cellars under [[place-velkunvartun|the Barred Gate]] hold more than tablets.

## Overview

The settlement of [[place-vahurdash|Vahurdash]] sits in the corner of the old city of [[place-velkunar|Veshkun]] and bars its gate each night against a knocking that speaks the words of the champion in [[lore-velkunsong|the Song of the Lost Town]]. The knocker is the **Gate-dead**, a thing the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]] made out of an unpaid oath and the unread dead of the record-house. It can be turned away, and it can be answered, and the only people who have answered it are gone.

The adventure runs over three days and two nights. Day one is the arrival and the first night at the gate. Day two is the cellars. The second night is the reckoning at the door. The scenes are arranged as five rooms, and each can be run in any order that the party's choices make natural, with the bypass notes below to carry play on.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A broker in the yards of [[place-kethramir|Kethramír]] will pay well for any one of the clay tablets in the record-house cellars, no questions asked as to which. He does not say which house he fronts for. The cellars are under the settlement, and the settlement does not like strangers who dig.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The House of Vahurdash keeps a token guard and cannot man its gate. Khorsád-Vahur hires hard men for the dusk watch at a daily wage in barter-weights of dates and iron, and does not say what the watch is for until the first night.
- **The leader.** A caravan chief of the Tellumi quarter in Kethramír has a string due at the rim in four days. She cannot afford to let it stand outside the wall at dusk. She asks a trusted captain to go ahead and make the road safe.
- **The lone wanderer.** The party arrives alone and benighted, and the Tellumi law of the guest says the gate must open. The warden has to choose between the law and the knocking, and the wanderer is the cause of the choice.

## Scene One: The Barred Gate

**Purpose.** Establish the rule of the place, put the party under the gate's discipline, and let them hear the knock.

**Situation.** The party reaches the gate near dusk. The cedar door stands open and the warden, **Hagele**, a broad woman with a bronze-bound staff and a face that has not slept in a month, is waiting for the last light to drain from the western wall. If the party is late, she meets them with the lamp in one hand and the bar in the other and does not open. She will explain the rule: the door does not open between the first lamp and the second. If a party insists on guest-right, she opens a wicket in the side wall, a hand's-breadth of iron grating, and passes water and bread through it and asks them to wait for morning. A fully outside party is not safe in the open rim, so the choice presses.

**NPCs and threats.** Hagele is stubborn, kindly and frightened, and her fear sounds like duty. She has opened the door once in the dark and does not mean to open it again. Behind her stand two watchmen, young settlers who have not been given any explanation they believe. The Gate-dead is beyond the door. By night it is a tall figure in a travel-stained cloak with a spear held butt-first, whose face the lamp does not light. It knocks, it speaks the oath and it waits. It does not shout and it never strikes with force. If the door is opened, it steps over the threshold with great courtesy and says _wake and walk_.

**Player choices.** Force: wrench the bar and meet the Gate-dead; this works once and costs the party the warden's help. Stealth: slip out of the town by a lane while the watch is turned and listen from the rim. Guile: take the watch with the town, standing the dusk in the gate-court and hearing the whole of it from within. Parley: speak through the cedar to the Gate-dead; it answers in the champion's words and in no others, and it asks to be let in as one stranger asks a house.

**Clues and rewards.** The outer face of the door is scored in two places, a man's chest and a boy's height. The Gate-dead speaks the oath in the order of the song and then repeats the last line of it, _I am the gate of the house of the rim_, and at the second repeat its voice changes: the dust in it clears and a younger voice says, once, _Stand off till the light_. The warden's goodwill is a reward in itself, and a place to sleep.

**Consequences.** The party has heard the knocking and cannot pretend it is a story. If they opened the door they meet the Gate-dead in the open and are walked north. The first hours of the night are the hardest to get through, and what the party does with them decides the rest.

**If bypassed.** A party that arrives by day, stays in the settlement, or passes through the gate before dusk still hears the knocking from the lanes at night. The sound carries through the whole town. The warden finds them in the first grey hour and asks them to see the Shekhul.

## Scene Two: The Song and the Priest

**Purpose.** Give the party the shape of the thing, through the song and through the people who own it.

**Situation.** **Murelul**, the Shekhul of Vahurdash, keeps a shuttered house near the lower well and a room full of copied tablets, and he receives the party at an hour when he knows they will come. He knows the whole of the song, and he knows where it is wrong. He tells the party that the Gate-dead is the oath without the man, that it will answer only the oath, and that the oath can be taken back by the one who has the right to take it. He also tells them what he has told no one in the settlement: the keepers of the record-house were never named.

**NPCs and threats.** Murelul is small, exact and weary, a man who has read the names of the dead of every household in the settlement and none of the household under it. He wants the keepers received. He cannot go into the cellars, because the warden has forbidden it and because he is afraid of what he will find. **Khorsád-Vahur**, head of the house, receives the party only in the presence of his steward and is courteous in the way of a man who has learned that anything he says is carried to Amradad. A rival house's agent, **Dulmenul**, a plump Tellumi clerk who passes as a cloth-merchant and sleeps in the caravan yard, watches the party and offers them a gift for any tablet.

**Player choices.** Force: threaten Murelul for the whole of what he knows; he gives it, and the settlement's goodwill turns. Stealth: read the copied tablets in his room while he sleeps; they carry the cellar plan. Guile: let Dulmenul believe the party is for sale and learn what the rival house wants. Parley: persuade Khorsád-Vahur that the party can end the knocking without opening the cellars to his rivals.

**Clues and rewards.** The song gives three keys: the oath-words, the boy's name on the shield, and the keepers' line, _we are the keepers, we keep_. Murelul's copied plan shows the way from the breach in the north wall to the record-house stair. The shield of Revunay, with the name cut into the rim, is said to lie where the boy fell, in the gate-court. It is under the floor of the guard-bench.

**Consequences.** If the party takes Dulmenul's offer, he knows their plans and tells his employer. If they betray Murelul, the settlement closes to them.

**If bypassed.** A party that never speaks to the priest finds the same information scratched on the walls of the record-house stair in a hand that is neither Tellumi nor Atárzadi: the keepers' own marks, which say _read us_.

## Scene Three: The Vaults

**Purpose.** Break the party's confidence, spend their resources and show what the Gate-dead is made of.

**Situation.** The record-house stair begins at the breach in the north wall, which the settlers have barred and walled. Below it the old city runs in barrel-vaulted rooms, some dry, some half filled with sand, with floors of baked brick that give way underfoot. The way to the record-house is a long gallery with a floor in three states: sound, hollow and treacherous. In the third room the party finds the keepers.

**NPCs and threats.** **Giant camel spiders** hunt the vaults by feel and vibration, and the pale ones that live nearest the stair are the biggest. The keepers are a line of twenty-two dead, seated on benches along the walls of the record-house with tablets in their laps. They do not decay and they do not move while the party is looking. They are the Gate-dead's mouth. In the dark, in the corner of the eye, a keeper's jaw works. The tablets in their laps are warm.

**Player choices.** Force: burn out the spiders with oil and fire, which also lights the room and shows the keepers plainly. Stealth: go barefoot along the sound brick and keep the lamp down. Guile: carry a tablet out of the first dry room and test the keepers' reaction. Parley: sit with the keepers and read to them, aloud and in the old hand, the first name on the nearest tablet.

**Clues and rewards.** The keepers' laps hold tablets that give each keeper's name. Reading one aloud makes the keeper sit up, bow and become still. Eleven of the twenty-two are named in this way if the party has the patience. The room beyond them holds the great archive: shelf on shelf of baked clay, the debts and genealogies of Vahurdash, worth more than anything else in the town.

**Consequences.** A party that rushes loses time to spiders and a floor that falls. A party that lingers is in the cellars when the sun goes down, and the keepers' stillness does not outlast the light. Tablets taken from the shelves without reading the keeper's name wake the keeper.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the cellars meets the keepers at the second night's gate, when the Gate-dead walks the line of them in the gate-court on its way to the door.

## Scene Four: The Second Night

**Purpose.** Face the Gate-dead, with the party's choices about the keepers and the oath determining its end.

**Situation.** At full dark the knock sounds, three and three, and the voice speaks. The party may open the door, answer through it, wait on the wall or go out by the breach. If the keepers have been named, the Gate-dead is weaker, and the voice at the door is the boy's. If they have not, it is the old man's, and the keepers rise from the record-house and file through the cellars toward the stair beneath the gate-court.

**NPCs and threats.** The Gate-dead has two shapes. In the open it is a man of great size, wrapped in the dust of five centuries, who fights with the butt of a spear and the courtesy of a champion, giving ground and taking none. In the doorway it is merely a voice. Behind it, in the gate-court, the keepers walk when they are unnamed. A rival house's agent, if the party has crossed him, waits at the back of the court with a lantern and the intention of seeing the party fail.

**Player choices.** Force: fight the Gate-dead in the court; arms alone do not end it, because each blow that breaks its shape is taken up again by the oath. Stealth: slip behind it and read the keepers' names from the cellar mouth. Guile: speak the boy's words from the wall, _I keep an oath too, stand off_, and the Gate-dead stops. Parley: answer as the one who has the right, saying _Revunay, Return_ and the name on the shield, and take the oath back.

**Clues and rewards.** The shield from under the bench is in the party's hands or it is not. A party that holds it and speaks the boy's name ends the knocking for good. A party that reads every keeper's name and then speaks the Gate-dead's last word, _enough_, ends it with the keepers at peace. The fire of a Delkoshekh, if one is present, also burns it, though the oath survives the flame and returns in a season.

**Consequences.** If the party fails to end it, the Gate-dead enters and walks a household north, and the settlement is a month closer to emptying. If the party ends it, the dead of the record-house are received and the floors of the cellars hold.

**If bypassed.** A party that flees the town leaves Murelul to try the reading alone. He dies in the attempt or succeeds at the cost of his sight, and the party hears of it from a caravan within the season.

## Scene Five: The Archive

**Purpose.** Pay out the reward, and turn it into a decision.

**Situation.** With the keepers received, the great archive is open. The party has an hour, or a night, with a hundred shelves. The last tablet, laid on a desk in a room without a door, is the prince's own, with a line pressed into its edge by a thumbnail. It says one word in the old hand, and the word is _Paid_.

**NPCs and threats.** Murelul, Khorsád-Vahur and Dulmenul converge on the archive within the hour. Each wants the tablets and each wants them differently. The genealogies show that the House of Vahurdash descends from the household of Berniray and Minesha and not from the princes, a better ancestry than most and not a princely one.

**Player choices.** Force: carry off what can be carried. Stealth: copy the genealogies and leave the tablets. Guile: sell the truth to Dulmenul's house, or to the House of Vahurdash, or both. Parley: bring the tablets to the Council of the Princes openly and let the seven houses read them.

**Clues and rewards.** A few tablets of debt, sound and legible, fetch a good price in the yards of Kethramír: a handful of silver by weight each. The shield of Revunay, with the name on its rim, is a relic the Tellumi will weep over. The word on the prince's tablet reads two ways. The Tellumi who have seen it read it as the prince's debt to the woman at his gate, settled by the boy's half-skin of water. The Atarzád scholars who have heard of it read it as the city's debt to the prince, settled by the sand.

**Consequences.** The genealogies change the quarrel over the House's title. The party's choice decides whether the House keeps it by acclamation, loses it to rivals or is publicly made the descendant of an honorable champion.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves without the archive is paid by Murelul in thanks and a letter to the Shekhul of Kethramír. The tablets stay where they are.

## Aftermath

- **If the Gate-dead is ended.** The Barred Gate opens at night. Caravans reach the rim at dusk and Vahurdash gains trade. The warden sleeps. Murelul begins the reading of the keepers' names in the open and the Tellumi of the belt send delegations.
- **If the keepers are named and the genealogies shown.** The House of Vahurdash keeps its seat but must say what it is. The exiled houses lose their lever.
- **If the tablets are sold.** Dulmenul's house learns the descent of the House before the Council does, and a quarrel starts that outlasts the knocking.
- **If the party fails.** The Gate-dead walks again, the next household goes north, and the settlers begin to leave.
