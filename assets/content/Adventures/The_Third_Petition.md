## Overview {#overview}

A warrior of [[affiliation-wrrscrcldnhrtrbs|the Warrior's Circle]] wears a strip of indigo leather on his left arm, and with it he has sworn the [Old Promise]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}: never to draw on another Dunhari while an outside enemy walks the desert. Twice the Circle has been asked to ride against a Dunhari tribe, and twice it has refused under that oath ([[lore-circlrefusl|The Two Refusals of the Circle]]). It holds that the Promise binds whenever any outside enemy is anywhere in the desert, and the [[lore-sowidesclt|Sovidur]] at the northern wells mean that it nearly always binds.

This spring the riders of [[being-shadznkmrz|Shadezan Kamruz]], a member of the Circle and the storm cults' most prominent vessel, killed two men of the [Soltrev Clan]{.name data-type="affiliation/polity"} at a well while Sovidur raiders stood at the northern wells. [[being-mahvaradparv|Mahvarad Parvaran]], chieftain of the Soltrev, is bringing the widow of one of them to the [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]] for the coming season-turn. He does not ask the Circle to ride against a tribe. He asks it to hold its own member to its own oath.

The party enters as the widow's escort from [[place-parzavad|Parzavad]], as strangers asked to learn what lies behind the petition, or as guests at the shrine when the case is heard.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The deed.** Some Soltrev households stayed under Shadezan's rule when he drove their clan from its pastures. This spring four of them set out by night to join Mahvarad on the plain round Parzavad. Kamruz riders overtook them at a well two days out and turned them back by force, and two Soltrev men died. One of the widows, [Layah]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, slipped away afterward and reached Parzavad with the story and no one to trust.

**Shadezan's answer.** Shadezan holds that a chieftain who turns back his own people at his own wells is keeping his clan, not making war, and that the Sovidur were a week's ride north and threatened no one. He answers for his riders as a chieftain must, and he does not deny the deaths.

**The riders.** Shadezan gave no order. His second son led the riders without his father's leave, to show that he could hold the clan's people as well as his elder brother. Shadezan learned it afterward and took the deed as his own, because a chieftain answers for his riders and because naming his son before the Circle would end the young man's standing in every camp.

**Tavura's reading.** At Shadezan's naming the wind told his mother, [[being-tavurakamruz|Tavura Kamruz]], that he would hold the desert and lose his sons to it. She has told no one, and she reads the wind each spring in the hope that it has changed. She knows which son led the riders. At the spring reading this year she spoke his name aloud, and a Kamruz rider who heard it has talked.

**The count at the pens.** Mahvarad reports fewer beasts at the fair's pens at Parzavad than the Soltrev own, and keeps the rest in a hollow of the plain north of the town, a herd large enough to mount the clan's return. He expects the Circle to refuse him. A refusal spoken at the Stone frees the Soltrev to take the matter into their own feud, as the petitioners of the second refusal did, and the hidden herd is ready for that day. A party that follows the Soltrev riders north finds it mounted and ready in its hollow, and the Soltrev ride from it if the Circle refuses.

**The Circle.** The [Council of Scars]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} is divided before the case is put. Its oldest members say the Circle has refused twice, paid for each refusal and owes nobody a third. Others say a ruling against a member for drawing on his own clan's people indicts half the chieftains in the Circle. The southern tribes' case against [the Unrecited]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} stands undecided on the Stone's list, and the Council of Scars will not frame it until the Circle has answered the Soltrev, so a refusal here is the Circle's third and the Unrecited case is still to come. Everyone knows the Circle needs Shadezan to lead it against the Unrecited and the Sovidur.

**The remedy short of a blade.** The Circle expels nobody, but a member's strip is renewed only by dipping it in the vat at the Stone, at a meeting of the Circle. The Circle can rule the vat closed to a member, and the Voice of the Quarter announces it. A strip left unrenewed for three meetings fades and marks its wearer as having stepped aside, and the Circle punishes without drawing on anyone.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Nobody here is a villain. The scenario ends when the Circle rules or refuses, and when the Soltrev either have their answer at the Stone or take it on the plain.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The widow's escort.** Mahvarad asks a party to see Layah safely out of the Parzavad fair and across the desert to the Stone, because Kamruz men have been seen at the pens.
- **The tagemader's question.** The _tagemader_ of Parzavad sees more armed Soltrev at the fair than any herder needs, and pays a party to learn what Mahvarad intends.
- **Shadezan's question.** Shadezan asks a party to learn who has been feeding the petition, because a widow with no friends does not reach the Stone alone.
- **The storm-shaman's road.** A storm-cult elder bound for the season-turn asks a party to keep the petitioners safe on the road, and to learn what each side would give for a ruling.

## The Opening {#opening}

The stone pens of Parzavad at the spring fair, thick with dust and the noise of sheep. A woman in a mourning veil sits on a pen wall with her hands in her lap, and three riders of the Soltrev stand round her facing outward.

> "She saw it," says the chieftain beside her. "She will say it at the Stone, if she lives to reach it."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Fair at Parzavad

**Purpose.** Meet the petitioner and the witness, and see what the petition is worth to each side.

**Situation.** [[place-parzavad|Parzavad]], a walled oasis town on the open plain of the Three Seas, at the spring fair, when the stone pens hold more beasts than the town holds people. The town holds more armed herders than garrison.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-mahvaradparv|Mahvarad Parvaran]], proud, patient and bitter, who tells a grievance as if it were a prophecy. Layah, who speaks little and exactly. Two Kamruz riders at the horse lines, who watch her and buy nothing.

**Player choices.** Agree to escort Layah, ask Mahvarad what he wants from the Circle, or talk to the Kamruz riders.

**Clues and rewards.** Layah saw the riders' leader, a young man with a Kamruz chieftain's horse-gear, but not his face. The Kamruz riders say they came to buy remounts, which is half true: they came to learn whether the widow reached Mahvarad.

**Consequences.** The party leaves Parzavad with the widow, and both clans know it.

**If bypassed.** Mahvarad sends Layah with his own riders, and the Kamruz treat a Soltrev escort as an armed party crossing their country.

### Scene Two: The Road to the Stone

**Purpose.** The desert crossing, a Kamruz embassy and the law of the well.

**Situation.** Days across gravel and sand to the hill-shrine where three tribal territories meet. On the third evening a Kamruz troop rides to the same well as the party. The [Laws of the Well]{.name data-type="lore/law"} hold, and the two parties water in turn.

**NPCs and threats.** The Kamruz troop's leader, who brings Shadezan's word: a blood-price in camels to Layah's household and to the other, and the Soltrev households free to leave, if the petition is not put. The heat, and the night cold.

**Player choices.** Carry the offer to Layah, refuse it on her behalf, or ask the troop who led the riders at the well.

**Clues and rewards.** One of the troop, angry that his chieftain is offering camels for another man's deed, says that the old woman spoke a name at the spring reading and that he heard it.

**Consequences.** Layah weighs the offer. A blood-price recited in public binds both sides and ends the matter, and Mahvarad has not authorized her to take one.

**If bypassed.** The offer reaches Layah at the Stone in front of the petitioners' camp, where refusing it costs her nothing and accepting it shames Mahvarad.

### Scene Three: Below the Stone

**Purpose.** The fullest shrine in years, and a member to carry the case.

**Situation.** The [[place-stoneofranaz|Stone of Ranaz]] at the eve of the season-turn. The petitioners' camp below the shrine holds the southern tribes with their case against the Unrecited and the herders from the edge of the Pale Floor. The storm cults' [Council of Elders]{.name data-type="affiliation/governmental"} sits on the same days, and it is deadlocked. Foreigners who arrive uninvited are received with courtesy and sent away with nothing.

**NPCs and threats.** [Nevaran]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an old warrior of humble birth, one of the Council of Scars, who says the Circle owes nobody a third refusal. A petition must be put by a member, and he is the one who might put it. [[being-tavurakamruz|Tavura Kamruz]], Shadezan's mother, seventy-eight, who reads the wind for his camp. Shadezan himself, arriving with his riders, greeted by every member he passes.

**Player choices.** Persuade Nevaran to put the case, speak to Tavura, or ask Shadezan for an audience as guests of his fire.

**Clues and rewards.** Nevaran agrees if Layah speaks for herself. Tavura, asked about the spring reading, says only that the wind does not change its mind to suit a mother. Shadezan receives the party, says the deaths are his to answer for, and asks them to tell the Circle so.

**Consequences.** The case is on the Circle's list, ahead of the southern tribes' case against the Unrecited.

**If bypassed.** No member puts the petition, and Mahvarad reads the silence as the third refusal.

### Scene Four: The Voice and the Oath

**Purpose.** The hearing.

**Situation.** The meeting of the Circle at the season-turn. Storm-cult practitioners open it. A Voice of the Quarter is drawn by lot from the marked stones, and about seventy warriors sit in the open below the Stone with the indigo strips on their arms.

**NPCs and threats.** The Voice, who frames each question. Nevaran, who puts the petition. Layah, who says what she saw. Shadezan, who answers that he turned back his own people at his own well while the Sovidur were a week away, and that if the oath reaches that far, half the men listening are forsworn.

**Player choices.**

- _Speak as witnesses._ Guests may speak if a member asks them to.
- _Name the son._ The petition becomes a case against a young man who is not a member, and the Circle has no authority over him.
- _Propose the vat._ The Circle punishes without a blade by closing the vat to a member.
- _Propose an exit._ A blood-price, the households freed, and a formal apology from one chieftain to another, recited at the Stone.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that speaks well earns the notice of the Council of Scars, which is worth more in the desert than silver.

**Consequences.** The Voice announces the ruling at sunset. If the Circle closes the vat to Shadezan, he keeps his place for two more meetings and loses it at the third unless the Circle relents. If it refuses, the refusal is spoken in the words of the first two.

**If bypassed.** The Circle hears Shadezan and not the widow, and refuses.

### Scene Five: The Night After

**Purpose.** What each side does with the ruling.

**Situation.** The camps below the Stone after sunset. The Soltrev riders saddle. The Kamruz riders keep their fires.

**NPCs and threats.** Mahvarad, who either has his answer or has his pretext. Shadezan, who either keeps his strip or watches the vat closed to him in front of every tribe. His second son, who comes to the party's fire in the dark to ask what his father will do to him.

**Player choices.** Ride with the Soltrev to the plain, warn the Kamruz, persuade the son to stand before the Circle, or carry Shadezan's apology to Mahvarad.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that follows the Soltrev riders north is turned back at a hollow of the plain by armed herders.

**Consequences.** A ruling the Soltrev accept ends the feud at the Stone. A refusal sends them back to the plain, and a war on the plain round Parzavad draws in the town's garrison.

**If bypassed.** The Soltrev ride before dawn, and the party hears of the war at Parzavad.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Layah does not reach the Stone, or reaches it with no member willing to put her case. The Circle refuses by silence, Mahvarad calls it the third refusal, and the Soltrev mount and ride against the Kamruz pastures within the season. The fighting crosses onto the plain round Parzavad, the tagemader shuts the fair, and the Kingdom's garrison is drawn into a feud of the tribes. Shadezan comes to the next season-turn with a war at his back, and the Circle that needs him to lead it has a feud of its own making on the plain.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The vat closed.** The Circle has acted against a member without a blade, and every chieftain in it reckons what he has done at his own wells. Shadezan has two meetings to make it right.
- **The exit taken.** A blood-price is recited at the Stone, the Soltrev households go to Parzavad, and Shadezan's apology to Mahvarad is the talk of every camp. The Soltrev go back to grazing the plain.
- **The son named.** Shadezan's second son loses his standing, and his brothers' quarrel for their father's favor sharpens.
- **The third refusal.** The Soltrev ride, and the Circle's oldest members say that the Promise was kept whole and that the price this time is the Soltrev.
