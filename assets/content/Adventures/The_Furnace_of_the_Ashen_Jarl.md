Caught by snow in a high pass of Norgaad, the party climbs to the only shelter in sight: a jarl's fortress over its own iron and silver mines, its chimney smoking into the storm. The household died in a single night, and its dead keep the walls and stoke the furnace, where the jarl himself is bound and still at work.

## Overview

[[place-vruthborg|Vruthborg]] was the odal of **Jarl Brildorv Braldendikh**, a smith-jarl of the [[place-flarnfjall|Flarnfjall]] dying of the forge-cough with no heir to take his forge. A guest no record describes came up the pass and taught him a way not to go: a sealed chamber of runed iron under the furnace, a binding renewed with every life burned in it, and the jarl's own body kept in the heat at the heart of it. When the fuel the staves call people ran short, the jarl fired the furnace through the flues under his own hall, and his household died in their beds. They are _hélthrall_ now, [[lore-undead|Helspawn]] of the driven kind, and they move only when the heat runs through the fortress. Each stroke of the jarl's hammer sends it.

Three facts carry the adventure, and the party can learn each of them in more than one place:

| What the party needs to know                                    | Where it can be learned                                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The dead move with the heat, and stiffen as it ebbs.            | The courtyard soldier that turns on the stroke; the barracks dead; Krildynda; the dead on the walls in Scene Three    |
| The furnace is fed with people, and the binding is in the iron. | The smith's rune-staves; the runed doors under the furnace; Krildynda; the jarl's own words in Scene Four             |
| Fire, then water: the quench-dam can crack the binding chamber. | The miners' rule Krildynda lives by; the smith's staves on the dam and its sluice; the cracked test-iron in the forge |

The cold of the old workings, where the heat never reaches, is the refuge and the alternative way into the foundry.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Tvarnmark silver-buyer knows what Vruthborg's treasury held the winter its tally stopped, and that no one has claimed it.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The district's officer will pay hard silver to a party that goes up the pass road and brings back word of why Vruthborg has sent no tally, so long as he does not have to go himself.
- **The leader.** The jarl's sister's son wants to stand at the ting and claim the odal. He needs proof the jarl is dead, and the ting will want to see the jarl's arm-rings.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer was crossing the pass late in the year, and the snow came early.

## The Opening

The storm has been building since noon. Read or paraphrase:

> Snow closes the pass behind you. In the space of a few breaths the road you came up is gone under it, and the wind is filling the hollow where you stand. Above the trail, on a spur of the mountain, stands a dark fortress, its chimney pouring smoke into the storm. You climb to it because there is nowhere else. The gate is warm under your hand, warm as a living flank. From within comes a hammer stroke, and the stones of the gateway tremble with it, followed by a scream. Then another stroke.

## Scene One: The Frozen Watch

**Purpose.** Get the party inside the walls, and show it that the garrison is dead and still on duty.

**Situation.** The gate is unbarred and swings in on hot hinges. In the courtyard stand twenty soldiers of the jarl's household, spaced as a watch is spaced, every one of them facing the foundry across the yard, every one rimed with ice from boots to helm. The snow has drifted against their legs. The hammer strikes in the foundry, slowly, a long count apart, and each stroke sends a breath of heat across the yard that melts the snow on the cobbles for a moment before the wind freezes it again. The gatehouse is dry and its stores are untouched: barrels of salt meat, meal, ale, cheeses, kindling.

**NPCs and threats.** The **frozen watch** is the household's drengir, dead. As the party crosses the yard the hammer strikes, the heat comes, and the nearest of them turns its head. Ice breaks from its jaw. Its sword has been kept in the furnace, and it glows a dull red and leaves steam hanging where it moves. It takes two steps toward the party, and as the heat ebbs it slows, and stops, and stands again.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Cut down the one that turned, while it is slow. The others turn at the next stroke.
- **Stealth.** Cross the yard between strokes, counting, and reach the gatehouse before the heat comes again.
- **Talk.** There is no one to talk to. The scream from the foundry comes after every stroke, and calling out to it brings no answer but the next stroke.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Keep to the shadow of the wall, where the heat of each stroke reaches last and leaves first.
- **Giving something up.** Go back out into the storm, which is death by morning.

**Clues and rewards.** The watch moves only with the heat. The scream comes after every stroke and is too regular to be a person, until the fourth or fifth time, when it breaks into words. The gatehouse stores keep the party alive for as long as the walls do.

**Consequences.** A party that cuts down a soldier of the watch learns that a body cut through still moves while the heat lasts. A party that counts the strokes has the rhythm it needs for the rest of the fortress.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that climbs the wall instead of using the gate drops into the yard behind the watch and meets the same thing from the other side. A party that runs from the turned soldier into the nearest door finds itself in the barracks, and Scene Two begins there. A party that tries to wait out the storm in the gatehouse is found by the scream, which goes on all night, and by the heat of each stroke, which grows.

## Scene Two: The Smith's Staves

**Purpose.** Show the party what the jarl did, and give it the records that explain the furnace.

**Situation.** The barracks range runs along the inner wall. Its floor is warm underfoot. In the bunks, under their blankets, lie the household's miners and servants, charred where they lay, the blankets scorched from beneath. Some of the bunks are empty and the blankets thrown back. The smithy is a lean-to against the foundry wall, cold, its tools racked. On a shelf over the bench, bound in a thong, are the smith's rune-staves.

**NPCs and threats.** The **barracks dead** lie still between strokes and stir with the heat, and a party that stays long among them is in a room full of the slowly waking. **Krildynda**, a miner, watches the party from the hatch to the workings at the back of the smithy, a lamp shaded in her hand. She has lived in the cold of the old galleries since the night the household died, and she comes up to the smithy for stores between strokes. She talks only once she is sure the party is alive.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Burn the barracks dead where they lie. The heat of the fire makes them rise.
- **Stealth.** Take the staves and leave the barracks before the next stroke.
- **Talk.** Win Krildynda over. She wants out of the mountain, and she wants someone to carry her kin out through a gap in the wall.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Read the warm floors: flues run under them from the foundry, and the hot ones lead to the furnace.
- **Giving something up.** Carry the dead out through a gap cut in the barracks wall, as the rite requires. It is hard, slow work between strokes, and the dead carried out that way do not rise again.

**Clues and rewards.** The smith's staves are the record of **Knurvmýl**, the jarl's smith, cut in runes. The early staves are the ordinary work of a foundry: blooms, bars, axe-heads, a tally of charcoal. Then come orders for a sealed chamber under the furnace, iron-lined, its doors cut with runes the smith did not know, and for a quench-dam of snowmelt above the foundry with a sluice to flood the casting floor. A stave notes, in the smith's hand, that iron heated in the chamber and quenched cracks like glass. The last stave is in another hand, and lists names where the earlier staves keep the charcoal tally. In the smithy, a test-bar of chamber iron lies split along its length.

**Consequences.** A party that carries the dead out lessens what will come at it in Scene Three, at the cost of time. A party with the staves knows what the furnace is fed with and how the iron can be broken.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that never finds the smithy meets Krildynda anyway: she comes up out of a floor-hatch in the gatehouse cellar, where an old gallery runs, because she saw the party's lamps. She knows what the staves say, because the smith read them to her. If the staves are burned or lost, the dam and its sluice can still be seen from the walls, and the chamber's runes speak for themselves in Scene Four.

## Scene Three: The Walls Come Down

**Purpose.** The fortress closes on the party, and the heat rises until staying is not possible.

**Situation.** The strokes come faster. The gate swings shut on its hot hinges and its bar drops. On the walls, the dead of the watch climb down from the wall-walk and come across the yard. The floors are hot now, too hot to stand on for long in the barracks and the hall, and the snow in the yard is gone. The scream after each stroke is close enough to make out a word: the name of someone in the jarl's household, spoken in a man's voice from inside the foundry.

**NPCs and threats.** The **dead of the walls** move with each stroke and stiffen between, but the strokes are closer now and the gaps shorter. **Krildynda** knows two things: that the jarl's body was never brought out of the foundry and that the hammering has gone on since that night, and that the coolest way to the foundry is through the original workings under the fortress, where the heat has never reached and the dead freeze solid. The scream is a living man: the last traveler the dead took off the pass, in the binding chamber, waiting to be fed.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight across the yard to the foundry doors, through dead who quicken with every stroke.
- **Stealth.** Go down into the old workings with Krildynda and come up under the foundry.
- **Talk.** Persuade Krildynda to lead, when every part of her wants to stay in the cold.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Close the flue dampers along the hall and barracks walls, which keeps the heat from those floors and the dead in them stiff.
- **Giving something up.** Hold the yard so that others can reach the quench-dam on the wall above the foundry.

**Clues and rewards.** In the workings the dead that followed the party slow and stop and stand rimed in the galleries. The workings come up beneath the foundry at an old ore-hoist, and the hoist shaft carries the sound of the chamber below the furnace: a man praying, and a hammer, and another voice counting the strokes.

**Consequences.** There is no going back through the gate. A party that went through the yard arrives at the foundry hurt and hunted. A party that went through the workings arrives cold, unseen, and late, because the strokes have quickened while it was underground.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that holes up in the gatehouse is cooked out of it by the floors, or found by the dead when the heat comes, and the only door that leads anywhere cool is the cellar hatch into the workings. A party without Krildynda finds her lamp-marks on the gallery walls, a chalk line she drew to the ore-hoist so that she could listen at it. A party that tries the haulage tunnel early finds it dark and long and, at the far end, a drift of snow it cannot dig through before the cold kills it, and comes back.

## Scene Four: The Furnace

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the bound jarl and the apparatus that keeps him.

**Situation.** The foundry is a high stone hall, red-lit and roaring. The furnace stands in the middle, taller than three people, its iron doors shut, a water-wheel's shaft turning its great bellows. Six dead stokers work at their stations: two at the feed-chute, two at the bellows, two at the doors. Under the furnace, reached by a stair behind it, is the binding chamber, iron-lined, its doors cut with runes, and in it a living man is chained to a ring. Above the foundry, on the wall-walk, stands the quench-dam, a stone cistern of snowmelt with a sluice that opens onto the casting floor.

Each hammer stroke comes from inside the furnace. **Jarl Brildorv** is there, in the heart of the fire, at an anvil the guest set in it, striking.

**NPCs and threats.** The **stokers** defend their stations and leave them for nothing else. **Brildorv** commands them from behind the iron doors, and he speaks: a hoarse, courteous voice, a host's voice, asking the party to stay, offering them the fortress's silver, telling them the furnace is cold without fuel. He believes he is still a jarl. Each stroke he strikes drives a surge of heat through the fortress and quickens every dead thing in it.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Fight the stokers off the feed-chute and jam it, so nothing more reaches the chamber.
- **Stealth.** Come up the ore-hoist into the chamber beneath the furnace, free the chained man, and reach the runed doors from inside.
- **Talk.** Speak to the jarl. A party that tells him his household is dead and names them from the last stave makes him stop striking, for a few strokes, which is time.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Open the sluice of the quench-dam onto the casting floor while the furnace is at its hottest, and let the miners' rule do the work: fire, then water.
- **Giving something up.** Feed the furnace with what it was built to eat, the jarl's own silver and arm-rings and the weapons on the walls, to stall it while someone reaches the dam; or let the chained man go to the fire.

**Met prepared.** A party that knows the dead move with the heat, what the chamber is for, and the miners' rule can do it in order: jam the feed, let the next strokes come, open the sluice. Cold water floods the casting floor and the chamber stair. The runed iron of the binding chamber cracks like the smith's test-bar, the doors split, and the furnace dies with a sound like a breaking bell. Opening it then shows the jarl, tallow-skinned and whole in the heart of the fire, his hammer still raised. Carried out through a gap cut in the foundry wall, he does not rise again.

**Met unprepared.** A party that knows none of this meets the stokers and the heat head on, and the hammer quickens as it fights. It can still survive. The old workings are cold, and nothing that follows it there keeps moving; the ore-hoist is the way down to them from the chamber beneath the furnace. A party that retreats there and waits finds that the strokes come slower when there is nothing left to feed the chamber, and that the dead in the foundry stand still between them, which is when it can come back up for the sluice.

**Consequences.** A party that breaks the binding ends the heat, and every dead thing in Vruthborg stops where it stands. A party that kills the stokers and leaves the binding whole hears the hammer start again within the hour. A party that frees the chained man has a witness for the ting.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party driven out of the foundry has the workings, and the workings lead under every part of the fortress; it can come up again at the ore-hoist, at the gatehouse cellar, or at the foot of the quench-dam's stair, where the snowmelt runs off the wall into the rock. A party that never reaches the sluice can break the dam's wall from below with the miners' own tools, which floods the foundry with the same result, slower.

## Scene Five: The Cold Treasury

**Purpose.** Pay the reward, and give the party a way off the mountain while the pass is closed.

**Situation.** When the furnace dies, the fortress cools fast. Frost forms on the walls of the hall within the hour, and every one of the dead stands where it was at the last stroke. The treasury is a vault cut into the rock behind the hall, barred with iron that is cold now, and its key hangs on the jarl's belt in the fire.

**NPCs and threats.** If the binding is broken, nothing in Vruthborg moves. If it is not, the treasury lies across the yard from the foundry, and the hammer is still striking. **Krildynda** knows the haulage tunnel, and she wants to be gone before anything starts again.

**Player choices.**

- **Force.** Break the treasury bars, if the key cannot be reached.
- **Stealth.** Go in between strokes, if the furnace still burns, and take what can be carried.
- **Talk.** Agree with Krildynda, the freed man and whoever sent the party, the jarl's sister's son or the district's officer, how the treasury is to be divided.
- **Cleverness with the place.** Load the treasury onto the ore-sledges and take them down the haulage tunnel.
- **Giving something up.** Leave the silver as the king's tally owed, and take only what the jarl owed the dead.

**Clues and rewards.** Silver ingots from the Vruthborg workings, stamped with the jarl's mark; the jarl's own gold arm-rings, which the ting will want to see; and on the treasury walls a rack of weapons from Knurvmýl's forge, axes, spearheads and swords of exceptional work. Only those cut with Knurvmýl's mark and the year-rune of the jarl's good winters were made before the guest came. The later ones are warm to the touch, and the dead of the watch carried their like.

**Consequences.** The arm-rings and the freed man's word settle the odal at the next ting. The later weapons stay warm, and a party that keeps one carries a little of the furnace with it.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** The haulage tunnel runs from the lowest gallery of the workings under the pass to a sledge-road below the snow line, and it is the way out whatever happened in the foundry. A party that escapes by it without the treasury can come back up it in spring, when the pass opens, to a fortress that is cold if the binding broke and warm if it did not.

## Aftermath

- **If the binding broke.** The chimney of Vruthborg stops smoking, and the district's officer sends men up the pass road in spring to count the ore and the dead.
- **If the dead were carried out.** Their kin raise stones for them, and the miners of Skulfheim say the Vruthborg workings are silent when they knock.
- **If the jarl still burns.** The hammer goes on, and every traveler the snow catches below the spur climbs to the warm gate.
- **If the guest is sought.** The black runes on the chamber doors are all the trail there is, and the north credits such runes to the [[affiliation-nghtwghtcncl|Nightwight Council]].
