## Overview {#overview}

[[place-batarencity|Bataren City]] is Provénzia's harbor on the cold ocean and its most awkward city. A great part of its people are [[lore-elavendrnppl|Élavendren]], descendants of settlers who came down from [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] centuries before the present crowns. They are Provénzian subjects and not, in any way that matters locally, Provénzian. Since the crown's officers first brought Provénzian law to the city after [[lore-charterwar|the War of the Charter]], the two communities have quarreled over land, inheritance, observance and title. Both crowns treat the quarrel as a Provénzian domestic matter and are careful not to look closely.

This year the quarrel has started to draw blood. An Élavendren merchant was beaten in the fish market, a Provénzian warehouse burned on the waterfront, and a crown notary who refused to register an Élavendren inheritance was found in the harbor. Each community blames the other, and each incident arrives just as the last one was being smoothed over. The crown's officers are more anxious to keep the news from [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] than to find who is responsible.

The party enters carrying a letter north, or in the hire of someone who wants to read it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The escheats.** Under the law the crown's officers apply in Bataren, an inheritance that is not registered with a crown notary does not pass, and the holding falls to the crown. Élavendren custom passes a house to the eldest daughter, or to a sister's son, in ways Provénzian law does not recognize, and the notaries refuse to register them. The holding then falls to the crown's bailiff, [[being-tlvzmlvrn|Tolveze Melvarandis]], who leases or sells it for the crown. For six years he has been buying the best of them himself, through his wife's brother's name, at prices he sets.

**The incidents.** Tolveze needs the notaries to go on refusing, and he needs nobody at Jalnasdal to ask why. A city at its own throat gives him both: the notaries refuse "while the city is unsettled," and the crown's officers keep the news from the capital for fear of being blamed. His agent hires men off the dying farms around [[place-bldfldyharvalen|Yarvalen]], hungry and in no position to ask questions, to beat an Élavendren here and burn a Provénzian there.

**The notary.** The notary in the harbor had refused the Orsel inheritance, a row of warehouses on the north quay that pass by the elders' custom to a sister's son. Under pressure from [[being-kiramailthor|Kirama Iltoris]], the young countess of Bataren, he changed his mind and told the bailiff he would register it the next morning. He was in the harbor by dawn.

**The elders.** The Élavendren elders meet behind closed doors in the house of [[being-hthrnorsl|Hethran Orsel]], whose family's warehouses are the inheritance in question. Their younger men want to answer the warehouse fire in kind. Hethran wants the law, and he has begun to doubt that there is any for them.

**The letter.** Kirama, sitting with the [[affiliation-councilpeers|Council of Peers]] at Jalnasdal, has written to Hethran offering to bring the refused registrations before the high courts at Aurelane as a breach of the [[lore-chrtrrghts|Charter of Rights]]. If the elders accept, the matter goes before the Justiciar, and the bailiff's purchases come to light in the first week of the hearing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The war is one man's business disguised as two peoples' quarrel. The scenario ends when the bailiff is named, the elders choose the court or the street, or the next fire is lit.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The letter.** A patron at Jalnasdal sends a party north with a sealed letter for an Élavendren house in Bataren. Two other patrons pay the party to learn what it says: an agent of the bailiff, and a secretary of [[affiliation-kngdmlvndr|Élavendre]]'s envoy, whose crown would rather know what it is not looking at.
- **The notary.** Kirama Iltoris does not believe the notary drowned by accident and asks a party to learn what inheritance he refused, and for whom.
- **The mercenary.** A Provénzian warehouse owner hires a party to guard his stores on the waterfront after the fire.
- **The wanderer.** A family off the dying farms asks a party to find their son, who took a stranger's coin in the fish market and has not come home.

## The Opening {#opening}

The fish market at Bataren in a cold drizzle. Two gutting tables have been pushed apart with a space between them that nobody crosses. On one side the stallholders speak Provenzal and on the other Élavendri, and both sides watch the party come in.

> "You'll want to pick a table," says the boy who carries their bags. "Everybody does now."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: Two Tables

**Purpose.** Show the city's division and its grievances.

**Situation.** [[place-batarencity|Bataren City]]: the fish market, the north quarter of the Élavendren, the harbor quarter of the Provénzian houses, the burned warehouse and the quay where the notary was found.

**NPCs and threats.** Stallholders of both communities, each sure of the other's guilt. Young Élavendren men who want an answer for the warehouse fire, which they did not set. Hungry men off the farms, idle on the quays.

**Player choices.** Deliver the letter, hear both sides, look at the burned warehouse, or ask about the notary.

**Clues and rewards.** The warehouse was fired from inside, by someone with a key or a bribe. The men who beat the merchant spoke with the flat speech of the farm country, not the city's.

**Consequences.** The party has been seen at both tables, and both sides mark it.

**If bypassed.** The young Élavendren men burn a Provénzian chandlery in answer, and the city is a step nearer open fighting.

### Scene Two: The Elders' House

**Purpose.** The Élavendren side, and the letter.

**Situation.** Hethran Orsel's house on the north quay, behind its warehouses. The elders sit in a room hung with the genealogies of the old families.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-hthrnorsl|Hethran Orsel]], grave and tired. His sister's son, whose inheritance it is and who wants to fight for it. The bailiff's agent, waiting at the door for the party to come out.

**Player choices.** Give Hethran the letter, open it first, tell him what the others have offered for it, or advise him.

**Clues and rewards.** Hethran explains the refused registrations and the holdings that fell to the crown. He has a list of eleven, and he has noticed that all eleven were leased within the year to the same family.

**Consequences.** If Hethran accepts Kirama's offer, the elders hold their young men back. If he refuses, they stop holding them.

**If bypassed.** The elders hear the offer from the bailiff's agent, garbled, and refuse it.

### Scene Three: The Notary's Last Day

**Purpose.** The murder.

**Situation.** The crown's notarial office by the customs house, the notary's lodging, the quay where he was found.

**NPCs and threats.** The notary's clerk, frightened. The crown's officers, who called it an accident and want it left that way. The bailiff's agent and two farm men he pays.

**Player choices.** Question the clerk, search the lodging, watch the bailiff's agent, or follow the farm men.

**Clues and rewards.** The clerk says the notary drafted the Orsel registration the night before he died, and that the draft is gone. The notary's day-book shows a last visit to the bailiff's office. The farm men are paid each week by the bailiff's agent at a tavern on the harbor quarter's back lane.

**Consequences.** The bailiff learns the party is asking and sends his agent to buy or threaten it.

**If bypassed.** The day-book goes into the harbor too.

### Scene Four: The Bailiff's Rolls

**Purpose.** The hand behind the war, and what to do with it.

**Situation.** The crown's bailiff's office: the rolls of escheated holdings and the leases granted on them. [[being-tlvzmlvrn|Tolveze Melvarandis]], affable, at his desk.

**NPCs and threats.** Tolveze, who offers the party a lease of its own. His wife's brother, whose name is on every lease. The crown's officers, who will protect the bailiff until they are sure the countess will not.

**Player choices.** Copy the rolls, take the matter to the countess, take it to the elders, or take the bailiff's offer.

**Clues and rewards.** The rolls show eleven Élavendren holdings fallen to the crown and leased to one family. [[being-kiramailthor|Kirama Iltoris]], given the rolls, can bring them before the Justiciar with the refused registrations.

**Consequences.** Named in both quarters at once, the bailiff ends the war in a morning. Named only to the elders, he is named in the street.

**If bypassed.** The next fire is lit on the north quay, and it is the Orsel warehouses.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The Orsel warehouses burn. The Élavendren young men answer with a Provénzian house, and the crown's officers can no longer keep the news from Jalnasdal. The crown sends soldiers, and the Élavendren of Bataren write to Élavendre. Both crowns are made to look closely at the city they agreed not to look at, and the bailiff buys the burned warehouses' ground at the crown's price.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that named the bailiff has given Kirama Iltoris the case her reformers need before the Council of Peers, that the towns are governed worse by the crown's officers than they would govern themselves, and given the Élavendren elders a hearing before the Justiciar that may settle their inheritances for a generation. The traditionalists in the Council will remember who carried the rolls. Élavendre's envoy will remember who would not sell the letter.
