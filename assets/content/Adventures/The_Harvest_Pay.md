In [[place-velanthrgn|Velanthia]] the Hosts are paid at harvest. The riders who hold the grass, the passes and the toll roads serve a river-prince for grain and silver, and they can walk away; in 697 AF the [[affiliation-hsttrvl|Tarvál Host]] took the port's pay instead of the lowland prince's after a short harvest ([[lore-tarvalturn|The Tarvál Host Changes Sides]]), and every prince has feared a change of sides since. This year the rain came late to the eastern fields. This frame runs from the late rain to the snow on the passes. Through that season every prince must find his Hosts' pay, every Host must decide what its prince's promise is worth, and every barn, ledger and fort in the land is asked to give up what it holds.

## The Situation and the Stakes {#the-situation}

**The short harvest.** The late rain has made a short harvest likely, and every sack is worth more than it was. [[being-zhtvddbrvdch|Zhitovod Dobrovodich]], prince of [[place-zhtvsh|Zhitavesh]], holds the grain, the [[affiliation-hstsvrt|Sevret Host]] and the [[affiliation-hstkhrd|Khorad Host]], and owes the forts of [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]] their grain. He must pay his two Hosts and feed the forts out of one harvest, and he cannot see whether it is enough.

**The count.** The [[affiliation-grnrymnstrs|Granary Monasteries of Lizelas]] hold the land's reserve against a bad year, and the houses want it left uncounted. A house that opens its barn to a prince has shown its price. The prince's keeper wants a count, because in a famine year the villages blame the prince, and the monks have twice refused him.

**The Hosts' price.** Each Host wants autonomy, pay and the right to what the frontier yields. The Tarvál Host lets the prince of [[place-brlvsh|Brelavesh]] hear what the steppe tribes offer it. The Sevret Host holds the passes, and its riders take their own toll there if the harvest pay is late. The Khorad Host has told the river communities to stop paying the [[affiliation-sqrtrtrb|Soqritur]], and the tribe has answered with riders. The three gather at [[place-bnnrfld|Banner Field]] before the harvest to hear one another's price, and this year they must settle whether they answer the tribes together or each alone.

**The houses' quarrel.** The [[affiliation-mrchnthsrvr|Merchant Houses of the Rivers]] buy the harvest before it is cut and lend against the next one. The port houses want to cut the credit they extend to the grain towns, and the grain-town houses call the cut a way to buy their crop cheap. Each prince depends on one side, and none can end the quarrel.

**The forts.** The prince of Brevavesh holds the forts that face the Grukar country, and he cannot feed them. The lowland grain and the port's silver both reach him late, and his wardens sell a night's passage to the fur hunters to make up their pay.

The stakes are whether the Hosts are paid and stay, whether the forts are fed through the winter, whether the barns are opened, and whether the rivalry of the three rivers stays checked for another year.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The frame prescribes no Host that changes sides and no prince that loses one. What the party meets are people who each hold one thing and lack another, and each knows what the others owe. Play every delivery as a message, since a late payment is read as one, and let the table decide what each message says.

## Who Moves It {#who-moves-it}

[[being-zhtvddbrvdch|Zhitovod Dobrovodich]], prince of Zhitavesh
: Heavy, patient and sparing of speech, who counts before he answers. His wealth is sacks, not silver, and the pay he owes is his whole hold on two Hosts.

[[being-mlgryrgrch|Milogor Yarogorich]], prince of Brelavesh
: Smooth and never hurried, who raises a toll in the same tone he lowers one. He has the silver and the fewest soldiers, buys mills upriver, and pays his Host more each year.

[[being-krdgrbrvgrch|Krodogor Brevogorich]], prince of Brevavesh
: Blunt and tired, who says what the garrison is owed before anything else. He has the forts and no grain, and he must trust two princes to keep them.

[[being-vlkrkdgrch|Volkar Kodogorich]], ataman of the Tarvál Host
: Easy, watchful and slow to promise, who laughs at a prince's offers. His Host changed sides once, under his father, and he holds the toll posts on the eastern caravan roads.

[[being-svdnvlktch|Sovidan Volketich]], ataman of the Sevret Host
: The youngest of the three atamans, quick and plain-spoken, who wants his riders paid on the day it is promised so that none takes a toll for himself.

[[being-blknkldv|Belukan Kelodov]], ataman of the Khorad Host
: Dry, patient and courteous, whom the tribes trust. He wants the river communities' toll for his Host and the prince's arrears paid, and each side watches which way he turns.

[[being-rdrbskvnch|Radora Boskvanich]], starbrat of the great granary at Zhitavesh
: Mild, exact and immovable, the mediator both the princes and the Hosts send to first. She wants the barns full when the villages are hungry and no prince's price on any sack.

[[being-tshdrslvnch|Teshodar Sulvanich]], Master of the Houses
: Genial and patient, never the first to name a figure, who presides over the houses' assembly and lends to every prince.

[[being-dvkrdvdch|Duvek Radovodich]], keeper of the princely granary
: Anxious and exact, who wants the monastery's barn counted and the prince told the truth.

[[being-vrklrvgrch|Ovrik Lervogorich]], captain of the forts
: Gruff and tireless, who keeps the wardens' rolls and their pay, and wants the silver in the barracks before the wardens sell another night.

[[being-trvkmvrnch|Torvik Mevranich]], port-warden of Brelavesh
: Neat and fair-spoken, who audits the houses' tolls by ledger and has no soldiers to make them accept it.

The bodies
: The three princes' halls and retinues; the councils of the three Hosts and their clan heads; the heads of the granary houses, who meet when a mediator is needed; the assembly of the merchant houses at Brelavesh, divided between the port and the grain towns; the paid wardens of the forts; the Soqritur, whose riders watch the gathering from within sight of its fires; and the March's customs officers on the far side of [[place-krvgr|Karvogora]].

## The Arcs

The frame follows the season from the late rain to the snow, and each arc turns one part of the reckoning.

1. **The barns.** At Zhitavesh the prince's keeper wants the great house's barn counted, and at [[place-dbrvsh|Dobravesh]] a monastic barn and the prince's granary face each other across one market square. [[place-frrygrnry|The Ferry Granary]], on the river crossing of the grain road, has been asked for a count and for an advance in the same week.
2. **The pass.** At [[place-tvskvsh|Tveskavesh]] the grain road ends at the foot of the range, the port's agents count the wagons that go south, and the monks' wagons go through the gate untolled. [[scenario-lsttrnkrvgr|The Last Train over Karvogora]] runs the last wagon trains of the season over a pass where the Host's riders are owed their pay.
3. **The forts.** The quarter's silver for the wardens waits at the mint in Brelavesh for an escort up the river road, and the forts at Brevavesh watch the forest edge and the Grukar country with fewer men than the prince pays. [[scenario-cnfrfrd|Coin for the Ford]] runs one village's ransom to the ford, and [[scenario-gthsthdbrg|The Gatehouse at Theodburg]] runs the heir of Brevavesh's bid to hold the shared fort again.
4. **The port.** At Brelavesh the port-warden audits the houses' tolls, the houses argue the harvest price, and the master of the houses wants a sealed offer carried to the grain towns without the port houses learning its terms.
5. **The grass.** At [[place-vlkvsh|Volkavesh]] the Tarvál Host feasts the port's envoys, at [[place-krdshv|Krodashov]] the Khorad Host breeds the horses the steppe prizes, and at [[place-smkrv|Semukarov]] a river village is asked to pay two masters. At Banner Field the three atamans sit in the ring and each names his price.

The arcs run in any order, and each moves the reckoning whether or not the party finishes it. A party that carries a delivery between two of these powers carries a message, and everyone who sees it pass reads one.

## Ways In {#ways-in}

1. **For a prince.** A prince's hall hires a party to escort its pay, to ride the eastern fields and report what the ear will yield, or to carry grain to Tveskavesh past the port's agents, and on the road someone asks it to carry something else.
2. **For a Host.** An ataman wants a stranger at a feast who can say afterward what a prince's envoy offered the clan heads aside, a witness to what a river community pays the tribe, or a go-between who carries his pledge to the starbrat.
3. **For the houses.** A merchant house wants a witness to the port-warden's audit, a granary house wants a sealed letter carried before the rains, and the master of the houses wants an offer carried to the grain towns unread.
4. **For the frontier.** The captain of the forts wants the quarter's silver brought up the river road, a village wants a ransom carried to the ford, and the heir of Brevavesh wants companions for the road to the forest.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- The harvest comes in short, the lowland prince's pay to his Hosts comes late, and the Sevret riders take their own toll at the upper pass until the drovers stop climbing.
- The keeper of the granary gets no count, and the prince goes into the winter blaming the monks for barns he cannot see.
- The houses' assembly does not agree the harvest price before the rains, and the grain towns sell to the port at the port's price.
- The quarter's silver reaches the forts late, the wardens sell more nights to the fur hunters, and the posts on the forest edge are thinner than the rolls say.
- At Banner Field each ataman names his price alone, and the princes' envoys outside the ring learn the price each Host would take from someone else.

## The Secret {#the-secret}

**Why the pay is late.** Zhitovod has pledged a third of the autumn tithe to a grain-town house against the silver for his Hosts' pay. A short harvest leaves him owing grain he does not hold, and he has told neither his keeper of the granary nor his Hosts. Teshodar Sulvanich's own house bought the paper on that pledge through a grain-town house. The man who presides over the houses' quarrel holds the lowland prince's debt, and he has told neither the assembly nor the prince.

**What the barns hold.** The reserve that could cover the short harvest exists, but not where it is expected. The barn of the Ferry Granary is nearly empty: the house lent most of its reserve to the merchant houses against the next harvest and advanced grain to the Sevret Host on the same terms. The great house at Zhitavesh holds enough to feed the lowland villages through two bad years, not one, and Radora keeps the figure from the prince, who would tax it, and from the heads' meeting, where a head would borrow it. The network's name for holding the land's reserve depends on nobody checking.

**The Host the princes watch.** Volkar holds a written promise from Milogor of a fixed share of the port's toll on the eastern caravans, kept from the merchant houses, and a larger standing offer from a steppe tribe that shares his roads. He has answered neither, and he takes the larger on the day his pay falls late. The port's pay is not the pay that is late. The Hosts whose pay is in doubt are the lowland prince's two, and Belukan's younger son lives in a steppe camp as the tribe's guest, which keeps the ataman from riding past the river to enforce the toll he demands. The princes fear that a Host which can change princes can also change to a side that is not a prince's at all.

**The forts' rolls.** Ovrik keeps two rolls. A third of the wardens the prince pays are not in the barracks, and their wages feed the bell-ringers, cooks and village watches beneath the forts. The forts are thinner than any prince knows.

This frame does not say which Host changes sides, or whether any does. The referee decides, and may let the party's actions decide.
