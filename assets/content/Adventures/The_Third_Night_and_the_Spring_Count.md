## Overview {#overview}

The [[affiliation-hsenorodari|Norodari]] lose three hulls in the autumn fog. Their insurer refuses all three bonds, saying the masters refuse pilots. The hearing at [[place-sealawhall|The Sea-Law Hall]] precedes the spring census of 720, which decides the next Harbormaster. Strangers hired to find evidence or count hulls discover that neither party’s simple story fits all three nights.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

On the first two nights the pilots’ boat keeps its station and the masters do not hail it. On the third, [[being-knurvhnrvn|Knurvorv Hnirvendik]] calls the boat home early because his son is aboard and the fog frightens him. The third hull is lost in the remaining hours before dawn. He gives the hall a roster showing a full watch. [[being-glurvmilhvr|Glurvmil Hvurnaruk]] is ashore with fever throughout; he is an honest investigator, not an eyewitness.

[[being-vraldkrldd|Vraldselda Kruldidrok]] refuses the bonds on a rule she believes applies and on a loss her syndicate cannot afford. [[being-kubasornrd|Kubasor Norodari]] owes more than his house is worth without them. [[being-knilthtvrn|Knilthorv Tvarnidrok]] pays an arbitrator’s grandson’s apprenticeship fee to influence the third chair, without receiving a promised verdict. None of those facts changes what happened on the approaches.

At the census, [[being-flarnulthd|Flarnthann Ulthidrok]]’s gang has been paid by a Norodari factor to warp three coasters to another berth after counting. Kubasor’s knowledge of that arrangement is not established. [[being-mirvirnlth|Mirvir Nulthumak]] has a separate old sale without proof of payment in his files. Keep the questions separate: bond liability, ownership, duplicate counting and election preference are not one verdict.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The Hall keeps rules and verdicts by recitation. Its written roll records parties and dates only. Do not give the party a conveniently written lawbook or a written verdict hidden in the chest.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- [[being-hrundthrsk|Hrundselda Thraskumak]] hires strangers to walk beside the census takers and record each hull once.
- Kubasor pays for evidence about the pilots’ station before the bond hearing.
- Glurvmil asks the party to help establish the truth of the roster while his guild insists its word should suffice.

## The Opening {#opening}

> Three chairs stand at the end of the hall. Two are occupied; the middle one is empty. Outside, men on both quays shout the names of hulls, and a clerk waits beside a closed roll with his pen dry.

The two chosen arbitrators have not agreed on the third. [[being-thilmrlthm|Thilminda Ralthumak]] can hear the case if both accept her. Meanwhile the Harbormaster asks what the party can establish before the census, without promising the election to either house.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Separate the nights

At the pilots’ house Knurvorv gives the same formal answer for all three losses. Glurvmil compares the roster with the boat’s ordinary routine: in fog it stays out through the night, sounding its horn. A party can inspect the roster openly with the parties’ consent or obtain a copy through its employer. Stealing it gives the hall a document but leaves its custody open to challenge.

**Clues and choices.** Ask who orders a recall and where the boat lies when recalled. The answers give the party a place to test the third night. Knurvorv’s son and the boat’s other crew members all repeat the full-watch claim. Interviewed separately about the recall horn, the berth and the order to come home, they must either sustain that agreement or admit their return two hours before dawn. Glurvmil can mediate those interviews as a fellow pilot. A witness who breaks ranks can give the party the recall’s time, order and landing place to put to the others. Watching the next fog establishes the routine, not the past. Do not let a successful present watch prove all three old entries true.

**If bypassed.** The discrepancy can emerge from the testimony in Scene Three. The roster is a statement to test, not a mandatory stolen object.

### What the light can prove

[[being-snurvdrskr|Snurvinda Druskaruk]] will speak about the lost nights at the Hall, where her word is recognized. She knows the lamps and the weather, not what each master thinks. Glurvmil can show how the Hounds, the station and the harbor mouth relate without claiming to have seen the wrecks.

**Choices and evidence.** Arrange for Snurvinda’s testimony, examine the approaches in fair weather, and compare any crew account of the third recall with the interval before dawn. A master’s departure from Sarmelis cannot prove whether that master later hails a pilot off Kalipsa. A witness from the bay remains useful for the voyage’s departure and condition; the case need not wait on that absent witness.

**If bypassed.** The hall can hear the pilot roster alone, but a confident statement about the light without its keeper is easier to challenge. Glurvmil explains what he knows and states the limits of it.

### The recall and the empty chair

Confronted with the distinction between nights, Knurvorv can admit the third recall. He is trying to save his son’s reputation and the guild’s, not conceal three identical acts. The party can ask for the truth in a private interview, bring the contradiction before the hall, or sell silence. A confession can be carried by a willing speaker; a forged roster never becomes sound evidence merely because it supports the truth.

At the same time Thilminda directs attention to the third chair’s independence. The apprenticeship payment can be established through the payment and the apprentice’s admission; an offer of a benefit is evidence of pressure, not proof of an agreed verdict. The party can disclose it before the chair is filled or let the appointment proceed and challenge it publicly.

**If this scene fails.** No single interview decides the whole case. The separate roster entries, testimony about the spoken recall order and the witnesses’ accounts can be compared at the hearing. The two parties can reject a compromised choice and agree on Thilminda; they need not accept her merely because the party prefers her.

### Hear one loss at a time

At the Hall, each party speaks, witnesses speak, and the arbitrators recite the rules before deciding. With the full evidence, the first two refusals stand and the third bond is payable: no pilot is available to refuse after the recall. The arbitrators distinguish that liability from the officers’ false account and the separate pressure over the chair. Assault or theft goes to the city’s courts, not to this trade hearing.

**Choices and consequences.** The party can testify, withhold what it learns, or correct a false statement even when it harms its employer. Vraldselda can seek a shared payment from another syndicate; Kubasor can negotiate with creditors. One payable bond does not automatically clear all his debts. The GM tracks the agreement the parties actually make rather than awarding the election as compensation.

**If proof remains incomplete.** Give a verdict on the evidence presented and identify the disputed point. A delayed hearing costs time before the census; a false verdict leaves witnesses and obligations that can return later.

### Count the hull, then the shares

[[being-tvilgrlthr|Tvilgorv Rultharuk]] takes the census; Mirvir records it; Hrundselda certifies it. The party walks the berths with an identifying sketch of each hull, its owners and shares. Flarnthann’s warping arrangement is exposed by the same coaster appearing at two berths, not by guessing a docker’s allegiance.

**Choices and evidence.** Stop the second count, have the hull identified before both tallies are entered, or quietly accept the altered total. Thilminda can recite the old winter-purchase verdict that treats a paid-for hull as its buyer’s before its first voyage. That addresses Knilthorv’s proposed rule; Mirvir’s old unpaid sale is a separate ownership question.

> [!WARNING]
> Nobody outside the houses addresses the Council as of right. The party reports through Hrundselda, a Councilor, or the Clerk for the matters his office holds. Correct counting gives the houses their lawful voices; it does not give the party a vote.

**If the party leaves early.** Mirvir receives whatever verified evidence it gives him. Flarnthann’s unobserved move can still inflate a tally. Hrundselda must decide whether she has enough certainty to certify the roll.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The hearing remains vulnerable to the false roster and the bought approach to the third chair. The unpaid bonds weaken Norodari at the census. The dockers’ move can add a false count even while the old houses press an unsupported rule against genuine purchases.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A truthful hearing can preserve the Hall’s credit while breaking a guild master’s account and straining the insurer. A clean census can still elect a party the adventurers dislike. Record separately which bonds are paid, which hulls belong to which houses, and which promises the party makes to obtain its evidence. Those decisions supply the next voyage’s employers and enemies.
