Every Helionite city-state sends athletes and a herald to [[place-gamessanct|the Sanctuary of the Games]], and the first act of the meeting is the heralds' oath at the altar, where one herald speaks the season's truce words and the rest swear to them. This season the herald chosen to speak them is found dead on the altar steps at dawn on the morning the Games open, with no wound on him. A herald's person is inviolable, so whoever killed him has broken the oldest oath the city-states keep, and the Sanctuary holds seven delegations: two principal rivals, one seatless and sore about it, and four that want only to go home. The bench of arbiters has no army and no power to order a city-state. It has the days of the Games to name the guilty, and a name nobody believes is worth less than no name.

## Overview {#overview}

The adventure runs from the discovery of the body at dawn on the first day to the closing of the Games on the sixth. The priests rule that a death on the altar steps stains the altar, and that nothing is sworn or inspected at a stained altar or in its portico, so the oath is postponed and the Games open at noon with no truce sworn. The bench gives its finding once, at the closing. The delegations stay only as long as they believe the bench can find who broke the oldest oath. When they stop believing it, they go home armed.

**The rule of the altar.** This season the priests hold that a hired hand answers for no one, and that a death at the altar is answered only when the person who paid for it is named. If the bench's finding names that person, the priests cleanse the altar at the closing, the heralds swear, and the Games end under the truce. If the finding names only a hired hand, only a city-state, or no one, the altar stays stained and the heralds leave without swearing.

The Games run six days in this scenario; the Game Master may lengthen or shorten them and keep the same steps.

This is a murder with a political shape. The party can find out how the herald died, who was with him that night, who paid for the hand that did it, and which city-state that person serves. It cannot find a name for the hand itself, because nobody in the Sanctuary knows one. What the bench does with the partisan who paid, and how his city-state answers for him, is the end of the story.

**What each body wants:**

Every other city-state at the Sanctuary wants the Games to go on and its own delegation unaccused. The priests of the Sanctuary serve no city-state and want the altar clean and the tablets undisturbed. Kostaros is a competitor and a suspect, not a rival of the two: it has no seat on the bench and nothing in the scenario puts it in the portico on the night.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The herald.** One herald is chosen each season to speak the truce words at the altar, and this season it is a herald of one of the four city-states outside the quarrel, as the Game Master chooses (see What the Game Master Decides). The priests also asked him to witness the inspection of the disputed oath-tablet, set for the morning the Games open, and to keep the night before it in the portico beside the tablet. A herald's person is the gods' at the altar, and a death there is not a quarrel between two cities. It is an attack on the thing every delegation came to swear.

**The hand.** In the night a person the herald took for a pilgrim keeping a vigil came into the portico and handed him a libation, and the cup was poisoned. The poison is slow. Before dawn the herald went out to wait at the altar for the inspection, as the partisan expected, and died on its steps. That person is the hand, and nothing in this scenario identifies the hand. Where it is now, and whether it knows whom it served, are left to the Game Master.

**The partisan.** The hand was paid and let into the portico by a partisan of one of the two principal rivals, [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]] or [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]], as the Game Master chooses. Call the city-state he serves the partisan's rival, and the other the framed rival. The partisan is a person of the partisan's rival's patron families or its delegation's train, and nobody the notes name. For whom he acts is a choice for the Game Master: for himself, for a patron family, or on his city-state's orders.

**Why this herald.** The partisan's rival claims that the framed rival's oath-tablet is forged, and the partisan fears the inspection will find the tablet true and turn the shame of a false claim on his own city-state. The witness's death on the altar steps stains the altar, and the inspection waits on a cleansing that needs a finding the partisan means the bench never to reach. The claim stays hanging over the framed rival, and the frame lays the murder on the framed rival as well.

**The frame.** The partisan lays two pieces of false evidence. He has the hand follow the herald out before dawn and, once the herald has fallen, leave a dedication on the altar steps beside him, bearing the mark of the framed rival's patron families, copied from a votive in the portico, with the dedicatory line half erased so that it reads as a hurried offering of thanks for a deed done. And he lets it be known in the camps that the envoys of Kostaros were out of their own camp that night, which is true, because they were arguing with the bench's clerk. Both pieces are meant to be found.

**His slip.** For this season the priests shut the portico at night and let in only the witness and those who pay a lamp-fee for a vigil. Each delegation's servants carry a lead tally stamped with the delegation's device, which the priests accept for lamp oil and note in the lamp ledger by its device, and which the valley's sellers accept on the delegation's account. The partisan borrows a tally from a servant of his own train, who is told only that a pilgrim keeping a vigil needs to be let in and that the master asks it as a kindness, and gives it to the hand. The priest who sold the oil saw a plain cloak in the lamplight and no face, wrote the tally's device in the ledger, and thought nothing of it. The device is the partisan's rival's, not the framed rival's. It is the one piece of evidence that points at the partisan's own city-state, and nobody arranged for it to be found.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The truth is a chain, not a name. The party reaches the partisan through what the Sanctuary's own priests, ledger and portico show, and never reaches a name for the hand. Do not give the hand or the partisan a name during play. Give the party roles, tokens and the places where the trail turns, and let the table's accusation end at the partisan.

### How the reveal lands

The party can hold two marks at once, and they disagree. The dedication carries the framed rival's mark, and the ledger carries the partisan's rival's device. The dedication was laid to be found. The ledger was written without anyone knowing it would matter. Set side by side, the mark someone wanted seen and the mark nobody noticed point at different city-states, and the one nobody noticed is the true one.

The reveal has two halves, and the party may find them in either order:

- **The herald was poisoned in the portico by a hired hand.** The body, the cup, the pilgrim and the lamp ledger show it, and none of it names a person.
- **A partisan of one principal rival paid for it.** The device in the ledger leads to the partisan's rival's train, and the train's servants to the one who lent his tally. The servant names the person who asked him to lend it, and that person is the partisan. The dedication then reads as a frame, since it bears the other rival's mark.

A party that brings only the first half gives the bench a murder without a master. A party that brings the dedication gives it a city-state's mark, which the framed rival's herald denies and the ledger contradicts, and the bench does not name a city-state on a mark. Only a finding that names the partisan cleanses the altar. The party decides what goes before the bench.

> [!TIP]
> Let the party reach the partisan through the servant and the tally, not through a confession. The best version of Scene Four has the partisan keep his composure and say, with the bench listening, that a servant's lent tally proves a servant's carelessness and nothing about his master.

### What the party wants afterward

Once it knows, the party holds what the bench cannot get by itself. The bench wants the guilty named without a war. The priests want the altar clean. The seatless want to be heard at last. The partisan's rival wants to be told before anyone else, and what each of them does with the party depends on whom it tells first.

## What the Game Master Decides {#what-the-game-master-decides}

The scenario fixes the shape of the murder and leaves these choices to the table:

- **Which rival the partisan serves.** [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]] and [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] are the principal rivals this season, and the clues serve either. For Athenikos, make the partisan a client of the patron families and the delegation's train a scholars' and merchants' one, and let the questions be about ledgers and guests. For Korinthea, make him a person of the temple's circle or a patron family, and let the questions be about honor and the judges' reputation.
- **For whom the partisan acts.** This choice does not change which ending the table reaches; the rule of the altar decides that. It sets how the partisan's rival answers for him. Asked before the bench (Both halves told), his city-state gives him up if he acted for himself, disowns him as a private man if he acted for a patron family, and defends him and denies the finding if he acted on its orders. Given the night first (The partisan's rival told first), it hands him to the bench if he acted for himself, and otherwise sends him home and disowns him.
- **Which city-state the herald served.** Any of the four city-states outside the quarrel, which excludes Athenikos, Korinthea and Kostaros. The herald is never named; the party meets him in the body and in what the priests say of him.
- **What the herald carried.** The truce words are the season's proclamation, and the Game Master decides whether he carried them in his head, on a tablet, or in a form his city-state's assembly gave him. The clock only needs that they are not spoken until the closing.
- **The disputed tablet.** The Sanctuary's notes name only a city-state claiming another's oath-tablet is forged. This scenario assumes the two city-states in that quarrel are the two rivals, with the partisan's rival making the claim against the framed rival's tablet, and assumes the priests set its inspection for the morning the Games open. If the Game Master prefers another pair or another hour, the herald's duty as a witness changes to match. The scenario also assumes the priests' arrangements for this season, which are not the Sanctuary's standing custom: the witness keeps the night before the inspection in the portico beside the tablet; the portico is shut at night to all but the witness and those who pay a lamp-fee for a vigil, with the delegations' stamped tallies accepted for the fee; and a death on the altar steps stains the altar and its portico, which the priests cleanse only on a finding that names who paid. A Game Master who changes one of these keeps the chain by giving the hand another way into the portico at night, or another reason the finding must name the payer. Whether the tablet is in truth forged is left to the Game Master, as the Sanctuary's own hook leaves it.
- **Who the hand is, and where it went.** Nothing here names or limits it. A traveler who has left the hills, a servant who is still in a delegation's train, and a pilgrim who is in the guest-house are all supported by the clues. Whatever the Game Master makes of it, the bench names it only as a hired hand.
- **Whether Dumares Rafiros comes to the Sanctuary.** The High Judge of the Temple of Árdavon is consulted in Korinthea on matters of law and civic honor and is known for holding every party to its oath. If he comes, he arrives on the third day as the bench's guest and sits at its side in Scene Four, where he asks the partisan's rival's herald to swear to whatever answer that city-state gives for the partisan. If the partisan serves Korinthea, the High Judge holds his own city-state to that oath before the bench. If the partisan serves Athenikos, the High Judge's presence lets Athenikos call the finding a Korinthean one, and the bench must rest it on the ledger and the servant, not on him.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The courtier: a herald's voice.** The herald of a city-state outside the quarrel asks the party to find out who killed the man who was to speak the words, because the herald who speaks them in his place swears with the same altar behind him.
- **The warrior: guards for an oath.** A priest of the Sanctuary hires the party to stand watch on the portico and the altar through the Games, and to say what it sees to the bench and to no delegation.
- **The trader: a petition.** An envoy of [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]] wants the party to carry the petition the bench has refused to hear, and says the Games depend on whether it is read. The petition now has a murder on top of it.
- **The scholar: the tablet.** The priests cannot inspect the disputed tablet while the altar is stained, and the witness they chose is dead. They ask the party, as strangers to every city-state, to read the tablet in the portico, which is no inspection, so that something can be said of it before the closing.

## The Opening {#opening}

Read or paraphrase:

> The valley of the Sanctuary is full on the last evening before the Games. Each city-state has pitched its camp on the ground its road reaches without passing through another's, and the smoke of seven kinds of cookfire lies across the stadium in one gray sheet. The priests are lighting the lamps in the portico behind the altar, one by one, and the oath-tablets show and go dark between them.
>
> At dawn a priest's cry carries across the stadium, thin and high. By the time you reach the altar, a ring of heralds in their wool cloaks has formed round the steps, and no one stands within an arm of what lies on them.

> [!TIP]
> Open on the ring of heralds, and on the cord the priests are stretching round the altar. The silence at the altar is what the scene needs.

## Scene One: The Altar at Dawn

**Purpose.** The shock. Put the party at the body on the first morning, and let it meet the three bodies that quarrel over it.

**Situation.** The herald lies on his back on the altar steps in his cloak, with his staff beside him and a small dedication on the step by his hand. There is no wound. The priests of the Sanctuary have drawn a cord round the altar and let no one within it, and they take the dedication into their keeping unread, for the bench. The bench of arbiters convenes at the head of the stadium within the hour and postpones the oath, since nothing is sworn at a stained altar. The events of the Games begin at noon without it. The priests keep the portico shut for the rest of the day while they watch over the body.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The priests of the Sanctuary**, who keep the altar and the tablets for no city-state. They say plainly that this season they cleanse the altar only when the bench names who paid for the death, and not before.
- **The heralds**, of every city-state, in a ring, silent. The herald of the dead man's own city-state wants the body carried home before the bench sits.
- **The bench of arbiters**, whose members are not named here, who want a finding before the delegations arm.
- **Envoys of Kostaros**, who have come to the altar to be heard and are the first to be shouted at.

**Player choices.**

- _Examine the body from the cord:_ the lips are dark, and a healer or an apothecary the priests let through says poison, in drink.
- _Ask the priests:_ what the herald was doing in the portico, and whether anyone paid for a vigil there that night.
- _Watch the ring:_ see which herald asks to carry the body home before the bench sits, and which does not.
- _Watch the cord:_ the dedication lies inside it, too far off to read, and the priests carry it away.
- _Stand by the seatless:_ the envoys of Kostaros are shouted at, and one speaks to the party.

**Clues and rewards.** The poison. The priests' word that one lamp-fee was paid for a vigil in the portico that night. The fact that the herald kept the night in the portico as the witness to the tablet's inspection this morning. The sight of an offering by the body that nobody may read.

**Consequences.** The bench hears the party briefly and asks it to report by the closing. The seatless envoy asks the party, quietly, to find the guilty before the bench decides it was Kostaros.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that misses the dawn is met by a priest with the same facts, a body already covered, the dedication already taken in, and a bench already sitting.

## Scene Two: The Portico and the Tablets

**Purpose.** The scene of the crime. Let the party read the portico as the priests read it.

**Situation.** On the second day the priests open the portico to the party. It is where the oath-tablets of the city-states hang, one to a city-state, each in its own script. This season it is shut at night, and the priests keep the lamps and let in only the witness and those who pay a lamp-fee for a vigil. The herald kept the night here beside the disputed tablet, as the priests asked of him. The dedication is with the bench, under its seal, and the priests do not show it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **A priest of the Sanctuary**, keeper of the lamps, who remembers every request for oil and is unwilling to guess.
- **Pilgrims** lodged in the Sanctuary's guest-house, some of whom were awake.
- **The delegations' servants**, who pass through the portico on errands and notice more than their masters do.

**Player choices.**

- _Search the portico:_ a libation cup rinsed and set back among the temple's, away from where the priests set their own.
- _Read the lamp ledger:_ one lamp-fee for the night, paid with a tally whose stamped device the priest copied beside the entry. Asked, he says whose device it is. He saw a plain cloak in the lamplight and no face.
- _Inspect the tablet:_ the disputed tablet shows nothing the party can settle, and the priests will not say it is forged or true until the altar is clean.
- _Question the pilgrims:_ one saw a person in a plain cloak carry a cup into the portico after it was shut, and cannot say more.

**Clues and rewards.** The cup. The ledger entry and its device, which is the partisan's rival's. The herald's night in the portico. A description of a person, unnamed, that fits a hundred people in the valley.

**Consequences.** The party has a poisoner and a stamped device and no name for either. The partisan learns that the portico has been searched.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that does not search the portico hears the ledger entry on the third day, when the bench asks the priests for every record of the night and the keeper of the lamps reads the entry and its device aloud.

## Scene Three: The Delegation Camps

**Purpose.** The rivalry. Take the party among the camps, where the principal rivals and the seatless are a few paces apart.

**Situation.** On the third day the camps of [[affiliation-athenikos|Athenikos]], [[affiliation-korinthea|Korinthea]] and [[affiliation-kostaros2|Kostaros]] are armed and watching one another over the open ground, and the other delegations have drawn together between them. A bench member goes from camp to camp asking every delegation to answer for its people on the night, and carries the dedication to the framed rival's camp for its herald to answer for it.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The Athenikan envoys**, who answer every question with a counter-question and offer their records.
- **The Korinthean envoys**, who answer in the language of honor, and ask the party whose oath it is swearing on.
- **The envoys of Kostaros**, who say they did nothing and have the bench's clerk to say where they were.
- **The partisan**, as the Game Master has chosen, in the train of the partisan's rival, courteous and helpful, the most useful man in the camp to a party with questions.
- **The servant**, of the partisan's rival's train, who lent his tally and is frightened that it is being asked about.
- **[[being-dumarsrfrs|Dumares Rafiros]]**, if the Game Master has brought him, arriving as the bench's guest. His reputation for holding every party to its oath makes his presence the one every delegation watches.

**Player choices.**

- _Follow the device:_ take the device from the ledger to the train that carries it, and ask its servants whose tally was out of his hands that night. They point to the frightened one, and he names the person who asked him for it.
- _Read the dedication:_ in the framed rival's camp, the bench member shows it. It carries the framed rival's mark and a half-erased line, and the framed rival's herald denies laying it. The mark matches a votive in the portico.
- _Press the seatless:_ the envoys of Kostaros give their account of the night, which nobody has asked for, and send the party to the clerk.
- _Court the partisan:_ he offers to help and means to learn what the party knows.

**Clues and rewards.** The servant and the tally. The dedication and its half-erased line. The clerk's confirmation, which clears Kostaros. A witness to put before the bench.

**Consequences.** A party that has the ledger device, the servant and the dedication has a partisan, and a frame laid against the framed rival. The delegations learn that someone is asking, and one of them sends word to the bench that the party is a danger.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that does not go to the camps hears the same people on the fourth day, when the bench takes statements in the portico from the envoys, from the bench's clerk, and from the servants who carry the device in the ledger. The frightened servant is among them, and the dedication is shown there.

## Scene Four: The Bench Names the Guilty

**Purpose.** The decision. Bring the party before the bench with whatever it has, and let the Sanctuary's custom and the party's proof meet.

**Situation.** On the sixth day, the closing of the Games, the bench of arbiters sits in the portico before the tablets, with the heralds in the ring and the delegations at the foot of the altar steps. This is the day it gives its finding.

**NPCs and threats.**

- **The bench of arbiters**, who want a finding that the seven city-states will not make a war of.
- **The priests of the Sanctuary**, who say what the portico showed and no more, and wait on the finding to cleanse the altar.
- **The partisan**, whose composure is the scene's chief obstacle.
- **The delegations**, who are armed.

**Player choices.**

- _Tell it all:_ the poison, the cup, the ledger device, the servant, the dedication and the partisan, before the bench and the delegations together.
- _Tell the first half:_ the poisoning and the hired hand, with no payer, so the bench can call the killing the work of a hired hand and keep the quarrel between city-states from the altar.
- _Tell the partisan's rival first:_ go to the partisan's rival on the night before the bench sits, give it the night to deal with him, and bring the proof to the bench in the morning.
- _Say nothing:_ let the bench rule on its own evidence.

**Clues and rewards.** None left. The reward is the finding.

**Consequences.** What the bench names, by what it has heard:

- **Both halves told.** The bench finds that a hired hand killed the herald and names the partisan as the man who paid for it. It names no city-state as the author, and asks the partisan's rival to answer for the man. The priests cleanse the altar and the heralds swear the truce at the closing. The partisan's rival answers as the Game Master has decided: a city-state that gives him up or disowns him leaves the Sanctuary in order, and one that defends him leaves in a standoff. Proof brought to the bench in private on the fifth day reaches this finding too; the bench keeps it to itself, so the partisan's rival first hears of it in the portico at the closing.
- **The first half only.** The bench finds a hired hand and names no payer, and holds the killing a crime against the altar with no master it can reach. The altar stays stained and the heralds leave without swearing, the partisan walks home with his delegation, the bench accuses no city-state, and nobody is satisfied. A party that adds the dedication to the first half gains nothing by it, since the bench does not name a city-state on a mark.
- **The partisan's rival told first.** The partisan's rival hears the truth the night before the bench sits and acts before it does. If the partisan acted for himself, his city-state hands him to the bench. Otherwise it sends him home in the night, and its herald disowns him before the bench as no man of theirs. The bench names him as the payer on the party's proof, the priests cleanse the altar, and the heralds swear at the closing.
- **Nothing told.** The bench finds on the priests' account alone: the herald was poisoned, by a hired hand it cannot name, and it names no payer. The altar stays stained, the heralds leave without swearing, the delegations leave the Sanctuary armed, and the seatless are blamed in every camp by morning.

**If bypassed, failed or cut short.** A party that does not reach the bench is not called; see If the Party Does Nothing.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

- The bench gives its finding on the priests' account: the herald was poisoned, by a hired hand it cannot name, and it names no payer.
- The altar stays stained.
- The envoys of Kostaros are blamed in the delegations' camps, since they are loud and have no seat to answer from.
- The partisan's rival says the Games are ruined by the seatless.
- At the closing the heralds leave without swearing the truce, and every herald who crosses a field after it carries the dead herald's unsworn oath on his own staff.
- The next meeting at the Sanctuary opens with seven delegations that suspect one another and a bench that is asked for its finding on this year's death before it is asked for anything else.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **If the partisan was named.** How his city-state answered decides how far the party's account carries. Given up, he is in the bench's hands, and the account stands in every city-state. Disowned, he is at home and out of the bench's reach, and the account stands at the Sanctuary and nowhere else. Defended, the account stands at the bench and is denied in his city-state, and the principal rivals stop speaking at the altar.
- **The hand.** The bench names it only as a hired hand, and every delegation in the Sanctuary keeps a servant at the portico's door at the next meeting.
- **If Kostaros was cleared.** Its envoys owe the party a hearing at the Sanctuary, and the bench's refusal to seat them looks smaller for a season.
- **If the Games closed under the truce.** The herald who swears at the closing swears in the dead herald's memory, and the priests hold the inspection of the disputed tablet on the morning after the closing, once a new witness has kept the night beside it.
- **If the Games ended unsworn.** The delegations go home armed, the truce for the meeting is not proclaimed, the disputed tablet stays uninspected, and the next season opens with every city-state unsure that a herald is safe on its field.
