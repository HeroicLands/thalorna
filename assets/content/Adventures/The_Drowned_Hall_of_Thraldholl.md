A jarl who could not put down his drowned sons has learned the names of the sea from a [[lore-undead|Helspawn lord]], and his sea-cave hall now holds the drowned of a whole coast at the oars of a ship that never sails. At midwinter the lord comes to collect what the jarl owes. The party goes in on the ebb to take the dead out, or to take what the jarl has gathered.

## Overview

Under the stones of the cape, half a day's row from [[place-vrathavan|Vrathavan]], the sea-cave of [[place-thraldholl|Thraldholl]] opens at the lowest ebb. Inside, Jarl [Svilthmil]{.name data-type="being/npc"} keeps a ship on a cradle of whale-ribs, and at its oars he keeps the drowned: his three sons, the crews of lost Vrathavan ships and the bodies the coast gave up to him as it gave up the rest. He calls them by the names cut on the stones above the cave. His teacher is [Thilmorv]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, the Reeve of Quiet, a [[being-tereb|nightwight lord]] of the [[affiliation-nghtwghtcncl|Nightwight Council]]. The price was a hall of the dead, and Thilmorv comes on the longest night of [[lore-jol|Jel]] to take delivery.

All of this is [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] working on the drowned, and on a grieving man who would not let them go.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Vrathavan ship-chandler says a southern factor's strongbox went down off the cape three winters back and was never found. A drowned man's skiff, the fishermen say, was seen going in at the cave with something heavy. The chandler wants the box and will pay for it.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The shield-maiden of [[place-shieldhall|the Shield-Hall]] needs three hard people to go down to the cape. A crew-bond she sealed was broken when a crew's dead were taken from their stones, and the bereaved are asking her what became of them. She cannot send her own warriors, who are sworn to the town.
- **The leader.** A skipstjori whose brother drowned with his ship and whose stone has been defaced by his own kin wants his crew's dead brought home for the grave-ale they never got. He will pay in silver and the loan of his longship.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer's brother is a name on the cape. Late at night, a voice outside the wanderer's door, in the brother's speech, says the wanderer's own name.

## Scene One: The Ebb-Door

**Purpose.** Get the party into the cave, and show them what the sea gives back.

**Situation.** The cape is a ridge of black rock crowned with a hundred carved stones. At the lowest ebb a mouth clears the water below the cliff, and a skiff pulls out from it with two rowers. On the cape the stones stand in rows with a name at each foot, and a few have been defaced by kin. The mouth stays open for an hour.

**NPCs and threats.** The two rowers are **hafnár**, the drowned dead, with their eyes open and the water running out of them when they turn their heads. They row and do not speak. The ebb is the other guardian: after the hour the vault fills to the roof, and the sea does not care for anyone inside it.

**Player choices.** Force: kill the rowers at the mouth and take their skiff, which alerts the cave. Stealth: climb down the gully behind the stones to the ledge above the mouth, and drop in at the ebb. Guile: wait with a lamp in the doorway of a boat and make the rowers think the party are their own dead. Parley: read a name off the nearest stone and say it to the rower. The hafnár answer to names.

**Clues and rewards.** The names on the stones are the handles the jarl pulls. A party that copies down the names of the stones before going in has them to use in Scene Two.

**Consequences.** If the skiff is killed, the cave knows there are intruders. If the party goes in with the ebb and no time to spare, it has an hour to come back.

**If bypassed.** A party that reaches the cave by boat on the lowest tide of the next day finds the same mouth, with the skiff already moored.

## Scene Two: The Oars

**Purpose.** Let the party speak to the dead, and learn what the cave is for.

**Situation.** The great vault is lined with lamps. The ship on its whale-rib cradle stands in the middle, with its oars manned by the drowned, who sit stiff and wet with their eyes open. There are more than thirty. A woman at the third oar turns her head as the party passes. Her name is [Skilfrinna]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, and she is a Vrathavan shipmaster's daughter, drowned in the autumn. She has kept more of herself than the others, because her stone was never raised.

**NPCs and threats.** The hafnár do not move against the party while the jarl is at his table. Skilfrinna speaks in the voice of a person underwater. She tells the party that the jarl calls them by their stones and that the ones with no stones, the stoneless, are the ones he cannot call, so they are the ones who can still speak. She wants someone to speak her name at her mother's hearth. The jarl's living servants, six thralls from a failed stead, bring the lamp-oil and keep their eyes down.

**Player choices.** Force: attack the oars and break the crew's bond to the ship, which costs the party hurt and wakes the jarl. Stealth: move among the dead without speaking to them, and reach the stair. Guile: call a drowned man by a name from the stones, and ask him to row for the party. Parley: promise Skilfrinna her grave-ale and ask her to open the way.

**Clues and rewards.** Skilfrinna tells the party that the stair behind the high seat leads up to the headland and the jarl's rooms, and that the Reeve comes on the longest night by a ship of his own. A drowned man can be called back to himself by his name spoken at the right oar, and then he can be asked to leave.

**Consequences.** A party that speaks the names frees some of the drowned from the jarl's call. They will not row for him again, and the cave grows restless. A party that leaves the oars alone may leave them to the jarl.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes straight to the high seat hears Skilfrinna's voice behind it, calling for someone to speak her name.

## Scene Three: The Flood-Lamps

**Purpose.** A trick of the cave, and a setback that forces the party onto its own wits.

**Situation.** The tide turns early. A deep bell sounds somewhere under the rock, and the water begins to rise in the vault. The sea-door is closing. The jarl's rooms are above the high seat, and the only dry way up is a lamp-shaft cut through the cliff to the headland, where oil is hauled down for the lamps. The lamps along the shaft burn a pale flame that draws the eye.

**NPCs and threats.** The water is the threat, and the lamps. A person who gazes into the flame sees their own face drowned, with weed in the hair, and cannot look away. The hafnár do not hinder the climb. They watch from the oars, and some of them sink beneath the rising water and stand on the floor of the vault below.

**Player choices.** Force: climb fast and ignore the lamps, which exhausts the party. Stealth: put out the lamps one by one with a cloak, which hides the party from the jarl's eyes in the shaft above. Guile: haul the thralls up with the party, who know the shaft and can keep a hand on the rope. Parley: ask the thralls the way, and take the shortest rung.

**Clues and rewards.** The thralls tell the party that the jarl has not eaten at his own table in three winters, and that the Reeve is expected on the longest night. One of them carries a key to the jarl's strongroom.

**Consequences.** A party that stays in the vault drowns. A party that climbs arrives at the jarl's rooms exhausted.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves the cave by the sea-door before the tide turns comes back on the next ebb, and the jarl has had time to prepare.

## Scene Four: The High Seat

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the sorcerer-jarl and the Helspawn lord.

**Situation.** On the longest night of Jel, a ship with no sail comes into the cave on the flood and the Reeve of Quiet walks across its gunwale into the hall. Thilmorv looks like a courteous old steward in a gray cloak, with his hands folded and his eyes lowered, and the cloak of his living face frays in the lamp-light. The jarl rises from his high seat. Behind him, at the oars, the drowned stir.

**NPCs and threats.** Svilthmil is a tall cold man the color of tallow with a jarl's ring-mail rusted to him. He is not mad. He is a father, and he is willing to pay any price for the dead he has. Thilmorv is the Reeve of Quiet, a tereb of the Council, polite as a clerk and without mercy. He holds a ledger of hazel tally-sticks. He needs the drowned of the cave for the Council's debt, and he is willing to leave the jarl alive to be useful or kill him to be tidy.

**Player choices.** Force: attack the Reeve in the lamp-light, where the cloak of his life frays and his Helspawn nature shows. Stealth: cut the tally-sticks and burn the ledger while he talks. Guile: offer Thilmorv something he wants more than the cave, a sacrifice or a secret. Parley: stand before the jarl and speak the names of his three sons, and ask him to let them go.

**Clues and rewards.** The jarl's sons sit at the first oars. If the party speaks their names truthfully, the drowned stop rowing, and the jarl has to choose between his sons and his bargain. The ledger records the Council's other debtors.

**Consequences.** A party that frees the sons breaks the jarl's craft, and the Reeve leaves with nothing but a ledger and a grudge. A party that kills the jarl leaves the drowned bound to Thilmorv. A party that destroys the ledger destroys the proof of the bargain.

**If bypassed.** A party that flees the cave before the Reeve arrives hears a month later that the longest night went as the jarl and Thilmorv planned.

## Scene Five: The Strongroom

**Purpose.** Pay the reward and reveal the Council's reach.

**Situation.** The jarl's strongroom lies off the high seat, behind a door of whale-bone. It holds the wreck-goods of three winters.

**NPCs and threats.** If Thilmorv escaped, he reaches the headland and sets the hafnár on the cape's stones. If the jarl lived, he follows the party to the door.

**Player choices.** Force: carry the chests out through the lamp-shaft. Stealth: take only the ledger and leave the goods. Guile: sell the goods back to the owners' families at a fair price. Parley: give the goods to the shield-maiden and let the Shield-Hall divide them among the bereaved.

**Clues and rewards.** Amber, whale ivory, barrels of rendered oil, a chest of Vylarian Ardini from a southern factor's ship, and the arm-rings of three drowned captains. It is a modest hoard and an ill-gotten one. The ledger, if found, names a Vrathavan captain who has promised the Council a crew of his own.

**Consequences.** The goods belong to the families of the drowned. A party that keeps them is a grave-robber twice over in the eyes of the coast.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves without the goods hears of the strongroom from the thralls within the month.

## Aftermath

- **If the sons were freed and the ledger burned.** The jarl dies on the ebb, a man at last, and the bereaved of Vrathavan raise their stones again. The cave floods and does not clear.
- **If Thilmorv escaped.** The Council has a new debtor, and the hafnár that remain are his.
- **If the drowned were freed.** The kin of Vrathavan hold a grave-ale on the cape. The Shield-Hall seals a new bond in the party's honor, and the shield-maiden asks them to find the captain named in the ledger.
- **If the party failed.** The cave keeps its hall, the Reeve leaves with the jarl's debt paid, and the [Jel-Ride]{.name data-type="lore/spirit"} has more riders than the coast can name.
