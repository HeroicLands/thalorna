## Overview {#overview}

In summer the raiding canoes come down the slow rivers out of the Grukar country of [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm Region]], and the village beneath [[place-trvgrfrt|Torvogor Fort]] lives by the bell on its hill. The garrison is owed months. The quarter's silver has come late again, and [[being-vrklrvgrch|Ovrik Lervogorich]], captain of the forts of the [[affiliation-prncdmbrvvsh|Princedom of Brevavesh]], sends Torvogor's share up the river road from [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]] with whoever will carry it.

At the fort [[being-krvgrlrvkv|Karvogor Lervikov]] keeps the bell, the roll and the watch on the landing at [[place-zvekbregad|Zvek Bregad]], some hours from the walls, where raiding canoes come ashore. [[being-brlndvshv|Brelana Duvashov]]'s flat boat is drawn up below the fort with salt and iron. [[being-skgrdbrshv|Sokogor Dobrashov]], the rider of the [[affiliation-hsttrvl|Tarvál Host]] whom [[being-vlkrkdgrch|Volkar Kodogorich]] lends to [[place-sdrvdfrt|Sedrovod Fort]] each season, has come down to Zvek Bregad from Sedrovod and found a track at the marsh edge that stops at a bank with no crossing. [[being-brvnbrvkv|Brevana Brevekov]], the village's healer, packs her bag every evening against the bell.

The party enters as the escort of the silver, as passengers on Brelana's boat on its last landing, or as strangers whom Sokogor asks to follow his track. The work is defense: the village must come through a raid with its people.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The roll.** The silver pays Torvogor's garrison by the roll the prince reads each quarter. Ovrik keeps a second roll, which shows that a third of the wardens the prince pays are not in the barracks; he pays their wages to the bell-ringers, cooks and village watches beneath the forts, because the silver comes late and the grain later. The men who can turn out of Torvogor's hall are fewer than the names the party's silver pays.

**The dusk watch.** On most dusks the one man the village sends to the landing is a boy of twelve, because the men are in the fields. Karvogor writes the watch on the roll as a man's and has told the captain of the forts nothing different. When canoes come down at dusk, the boy must see them and carry the word to the hill, hours away, before the fire is lit.

**The garrison's habit.** Every time the fire burns, Karvogor turns the garrison out and marches it to the landing, and Brevana follows with her cart for the wounded. The fort and the village beneath it are emptiest in the hours after the fire is lit.

**The hidden walk.** Sokogor's track that stops is a nest's path: a line of logs laid under the water along the bank, a cause-walk no outsider can see, which runs from above the landing to the fields below the fort. The nest's parties have taken penned stock along it all summer, which is the loss Brelana has heard of. Nobody at Torvogor knows it is there.

**The raid.** The nest's raids come the way they always come. The canoes land at Zvek Bregad, where canoes have always landed, and a few [[being-grukaruk|Grukar-Uk]] stay with them. The party itself, a [[being-grkrhshk|Grukar-Hushk]] at its head and a few dozen Uk behind it, takes the nest's walk along the bank toward the village fields. Nothing in it is aimed at the garrison. The harm lies in the garrison's own habit: the fire sends every warden to the landing, and the village is emptiest of spears when the raid reaches it.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The Grukar in this scenario are the danger and nothing else. They raid, take food, stock and people, and fight; a person they carry off is eaten. Nobody bargains with them, and nothing they do is a message to anyone. Their numbers and their fighting are on the linked bestiary notes.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The mercenary or soldier: the quarter's silver.** Ovrik wants Torvogor's share carried up the river road and paid at the fort before the wardens sell another night.
- **The thief or the scout: logs under the water.** Sokogor wants a party to follow a track at the marsh edge to the cause-walk he cannot see.
- **The leader: the healer's cart.** Brevana wants a party beside her cart when the bell rings, because a raid leaves more wounded than one woman can bring up to the fort before dark.
- **The wanderer: what the villages say.** Brelana has heard at the villages along the bank that stock goes from pens near the water with no canoe mark on the mud, and she wants someone to tell the warden what the forts do not hear.

## The Opening {#opening}

The hill beside Torvogor Fort in the long light of a summer evening. The bell hangs on its frame at the crest with the signal fire laid beneath it, and the river bends away toward the forest. Below the stone walls the village is coming in from the fields, and a boy with a stick over his shoulder is walking the other way, toward the water and the bend.

> The warden counts the silver onto the table twice, and then looks at the roll. "It is the quarter," he says. "It is not the months." He sweeps the coins into the box. "They will take it, and they will ask where the rest is, and I will tell them what I told them last time."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: Pay-Day in the Hall

**Purpose.** Deliver the silver, see the garrison, and see who goes down to the landing.

**Situation.** The garrison's hall inside the stone walls on the afternoon the silver arrives. Karvogor pays each man on the roll in turn, from the box the party carried, while the men who are owed most stand at the back. Brelana's boat lies below the fort, half unloaded. At dusk the village sends its watch down to the landing.

**NPCs and threats.** Karvogor, gruff, tired and fair, who says little until he has seen how the party treats his men. The wardens, paid a quarter and owed the months, and short-tempered with it. Brelana, dry and practical, who asks the party where the marsh has moved before she asks their names.

**Player choices.** Watch the pay, count the men in the hall against the roll, talk with Brelana, or walk down to the landing with the watch.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that counts finds fewer wardens in the hall than names on the roll, and the warden sets the missing names' coin in a second pile without a word and pays the bell-ringer, the cook and the village's watch from it. The watch that goes down to the landing is a boy. Brelana says that the villages along the bank have lost pigs and sheep from pens near the water this summer, with no canoe mark on the mud below them, and that the forts never hear such things. A party that asks Karvogor what he does when the fire burns hears that he takes every man to the landing, every time.

**Consequences.** The garrison is paid in part and grumbles in full. The party knows the garrison is thin, and may know that the watch is a boy and that the fort empties when the fire is lit.

**If bypassed.** The silver arrives with the party or without it, and the first evening passes quietly.

### Scene Two: The Track That Stops

**Purpose.** Find the hidden walk.

**Situation.** The next morning, on the marsh edge above the landing. Sokogor has found prints in the mud of the bank that come out of the reeds, go down to the water and stop. There is no ford, no log and no canoe mark.

**NPCs and threats.** Sokogor, silent, exact and unhurried, who answers only with what he has seen and cannot follow a track onto ground he cannot read. The bank, where a step off the hidden logs is a step into deep mud.

**Player choices.** Wade along the bank feeling for footing, cut a pole and probe, follow the prints back into the reeds, or go back to the fort with what Sokogor has found.

**Clues and rewards.** A pole finds logs a hand's depth under the brown water, laid end to end along the bank toward the fort. Among the bank prints are broad, splay-toed ones in an orderly file, and one set of huge clawed prints deeper than an ox's. Sokogor reads them as a raiding party that has walked this way and back without coming near the fort, and says that a party that comes by water does not need a path under it unless it means to come where nobody watches.

**Consequences.** The party knows that a path runs along the bank from above the landing toward the village.

**If bypassed.** Sokogor tells the warden that a track stops at the bank, and the warden, who has a garrison to pay and a roll to keep, does nothing with it.

### Scene Three: Dusk at Zvek Bregad

**Purpose.** The canoes come down, and the watch must carry the word.

**Situation.** The low bank at Zvek Bregad on the second evening, with the forest behind it and the marsh upstream. The boy is on the watch. In the last light the war-canoes come down the river and are drawn up on the bank in plain sight.

**NPCs and threats.** The boy, frightened and fast, who knows only that he must reach the hill. A few Uk by the canoes, who stay with them and do not come up the bank. The rest of the canoes are empty.

**Player choices.** Run with the boy, send him and stay to watch, count the canoes and the Grukar beside them, or go down the bank toward the hidden logs.

**Clues and rewards.** There are far more canoes than there are Grukar beside them. A party that knows about the hidden walk can see that the raiders the canoes carried have already gone along the bank. A party with Brelana's boat can carry the word down the river faster than the boy can run.

**Consequences.** The word reaches the hill early, late, or carrying the truth about the empty canoes.

**If bypassed.** The boy runs, and reaches the hill in the dark.

### Scene Four: The Bell on the Hill

**Purpose.** The fire, and the garrison's habit.

**Situation.** The fire is lit and the bell rings over the village. Karvogor turns the garrison out for the landing, as he does every time the fire burns, and the men who are in the hall take their spears down. In the village Brevana loads her cart.

**NPCs and threats.** Karvogor, who knows the river, the landing and the raiding paths, and who is about to empty his fort the way he always has. Brevana, brisk and plain-spoken, who never lets a wound wait and means to follow the garrison down the road. The wardens go where the fire sends them, and the fire sends them to the landing.

**Player choices.** Persuade Karvogor to stay and hold the gate himself while the garrison goes to the landing, go down to the landing with the garrison, stop Brevana's cart, or bring the village in.

**Clues and rewards.** Karvogor stays at the gate with Sokogor if the party gives him a reason he can see: Sokogor's logs, the empty canoes or the pens that lost their stock. He will not stay on a stranger's guess, and he sends the garrison to the landing either way, because the fire is lit. Brevana stays if someone tells her where the wounded will be.

**Consequences.** The garrison goes down to the landing. Karvogor and Sokogor go with it or stay at the gate. Brevana's cart goes down the road, stays, or goes with an escort.

**If bypassed.** The garrison marches for the landing, and Brevana follows with her cart.

> [!WARNING]
> The garrison goes to the landing whatever the party does, so the village has only the party, and whoever of Karvogor and Sokogor stayed, between it and the raid. Let Brevana's cart on the empty road make the cost plain before the last warden leaves the gate.

### Scene Five: The Village beneath the Walls

**Purpose.** The raid comes where the watch is thinnest.

**Situation.** Full dark. The raiding party comes up off the bank into the village fields below the fort: a Hushk at its head and a few dozen Uk in a file behind it. The village's stock is penned and its households are at their doors, listening to the bell.

**NPCs and threats.** The Hushk, who wades in without let-up and does not plan beyond the fight in front of it. The Uk, who do not stand once their Hushk is dead. A fight against the whole raiding party in the open is one a seasoned party does not win; at the gate, with Karvogor and Sokogor beside it, it wins at a cost.

**Player choices.** Bring the village up inside the fort's walls, hold the gate with Karvogor and Sokogor if they stayed, put the children in Brelana's boat on the open water where a raiding party on foot cannot follow, go for the Hushk, or pull the logs out of the hidden walk behind the raiders.

**Clues and rewards.** Kill the Hushk and the Uk scatter back toward the bank. A village brought inside the walls in time loses its stock and keeps its people. A walk with its logs pulled leaves the raiders to go back through the mud or along the open bank, where the garrison returning from the landing meets them.

**Consequences.** The raid takes what it can carry and goes back along the hidden walk to the canoes, or breaks and runs.

**If bypassed.** The raid takes the village's stock and whoever is outside a door, and is back at the canoes before the garrison returns from the landing.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The silver comes up late with another escort. At dusk the canoes come down, the boy runs and reaches the hill in the dark, and the fire is lit. Karvogor takes the garrison to the landing and finds empty canoes and a few Uk, who push off when the wardens come. Brevana goes down the road with her cart. The raiding party comes up the bank into the village, takes the penned stock and the people it finds outside their doors, and is gone along the hidden walk before the garrison comes back. At the next quarter the prince asks how a fort with a full roll lost a village's people, and the second roll and the boy on the watch come out together.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The village kept.** The people are inside the walls when the raid comes, and the raiders go home with stock and nothing else.
- **The Hushk dead.** The Uk scatter, and the raid breaks. The wardens tell the story at every fort on the frontier for a year.
- **The walk found.** With the hidden logs known, Karvogor sets a watch on the bank as well as the landing. The walk runs upstream toward the nests whose canoes use the landing, and which nest that is nobody at the fort knows; finding it, and stamping it out, is the work the prince's silver does not pay for.
- **The roll read.** If the party tells the prince why the garrison was thin, the captain of the forts answers for his second roll and the warden for his boy. Neither of them hires the party again.
- **The track ignored.** If the raid got in, the village beneath the fort sends no one to the landing for the rest of the summer, and Karvogor writes the watch on the roll anyway.
