## Overview {#overview}

[[place-gharzadez|Gharzadez]], the barred fort, holds the western end of [[place-marchroad|the March Road]] on the eastern shore of [[place-mirrorsea|the Mirror Sea]]. Here the trains coming out of the [[place-dunhardsrtrgn|Dunhara Desert]] give up their tribal escorts to the riders of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], and here the Kingdom buys the Vedyaran grain and salt that feed the fort. A train crosses the desert in stages, each under the escort of the tribe whose country it is crossing, and the last stage into the town is the one in dispute. The riders of [[being-shadznkmrz|Shadezan Kamruz]] and of [[being-khavarivarak|Khavari Varakan]] each claim it, and while they quarrel the caravans bound east wait inside the walls.

This season a wagon train of grain has reached the handover well a day east of the gate, where the escort that brought it across the desert has turned back. Kamruz riders camp on one side of the well and Varakan riders on the other, and each refuses to let the other take the train in. The fort's granary is low, and the kinless of the desert are circling the camp.

The party enters as hired guards for the train, as the garrison's messengers to both camps, or as strangers whom a chieftain trusts to carry an offer.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The claims.** Both claims have grounds. Khavari's clan has ridden the last stage for as long as it has held the edge south of the sea, and her lands touch the road there. Shadezan's confederation rides more escorts than any other and wants the whole road, because he holds that a road with two escorts is a road with a gap in it. Neither can take the stage without the other losing, and neither can afford to be seen to lose it.

**The price.** Khavari's escort is cheaper than Shadezan's, and her trains always arrive. The caravan-masters hire her for both reasons and ask no further.

**The fort.** [[being-bashvarddarv|Bashvard Darvani]], who commands the garrison, wants the train in before the grain runs out, and he wants a shared escort that neither chieftain has to call a defeat. The town's _tagemader_ wants every caravan-master to pay the Kingdom's escort for the last stage instead, and each raid argues his case for him.

:::secret {title="Khavari's tithe"}
The kinless band that raids the last stage of the road pays a tithe to Khavari's camp in exchange for water and a place to sell its plunder. Her escorts always arrive because the raiders she shelters spare her trains. The band is about thirty riders under a hard, quiet man named [Eskaram]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, and it strikes Kamruz pickets and unescorted trains and leaves Varakan riders alone. If the party accuses her before the camps and the accusation holds, her escorts stop arriving.
:::

**The kinless.** Eskaram's riders were cast out by their own kin and are claimed by none. They are owed no water, no hospitality and no vengeance, and they live on the gap between the two escorts. What they want is not plunder but a claim: a tribe, or any power, that will take them in. They turn back when [[being-delnshfrdn|Delanash Ferudan]] rides out from the fort, because they know her story, a tribeless girl taken onto the wall, and they say among themselves that she is what a kinless rider could become.

:::secret {title="Razashar's tally"}
[[being-razasharperu|Razashar Perudan]], a captain of the garrison, writes down each order of Delanash's that a Dunhari warrior would not have given and sends the tally each month to [[being-zarvashkaver|Zarvash Kaveran]], an elder at [[place-delmavad|Delmavad]], who reads it aloud to the conservative elders. This month's rider was waylaid by Eskaram's band on the way to Delmavad, and the packet lies in the plunder at Khavari's camp. Carried to Delanash, the tally splits the garrison. Carried to Zarvash, it reaches the elders on time. Carried to Bashvard, it settles the order of his successors.
:::

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Neither chieftain is lying about the claim. The scenario ends when the train comes in or is lost, and when the last stage has an escort that both camps can live with or a quarrel that ends in blood.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The caravan-master's train.** [Tirashan Zandun]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, an Amradi caravan-master of Tahrodan, has thirty wagons of grain and salt at the handover well, with their teams, and no escort he is allowed to hire. He pays a party to ride out and bring the train in by any road.
- **The shared escort.** Bashvard Darvani wants a party to carry the Kingdom's offer of a shared escort to both camps and to bring back what each would take.
- **Shadezan's offer.** Shadezan Kamruz sends a party to Khavari's camp with an offer: the last stage for his riders, and a share of every fee for hers.
- **The face on the road.** Delanash Ferudan asks a party to learn what the kinless say of her, because the story that guards the road is the one her enemies use against her.

## The Opening {#opening}

The yard inside the desert gate of Gharzadez, an hour after dawn. Loaded wagons stand in long lines, their teams in the traces and going nowhere. A gray officer with a ledger under his arm walks along them counting.

> "Twelve trains waiting to go out, and the one we need cannot come in," he says. "It is a day's ride away, and two sheikhs are sitting on it."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: Inside the Walls

**Purpose.** Show the waiting caravans, hire on, and learn the shape of the dispute.

**Situation.** Gharzadez is full. Caravan-masters bound east crowd the yards, paying for water and fodder by the day while they wait for the chieftains to settle who escorts them. The garrison's granary is down to weeks.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-bashvarddarv|Bashvard Darvani]], dry and exact, who keeps the fort's accounts himself. Tirashan Zandun, who has a train at the well and silver tied up in it. [[being-razasharperu|Razashar Perudan]], a young Dunhari captain, who offers to ride out with the party as an outrider. The tagemader's clerk, who reminds every caravan-master that the Kingdom's escort is always for hire.

**Player choices.** Take Tirashan's silver, take Bashvard's offer to the camps, or ride out with Razashar.

**Clues and rewards.** Bashvard says that Khavari's trains always arrive, and that his patrols find no tracks after a raid because the raiders ride south into the dunes. Razashar, asked about Delanash, praises her soldiering and goes quiet.

**Consequences.** The party rides for the well with the garrison's word, with a caravan-master's purse, or with both.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the well with no letter from the fort, and neither camp has a reason to hear them.

### Scene Two: The Handover Well

**Purpose.** The stranded train, two camps that will not yield, and a raid at night.

**Situation.** A stone well in a gravel hollow a day east of the gate, with the train's wagons drawn up round it and their teams picketed. The eastern escort turned back when its stage ended. Kamruz riders camp on the north side of the hollow under [Farzahan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a kinsman of Shadezan's. Varakan riders camp on the south side under [Gidana]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a lean woman who answers every question with a smile. The Laws of the Well hold, and neither side draws a blade within sight of the water.

**NPCs and threats.** Farzahan, proud and blunt, who says his chieftain's riders hold more of the road than any. Gidana, who offers to take the train in at half Farzahan's price and promises that it arrives. The train's drivers, who want to go home.

**Player choices.** Bargain with each camp, try to lead the train in under the party's own escort, or wait for the night.

**Clues and rewards.** At night the kinless strike the Kamruz pickets and cut out six wagons with their teams from the train's northern line, and do not come near the Varakan fires. In the morning a dead tribal rider lies in the gravel where the raiders passed, a garrison horse's brand on his saddle and an empty dispatch pouch on his belt.

**Consequences.** Farzahan accuses Gidana's riders of riding with the raiders. Gidana laughs and says the kinless go where the lance is weakest.

**If bypassed.** A party that leads the train in at once draws both camps after it, and the kinless strike the column on the road while the riders argue.

### Scene Three: The Outer Gardens

**Purpose.** Follow the raiders' trade and meet the elder who opposes the fort.

**Situation.** [[place-delmavad|Delmavad]], round the sea on the western shore, where palms and gardens stand on a line of fresh springs and the tribes water their herds at the outer springs under a compact with the Kingdom. The kinless raid the outer gardens at night.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-zarvashkaver|Zarvash Kaveran]], a pure-blood elder of the tribes at the outer springs, who will not let the garrison patrol the springs the compact gives the tribes. He offers the party his salt and his case against the garrison, which promotes by merit instead of blood.

**Player choices.** Eat his salt and guard the gardens without the garrison's colors, track the raiders, or ask him what he expected from the dead rider.

**Clues and rewards.** A raider taken in the gardens says the band sells what it takes at a camp south of the sea, where the open dunes begin. Zarvash, if pressed about the dead rider, admits that he was waiting for a packet from the fort and will not say what was in it.

**Consequences.** A guest who eats Zarvash's salt is bound to him for the stated days, and he asks for that time at once.

**If bypassed.** The party follows the raiders' tracks south from the well instead, and reaches Khavari's camp knowing less.

### Scene Four: The Sorcerer's Camp

**Purpose.** Khavari's hospitality, her captive and the plunder.

**Situation.** Khavari's camp on the desert edge south of the Mirror Sea: black tents, a deep well, horse lines and a market of goods with no owners present. Khavari receives the party with salt and bread.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-khavarivarak|Khavari Varakan]], patient and ironic, who speaks lightly of power she holds seriously. A young Kamruz rider taken on the edge, held in her tents, whom she will exchange on terms. Her riders, who are fewer than Shadezan's and know it.

**Player choices.** Deliver whatever offer the party carries, ask to see the market, or bargain for the captive.

**Clues and rewards.** In the market are the six wagon teams cut out at the well, still in the train's harness, and a garrison dispatch packet addressed to Delmavad, its seal unbroken. Khavari makes the party an offer of her own: she dares Shadezan to settle the last stage by a contest of champions instead of clans, and she wants the party to carry the challenge.

**Consequences.** A party that names the plunder in her tents breaks the peace of her salt. A party that keeps quiet carries the challenge and the knowledge together.

**If bypassed.** The party never learns where the raiders sell, and Gidana's offer at the well looks like the only sense in the desert.

### Scene Five: The Champions at the Well

**Purpose.** The climax at the handover well, where the stage is decided.

**Situation.** The handover well, three days after the party first came. Shadezan accepts Khavari's challenge, because he cannot be seen to refuse a fair fight, and sends a champion. Both camps gather at the hollow. The train is still there, short of fodder. Garrison riders from Gharzadez watch from the ridge, and Eskaram's band watches from farther off.

**NPCs and threats.** The two champions. Farzahan and Gidana, each sure of the outcome. Delanash Ferudan, if the party has sent for her, at the head of the garrison's riders. Eskaram's band, which strikes the Kamruz horse lines while every eye is on the fight.

**Player choices.**

- _Let the contest run._ The winner's clan takes the stage, and the loser's clan owes the winner a grievance.
- _Accuse Khavari before the camps._ If the accusation holds, Khavari is shamed in front of both, Farzahan's riders draw on the kinless, and Shadezan claims the whole road.
- _Bring Bashvard's shared escort._ Each camp rides half the stage and takes half the fee, under the garrison's eye at the gate.
- _Treat with Eskaram._ The kinless want a claim more than plunder. A party that offers them one, the garrison's pay or a tribe's adoption, takes the band out of the gap.

**Clues and rewards.** A train brought in earns Tirashan's purse and the garrison's grain. Bashvard pays the party in silver and in the fort's goodwill on the road.

**Consequences.** Whatever the party does decides who rides the last stage until the next season-turn at least.

**If bypassed.** The contest runs without the party, Eskaram's band takes half the train in the confusion, and the fort starts rationing.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The two camps sit at the handover well until the train's fodder runs out. The drivers unhitch the teams and turn them loose rather than watch them die, and Eskaram's band takes them. The fort's granary runs short, Bashvard rations the garrison, and the tagemader writes to [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]] asking for authority to make every caravan pay the Kingdom's escort. The caravans inside the walls go out in a large train under garrison riders, and both chieftains take it as an insult.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **Khavari accused.** Her clan's standing on the edge falls and Shadezan's riders take the whole last stage. The smaller tribes at the common council at [[place-ghavulavad|Ghavulavad]] take it as proof that his terms for the Kingdom's compact are coming true.
- **The shared escort.** Both camps ride the stage, the fees are split at the gate, and the tagemader's clerks count every one. The kinless still live on the gap, which is narrower.
- **The kinless claimed.** Bashvard takes Eskaram's riders into the garrison's pay, or a tribe adopts them, and the last stage goes quiet. Zarvash Kaveran tells every elder at the outer springs that the fort now enlists outcasts.
- **The packet.** The dispatch packet from Khavari's market is worth more than the wagons. Where the party carries it decides who learns what it holds: Delanash, Zarvash and Bashvard each answer it differently.
