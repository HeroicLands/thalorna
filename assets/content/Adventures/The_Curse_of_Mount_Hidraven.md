## Overview {#overview}

[[place-hydravenmnt|Mount Hidraven]] is the source of the kingdom's water and the most sacred mountain of the [[affiliation-aelndntrbs|Áelendan Tribes]], who let shepherds and pilgrims use it by arrangements older than the kingdom and turn back anyone who comes to survey it. The crown has never pressed the point, because every argument it could make about sovereignty is worth less than the water.

This spring the foothill villages below the mountain are frightened. Shepherds who came down from the lower pastures tell of bad dreams on the high ground, two flocks have sickened, and a pilgrim who went up the customary way came down without speaking. The village priests are divided over whether the mountain is angered or something on it is wrong, and the Áelendan have set a closer watch on the paths to the springs.

The party enters on any of three errands: the villages', the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s, or a quiet one from the Chancellor's household. [[being-orntstrs|Orneta Sateris]], the pilgrim who came down silent, knows the whole of it and has sworn to say none of it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The survey.** Three Provénzian surveys of the watershed have been turned back. The fourth went up in disguise. Last spring [[being-zdmdrvs|Zedime Diravis]], the Order's magister at [[place-batarencity|Bataren City]], sent a young fellow of the Order and two assistants up the customary way among the first pilgrims of the year, with instruments packed under pilgrims' bread. Orneta Sateris, a mule-guide of Bataren, led them for good money and asked no questions. Zedime's writ for the work carried the crown's oversight, and the Chancellor, [[being-velzoreaitv|Velzore Aitevis]], had sealed it with the great seal.

**The spring.** The surveyors reached the great spring on the mountain's shoulder, measured it, and dug at its mouth to find the throat it rises from. Their mule broke a leg in the diggings and they cut its throat there. The carcass lies in the spring's mouth under the spoil, and the stream that waters the lower pastures has carried its foulness down ever since. That is what is killing the flocks.

**The Áelendan.** The watchers on the paths found the party at the spring. They took the instruments, struck no blow, and walked the lowlanders down. The young fellow slipped his escort at the snow line to take one last measure, and the cold took him. He lies in the ice above the spring with his satchel, and the satchel holds his notebook and the sealed writ. The Áelendan let Orneta go on her oath that she would speak of the mountain to no one, and she has kept it to the letter, which in her case means speaking to no one at all.

**The dreams.** Every shepherd who has slept above the spring this year has dreamed of a man in the ice calling for his mule. Whether the dreams are the mountain's or the dead man's, nobody in the scenario can say. They stop when the spring runs clean and the body comes down.

**What each party wants.** The Áelendan want the spring cleared and the cairn the surveyors raised thrown down, by the people who brought the harm, and nobody on the mountain with an instrument while it is done. Zedime wants the notebook, which holds the first measures of the springs anyone has taken. The Chancellor wants the writ back unread. If the Earl of Degavel's party learned that the Chancellor had sealed a survey of Hidraven and broken the arrangement that keeps the kingdom's water, the [[affiliation-councilpeers|Council of Peers]] would have the weapon it has wanted against him for a year.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The curse is a fouled spring and a dead man. Nobody on the mountain is a villain, and the Áelendan are not enemies. The scenario ends when the spring runs clean or the villages give up the lower pastures, and when somebody holds the satchel.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The villages.** A foothill priest, tired of arguing with the other priests, pays a party to go up the customary way and see what is on the mountain.
- **The Order.** Zedime Diravis hires a party to bring down "a colleague's effects" from the high pastures, and says no more than that.
- **The seal.** A clerk of the Chancellor's household finds a party in Bataren and asks it to recover a dead man's satchel without opening it, for a fee that is plainly too large.
- **The guide.** Orneta Sateris's sister asks a party why Orneta came down from the mountain and has not spoken since.

## The Opening {#opening}

A foothill village at dusk, the flocks folded early in the lowest pasture. A shepherd sits by the fold with his crook across his knees and will not go back up.

> "Every night the same man," he says. "Up in the white, calling for his mule. I'm not the only one. Ask them."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Fold

**Purpose.** Show the fear, the flocks and the stream.

**Situation.** A foothill village of stone folds and steep fields, under the shoulder of the mountain. The stream that comes down from the lower pastures runs gray at midday and clear by morning.

**NPCs and threats.** The village priests, one sure the mountain is angered by disrespect and one sure something on it is wrong. Shepherds who have dreamed the same dream. The headman, who wants the lower pastures back before the summer grass is gone.

**Player choices.** Examine the flocks, follow the stream up, listen to the dreams, or ask who went up this spring.

**Clues and rewards.** The sick sheep drank from the stream, not the wells. A shepherd says the first pilgrims of the year went up with a guide from Bataren and four packs too heavy for bread. The dreamers all slept above the great spring.

**Consequences.** The party knows the harm comes down the water, and it knows the guide's name.

**If bypassed.** The headman sends a boy to Bataren to fetch Orneta Sateris, and the party meets her there.

### Scene Two: Bataren

**Purpose.** The silent guide and the man who hired her.

**Situation.** [[place-batarencity|Bataren City]], gray and low on its gulf. Orneta's lodging in the mule-yards by the north gate, and Zedime Diravis's rooms above a bookseller's, where the Order keeps its license for the work it does in the north.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-orntstrs|Orneta Sateris]], who answers questions by looking at the floor. Her sister, who wants her back. [[being-zdmdrvs|Zedime Diravis]], courteous, who says his colleague went to the mountain on the Order's lawful business and has not written. The Chancellor's clerk, who arrives a day behind the party with the same question.

**Player choices.** Persuade Orneta to show the way without speaking of it, press Zedime for the writ, or follow the clerk.

**Clues and rewards.** Orneta will not speak, but she will draw the route to the great spring in the dust with her stick if the party promises to go up as pilgrims. Zedime admits, if pressed, that the work had the crown's oversight and that the oversight was sealed. The clerk carries a letter of the Chancellor's household and no name on it.

**Consequences.** The party knows the survey went up under the great seal. Zedime and the clerk both know the party knows.

**If bypassed.** Zedime sends two hired men up the customary way with a rope and a pick, and the Áelendan turn them back at the first watch.

### Scene Three: The Customary Way

**Purpose.** Show the mountain and the people who keep it.

**Situation.** The pilgrims' path above the foothills: shrines of piled stone, the high pastures, the cold. The Áelendan watchers stand on the path in pairs.

**NPCs and threats.** The watchers, who search every pack. The cold above the pastures. Any hired men of Zedime's or the clerk's who are on the path with the party.

**Player choices.** Go up as pilgrims, with nothing in the packs a pilgrim would not carry. Argue with the watchers. Try to slip past them.

**Clues and rewards.** A party that goes up the customary way is let through and followed. The watchers say, in halting Provenzal, that the people who broke the spring must mend it.

**Consequences.** A party that carries instruments, or slips the watch, is walked down the mountain without a blow and is not let up again this year.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the spring only if it found the way without the watchers, and the watchers find it there.

### Scene Four: The Great Spring

**Purpose.** The harm, the body and the satchel.

**Situation.** The great spring on the mountain's shoulder, where the water comes out of the rock in a pool the color of glass. The surveyors' spoil heap at its mouth, a stinking hump under the stones. A cairn with a marker pole. Above, at the snow line, a shape in the ice.

**NPCs and threats.** The watchers, who stand off and watch what the party does. The cold. The spring's mouth, which must be dug out by hand.

**Player choices.** Clear the spring and throw down the cairn, bring down the body, open the satchel, or leave the satchel in the ice.

**Clues and rewards.** The mule's carcass under the spoil. The young fellow's body, his satchel, his notebook of measures and the writ under the great seal. If the party clears the spring and throws down the cairn, the watchers give back the instruments, broken, as a sign the matter is closed.

**Consequences.** The stream runs clean within a week and the dreams stop. Whoever holds the satchel holds the Chancellor's good name.

**If bypassed.** The spring stays foul through the summer, and the body comes down at the next thaw.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The flocks keep dying, and the villages give up the lower pastures for the year. The Áelendan close the customary way to pilgrims until the spring is mended. At the next thaw they bring the young fellow's body down to the nearest village, as they bring every body, with his satchel beside him, and the village priest sends the sealed writ to [[place-jalnazdal|Jalnasdal]] by the first carrier. It reaches the Earl of Degavel's people before it reaches the Chancellor's.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that mended the spring has the foothill villages' gratitude and the watchers' grudging knowledge of its faces. The satchel decides the rest. Returned to the Chancellor unread, it makes Velzore Aitevis a quiet friend. Given to Zedime, it gives the Order the first measures of Hidraven's springs and the Áelendan a reason to watch the Order. Carried to the Earl of Degavel, it puts the Chancellor's seal before the Council of Peers and the kingdom's water into its quarrel. Left in the ice, it waits for the thaw.
