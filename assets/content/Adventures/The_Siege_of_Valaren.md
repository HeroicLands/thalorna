## Overview {#overview}

[[place-valarencity|Válaren City]] is the great port of the warm southern shore, rich, polished and wholly dependent on the sea. Nobody has ever taken it by assault. A blockade is the one weapon it fears, because a closed harbor ruins it within weeks: prices go first, then credit, then the partnerships that hold the merchant houses to one another. The last blockade, when forces nobody identified held the roads and the water for a quarter of a year, is the event the city dates itself by.

This season three hulls without colors have been seen standing off the coast, never together. The merchant houses read them as the first move of a blockade. The great houses are calling in short loans, the smaller ones are selling stock below cost to stay liquid, and the partnerships hold only because nobody wants to be the first to break one. The city's commander has few men and no fleet, and the houses that could hire one are each waiting for another to pay.

The party enters in the hire of a merchant house that wants to break the blockade's grip on its credit before its partners desert it.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The hulls.** The three hulls are hired, with picked crews, out of a cove down the coast. They have stopped no ship and taken nothing. Their work is to be seen: to stand off the harbor mouth on a clear morning, one at a time, and to sail away. That is enough. Válaren has learned from the last blockade to lend short and call early, and its houses are doing it.

**Who pays.** [[being-lrrmtlms|Lorrame Tulemis]], head of a Válaren merchant house and a client of the Earl of Degavel, hired the hulls through a factor in Kalveza. His house lends short and holds its silver close, so the scare costs him nothing, and he is buying the stock the smaller houses are selling below cost.

**Why.** The crown's money is the reason. The frontier and the ships cost more than the crown takes, and the Treasurer, [[being-gredalekalv|Gredale Kalvezis]], has asked the coastal houses for a loan against the salt revenue of [[place-lizela|Lizela]] rather than ask the [[affiliation-councilpeers|Council of Peers]] for a levy. Válaren's great houses are the likeliest lenders. While the hulls stand off the harbor, no house in Válaren can lend a single Solden to anyone, and the crown must go to the Council, where the Earl of Degavel holds the office of First Peer and the Council names its price.

**The Earl.** Lorrame heard his patron say at table that a crown that must ask the Council is a crown that can be governed, and took it as an errand. Whether the Earl of Degavel knew what his client would do with the remark is the referee's to settle.

**The Admiral.** [[being-jaltezamlvt|Jalteza Malvotis]], the Admiral, commands [[affiliation-crownships|the crown's ships]] from Válaren and has fewer than she asks for. Every one of them is on the Tarvénian coast or the salt route. She would clear the harbor mouth in a day if she had two galleys, and the Treasurer has not paid for them.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The hulls are a scare, not a siege, and the harm is all on paper. The scenario ends when the hulls are named, driven off or bought out, or when a partnership breaks and the panic is real.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The merchant.** [[being-chalzimechol|Chalzime Cholsamis]], a wine merchant whose cargo cannot sail while the hulls stand off, hires a party to prove that a rival is paying them, and to do it before his partners desert him.
- **The perfumer.** [[being-ureleralwn|Urele Ralven]], whose season's oils were bought on a house's paper and whose loan has been called, has met a sailor who swears he traded with one of the hulls at a cove by night.
- **The Admiral.** Jalteza Malvotis asks a party to find out what the hulls are, so that she can tell the Treasurer what two galleys would save.
- **The Treasurer.** A clerk of Gredale Kalvezis's asks a party why no house in Válaren will answer the crown's letter about the salt loan.

## The Opening {#opening}

The harbor mole at Válaren on a clear morning, the water white with light. A crowd stands along the wall, merchants and clerks among the porters, looking south. Far out, a hull with no colors sits on the sea with her sails half furled.

> "There she is," a clerk says. "That's the third this month. My master called in every loan he has last night."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Counting Houses

**Purpose.** Show the panic and who profits.

**Situation.** [[place-valarencity|Válaren City]]'s merchant quarter, the counting houses, the exchange under its arcade, the quays where nothing sails.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-chalzimechol|Chalzime Cholsamis]], anxious and courteous. His partners, who are looking for an excuse. Small houses selling stock below cost. [[being-lrrmtlms|Lorrame Tulemis]]'s buyers, who are buying.

**Player choices.** Ask who is selling, ask who is buying, or ask which houses are still lending.

**Clues and rewards.** One house is buying when every other is selling, and its silver is not tied up anywhere. No hull has stopped a ship or taken a cargo.

**Consequences.** The party knows the blockade has never blockaded anything.

**If bypassed.** Chalzime's partners break the partnership, and his house is the first to fall.

### Scene Two: The Cove

**Purpose.** The hulls.

**Situation.** A cove down the coast, reached by a cliff path or by sea, where a hull puts a boat ashore by night to take on water and trade.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-ureleralwn|Urele Ralven]]'s sailor, who wants his price. The hull's boat crew, mercenaries who are bored and well paid. Their captain, who has orders and a factor's name.

**Player choices.** Watch, buy from the boat, take a prisoner, or parley with the captain.

**Clues and rewards.** The crews are paid monthly in Kalveza by a factor who serves several Válaren houses. The captain's orders say: be seen, take nothing, stop no ship.

**Consequences.** A prisoner taken makes the captain move the cove. A parley makes the captain name his price to stop.

**If bypassed.** The party has rumor and no proof.

### Scene Three: The Factor in Kalveza

**Purpose.** Follow the money.

**Situation.** [[place-kalveza|Kalveza]], the other southern port, a day along the coast. The factor's office by the harbor.

**NPCs and threats.** The factor, who serves many houses and keeps a ledger for each. A guard or two.

**Player choices.** Buy the ledger, steal it, frighten the factor, or follow his next payment.

**Clues and rewards.** The ledger for the hulls is kept under the name of Lorrame Tulemis's house.

**Consequences.** Lorrame learns within days that his factor has talked.

**If bypassed.** The party brings the matter to Válaren without proof of the paymaster.

### Scene Four: The Admiral and the Exchange

**Purpose.** Breaking the grip.

**Situation.** Válaren again. The Admiral's house on the harbor, the exchange at noon, and Lorrame's counting house.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-jaltezamlvt|Jalteza Malvotis]], who has one galley in port. Lorrame, who offers the party a share of everything he has bought. The houses on the exchange, who will believe a proof read aloud at noon.

**Player choices.** Read the proof on the exchange, give it to the Admiral, give it to the Treasurer's clerk, or take Lorrame's share.

**Clues and rewards.** The Admiral, given the cove, takes her one galley out and the hulls run. The proof read on the exchange ends the panic in an afternoon.

**Consequences.** With credit loosened, the great houses can answer the Treasurer, and the crown borrows against the salt without asking the Council. Given to the Treasurer instead, the proof is a weapon against the Earl of Degavel at court.

**If bypassed.** The scare runs its course.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

A partnership breaks in the second month, and the panic becomes real: three houses fail, the city's credit is frozen through the winter, and no Válaren house can lend to the crown. The Treasurer goes to the Council for a levy, and the Council, held by the First Peer, grants it on terms the crown spends the next year undoing. The hulls sail away in the spring. Lorrame's house is the largest in the city.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A party that ended the scare has saved the house that hired it and earned the gratitude of a city that remembers its last blockade. What it did with the proof decides who wins at court: the Treasurer and the crown, the Earl of Degavel and the Council, or nobody, if the proof stays in a drawer. Lorrame Tulemis has an enemy, and he has a patron.
