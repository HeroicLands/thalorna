## Overview {#overview}

On the [[place-thornwald|Thornwald]] frontier, at a crossing that the forest villages and the river-folk both use, a half-burned timber fort stands with its stone gatehouse still whole. It is Theodburg, the folk's stronghold, which a river-prince of [[place-brvvsh|Brevavesh]] and the war-bands of the forest held together against the Grukar for a generation, until the prince's son asked tribute for it and the war-bands walked out ([[lore-princesfort|The Prince's Fort]]). Velanthian agents, Varokh scouts and the Grukar all watch it, and nobody holds it.

The elder son and heir of [[being-krdgrbrvgrch|Krodogor Brevogorich]] wants it back, with a war-band of the forest in it beside his own men, and he has begun to ask which village would send one. He is twenty-seven, earnest and impatient, and he has ridden every forest post of his father's frontier himself. He has no silver, and he knows the story of the last prince's son who asked.

The party enters as the heir's companions on the road to [[place-thornhaven|Thornhaven]], as strangers the forest village asks to sit at a bargain neither side trusts the other to keep, or as riders the captain of the forts sends after the heir to see that he comes back.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The heir's count.** The heir has counted the wardens at the forest posts and found fewer than his father pays. He takes the gap for desertion, and he believes the forts cannot hold the forest edge through another autumn. He wants a war-band at Theodburg to watch the stretch the thin posts cannot, and he will not ask his father for silver the princedom does not have.

**The captain.** [[being-vrklrvgrch|Ovrik Lervogorich]] knows why the posts are thin: a third of the wardens the prince pays are not in the barracks, and their wages go to the bell-ringers, cooks and village watches beneath the forts. A war-band at Theodburg would fill the gap, and nobody would count the wardens again. Ovrik helps the heir quietly, and asks him to keep his count from the upper hall until the fort is held.

**The prince.** Krodogor neither forbids his son nor pays for him. He remembers what Theodburg cost his house, he will not ask a war-band for an oath, and he will not pay a war-band for a season he cannot pay his own wardens for. If the heir comes back with a bargain, the prince keeps it. If he comes back with a quarrel, the prince disowns the bargain and keeps his son.

**The offer.** The heir has one thing to give: the crossing. He offers the village that holds the fort with him half the toll of the crossing and the fort's protection for its clearings, his stone and their timber, the arrangement the first fort was built on. The toll is his own new term. Nothing in the offer is tribute, and nothing in it pays a garrison when the port's silver is late.

**Thornhaven.** Since the refusal at Theodburg, the princes' agents have gone straight to Thornhaven's War Chief and never to its Other Chief, who in every other Varokh village speaks for the village to strangers. The War Chief wants the season's pay and the crossing, and he is ready to agree. But a bargain between a village and outsiders is not a bargain until the Other Chief witnesses it, and no prince's agent has asked her seat for anything in a century. The present Other Chief witnesses a bargain only if it is spoken before both peoples at the fort, as the last one was, and she refuses it if anything in it is tribute. She also sets a condition on this bargain: who feeds the garrison in the season the prince's silver does not come.

**The Grukar.** The gatehouse stands on the river path that Grukar raiding parties take toward the clearings. They come out of the north-east in small parties, they bargain with nobody and they take nobody back, and they have watched the ruin since it burned.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This scenario does not say whose torch fired the fort a century ago, and nothing at the gatehouse settles it. The question is whether a shared fort can be shared twice, and the referee decides what the Other Chief makes of the party's answer to her question.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The courtier: the heir's road.** The heir wants companions who are neither his father's wardens nor a war-band's men to ride with him to Thornhaven and stand beside him when he speaks.
- **The warrior: the captain's errand.** Ovrik wants the heir brought home with a bargain, or at least brought home, and he pays from a purse he does not explain.
- **The warrior: a season's service.** A Thornhaven war-band has offered its service for the season at a paid fort, and the warden has no silver. The war-band wants to know who else pays on this frontier.
- **The courtier: the witness.** Thornhaven's Other Chief wants strangers at the bargain who belong to neither side, because the last bargain was spoken before both peoples and only one of them remembers it the same way.

## The Opening {#opening}

The upper hall of the stone fort at Brevavesh, after the quarter's rolls. A young man with his father's square face and none of his father's tiredness unrolls a strip of birch bark on the table, marked with the posts of the forest edge and a number beside each.

> "My father pays for more men than I found," says the heir. "Theodburg had room for twice as many, and half of them came from the forest." From the door, the captain of the forts watches the strip of bark and does not come in.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Upper Hall

**Purpose.** Meet the heir, the prince and the captain, and learn what each will and will not say.

**Situation.** The prince lives in the upper hall of the fort, not in a house of his own. The heir's bark strip lies on the table. The prince has seen it, said nothing, and gone to the bell hill.

**NPCs and threats.** The heir, earnest and impatient, who believes the war-bands will come if the bargain is fair. Krodogor, blunt and tired, who says what the garrison is owed before he says anything else, and who tells the party the story of Theodburg as his house tells it. Ovrik, gruff and loyal, who offers the heir horses, a guide and his blessing, and asks for the bark strip.

**Player choices.** Take the heir's commission, take the captain's, ask the prince what he wants, or read the bark strip closely.

**Clues and rewards.** The strip's numbers fall short most at the posts nearest the villages, where the village watches stand. The prince's story ends with the son who asked for tribute and the war-bands walking out at the gate, and he tells it as a warning to his own son. Ovrik wants the strip because it is the only written count of the posts besides his own.

**Consequences.** The heir rides with the party, and either the strip goes with him or Ovrik has it.

**If bypassed.** The heir rides out alone with his strip and his offer.

### Scene Two: The Thornwald Road

**Purpose.** The frontier between the forest and the grain country, and what each side remembers.

**Situation.** Thornwald is the forest's most permeable border: the families of its landings and the river-folk beyond have traded and married across the frontier for generations, and the road passes landings where a Velanthian is a neighbor and a Varokh is a cousin. A day out, the road passes in sight of the gatehouse at the crossing, standing gray above the half-burned timber of the fort.

**NPCs and threats.** Landing-folk who know the heir's house by its badge and ask whether the prince wants tribute again. Varokh scouts on the bank opposite the gatehouse, watching the party without hiding.

**Player choices.** Stop at the landings and ask what the frontier remembers of the fort, cross to speak with the scouts, or go straight to Thornhaven.

**Clues and rewards.** The landing-folk tell the end of the fort two ways: in the forest tellings the prince's men fired it as they left, and in the grain villages the war-bands fired it as they went. The scouts belong to Thornhaven's war-band, and they report what they see to the War Chief before the party reaches the village.

**Consequences.** Thornhaven knows the party is coming, and why.

**If bypassed.** The heir reaches Thornhaven unannounced, and the War Chief receives him in front of the war-band.

### Scene Three: The Village by the Lake

**Purpose.** The three seats of Thornhaven, and the one that has not spoken.

**Situation.** Thornhaven sits on its lake where the forest thins toward the grain belt. Its War Chief treats with outsiders, and its Other Chief, whose seat speaks for the village to strangers everywhere else in the forest, has had less to say to the world than any Other Chief in Vristwald.

**NPCs and threats.** The War Chief, who receives the heir at once and likes the crossing toll. [[being-wlfhrthdwld|Dorghár ira Theodwald wur Thornhaven]], his huscarl, who follows him for a muster and not for a lifetime, and who asks who commands the fort when the prince's men and the war-band disagree. [[being-wthrnthrnhvn|Withrún ira Othvith wur Thornhaven]], a warrior of the war-band who still seeks a witnessed settlement for a raid on her own household, and who will not hold a gate on a bargain no one witnessed. [[being-thrnlrkhld|Thornila ira Rikhild wur Thornhaven]], a priestess of the [[affiliation-ardavon|Faith of Árdavon]] with Velanthian kin, who wants the war-band to defend the clearings against the Grukar and argues for the fort louder than anyone asks her to. The Other Chief, an old woman who listens to all of it and says only that a bargain is spoken at the place it binds.

**Player choices.** Bargain with the War Chief alone, ask the Other Chief what she needs, speak with Withrún, or answer Dorghár's question about command.

**Clues and rewards.** The War Chief agrees readily, and the war-band follows him for the season. Withrún and half the band say they will hold the gate only on a witnessed bargain. The Other Chief names her condition: the bargain spoken at the gatehouse before both peoples, nothing in it tribute, and an answer to who feeds the garrison in a season without silver.

**Consequences.** The party leaves for the gatehouse with the Other Chief or without her, and the war-band divided or whole.

**If bypassed.** The War Chief agrees with the heir in the hall, and the Other Chief does not come.

### Scene Four: The Night at the Gatehouse

**Purpose.** The ruin, the river path and the reason the fort was built.

**Situation.** The stone gatehouse still stands, roofed in turf where the upper floor burned, with the half-burned timber walls of the fort around it and the crossing below. The bargain is to be spoken at first light. The party and whoever came with it spend the night inside the gatehouse.

**NPCs and threats.** A Grukar raiding party, a handful of warriors in a canoe and on the bank, coming down the river path in the dark toward the clearings. They do not stop for the gatehouse, and they do not turn aside for anyone who leaves them alone. They are what Theodburg was built against.

**Player choices.** Hold the gatehouse and let the party pass, ring an alarm toward the clearings, follow the raiders, or meet them at the crossing.

**Clues and rewards.** Prince's men and forest warriors who meet the raiders side by side at the crossing have done, for one night, what the fort was for. Withrún fights beside the heir's men if the bargain is honest, and Dorghár watches who gives the orders.

**Consequences.** Both peoples come to the bargain at first light having fought together, having watched each other hide, or having watched the clearings burn.

**If bypassed.** The raiders pass the gatehouse in the dark and strike a clearing upriver, and the bargain at dawn is spoken in the smell of its smoke.

### Scene Five: The Bargain at the Gate

**Purpose.** Speak the bargain before both peoples, and see whether it is witnessed.

**Situation.** First light at the gatehouse. The heir and his men stand on one side of the gateway and Thornhaven's war-band on the other. The War Chief speaks for the village, and the Other Chief stands where the last Other Chief stood.

**NPCs and threats.** The heir, who has the crossing to give and nothing else. The War Chief, who wants the toll. The Other Chief, who asks her question in front of everyone: who feeds the garrison in the season the silver does not come.

**Player choices.** Answer the question for the heir, let him answer it, offer a shared answer, such as a store of grain held at the fort by both peoples, or advise the Other Chief to refuse.

**Clues and rewards.** The Other Chief witnesses a bargain that answers her question without tribute in it. She refuses a bargain with any tribute in it, and a promise of the prince's silver leaves her question unanswered. A witnessed bargain restores her seat's voice at Thornhaven, and the War Chief knows it.

**Consequences.** Theodburg is held again, held by the heir's men alone, or left to the scouts and the Grukar.

**If bypassed.** The War Chief and the heir clasp hands without a witness, and the war-band comes for the season.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The heir rides to Thornhaven alone and strikes his bargain with the War Chief in the hall. Half the war-band comes to the gatehouse for the season and holds it while the crossing pays. At the first late payment of the season the War Chief asks for silver the heir does not have, and the war-band walks out at the gate. Ovrik's thin posts are counted again by a prince who now has two counts to compare, and the gatehouse stands empty with a new ring of ash around it.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **A witnessed bargain.** Theodburg is a shared fort again, with a store both peoples keep and a toll both peoples take. Thornhaven's Other Chief speaks for the village to the princes' agents for the first time in a century, and the War Chief has less to decide.
- **An unwitnessed bargain.** The war-band holds the fort by the season and leaves it when the season ends, as the princes' war-bands always do.
- **The count read.** If the heir's strip of bark reaches his father beside the quarter's rolls, the prince knows his posts are thinner than his rolls say, and he asks his captain why.
- **No bargain.** The heir comes home with a story, the forest edge stays as thin as it was, and the gatehouse stays watched by everyone and held by no one.
