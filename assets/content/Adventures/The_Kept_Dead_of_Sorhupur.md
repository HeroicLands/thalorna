Something is taking the dead out of the villages at the edge of the forest, and the road it takes runs to [[place-sorhupur|Sorhupur]]. The party must follow it through the lamps to the assembly that never adjourned, and decide what to do with a city that will not let its dead gho.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** An Arghakara of [[affiliation-adanshreni|the Adanshreni]], a careful man named [Satyarava]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, lays a bronze lamp on the table. It burns without oil. A cell of the [Chinnasutra]{.name data-type="affiliation/criminal"} carried it out of the forest and has not come back, and the shrena wants to know whether more of the same lies where it came from. He cannot be seen asking.
- **The mercenary or soldier.** An ordained keeper of a lac estate on [[place-bharavarivr|the Bharava]] is paying double to guard a felling crew. The crew has cut into a road of dressed stone where his survey shows nothing, and three of the crew have walked off at night to visit their mothers, who are dead.
- **The leader.** A woman of the gathering peoples named [Valsi]{.name data-type="being/npc"} asks the chief of a company or a household to bring her people's dead home. Last season's dead were not in their graves when the rains ended, and her grandmother says where they went.
- **The wanderer.** A Jvelita of the [[affiliation-agmipantra|Agmi-pantra]], [Dahanadasa]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, whose ember-vessel burns low whenever he turns toward the forest, asks a traveler to walk the interior with him. He does not say what he thinks is there.

## Scene One: The Causeway of Lamps

**Purpose.** The entrance and the guardians: show the city as old, lit and wrong.

**Situation.** The path ends at a flight of moss-covered steps, and a straight causeway of dressed stone runs on into the dusk. Lamp-posts line it on both sides, each with a bronze bowl and a steady flame. The light is the colour of a threshold lamp and does not flicker. Birds do not call here.

**NPCs and threats.** The [Standing]{.name data-type="being/creature"} are the city's first guardians. They are kept dead, dry and brown in cord-wrapped silk, who stand among the posts as lamp-tenders. They do not hurry and do not strike. They hold out a lit lamp, hand first, with a courtesy a traveler cannot fault. A lamp taken from them cannot be set down or put out by its bearer, and its bearer walks toward the Gate. Every flame on the causeway is a kept soul, and the Standing tend them. Valsi, a gatherer who has lost kin to the forest, guides the party this far and not beyond. She knows the first rule: no flame carried in may be lit from the city's, and none carried out burns true.

**Player choices.**

- _Gho dark._ A party without a flame of its own passes between the posts unoffered, if it moves slowly and keeps to the center.
- _Refuse the lamp._ A courteous refusal in the manner of the [[lore-vedyariclt|Vedyari]], hand to heart, turns a Standing aside. A hurried one does not.
- _Snuff a lamp._ A flame put out frees the soul in it, and the Standing nearest turn together. It is mercy, and it is also an alarm.
- _Fire of their own._ A Jvelita's ember closes the Standing's hold on whatever it touches, and costs the ember a day of its life.
- _Force._ The Standing are brittle and numerous, and each broken one leaves its lamp burning in the road.

**Clues and rewards.** The base of every bowl is stamped with the sign of a household. Rubbings of a handful of them are the names the party needs in Scene Two. A Standing's mantle carries a dead sect-priest's cord, knotted in the sect's own fashion and unreadable to anyone who has not seen the knots.

**Consequences.** A bearer who has taken a lamp is drawn to the [Gate of Lamps]{.name data-type="place/structure"} at nightfall, and cannot sleep elsewhere until the lamp is dealt with. Snuffing lamps wakes the Hall.

**If bypassed.** A party that crosses the terraces by the stream bank avoids the causeway and arrives at the Gate of Lamps without any names, in the middle of Scene Two.

## Scene Two: The Assembly That Never Adjourned

**Purpose.** A roleplay and a puzzle: the sect's last assembly, and a vote the party must survive.

**Situation.** The [Hall of Seats]{.name data-type="place/site"} is a roofless floor with ranks of stone chairs rising on three sides. The seated dead fill them: hundreds of brown, still figures with their hands in their laps. A dais at the open end carries a tall chair, and the chair is occupied. A sourceless light, the same colour as the lamps', lies evenly on everything. The guest-row at the front is empty and swept.

**NPCs and threats.** The [Speaker of the Seats]{.name data-type="being/creature"} is the last Sabhapavi of the sect, a tall dry figure in cloth-of-silver gone the colour of tea. He is courteous, dignified, and entirely mad in one respect: he is certain that the assembly has not ended. He seats guests, hears their business, and puts motions to the Hall in a voice like paper. The motion tonight is whether the Gate of Lamps should be opened to the villages' new dead. The Hall votes by silence. An unanswered motion carries. The seated dead are not hostile; they are the electorate, and a motion that carries sends the Standing out.

**Player choices.**

- _Speak in turn._ Any guest who is recognized may address the Hall. The Speaker recognizes whoever gives their station and kulpa, and a bare wrist is a mark of a foreigner here that earns courtesy.
- _Read the names._ Each seat has a name cut under its footrest, scraped almost smooth. A name read aloud in full, with the household's sign, makes the dead in that seat rise out of the vote. The party needs a name for every seat that could carry the motion, and the rubbings from Scene One give a few. Valsi's people know others.
- _Object._ A formal objection, spoken in the proper form, delays the vote to the next sitting, which the Speaker sets for the next night.
- _Bribe and bargain._ The Speaker takes gifts for his Hall, and a gift of salt is the first one it asks for.
- _Disrupt._ Overturning the guest-row or breaking a seat ends the sitting. The Hall remembers.

**Clues and rewards.** The sect's rule, as the Speaker recites it, is that no member leaves while a hearth is cold. He tells the party where [The Mother of the Kept]{.name data-type="being/creature"} sits and what she wants: more hearths, filled. A name freed from a seat gives the party its first ally, a dead household that guides one of them on the way to the [Hearth]{.name data-type="place/structure"}.

**Consequences.** A carried motion sends the Standing into the nearest villages by the next dawn. A delayed one gives the party a night.

**If bypassed.** A party that goes past the Hall unseen reaches the Tank without the names, and finds the way to the Hearth guarded rather than guided.

## Scene Three: The Offering Vaults

**Purpose.** A trick and a setback: the city turns the party's greed against them.

**Situation.** Behind the Hall, steps lead down to the [Offering Vaults]{.name data-type="place/structure"}, low chambers of the sect where every household that kept a dead one deposited a lamp. They are stacked with bronze bowls chased with gold, small gilded figures of the dead in their seats, strings of beads in jade and carnelian. Much of it would pay a caravan's wage. A pale light comes from every lamp at once.

**NPCs and threats.** The Chinnasutra cell that preceded the party came here. Its leader, [Orgha]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, a former appraiser with a precise hand, is sitting among the figures on a shelf, a lamp in his lap. He is one of the kept now. His dead face still wears the look of a man pricing something. The rest of his cell stands along the wall, wrapped in the sect's silk. They carry the lamps that pulled them down and cannot set them down. They attack whoever carries another lamp out, to take it from them. The vault itself is the threat: any lamp lifted lights in its bearer's pack and cannot be extinguished, and the Standing from the causeway follow it.

**Player choices.**

- _Take nothing._ The cell does not stir, and the vault lets the party pass.
- _Take one and leave an exchange._ A lamp of the party's own, lit from the city's and set in the vacant place, satisfies the vault and costs the party a lamp.
- _Take and run._ A gilded figure is light, and can be hidden. The lamp is not. The cell rises, and the Standing follow the flame out of the city and into the forest.
- _Release Orgha._ Reading his name from the roll on the shelf frees him. He is grateful, and he knows where the cell hid its coin before the lamps took them.
- _Bargain with the cell._ They speak only in the shrena's trade-talk. They will give up the lamps, and the lamps' secret, in exchange for the party carrying their ashes home.

**Clues and rewards.** Orgha's leather satchel, tucked under his shelf, holds a modest purse of chendra and tomra and a map drawn from the cell's three trips, which shows a hidden stair from the Tank to the Hearth. The gilded figures are worth a good wage each to a receiver.

**Consequences.** A party that leaves the vault carrying a lit lamp has a following. A party that leaves with Orgha's ashes has a debt of the shrena.

**If bypassed.** A party that skips the vaults reaches the Hearth by the Tank stair and finds it unguarded by the cell, but without the map.

## Scene Four: The Hearth

**Purpose.** The climax: the sect's last priestess and the door she holds.

**Situation.** The Hearth is a low dome, and its door is open. Inside is a round chamber of black stone with a hearth in the center, cold, and around it the lamps of the sect's first dead. A woman sits at the hearth's edge. Behind her is an arch in the dome's wall, and the air in the arch does not move. Cold comes out of it.

**NPCs and threats.** The Mother of the Kept is one of the [[being-tereb|Tereb]]: a learned term for an undead made by an elaborate rite during life, ending in the candidate's death and a delayed rising. She prepared herself for it. She is slight and ancient and her eyes are two flames, and she speaks in a voice that shifts between the sect's first Sabhapavi and her own. She is a grieving mother. Her children drowned in a flood in the age before the count, and she founded the sect to keep them from the gate. She keeps every kept soul so that none of them can be lost, as hers were. The arch behind her is the way [[lore-theshadow|the Shadow]] came in, through the opening her holding made, and her hold on the city is also her hold on the arch. She is sad, patient and implacable. She fights with the lamps: the Standing, the [Seated]{.name data-type="being/creature"}, and the chill.

**Player choices.**

- _Fight._ The Mother cannot be killed while the lamps burn. Every flame the party snuffs weakens her and wakes the Hall.
- _Names._ The names read from the seats and the vault give the party a way to release the lamps in sequence. Each released soul passes in its own time.
- _Fire._ A Jvelita's ember kindled at the Hearth burns it, the lamps and the Mother with it. It is the Agmi-pantra's rite, the orthodox temples' scandal, and it works.
- _The conch._ A conch sounded in the dome, a coin of passage in the Mother's hand and a tomra filed blank in the party's, ends the holding as a household would. This is the way the sect refused, and it is the orthodox way.
- _Parley._ She will speak with someone who has lost a child. She cannot be argued out of her grief, only into a second one.
- _Steal._ Her own lamp, cupped in the hearth, would be worth more than a city.

**Clues and rewards.** The Mother's account of the flood is the city's true story. The Standing are the sect's lamp-tenders, set to keep the flames; the Seated are its first families. She has never allowed the arch to be closed, for it is cold and her dead are cold with it.

**Consequences.** If the lamps are released and the Mother is not, the arch stays open. If both are ended and the arch is not closed, something comes through.

**If bypassed.** A party that comes to the dome from the Tank stair meets the Mother in her own chamber before the Standing can warn her.

## Scene Five: What the Arch Keeps

**Purpose.** The twist and the reward.

**Situation.** The lamps gho out, one after another, down the causeway and across the terraces, and each time a faint shape rises from the bowl and goes away through the trees toward the east. It takes a long time. When the Mother's flame goes out, the arch starts to breathe.

**NPCs and threats.** What comes through the arch is not one of the dead. It is a **gap-wolf**, long, grey and indifferent, a thing of the Shadow that has been waiting at the arch since the first lamp was lit. It does not hunt the living. It feeds on whatever holds a gap open, and when the lamps are gone it feeds on the city. It has no face, and it moves only in the dark the lamps leave behind. The Hearth is the one place that holds it back.

**Player choices.**

- _Close the arch._ The conch sounded once at the arch, and the coin of passage laid on the threshold, close it. It costs whoever sounds it a year of dreams.
- _Burn it._ A Jvelita's ember kindled in the arch drives the gap-wolf back and seals the city in fire. The priest does not come out.
- _Run._ The party can leave the city by the causeway before the wolf has finished with the Hall, at the price of leaving the arch open.
- _Carry the Mother._ She asks to be burned on a pyre of the city's seat-wood, at the forest's edge, with a coin in her hand. It is the first time in an age that anyone has offered.

**Clues and rewards.** If the Mother is burned in the proper way, she gives the party her children's names, the last of the kept, and they rise out of the Hall into the dark. Her gift is the sect's cord, which a Jvelita can read and which opens any lock the sect's craftsmen made. The vault's bronze and gold is the city's honest wealth. Orgha's cell, if it was released, adds a debt of the shrena.

**Consequences.** What the party carries out is a debt, a flame, a name or a warning, depending on what they did in the dark.

**If bypassed.** The arch closes by itself with the last lamp, and the forest moves back over the stone.

## Aftermath

- **The arch closed and the lamps released.** The estates' surveyors redraw the district, and the gathering peoples cross the interior freely for the first time. Valsi's people bring their dead home to burn them.
- **The city burned.** The Agmi-pantra counts it among the grounds it has cleansed, and the orthodox temples do not forgive it.
- **The arch left open.** The forest around the interior sickens. The villages nearest it lose their dead in the night, and the Standing walk the pilgrim road.
- **A lamp carried out.** A receiver in a river town sells it to a Danhija household, and the household's next death is the one that goes missing.
- **The shrena's debt.** The Adanshreni asks, in the spring, for the cell's ashes.
