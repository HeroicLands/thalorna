A relay house on a road older than any realm lights its lamps every dusk, takes in every traveler and lets none go. The keeper is a dead road-reeve whose oath was caught by the Shadow, and he serves supper to the living with perfect courtesy. The party goes in to find what lies in the strong-room, and must find a way to leave.

## Overview

[[place-wenlornestage|Wenlorne Stage]] stands halfway along a whole stretch of Crown Road between the [[place-wyndmarch|Wyndmarch]] and the [[place-hallowwood|Hallowwood]]. Its keeper, the **Reeve**, is the dead officer of the elder kingdom, held to his post by an oath the [[lore-theshadow|Shadow]] reads as a command to keep every guest under his roof until the road is safe. He lights the road to the gate, feeds those who come, writes their names on a board and does not open the gate again. His **porters** are the dead of the last night of the Great War, and they serve him because they believe he is protecting them.

## Hooks

- **The thief.** A Caerwynd quartermaster who has lost his nerve offers a purse for the crown strongbox the old reeves kept. He has a copy of the stage's plan from a surveyor's book and says it is under the tower.
- **The mercenary or common soldier.** The commander at Caerwynd needs men to find the ninth patrol, which took the old road in the autumn and did not return. He cannot spare his own.
- **The leader.** A Vardain elder from the valleys below the Ironfells wants the stage's ledger. The Khazári say it carries a list of the Vardain who passed the gate in the last year of the war, and the elder's family is on it.
- **The lone wanderer.** The wanderer is caught on the old road at dusk, with the forest dark on either side and a line of pale lamps showing the way.

## Scene One: The Lit Mile

**Purpose.** Draw the party in, and make the stage's courtesy the first menace.

**Situation.** The last mile before Wenlorne is lined with iron lamp-posts, each lit with a still, pale flame that gives no heat. The light does not flicker in wind. At the gate-arch a figure in a grey coat stands holding a lantern and bows. Behind him the court is swept and the stable doors are open. The forest on either side of the road is wet and loud with the small noises of the dark, and the road itself is the only quiet thing in it.

**NPCs and threats.** The **porters** are the stage's dead. They look like thin, pale servants with the dusty courtesy of people who have had no sleep for a long time, and they greet each guest by name if the name has been given anywhere on the road. They do not threaten. If a guest refuses the gate they step in front of the road behind, and there are always more of them than the party counted. The lamps are the other guard: a lit lamp near a refused guest marks them for the Reeve.

**Player choices.** Force: cut down the porters, who rise again at the next dusk. Stealth: slip past the gate through the stable court and avoid the lamps. Guile: give a false name, since the porters can only call a guest by what the guest says. Parley: ask the greeter what stage this is and who keeps it.

**Clues and rewards.** The lamp-posts carry a mason's mark of the Khazári, and a crown stamp worn nearly smooth. A Vardain guide can read the stamp as a toll mark, the kind cut on a stage's wall to show that it was kept in good repair. A guest who takes the gate gets a bed, a fire and supper at no charge.

**Consequences.** The porters remember every name. A party that gives true names is addressed by them for the rest of the night, and a false name is written on the board all the same.

**If bypassed.** A party that skirts the stage on the forest side meets the Grukar who hunt the road at night. The lamps remain in sight behind them wherever they go, and the road to the south is the only way out of the trees.

## Scene Two: Supper in the Common Room

**Purpose.** Give the party the stage's history and its rule of hospitality.

**Situation.** The common room is warm. A long table is laid for a company three times the party's size, and there are bowls of steaming food whose smell is of bread and onions and nothing else. Porters stand at the walls. The Reeve sits at the head of the table in his grey coat and asks each guest for news of the road, as a reeve must, and writes the answers in a ledger.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Reeve** is a tall, dry thing with a long grey face that wears a reeve's courtesy as a skin. His voice is level and dusty. He believes the war is in its last night and that the road is full of the crown's enemies. The food is neither poison nor harmless: a guest who eats at his table is a guest by the oath and cannot be turned out, and a guest who refuses insults the house. The porters bring more to a guest who stays at the table than to a guest who rises.

**Player choices.** Force: attack the Reeve in his chair. Stealth: take no food, take notes and slip away. Guile: tell the Reeve the war is over and the road is safe, in terms he will believe. Parley: ask what the Reeve needs to be released from his office.

**Clues and rewards.** The Reeve will say what he owes the crown. The oath can be undone by a proper officer of the crown, by the king's writ or by a reeve of equal rank. No such person exists. The ledger records the names of the last night's guests in a clerk's hand: Khazári, Vardain and small Pelwar parties, with the sum paid. The rooms the Reeve will not enter are the north-west tower and the strong-room under it.

**Consequences.** A party that eats is held by the oath. A party that tells the Reeve the war is over wins his attention and his doubt, which is dangerous in its own way.

**If bypassed.** A party that refuses the table is shown to a bed by the porters, and the Reeve visits the room in the night.

## Scene Three: The Board

**Purpose.** Turn the stage's hospitality into a trap, and force a choice.

**Situation.** A party that sleeps at Wenlorne wakes to find the gate closed and barred, though no one touched it. The board in the court, a slate with names scratched on it, has grown a new line for each guest. One name has been struck through. The porters say gently that the road is not safe today and that the guests will be glad to stay.

**NPCs and threats.** The porters stand in the doorways and do nothing. The bar on the gate is oak and iron and weighs more than a man, and when it is lifted the wall of the court has no gate behind it. The board holds a worse truth: a name struck through means the guest has gone to the Reeve's keeping. Whoever is struck is pale by morning and speaks more slowly, and by the third night follows the porters about the house.

**Player choices.** Force: break out over the wall, a climb of three men's heights on blocks too true to grip. Stealth: wait until the porters sleep, which they do not. Guile: scratch out the party's own names on the board, which the Reeve notices and the porters do not. Parley: ask a porter to say what name was struck first.

**Clues and rewards.** The board's oldest names are those of the last night. One porter, a girl of the Vardain who has kept her wits, answers if asked about the road. She says the reeve's oath ends when the road is declared safe by the one who set the lamps, and the lamps were set by the stage's first reeve, whose bones are under the court.

**Consequences.** Each night spent in the stage costs one party member their name on the board. A hero struck through begins to serve the Reeve.

**If bypassed.** A party that breaks out of the court by the stable roof finds the road beyond is the one they came by and the lit lamps lead back to the gate.

## Scene Four: The Tower

**Purpose.** The confrontation with the Reeve, and the end of the oath.

**Situation.** The north-west tower holds the road-horn, a great curved thing of bronze on a bracket at the top. Below it is the reeve's chamber and below that the strong-room. The Reeve comes up behind the party with his keys and his lantern, polite, and begins to count them.

**NPCs and threats.** The **Reeve** fights only to keep guests in his house. His touch is dry cold and his strength is the strength of the oath: he cannot be struck down while a guest is in the court and a name stands on the board. He counts the party and takes out the cord of a road-reeve's office. The porters come up the stair in a line, each carrying a lamp.

**Player choices.** Force: break the lamps and the board, which weakens the Reeve until dawn. Stealth: reach the horn and sound it, which brings the stage's dead out into the court and gives the party the tower. Guile: declare the road safe as the horn-keeper, using the horn's own call. Parley: kneel and ask to be released from his hospitality, as a guest may.

**Clues and rewards.** The horn's call that closes a reeve's day is cut into its rim in Khazári letters. The first reeve's bones lie under the court, with the stage's seal ring. Whoever carries the bones out of the gate and sets them in the ground beyond the road gives the oath its end.

**Consequences.** If the Reeve is released, he thanks the party and goes out of his chair as a sleeper does. If he is destroyed with the board standing, the stage keeps its lamps and the porters keep their post. If the horn is sounded wrongly, every dead thing in the house comes up the stair.

**If bypassed.** A party that flees the tower is met at the foot of the stair by the porters, who walk them back to their beds without a word.

## Scene Five: The Strong-Room

**Purpose.** Pay the reward and give the party a truth they did not seek.

**Situation.** The strong-room is dry and cold. A crown strongbox stands against the wall, its lid sealed with a stamp of the elder kingdom. Shelves of ledgers line the far wall in neat order, covered with a thin fur of dust.

**NPCs and threats.** None remain, unless the Reeve was destroyed with the board standing, in which case he is seated at the table in the strong-room's middle, waiting for a guest.

**Player choices.** Force: break the box and carry the silver. Stealth: copy the ledgers and leave the silver. Guile: take the stage's seal ring, which every Crown Road gate will recognize. Parley: bring the ledgers to the Vardain and the Khazári.

**Clues and rewards.** The box holds a modest sum in silver bars stamped with the crown mark, worth a few dozen Ard in all, and a roll of writs that once authorized a rider to take horses from any stage. The last ledger records the final entries of the war: the reeve of Wenlorne wrote, on the last night, that a Sinalë envoy had come down the road from the Hallowwood to ask for the king's body and been turned away at the gate because the reeve would not open to anyone. The road that stops at the Hallowwood's edge was a road someone expected to be opened.

**Consequences.** The ledger is wanted by the Vardain, who want their dead named, and by the Khazári of Vorgald, who want the list of who passed the gate. The envoy's entry is not wanted by anyone, and the Sinalë, if they learn it was written down, will want it least.

**If bypassed.** A party that leaves the strong-room unopened hears of the silver from a Wyndmarch drover within the year.

## Aftermath

- **If the oath is ended.** The lamps go out and stay out. The road is open again, and drovers begin to use it. The Vardain bury the dead in the stage's court.
- **If the Reeve is destroyed with the board standing.** The stage keeps its lamps and the road swallows another patrol within the year.
- **If the ledger reaches Vorgald.** The Khazári learn what passed at the gate on the last night, and have more to say about the Sinalë than they did.
- **If the party fails.** The stage holds the party's names, and the lamps are lit for them.
