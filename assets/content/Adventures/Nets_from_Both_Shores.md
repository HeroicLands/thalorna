## Overview {#overview}

[[being-elfithwgdn|Elfith Wigden]] wants Koldmere’s missing boat and two cutters found. She blames [[place-brynwold|Brinwold]], whose tenants really have impounded other boats. [[being-stillwader|Stillwader]], the Sha of [[place-reedgill|Reedgill]], keeps the shores looking at each other while its nest raids their beds. The party’s evidence can stop a reprisal without settling who owns the water.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

The autumn boat and its two cutters fall to Reedgill. Both cutters are dead; their remains and belongings lie in an outer den beside the boat. The nest cuts about half the nets on both shores. The rest of the damage and the winter impoundments are human. Stillwader understands enough shore speech to steer suspicion, but the nest is not a diplomatic party and offers no lasting peace. Its need is fish, boats and access to the reed-beds.

Use [[being-grukaruk|Grukar-Uk]] for raiders and [[being-grkrhshk|Grukar-Hushk]] for the warriors holding the bank. Stillwader’s record refers to [[being-grkrsh|Grukar-Sha]]. The spawner stays deep in the nest; this adventure does not require reaching or fighting it. [[being-alfmundkrtn|Alfmund Kaerton]] is the Stoniford breeder and senator to whom the village’s petition can go, while his northern levy competes for his attention.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- Elfith pays in fish and reed for the lost boat, its cutters, or an honest account of what happened to them.
- A party taking the village’s petition to Stoniford is asked to inspect the damage before carrying an accusation to Thornburi.
- Travelers using the beds find their own mooring line cut and need to discover which shore will let them land safely.

## The Opening {#opening}

> Two sets of cut net lie on the beach. One has pale floats, the other dark ones. The reeve turns over a strand, shows the clean edge and asks which shore uses that knife. Beyond her, across the bays, a thin figure stands motionless below the ravine.

## Scenes {#scenes}

### The loss that is actually proved

At [[place-koldmere|Koldmere]], Elfith lays out the autumn loss separately from the two boats seized this winter. The winter seizure has witnesses. The autumn disappearance has an empty berth and no returned cutter. She initially tells both as one accusation.

**Choices and clues.** Ask her to separate dates and witnesses; inspect the cut strands; or carry the accusation as given. No distinctive knife proves the culprit. The useful evidence is where a boat or its equipment goes. Elfith can identify her village’s net floats and the repair on the lost boat’s bow.

**If bypassed.** The distinctive bow repair is visible on the missing boat later, and its identity can be confirmed on returning. The party need not accept Elfith’s accusation to take the job.

### Across the disputed beds

The Brinwold shore admits impounding the winter boats and refuses to return them without its reed. Its damaged nets have the same clean cuts. A party arriving under Koldmere’s banner meets a hostile landing; a party arriving to compare losses can lay the two kinds of net together.

**Choices and clues.** Secure witnesses from both shores, arrange a night watch, or try to steal back the impounded boats. Theft answers the winter loss and leaves the autumn one unsolved. From either beach the party can see Stillwader at dusk; from the water it can follow the stream to Reedgill without guessing a hidden path.

**If bypassed.** Evidence from the nest still identifies both shores’ equipment, but Brinwold will demand its own opportunity to inspect it. No speech by the party obliges the tenants to trust Elfith.

### The watch below the alders

Stillwader retreats from a boat that approaches openly. After dark the nest’s raiders move through the beds to cut lines and take what lies unguarded. A quiet watch sees their route back to the ravine. A noisy pursuit warns the bank’s guards.

**Choices and consequences.** Follow a raider, defend a moored boat, or leave a marked net where it can be taken. A fight on open water favors a prepared boat crew, but a grounded boat gives the raiders a foothold. Stillwader does not risk itself to recover a dead raider; it changes the route when the party reveals a watch.

**If bypassed.** The ravine is already known and can be entered by day. The party loses the chance to see how the nest uses the shores’ habits and meets an alert bank instead.

### The den with two kinds of net

The stream runs between rooted banks. Cut nets from both shores line the outer dens. The missing boat lies drawn up among the spoil near the stream, its repaired bow identifiable. The cutters’ belongings remain with it; their remains lie in the same outer den. The party can account for both without a journey to the spawn-chamber.

**Choices and clues.** Take a sample from each shore’s net, recover the boat, or search for the cutters’ remains. Each load slows the retreat. A quiet approach can reach the outer stores while the raiders are on the beds; a frontal fight brings Hushk down the narrow stream path. Clearing the outer dens does not destroy the whole nest.

> [!TIP]
> Give proof a physical form: identifiable equipment together in the same den, witnessed by people from both shores if the party secured them. A severed Grukar head proves a kill, not which boat it took.

**If driven back.** Marked equipment carried by the raiders, the stream route and the recovered boat each provide independent evidence. The party can bring a partial case home and return with help. Capturing Stillwader is not a required answer.

### What the proof does not settle

Elfith receives the boat and the account of the cutters. Brinwold demands its netting back. Both must distinguish the nest’s work from their own seizures, and neither has to abandon its claim to the reed-beds to coordinate a watch.

**Choices and rewards.** The party can arrange a witnessed return of the winter boats against the disputed reed, carry a joint petition to Alfmund, or leave the shores with proof and no agreement. A common watch is a practical bargain about boats and signals. It is not a grant of the lake to Ondareth, Aldorath or Reedgill.

**If bypassed.** Each shore hears a different account of the raid. Stillwader, if alive, has room to make the next loss another accusation.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The shores seize more boats and keep fewer cutters on the open beds. Reedgill takes the gaps. The senators promise attention while the levy takes the men who might otherwise patrol the grazing.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A joint watch makes the bays harder to raid, even while ownership remains disputed. A nest driven from an outer den can dig again; victory means the recovered boat, remains and belongings and a safer cutting season, not the end of the Grukar. If the party kills Stillwader, the nest loses its present organizer and still needs to eat.
