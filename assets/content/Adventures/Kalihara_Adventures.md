Here are five seeds and one campaign, designed so the seeds can be run independently as one-offs or woven together to form the opening arc of the campaign. I've deliberately made each seed hit a different beat – dungeon, horror/investigation, heist, extraction, diplomatic – so they don't feel like the same job five times. Specific Kalihari personal names I've left as placeholders; those should come from the Kalihari_Language naming conventions in your vault.

## Seed 1 – The Starfall Quarry

_Flavor: wilderness expedition / site clearance._

A Qasirahn intermediary named Dessama (a fixer who works out of a trading-house in the cosmopolitan quarter) commissions the party to travel to a specific impact site in the highland ridges of Mídhalión, where a meteor fell within the last decade. A small crew of six miners was sent to extract a specific volume of meteoric iron two seasons ago; they have not returned, and their last signal-fires stopped weeks before they were expected to reappear. The party is asked to find out what happened, recover any workable metal, and bring it back. The pay is extraordinary. The purpose is not explained.

What actually went wrong: the meteoric iron in this site has properties that destabilize something dormant beneath the impact crater – a pre-human ruin, a bound spirit of the local land, or a seam of some mineral that reacts dangerously to the star-metal's presence. The miners are dead or worse. Recovering the metal requires resolving whatever killed them. The clever adventuring question is how much iron to take – the more, the greater the intermediary's satisfaction, but also the greater the disturbance to whatever is down there.

Stakes: nothing world-shaking. But the intermediary is willing to pay enough that the party's curiosity about _why_ can become the hook for Seed 3 or the campaign proper. And the iron, once delivered, will be used – somewhere, somehow, by someone.

## Seed 2 – The Silent Village

_Flavor: investigation / quiet horror._

A minor official in Qasirah (not the port council proper – someone adjacent, a harbor-warden or a customs scribe) engages the party under unusual circumstances: the commission comes through back channels, and the official is anxious about being seen to issue it. A coastal village half a day's ride from the port has gone silent. No trade, no messenger, no response. The official wants the party to investigate and report back. They are emphatically not to contact anyone else about what they find.

What actually went wrong: the small Ithári structure that protected the village's fishing grounds and fresh-water spring failed completely three months ago. The local Ithári'kaan elder, unable to admit this to the orthodox hierarchy in the interior, tried something – a foreign ritual, a bargain with a minor undead entity, a half-remembered fragment of old lore, take your pick – and it has taken the village over. The survivors, if any, are not quite themselves. The elder is alive but will beg the party not to report what they find: to do so is to expose the whole decay problem in a way that will fracture Kaliharan society.

Stakes: the party has to decide whether to report honestly (which damages the Ithári'kaan and accelerates the schism) or lie by omission (which protects orthodoxy but leaves the actual problem in place). Either way, they now _know_ something that most people in Thalorna do not. That knowledge is worth money, and it is also dangerous to possess.

## Seed 3 – The Obsidian Consignment

_Flavor: heist / escort under hostile surveillance._

The party is hired to escort a large, discreet shipment of an obscure volcanic obsidian from a specific quarry on the Ankarian coast to a warehouse in Qasirah. The shipment is large enough to be conspicuous, but the buyer has paid for it to travel inconspicuously. Three separate parties are trying to intercept it: a rival port-city faction from Korinthel (who want to know what Qasirah is up to), a Byzarian merchant house that has figured out the strategic significance (and wants leverage, not the obsidian), and a small band of Ordo Arcanis scholars who have concluded that they need a sample for their own research.

What actually matters: getting the obsidian to the warehouse is only the start. The party discovers – through an accident, a leak, or a moment of curiosity at the warehouse – that the shipment is meant to be taken further, into the interior, past the boundary where foreigners are not permitted. The buyer asks them to continue. This is the first time the party is asked to set foot in true Kalihara. The pay doubles. The legal risk is enormous. And if they take the job, they meet someone on the other end who is waiting for them.

Stakes: this is the seed that most naturally opens into the campaign. The person waiting on the other end is a key figure in what comes later.

## Seed 4 – The Defector

_Flavor: extraction / moral ambiguity._

Magister Velaro Thessan, a senior Ordo Arcanis scholar who has spent eleven years quietly studying Kalihara from Qasirah, has achieved a breakthrough. Using some combination of arcane observation, social engineering, and an unwise amount of personal risk, he has taken measurements of a failing Ithári structure at close range – closer than any outsider in living memory. He has notes. He has diagrams. He has, he believes, the theoretical basis for replicating ithári'sul effects using Ordo arcane techniques.

He is now being hunted. Not by official Kaliharan forces (Kalihara does not officially have foreign-facing enforcement), but by a small group of port-city operatives working for a faction that regards what he has done as an unforgivable violation. The Ordo wants him extracted alive with his notes intact. The party is hired.

Complication: Thessan is not entirely trustworthy. His stated intention is scholarly publication, but the party will discover evidence – a letter, a conversation overheard, a secondary buyer quietly contacted – that he intends to sell his findings to one of the great Ankarian powers. His work could genuinely help Kalihara if shared back. It could also be used to _replace_ Kalihara as the sole keeper of the Ithári legacy. The party decides whether to deliver him intact, deliver him minus his notes, kill him, or turn him over to the Kaliharans – each of which has a different buyer willing to reward them and a different long-term consequence.

Stakes: whatever the party does, a copy of Thessan's work (full, partial, or corrupted) reaches some faction in Thalorna. The ithári'sul problem becomes slightly less Kalihara's exclusive concern.

## Seed 5 – The Pilgrim's Request

_Flavor: diplomatic / exploratory / slow-burn._

A Kaliharan woman arrives wherever the party keeps their base – a tavern, a mercenary guild hall, a scholar's hostel. She is unaccompanied, travel-worn, and utterly calm. She asks for the party by reputation. She will not give her name, but she offers credentials: she claims to be Ithári'kaan, and when pressed she produces a held stone the size of a knuckle – bone-white, hard as bone is hard, with none of the give a living work has under the hand. Only a custodian would have one, and anyone who has handled a work knows at a touch what it is. She asks the party to carry a specific object – a sealed lacquered case about the size of a child's hand – to a Sinalëan enclave, and to deliver it with a specific spoken message, in a language she will teach them phonetically, to whichever Sinalo receives them.

She cannot go herself. To leave Kalihara, she says, would be to sever something she is not willing to sever. She cannot send another Kaliharan, for reasons she does not explain but which the party can infer: this errand is heretical, and if she is seen to send a Kaliharan the schism becomes visible.

Complications are layered. Finding a Sinalëan enclave requires being _permitted_ to find one, which means either having prior connections to the Sinalë or finding a way to be interesting enough that the Sinalë allow themselves to be found. The orthodox Ithári'kaan, if they learn of her commission, will treat her as a heretic; she will be killed or vanished, and anyone in contact with her will be suspect. The object itself is a _key_, though to what she will not say. And the message, once the party learns the phonetics, turns out to carry a specific name – a name the Sinalë are likely to recognize, though the party will not know why.

Stakes: this seed, if successfully completed, initiates a relationship between the party and a faction of the Ithári'kaan that will shape everything afterward. The Sinalë, once contacted on these terms, remember.

## The Campaign – _The Fading Lights_

A multi-year arc in five acts. The shape is: the party begins as outsiders hired for discrete jobs, gradually discovers the pattern those jobs belong to, becomes deeply entangled in Kaliharan factional struggle, and is eventually asked to make a civilizational choice that no Kaliharan can make and remain Kaliharan.

**Act I – The Commissions.** The party accepts several of the seeds above (or functional equivalents) across a span of months. No single job tells them much. But they begin to notice a pattern: the same Qasirahn intermediaries, the same extraordinary gold, the same specific kinds of material and information being sought. A persistent player will start asking questions. The climax of the act is the moment when those questions are answered by someone – a handler, a mentor, a defeated foe, the woman from Seed 5 – and the party is told, plainly, what has been happening: the structures are failing, a faction within Kalihara is trying to save them, and the party has been working for that faction whether they knew it or not.

**Act II – The Reformers.** The party is invited into the confidence of the reformist faction, the Ithári'kaan who have concluded that orthodoxy alone cannot save the island. They meet named figures on both sides: an elder reformer, a severe orthodox custodian, a port-city councilor playing both against the middle. They begin to understand the shape of the schism and the cost of keeping it hidden. Their work in this act is more intimate: protecting reformers from orthodox sanction, moving people rather than materials, carrying messages whose meaning they are allowed to know. They see Kalihara from inside for the first time. They meet a Kaliharan child being trained in the custodial disciplines and understand, in a way outsiders rarely do, what this civilization has been preserving. The act closes with a disaster: one of the major structures – the one that has been softening the monsoon – fails catastrophically. A storm hits a coastal district unprotected. Thousands die. Orthodoxy blames the reformers for their interference; the reformers blame orthodoxy for its inflexibility. The faction war goes hot.

**Act III – The Keystone.** A single structure is identified as the one whose failure would render the island indefensible – the installation that makes the sea-approaches uncanny and unnavigable. It is days, perhaps weeks, from total failure. No amount of meteoric iron or obsidian will save it. It needs something the Ithári used as a power source, something the Kaliharans have never identified because they have never needed to. The reformers dispatch the party on a long expedition to find out what that something is. This is the heart of the campaign, and it can branch three ways depending on what the players pursue:

- _The Khazárian branch._ The party seeks out the Khazári – breaking millennia of non-contact – and attempts to learn whether the ithári'sul material was known in the Khazári's prior realm. The Khazári can be reached, at extraordinary cost, and what they know is both more and less than hoped. They recognize the material. They will not say what it is. They may, for the right consideration, offer help – on their own terms.
- _The Sinalë branch._ The party, having made contact in Seed 5 or its equivalent, follows the thread of whatever was in the sealed case. The Sinalë's histories reach back far enough that they may have witnessed the Ithári's departure – perhaps even know where the Ithári went. The Sinalë will not intervene. But they may, if persuaded, share a truth the Kaliharans would find shattering: the Ithári did not leave. They finished.
- _The distant-works branch._ The party follows a lead – a fragment in Thessan's notes, a rumor in Khazárian oral tradition, a passage in an Ordo Arcanis manuscript – to an Ithári installation elsewhere in Thalorna. Not a Kaliharan site. A site of the same people, in a place no one expected them to have gone. The people who live beside it call it a ruin and have a story about it that has nothing to do with precursors; it is not a ruin, it is intact and still working, and what it is doing there – and why the Kaliharans have never known about it – is the central mystery of this branch. It is possibly the campaign's most shocking revelation. The same trail turns up others in other countries, and at least one of them whitened and cracked long ago.

Each branch changes the final act. A rich campaign will touch all three.

**Act IV – The Choice.** Whatever the party has found in Act III, it produces an option – or several options – none of them clean. Possibilities include:

- Taking a held stone from a distant work. The Council's own proposal is to cannibalize a working installation somewhere far away, and the party may be the ones who have to tell them it cannot be done: nothing is ever taken from a living work, by anyone, by any means. What can be had is the held stone a cracked work will give up – and the cracked work the trail turns up stands in country where a people have lived beside it for generations, call it holy, and hold that it keeps something away from them. It is dead and it keeps nothing. They do not know that, they will not be told it by strangers, and taking their stone is taking their god's bone. This saves Kalihara. What it costs is somebody else's, and is not measurable from here.
- Sending a signal to the Ithári. The Kaliharan liturgies, amplified in a specific way the party has learned to perform, can reach across whatever distance the Ithári went. The Ithári may return. They may return unchanged, or changed, or indifferent. They may arrive to find their students badly transformed.
- A Khazárian partnership. The Khazári will help, in exchange for a price – perhaps access to the ancient Kaliharan sites for their own study, perhaps a hostage exchange of cultural knowledge, perhaps something more symbolic and harder to give. The price is always sized to hurt.
- The original protocol. One of the branches reveals that the Ithári's intended maintenance procedure – long forgotten – involves something the Kaliharans abandoned for ethical reasons they no longer remember making. Restoring it requires restoring a practice that the living Ithári'kaan will find monstrous. Orthodox custodians will refuse. Reformers will struggle. The party has a vote they should not have a vote in.

**Act V – The Consequences.** Whatever the party chooses, consequences cascade. The ten-thousand-year silence between Kalihara and the rest of Thalorna ends, in one direction or another. The Ithári'kaan fracture or reconstitute. The port cities gain or lose standing. The Sinalë acknowledge something. The Khazári are changed by the event, or unchanged in a way that is itself shattering. The party is now known – to every faction in the setting – as the people who were at the heart of the Kaliharan question. That reputation is an asset and a target for the rest of their careers. And somewhere, someone, is beginning to understand what the Ithári actually were.

---

The thematic point to keep firm across the whole structure: every path to saving Kalihara costs Kalihara something of what makes it worth saving. There is no clean outcome. The campaign is interesting precisely because the players cannot "win" it in the sense they win most campaigns – they can only choose what kind of Kalihara survives, if any, and at what cost to themselves and to the rest of Thalorna.
