> **GM-only adventure material.** The factions’ fears and proposed remedies do not establish the works’ purpose or the teachers’ fate. The open questions belong to [[doc-itharigm|The Ithari (GM)]].

Five adventure seeds and one campaign begin on the Kaliharan coast. Each seed runs on its own as a single adventure, and together they form the opening arc of the campaign. Each asks something different of a party: a wilderness expedition, a quiet investigation, a heist, an extraction and a negotiation.

## Seed 1—The Starfall Quarry

_Flavor: wilderness expedition / site clearance._

An [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] intermediary named [Dessama]{.name data-type="being/npc"} (a fixer who works out of a trading-house in the cosmopolitan quarter) commissions the party to travel to a specific impact site in the highland ridges of [[place-midhalnrgn|Midhalión]], where a meteor fell within the last decade. A small crew of six miners was sent to extract a specific volume of meteoric iron two seasons ago; they have not returned, and their last signal-fires stopped weeks before they were expected to reappear. The party is asked to find out what happened, recover any workable metal, and bring it back. The pay is extraordinary. The purpose is not explained.

What actually went wrong: the meteoric iron in this site has properties that destabilize something dormant beneath the impact crater—a pre-human ruin, a bound spirit of the local land, or a seam of some mineral that reacts dangerously to the star-metal's presence. The miners are dead or worse. Recovering the metal requires resolving whatever killed them. The clever adventuring question is how much iron to take—the more, the greater the intermediary's satisfaction, but also the greater the disturbance to whatever is down there.

Stakes: nothing world-shaking. But the intermediary is willing to pay enough that the party's curiosity about _why_ can become the hook for Seed 3 or the campaign proper. And the iron, once delivered, will be used—somewhere, somehow, by someone.

## Seed 2—The Silent Village

_Flavor: investigation / quiet horror._

A minor official in Ilovanu (not the port council proper—someone adjacent, a harbor-warden or a customs scribe) engages the party under unusual circumstances: the commission comes through back channels, and the official is anxious about being seen to issue it. A coastal village half a day's ride from the port has gone silent. No trade, no messenger, no response. The official wants the party to investigate and report back. They are emphatically not to contact anyone else about what they find.

What actually went wrong: the village's tended structure stopped responding to its custodian's procedures three months ago. The local [Ithakan]{.name data-type="affiliation/order"} elder, unable to admit this to the orthodox hierarchy in the interior, tried something—a foreign ritual, a bargain with a minor undead entity, or a half-remembered fragment of old lore—and it has taken the village over. The survivors, if any, are not quite themselves. The elder is alive but will beg the party not to report what they find: to do so is to expose the whole decay problem in a way that will fracture Kaliharan society.

Stakes: the party has to decide whether to report honestly (which damages the Ithakan and accelerates the schism) or lie by omission (which protects orthodoxy but leaves the actual problem in place). Either way, they now _know_ something that most people in [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]] do not. That knowledge is worth money, and it is also dangerous to possess.

## Seed 3—The Obsidian Consignment

_Flavor: heist / escort under hostile surveillance._

The party is hired to escort a large, discreet shipment of an obscure volcanic obsidian from a specific quarry on the [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankarian coast]] to a warehouse in Ilovanu. The shipment is large enough to be conspicuous, but the buyer has paid for it to travel inconspicuously. Three separate parties are trying to intercept it: a rival port-city faction from [[place-nalurithan|Nalurithan]] (who want to know what Ilovanu is up to), a [[lore-byzarianclt|Bizarian]] merchant house that has figured out the strategic significance (and wants leverage, not the obsidian), and a small band of [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] (the Arcane Order) scholars who have concluded that they need a sample for their own research.

What actually matters: getting the obsidian to the warehouse is only the start. The party discovers—through an accident, a leak, or a moment of curiosity at the warehouse—that the shipment is meant to be taken further, into the interior, past the boundary where foreigners are not permitted. The buyer asks them to continue. This is the first time the party is asked to set foot in [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]] proper. The pay doubles. The legal risk is enormous. And if they take the job, they meet someone on the other end who is waiting for them.

Stakes: this is the seed that most naturally opens into the campaign. The person waiting on the other end is a key figure in what comes later.

## Seed 4—The Defector

_Flavor: extraction / moral ambiguity._

_Marendos_ (master) [Velaro Thessan]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, a senior Thordimalon scholar who has spent eleven years quietly studying Kalihara from Ilovanu, has achieved a breakthrough. Using some combination of arcane observation, patient cultivation of the right people, and an unwise amount of personal risk, he has taken measurements of a failing Kaliharan work at close range—closer than any outsider in living memory. He has notes. He has diagrams. He believes he has a theoretical basis for replicating observed [[lore-itharisul|ithasul]] effects using Arcane Order techniques. He has not demonstrated that claim.

He is now being hunted. Not by official Kaliharan forces (Kalihara does not officially have foreign-facing enforcement), but by a small group of port-city operatives working for a faction that regards what he has done as an unforgivable violation. The Arcane Order wants him extracted alive with his notes intact. The party is hired.

Complication: Thessan is not entirely trustworthy. His stated intention is scholarly publication, but the party will discover evidence—a letter, a conversation overheard, a secondary buyer quietly contacted—that he intends to sell his findings to one of the great Ankarian powers. His work could genuinely help Kalihara if shared back. It could also give a foreign power leverage over the custodians whose work he observed. The party decides whether to deliver him intact, deliver him minus his notes, kill him, or turn him over to the Kaliharans—each of which has a different buyer willing to reward them and a different long-term consequence.

Stakes: whatever the party does, a copy of Thessan's work (full, partial, or corrupted) reaches some faction in Thalorna. The ithasul problem becomes slightly less Kalihara's exclusive concern.

## Seed 5—The Pilgrim's Request

_Flavor: diplomatic / exploratory / slow-burn._

A Kaliharan woman seeks out the party at a tavern, mercenary guild hall or scholar's hostel in [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]]. She is unaccompanied, travel-worn, and utterly calm. She asks for the party by reputation. She will not give her name, but she offers credentials: she claims to be Ithakan, and when pressed she produces a held stone the size of a knuckle—bone-white, hard as bone is hard, with none of the give a living work has under the hand. Only a custodian would have one, and handling a work teaches at a touch what it is. She asks the party to carry a specific object—a sealed lacquered case about the size of a child's hand—to a Senalian enclave, and to deliver it with a specific spoken message, in a language she will teach them phonetically, to whichever Senalo receives them.

She cannot go herself. To leave Kalihara, she says, would be to sever something she is not willing to sever. She cannot send another Kaliharan, for reasons she does not explain but which the party can infer: this errand is heretical, and if she is seen to send a Kaliharan the schism becomes visible.

Complications are layered. Finding a Senalian enclave requires being _permitted_ to find one, which means either having prior connections to the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] or finding a way to be interesting enough that the Senali allow themselves to be found. The orthodox Ithakan, if they learn of her commission, will treat her as a heretic; she will be killed or vanished, and anyone in contact with her will be suspect. She calls the object a _key_, but will not say to what. And the message, once the party learns the phonetics, turns out to carry a specific name—a name the Senali are likely to recognize, though the party will not know why.

Stakes: this seed, if successfully completed, initiates a relationship between the party and a faction of the Ithakan that will shape everything afterward. The Senali, once contacted on these terms, remember.

## The Campaign—_The Fading Lights_

A multi-year arc in five acts. The shape is: the party begins as outsiders hired for discrete jobs, gradually discovers the pattern those jobs belong to, becomes deeply entangled in Kaliharan factional struggle, and is eventually asked to make a civilizational choice that no Kaliharan can make and remain Kaliharan.

**Act I—The Commissions.** The party accepts several of the seeds above (or functional equivalents) across a span of months. No single job tells them much. But they begin to notice a pattern: the same Ilovanu intermediaries, the same extraordinary gold, the same specific kinds of material and information being sought. The repeated commissions give the party reason to question its employers. The climax of the act is the moment when those questions are answered by someone—a handler, a mentor, a defeated foe, the woman from Seed 5—and the party is told, plainly, what has been happening: the structures are failing, a faction within Kalihara is trying to save them, and the party has been working for that faction whether they knew it or not.

**Act II—The Reformers.** The party is invited into the confidence of the reformist faction, the Ithakan who have concluded that orthodoxy alone cannot save the island. They meet named figures on both sides: an elder reformer, a severe orthodox custodian, a port-city councilor playing both against the middle. They begin to understand the shape of the schism and the cost of keeping it hidden. Their work in this act is more intimate: protecting reformers from orthodox sanction, moving people rather than materials, carrying messages whose meaning they are allowed to know. They see Kalihara from inside for the first time. They meet a Kaliharan child being trained in the custodial disciplines and understand, in a way outsiders rarely do, what this civilization has been preserving. The act closes with a disaster: one of the major structures fails, and a storm strikes a coastal district. Thousands die. The sequence does not prove that the work controlled the storm. Orthodoxy blames the reformers for their interference; the reformers blame orthodoxy for its inflexibility. The faction war goes hot.

**Act III—The Keystone.** The reformers single out a failing work they call the keystone. They fear that losing it will make the other failures impossible to manage, but cannot establish what it was built to do or how it relates to the other works. Their available iron and obsidian have not restored its response to tending. They dispatch the party to seek help, observations and records beyond the island. The expedition can follow three paths:

- _The Khazárian branch._ The party seeks out the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]], breaking the island's long non-contact. Its commission is to obtain skilled help and ask whether anything in Khazari practice bears on the material the custodians handle. Assistance comes with conditions; an agreement need not include an account of the works' origin.

- _The Senali branch._ After Seed 5 or its equivalent, the party asks what the [[lore-flksenali|Senali]] remember of the Earlier Strangers and the works they received. The Senali can share their own limited testimony, or refuse it. Their account supplies no destination for the Kaliharan teachers and no answer to why they left.

- _The distant-works branch._ A fragment in Thessan's notes or a Thordimalon manuscript leads the party to another site whose appearance interests the scholars. The people beside it know their own place and their own stories. The party must earn access and record what it can observe; resemblance does not establish common makers or purpose. A reported white, cracked work offers a practical lead for the custodians seeking held stones, without settling those questions.

Each branch changes the final act. A rich campaign will touch all three.

**Act IV—The Choice.** Whatever the party has found in Act III, it produces an option—or several options—none of them clean. Possibilities include:

- Obtaining a held stone from a distant work. [[affiliation-councilisland|The Council of the Island]] wants material for the custodians' attempts at tending. Nothing is taken from a living work; only a white, cracked work has ever given up a piece. The place the party reaches is sacred to its neighbors, who hold that it keeps something away from them. The party cannot settle that belief by declaring the work dead. Permission, theft and refusal each carry a cost, and bringing a stone home does not guarantee a repair.
- Opening the records. A faction offers to share its observations with foreign scholars and craftspeople in return for sustained help. Doing so surrenders control of knowledge the custodians have kept for generations. The party must negotiate what is shared, who may see it and what protection the custodians receive.
- A Khazárian partnership. Skilled assistance may cost access to the Kaliharan sites, an exchange of cultural knowledge or a concession the custodians find humiliating. The bargain concerns present work and obligations; it supplies no secret account of the ancient makers.
- Changing custodial authority. The reformers seek permission to alter the inherited tending and admit helpers whom orthodoxy excludes. The party can support them, defend the existing custodians' control or broker a narrower trial. Nobody can promise that a change will restore a failing work, and those asked to bear the risk have their own interests.

**Act V—The Consequences.** Whatever the party chooses, consequences cascade. The ten-thousand-year silence between Kalihara and the rest of Thalorna ends, in one direction or another. The Ithakan fracture or reconstitute. The port cities gain or lose standing. The Senali acknowledge something. The Khazari are changed by the event, or unchanged in a way that is itself shattering. The party is now known—to every faction in the setting—as the people who were at the heart of the Kaliharan question. That reputation is an asset and a target for the rest of their careers. What the party has changed is the custody of knowledge and the lives bound to it. The identity and purposes of the teachers remain unresolved.

---

The thematic point to keep firm across the whole structure: every path to saving Kalihara costs Kalihara something of what makes it worth saving. There is no clean outcome. The campaign is interesting precisely because the players cannot "win" it in the sense they win most campaigns—they can only choose what kind of Kalihara survives, if any, and at what cost to themselves and to the rest of Thalorna.
