## Overview {#overview}

Every caravan from the desert interior pays its toll at [[place-tahrodan|Tahrodan]], the fortress city at the crossroads of the [[affiliation-kngdmmrdd|Kingdom of Amradad]], on its way down to the port of [[place-valmakhend|Valmakhend]]. Every load is weighed, counted and sealed at the gate, except a load that comes in under a fire-temple's seal. The temples hold estates in the oases round the city, and by the right of their endowments what comes in under their seal pays nothing. A priest will lend his seal to a bale of silk for a share of its price, and the toll-house loses more to sealed loads than to smugglers.

This week a caravan of twenty-four camels came in on the Khazrin road with every bale under the seal of one temple. The toll-house's clerk has held it at the gate for three days and cannot open it without the priests' consent. In the same toll-house sits a scribe of the High Shekhul's observatory, held for noting down the weights of the sealed bales.

The party enters as the governor's hired eyes, as the High Shekhul's discreet messengers, or as guards for the caravan when it is let go down to the coast.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The quarrel.** [[being-ghzrnmzhn|Ghezrun Mazhen]], the _tagemader_ of Tahrodan, wants the temple seals opened and checked at the gate. The priests call it sacrilege to search an offering, and they hold the rites the governor must be seen to keep. Neither can rule the city without the other, and Ghezrun has spent years keeping it that way.

**The seal.** The seal on the caravan is that of [Maneshir Khormaz]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, Shekhul of one of the fire-temples of the temple district. He takes no share for himself. The load's owner has endowed his temple for years, and his seal is the return on that endowment.

**The load.** Under the silk, in six of the twenty-four bales, is silver sewn in as coin, a few hundred pounds of it in all. It is the money of the [[affiliation-cnclprinces|Council of the Princes]], the donations of Tellumi water-masters, bankers and physicians who work for Atarzád employers in the held cities, on its way down to the Council's schools and shrines across the strait. A Tellumi factor of the Council rides with the caravan as a pilgrim. The toll would cost the Council silver it can spare. The ledger would cost it more: the toll ledgers go in audit to [[place-shebarakhend|Shebarakhend]], and the court's great houses, who resent being outspent by the exile courts in their own capital, would read there exactly how much silver the exile houses move.

**The High Shekhul's silence.** [[being-aravdnstrgz|Aravdán the Stargazer]], High Shekhul of the [[place-grtfiretmpl|Great Fire-Temple]] and convenor of the Council of the Princes, judges that some priests at Tahrodan lend their temples' seals to loads no temple owns. He has said nothing, because a public word would give the governor a victory over every temple. He has asked the Keeper of the Fire-Lineage to list which seals are lent and by whom, and he sent a scribe of his observatory, [Girnah]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, to the toll-house to note the weights of sealed loads against what their seals declare. He does not know that this caravan's load belongs to the Council he convenes.

**The tablet.** Girnah's wax tablet shows that six of the bales weigh three times what silk of their size should. Whoever reads it has proof that the load is not an offering without breaking a seal. [Qavadar Namiran]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the toll-house's clerk, has not read it yet, because he wants to read it in front of witnesses.

**The escort.** The riders who brought the caravan from the handover well were [[being-deyenashamay|Deyena Shamayan]]'s. She expects her troop to be named in whatever comes of it.

**The law.** The law-desk of the Great Fire-Temple rules on every question of rite put to the senior house. Its Delkoshekh, [[being-mazinanshirz|Mazinan Shirzari]], rules by precedent, and a ruling from her that a load no temple owns is no offering would let the governor open such loads and leave true offerings sealed.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Nobody in this story is stealing for himself. The scenario ends when the bales are opened, cross the strait or are bargained for, and when the city's quarrel between the toll-house and the temples has a ruling or a scandal.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The governor's eyes.** Ghezrun hires a party to travel with the caravan to the coast and learn what was under the seal and who sold it.
- **The scribe's tablet.** Aravdán asks a party to bring Girnah's tablet out of the toll-house before the clerk reads it, and does not say why it matters.
- **The escort's name.** Deyena Shamayan wants strangers to learn what the bales held before her troop is named.
- **The caravan's guards.** The caravan-master, [Salmara]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, hires guards for the road down to Valmakhend, because she has heard that the toll-house means to cut a bale on the way.

## The Opening {#opening}

The yard inside Tahrodan's eastern gate at noon, hot and full of flies. Twenty-four camels kneel in a row, every bale stamped with the same red wax disc. A clerk in a dusty robe sits on a stool in front of them with a tablet on his knee.

> "Offerings," he says, without looking up. "Twenty-four camels of offerings. The god is very well supplied this year."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Toll-House

**Purpose.** The sealed caravan, the clerk and the scribe.

**Situation.** The toll-house inside the citadel gate, where every load is weighed, counted and sealed, and the clerks of the Yorvabun keep the ledgers that go to Shebarakhend. The sealed caravan has been held in the eastern yard for three days.

**NPCs and threats.** Qavadar Namiran, patient and certain, who has watched sealed loads pass for twenty years. Girnah, a young scribe in an observatory robe, held in a side room with her tablet in a locked box on the clerk's table. Salmara, the caravan-master, paying for fodder by the day. The Tellumi pilgrim who rides with the caravan and prays a great deal.

**Player choices.** Talk the clerk into releasing Girnah, take the tablet from the box, examine the bales without breaking a seal, or talk to the pilgrim.

**Clues and rewards.** Six of the camels stand lower under their loads than silk would make them. Girnah says only that she was told to write weights and nothing else. The pilgrim's hands are soft, and he asks too often when the caravan may go.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the tablet has the proof and the clerk's enmity. A party that leaves it lets Qavadar read it in his own time.

**If bypassed.** Qavadar reads the tablet before witnesses the next morning, and the quarrel moves to the temple district.

### Scene Two: The Temple District

**Purpose.** The priests' side, and the seal's owner.

**Situation.** The fire-temples in the center of the city, where every household's flame is lit from a temple's and the governor must be seen to honor the priests. The priests are meeting in the court of Maneshir's temple.

**NPCs and threats.** Maneshir Khormaz, grave and kind, who says that what comes under his seal is a gift to his temple and that searching a gift is sacrilege. The other priests, some of whom lend their seals for a share and want him defended, and some of whom do not and want him quiet.

**Player choices.** Ask Maneshir who endowed his temple, threaten him with the tablet, or ask the priests what they will do if the governor opens the bales.

**Clues and rewards.** The temple's endowment deeds, if the party sees them, name a Tellumi benefactor and no Amradi house. The priests say that if a sealed bale is cut at the gate they will not kindle the citadel's flame at the next festival, and the governor will have to be seen without it.

**Consequences.** The priests know the party's errand.

**If bypassed.** The priests hear of the party from the clerk, and Maneshir's door is shut.

### Scene Three: The Governor's Table

**Purpose.** Ghezrun's offer and Deyena's fear.

**Situation.** The governor's palace inside the citadel gate, at dinner. Ghezrun's table is where a delay is forgiven.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-ghzrnmzhn|Ghezrun Mazhen]], warm and fluent, who remembers the party's names and the price of everything. [[being-deyenashamay|Deyena Shamayan]], commander of the mounted garrison, who sits at the foot of the table and says little.

**Player choices.** Accept Ghezrun's commission, sell him the tablet, or ask Deyena what her riders saw.

**Clues and rewards.** Ghezrun lets the caravan go down to the coast, so that the party can learn what is under the seal away from the priests' eyes. Deyena says her riders took over the caravan at the handover well, that it came down the Khazrin road from the held cities, and that the pilgrim paid the escort in Tellumi coin.

**Consequences.** The caravan leaves Tahrodan the next morning with the party in it.

**If bypassed.** Ghezrun hires others to cut a bale on the road, and the party meets them there.

### Scene Four: The Road Down

**Purpose.** The truth under the silk, and an attempt to take it.

**Situation.** The road from Tahrodan down to Valmakhend, the one road that leaves the crossroads for the sea. On the second night the caravan camps at a walled well.

**NPCs and threats.** Men hired by Ghezrun or by the toll-house, who come in the dark to cut a bale. The Tellumi factor, who drops the pilgrim's manner when the knives come out.

**Player choices.** Stop the cutters, let them cut, or cut a bale themselves.

**Clues and rewards.** A cut into one of the heavy bales spills silver among the silk. The factor, caught, tells the truth: the Council's money, the schools across the strait, the endowment, and what the ledger in Shebarakhend would show the great houses. He offers the party Council silver to see the bales onto the ferry, and says that a messenger who alters what he carries is never hired by any of the seven houses again.

**Consequences.** At [[place-valmakhend|Valmakhend]] the factor means to put the silver in the Balinuni strongroom on the quay, which the harbor-watch does not search, until a ferry takes it across.

**If bypassed.** The bales reach the quay unopened, and the party learns the truth only if it searches the strongroom.

### Scene Five: The High Shekhul's Rooms

**Purpose.** The climax before the man who must break his silence.

**Situation.** The rooms of the High Shekhul in the halls round the fire-house of the [[place-grtfiretmpl|Great Fire-Temple]] in Shebarakhend. Pleaders from the governor and from the priests of Tahrodan are already waiting in the outer court.

**NPCs and threats.** [[being-aravdnstrgz|Aravdán the Stargazer]], mild and exact, who answers a quarrel with a question about the stars. [[being-mazinanshirz|Mazinan Shirzari]], the Delkoshekh of Law, who presses for a ruling. Both pleaders.

**Player choices.**

- _Give Aravdán the truth._ He learns that the silver is his own Council's, and he must choose in front of the party what to say of the seals.
- _Ask for a ruling from the law-desk._ Mazinan rules that a load no temple owns is no offering, and the governor may open such loads at the gate.
- _Broker a quiet bargain._ The Council pays the toll at Ghezrun's table in private, the ledger records the silk and not the silver, and the governor holds the secret over the Council and the temples both.
- _Carry the truth to the great houses._ The court's great houses pay for it, and the quarrel with the exile courts sharpens.

**Clues and rewards.** Aravdán pays in the temple's goodwill and a letter of standing from the senior house of the faith. Ghezrun pays in silver. The factor pays in the Council's silver and in future work.

**Consequences.** Whatever the party chooses, the temples of Tahrodan and the toll-house have a ruling or a scandal before the next festival.

**If bypassed.** Aravdán gives no ruling, the pleaders go home, and the seals go on being lent.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Qavadar reads Girnah's tablet before witnesses, and Ghezrun orders one bale cut at the gate. Silver spills into the yard. The priests refuse to kindle the citadel's flame at the next festival, the governor is seen without it, and the city's quarrel between the toll-house and the temples breaks into the open. The toll is levied, the ledger goes to Shebarakhend with the Council's silver written in it, and the great houses read the sum aloud in the divans. The pleaders carry the quarrel to the High Shekhul, who cannot leave it unanswered.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The law-desk rules.** Loads no temple owns are opened at the gate, true offerings pass sealed, and the seal-lending at Tahrodan ends or grows cleverer. Ghezrun has his victory and loses some of the priests' need for him.
- **The quiet bargain.** The silver crosses the strait, Ghezrun holds a secret over the Council of the Princes and the temples of Tahrodan, and the party is the only one outside the bargain who knows of it.
- **The great houses told.** The quarrel between the court and the exile courts sharpens in every divan, and the exile houses look for whoever sold them.
- **Girnah freed.** The scribe goes home to the observatory with or without her tablet, and Aravdán knows who brought her.
