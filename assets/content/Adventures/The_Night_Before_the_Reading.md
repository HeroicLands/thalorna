## Overview {#overview}

[[place-themeasure|The Measure]] stands at the upstream point of [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]], the Sacred Island of [[place-galezkara|Galezkara]]: a stone stair and shaft running down to the river, its wall cut with the mark of every cubit of the flood. Through [[lore-azletssn|Azlet]], the Inundation, the Reader of the Measure goes down to the water each day and carries the count to the [[place-greattempluqaa|Great Temple of Uqa'a]], and at the crest the priests proclaim the number to the city and the empire. A full Measure means plenty and a heavy levy, a low one means dearth, and a failed one means famine. The Measure has read short at two Inundations, and the river is coming to its third crest low again.

The number this year carries more than the harvest. [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]], the Gar-Au, is addressed as divine. Nobody believes it, everyone knows nobody believes it, and the fiction holds as long as the river keeps its side of it. The granaries are thinning, the selat governors quietly hold back their render, and the succession is disputed between factions of the bureaucracy and the priesthood. Her nephew [[being-anlaghur|Anlaghur]] stands near enough the throne that the court asks the question, and [[being-gerragezeelan|Gerra Geze'el'Anlaghu]], Halzi'a of [[affiliation-selatlennnzt|The Selat of Lenen-Nezut]], presses the descent of [[affiliation-gezeelanlaghu|Geze'el'Anlaghu]], the house of three old Gar-Auu. Both have come to the capital for the proclamation.

The Gar-Au wants someone who belongs to no bureau to stand at the gauge through the night before the reading and tell her what they see. The party enters as her summoned strangers, as guests at the nephew's table on the same night, or as extra hands the keeper of the Measure takes on for the crest.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The water.** The river is low again. Anyone who goes down the stair with a lamp can read the wall, and the cut cubits show a low crest, not a failed one: dearth, a lighter levy and a hard year, but no famine. The referee decides how near the line the water stands. It stands above it.

**The carrying.** The count is read on the stair and walked across the island to the temple, and the number the priests proclaim is the number that arrives. A Gar-Au of [[affiliation-garzinelrelu|Gar-Zin'el'Relu]] once had the Measure read by other hands to hide a failing flood ([[lore-failfloods|The Failing Floods]]), and since then every reading has had to be checked against the wall. The check is made on the stair, where anyone with a lamp can read the cubits, and the count is then walked across the island unchecked, so the carry is the weak point. A number proclaimed at dawn goes up and down the river on the first boats, and any correction follows it slowly.

**The other hands.** The keeper's senior apprentice has been paid to carry a count one cubit lower than the water stands, low enough that the priests proclaim a failed Measure. At the temple supper on the eve of the crest he puts a purge in the Reader's cup, enough to keep the old priest off the stair until noon, and on a crest morning when the Reader cannot go down, the keeper's practice is to send his senior apprentice down with the Reader's tally-board in his place. He was paid in silver pieces attested at a temple upriver, by the steward of an upper-river Halzi'a whose render has come in short. A failed Measure excuses every short render in the empire and lays the dearth at the throne's door. The referee chooses the selat. [[being-zinurilsltr|Zinuri Les'Leretu]]'s reckoning names it among those that held back.

> [!WARNING]
> Keep the apprentice's payment back until the party has a reason to follow him. The new sandals and the paid gatehouse debts are the early signs, and a party that reads the wall learns the true count without learning that someone has paid to change it.

**The Gar-Au's date.** Meqes has drawn on the throne's household treasure to buy small lots of Bethuan grain through the mediators of [[affiliation-genzetpalu|Genzet'Palu]] and has told the bureaus nothing. She has set a date by which she announces a failed flood or confesses the purchases, and she has tied it to this proclamation. A failed Measure lets her announce the failed flood and bring the grain out as the throne's provision. A low one leaves her the confession. She knows which number she would rather hear, which is why she has sent for strangers.

**The table.** Anlaghur gives a dinner in the city on the night before the reading, and the Gar-Au wants to know who else is seated. His guests are Halzi'a of the upper river who are in the capital for the proclamation, a Lem'Nelgir of the Great Temple, and [[being-khelansisgth|Khelansi Sa'Gathau]], Halzi'a of [[affiliation-capitalselat|The Capital Selat]], who sets the capital's ration by the number and leaves early to see to the granary watch.

**The nephew's roll.** Anlaghur holds a full copy of Zinuri Les'Leretu's reckoning of the upper selatu, which names each Halzi'a that held back render and by how much. The Overseer sent it to the nephew's household himself. Anlaghur seats those Halzi'a nearest him and talks to them as a creditor talks to debtors. A party that reads the roll can name the selat whose silver bought the apprentice.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The water does not lie and the wall cannot be commanded. The scenario ends at dawn, when a number is proclaimed at the Great Temple, and what the party has done by then decides which number it is and who learns why.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The leader.** The Gar-Au summons a party from outside the bureaus to stand at the Measure through the night before the reading and report to her what they see.
- **The wanderer.** Anlaghur invites the party to dine on the same night, and the Gar-Au asks them to go and tell her who else is seated.
- **The thief or the soldier.** The keeper of the Measure takes on extra hands for the crest to keep the curious off the stair head, and pays by the night.
- **The scholar.** Khelansi Sa'Gathau trusts a tally-cord over an assurance, and he pays a party to bring him the reading off the wall before the temple proclaims it.

## The Opening {#opening}

The foot of the [[place-bridgecomingfrth|Bridge of the Coming-Forth]] at dusk, the last evening before the crest. The queue for the island has thinned to petitioners who mean to sleep on the ramp. Below the causeway the river slaps at the stone well under the line the pilgrims' lamps were set for. A sergeant of the royal guard reads the party's summons twice by torchlight.

> "Everything on the island is spoken for," he says, and steps aside. "Tonight that includes you."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Foot of the Bridge

**Purpose.** Cross to the island, and hear the city argue about the number before anyone knows it.

**Situation.** [[place-qeltge|Qelt-Ge]], the plaza where [[place-waluqaa|Wal-Uqa'a]], the Sun's Road, meets the river, is full on the eve of the crest. Hawkers sell clay cubit-marks for luck. A burial club of [[place-garqethar|Gar-Qethar]] puts up its awning to read [[lore-qethgezerker|The Tale of Gezerker]], the scribe who wrote the flood false for a king who called himself a god. Gerra Geze'el'Anlaghu's partisans stand along the ramp and tell anyone who stops that the river answers the house that holds the throne. The royal guard holds the island end, and a summons is the only thing that carries a stranger across.

**NPCs and threats.** The sergeant of the royal guard, who keeps a list of everyone he passes. Gerra Geze'el'Anlaghu among his partisans, courteous and certain, who asks the party what a third low crest says about the throne. The Gar-Au, who receives the party in a small court inside the island gate. She is practical, exact and patient, and she asks for what they see, not what they think.

**Player choices.** Take the Gar-Au's charge, hear Gerra out, accept the invitation that a servant of Anlaghur's brings down the ramp, or ask the sergeant who else has crossed tonight.

**Clues and rewards.** The sergeant's list: the keeper's apprentices crossed at noon, the Reader of the Measure crossed for the temple supper, and a steward in upriver dress crossed with a petition for the Chancellor of Records and has not come back. The Gar-Au gives the party a household token that the guard at the stair head honors.

**Consequences.** The party has the throne's leave to be on the island all night. Gerra knows their faces.

**If bypassed.** A party hired by the keeper crosses with the apprentices at noon and first meets the Gar-Au at dawn, in the forecourt, as one more face in the crowd.

### Scene Two: The Stair

**Purpose.** Learn the Measure, read the wall, and meet the people who carry the number.

**Situation.** The upstream point of the island at nightfall, where the river first strikes the sacred ground. The stair runs down the shaft to the water, and the cut cubits climb the wall beside it. The keeper of the Measure, an old man who has not left the stair head on a crest night in thirty years, keeps a lamp on the top step. His apprentices sleep by turns in the gatehouse. The Reader of the Measure, an old priest of the [[affiliation-uqaa|Faith of Uqa'a]], has gone across the island to the temple supper.

**NPCs and threats.** The keeper, who makes every newcomer read the cubits aloud from the bottom before he lets them stand at the top. The senior apprentice, quick and pleasant, who offers to show the party the stair and leaves for the temple supper with the Reader's cloak over his arm. The water at the foot of the stair, black, low and fast.

**Player choices.** Read the wall themselves, sit the night with the keeper, follow the senior apprentice to the supper, or go to Anlaghur's table.

**Clues and rewards.** The wall gives the water's height to anyone who reads it, so the party can know the true count before the priests do. The keeper says the rule he teaches every apprentice: the number is read as the water stands, and it is not bent for the god-king or for anyone. The senior apprentice wears new sandals and has paid his gatehouse debts this week. If the Reader cannot go down at dawn, the keeper's practice is to send his senior apprentice with the tally-board.

**Consequences.** A party that has read the wall carries a number of its own, and every faction in the city wants it first.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches the stair at dawn with the procession and has the count only on the word of whoever carries it.

### Scene Three: The Nephew's Table

**Purpose.** See who courts whom on the night before the number, and learn who has been holding back.

**Situation.** A house in Galezkara lent to Anlaghur for the festival, where he gives a dinner while the city waits. Musicians play in the court. His guests are two Halzi'a of the upper river, a Lem'Nelgir of the Great Temple and Khelansi Sa'Gathau, and the talk at every couch is the number.

**NPCs and threats.** Anlaghur, an easy host whose temperament keeps the question of his succession open. Khelansi Sa'Gathau, dry and precise, who speaks in figures and trusts none he has not counted. The two upriver Halzi'a, who laugh too readily at the nephew's jokes. A household steward who notices anyone who leaves the court.

**Player choices.** Watch the seating, talk to Khelansi about the capital's ration, ask Anlaghur what he expects at dawn, or slip away into the house's upper rooms.

**Clues and rewards.** Anlaghur seats the upriver Halzi'a nearest him. In his writing room a sealed roll in the hand of an Overseer lies among his letters. Khelansi tells the party what the capital's granaries hold against a failed Measure, and goes quiet when asked where the most recent lots came from.

**Consequences.** The Gar-Au wants the guest list, and the party has it. Anlaghur notices whoever reads his papers, and he answers it with an invitation, not a guard.

**If bypassed.** The party hears at dawn who dined with the nephew and learns nothing of what he holds.

### Scene Four: The Annex

**Purpose.** Check the night's number against the record, and find where the silver came from.

**Situation.** The archive's annex on Yath-Telgu, where the reckonings of the selatu are shelved beside the king-list. [[being-amnagezu2|Amnagezu]], the Chancellor of Records, keeps it, and she is on the island for the proclamation. Lamps burn among the shelves through the night.

**NPCs and threats.** Amnagezu, who knows where every document sits and answers a vague question vaguely. A clerk who opens nothing after dark without her word.

**Player choices.** Ask for the old crest readings, for a selat's renders, for the meaning of a temple's attestation on a silver piece, or for the names of everyone who has asked for the same things this week.

**Clues and rewards.** The old readings set this year's wall beside the years of the Failing Floods. Silver attested at a temple can be traced to the selat that temple serves, and a party carrying a piece of the apprentice's money learns where it came from. The renders show which upper selatu came in short. Amnagezu, asked plainly, says that a steward from upriver asked this week to see the readings of the Failing Floods.

**Consequences.** A party that leaves the annex with the number, the silver's selat and the shortfall can make the dawn go any of several ways.

**If bypassed.** The party reaches dawn knowing the count is wrong but not who paid for it.

### Scene Five: Dawn at the Temple

**Purpose.** The number is proclaimed.

**Situation.** First light. The Reader of the Measure lies sick in the temple's lodging, and the keeper sends his senior apprentice down the stair with the tally-board. The apprentice reads, climbs and walks the count across the island to the Great Temple's forecourt, where the Thaz'Lekhau of Uqa'a waits with the priests, the court stands on the steps and the city crowds the far bank and the water's edge. The Gar-Au is in the forecourt. Gerra's partisans are on the far bank and Anlaghur's guests on the steps.

**NPCs and threats.** The senior apprentice, who cannot turn back now. The keeper, who does not leave the stair head. The Thaz'Lekhau, who proclaims the number he is handed. The royal guard, who move only on the Gar-Au's word. The upriver steward from the sergeant's list, near the forecourt gate.

**Player choices.**

- _Let the count go._ The priests proclaim the number the apprentice carries.
- _Stop the apprentice on the island._ The keeper sends another hand down, and the count off the wall reaches the temple late, while the court waits on the steps.
- _Charge the carrier in the forecourt._ Anyone can read the wall, and the priests send down to read it. The falsifying of the Measure is among the gravest crimes a Khelathi can name, and a public charge on the morning of the proclamation is a scandal the city remembers.
- _Go to the Gar-Au first._ The party tells her what they saw before the count arrives, and the choice is hers.

**Clues and rewards.** The Gar-Au pays in favor and in the household's token, which opens doors on the island. Khelansi pays for the true number. The steward pays for silence, and Anlaghur pays for the steward's name.

**Consequences.** The number proclaimed sets the year's levy, and every selat reckons its render by it.

**If bypassed.** The apprentice walks his count across the island unchallenged, and the priests proclaim it.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The apprentice carries his count to the forecourt and the Thaz'Lekhau proclaims a failed Measure. The boats carry the number upriver and down by noon. Every Halzi'a who held back render now has the flood to answer for it, Gerra's partisans call a failed flood the throne's third judgment, and the Gar-Au announces famine and opens grain the bureaus did not know the throne held. The keeper saw the water at the crest by his own lamp, and he says so to the Thaz'Lekhau before the day is out. Within the month the city knows that the number proclaimed in the reign of Meqes XVI and the water on the wall disagree, and every faction names a different culprit.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

- **The true number.** A low Measure is proclaimed. The levy is lighter, the year is hard, and the Halzi'a who held back render have no flood to stand behind. The Gar-Au has the number she sent for.
- **A failed Measure stands.** The selatu are forgiven their short renders by the flood and the throne has its famine. The keeper still reports his own true reading to the Thaz'Lekhau before the day is out, and within the month the city knows that the number proclaimed and the water on the wall disagree.
- **The carrier charged.** The apprentice answers for the count, and the court's question turns from the flood to who paid. The steward's selat answers for its steward, and the Halzi'a who sat nearest the nephew at dinner learn what it costs to be named.
- **The nephew's roll.** A party that carries word of Anlaghur's papers to the Gar-Au, or the steward's name to Anlaghur, has taken a side in the succession, and both of them remember it.
