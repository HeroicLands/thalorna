## Overview {#overview}

In [[place-beravel|Péravel]] the Order and the native institutions fight with patronage, appointments and the placing of students, and the booksellers do well out of it. This scenario follows a single leaf of paper through that fight. A copyist of the book quarter, [[being-ulwendareth|Ulwen Dareth]], has a page of a cipher that someone paid him to copy and then did not collect. He hires the party to learn what it says. A grove-elder of the basin, [[being-gulmandelvan|Gulmand Élvannde]], hires them to carry a petition into the Oldest Court past the Proctor, who has been told not to admit it. Both errands lead to the same woman, [[being-netharahalre|Néthara Halren]], the Keeper of the Collections.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The ledger.** The [[affiliation-univperavel|University]]'s catalog gives each object in the four collections its form and its place on the shelf and records no provenance. Néthara keeps provenance elsewhere, in a private ledger in a cipher of her own: each object is entered with the ground it came from, the day it was cut or lifted, and the name of the guard who did it. She keeps it for the Order, so that an Araelwen can be found again, and for herself, because she believes the day will come when what has been taken must be put back. She has told no one which of those she thinks the more likely.

**The page.** Ulwen's page is a copy of one leaf, made from the original by a servant of the [[affiliation-fisera|Fisera]] house who was paid by someone outside the University and who then took fright. The leaf lists four objects: a bowl from a holy well in the basin above Péravel, a cairn-stone, a root-and-stone figure from [[place-siralonke|Siralonke]] and a green strip of bark. Each has its ground, its day and a guard's name.

**The petition.** Gulmand's grove keeps the well. The Council has petitioned the Oldest Court about objects taken from groves and holy wells, and the Rector, [[being-thorenveldar|Thoren Véldarin]], answers each petition with an invitation to submit it formally, which is to say, to wait. The Proctor of the gate, [[being-merodansalth|Merodan Salthar]], has been told to turn this one back. The page would turn a petition into evidence.

**The Sekran.** An inquirer of the [[affiliation-sekran|Arcane Inquisition]] has known of the ledger for years. The inquisition wants it, because it names the ground and the guards, and the Keeper's cipher has not yet beaten it. The Fitter of Expeditions, [[being-pelraorsel|Pelra Orsel]], does not know the ledger exists and would be dismayed to learn of it.

**The guards.** The names on the leaf include a guard who is now a sellsword at Liranel and another who is dead. Whoever holds the leaf knows who lifted a bowl from a well and could make him answer to the Wardens. The Wardens are not at Péravel, but the elder's runners reach them.

**The Colleges.** The Bardic Circle is split on the Order. The Envoys' College takes its patronage and the other three do not. A Bard of one of the refusing Colleges, [[being-kurthandlauv|Kurthand Láuvinnde]], wants to know what the Envoys' College was paid. The page does not say. The ledger's other leaves might.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Néthara has not decided. The party's behavior toward her decides it, and the scenario ends when she does.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The copyist.** Ulwen pays the party to learn what the page says and who wanted it copied.
- **The petition.** Gulmand pays the party to carry a petition past the Proctor into the Oldest Court.
- **The bard.** Kurthand asks the party to learn what the Envoys' College was paid before the Circle sits.
- **The inquirer.** A quiet man in gray offers the party a purse for any paper in a cipher it comes across, and asks nothing about where it was found.

## The Opening {#opening}

A stall in the book quarter at dusk, between a binder's shop and a stall that sells tinted maps. Ulwen puts a sheet of paper down on a bench with three fingers and does not let go of it.

> "Somebody paid me for this and never came," he says. "Whatever it is, it is not a recipe."

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Book Quarter

**Purpose.** The page, the trade and the first watchers.

**Situation.** The book quarter of Péravel. Copyists, binders, translators, licensed by the University. A scribe without a license sells nothing a court will buy.

**NPCs and threats.** Ulwen, nervous and honest. A Fitter's clerk who walks past twice. A man in gray at the corner of the lane.

**Player choices.** Read the page, copy it, take it, or follow the man in gray.

**Clues and rewards.** The page is a cipher of letters and ground-marks. A binder who has seen Néthara's hand says it is hers. A servant of the Fisera house has been paying for lodgings out of his savings.

**Consequences.** A party that takes the page is a party the Sekran has noticed.

**If bypassed.** The Fitter's clerk buys the page from Ulwen at twice the price and burns it.

### Scene Two: The Oldest Court

**Purpose.** The petition and the gate.

**Situation.** The dark stone hall beside the river, the Proctor's lodge, and the courts that stand around it. The Chapter of Courts meets at the turn of the year, and the court's business is stacked on the Proctor's table.

**NPCs and threats.** Merodan, who has been told what to say. Gulmand, who has waited six weeks. Thoren Véldarin, who is courteous and learned in the law and has not read the petition.

**Player choices.** Carry the petition openly, find a fellow to sponsor it, wait, or put the page before the Rector.

**Clues and rewards.** The Rector says what he always says. He does not say that the University is hiding anything, and he does not say that it is not.

**Consequences.** A petition carried openly is turned back. A petition carried with a leaf of the Keeper's cipher is another matter, and the Rector sees it for the first time.

**If bypassed.** The petition waits, and Gulmand goes back to the basin with it.

### Scene Three: The Keeper

**Purpose.** The woman who knows every object by weight.

**Situation.** The Fisera house's collection, a long room with shelves, tags and a herbarium that holds a strip of bark that has stayed green. Néthara works at a table under a lamp.

**NPCs and threats.** Néthara, meticulous and unhurried. A servant who has not slept. The Sekran's inquirer, who comes in quietly and asks for a catalog.

**Player choices.** Put the page before her, ask about the bowl, ask about the guard, or ask what the ledger is for.

**Clues and rewards.** Néthara recognizes her own hand. She says nothing for a long time. She says the ledger exists, and that she has never decided what it is for.

**Consequences.** A party that threatens her is a party she gives nothing. A party that asks her what she believes may be the first person who has.

**If bypassed.** The Sekran's inquirer takes the servant instead, and the servant gives up the ledger's hiding place.

### Scene Four: The Bridge at Dusk

**Purpose.** Her answer.

**Situation.** One of the pale stone bridges, grown over with trained vine, at the hour the lamps are lit. Néthara has asked the party to meet her there.

**NPCs and threats.** Néthara, with a bundle. The inquirer, who is behind her on the bridge. The Fitter's clerk, who is behind the party.

**Player choices.** Take the ledger, take the bowl, let the Keeper keep both, or let the inquirer take them.

**Clues and rewards.** If she has been treated as a person, she gives the bowl to the party to carry to Gulmand's grove. If she has been threatened, she drops the bundle in the river.

**Consequences.** Return of the bowl begins the Wardens' accounting. Loss of the ledger ends it. The Sekran keeps the Keeper either way.

> [!WARNING]
> The ledger names guards. A party that hands it to the Wardens, or to a grove-elder who hands it to the Wardens, starts a reckoning with every guard on its pages and the Order that hired them.

**If bypassed.** The inquirer takes the Keeper from the bridge before she says anything.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

Ulwen's page is bought and burned. Gulmand's petition waits, and the Rector answers it in the spring with an invitation to submit it formally. The Sekran's inquirer takes the servant and finds the ledger's hiding place within the month. Néthara is questioned, and the cipher is read. The Order learns the ground of every object in its collections, and the Wardens learn that it has.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

The Order holds the ledger, or the Wardens do, or Néthara carries it into the river. The bowl is at a well in the basin, or in a cellar. Some guard on the leaf answers to the Wardens. The Circle sits on the Envoys' College with or without the page. The University teaches on, and the book quarter sells the argument.
