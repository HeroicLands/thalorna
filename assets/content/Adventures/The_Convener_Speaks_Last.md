## Overview {#overview}

Every fourth dry season the paramounts of the five clans of the [[affiliation-nylbtrblntn|Nyáluba Tribal Nation]] meet as the [[affiliation-greatlubemo|Great Lubemo]] in the dry-stone enclosure of [[place-hetolubemo|Heto Lubemo]]. The Lubemo does not vote. It talks until the objections stop, in a fixed order with the senior speaker last, and a matter the paramounts cannot agree on waits four years.

This year, 720, the first business is a war. The [[affiliation-ruwozi|Ruwozi]], the cattle-clan, and the [[affiliation-kolembozi|Kolembozi]], the crocodile-clan, are fighting over the rivers after a dry season and a diverted channel. A herd driven up a Kolembozi channel ended in a killing, the lineages on both sides took up the feud, and the feud became a war when the paramounts ordered their musters. The Ruwozi say the Kolembozi cut the channel. The Kolembozi Reach-Elders say it was cut by hand and name no hand.

The Convener, who summons the Lubemo, keeps its order of speaking and speaks last, is [[being-nzoburwz|Nzobu Ruwozi]], paramount of the Ruwozi and a party to the war. The other three clans must choose sides or hold the [Long Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} by convergence, and the first term of the Pact is that the five do not lift a weapon against the five.

The party enters as a guest of one of the delegations: a witness, a reader of ground, a carrier of messages or a trader's companion. It arrives in the weeks before the sitting, while the delegations gather and the channel has still not been read.

## What Is Really Happening {#what-is-really-happening}

**The channel.** The households of a Kolembozi reach upstream turned the channel into their own backwater when the dry season left the river low, to keep their landing and their fish-camps wet. The clan holds that a reach answers for its flow and that the reeds, the fish-camps and the landings come first in a dry year, and the households did what they believed a reach may do. The cut left the channel below them dry: a Kolembozi landing that fed a dozen households, a pool beyond it where a Ruwozi household watered its herd by custom, and the Ruwozi pans the channel ran on to.

**The killing.** The Ruwozi household found its pool dry, drove its herd up the channel to the reach's water without leave, and met the reach's households coming out to turn it back. A Ruwozi herder died at the reach's landing. His lineage took up the feud, the reach's lineage answered, and both paramounts mustered.

**Why nobody has named the hand.** When the war began, the clan asked its Reach-Elders who had cut the channel. The elder of the reach that cut it said nothing, and the elder of the dry landing below it does not know. The clan believes, honestly, that another hand cut it. [[being-nyabesnklm|Nyabesa nu Kolembozi]], its paramount, believes it too, and when she learns otherwise she holds that the reach acted within its right. She sits at Heto Lubemo to ask the other three clans to choose, and she can block any convergence alone.

**The reading.** Both clans have asked the [[affiliation-fenjara|Fénjara Tribe]] to read the channel. Fénjaran trackers have already walked it, and [[being-mbadekofnjr|Mbadeko nu Fénjara]], their chieftain, knows what a reading shows: the cut made from the reach's bank, the spoil heaped on the reach's side, the bank shored with bundled papyrus in the Kolembozi manner, and canoe-marks at the reach's landing. He waits for a witness from outside the clans, so that neither can say the reading was bent. A reading shows the hand and not the right, and that changes the war's question from who cut the channel to whether a reach may keep a channel the Pact's herds drink from.

**The Convener's message.** Since the muster Nzobu has sent word to a chieftain of the dry-country tribes west of Ruwozi country, who are not party to the Pact. She asks for the dry-country wells and the grass beyond the pans through the dry months for the Ruwozi households cut off from the rivers and the canoes, and offers cattle for it. The other clans, if they learn of it, read it as a bid for fighters. She keeps the message from the other four paramounts, because a Convener who buys water outside the Pact concedes that the Pact has not watered her people. Carried openly, it is a way out of the war. Discovered, it reads as a Convener reaching for spears.

**The three.** [[being-nkesheyasngl|Nkesheya nu Sengala]] leans toward the Kolembozi, whose river-trade her clan carries north, and has told no one. The leaning holds while the Kolembozi keep the trade open and the Ruwozi keep their regiments at home, and it changes if either fails. The Fénjara lean as the Sengala lean and say nothing of it. [[being-ndamesongnz|Ndameso nu Ngonzi]] hosts the sitting and takes no side; a war inside the enclosure's walls would fail his clan's one duty. [[being-mbudelolbwz|Mbudelo nu Lobwezi]] holds the Pact by convergence and carries his clan's claim that the war has put its first term in question.

**The record.** [[being-bonzimbelbwz|Bonzimbe of the Lobwezi]], the blind senior griot of the Long Pact, carries the recorded judgments of twenty-three Lubemos, and none of them is a judgment on a war between two of the five. He rules nothing. When the paramounts ask, he says what the Pact's terms say, and the third term shares the grass, the rivers and the rains.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The party cannot end the war, and nobody at Heto Lubemo wants a foreigner to try. What it can do is put a fact in front of the paramounts before the first business opens: the reading of the channel, the Convener's message, or the Sengala's terms. The Lubemo decides what the fact means.

> [!WARNING]
> Nyabesa does not know that her own reach cut the channel. A party that announces it at the Kolembozi fire, before the reading is witnessed, makes her answer an accusation in front of her delegation, and she objects to everything after.

## Hooks {#hooks}

- **The scholar or witness.** Mbudelo wants a witness from outside the clans to attend the entering of the claims at the sitting, so that no clan can say the Lobwezi record was taken in its favor.
- **The tracker or hunter.** Mbadeko wants an outsider to stand beside the Fénjaran trail-reader when the channel is read, and takes the party to the dry reach himself.
- **The messenger.** Nzobu wants a word carried to a dry-country chieftain, outside the Lubemo and outside the other four paramounts' knowledge, and does not say what it is.
- **The trader.** A Sengala trading house that has carried goods south for the party asks it to come on to Heto Lubemo as its foreign guest, where Nkesheya wants to hear what the foreigners have seen on the roads.

## The Opening {#opening}

The edge of the southeastern escarpment, at the wall of Heto Lubemo, an oval of dry stone higher than a tall man with the plains falling away below it. Camps fill the grounds around it, apportioned among the five clans, and the Ruwozi and the Kolembozi camps stand at opposite ends, out of each other's sight. The [[being-hetoguardian|Guardian of Heto Lubemo]], an eagle-shaman of the [[affiliation-ngonzi|Ngonzi]], stands at the gap in the wall with empty hands.

> "No weapon passes this stone," the Guardian says. "And nobody passes it who is not somebody's guest. Whose are you?"

## Scenes {#scenes}

### Scene One: The Grounds

**Purpose.** Show the gathering and its strains.

**Situation.** The camps outside the wall, where several hundred people in support roles cook, herd, mend and talk. The [[affiliation-sengala|Sengala]] delegation keeps close to the Kolembozi camp, the [[affiliation-lobwezi|Lobwezi]] griots go between the two ends of the grounds, and the [[affiliation-ekunda|Ékunda Tribe]]'s delegate camps apart on neither clan's ground.

**NPCs and threats.** Ndameso, courteous and exact about precedence, who has placed every camp; young lineage representatives of both warring clans, each a [[being-younglinrep|Young Lineage Representative]], who come to watch and learn and are spoiling for an incident; griots, who hear everything.

**Player choices.** Present themselves to Ndameso, eat at the griots' fire, or walk the support camps.

**Clues and rewards.** What the support camps say reaches the paramounts before the paramounts speak. The party hears that the channel has not been read, that the Fénjara wait for a witness, and that the dry-country tribes have had a message from the Convener.

**Consequences.** A party that offends Ndameso is put outside the grounds' order and can reach no paramount except as a nuisance.

**If bypassed.** The party learns the same at the wall from the Guardian's questions.

> [!TIP]
> Ndameso prefers a gift to a speech, speaks softly and sits very still. Let him be slow, and let the party feel that slowness is the method here.

### Scene Two: The Reading of the Channel

**Purpose.** Find the hand.

**Situation.** [[place-dryreach|The Dry Reach]] in Kolembozi country, some days from the enclosure: a channel bed cracked to the papyrus, a dry landing below it, a dry pool, and above them a reach with water standing in a new backwater. Mbadeko brings the party and a [[being-trailreader|Fénjaran Trail-Reader]].

**NPCs and threats.** Mbadeko, who answers a question with a question; the trail-reader; the [[being-dryreachelder|Reach-Elder of the Dry Landing]], who wants the hand found and named; canoe parties of the reach above, each boatman a [[being-reachcanoer|Canoe Warrior of the Reach]], who are wary of strangers and are boatmen with spears in their own water.

**Player choices.** Stand witness to the reading, read it themselves, speak to the dry landing's households, or cross to the reach above.

**Clues and rewards.** The reading: the cut made from the reach's bank, the spoil on its side, papyrus-bundle shoring, canoe-marks at its landing. Mbadeko's trail-reader says what was found and where, and nothing about what it means.

**Consequences.** The reading, witnessed, is a fact both clans asked for and must hear. The reach above learns that strangers have read its channel, and its elder sends a runner to the Kolembozi camp at Heto Lubemo.

**If bypassed.** The reading is never witnessed, and the first business opens on two accounts and no fact.

> [!WARNING]
> A fight with the reach's canoes in the papyrus is one the party loses. The Kolembozi move through channels no other clan can read. Anyone who wants to cross to the reach goes as a guest or not at all.

### Scene Three: The Convener's Message

**Purpose.** Optional: the other way out of the war.

**Situation.** The Ruwozi camp, where Nzobu sits among her advisers, then the road west toward the dry country if the party carries her word.

**NPCs and threats.** Nzobu, dry, patient and exact; her senior griot, the [[being-ruwozigriot|Ruwozi Griot]]; young Ruwozi of the dead herder's lineage, who want the regiments to march.

**Player choices.** Carry the message unopened, ask what it is, or tell another paramount it exists.

**Clues and rewards.** If the party asks and Nzobu trusts them, she tells them: grazing and water in the west for the dry months, paid in cattle. She asks them to say nothing of it before the sitting.

**Consequences.** If the Kolembozi learn of the message from anyone but Nzobu, they say the Convener is reaching for spears outside the Pact, and the Sengala's leaning hardens. If Nzobu puts it before the Lubemo herself, she concedes the rivers, and a convergence becomes possible.

**If bypassed.** The dry-country chieftain answers by his own messenger, after the sitting.

### Scene Four: The Leopard's Fire

**Purpose.** Learn what the Sengala want.

**Situation.** The Sengala camp beside the Kolembozi, with traders' bundles and rainforest goods among the cattle.

**NPCs and threats.** Nkesheya, composed, courteous and hard to read; her traders, each a [[being-sengalatrdr|Sengala Trader]], who have been in every clan's country this season.

**Player choices.** Trade news, ask her plainly, or offer her the reading.

**Clues and rewards.** Nkesheya makes one offer and does not make it twice. She tells a party that has earned it what would move her: the river-trade kept open and the Ruwozi regiments kept at home. She remembers what the drought-war cost the country it crossed.

**Consequences.** A party that carries her terms to Nzobu has given the Convener the price of the third clan.

**If bypassed.** Nkesheya says nothing until the sitting, and then says it in her turn.

### Scene Five: The First Business

**Purpose.** The climax: the war heard at the sitting.

**Situation.** Inside the enclosure. The paramounts sit with their delegations, the Lobwezi griots enter which clan holds each claim, and the war is heard first. The paramounts speak in turn, and the Convener speaks last.

**NPCs and threats.** All five paramounts; Bonzimbe, who answers when asked; the [[being-ekundadelegat|Ékunda Delegate]] and the Fénjara delegate, who may speak for their tribes and bind them only with their elders' agreement.

**Player choices.** A guest speaks only through the delegation that brought it. The party can give its witness to the delegation it came with, to the Lobwezi for the record, or to Mbadeko to carry. It can also give nothing and watch.

**Clues and rewards.** Bonzimbe's recital of the terms when a paramount asks for it. What the party's witness does to the order of speaking.

**Consequences.** With the reading before them, the paramounts argue the right and not the hand, and a convergence is possible: the reach keeps its landing wet and opens the channel to the herds below, or the Ruwozi water in the west through the dry months and the canoes return. Without it, Nyabesa objects to every account that names her clan, the matter waits four years, and the renewal of the Pact comes at the end of the sitting with its first term broken in plain sight.

**If bypassed.** The sitting runs as it runs if the party does nothing.

> [!TIP]
> Play the sitting slowly. Each paramount speaks in turn and nobody interrupts. Let the party sit through Nyabesa's silence before she objects, and let Nzobu, who speaks last, use her silence the same way. The tension is in what each of them does not say.

## If the Party Does Nothing {#if-the-party-does-nothing}

The first business opens on two accounts of the channel and no reading. Nyabesa objects to every proposal that blames her clan, and the Lubemo cannot converge over her objection. The war waits for the next sitting. The regiments stay mustered at [[place-mbolewevlg|Mbolewe]], the canoes stay withdrawn, and the Sengala hold their leaning while the trade runs. At the renewal, the five paramounts say the Pact again in their own bodies, and two of them stand at opposite ends of the enclosure while they say its first term.

## Aftermath {#aftermath}

A convergence at Heto Lubemo is a commitment of the Pact, and the paramount who stopped objecting answers for it at home. If the reading was heard, the reach's households answer to their own clan for a cut they made in good faith, and the dead herder's lineage has a hand to name and a settling to ask for. If the Convener's message came out in the open, the other clans learn that the Ruwozi asked for grass and not for spears. The party leaves as guests who saw the Lubemo work, and the griots enter into the record who brought the fact that moved it.
