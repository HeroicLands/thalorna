[[place-orzara|Orzara City]] races two factions at [[place-rathaunamelvar|The Racecourse]], and a man shows which he shouts for with a ring. The [[affiliation-zargoi|Zargoi]], the Irons, wear a plain ring of gray iron; the [[affiliation-ethloi|Ethloi]], the Golds, wear a gilded one. Anyone who wears the ring on a race day is a Wearer of his faction, and most of [[place-warrensorz|the Warrens]] wear the gray for nothing. A Brother of the Stand, sworn to one of the stand-clubs, wears the same ring with his club's mark filed into it.

**What it opens.** In its own faction's stands the ring gets a man a seat, a drink and a fight's worth of friends. A Brother's ring gets him buried by his club when he dies, and it brings his club into the street when he calls. Worn into the other faction's quarter on a race night, either ring is a challenge, and the Watch treats a man found beaten with the wrong ring on his hand as having asked for it.

**At the table.** The rings cost a few bits at any market stall, and the Golds' gilt wears off by the end of a season. What a ring is worth is who sees it. A man who has worn the gray and then the gold is the one thing both factions agree on: his stand-club strikes his name, buries nobody of his, and does not forget his face. A party that needs a crowd in Orzara, or needs to pass through one, starts by choosing a ring.
