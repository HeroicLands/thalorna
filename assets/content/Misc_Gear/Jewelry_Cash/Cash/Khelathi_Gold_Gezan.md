A wedge-stamped piece of gold at the full gezan weight, bearing the seal of the temple that attested it, the metal and mass, and the regnal cypher of the Gar-Aû in whose reign it was struck. It is not a coin and the Khelâthi do not call it one: it is attested metal of known weight, five parts in six gold, and its worth is the worth of the gold in it: ten silver gezan.

Pieces of this size settle land, tribute and temple offering rather than daily trade. Producing one at a market stall is ostentatious to the point of insult, and a stallholder may not be able to weigh against it.
