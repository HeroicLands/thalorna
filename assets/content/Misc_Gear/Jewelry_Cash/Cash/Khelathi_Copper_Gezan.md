A bronze piece struck to the gezan weight and sealed by the temple. Unlike the gold and silver pieces it is **not** worth the metal in it: the bronze alone comes to little more than a twentieth of the face the seal attests. The face is backed by silver. The Gár-Hálzi issues copper pieces only in exchange for silver, and any of its chapters redeems a copper gezan for a silver qelu on demand.

That gap is deliberate and understood. Melting the pieces down returns a fraction of their value, so they stay in circulation as money instead of draining away as metal, and only the Gár-Hálzi can make more. Foreign copper and bronze carry no seal; the temples buy them by weight at the price of the metal, about one copper gezan for a pound.

At this face a copper gezan settles a meal or a night's lodging at a common inn.
