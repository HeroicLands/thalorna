Merchants who cross the [[place-kervaron|Kervaron Spine]] pay the [[affiliation-aelndntrbs|Áelendan clans]] at the first camp below each pass, and what they pay is the passage-gift: a leather bag of silver coin, tied at the neck, handed over by a factor's steward to whoever of the clan meets the train. The clan's man checks its weight in his hand and does not count it. A train of twenty mules carries about a hundred Ardin for each pass, in [[miscgear-ard|Vilérian Ardin]] or anything else that is silver.

**What it opens.** The pass. A train that has given the gift goes up with nobody in its way, and one that has not finds the road closed by snow, rockfall or men, depending on the season. The gift is never haggled, and nobody on either side calls it a toll: the clans hold the range as the dwelling of [[affiliation-firstgods|the First Gods]] and owe the Senate's boundary-stones nothing, and [[affiliation-seldan|Seldan]] lists the range as imperial territory and pays no tribute to anyone. A merchant who calls the gift a toll in the clans' hearing has insulted them, and one who calls it a toll in a Vilérian court has confessed to paying one.

**Why it is paid without question.** The mule-train masters at the first camps give one answer: the Twelfth. Before the [Doran Hilvan]{.name data-type="affiliation"} went up into the range, the Senate sent men to read the stones to the clans; after it, the Senate sent grain merchants ([[lore-twelfthloss|The Loss of the Twelfth]]). A steward who is told to bargain the gift down is being told to forget that.
