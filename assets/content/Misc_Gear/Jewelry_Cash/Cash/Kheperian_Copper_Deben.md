A bronze piece struck to the deben weight and sealed by the temple. Unlike the gold and silver pieces it is **not** worth the metal in it: the bronze alone comes to well under the face the seal attests, and the difference is the temple's charge for making small money exist at all.

That gap is deliberate and understood. Melting the pieces down returns a fraction of their value, so they stay in circulation as money instead of draining away as metal, and only the Pér-Háti can make more. Foreign bronze carries no seal and buys nothing at this rate.

At this face a copper deben settles a meal, a night's lodging, or a common tool.
