The workhorse of imperial commerce, struck at Magnápolis and at the provincial mints under Aerarium standards. The obverse bears the imperial eagle and cypher, the reverse the value mark and the issuing mint's stamp, and the reverse face carries the eight-segment pattern along which the coin is cut for change.

It is not fine silver. The coin is alloyed, and between the alloy and the mint's charge for striking, an Argentus is worth an Argentus because the Aerarium says so rather than because of the metal in it. Issues from a mint known to debase are weighed rather than counted.

Formal contracts and imperial accounting say _Argentus_; everyone else says _Argo_.
