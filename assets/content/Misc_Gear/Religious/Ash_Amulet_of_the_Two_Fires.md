In the gate passage of the [[place-twofirestemple|Temple of Two Fires]] in [[place-qudumel2|Kudumel]] the fire-keepers sell ash-amulets: small discs of clay, pressed with ash swept from the altar of the eternal flame, the size of a coin and pierced for a cord. One face is stamped with the anvil under its sheltering flame, the sign of [[lore-ustarondty|Ústaron]], whose forge-fire burns in the temple's hall of the Twelve. The other carries the altar-flame in its ring of sparks, the flame of [[lore-atarosdty|Ataros]] that has burned in the fire-house since before the empire came to the islands.

**Who carries it.** A caravan master hangs one round his neck for the desert road, and a western captain nails one over the door of his cabin. Kudumel families who keep both rites give one to a son leaving the city. The two faces mean that the amulet serves a traveler of either faith, and the fire-keepers sell them to both without asking which.

**At the table.** An ash-amulet costs two bits and protects nothing anyone can measure; what it shows is that its wearer passed through Kudumel and stopped at the temple. A cracked amulet is taken badly. A ship's master who swears his cracked across the anvil face on the night his ship was lost under him wants the temple to tell him which god refused him, and the temple has not answered.
