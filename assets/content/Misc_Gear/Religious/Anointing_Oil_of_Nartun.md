A man of the royal house of the [[affiliation-hlykngdmnrtm|Holy Kingdom of Nartun]] is of the blood by birth, and he is king only once the oil has touched him. The Holy Mother pours it on his forehead in the Mother's Hall of [[place-nartum|Nartun]], before the assembled high priests, and the crown goes on his head after it. The oil leaves a pale, shiny patch on the skin that never quite tans again, and [[being-klvrsdrns|Kelvaras Daronis]], the present king, wears his under the crown.

The temple of [[affiliation-marendas|Marendas]] keeps the oil in its vessel, a lidded jar of old silver in a niche of the temple's treasury, and draws from it only for an anointing. A phial filled from the vessel and carried down to the Mother's Hall is the whole of the oil that leaves the temple, and what the phial does not pour goes back into the vessel the same day.

**What it proves.** The oil is the kingdom's law in one gesture. The Holy Mother, [[being-ilvnssrkls|Ilvenas Sarkelis]], may refuse to pour it, as she refused Kelvaras's elder cousin for a winter, and when the high priesthood deposes a king she takes the anointing back. A king who loses the priesthood's favor has lost the one thing that made him king, whatever the host and the court say.

**At the table.** The oil has no price, and no one outside the temple of Marendas has any business with it. A phial of it in a stranger's luggage, or a vessel that has been moved from its place, means someone is preparing to anoint another man, or wants it believed that someone is.
