A legionary of the [[affiliation-vylrnmltry|Vilérian Military]] who lives through his twenty years leaves the legion a citizen of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Empire]], and he leaves it with a citizenship diptych: two bronze leaves the size of a man's palm, hinged with wire and closed face to face. Inside, an engraver of the legion cuts his name, his legion, the rank he held at discharge and the year of it, and the names of the officers who witnessed it. The same entry goes into the citizen roll of the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]].

**What it proves.** Outside [[place-orzara|Orzara]] the diptych is a veteran's whole case. It shows a provincial magistrate that he is a citizen with the standing of the law, and it shows the Watch at a city gate that he may sleep inside the line by right. Inside the Offices it proves nothing on its own, because the rule the law schools teach first is that what is not on the roll is not in the city ([[lore-firewords|The Words Kept from the Fire]]). A diptych whose entry a clerk cannot find has lost its veteran his citizenship, and a man with the right friends can lose an entry for another.

**At the table.** A diptych is worth nothing to anyone but the man it names, so a stolen one is sold to a forger for its bronze and its engraving, and a forged one passes a gate and fails at a clerk's desk. A veteran shows his diptych once at the pay-hall when his [[miscgear-pensiontoken|pension token]] is struck, and after that he keeps it in the family's chest. His widow keeps it after him, because it is the only thing in the house that says what he did with his twenty years ([[doc-vylrnctznshp|Vilérian Citizenship]]).
