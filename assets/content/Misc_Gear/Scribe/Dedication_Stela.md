The Kulumbal is the keeper of the stones: a P'ilambal of the royal house who chooses what each new stone records of the Ts'enmah's reign and raises one at every turn of the count ([[affiliation-kankul|K'ankul]]). The stone is cut with the day, the ruler, the rite and the glyphs the Kulumbal decided to keep.

**Read it.** The stone says what the court wants said. A reader learns the date, the ruler and the claim, and learns little of what the Kulumbal left out.

**Forge it.** A stone is a heavy thing to forge, and a rubbing is not. A false rubbing is a way to put words in a court's mouth.

**Sell it.** A stela cannot be moved by a party. A rubbing or a copy of its text can, to a scholar, a rival court or a noble who wants his house to appear on it.

**Be hanged for it.** A rubbing of a stela carried out of its city can pass for a claim the court never made, and the court hangs the carrier and the carver alike.
