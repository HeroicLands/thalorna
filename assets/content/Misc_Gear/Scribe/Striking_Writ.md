When a merchant house of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Taruzelian States]] strikes a member's name, it does not tell him so to his face. It sends a striking-writ: a short notice under the house's seal naming the one struck and the day, copied by the house's clerks and carried to every counting house, warehouse, quay and ferry the house deals with. The writ is read aloud at the house's own desks and pinned up where its factors trade.

**What it does.** Each house's striking closes a different door, and the writ says which.

- **The [[affiliation-hitusuni|Hitusuni]]** withdraw the name and the seals with it. No Hitusuni warehouse takes the goods of the one struck, and the house forgets anything it ever kept for him.
- **The [[affiliation-qizikuni|Qizikuni]]** withdraw the name. No counting house reads it again, and every bond the house stood behind falls on the one struck.
- **The [[affiliation-balinuni|Balinuni]]** withdraw the name. The ferries do not carry the one struck, and no caravan master at the Gate shares a venture with him.
- **The [[affiliation-tanaduni|Tanaduni]]** withdraw the name and the flag. No Tanaduni galley escorts the one struck, and no League captain signs him aboard.
- **The [[affiliation-rusakuni|Rusakuni]]** withdraw the name and close the loft. No yard that builds to Rusakuni lines hires the one struck.

**At the table.** A striking-writ is how a Taruzelian learns that a man is finished, often before the man does. A party that meets a factor still trading on his house's name a week after the writ went out has met a man with a week's head start, and anything he seals is worth nothing ([[miscgear-factorseal|Factor's Seal]]). The first Free Captains learned what the Grand Council's outlawing meant the same way, one closed door at a time ([[lore-taruzelbetrayal|The Betrayal of the Revolution]]).
