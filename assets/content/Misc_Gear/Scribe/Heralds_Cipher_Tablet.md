A herald of the [[affiliation-imprlhrlds|Imperial College of Heralds]] goes to every ceremony with a cipher-tablet hung at his belt: two leaves of boxwood in a leather case, filled with black wax and written with a bronze stylus. He writes on it in the College's cipher of arms, a shorthand of marks for the four estates, the metals of the liveries and the charges on the shields, so that a herald at a funeral can note in a dozen strokes which houses stood where, who wore whose colors and who walked beside whom. The marks are taught to pursuivants with the roll of arms, and outside the College almost nobody reads them.

**What it is for.** The tablet is the College's memory of precedence. A herald carries his notes back to the compound of the [[affiliation-imperialoffices|Imperial Offices]], where the Keeper of the Roll of Arms enters what matters and the wax is smoothed for the next ceremony. A house that petitions the Master over its place in a procession is answered from tablets like these.

**At the table.** A cipher-tablet lost at a ceremony is read by nobody who finds it, and that is the College's comfort. A tablet stolen from a herald on the eve of an accession holds the draft order of the procession in a hand only heralds can read, and the claimants' stewards are paying for that order now.

:::secret {title="The second key"}
The cipher has two keys. The first is the cipher of arms that every pursuivant learns. The second, taught only to the heralds who serve the throne's network under [[being-lrdmlnvsbr|Lord Aimilon Visbari]], uses the same marks to carry reports: the charges and metals that read to a pursuivant as a house's livery read, to Aimilon's people, as a name, a place and what was overheard. A tablet that holds a perfectly ordinary note of a funeral may hold a report from an embassy under it, and the College's honest heralds cannot tell the difference.
:::
