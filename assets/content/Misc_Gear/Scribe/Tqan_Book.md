The Itsani faith holds that time moves in cycles and that the gods take part in each one ([[affiliation-itzanpnthn|The Itsani Pantheon]]), and the Ch'alish priesthood keeps the record of it. A Tq'an Book records for each of the twenty day-signs of the 260-day count ([[lore-itsaniclndr|The Itsani Calendar]]) what the last turn of that sign brought: the rains, the deaths, the wars, the harvest. A city's Keeper of the Codices holds one, and its entries are read aloud at the close of the month after the Warden of the Debt gives his figure ([[lore-chumtsena|The Ch'um Ts'ena]]).

**Read it.** A reader who knows the count finds, for any day, what the last such day brought. The book is a forecast only for those who believe in recurrence, and the priests who read it aloud do.

**Forge it.** A forged entry is cheap. The scribes' hands differ and the pigments vary by temple, but few in a city could check a book against its original, and a false entry that names a harvest or a death can move a Ts'enmah.

**Sell it.** A temple's book has no market except the temple itself, a rival temple, or a noble who wants his own sign read. A scholar abroad pays well for a copy.

**Be hanged for it.** A book carried out of a city without the Keeper's leave is taken for theft of the archive.
