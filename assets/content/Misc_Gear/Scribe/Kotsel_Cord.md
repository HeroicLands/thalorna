A k'otsel is a knotted cord record. Colored threads and the positions of the knots encode numerical data, tribute tallies and calendar reckonings, legible only to trained readers. The Ch'umbal carry them along the white roads with their spoken messages, and the central dispatch at Kul'taq'an decodes, copies and redistributes them ([[place-kiichchik|Kì'ìch'chik Continent]]). The Reckoners send the day's count the same way, and every road shrine speaks it at dawn ([[lore-chumtsena|The Ch'um Ts'ena]]).

A cord lost from a runner's pack is a missing day, a missing tribute or a missing figure, and whoever reads it holds the number. A forged cord needs a reader who knows the knotter's convention; one that is nearly right is worse than none.
