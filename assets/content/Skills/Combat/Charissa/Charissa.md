The **Charissa** ("the Grace") is the unarmed fighting art of the [[affiliation-mtrrchybth|Matriarchy of Bethua]]: an austere open-hand and kicking style built on speed, which turns the agility Bethuan women are prized for into a weapon in its own right. It is a specialization of Melee. Its masters, the _Charissai_, are figures of real prestige in the realm.

## Purpose

The art is built around the advantage in speed and agility a trained woman holds over a larger man. A Charissa adept can kill unarmed, can disable a stronger opponent before he closes, and fights with a quickness that heavier, more powerful foes struggle to answer. It is neither a sport nor an ornament. The security of the realm rests on the proposition that a trained woman defeats an untrained man, and the Charissa is what keeps that proposition true. An unarmed Bethuan woman is never a defenseless one.

## Who learns it

Every woman of the Spear-Sisterhood, the _Lonkhai_, trains in the Charissa from youth alongside the spear and the tactics of the line, and a free Bethuan girl learns it young in her village levy. The women who train a levy watch for the quick girls, because quickness is the gift on which the art depends, and a promising one may be taken into a House's guard to learn its finer points from veteran women.

## In war

The fleet's marines, drawn from the Spear-Sisterhood and the eunuch Spadai, fight with blade, with javelin and with the Charissa in the press of a boarding action, where a longer weapon is useless. The fighters who come over the rail of a Bethuan galley are faster and deadlier in the crush than their size suggests.

## Techniques

The Charissa teaches two kinds of technique. Its **strikes** are blows of its own, which take the place of the folk punch and raise the folk kick. Its **maneuvers** change how a blow or a grip the body already has is used. A fighter chooses one for each exchange, or uses the plain blow or grip instead.

### Strikes

[[skill-chrssachop|Charissa Chop]]
: the hard edge-of-hand blow

[[skill-chrssajab|Charissa Jab]]
: the fast, precise open-hand strike

[[skill-chrssakick|Charissa Kick]]
: a kick that reaches high, which the folk kick cannot

### Maneuvers

Three of the five maneuvers, the Sweep, the Lock and the Choke, work by leverage rather than strength, and so their Strength Trial rolls Agility in place of Strength. That is the art's answer to a larger opponent: once she has hold of him, his weight counts for less than her quickness.

**Charissa Sweep**
: A throw used with the Trip. It takes an opponent's legs from under him by leverage, so the Strength Trial rolls Agility.

**Charissa Deflection**
: The open-hand parry, used with the Limb Block. The hand turns the blow aside where a folk limb block meets it, and it takes a better block modifier.

**Charissa Point Strike**
: Used with any blunt strike, the Chop, the Jab and the Kick among them, to put the blow on a vital point. It tightens the strike's placement, and a Precision advantage won in the exchange lets the striker choose the location within the part struck. A light blow to the neck or the eyes can stun, because a location's Shock Value counts toward the Shock a wound causes. A blow that wounds even lightly at a location where a wound threatens a fumble or a stumble, such as the forearm or the thigh, calls for the keep-control test that a serious wound would.

**Charissa Lock**
: Used with the Hold on an arm or a leg, bending a joint against itself. It works by leverage. The held limb is Immobilized, as with any hold, and also **Wrenched**: on a narrow win the limb is impaired by −10 while the lock holds, and on a wide one it is unusable and drops whatever it held. A lock does no lasting harm. When it is released, the Wrenched limb improves by one grade at once and recovers fully after a few minutes.

**Charissa Choke**
: Used with the Hold, declared against the neck when the Hold is declared. It works by leverage. Each round the choke is held, the victim makes a Shock test at an index that starts at 5, the neck's Shock Value, and rises by one a round. An unbroken choke reaches Stunned in its second round and Unconscious in its fourth, unless the victim's Shock test holds it off. The choker must win the Strength Trial every round to keep the hold, and the victim keeps every defence.
