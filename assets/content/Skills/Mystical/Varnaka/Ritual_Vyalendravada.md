## Ritual: Vyālendravāda

- **Rite:** The Tracing of the Pattern. The temple's central diagram is refreshed in chalk on the sanctum floor each day and the priest walks its lines, naming each aspect of [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyālendra]] in turn as he crosses it. When a temple or a major civic work is finished, the same reading is made at the larger scale as the Consecration of the Pattern, setting the finished proportions against the canonical diagram
- **Cost:** A morning's chalk and an unhurried walk for the daily rite. At a Consecration the finished work stands or falls by the reading, and a building whose proportions do not hold the pattern is not consecrated
- **Performed by:** An Ácārya, a scholar-priest trained in architecture, mathematics and theological argument as well as liturgy; the Parama-Ácārya ("Supreme Master") presides over the Great Dialectic and any reading of consequence
- **Licensed by:** The [[affiliation-vyalendravada|Vyālendravāda]] alone, the monistic sect holding Vyālendra the single deity from whom all others emanate. In that doctrine, to read his pattern correctly is to read the whole of the divine order at once

Ordination to Parama-Ácārya follows one of two ordeals. In **the Geometer's Ordeal** the aspirant designs a small temple from first principles, holding every proportion to the canonical diagram, and defends the design in public before a council of Ácāryas. **The Silent Reading** is a year of philosophical study in the temple library, without speech and without the company of any living soul but the librarian.
