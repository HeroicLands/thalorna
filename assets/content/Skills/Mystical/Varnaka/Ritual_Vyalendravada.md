## Ritual: Vyālendravāda

- **Rite:** The Tracing of the Pattern—a daily rite in which the temple's central diagram is refreshed in chalk on the sanctum floor and the priest walks its lines, naming each aspect of [[affiliation-vyalendra|Vyālendra]] in turn as he crosses it; at the completion of a temple or a major civic work, the same pattern-reading is performed at the larger scale of the Consecration of the Pattern, demonstrating the finished proportions against the canonical diagram
- **Cost:** A morning's chalk and an unhurried walk for the daily rite; for the Consecration, the finished work itself stands or falls by the reading—a building whose proportions do not hold the pattern is not consecrated
- **Performed by:** An Ácārya, a scholar-priest trained in architecture, mathematics and theological argument as well as liturgy; the Parama-Ácārya ("Supreme Master") presides over the Great Dialectic and any reading of consequence
- **Licensed by:** The [[affiliation-vyalendravada|Vyālendravāda]] alone—the monistic sect holding Vyālendra the single deity from whom all others emanate, so that reading his pattern correctly is, in this doctrine, reading the whole of the divine order at once

Ordination to Parama-Ácārya is granted only after **the Geometer's Ordeal**, in which the aspirant designs a small temple from first principles, conforming in all its proportions to the canonical diagram, and defends the design publicly against a council of Ácāryas; or after **the Silent Reading**, a year of philosophical study in the temple library without speech and without the company of any living soul but the librarian.
