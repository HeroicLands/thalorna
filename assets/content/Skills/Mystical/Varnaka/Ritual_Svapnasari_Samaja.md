## Ritual: Svapnasāri-samāja

- **Rite:** The dream-reading — a petitioner sleeps at the monastery, a Darśaka records what is dreamed, and for a great question the Svapna-Ácārya convenes a Great Vigil, three nights in which several sleepers' dreams are pooled and read as one body before a formal statement is issued
- **Cost:** To the petitioner, three nights' sleep under a Nidrāpāla's watch and the fee the monastery names, or none at all from a Darśaka undertaking the Mirror Year; to the reader, the years of training a true oneiromancer's reputation is built on
- **Performed by:** A Darśaka ("Seer"), the sect's working priesthood, trained in dream-reading, meditation and the careful recording of what petitioners bring; a Svapna-Ácārya presides over the Great Vigil in person
- **Licensed by:** The [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]] alone. The orthodox hierarchy does not contest the sect's competence here, and the [[affiliation-svapnadevas|Svapnadēvas]] — the Dreaming Host this rite serves — keep no separate priesthood of their own; this is the whole of their formal devotion made a vocation

**The Seven-Night Vigil** and **the Mirror Year** are the path into the priesthood proper: seven consecutive nights of light sleep in a monastery chamber, reported each morning and rarely completed without a Darśaka's intervention, or a year spent reading the silver mirror for every petitioner who comes, refusing no reading and accepting no fee.
