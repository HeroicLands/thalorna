## Ritual: Svapnasāri-samāja

- **Rite:** The dream-reading. A petitioner sleeps at the monastery and a Darshaka records what is dreamed. For a great question the Svapna-Āchārya convenes a Great Vigil, three nights in which several sleepers' dreams are pooled and read as one body before a formal statement is issued
- **Cost:** To the petitioner, three nights' sleep under a Nidrāpāla's watch and the fee the monastery names, or none at all from a Darshaka undertaking the Mirror Year; to the reader, the years of training a true oneiromancer's reputation is built on
- **Performed by:** A Darshaka ("Seer"), the sect's working priesthood, trained in dream-reading, meditation and the careful recording of what petitioners bring; a Svapna-Āchārya presides over the Great Vigil in person
- **Licensed by:** The [[affiliation-svapnasarisamaja|Svapnasāri-samāja]] alone. The orthodox hierarchy does not contest the sect's competence here. The [[affiliation-svapnadevas|Svapnadevas]], the Dreaming Host this rite serves, keep no priesthood of their own, and this is the whole of their formal devotion made a vocation

**The Seven-Night Vigil** and **the Mirror Year** are the path into the priesthood proper. The Vigil is seven consecutive nights of light sleep in a monastery chamber, reported each morning, and is rarely completed without a Darshaka's intervention. The Mirror Year is a year spent reading the silver mirror for every petitioner who comes, refusing no reading and accepting no fee.
