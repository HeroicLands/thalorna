Standing with Kiltas measures how well a shaman argues the size of the red fox's share. Kiltas holds the lower wood, the field-edges and the middens, and takes poultry, cached meat and small worked things as a levy that has never been settled. The relationship is a running argument, and the skill is the shaman's side of it: appeasing him, naming what he has taken and making the case for a smaller levy over a season. He is not moved by need and is moved by cleverness, so a plain plea fails where a good case succeeds. Shamen who deal with him often are suspected by their elders of enjoying it, and keep that quiet.
