## Ritual: Nal'ik Ts'uqal

Working
: The true dream. A petitioner admitted to a dream-chamber with a stated question, prepared by prayer and breath and sometimes a mild draught, dreams an answer to it. On waking he recites what he saw before he speaks to anyone else, and a Reader of Symbols reads it against the archive and states its reliability with the reading. A bled petitioner may, on such a night, dream the answer of an ancestor

Position
: The dark of the moon, in the Ts'uqal count, when the boundary between waking and dream is thinnest. The Warden of the Dark Moon fixes the nights, and admission on them is oversubscribed for months

Cost
: No blood. The goddess takes cacao, flowers, incense and the spoken word, and a composed prayer is worth more to her than any cup of blood

Performed by
: The Master of the Dream-Chambers admits the dreamer; the Reader of Symbols reads; the Ch'alish Ts'uqal carries a reading that bears on the city to the Ts'enmah

The working's force is in the reliability it states. A reading that says it does not know is still a correct working, and the archive records it beside the outcome. It is one of the ten workings of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]].
