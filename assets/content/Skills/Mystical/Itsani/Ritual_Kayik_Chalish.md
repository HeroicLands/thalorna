## Ritual: K'ayik Ch'alish

Working
: The feeding of the flame, by which an offering burned with the branch's formula is delivered to the god it was given for. Every great working of the other nine passes through it, since an offering that is not burned has not been delivered. Its greatest form is the drawing of the [K'ayik Kul'al]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the Living Flame, from the rising sun with obsidian mirrors and polished jade. Its residue is the graded ash, which heals when it is rubbed on the sick or worked into the water priestesses' paints

Position
: The Reckoner of the Feeding fixes the hour at which each flame is fed, so this working is placed within the day as well as in the counts, and an early feeding is a fault. The Living Flame is drawn at sunrise on the winter solstice that closes the fifty-two-year round

Cost
: The offering itself, whatever it is, received with the same formula for a king's heart and a farmer's kernel. A flame that goes out is paid for with a human sacrifice in expiation. Initiates who seek it bear the ritual burning of the forearms

Performed by
: The Warden of the Eternal Flame for his own fire; the Master of the Living Flame with the highest hierarch at the round's close; the Keeper of the Ash for its issue

The fire-keepers are the least doctrinal branch of the priesthood and the one through which all the others' doctrine is paid. It is one of the ten workings of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]].
