## Ritual: Ts'en'ul Tq'an

Working
: [The Binding of the Return]{.name data-type="lore/custom"}, the chant that holds the sun to its road at the winter solstice, and at the scale of one person the binding of a vow. A vow sworn to the sun on his opened day is entered in the god's own accounts, and a vow broken leaves a hole that is filled from the breaker's household

Position
: The great binding falls on the winter solstice, the first day of Ch'ulikal, when the Ts'en count opens the year. A vow is bound at dawn on a day the Reckoner of Conjunctions has opened to the sun, as the first light reaches the summit

Cost
: The heaviest in the tradition. At the winter solstice the Ts'enmah opens his own veins into the eternal flames; at the summer solstice the hearts of dedicated captives pay the year's binding into the ledger, and the Master of the Channels reads the flow. A vow costs the swearer's blood from tongue, earlobe or genitals, given on the pyramid's eastern stair

Performed by
: The Keeper of the Binding at the solstice, with the Ts'enmah; any solar Ch'alishbal for a vow

The binding of the sun spends from the city's whole account, and a city in deficit at the year's close goes into the solstice afraid. The binding of a vow spends only from the swearer, which is why warriors bind their captives' coming sacrifices in advance: the vow holds the captive to the rite as the chant holds the sun to its road. It is one of the ten workings of [[lore-chumtsena|Ch'um Ts'ena]].
