Thurarth Script is the third branch of the graven work. Proto-Pelwar _thura-vark_ gives [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thuravark Runic Script]] in [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal Language]] and imperial [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon Script]]; in the mouths of the highlanders of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan People]] it came out **Thurarth**, and the letterforms softened with the name. The name is made of the tongue's own pieces. [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]] has no `v`, so _vark_ loses its lip sound and is left as _ark_; the highlanders lenite where their cousins harden, so the final stop softens to `th`; and the vowel at the join falls away, so _thura_ and _arth_ close up as _thúr-arth_, stressed on the first syllable as every Áelendi word is. The ending is one the tongue gives to words of its own, as in _khelmarth_, accursed. All three descend, at a distance no one can measure, from [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath Script]] of the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]]—by way of a single Proto-Pelwar row taught to the mountain tribes when those tribes were Khazari subjects, if the usual account is right. The Áelendan find this unremarkable and say so: the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} tradition holds that everything worth having was received from something older, and a people embarrassed by that has misunderstood its own position. The [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordheimn]] refuse it outright. The staves are still cut rather than written, but curves are permitted, the terminals are hooked, and the whole row looks like something between a rune and a vine.

It carries Áelendi and older [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri Language]]. A character needs **both** the script and the language.

## A script in retreat

[[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] writes in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron Script]] now, and has for centuries—the imperial alphabet arrived with trade and stayed because it is faster, and a modern Élavendri book of hours or land grant is in Vylarian letters with Élavendri diacritics added for pitch. Thurarth survives in three places: **magical texts**, where the older form is held to matter and where nobody willing to risk a substitution has yet proposed one; **poetry**, where the runes' associations are part of the effect; and **objects of great antiquity**, which is to say anything nobody has recopied.

That last is not a small category. The Áelendan highlands are full of cut stone, and the Áelendan habit of _not_ writing important things down—the Kindred Pact is deliberately unwritten, and the [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] forbids writing in certain rooms altogether—means the inscriptions that do exist were cut by people who thought the matter exceptional.

## Marks the others do not have

Thurarth carries a set of enclosing and binding marks that Thuravark lacks entirely and Tharavarkon has only in its crowned-rune form: **wards**, cut around a name or a threshold, which the Áelendan tradition treats as operative rather than decorative. It also has a convention for writing a **shadow name**—the true name, as against the sun-name a person is called by—in a form that can be read only by someone who already knows it, which is either a cipher or a piece of practical magic depending on who is explaining it.

## Who has it

Those training in the magical arts, for whom it is a working necessity rather than an accomplishment; a portion of the Élavendri priesthood; antiquarians; and the scholars of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]], whose interest in Áelendan inscriptions is the same interest they take in pre-imperial ones. Ordinary literate Élavendri cannot read it. [[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]] mostly decline to, on principle.

## In play

The classic Thurarth problem is that the character reading the inscription is a mage, the inscription is a ward, and reading it aloud is an act rather than an observation. Beyond that: the language in old cuttings is archaic Áelendi, not modern Élavendri, so a reader's Language skill is doing more work than they expect, and the two are close enough that a wrong reading will still make a kind of sense.
