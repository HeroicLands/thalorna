Thurarth is the third branch of the graven work. Proto-Pelwar _thura-vark_ gives [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thuravark Runic Script]] in [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal Language]] and imperial [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon]]; in the mouths of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan highlanders]] it came out **Thurarth**, and the letterforms softened with the name. The name is made of the tongue's own pieces. [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] has no `v`, so _vark_ loses its lip sound and is left as _ark_; the highlanders lenite where their cousins harden, so the final stop softens to `th`; and the vowel at the join falls away, so _thura_ and _arth_ close up as _thúr-arth_, stressed on the first syllable as every Áelendi word is. The ending is one the tongue gives to words of its own, as in _khelmarth_, accursed. All three descend, at a distance no one can measure, from [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath Script]] of the [[lore-flkkhazar|Khazár Folk]]—by way of a single Proto-Pelwar row taught to the mountain tribes when those tribes were Khazari subjects, if the usual account is right. The Áelendan find this unremarkable and say so: the [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} tradition holds that everything worth having was received from something older, and a people embarrassed by that has misunderstood its own position. The [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordmen]] refuse it outright. The staves are still cut rather than written, and they are the letters of [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thuravark]] in the same order. The highlanders add their own touches: curves are permitted, the terminals are hooked, and the whole row looks like something between a rune and a vine.

It carries [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]]. A character needs **both** the script and the language.

## Carved, not written

The settlers who came to the highlands had only these runes, and they are for carving: the first Áelendan had nothing to write with in the ordinary way. They took the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]] from the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]] for everything written, and write [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] in it. Thurarth is kept for the work that is cut, which is **monuments** and boundary stones, **old magic**, where the older form is held to matter, and **poetry**, where the runes' associations are part of the effect.

The Áelendan habit of _not_ writing important things down—the Kindred Pact is deliberately unwritten, and the [[place-eshalshldg|Ésalosa Lodge]] forbids writing in certain rooms altogether—means the inscriptions that do exist were cut by people who thought the matter exceptional. The highlands are full of cut stone, and anything in the region old enough to be called ancient is Áelendi, carved in Thurarth or set down in the Veldikko.

[[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] does not write in it. The [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] write in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], and a book of hours or land grant is in Vylarian letters with Élavendri diacritics added for pitch.

## Marks the others do not have

Thurarth carries a set of enclosing and binding marks that Thuravark lacks entirely and Tharavarkon has only in its crowned-rune form: **wards**, cut around a name or a threshold, which the Áelendan tradition treats as operative rather than decorative. It also has a convention for writing a **shadow name**—the true name, as against the sun-name a person is called by—in a form that can be read only by someone who already knows it, which is either a cipher or a piece of practical magic depending on who is explaining it.

## Who has it

Those training in the magical arts, for whom it is a working necessity rather than an accomplishment; a portion of the Élavendri priesthood, who learn it as a second script; antiquarians; and the scholars of the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]], whose interest in Áelendan inscriptions is the same interest they take in pre-imperial ones. Ordinary literate Élavendri cannot read it. [[affiliation-bardicolgs|The Bardic Colleges]] mostly decline to, on principle.

## In play

The classic Thurarth problem is that the character reading the inscription is a mage, the inscription is a ward, and reading it aloud is an act rather than an observation. Beyond that: the language in old cuttings is Áelendi, not Élavendri, so a reader's Language skill is doing more work than they expect, and the two are close enough that a wrong reading will still make a kind of sense.
