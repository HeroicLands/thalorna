The Semaron is the working alphabet of the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]]—twenty-four letters written left to right, each with a majuscule and a minuscule form, one sign to one sound. Its name is simply the [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]] word for _the letters_, from _sema_, "mark"; a single character is a _sema_, and to a Vylarian clerk the word carries no more romance than "the alphabet" does. That flatness is a large part of why it traveled.

As with any script, it is a writing system and not a tongue. A character needs **both** the Semaron and the relevant [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] to read anything set down in it, and the Semaron carries a good many languages that have nothing to do with each other—see [[sohl-none-docskill-script|Script]] for how the two skills combine.

A scribe marks a stressed word in Semaron by writing it in rubric red.

## The letters

The letterforms descend from the older carved runes of [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon]], but centuries of reed and quill have rounded every angle out of them. Strokes are continuous and slightly forward-leaning; the pen is meant to leave the page as seldom as possible. The twenty-four letters are the sounds of Vylari plus _j_ and _w_ for its neighbours' tongues, and a sound with no letter of its own is spelled out from the letters there are: _ch_ is written _t_ and _sh_. Vowel length is carried by a curl above the letter, typed by doubling the vowel (_aa_, _ii_, _uu_), and the breathed _f_ and _th_ by a small hook, which is why a hurried provincial hand is so often ambiguous about precisely which lord is meant. The script has no quotation marks: a Vylari writer marks reported speech with the word _vethor_. One diamond point, set at mid-height, marks every pause, from a comma to the end of a sentence. A glottal stop is a short stroke above the letter it follows, on any letter, so the catch in _k'aralzon_ is a stroke over its _k_; it is how a Vylari writer spells the catch in a foreign name such as _Au'Khelathu_, and the script has no hyphen, so the break in a built name is written the same way.

The opening of a sworn oath in High Vylari, _Voza ulthoi okethen denon zaron, ul kalmetharoi_ ("May the gods see this oath, and the ancestors"), in the Kuriale hand:

::: {lang=vyl}
Voza ulthoi okethen denon zaron, ul kalmetharoi.
:::

## Hands

[[affiliation-imprlscrblgld|The Guild]] recognizes three, and a reader's fluency is not the same in all of them:

- **_Semaron Kuriale_**, the chancery hand—forward-leaning, close-set, unligatured, and slow. Edicts, land registers, treaty texts, anything that must still be legible in two hundred years.
- **_Semaron Kursiva_**, the running hand—the daily hand of clerks, factors and correspondents, heavily ligatured and abbreviated, three times faster to write and markedly harder to read. Most surviving Vylarian documents are in it.
- **Provincial hands**—the local drifts of the cursive, in [[place-hylen|Hilen]] and [[place-alyssa|Alessa]] and the frontier garrisons. A scribe raised on one reads another slowly.

## Who has it

Literacy in the Semaron is what the Imperial Scribal Guild sells, examines and defends. Its seven-year course teaches the formal hand, the cursives, the certification formulas and the detection of forgery, and a Guild seal at the foot of a document is what makes the document enforceable. Outside the Guild, the script is held by the clergy, by perhaps a fifth to a third of the nobility, and by the senior clerks of the great trading houses. Guildsmen and prosperous merchants commonly know their numerals, their trade marks and the shape of their own names and no more, and hire a scribe for anything longer.

## Beyond the Empire

The Semaron is the nearest thing [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] has to a common alphabet. It carries Vylari, Provenzal, Tarveni, modern Élavendri and western Valtari. [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] is written in it; [[skill-provnzlng|Provenzal]] is written in nothing else; the [[affiliation-byzarianlg|Bizarian League]] keeps its international books in it, whatever tongue the entries are in, while a house's local books and the Bizarian language itself are written in the [[skill-helnkscrpt|Helónika]]; and [[skill-varokhlng|Varokhi speakers]] who want anything written down borrow it, while insisting it is a foreign tool and not Varokhi writing. Foreigners generally call it "the Vylari letters," a name no Vylarian uses.

## In play

A character with the Semaron and a language written in it can read a clean text at ordinary speed. Cursive and provincial hands bite: they are exactly the sort of poor hand the Script rules penalize, and a document that has been copied twice by clerks who did not understand it will have collected errors that no amount of Mastery recovers. Forgery is the standing plot: Guild authentication is a Semaron test in its own right, and the empire's whole administrative fabric rests on the assumption that it works.
