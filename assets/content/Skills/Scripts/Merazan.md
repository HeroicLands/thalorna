The Merazan, the ledger-hand, is the writing of [[place-taruzelrgn|Taruzel]] and the oldest working script in the west—twenty-four letters, all of them consonants, written left to right, with the vowels supplied by the reader out of context or added above and below the line as circumflex, macron and acute. It descends from the proto-Ankarian writing that also produced [[skill-dnshkscrpt|Dunshak]] and [[skill-okhthztscrpt|Okhatha-zethu Script]]; the three are visibly cousins and mutually unreadable.

It is a script, not a tongue: a character needs **both** the Merazan and the relevant [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]]. It carries [[skill-taruzelilng|Taruzeli]] natively, [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]] in the southern ports, and, in the hands of a factor working far from home, whatever else needs recording.

## Pointing

The abjad's ambiguity is the whole of its character. Unpointed, _t-l-m_ may be read a dozen ways, and a practiced reader resolves it from the sentence without noticing the work. Custom therefore draws a hard line: in letters, poetry and ordinary correspondence the vowels are left out, and **in a contract, a bill of lading, a letter of credit or a ledger entry they are always written**. Unpointed commercial text is not merely informal—before a Taruzeli magistrate it is evidence of intent to equivocate, and the [[affiliation-vezakuni|Vezakuni]] will not honor a draft in which the sum is unpointed.

## The apparatus around it

Merazan literacy is inseparable from the machinery of Taruzeli commerce: houses of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation]] issue on paper of a known cut struck with the eight-pointed star, in a known hand, under a known wax. Forgery is therefore attacked on four fronts at once—the paper, the hand, the seal and the pointing—and the scribe who can read all four is worth more than the clerk who can merely read.

## Who has it

The nobility, the clergy, and the professional scribes. Guild merchants hold what the guilds call **counting literacy**—numerals, the trade marks, the standing abbreviations for weights and coin, and their own name—and hire a scribe the moment continuous prose is required. Ships' masters carry a pointed cargo manifest they cannot themselves compose.

## In play

Reading unpointed Merazan that a character did not expect—an old letter, a name on a boundary marker, an inscription with the vowels worn off—is the standard difficulty; the reader will produce _a_ reading, confidently, and it may be the wrong word entirely. Pointed commercial text is easy by comparison. Note also the direction: a Taruzeli factor and a Dunhari poet, both literate, both writing an abjad, cannot read each other at all, and one of them is writing the other way round.
