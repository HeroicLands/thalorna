Jurthati is a tongue of the Tantsou family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Jurthati, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Jurthati is the speech of the [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai Empire]]—the great island and the lesser island east of the mainland, which their inhabitants call **Teyan Jenjai**, the Nine Mountains Above the Clouds of Heaven: the peaks stand above the clouds, and the people who live on them stand above every other people, the western mainland first among them. In speech the name shortens to **Jenjai**, the Nine Mountains, the native reading of the name the court writes _Jenchai_. It descends from the Tantsou brought across the strait a thousand years and more ago, and a thousand years of separation, mutual non-recognition and open war have made it a distinct language rather than a dialect. [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou]] does not concede this, and Tantsou grammarians still describe Jurthati as a provincial corruption; Jurthati scholars return the compliment by describing mainland speech as what happens to a language administered by clerks.

The [[skill-jurthatlng#lexicon|Jurthati Lexicon]] gives the words in their native reading with the changes that make them from Tantsou, the endings of the honorific grid, and the rules a house or a granted name is built by.

## Overview

Where [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]] is a language of ministries—precise, compounding, built to specify—Jurthati is a language of obligation. Its distinctive machinery is social: an elaborate apparatus of honorific, humble and neutral verb forms that encodes the relative standing of speaker, listener and subject in nearly every sentence uttered. There is no neutral way to say that a man arrived. The verb must place him, and place the speaker relative to him, and a wrong choice is not a grammatical error but an insult with consequences in a society where a retainer's honor is a live instrument.

The language is correspondingly indirect. A refusal is very rarely a negation; it is an expression of the difficulty of the thing, or of the speaker's own inadequacy to it, and a listener who takes it literally has misunderstood. This has ruined more than one mainland embassy.

## How Jurthati sounds

Jurthati is open-syllabled and even: a consonant and a vowel, again and again.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_, _i_, _o_ and _u_. A long _ō_ is written _ou_.

**The consonants.** _w_, _j_, _ts_, _z_, _r_, _y_, _d_, _k_, _m_ and _n_.

**Clusters.** None. A syllable may close only on _-n_.

**Endings.** Jurthati words end in _-ai_, _-ei_, _-wu_, _-wo_ or _-ja_.

**What Jurthati leaves out.** It has no Tantsou _-ng_, no _zh_, and no closed syllable except in _-n_.

## Phonology

**Vowels:** Five, **a e i o u**, each short or held long. The mainland's gliding pairs have mostly settled into long vowels—_ao_ and _ou_ are single long vowels on the islands—and only _ai_, _ei_ and _ui_ survive as pairs.

**Pitch:** The mainland's four tones have collapsed into a **pitch-accent** that the islanders do not write. A word the mainland sings in the high level or the rising tone is said high on the islands; one in the falling or the low tone is said low. The tones simply drop: _lei_, spirit, is _rei_; _shou_, water, is _shu_; _yan_, cloud, is _yan_; _wei_, jade, is _wei_. The high level tone left length behind, so the mainland's _tan_, celestial, is still _tan_. The pitch distinguishes word pairs rather than carrying grammatical weight—_tsu_ said high is earth, said low a tribe—and many mainland words have fallen together outright, which the shared script keeps apart in writing. The tone-poor result is the plainest difference between island and mainland speech.

**The lost _-ng_:** A closing _-ng_ has gone into the vowel before it, which is held long: _teng_, heaven, is _te_; _jing_, metal, is _ji_; _wong_, the sea, is _wo_. A closing _-n_ stays, held for a beat of its own.

**Consonants:** The islands have no _zh_ and no _l_: _zh_ became _z_ and _l_ a tapped _r_, so the mainland's _zho_, dragon, is _zo_. An _h_ before _u_ became _f_, and a _t_ before _u_ became _ts_, so _hou_, harmony, is _fu_ and _tou_, earth, is _tsu_. Inside a compound the second part's voiceless opening is voiced: _chai_, mountain, stands alone with its _ch_ and closes a compound as _-jai_, so the Nine Mountains are _Jenjai_. Two consonants meet only where a syllable's _-n_ stands before the next. The overall effect is a language of open, evenly weighted syllables—flatter than Tantsou, and markedly easier for a foreigner to pronounce badly enough to be understood.

**Prosody:** Even mora-timing, a long vowel and a closing _-n_ each counting as a beat. Jurthati verse is counted rather than stressed or rhymed, which is why the short forms—the nine-mora and eighteen-mora shapes composed at every level of society—travel so poorly into western tongues.

## Grammar Notes

**Word order:** Subject-Object-Verb, rigidly. The verb is final and carries the honorific load, so the socially decisive information in a Jurthati sentence arrives last, and interrupting a speaker before the verb is both rude and uninformative.

**Particles:** Grammatical relations are marked by postposed particles rather than by suffixed endings—_ku_ after the doer of the act, _ri_ binding a word to the one after it, _bo_ closing a question. Tantsou grammarians treat this divergence from mainland practice as the clearest evidence of decay.

**Honorific register:** Three tiers of verb (humble, neutral, honorific) interact with a further distinction between plain and polite forms, and with a witnessed form for speech before a third person of standing, giving a practical grid of twelve ways to say the same act. The verb _wa_, to answer, is _wanu_ when the speaker humbly answers, _wadan_ when a lord answers, and _wadanrewu_ when a retainer reports his lord's answer to a guest in the lord's hall. Retainer speech to a lord, lord to retainer, lord to lord of equal rank, and any of these in the presence of a third party are all formally distinct.

**No grammatical gender**, and no number marking on the noun; both are supplied by context or by counters, of which there are many, sorted by the shape and nature of the thing counted.

## Script & Literacy

Jurthati is written in [[skill-tengchouscript|Teng Chou]], the same logographic Celestial Record the mainland uses—the script is not tied to either language, and a Jurthati retainer and a Tantsou minister who cannot exchange three spoken sentences can negotiate in writing without difficulty. Neither court will concede a separate script to the other, so the islands write the mainland's characters and read them aloud in their own tongue.

Two island habits diverge. First, the reading of a character in Jurthati is frequently not its Tantsou reading, and educated Jurthati hold **two** readings for many characters—the native word and the borrowed mainland pronunciation—with the choice between them carrying its own register. The realm's name, its ranks and castes, its titles and its rites are spoken in the mainland pronunciation, tones and all, while the same characters read in the native way give _Jenjai_ for _Jenchai_ and _dozo_ for a great lord's _Daozhuo_. Second, calligraphy in the formal hand is a spiritual discipline in Jenchai rather than a bureaucratic qualification, studied by warrior-retainers alongside blade-work and tea-craft, so literacy runs higher among the island aristocracy than among the mainland rank they correspond to.

## Regional Dialects

**Court Jurthati (Chai Kowen):** The prestige speech of the ceremonial Emperor's household and the great lords' courts. Slowest, most honorific-dense, and the standard for poetry.

**Coastal dialects:** The speech of the harbor provinces, with a considerable Valtari and Tantsou trade vocabulary and much of the honorific grid worn smooth by use.

**Mountain dialects:** Conservative, keeping a closing _-ng_ the standard has lengthened away, and often unintelligible to a coastal speaker at speed.

**Shrine register:** Archaic, used in Celestial Order rites, and preserving mainland forms whole, tones and all, centuries after ordinary speech abandoned them.

## Sample Phrases

- **"Jen chai, kei te."**—"Nine mountains, one heaven." (Proverbial; the claim to an undivided Mandate.)
- **"Ja ku wa."**—"The blade answers." (Of Ruising, the island form of ritual death, taken as a mode of speech.)
- **"Chai ku jo bo?"**—"Does the mountain fall?" (Of patience under pressure; the question needs no answer.)
- **"Kobei ku wanure."**—"This unworthy one humbly answers." (A formal acceptance, in the humble and polite forms.)
- **"Tanzo ku wadan bo?"**—"Does my lord answer?" (The furthest a retainer may press a lord for a decision.)

## Related Languages

Jurthati's nearest relative by far is [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]], from which it descends; the relationship is close enough that a great deal of vocabulary is transparently shared, and distant enough that spoken mutual intelligibility is poor to nil, particularly in the honorific machinery, which Tantsou simply does not have. Written communication is unaffected. Sustained trade contact has left Jurthati with a stratum of [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]] harbor vocabulary and a smaller one of [[skill-tantsoulng|Tantsou]] administrative terms readopted in their mainland pronunciation.

## Naming Traditions

A Jurthati name is house first, personal second—the reverse of western practice and a direct statement about which of the two matters. Personal names are granted rather than inherited among the retainer classes, most often at coming of age or on the occasion of a service worth marking, and may be granted again: a man who changes lords, takes vows, or survives a defeat that ought to have killed him may be renamed, and the old name is not used afterward. A granted name is two words, the second voiced as in any compound: _Rewei_, dawn-jade; _Tsuija_, iron-blade.

Great Lords' houses take their names from features of their mountains—a peak, a strait, a hot spring, a stand of cedar—so that a house name is also a map reference: _Yanjai_, Cloud Peak; _Hojujai_, Hot Spring Place. Retainer families take theirs from the office they have held longest, which is why so many Jurthati surnames translate as things like Gate-Keeping, Second Fletching, and Records of the Salt, _Besheju_.

## Jurthati Lexicon {#lexicon}

The islands of [[affiliation-jenchaiempr|Jenchai]] speak the mainland's words with the music taken out. Where a Tantsou minister sings _teng_ for heaven, rising through the syllable and closing on _-ng_, a Jurthati fisherman says _te_, the nasal gone into a long vowel and the rising tone left behind as a high pitch. A thousand years of separation have made [[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthati]] a language of open syllables and even beats: every word closes on a vowel or on _n_, two consonants meet inside a word only where an _n_ stands first, and the verb at the close of a sentence carries the speaker's whole relation to the listener and the subject.

This page is the word-hoard of the island tongue. It gives the inherited stock as the change table makes it from [[skill-tantsoulng#lexicon|Tantsou]], the island words beside it, the endings of the honorific grid, the rules a new word or name is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in Jenchai.

Jurthati holds two readings for much of its vocabulary. The **native reading** is the word as the islands say it, made from the mainland word by the changes below. The **learned reading** is the mainland pronunciation, written as the mainland writes it, tone marks and all, and kept for the realm's name, titles, rites, the names of castes and offices, and the set phrases of the court and the shrines. The learned reading keeps the _-ng_, the _zh_, the _l_ and the four tones the native reading loses, so _Ruising_, the ritual death, is a learned word whose native reading is _ruizi_. The dictionary gives the native reading; the learned forms the notes write stand in the table of names outside the pattern below.

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

Jurthati writes five vowels, each short or long, and the mainland's consonants less _zh_, _l_ and _ng_. The mainland's four tones are a pitch-accent on the islands that the pages do not write: a word is said high or low, the pitch parts word pairs and carries no grammar, and where the loss of tone has left two words alike in writing, the pitch is often the only thing that tells them apart.

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, and closes on a vowel or on _n_.

SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may open a word' WHEN 'closeConsonant' THEN 'Consonants that close a word' WHEN 'vowelPair' THEN 'Vowels that may stand together' WHEN 'innerCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may stand inside a word' END AS "Edge", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), ''), CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN 'none' ELSE '—' END) AS "Sounds"

### Clusters

Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel, or a vowel alone, and an _n_ may close it as a beat of its own; verse is counted in these even beats. So two consonants meet inside a word only where a syllable's _n_ stands before the next consonant, and the next consonant is voiced, as the voicing rule below makes it.

### From Tantsou to Jurthati

```sql
SELECT step AS "Step", change AS "Change", coalesce("where", '—') AS "Where"
FROM (SELECT step, step_position, "where", unnest(string_split(change, '; ')) AS change,
             generate_subscripts(string_split(change, '; '), 1) AS part
      FROM sound_changes
      WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-jurthatlng' AND name = 'From Tantsou to Jurthati')
ORDER BY step_position, part
```

The rows of one step are one change, written out sound by sound.

1. **The tones become pitch.** The acute and the grave fall away. The macron stays as length: a vowel in the mainland's high level tone is a long vowel on the islands.
2. **The _-ng_ becomes length.** A closing _ng_ is lost into the vowel before it, which is held long: _teng_ gives _te_, _jing_ gives _ji_, _wong_ gives _wo_. A closing _n_ stays, and is held as a beat of its own.
3. **Gliding vowels settle.** _ao_ and _ou_ become single long vowels, _iao_ becomes _yo_ and loses its glide after _ch_, _uo_ becomes _o_, _ie_ becomes _e_, and a long _ei_ becomes one long vowel. The pairs _ai_, _ei_ and _ui_ stay.
4. **Two consonants change.** _zh_ becomes _z_ and _l_ becomes the tapped _r_.
5. **Before _u_**, an opening _h_ becomes _f_.
6. **Before _u_**, an opening _t_ becomes _ts_.

**Pitch.** A root in the high level or the rising tone is said high; a root in the falling or the low tone is said low. The pitch is not written. Where two roots fall together in writing, the pitch parts them when it differs, and nothing in speech parts them when it does not.

## Building words {#building-words}

A compound sets two words side by side, the modifier first, as on the mainland: _ho_, fire, and _shu_, water, make _hoju_, a hot spring. A name is written as one word; a title or an office keeps its words apart.

### Voicing

Inside a compound, a second part that opens on a voiceless consonant takes the voiced one. A word with such an opening stands alone in its plain form and closes a compound in its voiced form, and that compound form stands in the suffix table, where the guard holds it to this table.

```sql
SELECT '`' || sound || '`' AS "Plain", '`' || becomes || '`' AS "Voiced"
FROM mutations WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-jurthatlng' AND mutation = 'voicing' ORDER BY position
```

The mountain _chai_ stands alone with its _ch_ and closes a compound as _-jai_: _jen_ and _-jai_ make _jenjai_, the Nine Mountains in the native reading. A word that opens on a vowel, a nasal, _r_, _w_, _y_ or a voiced stop closes a compound unchanged, and two words otherwise meet without change.

### Suffixes

The endings below are added to a word as a second word is.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-jurthatlng'
ORDER BY position
```

### Tiers and forms

[[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthati]] places every act it names. A verb takes one of three tiers: the **humble** `-nu` for the speaker's own act and his household's, the plain stem for an act of no standing, and the **honorific** `-dan` for the act of anyone above the speaker. On the tier sits the **polite** `-re` when the listener is owed courtesy, and on that the **witnessed** `-wu` when a third person of standing hears it. Three tiers, polite or plain, witnessed or not, make twelve forms of every verb, and the Honorific grid table below gives them for _wa_, to answer. The tier is decided by the subject, the polite form by the listener and the witnessed form by whoever else is in the room, so a retainer reporting his lord's answer to a guest in the lord's hall says _wadanrewu_.

Counters are set on a number, and the number on what it counts: _wenno wo_, five warriors. A counter is chosen by the shape of the thing counted.

## Making names {#making-names}

**A person** of Jenchai carries the house name first and the personal name after it. Among the retainer classes the personal name is granted, most often at coming of age or for a service worth marking, and it is granted again when a man changes lords, takes vows or survives a defeat that ought to have killed him; the old name is not used afterward. A granted name is a compound of two words, as _Rewei_, dawn-jade, or _Tsuija_, iron-blade, and its second part is voiced as any compound's is. The lower castes hold no personal name.

**A great lord's house** takes its name from a feature of its mountain, so that the name is also a place on the mountain: a peak, a strait, a hot spring, a stand of cedar. The feature closes on _-jai_, place, as _Yanjai_, Cloud Peak, and _Hojujai_, Hot Spring Place.

**A retainer family** takes its name from the office it has held longest, and an office that keeps records closes on _-ju_, as _Besheju_, Records of the Salt.

**A title, a caste or a rite** is written in the learned reading, and every such name the notes give stands under `kept` in the register and in the table below with its native reading where the words reach it. A new native name follows the voicing and suffix rules on this page.
