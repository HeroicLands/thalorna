Áelendi is a tongue of the Pelwar family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Áelendi, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill; see [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]] for the linguistic and cultural background of the tongue itself.

Áelendi is the most conservative member of the entire Pelwar language family, preserved and spoken by the Áelendan people—the "People of the Pact"—who dwell in the mountains of central Élavendre. The Áelendan consider themselves the guardians of an ancient compact with the land itself, and their language reflects this sacred role.

## Overview

Áelendi represents a direct descent from an ancient form of [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar People]], preserved with minimal change over millennia. The Áelendan deliberately minimize linguistic change as a matter of cultural practice, viewing language change as a form of cultural loss. New concepts are incorporated through loan translation (creating new words from ancient roots) rather than loanwords.

## Phonology

Áelendi preserves a complex phonemic inventory lost in younger Pelwar languages:

**Consonants:** Áelendi distinguishes plain, aspirated, and ejective consonants for most stop consonants (t/th/t', d/dh/d', k/kh/k', g/gh/g'). These distinctions, which have merged or been abandoned in Élavendri proper, remain phonemic and meaningful. The language employs only one fricative (s), one liquid of each type (l, r), and the nasals m and n, with other Pelwar fricatives represented as secondary articulations of stops.

**Vowels:** Áelendi maintains eight vowel positions with clear length distinctions, written with the circumflex—â, ê, î, ô, û. The nasal vowels of Élavendri are not true phonemes in Áelendi but rather the result of n dropping out after a vowel. The vowel inventory is more stable and resistant to diphthongization than in Élavendri.

**Stress:** Stress consistently falls on the first syllable of roots, following a clear predictable pattern. The acute stands over that vowel where it is short and the circumflex where it is long, and neither mark appears later in a simple word, so a word met cold is stressed correctly on sight. This predictability, combined with conservative phonetics, makes Áelendi relatively easy to learn despite its phonetic complexity.

### Áelendi and Élavendri

The two tongues descend from one, and nearly every difference between them is a distinction [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] has given up rather than one Áelendi has invented. This is what makes a conservative language conservative, and it is the most useful thing to know about Áelendi: hearing it, a lowlander is hearing his own speech with the losses put back.

**The ejectives.** Proto-Pelwar held three stop series, and Áelendi holds all three. Élavendri holds two: its ejectives have fallen together with the plain stops, which is why its inventory runs to four common stops where Áelendi's runs to twelve.

**The aspirates.** Where Áelendi closes the mouth, Élavendri opens it. Its th and dh are breath rather than closure, and the back pair went further—kh has weakened to plain h and gh has dropped out between vowels with the vowels closing over the gap. Áelendi keeps all four as stops, and is left with a single fricative for the whole language.

**The long vowels.** An Áelendi long vowel is held on one colour and let go. Élavendri broke those vowels into the diphthongs it is admired for, so an Áelendi â stands where Élavendri writes ae, an î where it writes ia or ie, an û where it writes ýe.

**The stress.** Áelendi has not moved its stress off the first syllable in the whole of its recorded life. Élavendri traded fixed stress for the melodic accent it sings by, pitch carrying as much as weight, which is the feature that defeats learners of the lowland tongue and the one an Áelendan finds hardest to take seriously.

Cognate pairs, the Áelendi form beside the Élavendri one:

| Áelendi  | Élavendri | What Élavendri let go                 |
| -------- | --------- | ------------------------------------- |
| T'áldor  | Táldor    | the ejective, merged with the stop    |
| Khálden  | Hâlden    | the aspirated stop, weakened to h     |
| Móghoren | Móren     | gh, dropped between the vowels        |
| Dêran    | Déraen    | the long vowel, broken to a diphthong |

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Áelendi employs a rigid SOV word order that has remained constant throughout its history. Poetic reordering is permitted but rare and marked as archaic even in ancient poetry.

**Noun Cases:** Áelendi preserves a four-case system (nominative, accusative, genitive, locative), and case marking is thorough and pervasive. Élavendri counts four as well, but not the same four: its locative has fallen together with the dative, and a relation Áelendi states by an ending Élavendri states with a preposition or not at all.

**Gender:** Áelendi maintains three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter) with consistent agreement patterns. The gender system correlates closely with semantic features (masculine = strength/light; feminine = growth/darkness; neuter = static/eternal).

**Verbal System:** Verbs employ both tense and aspect distinctions, resulting in a complex conjugation system. Strong verb forms are more numerous in Áelendi than in Élavendri, and the pattern of ablaut (vowel changes) follows predictable rules.

**Articles:** Áelendi employs a simpler article system than Élavendri, with only definite and indefinite forms. The demonstrative system is more elaborate, reflecting Áelendan concerns with spatial and temporal precision.

## Liturgical Role

While Áelendi is a living language among the Áelendan, it also serves a sacred function for the broader Élavendre realm. Magical texts of great antiquity are written in Áelendi, and scholars of magic often learn Áelendi specifically to read these texts in their original form. The language is used in the highest magical workings, as the Áelendan believe it carries greater power due to its connection to ancient pact-making.

## Mutual Intelligibility

An Élavendri speaker cannot understand Áelendi without study. The phonetic differences alone create comprehension barriers, and the grammatical divergences multiply the difficulty. However, shared roots and similar overall structure allow motivated learners to acquire Áelendi, and many educated Élavendri speakers maintain passive comprehension of at least formal Áelendi.

Áelendi speakers generally understand Élavendri quite well, having borrowed or kept many Élavendri innovations. Educated Áelendan often code-switch between Áelendi and Élavendri for pragmatic reasons.

## Script & Literacy

Áelendi is cut in [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thúravardh]], the old rune-row of the highlands—the western, lenited branch of the same Proto-Pelwar graven work that gives the Nordmen their [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thurávark]] and imperial Vylaria its [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Thâravárkon]]. It survives in better health here than in [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] below, where the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Sêmarion]] has displaced it for everything but poetry and magic. The row keeps more than the lowland speech does: Thúravardh carries older Élavendri as well, and its cuttings still hold separate staves for the ejectives and the aspirated stops Élavendri has since let fall together, so a lowland scholar reading an old inscription is spelling sounds his own mouth no longer makes.

What is written rather than cut is written in the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Calathindë]], the Sinalë starlight-hand, which the Áelendan took from their neighbours rather than received from any ancestor. Nothing about the tongue came with it—Áelendi is Pelwar to the root and shares no descent whatever with Sinalë—but the [[lore-flksinale|Sinalë]] enclaves are near, old, and on terms with the highlands they keep with nobody else, and the hand came across the way everything else between them has. Why the Sinalë opened it to these neighbours and not to others is the same question their whole closeness raises, and it has never been answered by either side.

[[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] did not take it. The lowlands went the other way and adopted the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Sêmarion]], the working alphabet of the Vylarian Empire, and a modern Élavendri book is in imperial letters. So the two branches borrowed their writing from opposite neighbours, and that is the whole account of how they came to differ: one people stayed in the Sinalë's orbit and took what the Sinalë had, the other moved into Vylaria's and took what Vylaria had, and the speech of each went the way of the company it kept.

What the two hands reach is not comparable. An Élavendri page can be read by anyone holding the Sêmarion, which is most of the educated across Mídhalión and western Ankaris; an Áelendi page can be read by the Sinalë and by almost nobody else alive. And the two peoples can speak and cannot read—the tongues are near enough for a conversation, and the highlander commonly has the lowland one besides, but neither makes out a line the other has set down. Only in magic and old poetry, where both still reach for Thúravardh, do they meet on a page.

A borrowed script never fits, and this one misfits in both directions at once. Calathindë was made for a soft-consonant tongue and has no letter for an ejective or for an aspirated stop, so Áelendan scribes write both as ligatures—the hand's constant ligaturing put to a job Sinalë never asked of it, and the first thing a Sinalë reader notices. Running the other way, the marks the hand keeps for stress variation have nothing here to mark, Áelendi stress never moving off the first syllable; the scribes spend them on vowel length instead, where eight positions and a length distinction need more than Sinalë ever asked the marks to carry.

The Áelendan nonetheless write very little. The Kindred Pact is deliberately unwritten, and the tradition holds that a thing committed to stone stops being renewed in the mouth. What is cut in Thúravardh is therefore exceptional by definition: wards, boundary and grave markers, and the magical texts of great antiquity that draw mages up out of the lowlands to read them in the original. True literacy is confined to those training in the magical arts, a portion of the elders, and the occasional antiquarian from the Ordo Arcanis who has been permitted—rarely, and under watch—to copy an inscription.

## Sample Phrases

- **"Al-síren ek dûl-thân, al-kír ek dûl-síl."** – "Life flows from the deep dark, light rises from the deep-still." (Ancient creation phrase, perfectly balanced.)
- **"Ek-thalíren en símat dûl-el."** – "The pact holds through all time." (Oath formula.)
- **"Síres en thal-kír, síres en thal-dûn."** – "The people of light, the people of darkness." (Archaic dualistic reference.)

## Naming Traditions

A name is inherited. A child receives the name of an honoured ancestor and is expected to carry what came with it, and the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] do not coin new ones lightly—a name entering a lineage for the first time is an event the Tradition Keepers record and recite afterward among the genealogies.

**A given name is a root, or two roots set together, and nothing else.** It carries one mark, over its first syllable, because that is where the stress falls. Masculine names close on a consonant and feminine names on -a; the neuter takes no personal names at all, belonging in the grammar to what is static and eternal.

**A clan name is the founder's name in the genitive plural**—"those of X"—and that ending is the whole difference between the two lists. After a vowel it is -ndi; after n it is -di, the nasals fusing; after any other consonant it is -andi, -endi, -indi, -ondi or -undi, taking its vowel from the last full vowel of the stem. No given name ends this way and no clan name fails to, so a highlander never has to ask which he has been told. The tongue's own name is built by the rule: áel, "first light", with the genitive plural gives Áelendi, "those of the Áel", and the people are the Áelendan by the same stem under the collective ending.

The roots are ordinary vocabulary, and a speaker hears the sense as he hears the name:

| ---- | -------------- | ---- | ---------------- |
| áel  | first light    | mîr  | ground, footing  |
| kír  | light          | rôn  | summit, horn     |
| sîl  | song           | ken  | kindred          |
| sír  | living water   | dhár | oath, the Pact   |
| thân | the deep dark  | gan  | pass, gate       |
| dûl  | deep           | k'ar | kept fire        |
| thal | shadow         | t'en | unbroken         |
| tal  | standing stone | g'ol | root             |
| nel  | snow           | sath | wind off the ice |
| khal | hall           | tír  | pasture          |
| môgh | memory         | ûr   | elder            |

Élavendri names are the lowlanders' own and are not taken up here. An Áelendan meeting one places its bearer below the tree line before the second syllable is out.
