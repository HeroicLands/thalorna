Áelendi is a tongue of the [Pelwar Family]{.name data-type="lore"}. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Áelendi, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill; see below for the linguistic and cultural background of the tongue itself.

Áelendi is the most conservative member of the entire Pelwar language family, preserved and spoken by the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan people]]—the "People of the Pact"—who dwell in the mountains of central [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]]. The Áelendan consider themselves the guardians of an ancient compact with the land itself, and their language reflects this sacred role.

## Overview

Áelendi represents a direct descent from the ancient Pelwar tongue (see [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar People]]), preserved with minimal change over millennia. The Áelendan deliberately minimize linguistic change as a matter of cultural practice, viewing language change as a form of cultural loss. New concepts are incorporated through loan translation (creating new words from ancient roots) rather than loanwords.

The [[skill-aelendlng#lexicon|Áelendi Lexicon]] gives the words with their sources and the elements the names are built from.

## How Áelendi sounds

Áelendi is the conservative highland tongue that [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] came from. It keeps the old, hard sounds that the lowland softened.

**The vowels.** _a_, _o_, _e_ and _i_ are held single and full. One acute moves stress where it falls off the rule.

**The consonants.** _dh_, _gh_ and _th_ carry the old sound, beside _r_, _l_, _n_, _d_, _g_ and _k_.

**Clusters.** _rg_, _rn_, _ld_, _nd_ and _rth_ meet inside a word. A word may close on _-n_, _-r_, _-d_ or _-th_.

**Endings.** Áelendi words end in _-on_, _-and_, _-is_, _-en_, _-i_ or _-a_.

**What Áelendi leaves out.** It has no apostrophe and none of the lowland vowel pairs (_ou_, _ae_). It avoids words of more than four syllables.

## Phonology

Áelendi preserves a complex phonemic inventory lost in younger Pelwar languages:

**Consonants:** Áelendi distinguishes plain, aspirated, and ejective consonants for most stop consonants (t/th/t', d/dh/d', k/kh/k', g/gh/g'). These distinctions, which have merged or been abandoned in Élavendri proper, remain phonemic and meaningful. The language employs only one fricative (s), one liquid of each type (l, r), and the nasals m and n, with other Pelwar fricatives represented as secondary articulations of stops.

**Vowels:** Áelendi maintains eight vowel positions with clear length distinctions. The nasal vowels of Élavendri are not true phonemes in Áelendi but rather the result of n dropping out after a vowel. The vowel inventory is more stable and resistant to diphthongization than in Élavendri.

**Stress:** Stress consistently falls on the first syllable of roots, following a clear predictable pattern. This predictability, combined with conservative phonetics, makes Áelendi relatively easy to learn despite its phonetic complexity.

### Áelendi and Élavendri

The two tongues descend from one, and nearly every difference between them is a distinction Élavendri has given up rather than one Áelendi has invented. This is what makes a conservative language conservative, and it is the most useful thing to know about Áelendi: hearing it, a lowlander is hearing his own speech with the losses put back.

**The ejectives.** Proto-Pelwar held three stop series, and Áelendi holds all three. Élavendri holds two: its ejectives have fallen together with the plain stops, which is why its inventory runs to four common stops where Áelendi's runs to twelve.

**The aspirates.** Where Áelendi closes the mouth, Élavendri opens it. Its th and dh are breath rather than closure, and the back pair went further—kh has weakened to plain h and gh has dropped out between vowels with the vowels closing over the gap. Áelendi keeps all four as stops, and is left with a single fricative for the whole language.

**The long vowels.** An Áelendi long vowel is held on one colour and let go. Élavendri broke those vowels into the diphthongs it is admired for, so an Áelendi long _a_ stands where Élavendri has _ae_, a long _i_ where it has _ia_ or _ie_, and a long _u_ where it has _ye_.

**The stress.** Áelendi has not moved its stress off the first syllable in the whole of its recorded life. Élavendri traded fixed stress for the melodic accent it sings by, pitch carrying as much as weight, which is the feature that defeats learners of the lowland tongue and the one an Áelendan finds hardest to take seriously.

Cognate pairs, the Áelendi form beside the Élavendri one:

| Móghoren | Moren     | gh, dropped between the vowels        |

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Áelendi employs a rigid SOV word order that has remained constant throughout its history. Poetic reordering is permitted but rare and marked as archaic even in ancient poetry.

**Gender:** Áelendi maintains three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter). The gender system correlates closely with semantic features (masculine = strength/light; feminine = growth/darkness; neuter = static/eternal).

**Articles:** Áelendi employs a simpler article system than Élavendri, with only definite and indefinite forms. The demonstrative system is more elaborate, reflecting Áelendan concerns with spatial and temporal precision.

## Liturgical Role

While Áelendi is a living language among the Áelendan, it also serves a sacred function for the broader Élavendre realm. Magical texts of great antiquity are written in Áelendi, and scholars of magic often learn Áelendi specifically to read these texts in their original form. The language is used in the highest magical workings, as the Áelendan believe it carries greater power due to its connection to ancient pact-making.

## Mutual Intelligibility

An Élavendri speaker cannot understand Áelendi without study. The phonetic differences alone create comprehension barriers, and the grammatical divergences multiply the difficulty. However, shared roots and similar overall structure allow motivated learners to acquire Áelendi, and many educated Élavendri speakers maintain passive comprehension of at least formal Áelendi.

Áelendi speakers generally understand Élavendri quite well, having borrowed or kept many Élavendri innovations. Educated Áelendan often code-switch between Áelendi and Élavendri for pragmatic reasons.

## Script & Literacy

Áelendi is cut in [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thurarth]], the old rune-row of the highlands—the western, lenited branch of the same Proto-Pelwar graven work that gives the [[lore-nordheimnclt|Nordheimn]] their [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thuravark Runic Script]] and the [[affiliation-vylarinmpr|Vylarian Empire]] its [[skill-thrvrknscrpt|Tharavarkon Script]]. It survives in better health here than in [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] below, where the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]] has displaced it for everything but poetry and magic. The row keeps more than the lowland speech does: Thurarth carries older Élavendri as well, and its cuttings still hold separate staves for the ejectives and the aspirated stops Élavendri has since let fall together, so a lowland scholar reading an old inscription is spelling sounds his own mouth no longer makes.

What is written rather than cut is written in the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]], the Sinale starlight-hand, which the Áelendan took from their neighbours rather than received from any ancestor. Nothing about the tongue came with it—Áelendi is Pelwar to the root and shares no descent whatever with [[skill-sinalelng|Sinale]]—but the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]] enclaves are near, old, and on terms with the highlands they keep with nobody else, and the hand came across the way everything else between them has. Why the Sinale opened it to these neighbours and not to others is the same question their whole closeness raises, and it has never been answered by either side.

[[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] did not take it. The lowlands went the other way and adopted the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], the working alphabet of the Vylarian Empire, and a modern Élavendri book is in imperial letters. So the two branches borrowed their writing from opposite neighbours, and that is the whole account of how they came to differ: one people stayed in the Sinale's orbit and took what the Sinale had, the other moved into Vylaria's and took what Vylaria had, and the speech of each went the way of the company it kept.

What the two hands reach is not comparable. An Élavendri page can be read by anyone holding the Semaron, which is most of the educated across [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]] and the western [[place-ankrscntnnt|Ankaris Continent]]; an Áelendi page can be read by the Sinale and by almost nobody else alive. And the two peoples can speak and cannot read—the tongues are near enough for a conversation, and the highlander commonly has the lowland one besides, but neither makes out a line the other has set down. Only in magic and old poetry, where both still reach for Thurarth, do they meet on a page.

A borrowed script never fits, and this one misfits in both directions at once. Veldikko was made for a clean-consonant tongue and has no letter for an ejective or for an aspirated stop, so Áelendan scribes write both as ligatures—the hand's constant ligaturing put to a job Sinale never asked of it, and the first thing a Sinale reader notices. Running the other way, the marks the hand keeps for stress variation have nothing here to mark, Áelendi stress never moving off the first syllable; the scribes spend them on vowel length instead, where eight positions and a length distinction need more than Sinale ever asked the marks to carry.

The Áelendan nonetheless write very little. The [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} is deliberately unwritten, and the tradition holds that a thing committed to stone stops being renewed in the mouth. What is cut in Thurarth is therefore exceptional by definition: wards, boundary and grave markers, and the magical texts of great antiquity that draw mages up out of the lowlands to read them in the original. True literacy is confined to those training in the magical arts, a portion of the elders, and the occasional antiquarian from the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] (the Arcane Order) who has been permitted—rarely, and under watch—to copy an inscription.

## Naming Traditions

A name is inherited. A child receives the name of an honoured ancestor and is expected to carry what came with it, and the Áelendan do not coin new ones lightly—a name entering a lineage for the first time is an event the Tradition Keepers record and recite afterward among the genealogies.

**A given name is a root, or two roots set together, and nothing else.** It carries one mark, over its first syllable, because that is where the stress falls. Masculine names close on a consonant and feminine names on -a; the neuter takes no personal names at all, belonging in the grammar to what is static and eternal.

**A clan name is the founder's name with an ending**—"those of X"—and that ending is the whole difference between the two lists. After a vowel it is -ndi; after n it is -di, the nasals fusing; after any other consonant it is -andi, -endi, -indi, -ondi or -undi, taking its vowel from the last full vowel of the stem. No given name ends this way and no clan name fails to, so a highlander never has to ask which he has been told. The tongue's own name is built by the rule: ael, "first light", with the ending gives Áelendi, "those of the Ael", and the people are the Áelendan by the same stem under the collective ending.

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

The tongue has no `f`, no `v`, no `b`, no `h` alone and no `w`: every sound the ancient tongue made with the lips fell away. So the yew, _eiva_ in the Sinale's mouth, is _eipa_ in an Áelendan's. Hearing the tongue, a scholar of the empire is hearing the ancient tongue with its lips closed.

## The Sinale loans {#the-sinale-loans}

When the band came over the eastern pass, about two thousand one hundred years ago, the Sinale met them at the lowest pool and set a watch at [Talve]{.name data-type="place/site"}, and what they taught was the wood: "You learn it: tree, path, pool. And there are places where it's thin." The words came with the teaching. They are thickest where the teaching was, in the trees, the water below the snow line, the veil and the rites, and they are absent from the herds, the kin, the body, the numbers and the oath, which the band brought with it. A second band of loans came later with the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]], the Sinale hand. The loans are almost all nouns. No pronoun, number, kin word, part of the body or ending was ever borrowed, and no given name, because names are inherited.

A loan noun takes its gender from its last sound: feminine on `-a`, neuter on any other vowel, masculine on a consonant.

The Áelendan do not count these words as borrowed. Asked, a Keeper says that they came with the keeping of the wood, and that a thing kept is part of the people who keep it. What the Áelendan refuse are the lowland words and the empire's: for a new thing from below they coin a word from their own roots, and a lowland word said in council is said once and corrected.

## Words {#words}

### The Pact, the oath and the keeping

The Pact is never written, so the words for it are the oldest in the tongue and the most carefully said. An Áelendan who swears does not promise to do a thing; he puts himself under _semnalis_, a compact between two kins that the whole valley is witness to, and a man who breaks one owes _kuthresa_, a forfeit his kin pays with him. The worst sentence the elders can give is _enthodis_, the word the empire uses for exile. In the highlands it means being walked past the tree line and not let back, which is a death that takes longer.

The verb at the heart of the field is _tháurathen_, to keep. The empire's speech kept the same word for its palace guard. The highlands kept it for what a Warden does to a spring, a grove or a cairn: stands by it, turns away whoever comes for it, and answers for it to the kin. A keeper is _thaurand_, and his duty, the whole of it, is _tháuralon_.

### Kin, the clan and the name

A clan is its founder's name with an ending, "those of" him, and the word for kin is built the same way: _sorethendi_, those of one blood. Nobody in the highlands belongs to a household the way a Vylarian belongs to one, under a master who answers for him. The head of a household is _sorkhand_, a man who speaks for the hearth in council, and the council can overrule him.

A child is _nurison_ until it is named, and the naming is the event. The genealogies, recited by the Tradition Keepers at every gathering, are lists of names and nothing else, and to have a name in them is to exist. An earned name, _kíramarlen_, is sung once at the gathering after the deed and is the bearer's from then on. A name inherited through a clan is _arlendi_, the same ending again. The lowland houses that claim Áelendan descent claim it in exactly these words, and the Keepers are the only people who can say whether the claim is true.

### The lodge, the fire and the guest

The lodge is a fire with a roof over it. Every hearth keeps _káralson_, the kept fire, which is banked at night and never let die, and a household whose fire goes out borrows a coal from a neighbor and owes for it. Where a guest sits is settled before he is fed: _sirothathen_ is to give a man his place at the fire, and _sirothalon_ is the order of the seats, worked out again for every gathering.

Half the courtesy in this field came over the eastern pass from the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]], and the loans are the gentler half. The native _dumaralon_ is guest-right as a duty: what the hall owes the man under its roof. The loan _éllepirsa_ is guest-right as a welcome, the word the Áelendan use for what passes between them and their neighbors in the wood. _Tárnepi_, bread, is a loan as well, because the first bread the band was given on the far side of the pass was Sinale bread, and the offering is still made with it.

### Herds, pasture and the high grass

The Pelwar came out of the grasslands, and the highlands kept the grass words when they had little grass left to use them on. _Ghulm_, the horse, is the same word the empire uses for its cavalry. In a valley of the Spine it means the small hard-footed beast a herder rides up to the summer grazing, and the herd a clan keeps between its valleys is _ghulmalon_, a word the empire turned into an order of knights. The move up the mountain each spring, sheep and horses and families together, is _komrelalon_. In the coastal speech the same word names a country estate with slaves.

### Mountain, snow and stone

The Áelendan live where the snow lies half the year, and the snow word is one they share with the empire: _nélamis_. The coastal speech means a thing seen on the far peaks. In the highlands it is the condition of life from autumn to the thaw, and winter is simply _nélamesa_, the snow-time. A valley is _neralis_, a tarn in its corrie _tholmara_, and a torrent in spate _ilaras_, the word the empire keeps for its one great river and declines as a god.

Stone is cut for two purposes, to raise as a boundary and to cover the dead. _Dorkima_ is a pillar, _althokis_ a dressed block, and _grumalon_ the cut in a hillside where the stones for a ring were taken, which is left open and never quarried again.

### The body, life and death

The dead are buried under cairns, a stone for each kinsman who comes to the burial, so the size of a cairn is the size of a life. A single grave is _dhemrolon_. The field of cairns at the edge of a valley is _dhemrolara_, and the same word in imperial Vylari names the rows of tombs along the roads out of a city. The Sinale words for death and grief, which the Áelendan use beside these, stand under the rites.

### Light, sky and the turning year

Here the two layers of the tongue lie side by side. The sun, the moon, the day and the month are inherited words, the ones the band brought over the pass. The light itself, and the hours when it changes, are Sinale: _áulira_, light, _írpalo_, dawn, _ólpessa_, the long dusk, and _nupa_, starlight, the Sinale's own word for what they are. An Áelendan says _ílthera_ for a star and _nupa_ for the light it gives, and does not hear the two as belonging to different tongues.

The year is counted from the crossing, _serthasis_, the word the empire uses for the founding of its capital.

### Water and the wood's weather

Above the tree line the water is inherited and the weather is the mountain's: snow, the torrent, the tarn. Below it, in the wood the band was taught to keep, nearly every word is Sinale, because the Sinale named that water first and the newcomers learned it from them. The slow river is _lánthapo_ and the cold spring _kélpasi_, and the smell of rain coming, for which the grassland tongue had no word at all, is _sólmeri_.

### Trees and the living wood

The grassland people had a word for a wood and not much more, and the Áelendan have kept it: _kaldrema_. Every tree that grows in it is called by a Sinale name. _Torpel_ is the oak, _khersel_ the pine, _eipa_ the yew, and a stand of birch is _kálpirno_. This is the field where the loans are thickest, because the trees were what the Sinale taught first: "You learn it: tree, path, pool." A birch stand within the deep wood, _khálpilonpu_, is a Sinale compound the Áelendan say whole and never take apart.

### The fae, the thin places and the wards

Nearly every word here is Sinale, and the Áelendan do not count a single one of them as foreign. The lore of the veil came with the keeping of the wood, and a thing that is kept is part of the people that keeps it. _Audhi_ is the veil, _sírnasto_ a thin place where it wears, and _orthi_ a ward. The fae themselves are _pénallanu_, one at a time; the tongue has a plural for them and the highlands do not say it.

The art the empire calls magic is _simme_ here, the Sinale word, and the Áelendi words for it share nothing with the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s. The Arcane Order's scholars, who come up the valleys to study what the tribes exist to keep, have never been taught these words, and this is the field they most want.

### Rite and the dead

A rite is done the way the Sinale showed it, so its words are theirs, and the burial of the dead is a rite. _Sélporo_ is death, _ílparo_ grief, and _nélthapa_ the lament sung at the cairn. The Sinale plant a tree for each of their dead, and the Áelendan have kept the custom and its word, _sélposalpu_, beside their own cairns. A lingering spirit, one that will not go on, is _áipelo_. The binder who settles one is _náuldinno_, and the highlands keep two or three in every generation and do not speak of them.

### Song, speech and the letters

The Kindred Pact lives in the mouth, and the people who carry it are the Tradition Keepers, the _loresand_: the word the empire uses for a lecturer at its University, and which here means a reciter, one who says the old words aloud at the gathering exactly as they were said to him. To recite is _loresathen_; an account of what was done, which in the empire is a history book, is _oldhemon_, a recitation that is never written down.

Letters are a borrowing, and late. The Áelendan cut a few words in [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thurarth]], their own rune-row, and a stave of it is _semarson_, the word the empire uses for one letter of its script. What they write with ink they write in the Sinale hand, the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]], which they call _példirno_; its scribe is _példinno_, and a roll of names kept in it _sírnerno_. These words came with the hand, a band of loans later than the rest.

### The Wardens and the border

The Áelendan keep no army and have needed one rarely, and the war words they inherited have shrunk to the size of the border they hold. _Kamdor_ is the empire's word for a general; here it is the one who leads the Wardens of a valley. _Thurgeson_, a siege in the coastal speech, is the holding of a pass against whoever is coming up it. The word for peace is the one the whole family shares, _redha_, and in the highlands it means the quiet between neighbors more than the end of a war.

### Craft and the metals

A smith in the highlands works bronze and copper more than iron, and the metal words are the inherited ones. The verb _kelthathen_, which in the empire means to keep a guild's secret, means here what it meant before the guilds: to keep a craft in one family, taught to sons and daughters and to nobody else.

### Doing, going and being

The verbs are almost all inherited, and every inherited verb closes on the old infinitive ending, as _tigathen_ (to go) and _okhethen_ (to see) do. The few Sinale verbs in the tongue belong to the veil and the wood, and keep the Sinale shape.

### Numbers and grammar words

The numbers and the small words are the most worn part of any tongue, and Áelendi is no exception. A few of them have worn down further, and these are marked as worn, with the ancient form they come from. The article _al_ is the old "yonder" worn to its first syllable, the same word imperial Vylari is wearing down in the streets of [[place-alyssa|Alessa]].

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name in the scope of the Áelendi tongue—the Áelendan, their spirits and wardens, their lodge and mountains, the tongue and its rune-row—with every note where it is met. A name in the reader's tongue is listed so that the register is complete, and it is not built. An Áelendi name whose **Built from** cell shows `—` is one the lexicon does not yet make.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-aelendlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + '), '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-aelendlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Retired names

A retired name is one the setting does not write. Each stands below with the name written in its place, and no page writes it.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-aelendlng' ORDER BY position
```
