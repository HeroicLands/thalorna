Tarûzeli is a tongue of the Tarûzelic family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Tarûzeli, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Tarûzeli is the tongue of the [[affiliation-cnfdrtntrzlnstts|Confederation of Tarûzelian States]], a maritime and mercantile language shaped by centuries of trade, commerce, and seafaring. Spoken across the Tarûzelian islands, in the harbors of the Vylarian Sea and in the port cities of [[affiliation-kalihara|Kalihara]], Tarûzeli dominates the merchant guilds and shipping routes. The language carries extensive vocabulary for navigation, commerce, and maritime activities, reflecting its speakers' deep connection to the sea and to trade networks spanning the known world.

Tarûzeli is characterized by rhythmic, sonorous tones. The language employs circumflex and macron accents to mark long vowels, giving names and words a flowing, measured quality suited to contract negotiations and formal agreements.

## Phonology

### Consonant System

Tarûzeli features a rich consonant inventory with several distinctive sound clusters:

- **Stops**: /p/, /b/, /t/, /d/, /k/, /q/ (uvular)
- **Fricatives**: /f/, /z/, /ş/ (voiceless sh-sound), /kh/ (velar fricative), /gh/ (voiced velar)
- **Nasals**: /m/, /n/
- **Liquids**: /r/, /l/
- **Affricates**: /dz/, /ts/

Characteristic clusters appear in given names: _Sharrûsh, Nadûmar, Iddînash, Tarrûk, Bîssûsh_. Double consonants (_mm_, _rr_, _dd_, _tt_, _kk_, _ss_, _zz_) are phonologically significant, often marking emphasis or etymological depth.

The velar _kh_ and uvular _q_ give Tarûzeli a guttural quality that distinguishes it from neighboring Ankarian languages.

### Vowel System

Tarûzeli employs five primary vowel qualities, each with both short and long forms:

- **a / â**—front open vowel (short/long)
- **e / ê**—front mid vowel (short/long)
- **i / î**—front close vowel (short/long)
- **o / ô**—back mid vowel (short/long)
- **u / û**—back close vowel (short/long)

Long vowels (marked with circumflex or macron) carry heavy semantic weight. Compare _Shamut_ (short) vs. _Shamûlû_ (long vowel emphasis). The interplay of long and short vowels creates a distinctive rhythmic pattern in speech.

Diphthongs are rare; vowel length and stress are preferred for emphasis.

### Stress and Prosody

Tarûzeli exhibits stress patterns that tend toward the penultimate syllable in multi-syllable words, with secondary stress on earlier syllables in longer forms. This creates a measured, rolling cadence suited to oral recitation of contracts and maritime commands.

Examples from given names:

- _NAD-û-mar_ (stress on first syllable, secondary on third)
- _SHA-mûl-û_ (stress on first, secondary on third)
- _TAR-rûk_ (stress on first)

The language is generally non-tonal but employs pitch accents for emphasis in formal contexts.

## Grammar Notes

### Word Order and Structure

Tarûzeli follows a Subject-Verb-Object pattern in declarative sentences. Nouns and verbs are typically root-based with rich affixation systems.

### Nominal System

Tarûzeli nouns carry grammatical gender (masculine/feminine) and number (singular/plural). Case distinctions exist for nominative, accusative, genitive, and locative functions, marked through suffixal systems.

Family names characteristically employ suffixes:

- **-î** (masculine lineage marker)—_Bibûdî, Shamûrîn, Girmâlî_
- **-ûnî** (feminine/collective lineage)—_Pasharûnî, Beshûnî, Pîlmûnî_
- **-ûm** (emphatic lineage)—_Bilûlûm, Kalûrûm, Eshkarûm, Ilammû_

These suffixes denote family affiliation and are fundamental to Tarûzeli identity.

### Verbal System

Verbs conjugate for aspect (perfective/imperfective), mood (indicative/subjunctive/imperative), and tense. The language distinguishes completed actions from ongoing states through aspect marking rather than strict tense.

## Script and Literacy

Tarûzeli employs the [[skill-merazanscrpt|Merâzan]] script, a consonantal alphabet derived from ancient proto-Ankarian writing systems. The script consists of 24 letters representing consonantal phonemes; vowels are optionally marked through diacritical marks (circumflex, macron, acute accent). In formal mercantile documents, vowels are always marked to avoid ambiguity in contracts and agreements.

True Tarûzeli literacy is confined to the nobility, the clergy, and the professional scribe class. Merchant guildsmen often possess functional literacy—enough to recognize trade marks, numerals, and common abbreviations—but rely on hired scribes for contracts, ledgers, and legal documents. The language is written left-to-right.

## Historical Development

Tarûzeli evolved from proto-Ankarian maritime pidgin languages that developed in the trading ports of the southern coast over the past eight centuries. As merchant guilds consolidated power, Tarûzeli became codified and standardized. The language preserves influences from older Ankarian substrate languages, particularly in its consonantal system and maritime vocabulary.

The characteristic long-vowel marking system was systematized during the Merchant Guilds' formalization period (approximately 400 years ago), when written contracts—prepared and read aloud by professional scribes—became binding legal documents. The need for precise, unambiguous scribal reading led to the adoption of the vowel-marking diacritical system still in use today.

## Regional Dialects

### Standard Tarûzeli (Confederation Dialect)

The prestige dialect, spoken in the major trading cities of the Confederation. Features full vowel marking and adherence to classical grammar. Used in formal diplomatic contexts and major contracts.

### Port Tarûzeli (Kaliharan Dialect)

Spoken in the mixed port cities of Kalihara, this dialect exhibits simplified vowel distinctions and loanwords from neighboring languages. Less formal than Standard Tarûzeli but mutually intelligible.

### Merchant Guild Register

A specialized register employed in high-level commercial negotiations, featuring archaic grammatical forms, technical vocabulary for maritime law, and heavily marked vowels for contractual precision.

## Sample Phrases

1. **Mardum qatûsh ilammû**—"A merchant's word is his bond" (lit. "Merchant-name holds-firm family-honor")

2. **Tû manî dammûsh kûrrîm**—"The sea gives and takes away" (lit. "The-sea gives-takes, justice-returns")

3. **Shamûlû zarûtan belginû**—"Fair winds and profitable voyages" (traditional merchant blessing)

4. **Kadûmû ashûram nûmarû**—"Gold measures all value" (commercial maxim)

5. **Telmûsh qûmûnî tarûzî**—"A contract binds families for generations" (legal principle)

## Related Languages

Tarûzeli stands as the primary member of the **Tarûzelic language family**, which is an isolate within western Ankarian linguistic classification. Earlier contact with now-extinct Ankarian substrate languages shaped its consonantal system, but no modern languages are closely related to Tarûzeli.

Distant connections exist to the Dunhari language family through ancient shared maritime trade pidgin ancestors, but the languages diverged significantly over the past millennium.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure

Tarûzeli names follow a clear pattern: **Given Name + Family Name**. Given names are personal identifiers, while family names carry lineage and ancestry markers.

Male given names tend toward:

- Consonant-heavy structures with emphatic double consonants
- Long vowels in the final syllable (Khurûtûsh, Damashu, Iddînash)
- Root patterns emphasizing strength, commerce, or sea-related concepts

Female given names tend toward:

- Final vowel sequences, particularly **-a**, **-û**, **-î**, **-ûya**
- More flowing consonant patterns with fewer double consonants
- Root patterns emphasizing prosperity, water, and protection

### Family Names

Family names are prestigious markers and carry explicit gender/collective suffixes:

- **-î** endings mark patrilineal descent (masculine focus)
- **-ûnî** endings mark broader family collectives or matrilineal branches
- **-ûm** endings emphasize family honor and strength

Examples: _Jakûdûnî_ (the company of the hull), _Pasharûnî_ (Pashar's collective), _Eshkarûm_ (Eshkar's honored house)

### Merchant Diaspora

Tarûzeli merchants in Kalihara and other port cities maintain given names and family names even when adopting local customs, as these names carry commercial trust and guild affiliation.
