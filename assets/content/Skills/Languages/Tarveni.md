Tarveni is a tongue of the [Pelwar Family]{.name data-type="lore"}. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Tarveni, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Tarveni is the forceful, expressive tongue of the [[lore-tarvenanclt|Tarvenan]] kingdoms and city-states scattered across the southern peninsula. Blunt where [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]] is formal, passionate where [[skill-provnzlng|Provenzal]] is artful, Tarveni reflects the directness and fierce pride of its speakers—a people who have resisted imperial rule and cultivated independent city-state cultures for centuries.

## Overview

Tarveni is a language of assertion. Speakers pride themselves on clarity and force of expression; indirectness is considered weakness or deceit. The language employs strong stress patterns, rolled consonants, and emphatic vowels that carry emotional weight even in casual conversation. A heated argument in Tarveni can sound like warfare; a tender declaration sounds like a solemn oath.

The [[skill-tarvenlng#lexicon|Tarveni Lexicon]] gives the words with their sources, including the store the tongue keeps from the older speech of the mountains.

The language varies significantly across the kingdoms and city-states of [[place-tarvenirgn|Tarvénia]], yet all speakers maintain fierce loyalty to their regional variant as the "true" Tarveni. Despite these internal variations, mutual intelligibility remains high—Tarvenans from different realms can converse readily, though each is acutely aware of subtle differences in accent and vocabulary.

## How Tarveni sounds

Tarveni is blunt and hard-edged. Its everyday words are short, two syllables most often and some only one; its learned and built words run to four and five, and its names to two or three. It keeps its final consonants, trills its _rr_, and carries an older mountain speech under it.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_ and _o_ lead; _i_ and _u_ are fewer. Each is short and firm. _ie_ stands where a stressed vowel breaks, said as one syllable, as in _kiézilo_ and _Lienrik_.

**The consonants.** _r_, _n_, _l_ and _s_ are common, and the stops _d_, _k_, _t_, _b_ and _g_ carry the weight between them; _d_ stands between vowels and at the end of a verb (_odeth_, _lokad_). _th_ is the sound of _thin_ and is used sparingly, as in _korvath_. _z_, _ts_ and _rr_ belong above all to the mountain words and the old houses (_garrunda_, _ukuts_, _Tsukurreta_).

**Clusters.** Two consonants meet inside a word: _rr_, _nd_, _mb_, _rk_, _ld_, _lt_, and a stop before _r_ (_sembro_, _tavre_, _thidro_). Where _m_ meets _r_ in an inherited word, a _b_ stands between them (_sembro_, _kombra_); the learned _zemrolo_ keeps the empire's spelling.

**Endings.** A word ends on _-o_, _-a_ or _-e_ (_orne_, _tavre_), or on _-n_, _-r_, _-l_, _-s_, _-d_ or _-th_ (_orhen_, _ulmer_, _lizel_, _nelmis_, _lokad_, _korvath_). Verbs end in _-ad_, _-ed_ or _-id_. The mountain words also end on _-k_ and _-ts_ (_zork_, _ukuts_). Women's names end in _-a_; men's in _-o_, _-ek_ or a consonant (_Thelvar_, _Hilther_, _Oztuk_). Houses end in _-is_ or _-andis_, and the old mountain houses in _-eta_ or _-egi_ (_Ugarreta_, _Ozkuregi_).

**What Tarveni leaves out.** It has no _f_, no _r_ at the start of a word, and no _kh_, _gh_, _ph_ or _dh_. No word ends on _m_, _p_, _t_, _b_, _g_, _v_ or _z_. It has no acute except where stress moves off the guide's rule.

## Phonology

Tarveni employs a rich inventory of consonants with strong distinctions between voiced and unvoiced, and particularly emphasizes strong rhotic consonants:

**Consonants:** The language features a prominent rolled /r/ (rr represents the strongest form) and a strong distinction between dental and alveolar stops (d/t). Initial consonant clusters are common and often pronounced with clarity (_tr-, dr-, kr-, gr-_). Word-final consonants are regularly preserved, unlike in Provenzal.

**Vowels:** Tarveni maintains five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) with less systematic length distinction than Vylari. The language uses diphthongs sparingly and with specific meanings.

**Stress and Accent:** Stress is marked through increased loudness and vowel lengthening. It falls by the rule of [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]], as in Vylari: a word of two syllables on the first (FER-rik), a longer word on the second-to-last (ran-ZI-o), and an acute marks any stress the rule would not choose. How the dialects differ is set out under [[skill-tarvenlng#regional-dialects|Regional Dialects]].

**Distinctive Features:** Tarveni speakers trill their /r/ sounds, and this feature is associated with educated speech and formal contexts. The language permits complex consonant clusters at word boundaries and internal positions, making it sound more consonant-heavy than Vylari or Provenzal. A glottal stop—the catch in the middle of "uh-oh"—marks emphasis or separation between words in formal speech.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Tarveni employs a basic SVO word order but allows significant flexibility for emphasis and stylistic variation.

**Gender:** Tarveni maintains three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter). Gender assignment is largely semantic in origin but has become grammaticalized.

**Derivational Morphology:** Tarveni shows extensive use of suffix chains for derivation—a single root can be modified through multiple suffixes to create words with precise meanings. This allows economy of vocabulary and precision of expression.

## Ordinary expressions

_En oked korvath_ means "I see the sun"; _en thavid dokela_, "I hear a voice"; and _en thaurad ulmer_, "I keep water". These particular spoken utterances put the speaker first, then the action and its object, as in the older examples below. _En nelthad nelthis_ smells a scent; _en tirmad tirmis_ tastes a flavour; _en tolvad dekazu_ touches a hand; and _en zarved zarvandon_ feels a bodily sensation. _En silkad korvath_ directs the eyes toward the sun; _en silkared korvath_ watches it attentively.

_Dun dadro nelthis_ describes something fragrant, with a pleasant scent. _Dun korethos nelthis_ describes an unpleasant scent. In these fixed descriptions the quality belongs to the scent after _dun_, "with". _Ath lizel daimad dadro okandon_ means "The flower seems good": it gives a good appearance. This use reports an appearance rather than guaranteeing the flower's quality.

_En thaurad kremila_ expresses anger held within, and _en thaurad galeza_ joy. These are particular ordinary feeling expressions with _thaurad_, keep or harbor. _Zarved_ is bodily sensation. _Vethzamolis_ names felt guilt over one's own wrongdoing. The older _damla_ keeps the stain on a company's name, and _zamolis_ the law's guilt.

_Orhena_ is a kindness received and the social debt it leaves. _Orhen_ remains the debt of honor arising from an insult. _Membron ol orhena_ is gratitude, grateful remembrance of a kindness. _Velda ol ko_ names admiration, esteem felt or shown toward you; the whole expression is used for appreciative regard. A name or the thing admired can replace _ko_. _Melvar velda ol ko_ gives profound reverence, and _ternandon ek velda ol ko_ joins fearful wonder to that esteem as awe.

_Avrethon_ is contentment, peaceful satisfaction with what one has. _Avandon tambro_ names the feeling of a desire fulfilled; _avandon ar tambro_ the frustration of a desire blocked or unfulfilled. _Dorandar avandon_ is sustained yearning for someone or something absent. _Solmere_ retains longing in the manner of the Provenzian songs. _Varelon ol dunandon_ is confidence in one's ability; _zed varelon_ certainty that a claim is true, which can be mistaken. Truth itself is _varelza_.

_Nethoron_ is romantic love; _holvethon_ the affection of friends; _sorethon_ the love of family; and _ulthnethoron_ selfless love given without seeking a return. Sexual lust is _bruzeza_. _Zed gerpalo_ is wholehearted loving devotion, and _thaurandon ol konandon_ loyalty to a bond without requiring a formal oath. _Konandon dun ko_ is companionship with you; _konandon ol nethora_ emotional intimacy, closeness of hearts. _Zelmira hir ko_ is pity for your distress; _zelmira dun ko_ sympathy that shares your sorrow. _Hir_ names the person concerned or benefited, while _dun_ marks accompaniment or sharing in these expressions.

_En pazad paza_ means "I eat food", and _en vimad ulmer_, "I drink water". _En zirvad ir_ draws breath inward; _en zirvad hos_ sends it outward. _En ar dunad zirvad_ is the physical cry "I cannot breathe" when the airway is blocked or breathable air is absent; it is not a statement about lacking permission to breathe. _En lothad_ means "I sleep"; _en anlothad_, "I wake". These particular utterances retain the listed action citations.

_En holimad nur ir nurkovra_ describes conception; _en kerad nur ir nurkovra_ says "I am pregnant". The child is in the womb in these particular expressions. _Kerandon ol nur ir nurkovra_ names that pregnancy. _En nurezad_ says "I give birth" and _en sulmenad nur_, "I nurse a child at the breast". _Nur_ is an ordinary child; the ancient legal category in the imperial word is not needed here.

_En zarved irthena_ says "I ache": the speaker senses bodily pain. _En irthenad ko_ says "I hurt you": the speaker causes another's pain. _En thaurad irthena_ bears pain, while _en linkad brud_ bleeds, blood escaping the body. _Okarno_ names impaired sight. _Thavarno_ names impaired hearing, and _valmarno_ difficulty walking caused by bodily impairment. Darkness, silence or a blocked road alone do not establish those conditions.

_Aipsa_ keeps the old taking-in of a fosterling or stranger. _Sora_ includes kin of the household by blood or adoption. _Nurethos ol sembro_ is a brother's son; _nuretha ol sembro_, a brother's daughter. _Sembra_ replaces _sembro_ for the corresponding children of a sister. _Verezos ol holvethon_ is a personal friend, and _verezos dun ko_ a companion sharing your company; the old company roles retain their narrower uses.

_Karalzo_ is the kept fire. _Sedon ol karalzo_ is its physical place, the hearth. _Ombela teremos_ is a long seat for several people; the old master's bench and right to it retain their craft meaning. _Vembra_ is one garment, _vembrai_ clothing collectively. _Hunela_ is a cord; _hunela solteros_ a rope, and _hunela nis_ fine sewing thread. These are individually listed expressions, with no new agreement or plural rule implied.

_Kaldre_ is wood as a material; _kaldran_ a tree, and _kaldrema_ a forest. _Orma_ is land; _orma levros_ a geographical plain whether owned or not. _Oreza ek ulmer_ names mud as a wet mixture, and _oreza ol salkon_ sand even away from a shore. _Brazmo_ is a storm on land or sea; the older sea-storm and mountain-storm words keep their own settings.

_Ath tolkemon ared ir sedon ol neth_ puts the pot in seventh position. Replace _neth_, seven, with _zorma_, eight, to put it in eighth position. The ordinal expression names a numbered place in a sequence, without adding an ending to the number. _Arzun_ counts no units. _Zed konalon ol tolkemon_ includes every pot in the group under discussion; _zed tavre_ includes both members of a pair. _Urkis ol tavre_ chooses either one, and _ar urkis ol tavre_ chooses neither. These particular set expressions do not depend on the imperial adjective's agreement forms.

_En ared zeno_ means "I am wise"; _en ared ir dumva_, "I am in a house". _Dumva ared_ says that a house exists. _Ath tolkemon ared ol ko_ says the pot belongs to you: the pot is the subject and _ko_ the possessor. _En ertad gerpalo_ performs a duty; _en tambad tolkemon_ makes a pot. _Tambad_ keeps its making sense. _En kerad tolkemon_ has or holds a pot; _en kured tolkemon_ takes it.

_En tigad hos sedon ol dumva_ leaves the house; _en lokad hir dumva_ arrives at the house. The first expression names the place left after _hos sedon ol_; the second names the goal after _hir_. _En tigad ir dumva_ enters the house; _en tigad hos dumva_ exits it. _En antigad_ returns, without requiring a contract or pay. _En valmad zel_ crawls low along the ground; _en urzvalmad orneth_ climbs the mountain; _en urzalvad_ jumps. _En lozed us tolkemon_ lifts the pot. _En daimad nembandon hir tolkemon_ drops the pot: _hir tolkemon_ identifies the thing given a fall. Replace the pot with the actual dropped thing.

_En dokad dun thavandon nis_ whispers in a breathy, unvoiced sound. A quiet voiced murmur alone is not this conventional whispering expression. _En daimad harlen hir nur_ gives a child a name. _En membrad orhena_ expresses thanks for a kindness; a recipient is not silently included in that utterance. _En varelad dun ko_ agrees with your judgment. _En holimad kordandon hir ko_ begins a fight against you. _En vagad irthavon_ is a threat that signals my intention to cause harm; reporting an outside danger without that intention is not this threatening use.

_En aved mazeron paza mazer vima_ prefers food to drink: food is the preferred option and drink the alternative after _mazer_. _En ozimad tolkemon_ counts pots, and _en thradinad ulmer_ measures water. _Ozethi helza_ is approximately four. The listed spoken examples establish these uses of the citations; they do not supply a new person or tense paradigm.

_En sisted ir dumva_ waits in the house, and _denon tambired_ says "This happens". _En krined hir krinemon_ kneels, bending down onto the knee. _En kovrad tolkemon_ covers a pot; _en ankovrad tolkemon_ uncovers it. _Ath tolkemon ared ir alatho sedon_ puts the pot there, in the place pointed out. _Hir dumva_ means "toward the house" or "to the house" as a whole destination expression; a named place replaces _dumva_. _En tigad hir sedon thelvar_ goes up toward a high place, and _en tigad hir sedon zel_ goes down. _Ath tolkemon ared nol dumva_ places the pot beside the house; _hos dumva_ puts it outside or beyond the house's bounds.

_Teredon ol dumva ek orne_ is the distance between a house and a town: the two places after _ol_ and _ek_ are the endpoints. _En sisted hir kanel ol kolsama_ waits until the harvest; _en thernad hos kanel ol kolsama_ has lived since the harvest, with the starting event after _ol_. _En dokad ken ko lokad_ says "I speak while you come"; _ken_ introduces the simultaneous event. _En lokad tavre kanel_ comes twice, on two occasions. _En lokad zolmos kanel_ comes repeatedly; _en lokad ir ithros kanel_ comes rarely. These are cited occasion expressions, without new count or plural endings. _Ath dokandon ared nis ol kanel_ describes a short speech; _ath dokandon ared dorandar ol kanel_ a lasting one. _Urzandon ol korvath_ is the listed dawn expression, and _nembandon ol korvath_ dusk.

_En_, _ko_ and _enoi_ name I, you and we. _Ath tolkemon ol en_ is my pot; _ath tolkemon ared ol en_ says the pot is mine. _Ath tolkemon ol enoi_ is our pot. In these examples the possessive follows the possessed noun, as _meru_ follows _kothren_ in the older reconciliation formula. _En oked vethis_ says "I see myself": _vethis_ points back to the subject _en_. _Ko oked vethis_ similarly points back to _ko_, not a third person. _Zed konalon ol verezos_ includes everyone in the relevant group, and _zed konalon ol lathon_ everything under discussion.

_Eza ko lokad ken?_ asks "When do you come?"; _eza ko ared ir kethos sedon?_ asks "Where are you?". _Eza kethos ozon ol tolkemon ko kerad?_ asks how many pots you have, and _eza kethos zolmon ol ulmer ko kerad?_ how much water. _Eza ko daimad tolkemon hir kedis?_ asks to whom you give the pot; _eza ko lokad hos kethos sedon?_ asks from where you come. _Eza ko ertad denon hir kethis?_ asks for what purpose you do this. In these particular questions the opening identifies what is sought, and the remaining clause keeps its named subject and action. _Eza harlen ol kedis ared denon?_ asks whose name this is. _Eza ko dokad kethis?_ requests clarification of what you are saying.

_Denon ared varelin_ affirms "Yes, this is true"; _denon ar ared varelin_ denies it. _Denon ared varelin varlai_ asserts it emphatically as indeed or of course. _Sa dokandon ol ko, denon ared varelin_ reports "According to you, this is true", without adopting the claim as the speaker's own knowledge. _Dun denon_ adds "also", together with the preceding item or proposition. _En varelad zikai denon_ suspects tentatively that this is so; _en dokad dun ar kezdandon_ guesses without knowing. _En lendad varelon_ sets down a belief as a premise; _en alethvarelad dun ko_ holds a different judgment from yours.

_Zai ko lokad tha en tigad_ means "If you come, then I go". _Zai ko ar lokad tha en tigad_ gives "Unless you come, I go": not coming is the condition for my going. _Zai denon ar ared varelin tha en tigad_ says "Otherwise I go", with _denon_ pointing to the previous claim. _En ar kezdad zai ko lokad ves ko ar lokad_ does not know whether you come or do not come; the two complete alternatives remain open. _Ves en tigad ves ko tigad_ gives either my going or yours, while _ar en tigad ul ar ko tigad_ excludes both, and _ul en tigad ul ko tigad_ includes both. These are concrete paired clauses, without new finite forms.

_Ath tolkemon ared mazeron dadro mazer ath ombela_ says the pot is better than the chair. _Ath tolkemon ared dadro mazer zed konalon ol tolkemon_ calls it best against the whole relevant set of other pots, excluding the pot being described. The comparison includes every other relevant alternative. _En kerad solteros verdar_ describes substantial worth in the subject; _en kerad ar verdar_ no worth at all in the subject. _Verdar_ names worth beyond a price offered in trade; the older _verdo_ remains a price. _Gerpalo_ names duty; _solkimos ol gerpalo_ describes importance, weight in what is owed or at stake. _Nis ol gerpalo_ describes a trivial consequence.

_En dunad tigad_ says I am able to go; _en ar dunad tigad_ denies that ability. _En estharad tigad_ says I have permission to go. _Zikai denon tambired_ says this might happen, granting nobody permission. _En gerpalad tigad_ says I must go; _en gerpalad ar tigad_ says I must not go, the duty being to refrain. _En dadrad tigad_ says I ought to go, as advice rather than compulsion. _Ath gerpalo ared gerpalen_ calls a duty required; _ath gerpalo ared estharen ek ar gerpalen_ calls it optional, both permitted and not required. _Denon ared anestharen_ places the proposed act under prohibition. In a discussion about going, _en esthared ko_ grants you permission and _en anesthared ko_ forbids you. These individually listed modal uses distinguish ability, permission, obligation and advice, without a new mood or passive construction. The old _orhenad_ still owes the honor debt arising from an insult.

_Dadro nardelon_ greets someone by day. _Dadro lokandon_ welcomes an arriving guest. _En aved dadro kanel hir ko_ wishes you good time at parting. _En aved_ courteously requests the act understood in the exchange. _En membrad orhena_, addressed to the giver of a kindness, says thank you; _denon ared ar nezora_ answers that no repayment is owed. _En thaurad zelmira_ expresses sorrow and regret. _En aved ko anorhenad en_ asks you to forgive me, while _en aved ko linkad en_ courteously asks you to let me pass or leave room. These whole requests state both the acting person and the speaker affected; they do not introduce a general object or command paradigm.

_Ko thavrelad_, _ko silkad_, _ko sisted_ and _ko sistad_ are direct appeals to listen, look, wait and stop. _Ko lokad ir denon sedon_ calls you here; _ko tigad hos denon sedon_ sends you away; _ko zumbrad en_ asks you to help me. _Eza ko dokad antigai?_ requests a repetition. _En lambired_ and _en ar lambired_ acknowledge understanding or its absence. _Eza ko dokad denon sa kethis?_ asks how to say this; _eza kethis harlen ol denon?_ asks its name. _Eza varlai?_ asks really, including surprise or irony. These appeals and questions are whole spoken formulas, not a new imperative ending.

_Konalon ol sorthalon_ is an associated group of clans, a tribe. _Zesima ankelos_ names an inherited tradition. _Esthvelda_ is justice, fair regard for rights and due, and _anesthvelda_ their denial. _Ervon_ is the condition of being free, beyond the narrower freedom of a free city. _Hrenkon_ is property; _zolmos hrenkon_ wealth and _ithros hrenkon_ poverty. _Kerandon ol hrenkon_ is ownership, not merely holding a borrowed thing. _Keltheron lathon_ is a secret matter in general, without changing the craft secret. _Pelzandon ol enthamis_ names a transformation of form or appearance, and _tambando ol holethon_ an effect made by a cause. _Arelon_ names a being that exists; _arelos_ calls it real, _anarelos_ unreal.

## Script & Literacy

Tarveni is written in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], the Vylarian alphabet, which is the common script of [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia]] and was taken up here as everywhere else in the region—a people who spent centuries refusing the empire's rule write in the empire's letters and see no contradiction in it. Regional scribal traditions have developed distinctive features: the southern kingdoms favor rounder letterforms, while the more austere northern regions use angular, geometric styles.

The alphabet pinches in two places, and Tarveni has repaired neither. It has no letter for the glottal stop, so the catch that separates words in formal speech goes unwritten and the reader supplies it from the sense. And Vylari fixes stress by position, so the imperial letters were never given any way to mark it—where a Tarveni word takes its stress off the rule, as its acute shows in the roman, the script writes it exactly as it would a word that keeps the rule, so a pair a speaker would never confuse reaches the page as a single string of letters. A Tarvenan reads such a page aloud and decides as he goes, which is no hardship to a people who carry most of their literature in the mouth; an imperial clerk working from a Tarveni document has only the letters, and they do not tell him which of the pair is meant.

True literacy is confined to the clergy, professional scribes, and a minority of the nobility (perhaps 10-20%)—many Tarvenan lords consider reading a scribe's business, not a warrior's. Guildsmen in the coastal trading centers may possess basic functional literacy—recognizing trade marks, numerals, and simple words—but rely on scribes and solicitors for contracts and legal documents. Inland and rural populations are almost entirely non-literate. The Tarvenan cultural emphasis on poetry and epic tradition means that non-literate speakers preserve extensive oral literature through memorization and performance.

## Historical Development

Tarveni developed from Proto-Pelwar stock, but followed a distinctive evolutionary path that preserved many archaic features lost in other branches. The language shows evidence of significant contact with pre-Pelwar populations of the peninsula, particularly in the large number of "exotic" vocabulary items (words with no clear Pelwar etymology).

The political fragmentation of Tarvénia into competing kingdoms prevented the linguistic standardization seen in Vylari. Instead, Tarveni developed as a family of related dialects, each associated with a major city or kingdom. This diversity is a source of both pride (each kingdom claims its dialect is the "truest" Tarveni) and occasional difficulty in trade and diplomacy.

## Regional Dialects {#regional-dialects}

**Astúrathi Tarveni:** The speech of the mountainous interior heartland, characterized by interdental pronunciation of /z/ and strong stress patterns. Considered archaic by coastal speakers but prestigious among scholars and warriors of the High King's court.

**Lienrik Tarveni:** The dialect of the fertile central-southern lowlands, showing the most regular grammar and widest vocabulary. Tends toward clear vowel articulation and careful consonant distinction. Often treated as the closest thing to a "standard" Tarveni, though no kingdom would admit it.

**Galvari Tarveni:** The western coastal dialect, shaped by seafaring life and contact with maritime trade. Shows unique phonetic developments—vowel reduction in unstressed syllables, softened consonant clusters—and some vocabulary divergence from inland speech. Speakers of other Tarvenan dialects find this variant noticeably different.

**Valthari Tarveni:** The speech of the great southern port city, showing greater Provenzal and maritime influence than any other dialect. Lenient consonant articulation and heavy borrowing from trade languages make it the most accessible variant to outsiders, and the most common lingua franca between Tarvenan regions.

## Related Languages

Tarveni shares its deepest roots with Vylari and other Pelwar tongues, yet stands apart in many features. Speakers of Vylari find Tarveni difficult due to its phonetic complexity. Provenzal and Tarveni speakers can often understand each other in trade contexts, though the emotional and poetic registers differ markedly.

The relationship to [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] is limited; while both are Pelwar-derived, their phonological systems diverge significantly. Tarveni has no clear affinity with [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal Language]] or [[skill-varokhlng|Varokhi Language]], though scholars recognize ancient common ancestry.

## Naming Traditions

Tarvenan naming practices reflect the region's fierce independence and pride in lineage. Names are typically chosen to evoke qualities of strength, courage, or ancestral connection. Many names are compound forms, combining roots for strength, valor, or connection to specific geographic features (rivers, mountains, cities).

Patronymic traditions are strong—a person is often identified as "the son/daughter of [parent's name]" in formal contexts, and these patronymics can become hereditary surnames over generations. The use of place-names as personal identifiers is also common (_Alvaren of the Vale_, _Garveth of the Gray Highlands_).

Tarvenan women frequently retain their birth names after marriage, and in some kingdoms may choose to use a matronymic rather than patronymic. The practice of earning an epithet through deed or achievement is widespread and highly valued.

## The old mountain speech {#the-old-mountain-speech}

The speech the Pelwar found in the mountains left no inscription and no name for itself, and nobody has found its people. Its words stand in Tarveni in a shape no inherited word has. Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel, with at most one consonant closing it; `rr` stands between vowels and `ts` anywhere; and no word of it opens on `r` or holds an `f` or a `p`.

## The learned and borrowed words {#the-learned-and-borrowed-words}

A few words came back into the tongue from the empire's schools and temples, in the empire's spelling, keeping the `z` the imperial tongue made of an ancient `dh` and the hard stops it never voiced: the inherited altar is _kur_ and the learned one _kurzalis_. A handful more came up the coast from Provenzia with its songs and its silk.

## Words {#words}

### The word given

A Tarvenan owes the word he gave before he owes anything else, because it is the only thing about him another man can rely on, and the oath words are the oldest and plainest in the tongue. The contract a company signs is _semna_, the word the empire uses for any binding between two parties. A man under one is _daro_, a sworn man, and the ancient word for one under an oath is the word every Tarvenan mercenary is called by abroad.

Breaking a contract is worse than cowardice, because cowardice is a fact about one man and a broken contract is a fact about everyone who marched with him. The penalty is _kuthra_; the guilt is _damla_, and it attaches to the company's name and not only to its captain's. The oath itself is sworn on a stone older than the tongue, a _goitsa_, and that word is not Pelwar at all.

### The company and the trade of arms

Taking pay for arms carries no shame in Tarvénia, and the tongue's war words are the ones a hired company uses. _Thramo_, a legion in the empire's speech, is a company: a captain and the men who have sworn to him for a season. The captain is _thrumar_. The companion who rides beside him, _holvo_, is kin in every way that operates day to day, whatever the blood.

Most of the company's gear keeps its inherited name. The bow, _gaima_, names the crossbow every Tarvenan company is known for abroad, and the sling, _lirka_, is still the herd boy's first weapon. The return from a contract with money in hand is _velza_, the word the empire keeps for a general's triumph.

### The household

A household runs for years at a time without its men, and the word for the one who runs it, _zork_, takes no notice of whether that is a husband or a wife. The household absorbs cousins, fosterlings and the veteran of a dead brother's company, and the tongue has words for all of them. _Aipsa_, which in the empire is a legal adoption, is the taking of anyone into a household, and _sora_, those of one blood, is used with a straight face of people who share none.

### Kings, councils and the free city

A Tarvenan wants a king weak enough to be argued with, and the vocabulary of government is the empire's vocabulary with the edges filed off. The kingdom is _orzo_ and to rule is _orzad_, but the free city's council, _kurtha_, and its vote, _dil_, are the words a Tarvenan says with pride. The tax farmer, _sokedo_, is no more welcome here than a mage.

### The village and its shrine

A village keeps its shrine, its own priest who is usually a local man, and its own festival calendar, and resists any attempt to regularize any of the three. The shrine words are inherited and plain: _sarko_, a priest, _nekai_, a wayside shrine, _kur_, an altar. The learned _kurzalis_, the same word in the empire's spelling, means an altar in a great temple, and a Tarvenan uses it of the temple establishments he is cool toward.

### The old mountain speech

Before the Pelwar came into the peninsula the mountains had a speech of their own, and the Tarvenans call it nothing more than the old mountain speech. Its people left no history that anyone has found, and their words are still in Tarveni, thickest in the high valleys of [[place-asturath|Asturath]] and the marches. They hold the trilled _rr_ and the _ts_ that no inherited word has, and they never open on _r_ or carry an _f_ or a _p_.

Under the gods of the [[affiliation-arldnpnthn|Aureldian pantheon]] the mountain villages keep an older one, and every word for her is from this speech. She is _menutsa_, the Mother, and she is kept in caves, _ikorra_, where offerings, _itsarde_, are left at the mouth. Her wise-women, _tsinga_, are the village healers a Tarvenan tolerates and [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]]'s mage he does not. On certain nights the dead of a valley walk the roads in a line with a light carried before them, the _gatsa_, and a house sets an _ezkobe_, a lantern for the dead, on its threshold, _belarts_, so that they pass by.

The high country's own words are here too: the peaks and the scree, the mountain storm and the hail, the ewe, the ram, the bell on the bellwether and the shepherd who drives the flock up to the summer meadow.

### Land, the high country and the weather

The inherited land words are the lowland farmer's: field, harvest, valley, lake. The mountains have the old speech's words for their parts, and the inherited _orneth_ is a mountain only as seen from below. A pass is _gando_, the word the Áelendan kept as the name element _gan_.

### The body, life and death

A people this heavily armed has a short and exact vocabulary for wounds, and a longer one, from the old mountain speech, for what the dead do afterward. The inherited words are the ones a company's surgeon uses: _brud_, blood, _irtho_, a wound, _irthezo_, the man who closes one.

### Coin and trade

Money flows home from other people's wars, and a village of no importance can be surprisingly well supplied. The trade words are inherited and few; the moneylender, _hezmo_, lends against a contract as readily as against land.

### Craft and the forge

[[affiliation-sacredforge|The Sacred Forge]] is honored here, and the smith who makes a company's blades is a man of standing. _Thumba_ is a forge and _talbo_ steel.

### Speech, song and the learned

Tarveni is a language of assertion, and a speech, _dokme_, is said plainly or not at all. A few learned words came back from the empire's schools. Magic is _thórdima_ and a mage _thordimo_, a word said with the distaste kept for a tax farmer, _sokedo_.

The deeds of a company are recited in _oldemo_, a history, the word the empire uses for a book; in Tarvénia it is a long poem sung after supper by someone who was there. The learned _olzemo_, the same word in the empire's spelling, is a chronicle written down, and the two are not thought to be the same kind of thing.

### Towns, holdings and the road

A Tarvenan holding is _kombrelalo_, the land and the house together, run by whoever is home. The manor at its heart is _kombra_, which in the empire is a villa. The towns are smaller than Provenzia's and older, with an arch, _kambelo_, over the road in and a square, _emba_, inside it where a free city's council meets in the open.

### The coast and the sea

The [Galvari coast]{.name data-type="place/feature"} and the free port of [[place-valthari|Valthari]] look outward, and the sea words came down from the empire's fleet unchanged in sense.

### Time and the year

Every village keeps its own festival calendar, _lordo_ by _lordo_, and resists every attempt to make it agree with the next valley's.

### Qualities

The quality a Tarvenan most wants named in him is _ervo_, free.

### Doing, going and being

A verb is cited by its infinitive, and every verb closes on _-ad_, _-ed_ or _-id_.

### Numbers and grammar words

The article _ath_, one form for every gender, is the ancient "yonder" worn to its stressed syllable, the same word Provenzal wore to _at_.

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name in the scope of the Tarveni tongue—the Tarvenans, their kingdoms, free cities and marches, their orders and offices, the tongue itself—with every note where it is met. A name in the reader's tongue is listed so that the register is complete, and it is not built. A Tarveni name whose **Built from** cell shows `—` is one the lexicon does not yet make.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tarvenlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + '), '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tarvenlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Retired names

A retired name is one the setting does not write. Each stands below with the name written in its place, and no page writes it.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tarvenlng' ORDER BY position
```
