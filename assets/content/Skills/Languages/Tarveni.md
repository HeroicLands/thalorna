# terran_analog: Tarveni is patterned on Castilian in tone (hard finals kept, stressed short vowels broken to ie and ue, consonants voiced between vowels, one acute on every word of two syllables or more) over an older mountain speech shaped like Basque (the trilled rr, the ts, no f, no initial r), in the way Castilian grew up against the Basque country. Its earth mother and the dead who walk the roads at night are patterned on Mari and on the Galician night procession; their words, like every substrate word, are coined to the old speech's shape and checked against Earth word lists, Basque among them. Every inherited word is derived by the tables below from an ancient Vylari root.

Tarveni is a tongue of the Pelwar family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Tarveni, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. Tarveni is the forceful, expressive tongue of the Tarvenan kingdoms and city-states scattered across the southern peninsula. Blunt where Vylari is formal, passionate where Provenzal is artful, Tarveni reflects the directness and fierce pride of its speakers—a people who have resisted imperial rule and cultivated independent city-state cultures for centuries.

## Overview

Tarveni is a language of assertion. Speakers pride themselves on clarity and force of expression; indirectness is considered weakness or deceit. The language employs strong stress patterns, rolled consonants, and emphatic vowels that carry emotional weight even in casual conversation. A heated argument in Tarveni can sound like warfare; a tender declaration sounds like a solemn oath.

The [[skill-tarvenlng#lexicon|Tarveni Lexicon]] gives the words with their sources, including the store the tongue keeps from the older speech of the mountains.

The language varies significantly across the kingdoms and city-states of Tarvénia, yet all speakers maintain fierce loyalty to their regional variant as the "true" Tarveni. Despite these internal variations, mutual intelligibility remains high—Tarvenans from different realms can converse readily, though each is acutely aware of subtle differences in accent and vocabulary.

## How Tarveni sounds

Tarveni is blunt and hard-edged. It keeps its final consonants, trills its _rr_, and carries an older mountain speech under it.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_, _o_, _i_ and _u_ are all short and firm. _ie_ and _ue_ stand where a stressed vowel breaks, with both letters sounded.

**The consonants.** _rr_, _ts_, _z_, _k_, _d_, _b_, _g_ and _th_ are its mark. _th_ is the sound of _thin_, as in _ornetho_.

**Clusters.** _rr_, _ts_, _nd_, _rk_ and _lth_ meet inside a word. A word may close on _-k_, _-th_, _-r_ or _-s_.

**Endings.** Tarveni words end in _-o_, _-a_, _-ek_, _-ar_, _-is_ or _-eth_.

**What Tarveni leaves out.** It has no _f_, no _r_ at the start of a word, and no _kh_, _gh_ or _ph_. It has no acute except where stress moves off the guide's rule.

## Phonology

Tarveni employs a rich inventory of consonants with strong distinctions between voiced and unvoiced, and particularly emphasizes uvular fricatives and strong rhotic consonants:

**Consonants:** The language features a prominent rolled /r/ (rr represents the strongest form), a uvular fricative /kh/ that marks educated or emphatic speech, and a strong distinction between dental and alveolar stops (d/t). Initial consonant clusters are common and often pronounced with clarity (_tr-, dr-, kr-, gr-_). Word-final consonants are regularly preserved, unlike in Provenzal.

**Vowels:** Tarveni maintains five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) with less systematic length distinction than Vylari. Stress is phonemic—the placement of stress often distinguishes otherwise identical words. The language uses diphthongs sparingly and with specific meanings.

**Stress and Accent:** Stress is marked through increased loudness and vowel lengthening. The stress pattern varies lexically (not fixed by position like Vylari): some words are inherently stressed on the first syllable (RAN-zi-o), others on the final (fer-RIK). Dialect variation is most apparent in stress assignment.

**Distinctive Features:** Tarveni speakers trill their /r/ sounds, and this feature is associated with educated speech and formal contexts. The language permits complex consonant clusters at word boundaries and internal positions, making it sound more consonant-heavy than Vylari or Provenzal. A glottal stop—the catch in the middle of "uh-oh"—marks emphasis or separation between words in formal speech.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Tarveni employs a basic SVO word order but allows significant flexibility for emphasis and stylistic variation.

**Gender:** Tarveni maintains three genders (masculine, feminine, neuter). Gender assignment is largely semantic in origin but has become grammaticalized.

**Derivational Morphology:** Tarveni shows extensive use of suffix chains for derivation—a single root can be modified through multiple suffixes to create words with precise meanings. This allows economy of vocabulary and precision of expression.

## Script & Literacy

Tarveni is written in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], the Vylarian alphabet, which is the common script of [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia]] and was taken up here as everywhere else in the region—a people who spent centuries refusing the empire's rule write in the empire's letters and see no contradiction in it. Regional scribal traditions have developed distinctive features: the southern kingdoms favor rounder letterforms, while the more austere northern regions use angular, geometric styles.

The alphabet pinches in two places, and Tarveni has repaired neither. It has no letter for the glottal stop, so the catch that separates words in formal speech goes unwritten and the reader supplies it from the sense. And Vylari fixes stress by position, so the imperial letters were never given any way to mark it—where in Tarveni stress is phonemic and tells one word from another, with the result that a pair a speaker would never confuse reaches the page as a single string of letters. A Tarvenan reads such a page aloud and decides as he goes, which is no hardship to a people who carry most of their literature in the mouth; an imperial clerk obliged to work from a Tarveni document finds it maddening, and says so.

True literacy is confined to the clergy, professional scribes, and a minority of the nobility (perhaps 10-20%)—many Tarvenan lords consider reading a scribe's business, not a warrior's. Guildsmen in the coastal trading centers may possess basic functional literacy—recognizing trade marks, numerals, and simple words—but rely on scribes and solicitors for contracts and legal documents. Inland and rural populations are almost entirely non-literate. The Tarvenan cultural emphasis on poetry and epic tradition means that non-literate speakers preserve extensive oral literature through memorization and performance.

## Historical Development

Tarveni developed from Proto-Pelwar stock, but followed a distinctive evolutionary path that preserved many archaic features lost in other branches. The language shows evidence of significant contact with pre-Pelwar populations of the peninsula, particularly in the large number of "exotic" vocabulary items (words with no clear Pelwar etymology).

The political fragmentation of Tarvénia into competing kingdoms prevented the linguistic standardization seen in Vylari. Instead, Tarveni developed as a family of related dialects, each associated with a major city or kingdom. This diversity is a source of both pride (each kingdom claims its dialect is the "truest" Tarveni) and occasional difficulty in trade and diplomacy.

## Regional Dialects

**Astúrathi Tarveni:** The speech of the mountainous interior heartland, characterized by interdental pronunciation of /z/ and strong stress patterns. Considered archaic by coastal speakers but prestigious among scholars and warriors of the High King's court.

**Lienrik Tarveni:** The dialect of the fertile central-southern lowlands, showing the most regular grammar and widest vocabulary. Tends toward clear vowel articulation and careful consonant distinction. Often treated as the closest thing to a "standard" Tarveni, though no kingdom would admit it.

**Galvari Tarveni:** The western coastal dialect, shaped by seafaring life and contact with maritime trade. Shows unique phonetic developments—vowel reduction in unstressed syllables, softened consonant clusters—and some vocabulary divergence from inland speech. Speakers of other Tarvenan dialects find this variant noticeably different.

**Valthari Tarveni:** The speech of the great southern port city, showing greater Provenzal and maritime influence than any other dialect. Lenient consonant articulation and heavy borrowing from trade languages make it the most accessible variant to outsiders, and the most common lingua franca between Tarvenan regions.

## Sample Phrases

"Honrik vokath dun korath drengaken."
: "Honrik speaks with a warrior's heart." (A compliment implying authenticity and strength; _vokath_ "speaks," _dun_ "with," _korath_ "heart," _drengaken_ "warrior's," from _drengako_ "warrior.")

"Pra grandhen ek pra therrath!"
: "For honor and for the land!" (Battle cry or oath; _pra_ "for," _grandhen_ acc. of _grandh_ "honor," _ek_ "and," _therrath_ acc. of _therrá_ "land.")

"Kothren meru, ath predhen est enthráth nos."
: "My brother, peace is between us." (Formal reconciliation or alliance; _kothren_ "brother," _merro_ possessive, _predhen_ "peace," _enthráth_ "between.")

"Don est ath verath?"
: "Where is the truth?" (Philosophical question, also used ironically; _don_ "where," _verath_ "truth.")

"Ath kavren thorren dath ath vilkrath nostren gárevarren."
: "The river runs like the blood of our ancestors." (Poetic usage connecting land to people; _kavren_ "river," _thorren_ "runs," _vilkrath_ "blood," _gárevarren_ "ancestors.")

## Related Languages

Tarveni shares its deepest roots with Vylari and other Pelwar tongues, yet stands apart in many features. Speakers of Vylari find Tarveni difficult due to its phonetic complexity. Provenzal and Tarveni speakers can often understand each other in trade contexts, though the emotional and poetic registers differ markedly.

The relationship to [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] is limited; while both are Pelwar-derived, their phonological systems diverge significantly. Tarveni has no clear affinity with [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] or [[skill-varokhlng|Varokhi]], though scholars recognize ancient common ancestry.

## Naming Traditions

Tarvenan naming practices reflect the region's fierce independence and pride in lineage. Names are typically chosen to evoke qualities of strength, courage, or ancestral connection. Many names are compound forms, combining roots for strength, valor, or connection to specific geographic features (rivers, mountains, cities).

Patronymic traditions are strong—a person is often identified as "the son/daughter of [parent's name]" in formal contexts, and these patronymics can become hereditary surnames over generations. The use of place-names as personal identifiers is also common (_Alvaren of the Vale_, _Garveth of the Gray Highlands_).

Tarvenan women frequently retain their birth names after marriage, and in some kingdoms may choose to use a matronymic rather than patronymic. The practice of earning an epithet through deed or achievement is widespread and highly valued.

## Tarveni Lexicon {#lexicon}

On the road down from the passes a company is coming home. The captain is _thrumar_ and the men are sworn to him for the season, _daro_, sworn men, by the contract, _semnalis_, that every one of them can recite. In the villages they pass, a lantern stands on each threshold, because it is the season when the dead walk the roads, and every word the villagers have for that is older than the tongue the company speaks.

This is the word-hoard of [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni]], the tongue of the [[lore-tarvenanclt|Tarvenans]]. It is the daughter of ancient Vylari that kept the most of the ancestor's grammar after the empire's own speech: three genders. It kept them with help. Before the Pelwar came into the peninsula the mountains had a speech of their own, and that speech settled into Tarveni as the empire's never did, leaving its trilled _rr_, its _ts_, and a store of words for the high country, the flocks, and a goddess older than any of the Aureldian gods. This page gives every word with its source, the rules that make an ancient word Tarveni, the shape every word of the old speech keeps, and the few words taken back from the empire and from Provenzia.

## From ancient Vylari {#from-ancient-vylari}

Tarveni takes each word from its ancient form, and ten changes, in this order, make a Tarveni word of it. The plain stops `p`, `t` and `k` were voiced between vowels, to `b`, `d` and `g`; only then did the clicked stops lose their click, becoming the plain `t` and `k` that had just been emptied, so the ancient difference between a plain stop and a clicked one survives as the difference between a voiced and a voiceless one. So _sokata_ (a tax) is _sókada_, and _k'aralzon_ (the kept fire) is _karalzo_ with its `k` hard. The breathed `ph` became `h`, `dh` and `gh` lost their breath, `x` became `ks`; `w` became `v`, and `y` fell at the start of a word and became `i` elsewhere. A short `e` or `o` that took the stress in an open syllable broke into `ie` or `ue`. The ancient endings lost their `s` and their length, and every long vowel shortened. The ancient stress stayed where it was.

### Sound changes

## The old mountain speech {#the-old-mountain-speech}

The speech the Pelwar found in the mountains left no inscription and no name for itself, and nobody has found its people. Its words stand in Tarveni in a shape no inherited word has. Every syllable is a consonant and a vowel, with at most one consonant closing it; `rr` stands between vowels and `ts` anywhere; and no word of it opens on `r` or holds an `f` or a `p`.

## The learned and borrowed words {#the-learned-and-borrowed-words}

A few words came back into the tongue from the empire's schools and temples, in the empire's spelling, keeping the `z` the imperial tongue made of an ancient `dh` and the hard stops it never voiced: the inherited altar is _kurdalis_ and the learned one _kurzalis_. A handful more came up the coast from Provenzia with its songs and its silk.

## Words {#words}

### The word given

A Tarvenan owes the word he gave before he owes anything else, because it is the only thing about him another man can rely on, and the oath words are the oldest and plainest in the tongue. The contract a company signs is _semnalis_, the word the empire uses for any binding between two parties. A man under one is _daro_, a sworn man, and the ancient word for one under an oath is the word every Tarvenan mercenary is called by abroad.

Breaking a contract is worse than cowardice, because cowardice is a fact about one man and a broken contract is a fact about everyone who marched with him. The penalty is _kuthreza_; the guilt is _damolis_, and it attaches to the company's name and not only to its captain's. The oath itself is sworn on a stone older than the tongue, a _goitsara_, and that word is not Pelwar at all.

### The company and the trade of arms

Taking pay for arms carries no shame in Tarvénia, and the tongue's war words are the ones a hired company uses. _Thrámezo_, a legion in the empire's speech, is a company: a captain and the men who have sworn to him for a season. The captain is _thrumar_. The companion who rides beside him, _holvetho_, is kin in every way that operates day to day, whatever the blood.

Most of the company's gear keeps its inherited name. The bow, _gáimela_, names the crossbow every Tarvenan company is known for abroad, and the sling, _lírkeza_, is still the herd boy's first weapon. The return from a contract with money in hand is _velzora_, the word the empire keeps for a general's triumph.

### The household

A household runs for years at a time without its men, and the word for the one who runs it, _zorko_, takes no notice of whether that is a husband or a wife. The household absorbs cousins, fosterlings and the veteran of a dead brother's company, and the tongue has words for all of them. _Aipsena_, which in the empire is a legal adoption, is the taking of anyone into a household, and _sorethis_, those of one blood, is used with a straight face of people who share none.

### Kings, councils and the free city

A Tarvenan wants a king weak enough to be argued with, and the vocabulary of government is the empire's vocabulary with the edges filed off. The kingdom is _orzalo_ and to rule is _orzathen_, but the free city's council, _kurthema_, and its vote, _dilmara_, are the words a Tarvenan says with pride. The tax farmer, _sokado_, is no more welcome here than a mage.

### The village and its shrine

A village keeps its shrine, its own priest who is usually a local man, and its own festival calendar, and resists any attempt to regularize any of the three. The shrine words are inherited and plain: _sarkeno_, a priest, _nekaimo_, a wayside shrine, _kurdalis_, an altar. The learned _kurzalis_, the same word in the empire's spelling, means an altar in a great temple, and a Tarvenan uses it of the temple establishments he is cool toward.

### The old mountain speech

Before the Pelwar came into the peninsula the mountains had a speech of their own, and the Tarvenans call it nothing more than the old mountain speech. Its people left no history that anyone has found, and their words are still in Tarveni, thickest in the high valleys of Asturath and the marches. They hold the trilled _rr_ and the _ts_ that no inherited word has, and they never open on _r_ or carry an _f_ or a _p_.

Under the gods of the Aureldian pantheon the mountain villages keep an older one, and every word for her is from this speech. She is _menutsa_, the Mother, and she is kept in caves, _ikórrata_, where offerings, _itsarde_, are left at the mouth. Her wise-women, _tsingarra_, are the village healers a Tarvenan tolerates and the Arcane Order's mage he does not. On certain nights the dead of a valley walk the roads in a line with a light carried before them, the _gatsera_, and a house sets an _ezkobe_, a lantern for the dead, on its threshold, _belartsa_, so that they pass by.

The high country's own words are here too: the peaks and the scree, the mountain storm and the hail, the ewe, the ram, the bell on the bellwether and the shepherd who drives the flock up to the summer meadow.

### Land, the high country and the weather

The inherited land words are the lowland farmer's: field, harvest, valley, lake. The mountains have the old speech's words for their parts, and the inherited _ornetho_ is a mountain only as seen from below. A pass is _ganotho_, the word the Áelendan kept as the name element _gan_.

### The body, life and death

A people this heavily armed has a short and exact vocabulary for wounds, and a longer one, from the old mountain speech, for what the dead do afterward. The inherited words are the ones a company's surgeon uses: _brudamis_, blood, _írthezo_, a wound, _irthezo_, the man who closes one.

### Coin and trade

Money flows home from other people's wars, and a village of no importance can be surprisingly well supplied. The trade words are inherited and few; the moneylender, _hezumo_, lends against a contract as readily as against land.

### Craft and the forge

The Sacred Forge is honored here, and the smith who makes a company's blades is a man of standing. _Thúmbalis_ is a forge and _thalbezo_ steel.

### Speech, song and the learned

Tarveni is a language of assertion, and a speech, _dokemo_, is said plainly or not at all. The learned words keep the uvular _kh_ that marks an educated speaker, and a few came back from the empire's schools. Magic is _thórdima_ and a mage _thordimo_, a word said with the distaste kept for a tax farmer, _sokado_.

The deeds of a company are recited in _oldemo_, a history, the word the empire uses for a book; in Tarvénia it is a long poem sung after supper by someone who was there. The learned _olzemo_, the same word in the empire's spelling, is a chronicle written down, and the two are not thought to be the same kind of thing.

### Towns, holdings and the road

A Tarvenan holding is _komrelalo_, the land and the house together, run by whoever is home. The manor at its heart is _komrela_, which in the empire is a villa. The towns are smaller than Provenzia's and older, with an arch, _kambelo_, over the road in and a square, _embalis_, inside it where a free city's council meets in the open.

### The coast and the sea

The Galvari coast and the free port of Valthari look outward, and the sea words came down from the empire's fleet unchanged in sense.

### Time and the year

Every village keeps its own festival calendar, _lordemo_ by _lordemo_, and resists every attempt to make it agree with the next valley's.

### Qualities

The quality a Tarvenan most wants named in him is _ervotho_, free.

### Doing, going and being

A verb is cited by its infinitive, and every inherited verb closes on the old infinitive ending.

### Numbers and grammar words

The article _ath_, one form for every gender, is the ancient "yonder" worn to its stressed syllable, the same word Provenzal wore to _at_.
