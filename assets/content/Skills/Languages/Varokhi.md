Varokhi is a tongue of the Pelwar family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Varokhi, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Varokhi is the tongue of the tribal peoples of the [[place-vrystwald|Vrystwald]] forests and portions of western [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], spoken by the fierce and fiercely independent Varokhi clans. A language with no written form, preserved entirely through oral tradition, Varokhi stands as a testament to the power of spoken word and memory among peoples who reject the written traditions of settled kingdoms.

## Overview

Varokhi speakers define themselves against the writing cultures of the south and the formal structures of Nordmal. Where written languages are fixed and controlled, Varokhi celebrates variation, improvisation, and individual expression. A skilled Varokhi speaker is one who can adapt their speech to the moment, weave stories that captivate, and compose extemporaneous verse that honors both the past and the immediate present.

The language shares deep roots with [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] through common descent from ancient Proto-Pelwar, yet the two have diverged dramatically over centuries of independent development. Varokhi has simplified many of the complex grammatical features Nordmal preserves, while developing a richness of vocabulary for forest life, kinship relations, and the subtle distinctions of honor and obligation central to Varokhi tribal culture.

## Phonology

Varokhi carries a heavier stock of stops than Nordmal and a lighter stock of clusters. The language features significant regional variation, as oral transmission naturally admits local innovation:

**Consonants:** Varokhi keeps the stops (b, d, g, k, t) whole, alongside the fricatives (f, h, s, v), the glides (j, w) and the sonorants (l, m, n, r, y). Its habitual digraph is the dental fricative `th`, with `ch` standing in a handful of forest words. Initial consonant clusters are simpler and fewer than in Nordmal, and none runs to three sounds.

**Vowels:** Varokhi maintains six vowel positions (a, e, i, o, u, y), each with a long partner. The length distinction is phonemic and the spelling carries it, so a long vowel takes an acute. Diphthongs are few and sit inside a name rather than at either end of it.

**Stress and Rhythm:** Stress is more flexible than in Nordmal, typically falling on the first or second syllable, but poets and orators exploit stress shifts for rhythmic and emotional effect. The language employs alliterative patterning, as in Nordmal, but with greater freedom in execution.

**Distinctive Features:** Varokhi employs a system of tonal distinctions (rising, falling, level tones) on certain vowels, particularly in emphatic or emotional speech, though these are not always recognized as formal phonemes. This tonal element makes Varokhi sound melodic and expressive compared to the more monotone Nordmal.

### Openings and closings

Two inventories decide whether a name can be Varokhi at all, and both are closed. A name that opens or closes outside them belongs to some other tongue however well its middle behaves. Ordinary words are held to neither inventory: they write the same letters but may open and close more widely and run to a single beat, so _shal_ and _eth_ are Varokhi words and neither is a possible name.

**The letters are a closed set.** Varokhi writes the consonants `b c d f g h j k l m n r s t v w` and the digraphs `ch th`, and the vowels `a e i o u y`, each of the six having a long partner written `á é í ó ú ý`. It writes no `p`, no `q`, no `x` and no `z`, and it writes no `ö`. The `c` stands only where a closing carries it, and the `ch` only where an opening does.

**`w` and `c` are the letters that part the two tongues.** [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] writes neither and Varokhi writes both; Nordmal writes `ö` and Varokhi does not. The acute belongs to both tongues, so the mark settles nothing and the letters settle it. The closings part them a second time: a Varokhi name may close on `c`, `s` or `th`, a Nordmal name on `g`, `t` or `v`, and neither tongue closes a name the way the other does.

**What may open a name.** A single consonant, one of eight clusters, or a vowel with nothing in front of it.

| consonants | the openings                                 |
| ---------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| one sound  | `b` `d` `f` `g` `h` `m` `r` `s` `th` `v` `w` |
| two        | `br` `dr` `fr` `gr` `hr` `sk` `thr` `vr`     |
| none       | a vowel stands first                         |

Eight clusters open a Varokhi name where thirty-one open a Nordmal one, and no Varokhi opening runs to three sounds. That is the simplification stated above, counted: an element that opens a compound stands first in the name, so an element whose own spelling opens outside this table is an element no name can carry.

**What may close a name.** A vowel, or one consonant from a set of nine, or one of seven clusters.

| closing   | what may stand there                             |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| any name  | a vowel, or `c` `d` `k` `l` `m` `n` `r` `s` `th` |
| a cluster | `ld` `lf` `lm` `lt` `nd` `rg` `rn`               |

**The consonant band.** Varokhi carries between one and three and a half consonants for every vowel, and a name has to sit inside that band whatever class of name it is. Below the floor the name stops sounding like the forest: a name with more vowels in it than consonants belongs to a southern tongue. Every Varokhi name runs **two beats or three**, and never one or four, because a name is two elements and neither element is longer than two beats.

| class of name    | consonants per vowel | syllables  |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | ---------- |
| any Varokhi name | `1.0` to `3.5`       | `2` to `3` |

**There is no glottal stop.** No name carries an apostrophe. A vowel pair is a single diphthong spoken in one beat, and the free pairs are `ei` and `eo`, the second of them written `eó` because the long vowel is the one that carries the beat.

**A seam writes both pieces whole.** An opening and a closing that meet on the same letter write it twice, so Skathhelm and Balthhelm carry `thh` and the join swallows nothing. Confusing the join with a doubled letter inside an element is the commonest error in reading a Varokhi name apart.

**Stress is not fixed and alliteration is loose.** A name is said with its beat on the first syllable or the second, and which of the two belongs to the speaker and the region rather than to the name, so a Varokhi name cannot be said correctly on sight the way a Nordmal name can. A kindred binds its generations by repeating an element rather than by holding an onset steady, which is why one opening stands at the head of a whole run of names in the lists below.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Varokhi employs a flexible SVO word order with significant pragmatic variation. The language has largely abandoned the rigid case system of Nordmal, relying instead on word order and prepositions for grammatical relationships.

**Nominal System:** Varokhi has significantly simplified the case system, retaining only nominative, accusative, and genitive distinctions. The dual number has been abandoned; only singular and plural remain. Gender distinctions are weakly marked and often ignored in casual speech.

**Verbal System:** Verbs conjugate according to person, number, and tense (past/present/future), but the archaic strong verb forms of Nordmal have been largely regularized in Varokhi. Aspect is marked through particles rather than verb modification. The subjunctive mood is rarely used.

**Articles:** Varokhi employs free-standing articles (not enclitics as in Nordmal) that are somewhat variable in use, reflecting the language's flexibility. The distinction between definite and indefinite is present but not obligatory.

**Derivational Morphology:** Varokhi employs productive derivational suffixes to create new words from roots, allowing speakers considerable freedom to coin new terms. This makes the language highly adaptive to new situations and technologies.

## Script & Literacy

Varokhi has no traditional writing system. The language is preserved entirely through oral transmission—memorization, recitation, and storytelling. This is not considered a limitation but rather a distinctive cultural feature: the Varokhi valorize those with strong memories and eloquent speech.

In modern times, some Varokhi speakers have adopted the scripts of neighboring regions—the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Sêmarion]] from the empire, [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thurávark]] from the north—for practical communication, but these are viewed as foreign tools, not genuine Varokhi writing. The language remains fundamentally oral.

### Romanizing Varokhi

Varokhi is unwritten, so every spelling in these pages is a romanization rather than the language's own writing, and [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] § _Romanizing Nordmal_ states the table it is read off: the same table the north uses, with the row for the rounded back vowel struck out. The rule governing both is that a romanized name must be typeable, which is why the dental fricatives are written out as letters and the length mark is left as a mark an ordinary search folds away.

| sound                          | written       | never                   |
| ------------------------------ | ------------- | ----------------------- |
| the voiceless dental fricative | `th`          | a thorn                 |
| its voiced counterpart         | `d`           | an eth                  |
| the low front vowel            | `ae`          | an ash                  |
| a long vowel                   | `á é í ó ú ý` | `â ê î ô û` `ā ē ī ō ū` |

**Varokhi writes the acute for a long vowel**, exactly as Nordmal does, and writes no other mark: a circumflex or a macron on a page belongs to a southern tongue, never to the forest. What tells a Varokhi form from a Nordmal one is never the mark but the letters around it—`w` and `c` for the forest, `ö` for the north—and the letter a name closes on.

## Historical Development

Varokhi descended from Proto-Pelwar alongside [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], but the two languages diverged dramatically when the Varokhi peoples maintained a hunter-gatherer and tribal lifestyle rather than settling into kingdoms. This isolation and different cultural trajectory led Varokhi to simplify many features that Nordmal conserved while developing its own unique characteristics.

The absence of a written form may seem to place Varokhi at a disadvantage compared to standardized languages, but Varokhi speakers argue it preserves the language from the stagnation that writing imposes. Each generation of speakers continues to actively reshape and innovate within the language, preventing the kind of linguistic "fossilization" that affects written languages.

## Regional Dialects

**Central Vrystwald:** The prestige dialect spoken by the great clans of the deep forest. Most widely understood across Varokhi territories.

**Northern Vrystwald:** Spoken in the forest's northern regions, showing greater phonetic similarity to [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] and more conservative grammar. More nasal vowels and some archaic features.

**Aelwyth Mixed:** Spoken in western [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] where Varokhi speakers coexist with Élavendri peoples. Shows significant Élavendri vocabulary influence and some grammatical simplification due to bilingual contact.

**Riverside Dialect:** Spoken by Varokhi clans along major rivers, showing loanwords related to trade and boating, and more vocabulary for water-related phenomena.

## Sample Phrases

Varokhi has no writing, so almost nothing of ordinary speech reaches the page at all—a name, a clan's gloss, a handful of words a clan's dealings with outsiders have carried south. What survives whole is small:

- **"Hródar vel Fródbán."** – "Hródar, and Fródbán." (Two names joined by _vel_, the particle a longer sentence would turn on the same way.)
- **"Eth shal."** – "This, alone." (_eth_ and _shal_, the two ordinary words § _Openings and closings_ cites—neither a name, and _shal_ opening on a pair no name opens on.)

## Related Languages

Varokhi stands closest to [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], with which it shares fundamental vocabulary and much of its basic structure. Speakers with training can achieve mutual intelligibility, though the absence of shared writing systems and the differences in phonetics and grammar create barriers. The linguistic relationship is that of cousins who grew up in different households—recognizably related but with distinct personalities.

The relationship to southern [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar People]] languages (Vylari, Provenzal, Tarvéni, Élavendri) is more distant. Varokhi speakers find these languages overly formal and restrictive; southern speakers find Varokhi difficult to follow due to its phonetic variation and lack of standardization.

[[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] has had significant influence on Varokhi in borderland regions of western Aelwyth, particularly in vocabulary related to magic, nature, and spiritual matters. Some scholars hypothesize ancient contacts between proto-Varokhi and proto-Élavendri speakers that left linguistic traces.

## Naming Traditions

Varokhi naming practices emphasize the meaning and power of names. A name is considered a binding force in a person's life—a naming ceremony is a major ritual event, and the choice of name is made with great care and deliberation. Names often incorporate Varokhi language roots that evoke the qualities parents wish to cultivate: strength, wisdom, connection to nature, or martial prowess.

The practice of _eth-kethrun_ (taking a true name) marks the transition to adulthood. A young person earns their true name through a deed of significance, and thereafter this becomes their primary identifier. Some individuals hold multiple names from different periods of their life, each encoding a chapter of their biography.

Compound names are common, combining elemental roots (valor, battle, counsel, the forest) with closings that carry a sense of their own. The structure is transparent to speakers: _Vithár_ combines _vith-_ (valor) and `-ár` (the one who bears it), naming a man by what he withstands. Similarly, _Rádrún_ combines _rád-_ (counsel) and `-rún` (a secret kept), naming a woman who holds what the kindred has decided.

Clan identity is encoded in the clan name, which is used alongside the personal name. A typical full introduction would be: "I am Vithár of the Hildvith clan" or "I am Rádrún, daughter of Vithár, of Hildvith."

### How a name is built

Varokhi builds every class of name by one operation. A name is two **elements**, and an element means something on its own, so a Varokhi name states two things rather than one. Nothing in a name is empty: the piece that closes a name carries as much sense as the piece that opens it, which is the sharpest structural difference from [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], where a name-ending carries none at all.

Five rules settle the whole of it.

1. **A name is an opening element and a closing element**, and nothing formed any other way is a Varokhi name. _Vith-_ (valor) and `-rúnd` (a shield) give Vithrúnd, the clan whose shield is its valor.
2. **A linking piece may stand at the seam**, and the three are `-a-`, `-i-` and `-en-`. Each lets a closing beginning with a consonant sit against an opening that already ends in one: _wíth-_ and `-ric` give Wítharic, _edr-_ and `-gar` give Edrígar, and _eich-_ and `-grund` give Eichengrund. A linking vowel takes the acute where the name is said long.
3. **A seam writes both pieces whole.** _Skath-_ and `-helm` give Skathhelm with the `h` written twice, and no letter is dropped to tidy the join.
4. **A woman's name closes on `-a`, or on `-wyn`, `-rún` or `-ris`.** The `-a` is added to a name otherwise complete, which is why Dómric and Dómrica, Hródar and Hródara stand in the two lists as one name twice; the other three closings carry a woman's name without it, as Fródwyn, Rádrún and Theódris do. A man's name closes on a consonant and on none of those three.
5. **A clan name is built exactly as a given name is, and position tells them apart.** The second of two Varokhi names is the clan, which is why Véthar stands in a given-name list and in the clan list both. A clan name is unmarked for gender, since a clan holds men and women alike.

**A clan has more than one member.** A name in the clan list is a clan and not a person: brothers, cousins, a widow's household and three generations of a kindred all carry the same one.

**A clan name is glossed by its people.** Every gloss in the list below is plural and names people—"Eagle Helms", "Wise Slayers", "Battle Valor"—so the gloss supplies the kindred and the two elements supply what the kindred is about. A closing element may therefore name a person (`-skald`, a reciter) or a thing (`-helm`, a helm), and the gloss reads the same either way: Wulfhelm is the kindred that goes helmed as the wolf goes.

### The element lexicon

An **element** is a piece that means something and does not stand alone as a word. Every Varokhi name is elements and nothing else, so the lexicon is the whole of what a name may be made of. Each row names the names it is read from, because the lexicon is read out of the names the tradition already carries, and every row stands against the names behind it.

Several spellings may share one row. A long vowel and a short one are two spellings of the same element where the published names disagree about the length, which is why `gar-` and `gár-` stand together and why `skald-` and `skáld-` do.

### Ranks, offices and orders

An office is an element and one of the office closings, and the closing says what the office answers for. A rank is the standing a kindred or a company recognizes, and takes the same shape. An order is a company under a charge, and its name is either one name built this way or two joined by the particle `erund`.

| closing      | what it makes                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------------- |
| `-ar`, `-ár` | the one who answers for a thing |
| `-jagár`     | the one who hunts a ground      |
| `-mund`      | the one who holds a thing safe  |
| `-rád`       | the one who counsels on a thing |
| `-rith`      | the one who rides a ground      |
| `-skald`     | the one who recites a thing     |
| `-thúl`      | the one who bears a charge      |

**An office closing answers for something**—a hunt, a feud, a boundary, a judgement, a word kept—rather than naming what a member has become. A closing that marks a standing reached is not an office closing however senior that standing is: `-vith` names valor and `-helm` a helm, and both stay in the general lexicon, while `-jagár` names the one office of a hunting company and stands in the table above. The seven closings are closing elements like any other, so a name that takes one is judged the way every name is judged; what sets an office apart from a rank is never the element alone but what it is asked to answer for.

### Bynames

A byname is earned. Most are rendered in the reader's tongue: The Crow, the Weasel and the Liar are therefore not Varokhi words and are held to none of the name rules above. A byname may also be retained in Varokhi, as Thornak's **Blodtusc Bar** is. It means "bloody tusk of the Boar": _blod_ is blood, _tusc_ is a tusk, and _bar_ is the boar whose spirit he follows. The epithet is spoken as a phrase, so it is not judged as a two-element given or clan name. Position and the note's stated clan distinguish the byname from a clan name.
