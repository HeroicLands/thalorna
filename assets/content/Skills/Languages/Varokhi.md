Varokhi is a tongue of the [Pelwar Family]{.name data-type="lore"}. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Varokhi, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Varokhi is the tongue of the tribal peoples of the [[place-vrystwald|Vristwald]] forests and portions of western [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]], spoken by the fierce and fiercely independent [[lore-varokhiclt|Varokhi]] clans. A language with no written form, preserved entirely through oral tradition, Varokhi stands as a testament to the power of spoken word and memory among peoples who reject the written traditions of settled kingdoms.

## Overview

Varokhi speakers define themselves against the writing cultures of the south and the formal structures of [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]]. Where written languages are fixed and controlled, Varokhi celebrates variation, improvisation, and individual expression. A skilled Varokhi speaker is one who can adapt their speech to the moment, weave stories that captivate, and compose extemporaneous verse that honors both the past and the immediate present.

The language shares deep roots with [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] through common descent from ancient Proto-Pelwar, yet the two have diverged dramatically over centuries of independent development. Varokhi has simplified many of the complex grammatical features Nordmal preserves, while developing a richness of vocabulary for forest life, kinship relations, and the subtle distinctions of honor and obligation central to Varokhi tribal culture.

## How Varokhi sounds

Varokhi is the forest cousin of Nordmal. It is broader and slower, with _w_ and longer vowels.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_, _i_ and _u_, with _o_ rarer. One acute marks a long stressed vowel only where the stress moves.

**The consonants.** _w_, _th_, _d_, _g_, _r_, _l_, _h_, _f_ and _s_.

**Clusters.** Words open on _hr-_, _wr-_ or _dr-_ and close on _-ld_, _-nd_, _-rth_ or _-sk_.

**Endings.** Names end in _-helm_, _-win_, _-ulf_, _-und_, _-rith_, _-gar_ or _-eth_.

**What Varokhi leaves out.** It has no _hn-_ or _dv-_, which are Nordmal's, and no _kh_ outside the people's own name.

## Phonology

Varokhi carries a heavier stock of stops than Nordmal and a lighter stock of clusters. The language features significant regional variation, as oral transmission naturally admits local innovation:

**Consonants:** Varokhi keeps the stops (b, d, g, k, t) whole, alongside the fricatives (f, h, s, v), the glides (j, w) and the sonorants (l, m, n, r, y). Its habitual digraph is the dental fricative `th`, with `ch` standing in a handful of forest words. Initial consonant clusters are simpler and fewer than in Nordmal, and none runs to three sounds.

**Vowels:** Varokhi maintains five vowel positions (a, e, i, o, u), each with a long partner. The length distinction is phonemic. Diphthongs are few and sit inside a name rather than at either end of it.

**Stress and Rhythm:** Stress is more flexible than in Nordmal, typically falling on the first or second syllable, but poets and orators exploit stress shifts for rhythmic and emotional effect. The language employs alliterative patterning, as in Nordmal, but with greater freedom in execution.

**Distinctive Features:** Varokhi employs a system of tonal distinctions (rising, falling, level tones) on certain vowels, particularly in emphatic or emotional speech, though these are not always recognized as formal phonemes. This tonal element makes Varokhi sound melodic and expressive compared to the more monotone Nordmal.

### Openings and closings

Two inventories decide whether a name can be Varokhi at all, and both are closed. A name that opens or closes outside them belongs to some other tongue however well its middle behaves. Ordinary words are held to neither inventory: they write the same letters but may open and close more widely and run to a single beat, so _shal_ and _eth_ are Varokhi words and neither is a possible name.

**The closings part the two tongues.** A Varokhi name may close on `c`, `s` or `th`, a [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] name on `g`, `t` or `v`, and neither tongue closes a name the way the other does.

**What may open a name.** A single consonant, one of eight clusters, or a vowel with nothing in front of it.

```sql
SELECT consonants AS "consonants", openings AS "the openings" FROM (
    SELECT CASE WHEN edge = 'openConsonant' THEN 'one sound' WHEN max(length(replace(replace(s, 'th', 'T'), 'kh', 'K'))) = 2 THEN 'two' ELSE 'three' END AS consonants,
           string_agg('`' || s || '`', ' ') AS openings, min(position) * 10 + max(length(replace(replace(s, 'th', 'T'), 'kh', 'K'))) AS ord
    FROM (SELECT edge, position, unnest(sounds) AS s FROM name_edges
          WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND edge IN ('openConsonant', 'openCluster') AND of = 'name')
    GROUP BY edge, CASE WHEN edge = 'openCluster' THEN length(replace(replace(s, 'th', 'T'), 'kh', 'K')) END
    UNION ALL
    SELECT 'none', 'a vowel stands first', 1000 FROM name_edges
    WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND edge = 'openConsonant' AND of = 'name' AND vowel
) ORDER BY ord
```

Eight clusters open a Varokhi name where thirty-one open a Nordmal one, and no Varokhi opening runs to three sounds. That is the simplification stated above, counted: an element that opens a compound stands first in the name, so an element whose own spelling opens outside this table is an element no name can carry.

**What may close a name.** A vowel, or one consonant from a set of nine, or one of seven clusters.

```sql
SELECT CASE edge || '/' || of WHEN 'closeConsonant/name' THEN 'any name' WHEN 'closeCluster/name' THEN 'a cluster' END AS "closing",
       CASE WHEN vowel THEN 'a vowel, or ' ELSE '' END || array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' ') AS "what may stand there"
FROM name_edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND edge IN ('closeConsonant', 'closeCluster') ORDER BY position
```

**The consonant band.** Varokhi carries between one and three and a half consonants for every vowel, and a name has to sit inside that band whatever class of name it is. Below the floor the name stops sounding like the forest: a name with more vowels in it than consonants belongs to a southern tongue. Every Varokhi name runs **two beats or three**, and never one or four, because a name is two elements and neither element is longer than two beats.

```sql
SELECT CASE of WHEN 'name' THEN 'any Varokhi name' END AS "class of name",
       '`' || printf('%.1f', per_vowel_min) || '`' || ' to ' || '`' || printf('%.1f', per_vowel_max) || '`' AS "consonants per vowel",
       coalesce('`' || syllables_min || '`' || ' to ' || '`' || syllables_max || '`', '—') AS "syllables"
FROM name_shapes WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' ORDER BY position
```

**There is no glottal stop.** No name carries an apostrophe. A vowel pair is a single diphthong spoken in one beat, and the free pairs are `ei` and `eo`.

**A seam writes both pieces whole.** An opening and a closing that meet on the same letter write it twice, so Skathhelm and Balthhelm carry `thh` and the join swallows nothing. Confusing the join with a doubled letter inside an element is the commonest error in reading a Varokhi name apart.

**Stress is not fixed and alliteration is loose.** A name is said with its beat on the first syllable or the second, and which of the two belongs to the speaker and the region rather than to the name, so a Varokhi name cannot be said correctly on sight the way a Nordmal name can. A kindred binds its generations by repeating an element rather than by holding an onset steady, which is why one opening stands at the head of a whole run of names in the lists below.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Varokhi employs a flexible SVO word order with significant pragmatic variation. The language relies on word order and prepositions for grammatical relationships.

**Nominal System:** Varokhi marks only singular and plural. Gender distinctions are weakly marked and often ignored in casual speech.

**Articles:** Varokhi employs free-standing articles (not enclitics as in Nordmal) that are somewhat variable in use, reflecting the language's flexibility. The distinction between definite and indefinite is present but not obligatory.

**Derivational Morphology:** Varokhi employs productive derivational suffixes to create new words from roots, allowing speakers considerable freedom to coin new terms. This makes the language highly adaptive to new situations and technologies.

## Script & Literacy

Varokhi has no traditional writing system. The language is preserved entirely through oral transmission—memorization, recitation, and storytelling. This is not considered a limitation but rather a distinctive cultural feature: the Varokhi valorize those with strong memories and eloquent speech.

In modern times, some Varokhi speakers have adopted the scripts of neighboring regions—the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]] from the empire, [[skill-thuravarkscript|Thuravark]] from the north—for practical communication, but these are viewed as foreign tools, not genuine Varokhi writing. The language remains fundamentally oral.

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

## Historical Development

Varokhi descended from Proto-Pelwar alongside [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], but the two languages diverged dramatically when the Varokhi peoples maintained a hunter-gatherer and tribal lifestyle rather than settling into kingdoms. This isolation and different cultural trajectory led Varokhi to simplify many features that Nordmal conserved while developing its own unique characteristics.

The absence of a written form may seem to place Varokhi at a disadvantage compared to standardized languages, but Varokhi speakers argue it preserves the language from the stagnation that writing imposes. Each generation of speakers continues to actively reshape and innovate within the language, preventing the kind of linguistic "fossilization" that affects written languages.

## Regional Dialects

**Central Vristwald:** The prestige dialect spoken by the great clans of the deep forest. Most widely understood across Varokhi territories.

**Northern Vristwald:** Spoken in the forest's northern regions, showing greater phonetic similarity to [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] and more conservative grammar. More nasal vowels and some archaic features.

**Aelwyth Mixed:** Spoken in western [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] where Varokhi speakers coexist with [[lore-elavendrnppl|Élavendri peoples]]. Shows significant [[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] vocabulary influence and some grammatical simplification due to bilingual contact.

**Riverside Dialect:** Spoken by Varokhi clans along major rivers, showing loanwords related to trade and boating, and more vocabulary for water-related phenomena.

## Sample Phrases

Varokhi has no writing, so almost nothing of ordinary speech reaches the page at all—a name, a clan's gloss, a handful of words a clan's dealings with outsiders have carried south. What survives whole is small:

"Hrodar vird Frodbán."
: "Hrodar, and Frodbán." (Two names joined by _vird_, the particle a longer sentence would turn on the same way.)

"Eth shal."
: "This, alone." (_eth_ and _shal_, the two ordinary words § _Openings and closings_ cites—neither a name, and _shal_ opening on a pair no name opens on.)

## Related Languages

Varokhi stands closest to [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], with which it shares fundamental vocabulary and much of its basic structure. Speakers with training can achieve mutual intelligibility, though the absence of shared writing systems and the differences in phonetics and grammar create barriers. The linguistic relationship is that of cousins who grew up in different households—recognizably related but with distinct personalities.

The relationship to southern [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar People]] languages ([[skill-vylarilng|Vylari Language]], [[skill-provnzlng|Provenzal Language]], [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni Language]], Élavendri) is more distant. Varokhi speakers find these languages overly formal and restrictive; southern speakers find Varokhi difficult to follow due to its phonetic variation and lack of standardization.

[[skill-elvndrlng|Élavendri]] has had significant influence on Varokhi in borderland regions of western Aelwyth, particularly in vocabulary related to magic, nature, and spiritual matters. Some scholars hypothesize ancient contacts between proto-Varokhi and proto-Élavendri speakers that left linguistic traces.

## Naming Traditions

Varokhi naming practices emphasize the meaning and power of names. A name is considered a binding force in a person's life—a naming ceremony is a major ritual event, and the choice of name is made with great care and deliberation. Names often incorporate Varokhi language roots that evoke the qualities parents wish to cultivate: strength, wisdom, connection to nature, or martial prowess.

The practice of _eth-kethrun_ (taking a true name) marks the transition to adulthood. A young person earns their true name through a deed of significance, and thereafter this becomes their primary identifier. Some individuals hold multiple names from different periods of their life, each encoding a chapter of their biography.

Compound names are common, combining elemental roots (valor, battle, counsel, the forest) with closings that carry a sense of their own. The structure is transparent to speakers: _Vithár_ combines _vith-_ (valor) and `-ar` (the one who bears it), naming a man by what he withstands. Similarly, _Radrún_ combines _rád-_ (counsel) and `-rún` (a secret kept), naming a woman who holds what the kindred has decided.

A Varokh carries a clan name beside the given name, and a village beside both. The clan is the kindred and the village is the ground, and the two are never the same thing: a village holds several kindreds, and a kindred that has split holds ground in more than one village. § _A person's name in full_ below sets out how the three are joined.

### How a name is built

Varokhi builds every class of name by one operation. A name is two **elements**, and an element means something on its own, so a Varokhi name states two things rather than one. Nothing in a name is empty: the piece that closes a name carries as much sense as the piece that opens it, which is the sharpest structural difference from [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]], where a name-ending carries none at all.

Five rules settle the whole of it.

1. **A name is an opening element and a closing element**, and nothing formed any other way is a Varokhi name. _Vith-_ (valor) and `-rúnd` (a shield) give [Vithrúnd]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"}, the clan whose shield is its valor.
2. **A linking piece stands at the seam where the opening ends on a consonant and _r_ and the closing begins with a consonant, and may stand elsewhere**, and the three are `-a-`, `-i-` and `-en-`. Each lets a closing beginning with a consonant sit against an opening that already ends in one: _wíth-_ and `-rik` give Wítharik, _edr-_ and `-gar` give Edrigar, and _eich-_ and `-grund` give Eichengrund. A linking vowel takes the acute where the name is said long.
3. **A seam writes both pieces whole.** _Skath-_ and `-helm` give Skathhelm with the `h` written twice, and no letter is dropped to tidy the join.
4. **A woman's name closes on `-a`, or on `-wen`, `-rún` or `-ris`.** The `-a` is added to a name otherwise complete, which is why Domrik and Dómrika, Hrodar and Hródara stand in the two lists as one name twice; the other three closings carry a woman's name without it, as Falkwen, Radrún and Theodris do. A man's name closes on a consonant and on none of those three.
5. **A clan name is built exactly as a given name is, and position tells them apart.** The name after _yra_ in a name in full is the clan, which is why Vethar stands in a given-name list and in the clan list both. A clan name is unmarked for gender, since a clan holds men and women alike.

**A clan has more than one member.** A name in the clan list is a clan and not a person: brothers, cousins, a widow's household and three generations of a kindred all carry the same one.

**A clan name is glossed by its people.** Every gloss in the list below is plural and names people—"Eagle Helms", "Wise Slayers", "Battle Valor"—so the gloss supplies the kindred and the two elements supply what the kindred is about. A closing element may therefore name a person (`-skald`, a reciter) or a thing (`-helm`, a helm), and the gloss reads the same either way: Wulfhelm is the kindred that goes helmed as the wolf goes.

### A person's name in full

A Varokh is named in full by three names joined by two particles: the given name; then _yra_, "of the kindred", and the clan; then _wur_, "of the ground", and the village. [[being-athlwvthrnd|Athalwa yra Vithrúnd wur Eichengrund]] is Athalwa, of the Vithrúnd kindred, of the ground at [[place-eichengrnd|Eichengrund]]. Eichengrund is not a clan and Vithrúnd is not a village, so neither can stand in the other's place.

How much of the name is said depends on where the speaker stands, and each particle goes wherever the name it introduces goes.

- **In everyday speech, among people who know the person,** the given name alone: Athalwa.
- **Inside the person's own village,** where the ground goes without saying, the given name and the clan: Athalwa yra Vithrúnd.
- **Beyond the village,** at a market, before a muster, in another village's hall or in a reciter's telling, the whole name: Athalwa yra Vithrúnd wur Eichengrund.

A given name and a clan never stand side by side without _yra_ between them. An earned byname can stand in place of the clan and the village beyond a person's village, as [[being-thrnkbldtscbr|Thornak Bludtusk Barsk]]'s byname **Bludtusk Barsk** does, and the byname is said whole.

These pages name a Varokh the same way: the whole name at the first mention in a note, the given name and the clan at the first mention in the note of the person's own village, and the given name alone after that.

### The element lexicon

An **element** is a piece that means something and does not stand alone as a word. Every Varokhi name is elements and nothing else, so the lexicon is the whole of what a name may be made of.

Several spellings may share one row. A long vowel and a short one are two spellings of the same element where the published names disagree about the length, which is why `gar-` and `gar-` stand together and why `skald-` and `skald-` do.

**Elements that open a name.**

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' || coalesce(', ' || array_to_string(list_transform(variants, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '') AS "element", sense AS "what it names", array_to_string(examples, ', ') AS "read from"
FROM elements WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND element_position = 'opening' ORDER BY position
```

**Elements that close a name.** These are the pieces a reader coins with, because each carries a sense the lists settle.

```sql
SELECT string_agg('`' || form || '`', ', ' ORDER BY position) AS "element", gloss AS "what it names", array_to_string(examples, ', ') AS "read from"
FROM affixes WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng'
GROUP BY gloss, examples ORDER BY min(position)
```

### Place names

A place name is an element and a **ground-closing**, and the ground-closing says what kind of ground it is. Each is a closing element like any other, so the same piece that names a place can close a kindred's name.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "ground-closing", sense AS "what it names", array_to_string(examples, ', ') AS "read from"
FROM elements WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND kind = 'place' ORDER BY position
```

**What stands first is what the ground is held from.** Ground a kindred holds takes that kindred's own opening element, so the kindred and its country are named from one piece. Ground held from nothing but itself takes the element naming what stands on it—the oak, the falcon, the thorn. A people with no writing names the ground it walks, so a Varokhi ground name states what the ground is and never a title or a founder.

**The ground names the forest carries.**

```sql
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-varokhlng' AND kind = 'place' ORDER BY position
```

### Ranks, offices and orders

An office is an element and one of the office closings, and the closing says what the office answers for. A rank is the standing a kindred or a company recognizes, and takes the same shape. An order is a company under a charge, and its name is either one name built this way or two joined by the particle `erund`.

| closing      | what it makes                   |
| ------------ | ------------------------------- |
| `-ar`, `-ar` | the one who answers for a thing |
| `-jagár`     | the one who hunts a ground      |
| `-mund`      | the one who holds a thing safe  |
| `-rád`       | the one who counsels on a thing |
| `-rith`      | the one who rides a ground      |
| `-skald`     | the one who recites a thing     |
| `-thúl`      | the one who bears a charge      |

**An office closing answers for something**—a hunt, a feud, a boundary, a judgment, a word kept—rather than naming what a member has become. A closing that marks a standing reached is not an office closing however senior that standing is: `-vith` names valor and `-helm` a helm, and both stay in the general lexicon, while `-jagár` names the one office of a hunting company and stands in the table above. The seven closings are closing elements like any other, so a name that takes one is judged the way every name is judged; what sets an office apart from a rank is never the element alone but what it is asked to answer for.

### Bynames

A byname is earned. Most are rendered in the reader's tongue: The Crow, the Weasel and the Liar are therefore not Varokhi words and are held to none of the name rules above. A byname may also be retained in Varokhi, as Thornak's **Bludtusk Barsk** is. It means "bloody tusk of the Boar": _blud_ is blood, _tusk_ is a tusk, and _bar_ is the boar whose spirit he follows. The epithet is spoken as a phrase, so it is not judged as a two-element given or clan name. Position and the note's stated clan distinguish the byname from a clan name.

### The words the tongue keeps

These are ordinary words rather than names. Most are given whole; where a word also opens a name, the element table states that use separately. Varokhi can join ordinary words in an epithet without making it a given or clan name. The totem words below are new coinages for animals the Varokh could encounter in Vristwald or on its western shore; each row points to the totem it names.

The animal words are the spoken names of possible village totems. The eagle, falcon and wolf are named in names by the elements `arn-`, `falk-` and `wulf-`, which are pieces of names and not words; the animal words here differ from them and add no name elements. _Bar_ is attested in Thornak's epithet. The links identify the existing totem notes, not earlier attestations of these new Varokhi coinages.

Six totems have no Varokhi animal word here: [[lore-bisonttm|Bison]], [[lore-jaguarttm|Jaguar]], [[lore-leopardttm|Leopard]], [[lore-lionttm|Lion]], [[lore-parrotttm|Parrot]] and [[lore-turkeyttm|Turkey]]. None is established in Vristwald or its waters, so giving a forest village one as its totem would invent a contact the setting does not describe. Shore villages can meet sea bass, shark, tuna and whale through their fishing and trade; the inland villages need not use those words.

The reciters keep a further stock of ordinary words: the names of the things they recite, and the words for the dead, the spirits, war, craft, kin and water that the tellings turn on. Each is a root of the tongue or is built from pieces the tables above publish, and the third column says which. Where a word also serves as a name element, the element tables state that use separately.

The particle `vird` is a word and no element is spelled the same way, so a sentence never carries an element on its own and a name is never built from a kept word.
