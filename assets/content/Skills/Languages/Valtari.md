Valtari is a tongue of the Pidgin (contact) family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Valtari, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Valtari—also called **Váltongue** or simply "trade-talk"—is not a true native language but a **pidgin trade language** that arose organically in the maritime harbors, fish-markets, warehouses, and trading houses of Mídhalión. It emerged wherever speakers of Taruzeli, Helonic, Vylari, Nordmal, and a dozen other tongues needed to conduct commerce without interpreters. The language is fundamentally a **contact language**: a tool forged in necessity, spoken by those who need each other more than they need mutual understanding of grammar or etymology.

Valtari is the lingua franca of maritime merchants, dock laborers, ship captains, chandlers, harbor masters, fishmongers, and anyone whose livelihood depends on cross-cultural commerce. It is **never** a first language—no child grows up speaking Valtari as their mother tongue. Instead, it is picked up on the docks through constant repetition, error correction, and pragmatic negotiation. A Valtari speaker is identifiable not by mastery of the language but by their ability to make themselves understood despite imperfect knowledge.

The language is characterized by **heavy Taruzeli and Helonic substrate** (the two dominant maritime cultures), simplified grammar, extensive borrowing, and a vocabulary ruthlessly pruned to the essentials of commerce and daily port life. It is rarely written; when it is, speakers borrow whatever script they know—Taruzeli script, Helonic letters, or hastily invented symbols.

Valtari takes its name from the port of [[place-valthari|Valthari]], the free city whose quays gave the pidgin its first fixed form.

## How Valtari sounds

Valtari is the harbour pidgin of the inner sea. Every sound in it is one any sailor can say.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_, _i_, _o_ and _u_, with one vowel to a syllable.

**The consonants.** Plain consonants only: _b_, _d_, _g_, _k_, _l_, _m_, _n_, _p_, _r_, _s_, _t_, _v_ and _z_, and _sh_.

**Clusters.** None. Syllables are a consonant and a vowel.

**Endings.** Things end in _-u_. People end in _-or_, _-ela_ or _-eni_. Places end in _-an_.

**What Valtari leaves out.** It has no _th_, _kh_, _gh_ or _zh_, no apostrophe and no mark.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Valtari simplifies the consonant inventories of its parent languages, favoring sounds that are easy for speakers of multiple tongues to produce:

Voiceless stops
: P, T, K (unaspirated, gentle articulation)

Voiced stops
: B, D, G

Fricatives
: F, V, S, Z, SH

Nasals
: M, N

Liquids
: R (flapped or rolled depending on speaker origin), L

Glides
: W, Y

Valtari has no H and none of the throat sounds of its parents: the Taruzeli and Helonic _kh_ and _gh_ are said _k_ and _g_, _q_ is said _k_, and _th_ is said _t_. Consonant clusters are rare and usually occur where two words meet. Most syllables are open (ending in a vowel), which simplifies pronunciation across language backgrounds, and a word closes on a vowel or on _n_, _l_ or _r_.

### Vowels

Five basic vowels, said short, with some variation in pronunciation depending on speaker origin. The long vowels of Taruzeli and Helonic are all said short in Valtari:

A
: an open "ah", variable (flattening toward the "a" of "about" when unstressed)

E
: as in "bet" (flattening toward the "a" of "about" in rapid speech)

I
: as in "machine" (shortening toward the "i" of "bit")

O
: as in "note" (flattening toward the "a" of "about")

U
: as in "boot"

Vowels are often **reduced in unstressed syllables**, especially in the speech of hurried traders. A word like _telusu_ might be pronounced _tlmsu_ in rapid dock speech. Non-native speakers often simplify vowels further, contributing to the language's fluidity and mutability.

### Stress and Intonation

Stress is **unpredictable** and varies by speaker origin. Taruzeli speakers stress the first syllable; Helonic speakers stress toward the end. This makes Valtari speech rhythmically irregular, contributing to its "unfinished" quality. Rising intonation at the end of statements is common, turning assertions into questions—a feature borrowed from Helonic.

## Grammar Notes

### Radical Simplification

Valtari has **almost no inflectional morphology**. Nouns do not decline for case, number, or gender. Verbs do not conjugate for tense, mood, or person—these meanings are expressed lexically (through separate words) or contextually (through shared understanding).

### Sentence Structure

The dominant word order is **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)**, inherited from Taruzeli influence. However, flexibility is the norm. Word order variation often reflects semantic emphasis or speaker native language interference:

_Kiromu kusadu idiro_
: "The captain brings water" (standard SVO; _kusadu_ from Taruzeli _kusad-_ "carry")

_Idiro kusadu kiromu_
: "The captain brings the water" (topical rearrangement, emphasizing the water)

### Tense

Valtari has no tense system. **Temporal meaning is supplied by context or optional adverbs:**

_Kiromu denu_
: "The captain is here" / "The captain was here" (context determines meaning)

_Kiromu lokatu tapelu_
: "The captain is coming" (adverb _tapelu_ "now" clarifies immediacy)

_Olimu kiromu begi_
: "The captain has already left" (adverb _olimu_ "already" indicates completion)

This is one of the most striking features of the language: speakers must learn to infer time from context, much as they do in a standard trade negotiation.

### Plurals

Plurality is **optional and often unmarked**:

_Yezu yakodu lokatu_
: "One boat comes"

_Pulumu yakodu lokatu_
: "Many boats come" (or _yakodu-yakodu_, depending on speaker)

_Yakodu-yakodu lokatu_
: emphatic plural (reduplication)

Number is usually clear from context or explicit quantity markers (_fomul yakodu_ "five boats", _pulumu pelaro_ "many sailors").

### Negation

Negation is expressed through the **particle _zu_, placed before the verb or predicate**; speakers of Helonic say _ufi_ in the same place:

_Zu kusadu_
: "Do not bring" / "He does not bring" (context clarifies)

_Kiromu zu denu_
: "The captain is not here"

_Zu latara_
: "That is not good" / "Not good" (standalone; _latara_ from Taruzeli _lataran_ "fair")

### Questions

Questions are formed through **rising intonation and optional question words** (_mavu_ "what", _mavitu_ "where", _kedi_ "who", _gulodu_ "when"):

_Mavitu kiromu?_
: "Where is the captain?"

_Mavu kusadu?_
: "What are you bringing?"

_Kiromu denu?_
: "Is the captain here?" (intonation conveys question)

## Script and Literacy

Valtari is **rarely written**. When it is, speakers employ:

[[skill-merazanscrpt|Merazan]] letters
: Common in southern ports and Taruzeli-dominated cities

[[skill-helnkscrpt|Helónika]] letters
: Used in northern ports and Helonic trading centers

[[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]] letters
: Used in Vylarian and Aureldian harbors, and by any factor with an imperial education

Improvisational symbols
: Abbreviated marks and hastily invented notation for quick transaction records

There is **no standardized Valtari orthography**. A word like _kiromu_ might be written as _kiromu_, _kyromu_, _chiromu_, or _kiromou_ depending on the scribe's native language and knowledge. This orthographic chaos reflects Valtari's nature: a language of the moment, not preserved in books.

Literacy in Valtari is minimal. Most dock workers cannot read or write the language; communication is **oral and gestural**. Even merchants, who may keep written records, often rely on translators or script-speakers from their native communities to handle documentation.

## Historical Development

Valtari crystallized over centuries of maritime trade, reaching its modern form roughly 300 years ago as trade intensified. The language did not _replace_ native languages but rather _supplemented_ them—a lingua franca for the docks, not a language of home, family, or identity.

The vocabulary is **45% Taruzeli, 25% Helonic, 20% from the Vylari family, 7% Nordmal and 3% from other tongues of the setting**, every word drawn from the setting's own tongues. This breakdown varies regionally: southern ports lean more Taruzeli; northern ports, more Helonic.

Historical layers are detectable in the vocabulary:

- **Oldest layer** (100+ years old): Core trade terms, numerals, basic commands—very stable
- **Middle layer** (50-100 years): Names of new goods, evolving social roles, borrowings from expanding trade networks
- **Newest layer** (0-50 years): Modern commercial terminology, quick neologisms, ephemeral slang

The language is **constantly evolving**, with new words entering and exiting use as trade patterns shift and new goods appear in the markets.

## Regional Dialects

Valtari varies noticeably by port:

- **Ilovanu dialect**: The prestige form; favors clarity and slower speech. Influenced by the mixed Taruzeli-Kaliharan population. Closest to a "standard."
- **Vashurán dialect**: Fast, clipped, heavily Taruzeli. Difficult for non-native Taruzeli speakers to follow; prized for its efficiency.
- **Northern port dialects** (Velanthian coast): Softer, more Helonic-influenced. Slower, more vowel-heavy.
- **Other Kaliharan ports** (the island's eastern coast): Mix of Kalihari cadence with Valtari vocabulary; unique tonal patterns.

A trader from Ilovanu moving to Vashurán must re-learn the rapid-fire delivery and emphatic stress patterns. Similarly, a Nordmal merchant's Valtari will carry Nordmal phonemes and word order habits.

## Sample Phrases

_Kiromu kusadu latara pashin_
: "The captain offers a fair price" (Helonic _kiromu_ "master"; Taruzeli _kusadu_ "carry", _latara_ "fair" and _pashin_ from _pashinu_ "fair price")

_Zu funatu, asevu, mavitu yakodu?_
: "No trouble, friend—where is the ship?" (casual, reassuring)

_Idiro, vidu, durasu
: yepalu tapelu_—"We need water, bread, and salt immediately" (urgent trade request)

_Paramisu anevo lokatu, yakodu zu sotaru_

_Koru latara pelaro, temadu kerisu, geniru yu rusaku?_
: "You're a good sailor with a strong hand—will you take this work?" (recruitment)

## Related Languages

Valtari is **fundamentally a mixed language**, not genealogically related to any single parent. It borrows extensively from:

Taruzeli
: Grammar skeleton, 45% of vocabulary

Helonic
: Phonological innovations, 25% of vocabulary, some syntactic patterns

The Vylari family
: 20% of vocabulary, above all the pronouns, the questions and the words of time

Nordmal and its kin
: 7% of vocabulary, grain, the north and the day

Other tongues of the setting
: 3%, Sinale words for fish and for the questions

Valtari has **no descendants**—it does not natively reproduce itself. Children of two Valtari speakers revert to their parents' native languages or, in mixed families, learn whatever language the dominant parent or community uses. Valtari is a language of **individual transaction**, not of community continuity.

## Naming Traditions

### Valtari Names as Trade Names

Valtari names are not birth names but rather **trade names**—professional identities adopted for commerce. A person operating in the maritime world may carry two names:

1. **Birth name** (in native language): Used at home, among family, in religious contexts
2. **Valtari trade name**: Used on the docks, in business records, among colleagues

In cosmopolitan ports like Ilovanu and Vashurán, the trade name often becomes the name a person is known by to the outside world. Family and intimate community preserve the birth name; everyone else knows the trader by their Valtari name.

### Structure and Conventions

Valtari trade names follow **simple, memorable patterns** that function across multiple languages:

**Given Name + Occupational or Geographic Epithet**

_Kiromu_ (the word for "captain", from Helonic)
: common given name for merchants and masters

_Odisor_ (from _odisu_, "road")
: for caravan masters or widely-traveled traders

_Ushador_ (from _ushadu_, "coin")
: given name, often carried by moneychangers and factors

A given trade name is a word of the quay with a name ending in place of its closing vowel: _-or_ for a man, _-ela_ or the softer _-eni_ for a woman, and _-an_ for anyone.

**Clan or Trade Name**

A house, a crew or a trading firm takes the ending _-ari_:

_Yakodari_ (from _yakodu_, "boat")
: a family associated with small merchant vessels

_Kubasari_ (from _kubasu_, "count")
: commodity traders and counting-houses

_Zazurari_ (from _zazuru_, "cargo")
: a crew or family that lives by carrying other people's goods

Many traders eschew clan names entirely in Valtari, instead adopting **occupational epithets**:

_Ushador bazunu-kerisu_
: "Ushador the sail-hand" (a sailmaker)

_Odisor norodu-odisu_
: "Odisor of the north road" (a caravan master)

_Kusadela ula-dorasu_
: "Kusadela the fish-dealer" (a fishmonger)

Names are **fluid**. A trader who makes a fortune might take a new name. A person who changes trades or relocates might adopt a new Valtari name reflecting their new role. Unlike birth names in native cultures, Valtari trade names are **practical identities**, not sacred or ancestral.

### Gender in Names

Valtari names show **weak gender marking**. Many names are used by either gender; some have masculine and feminine variants (e.g., Ushador / Ushadela), and names closing on _-an_ belong to neither, but these distinctions are not rigidly enforced. A woman captain might insist on the masculine form if it carries prestige; a man in a traditionally female trade might use a feminine variant.

## Valtari Lexicon {#lexicon}

On any quay from [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] to [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]], a ship's captain is a _kiromu_, the cargo is _zazuru_, and the price is a _pashin_. None of the three is a word of one people, and the tongue itself is named for the port of Valthari. _Kiromu_ is the Helonic _kiros_ worn down on the docks, and _zazuru_ and _pashin_ are the Taruzeli _zazurush_ and _pashinu_ with their endings dropped. [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]], the harbor pidgin, is built this way throughout: a word of [[skill-taruzelilng|Taruzeli]] or [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]], stripped of the endings that carry its grammar and of the sounds a stranger cannot make, and set down in a sentence that has no grammar to give it. This page is the word-hoard of that pidgin: the core words every port understands, where each came from, the sound rules a borrowed word is bent to, and how a trade name is made.

A Valtari word does not change. _Lokatu_ is to come, to go and to arrive, at any time and for any speaker, and _yakodu_ is one boat or many. What the parent tongues say with endings, the pidgin says with a separate word or leaves to the context: _tapelu_, now, and _olimu_, already, are the nearest it has to tenses, and a plural is the word said twice, _yakodu-yakodu_.

The sound of Valtari is plain and open. It keeps only the sounds that speakers of every tongue on the quay can make, so the rasps, the throat sounds and the long vowels of its parents are gone, and nearly every word closes on a vowel.

## Building words {#building-words}

**A borrowed word** is bent to the sounds above. Its long vowels are said short. Taruzeli and Helonic _kh_ and _gh_ become _k_ and _g_, _q_ becomes _k_, _th_ and _dh_ become _t_ and _d_, _j_ becomes _y_, the rounded _ü_ becomes _i_, and _h_ is not said at all. The endings that carry the parent tongue's grammar fall away, and a word left closing on a consonant other than _n_, _l_ or _r_ takes a vowel after it, most often _-u_ in a Taruzeli word and _-o_ in a Helonic one: the Taruzeli _jakudu_, a ship, is _yakodu_, and the Helonic _ánevir_, the wind, is _anevo_. A word in daily use is often clipped further, and the words of the Vylari family, of Nordmal and of the setting's other tongues are bent the same way.

**Two words side by side** make a compound phrase, the second naming what the first is: _bazunu-kerisu_ is a sail-hand, a sailmaker. **A word said twice** is a plural, or an emphasis.

**A trade name** is a word of the quay with one of the endings below. The ending takes the place of the word's closing vowel.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-valtarlng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Words {#words}

### Asking, time and number

The question words share the opening _se-_ of _mavu_, what: _mavitu_ is where, the what-place. A Taruzeli speaker says not with _zu_ and a Helonic speaker with _ufi_, each keeping the negative of his own tongue, and every docker understands both. The numbers past five are counted on: six is _fomul yezu_, five-one, and nine is _fomul vubu_.

## Making names {#making-names}

**A trade name** is not a birth name. A trader carries the name of his own people at home and a Valtari name on the docks, and the [[skill-valtarlng|language note]] describes how the two are kept apart. The trade name is a word of the quay with a name ending: _Ushador_ is coin with the man's ending, _Ushadela_ the same word with the woman's, and _Bazunan_, sail with the ending for anyone, is carried by men and women alike. The word is chosen for the trade or for the luck it promises, so a sailmaker is often a _Bazunor_ and a moneychanger an _Ushador_. A word that closes on a consonant takes the ending after it: _pashin_, price, gives _Pashinela_.

**An epithet** sets a second pair of words after the trade name, naming the work: _Ushador bazunu-kerisu_ is Ushador the sail-hand, and _Kusadela ula-dorasu_ is Kusadela the fish-dealer.

**A house, a crew or a trading firm** takes the ending _-ari_: _Yakodari_, the ship-people, is a name for a crew, and _Kubasari_, the reckoners, for a counting-house.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of the harbor pidgin's own notes. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-valtarlng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 0 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives the tongue and its pages, with the note where it is met. A name the tongue uses shows `—` when it is not built from this page, and a name in the reader's tongue is tagged as such.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning || coalesce(CASE tag WHEN 'valtari' THEN ', [[' || tongue_language || '|Valtari]]' END, '') AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-valtarlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + '), '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-valtarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Retired names {#retired-names}

A retired name is one the setting does not write: a word or a name of an Earth language. Each stands below with the word written in its place, and no page writes it, however accented. A `terran_analog` comment names the Earth source on purpose and is not read.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-valtarlng' ORDER BY position
```
