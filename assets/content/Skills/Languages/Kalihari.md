Kalihari is a tongue of the Kalihari (ancient isolate) family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Kalihari, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Kalihari is among the oldest living languages on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]], predating all Ankarian human languages by millennia, and it is spoken today by the [[lore-kaliharanclt|Kaliharan]] peoples of [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]]. The language stands in complete **linguistic isolation**—no known relatives exist anywhere on Thalorna, and no philologist off the island can account for it. The likeliest explanation is also the dullest: a single people on a single landmass, out of contact with every other, speaking for eight thousand years whatever their ancestors happened to speak. Isolation of that length does not need a stranger to explain it.

To the untrained ear, Kalihari sounds exotic and flowing: vowel-heavy, with liquid consonants that cascade like water. The language rarely employs harsh stops or heavy consonant clusters. Instead, it privileges open syllables, gliding transitions, and a musical quality unlike anything heard on the Ankarian mainland. This flowing character reflects both the coastal geography of Kalihara and a philosophical worldview emphasizing harmony and interconnection.

Kalihari exists in two forms:

Interior Kalihari
: The pure, classical form spoken in the island's interior, known to outsiders only in fragments

Port Kalihari
: A hybrid variant used in cosmopolitan harbor cities, incorporating [[skill-valtarlng|Valtari]] loanwords and simplified grammar to facilitate trade

Outsiders rarely encounter true Interior Kalihari; most know only the bastardized port variant.

## How Kalihari sounds

Kalihari is liquid island speech, among the oldest tongues of the setting.

**The vowels.** _a_, _i_, _o_ and _u_, and the pairs _ai_, _ei_ and _ao_. A doubled _aa_ is sounded long, as in _-kaan_.

**The consonants.** _th_, _r_, _n_, _l_, _v_, _k_, _s_ and _m_. The apostrophe joins two words, as in _sari'kaan_.

**Clusters.** None but _th_.

**Endings.** Kalihari words end in _-ei_, _-anu_, _-ao_, _-ari_, _-oa_ or _-kaan_.

**What Kalihari leaves out.** It has no word of more than four syllables, and no more than one apostrophe.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Kalihari consonantal inventory is notably small and gentle:

- **Liquids**: L (flapped or trilled), sometimes R (borrowed or varied)—the most frequent consonants
- **Nasals**: M, N, NG (velar nasal)
- **Fricatives**: V (or sometimes W), TH (soft dental)
- **Glides**: W, Y
- **Hard stops** (rare): K (soft articulation), T (soft), P (soft)
- **Glottal stop** ('): Marks syllable boundaries in formal or poetic speech

The language **avoids consonant clusters** almost entirely. Consonants are always separated by vowels, creating the characteristic flowing, syllable-heavy quality. A word like "mantle" would not exist in Kalihari; instead, a Kaliharan would say something like "ma-ni-le" (three syllables).

### Vowels

Five distinct vowels, each pronounced with clarity and sometimes elongated for emphasis:

- **A** (open, father-like)—bright, warm
- **E** (mid, front)—gentle, clear
- **I** (high, front)—bright, close
- **O** (mid, back)—rounded, warm
- **U** (high, back)—rounded, distant

**Diphthongs** are common and meaningful. _ai_, _ei_ and _ao_ are one syllable each; other vowels side by side are said apart. A word like "ithavao" contains three diphthongs (i-tha, va-o) distributed across its syllables.

### Stress and Prosody

Stress typically falls on the **penultimate (second-to-last) syllable**, creating a regular rhythmic pattern. The language employs **tonal variation** more subtly than [[skill-kicheklng|Ki'ichek]]: rising tones at phrase endings signal questions or openness; falling tones close statements. Poetic and ceremonial speech employ exaggerated tonal variation that approaches singing.

Kalihari is often described as "musical" because pitch and rhythm are fundamental to comprehension, not mere inflection.

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

Kalihari employs **Verb-Subject-Object (VSO)** word order, unusual among world languages but reflecting a philosophy where action comes first, then the actor, then the acted-upon:

_Ithavao Kulanei marivoel_
: "Flows Kulanei the warm stone" (poetic: "Kulanei's warmth flows like stone")

This word order allows the verbal action to establish context before introducing participants—a stylistic choice reflected in Kaliharan storytelling traditions.

### Verbs

Verbs do not change. Temporal reference comes from context or optional adverbs.

Example: _Kuethori rithan_ = "The swift current flows"

### Nouns and Possession

Nouns take no endings. Plurality is optional and often understood from context:

- _Manei_ = "person" or "people" (singular and plural forms are identical)
- _Ithavanu koamanu_ = "Still water deep root" (two concepts in relationship)

**Possession** is marked through word order and optional particles:

- _Ithavanu reiluvao_ = "Still water's old vine" (possessive relationship implied by juxtaposition)
- _Reiluvao ia ithavanu_ = "Old vine of still water" (explicit possessive particle _ia_)

### Adjectives and Modifiers

Adjectives follow nouns, creating a natural flow:

- _Koamanu thuanei_ = "Deep root swift"
- _Ithavanu koruvei_ = "Still water clear"

Adverbs precede the verbs they modify:

- _Thuanei rithan_ = "Swiftly flows"

## Everyday speech

_Silanu ni vi_ means “I see you”; _silanu vi ni_ means “you see me”. The verb
comes first, then its actor and object. _Laruei silanu ni vi_ adds “now” before
the verb. The verbs retain the invariant forms the tongue already uses.
_Vaei ni selavei_ means “I am healthy”; _varurei ni selavei_, “I become healthy”.
The resulting quality follows the subject. _Vaei selua_ is the complete
existential “a house exists”.

_Vaarei ni vanarel_ means “I have a cup”. _Vanarel ia ni_, _vanarel ia vi_ and
_vanarel ia narei_ are “my cup”, “your cup” and “our cup”, using the existing
possession particle. _Vaei vanarel ia ni_ means “the cup belongs to me”. The
same explicit relation joins kin in _mava ia mava_, “a parent of a parent”, and
_mavua ia mavalo_, “a sibling's child”. _Manei varai maruvei_ and _manei marai
maruvei_ explicitly name an adult man and woman. _Mavua varai_ is son even when
grown; _mavua varai nu maruvei_ is a boy who is not yet adult. The corresponding
female phrases distinguish daughter and girl. The old household names, lineage
rules and custodial ranks retain their own uses.

_Vaarei ni nelua_ means “I feel joy”, literally holding joy. _Nelarei_,
_melaroa_ and _nualei_ give happiness, pleasure and contentment in that feeling
slot. _Morali puranei_ is the felt fulfillment of desire. _Vanirao_ is bodily
sensation, distinct from emotion, and _virei_ is confidence in one's ability,
while _varalo_ is subjective certainty and _varaelo_ truth. A certain belief
can still be false. _Narelia_ has the contextual senses of compassion with a
wish to relieve suffering, pity at misfortune, and sympathy sharing another's
feeling, including joy. _Nularue_ is contempt, a judgment beneath regard.

_Nelari a maorei_, _nelari a tavarei_ and _nelari a mavarei_ distinguish
romantic, friendship and kin love. _Nelari a manei lo sen savalo_ extends concern
to all people without seeking a return, including outsiders; it does not simply
rename tending one's assigned part of the Whole. _Navuroa_ is sexual desire.
_Vaei ni ke tavarei_ means “I share company with a companion”, including company
without close friendship. _Vaarei ni varelia sen siloari_ describes committed
belief without proof; it does not redefine the observed balance as a new rite.
_Rualei_ describes humility and _soranei_ reverence in their actual adjective
uses, as in _vaei ni soranei a mi_, “I am reverent toward that person”.

_Vaarei mi mulanu larei_ says that the person or thing has a pleasant scent;
_mulanu nurai_ gives an unpleasant scent. _Irei niruve ni_ means “I inhale”;
_tharei niruve ni_, “I exhale”. The directional adverb precedes the breathing
verb. _Vanore ni_ means “I sweat”, producing perspiration on the skin.
_Nu tunarei ni niruve_ describes an inability to breathe when the airway
is blocked or breathable air absent. _Vaei ni minurela_ describes being full
after eating or drinking. _Sen silareli_ is physical blindness, and _sen nerueli_
physical deafness, rather than merely not looking or listening. _Tavirua_
describes impaired walking. _Siruei_ is conscious; _nu siruei_ unconscious,
rather than merely asleep. _Kalarua ke sunaroa_ describes fever as heat with illness.

_Merarei ni vanarel a vi_ means “I give a cup to you”. _Tarurei ni vanarel a
vi_ means “I bring a cup to you”, carrying it toward you as the reference point.
_Tararei ni vanarel ir selua_ means “I put a cup inside the house”. _Tharurei ni
tha selua_ means “I leave the house”; _taruvei ni a selua_, “I arrive at the
house”. _Valovei ni vanarel_ is “I drop a cup”. _Sorarei ni ke naruei narua_
is “I shout”, speaking with a loud voice. _Sorarei ni ke naruei nirei niraloa_
is “I whisper”, using a quiet breathy unvoiced voice. _Sararai_ signals an
intention or agency to cause harm, rather than merely warning of danger.
_Selurei_ heals a body or repairs an ordinary object; it does not claim an
ability to mend the living precursor substance.

_Vaarei ni vanarel unao_ means “I have two cups”, with no noun plural ending.
_Vaarei ni vanarel raorei serei vanarel ia vi_ compares my count of cups to
yours. _Vaarei ni vanuei raorei serei vanuei ia vi_ instead compares amounts
of water. _Raorei serei seraroa nuruei lo_ is more than all other compared
amounts, hence most; _rirei_ gives least. Replace _seraroa_ with _seraloa_ for
compared counts. The explicit standard with _larelo_ or _nurelo_ gives best and
worst. _Unai seruao unao_ selects one from the two; _nurei seruao unao_, none
from the two. _Unaruei_ and _unaruo_ are equal halves and quarters.

_Vaei ni ir serarei unarua_ means “I am in position seven”; _vaei ni ir serarei
unarei_, “I am in position eight”. The actual number labels the position rather
than counting several positions. _Serarei_ also names a body's spatial position.
_Naruvei naruvoa unoro_ means “near but below ten”, the scalar “almost ten”.
_Sarue unoro_ is about ten; _sorue unoro_, exactly ten. _Veirua nival_ is tiny
and _veirua kaorei_ huge in absolute physical size. Spatial length _ralorei_
differs from long duration _loravei_.

_Leruei vanurai turei ni_ means “I come before the rain”; _lerua vanurai_
supplies after the rain. _Larue minaloa minu ni_ means “I eat during the meal”,
with the actual meal occasion also expressing while eating. _Laruarei vanurai_
continues until the rain; _laruaro vanurai_ begins at the rain and continues
through the reference time. Replace the named event or time in these slots.
_Ir loria unai_, _ir loria unao_ and _ir loria luei_ count one, two and many
occasions. _Ir loria arai nu turei mi_ says that the person never comes in the
relevant period: there is no relevant occasion on which they come. Its temporal phrase
precedes the negated verb; the separate _sen loria_ only means “without an
occasion”. _Lararei_ means again. _Laravei turei mi_ is “that person already
comes”; _varao turei mi_, “that person still comes”. _Lararua nu turei mi_ is
the current-context “that person has not come yet”. These time words precede
the verb and do not create a tense ending.

_Silanu norai vi_ asks “who sees you?”; _silanu vi norai_ asks “whom do you
see?” _Vaei norui larei_ selects “which is good?” from the stated set.
_Nu vaei mi ir serana arai_ says that the person or thing is nowhere in the
relevant domain: there is no relevant place where it is located. The literal
phrase _sen serana_ only says “without a place”.
_Vaei vi ir norava_ asks where you are; _ir noravo turei vi_ asks when you come.
_Taarei vi mirei norue_ asks the reason for going; _taarei vi tore norue_ asks
its intended purpose. _Noraru toravei vi_ asks how you do it. _Vaei seraloa ia
vanarel norue_ asks how many cups; _vaei seraroa ia vanuei norue_ asks how much
water. _Vaei vanarel sai ia norai_ asks whose cup this is. _Merarei vi vanarel
a norai_ asks to whom you give a cup; _turei vi tha norava_ asks from where you
come. _Turei vi_ with the documented rising phrase-final tone asks “do you
come?”; the same words with statement intonation say that you come. _Sarei vi
norue_ requests clarification. _Silanu ni nia_ means “I see myself”; _silanu
mi nia_, “that person sees themself”. _Nia_ refers to its own clause's subject.

_Varae vaei mi selavei_ means “that person is truly healthy”, asserting reality.
_Saravua turei mi_ means “supposedly that person comes”, a reported claim without
endorsement. _Varaloa_ gives assurance, _virarei_ possibility, and _virarua_
probability. _Sarunao_ specifically involves intent to deceive; _saruro_ also
includes an honestly mistaken false assertion. _Sorarua_ is dishonesty, which
differs from an incorrect answer given sincerely.

_Nire irenei vaei vi nirelo taarei ni_ means “if you are here, then I go”.
_Nunire irenei vaei vi nirelo taarei ni_ means “unless you are here, then I go”:
if you are not here, I go. _Turei vi nirela taarei ni_ means “you come,
otherwise I go”, making my going conditional on your not coming. _Tarele vaei
mi torovei, turei mi_ means “although that person is tired, they come”.
_Noelo turei mi noei taarei mi_ offers either coming or going; _nunoelo turei
mi nunoelo taarei mi_ excludes both. _Naelo turei mi naei sorarei mi_ affirms
both coming and speaking. _Sirurei mi norei turei mi noei nu turei mi_ means
“that person considers whether they come or do not come”. _Manei unao, varoli
tavarei naei mavarei_ names two people, a friend and a kinsman. _Taarei ni tore
selaroa_ means “I go for safety”, identifying purpose rather than cause.

_Tunarei ni taarei_ means “I can go”; _nu tunarei ni taarei_, “I cannot go”.
_Tuvarei ni taarei_ means “I may go”, giving permission rather than ability.
_Virarei turei mi_ means “that person might come”. _Tuvorei ni taarei_ means
“I must go”; _tuvorei ni nu taarei_, “I must not go”. _Nu tuvorei ni taarei_
instead means “I am not obliged to go”. _Tuvalei ni taarei_ gives advice to go.
_Tuvuroa_ is optional: permitted and not required. _Tuvare mi vi_ permits you
to perform the action already under discussion; _tuvurei mi vi_ prohibits you
from it. The action words stay invariant in these concrete citations.

The established _theiluori koruvei avaelith_ is the whole welcoming blessing,
used on arrival. Its poetic formula retains its old meaning; this is not a new
ordinary verb meaning for _koruvei_ or a new definition of the name _Avaelith_.
_Selaroa a vi_ wishes safety at parting. _Laru savarei vi ni_ is “help me,
please”. The cited requests use a courtesy appeal or a conventional request
context, without an imperative ending. _Larovei ni vi_ thanks you; _sen meralo_
answers thanks with “no debt”, the conventional “you are welcome”. _Vaarei ni
naloa ia toralo ia ni_ expresses regret; _laru sulurei vi ni_ asks forgiveness.
_Siraloa ni mi_ acknowledges understanding, while _nu siraloa ni mi_ denies it.
_Noraru sarei vi mi_ asks how to say the expression in question; _vaei saruero
ia vonarei sai norue_ asks what this is called. _Vaei mi varaei_ with rising
tone asks “really—is that true?”

## Script and Literacy

Ordinary Kaliharans do not write. They keep what they know by oral history and by [[skill-vaarthari|Vaarthari]], the learned discipline of deep memorization, and the people's archive is what they recite and remember. Some stone marks of [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]], who spoke mind to mind, remain, and only the keepers know their meaning.

The few stone marks that remain from the Ithari are too few to make a script or a language. Each is a sign of meaning, not text. What it stands for is held in memory by the keepers and recited, and the mark calls it up only for those who tend the relics, the Ithakan keepers and custodians.

Memorization does what writing does elsewhere. Vaarthari keeps a lineage, a pact or a season's account exact, and it is secure as a book is not, because knowledge passes only by being taught and nobody can take it by stealing a page or copying an inscription. The keepers know what the marks call up but seldom need them, since they carry on the memorization themselves, and they closely guard the elaborate secrets of how the Ithari instruments work. Valtari has no script of its own, so port Kaliharans who deal with foreigners hire a scribe who sets the transaction down in whatever foreign letters he knows.

## Historical Development

Kaliharans questioned at the ports trace their civilization to a people they call [[lore-theithari|the Ithari]], who they say came before them and departed long ago. They do not claim the tongue from them. By their own account those people left no word of any kind behind them and were never heard to speak—what was received was understanding, not language—so Kalihari is held to be the islanders' own, enlarged over eighty centuries by everything they were given to name. No scholar outside the island can test any part of that account, and none has been permitted to try. What the foreign evidence supports is the isolation itself and nothing further.

The language has **changed remarkably little in core structure** over recorded history—a testament to Kaliharan cultural conservatism and reverence for ancestral knowledge. However, **vocabulary has evolved** with geography and trade:

- **Ancient layer** (the stratum Kaliharans date to the teaching): Core spirituality, cosmology, natural phenomena
- **Classical layer** (the island's own development): Administrative, architectural, philosophical terms
- **Modern layer** (port contact): Valtari borrowings, trade vocabulary, new technologies

Port Kalihari retains perhaps 70% of Interior Kalihari vocabulary; the remaining 30% is Valtari loanwords and simplified forms.

## Regional Dialects

Geographic and cultural separation has produced subtle but noticeable dialects:

- **Interior High Speech** (the interior): Purest form, slowest pronunciation, most conservative grammar. Considered the prestige dialect by Kaliharans.
- **Coastal variants**: Faster speech, incorporation of Valtari elements, slightly simplified verb forms. Regional ports ([[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]], [[place-vashuran|Vashurán]]) have their own coastal flavors.
- **Sacred register**: Employed in rituals and formal contexts. More archaic verb forms, elevated vocabulary, exaggerated tonal patterns.

A trader from Ilovanu speaking Kalihari will sound notably different from an interior elder—one sounds practical and modern, the other ceremonial and ancient.

## Related Languages

Kalihari is a **complete isolate** with no known relatives on Thalorna. Its isolation raises historical questions nobody can answer: what the islanders spoke before the record begins, and whether lost languages lie buried at sites no one has been allowed to dig. Scholars remain baffled, and have nothing to work from but the language itself.

Linguistically, Kalihari shares **no demonstrable connection** to any other Thalorna language family. Phonologically, it is unique in its vowel emphasis and consonantal gentleness. Grammatically, its VSO word order is rare. Its deep antiquity and isolation make it invaluable to scholars of historical linguistics, who have no other example of anything like it.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure and Philosophy

Kalihari names are **melodic and compound**, reflecting a civilization that views all things as interconnected. Names typically comprise two elements:

1. **Personal name** (given name): Usually 2-3 syllables, often nature-inspired or relating to qualities of water, light, growth, or movement
2. **Lineage name** (family name): A compound form whose components reference natural features or forces

Lineage names are **matrilineal in the interior**; port cities may follow either parent's line depending on cosmopolitan influence.

### Personal Names

Personal names are chosen for their resonance and meaning:

_Kulanei_
: "One who flows" (adaptability, fluidity)

_Mahitua_
: "Great stone" (stability, strength)

_Avaelith_
: "High shelter" (protection, grace)

Parents often name children to reflect circumstances of birth:

- A child born during flood season might be _Kuethani_ ("swift current baby")
- A child born at dawn might be _Luavethei_ ("dawn fire one")

### Lineage Names

Lineage names are **compound descriptors of natural phenomena**, encoding both literal meaning and spiritual significance:

- _Ithavanu_ = "still water" (calmness, reflection, depth)
- _Koamanei_ = "deep root" (foundation, permanence, hidden wisdom)
- _Theiluori_ = "bright canopy" (protection, growth, abundance)
- _Marivoel_ = "warm stone" (strength, heat, steadfastness)
- _Naothuri_ = "green wind" (renewal, change, life force)

Understanding a Kaliharan's lineage name requires knowledge of [[affiliation-tharivaan|Thári'vaan]] cosmology—the Kaliharan spiritual system that sees all natural forces as expressions of sacred interconnection. To be born of "bright canopy" lineage is to carry the spiritual weight of growth and protection.

### Household Names

Inside a Kaliharan house, a long given name is not said in full. A woman named _Koaluethei_ is _Luethei_ to her mother, her sisters and her children, and _Koaluethei_ to everyone else. This short form is her **household name**, and nobody chose it: it is the end of her given name, cut where the language's own stress falls, so it was settled the day she was named.

The household name is formed by rule:

1. **Count the syllables of the given name.** Every vowel is a syllable, except that the diphthongs AE, OA, EI and OU count as one each. _Ilavoani_ is four syllables (i-la-voa-ni); _Theivoanei_ is three (thei-voa-nei).
2. **Only a name of four syllables or more has a household name.** A name of three or fewer is already the length a household says, and everyone uses it whole: _Kulanei_, _Avaelith_ and _Theivoanei_ have no household name.
3. **Cut at the second-to-last syllable.** That syllable carries the stress, and the household name runs from its consonant to the end of the name: _Marivano_ (ma-ri-va-no) gives _Vano_.
4. **Where that syllable is a bare vowel, reach back one syllable** so that the household name opens on a consonant: _Oruanei_ (o-ru-a-nei) gives _Ruanei_, not _Anei_.

Nothing is added. A household name takes no ending, no doubled syllable and no endearment, so it obeys the tongue's sound rules because the given name does. It is the part of the name a household hears loudest when the name is called across a garden.

The household name belongs to the house. In the interior that is the mother's line; in the ports it is whichever house a person was raised in. Kin use it, and so do those raised under the same roof. It is spoken only: the full name opens every account a Kaliharan holds, is recited in the household's and the lineage's memory, and is the only name a council or a custodian uses. Household names repeat freely from one house to the next—there are a great many _Vori_ on the island—which troubles nobody, because the name is never used where two houses meet.

An outsider who uses a household name is claiming a place in the house that he does not have. Between Kaliharans this is presumption of the same kind as unsolicited explanation, and it is answered the same way: with the full name, said back, and nothing more. A foreigner is not told the household name at all; a port Kaliharan who deals with foreigners gives a Valtari trade name and keeps both Kalihari names for his own people.

### Port Variants

Coastal Kaliharans in port cities sometimes adopt Valtari trade names alongside their true Kalihari names, similar to the merchant tradition. However, true Kaliharans consider their Kalihari name sacred and reserve it for family and intimate contexts.

## Kalihari Lexicon {#lexicon}

A ship bound for [[place-klhrcntnnt|Kalihara]] hears two registers of one tongue. On the quays of [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] and the smaller ports the islanders speak Kalihari quickly, threaded with the harbor pidgin; in the interior the custodians speak it slowly, and name their ranks and their tending in it: the _kaan'sili_ who watches, the _kaan'vaar_ who holds. This page is the word-hoard of both: the stems, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives on the island.

A Kalihari word is built of stems set side by side. _Itha-_ is still and _vanu-_ is water, and _ithavanu_ is still water, a lineage name. Where two stems meet in a custodial word, formal speech marks the seam with a catch in the throat, written `'`: _kaan-_ is to keep and _sili-_ is to watch, and _kaan'sili_ is the one who keeps by watching, a custodian in training. A handful of endings make a person of a stem: _kula-_ is to flow, and _kulanei_ is one who flows.

The sound of Kalihari is open and liquid. Consonants are few and soft, nearly every syllable ends on its vowel, and vowels stand together freely, each its own syllable unless it makes one of the diphthongs the language note names. Stress falls on the second-last syllable.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its bare form. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone. A word made of smaller parts names the stems and the ending the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what the suffix table makes of them.

### Fields

```sql
SELECT name AS "Field", holds AS "What it holds"
FROM fields
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kalihrlng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, and closes on a vowel or on one of the consonants below. A bound stem, written with a closing hyphen, closes on anything.

```sql
SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'closeConsonant' THEN 'Consonants that close a word' END AS "Edge", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Sounds"
FROM edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kalihrlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Clusters

Consonants stand apart, a vowel between each, inside a stem. Two meet where a stem closing on a consonant takes another stem, as in _kaan'sili_, and in a few old words such as _sílhari_.

```sql
SELECT 'Most consonants together inside a word' AS "Rule", max AS "Count"
FROM clusters WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kalihrlng'
```

## Building words {#building-words}

Two moves make every built word on this page. **Stems set side by side** make a compound, the first stem marking the second: _koa-_ deep and _manu-_ root give _koamanu_, a deep root. In the custodians' words the seam between the stems is written `'`. **An ending on a stem** makes a person of it.

### Suffixes

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kalihrlng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Making names {#making-names}

**A Kaliharan person** carries a given name and a lineage name, the given name first. A given name is often a stem with _-nei_, the one who does a thing: [[being-tvnrthr|Tavonei Reithuri]] is _tavo-_ with _-nei_, one who walks beside, a fitting name for a caravan guard. A lineage name is a compound of two stems of the island's ground and weather: _Reithuri_ is _rei-_ old and _thuri-_ wind. A given name of four syllables or more also has the short household name the language note describes, which is said and never written.

**A port** is a compound naming what the place is to the island: [[place-ilovanu|Ilovanu]] is _ilo-_ door and _vanu-_ water, the water-door through which the world is admitted, and [[place-nalurithan|Nalurithan]] is _nalu-_ a river mouth with _rithan_ to flow, the running mouth on which the town stands.

**A byname** is a word of this page given to a stranger the port has no other name for. The old scholar of the pilots' observatory on [[place-telvari|Telvari]] is _[Olitelinei]{.name data-type="being/npc"}_, the star-reckoner.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of every note on the island of Kalihara. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'folders' THEN 'folder' WHEN 'cultures' THEN 'culture' WHEN 'parents' THEN 'parent' WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kalihrlng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'folders' THEN 0 WHEN 'cultures' THEN 1 WHEN 'parents' THEN 2 WHEN 'addresses' THEN 3 END
```
