The Osetakona Language is the tongue of the [[affiliation-osetakona|Osetakona]], the Maple People of the northeastern lakes and forests. Fluency measures the range of what a speaker can say in it, from a One Fire trader's few dozen words of copper, furs and price at the river posts to the speech of a rememberer who can recite every agreement the Grove has made. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Every nation of the Maple People speaks the Osetakona Language, and every nation speaks it a little differently. The Copper People of the western lakes clip their words and drop the catch; the Dawn People of the eastern shore draw out their vowels; the Falls People, who most often host the great council, speak the form the councils use, and a speaker from any nation shifts toward it when he rises to talk. A speaker can be placed by his nation within a sentence, and nobody is thought the worse for it.

The tongue carries the marks of the people it has taken in. Many of the Maple People were born elsewhere and adopted, and an adopted grandmother who learned the language at twenty keeps her first tongue's vowels for life. A handful of words for lake fish and island birds survive from the Island People, the nation the Grove broke and took in, and nowhere else.

Nobody writes it. The Maple People keep their law in the memory of the rememberers and in the memory belts, whose rows of white and purple shell beads hold an agreement without spelling a word of it.

## How the Osetakona Language sounds

The Osetakona Language is long-worded and soft, and nothing in it is made with the lips. A speaker's mouth hardly closes, and a southern ear hears a run of open vowels broken by _t_, _k_, _n_, _r_ and _s_: _kahnisa_ is a longhouse and _kontiesa_ a clan mother.

**The vowels.** _a_ leads, with _e_ and _i_ close behind and _o_ after them. There is no _u_. _ai_ runs as one sound, as in _eye_, and _ie_ runs as one, as in a quick _yeh_: _kontiesa_ is kon-TIE-sa. Any other two vowels side by side are said apart, so _ohiatse_, a river, is o-hi-A-tse.

**The consonants.** _t_, _k_, _n_, _r_, _s_, _h_, _w_ and _y_, with _ts_ and _kw_ each said as one sound. An _h_ before another consonant is a breath, as the spelling guide reads it: _kahnion_, a lake, is kah-NI-on.

**The catch.** Between two vowels a word may break with a catch in the throat, written `'`. Few words hold one, and none holds two.

**Shapes and endings.** Most words have three or four syllables, many two, and the names run longer. A frequent word is short: _eno_ is I, _tsio_ you and _teha_ not. Many nouns open on _ka-_ or _o-_. A word ends on a vowel, most often _a_ or _e_, or on _n_, and now and then on _s_ or _k_.

**What the Osetakona Language leaves out.** It has no _p_, _b_, _m_, _f_ or _v_, nothing at all made with the lips. It has no _d_, _g_, _l_, _j_ or _z_, no _u_, no _sh_, _ch_, _th_ or _kh_, and none of the ejective pops of Ki'i'chek. No word opens on two consonants except _kw_, _ts_, _sk_ and _st_, and four consonants never stand together.

## Phonology

### Consonant System

**Stops:** t, k, kw

**Affricate:** ts

**Fricatives:** s, h

**Nasal:** n

**Liquid and glides:** r, w, y

**The catch:** a stop in the throat between two vowels, written `'`

### Vowel System

**Vowels:** a, e, i, o

**Vowel pairs:** ai and ie, each one syllable

A vowel before _n_ at the end of a syllable is often said through the nose, and the spelling writes it as the plain vowel and _n_: _ohsiren_, a name, is oh-SI-ren.

### Stress

A word of two syllables is stressed on the first, and a longer word on the second-to-last, by the rule of [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]]: _oseta_ is o-SE-ta, _osetakona_ is o-se-ta-KO-na. No word of the dictionary needs an accent.

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

The verb comes first. Then comes the one who acts, then whatever is acted on: _ratie eno tsio_, I see you. _Teha_, not, stands immediately before the word it denies: _teha tahkon ostero_, the rain does not come. A word of time or place stands at the end.

### Verbs

A verb does not change. _Ratie_ is to see, and _ratie eno_, _ratie tsio_ and _ratie rohehon_ are I see, you see and they see. Whether a thing happened, happens or will happen is said with a word of time when it matters: _ratie eno tetsiohwen_, I saw it yesterday.

### Nouns and Possession

A noun takes _-hon_ for more than one: _kahnisa_, a longhouse, _kahnisahon_, longhouses. A noun ending in _k_ or _s_ takes _-on_ instead, so _ostiaronk_, lightning, gives _ostiaronkon_. The owner stands before what is owned, with nothing between them: _eno kahnisa_ is my house, and _anesa otsenraten_ the mother's hearth.

### Pronouns

_Eno_ is I and _tsio_ you; _rohe_ is he, _iene_ she and _okon_ it. The plurals are made by the rule for nouns: _enohon_ we, _tsiohon_ you all and _rohehon_ they. A pronoun before a noun makes it the speaker's or the listener's: _tsio ohsiren_, your name.

### Questions

A question opens with _seka_ or with one of the question words built on it: _sekaron_ who, _sekatsi_ what, _sekaten_ where, _sekaniak_ when, _sekariwa_ why. _Seka tsio ohsakwe awero_ asks "do you drink water?" The roman spelling marks the question with `?`, and the speaker marks it with the opening word.

### Building Words

Five endings make most of the built words. _-hsa_ makes a noun of an act or a quality: _rosta_, to boil, gives _rostahsa_, a council, the boiling where many minds are talked down to one. _-hen_ makes a person of a thing: _katiro_, a word, gives _katirohen_, a speaker. _-kona_ makes a people or a clan: _oseta_, the maple, gives _osetakona_. _-ten_ makes a place: _ostaka_, a pot, gives _ostakaten_, the Kettle. _-kwen_ makes an ordinal of a number. Two words set side by side make a compound, the first saying which: _tsikenha_, copper, and _kahnion_, a lake, give _tsikenha kahnion_, the Copper Lake.

## Everyday speech

_Ahsiohne_ is the greeting, and _ehtsenha_ the word at parting. _Tewirotsi_ thanks. _Ehse_ is yes and _tsona_ no, and a refusal is given after a pause long enough to show it was thought over.

_Tahionha_, "you have come", is what a host says to anyone who walks into a longhouse, kin or stranger, and a bowl of _ohsterio_, the corn soup that is always on the fire, follows it. Nobody asks a guest's business until he has eaten. _Ratie eno tsio_, "I see you", is said to someone not seen for a season.

_Eno kahnisa_ is my house, and a man who says it means his wife's, or his mother's if he is unmarried, because the longhouse belongs to its women. _Anesa_ is mother and _rakwen_ father, _ehiatsi_ a child, _otsiterie_ a grandmother and _ohsotsien_ a grandfather. _Atiakwa_ is a clan, and every speaker can name theirs and the eight nations in which it has kin.

Numbers count by tens: _tsini_, _kiara_, _nonke_, _tiokwa_, _rensa_, one to five; _kensien_ is ten, _kiara kensien_ twenty, _ohkaron_ a hundred. A number takes _-kwen_ to give its place: _tsinikwen_, first, _rensakwen_, fifth.

## Script and Literacy

The Osetakona Language has no script. The Maple People keep their agreements in memory belts (_ohniakaro_), belts of white and purple shell beads strung in rows, whose patterns a rememberer can read: a row of white for a road kept open, a purple diamond for each nation that agreed, a broken row for a promise broken. A belt holds what was agreed and who agreed it. It does not hold the words, and the words are the rememberer's to recite. A belt without a rememberer is a string of beads.

The Maple People have heard of the south's glyphs from the One Fire's traders and want nothing to do with them. A thing written down can be read by anyone, in secret, without the council present, and that is reason enough.

## Naming Traditions

A name belongs to a clan, not to the person who carries it. Each clan holds a stock of names, and a name is carried by one living person at a time. When the carrier dies, the name lies down (_ohsiren tsiatak_), and the clan is a person short until the name is raised (_ohsiren tsieren_) and given to someone new: a child, or an adopted captive who takes the dead person's place in the house along with the name. A child is named by its clan mother at the next Green Corn or Long Night after its birth, from the names lying down.

A name is two words of the dictionary set together, most often a thing and what it does: _Kiontsarosta_, Boils the Sap; _Skahniotiaro_, Watches the Elk. A child carries a child's name until its youth, and then takes an adult name from the same stock. A speaker's title is a name too, and the one name that is never left lying down.

```sql
SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may open a word' WHEN 'closeConsonant' THEN 'Consonants that close a word' WHEN 'vowelPair' THEN 'Vowels that run as one' END AS "Edge", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Sounds"
FROM edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-osetakonalng' ORDER BY position
```

### Clusters

Two consonants often meet inside a word, and three now and then; _ts_ and _kw_ count as two letters each, so _ohnionkwa_, a strawberry, holds three together and never four.

```sql
SELECT 'Most consonant letters together inside a word' AS "Rule", max AS "Count"
FROM clusters WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-osetakonalng'
```

## Building words {#building-words}

Two moves make every built word on this page. **Words set side by side** make a compound, the first marking the second: _tsiorhen_, the dawn country, and _-kona_ give _tsiorhenkona_, the Dawn People. **An ending on a word** makes a noun, a person, a people, a place or an ordinal of it.

### Suffixes

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-osetakonalng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Making names {#making-names}

**A person** carries a name from the clan's stock, two words set together: _kiontsarosta_ is _kiontsa_, sap, and _rosta_, to boil. **A clan** and **a nation** are a word with _-kona_: _tiahwenkona_, the Otter clan, and _tsikenhakona_, the Copper People. **A place** names what it is or what stands there: _ostakaten_ is the place of the pot, the Kettle, and _tsikenha kahnion_ is the Copper Lake.

**The people** are _osetakona_, the people of the maple, and the name these pages give them is the Maple People. They picture their league as a sugar bush (_osetaten_), many trees tapped for one sugar, and call it the Grove. The south calls them _Shik'ul Ch'akal_, after the great lakes of their country, and its traders call them the Otter People, after the otters carved on their canoe prows.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of every note of the Maple People. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'cultures' THEN 'culture' WHEN 'parents' THEN 'parent' WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-osetakonalng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'cultures' THEN 0 WHEN 'parents' THEN 1 WHEN 'addresses' THEN 2 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning || coalesce(CASE tag WHEN 'osetakona' THEN ', [[' || tongue_language || '|Osetakona]]' END, '') AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-osetakonalng' ORDER BY position
```
