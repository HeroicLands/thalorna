The Shuqash Language is the tongue of the [[affiliation-shuqash|Shuqash]], the Shell-Bead People of the western coast and the oak valleys behind it. Fluency measures the range of what a speaker can say in it, from a Cedar trader's few dozen words of beads and prices to the speech of someone raised in a chief's house and taught the sky by the astronomer-priests. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Every town of the Shell-Bead People speaks the Shuqash Language, from the drilling town of [[place-shuqwil|Shuqwil]] on Bead Island to the last valley village under the snow mountains. The islands and the channel shore speak it quickly and drop the ends of their words in a hurry; the [[place-tulishlumuqrgn|Oak Valleys]] speak it slowly and give every word its last consonant. The coast hears the valley speech as countrified, and the valleys hear the coast's as careless. The astronomer-priests sing the old songs in the valley way, and say that is how the songs were made.

The tongue is related to nothing spoken by the [[affiliation-kelwanuk|Cedar People]] to the north or the [[affiliation-teyumi|Turquoise People]] over the mountains, and the south has never heard it. Cedar and Turquoise traders bargain with signs, numbers and a few dozen words, and the first words they learn are the numbers, _shuq_, a bead, and _hamil_, a strand.

Nobody writes it. The Shell-Bead People keep their counts, their songs and their law in memory, and the paintings on the walls of their caves keep visions and days, never words.

## How the Shuqash Language sounds

The Shuqash Language is clipped and busy, and most of its words close on a consonant. A sentence sounds to a stranger like beads poured from hand to hand: short words, many of them ending on _sh_, _q_, _l_ and _n_, with a vowel between each pair of consonants. _Shuq_ is a bead, _kulay_ a canoe, and _hilaq_ a chief.

**The vowels.** _a_ and _i_ lead, then _u_ and _o_, with _e_ well behind. Each has its plain value, and two vowels never stand side by side: a consonant always comes between them.

**The consonants.** _k_, _sh_, _s_, _t_, _l_, _h_, _q_, _m_, _w_, _p_ and _y_ lead, with _n_, _ch_ and _ts_ behind them. _q_ is a _k_ said farther back in the throat, and a plain _k_ is a good enough way to say it.

**Shapes and endings.** Many of the commonest words have one syllable, most have two, and fewer have three or more. A syllable opens on one consonant and may close on one, so two consonants meet inside many words: _hipalshuq_, a crossing strand, _tumshaw_, a storm. Three in four words end on a consonant, most often _q_, _sh_, _l_, _n_ or _k_, and many end on _-ay_, _-aw_ or _-uy_, a vowel and a glide.

**What the Shuqash Language leaves out.** It has no _b_, _d_, _g_, _f_, _v_, _r_, _z_ or _j_, no _th_, _kh_ or _ng_, and no catch in the throat, which the south's tongue is full of. No word opens on two consonants, and two vowels never meet.

## Phonology

### Consonant System

**Stops:** p, t, k, q

**Affricates:** ch, ts

**Fricatives:** s, sh, h

**Nasals:** m, n

**Liquid and glides:** l, w, y

### Vowel System

**Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u, each with its plain value. The tongue has no long vowels and no vowel pairs; _-ay_, _-aw_, _-uy_ and _-oy_ are a vowel followed by the consonant _y_ or _w_.

### Stress

A word of two syllables is stressed on the first, and a longer word on the second-to-last, by the rule of [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]]: _kulay_ is KU-lay, _hipalshuq_ is hi-PAL-shuk, _kulaywil_ is ku-LAY-wil. No word of the dictionary needs an accent.

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

The verb comes first, then the one who acts, then whatever is acted on: _putal ki pi_, I see you, is "see I you". A word of time or place comes after these. _Mak_, not, stands immediately before the word it denies: _mak putal ki pi_, I do not see you.

### Verbs

A verb does not change. _Putal_ is to see, and _putal ki_, _putal pi_ and _putal siwal_ are I see, you see and they see. Whether a thing happened, happens or will happen is said with a word of time when it matters, such as _lomihim_, yesterday, or _soshowik_, tomorrow.

### Nouns and Possession

A noun is the same for one or many: _shuq_ is a bead or beads. A speaker who wants to stress a great number repeats the noun's first consonant and vowel: _shushuq_, many beads, is the word for wealth, and _kukulay_ is a whole fleet of canoes. The owner stands before what is owned, with nothing between them: _kitu hukay_ is my house, and _hilaq hukay_ the chief's house.

### Pronouns

_Ki_ is I, _pi_ you and _si_ he, she or it; _kiwal_ is we, _piwal_ you, more than one, and _siwal_ they. _Kitu_, _pitu_ and _kiwalu_ are my, your and our. There is no separate word for a man or a woman spoken of.

### Questions

A question asking for a yes or a no opens with _lun_: _lun putal pi_ is "do you see?" Every other question word opens on _t_: _tesh_ who, _tapat_ what, _tet_ which, _tapon_ where, _tahim_ when, _taliq_ why, _tahu_ how, _tamuk_ how many. The roman spelling marks the question with `?`, and the speaker marks it with the opening word.

### Building Words

Five endings and one prefix make most of the built words. _al-_ makes a person of a thing or an act: _tiyup_, the sky, gives _altiyup_, an astronomer-priest, and _pilik_, to count, gives _alpilik_, a counter. _-ash_ makes a people or a body of kin: _shuq_ gives _shuqash_, the people of the bead. _-in_ makes a noun of an act or a quality: _mul_, to die, gives _mulin_, death. _-wil_ makes a place: _kulay_ gives _kulaywil_, Canoe Place. _-li_ makes an ordinal of a number. Two nouns set side by side make a compound, the first saying which: _tikoq_, the north, and _hipay_, a door, give _tikoq hipay_, the North Door.

## Everyday speech

_Qoy_ is the greeting on a beach, and _chiw_ the word at parting. _Niq_ thanks. _Hu_ is yes and _mahu_ no.

A bargain is closed with one word, _pilik_, count: the buyer says it as he hands over the strand, and the seller says it back as he takes it. Until both have said it nothing has changed hands, and after both have said it the bargain cannot be undone. _Lun hamil_, "a strand?", is how a price is asked, and the answer is a number.

_Kitu hukay_ is my house, and _hilaq hukay_ the chief's. _Lusha_ is mother and _hawi_ father, _chak_ a child, _kupoy_ a grandmother and _kupaq_ a grandfather.

Numbers count by tens: _hika_, _shimu_, _kalaq_, _suyuk_, _matish_, one to five; _tasul_, _wiqay_, _holok_, _tipan_, six to nine; _lushu_ is ten, _shimu lushu_ twenty, _halup_ a hundred and _lushu halup_ a thousand. A number takes _-li_ to give its place: _hikali_, first, _kalaqli_, third. A counter counts beads four at a time, sliding them along the cord with a thumb, so that _suyuk_, four, is the word a counter says most.

## Script and Literacy

The Shuqash Language has no script. A counter who must hold a large number until a feast ties knots in a cord, one knot for each ten strands; the knots hold the number, and the counter holds what it was for. The astronomer-priests paint suns, wheels and sky beings on the walls of their caves, and a painting may mark a day or keep a vision. None of these marks writes a word.

## Naming Traditions

A child is named on its fourth day by an astronomer-priest, who reads the sky of the night it was born and joins a being of the sky or the sea to an act: _Ayulchim_, Moon Rising, _Sitiqnush_, Star Walking. A family that dislikes the name may pay for a second reading. The name is the one a person uses for life. A person away from home adds the town: _Sitiqnush of Tikoq Hipay_.

## Shuqash Lexicon {#lexicon}

A bead is _shuq_, a strand of beads _hamil_, and a plank canoe _kulay_. A chief is _hilaq_, a counter _alpilik_, and the crossing strand a person keeps for the dead _hipalshuq_. This page is the word-hoard of the Shuqash: its words, the endings that build new ones, and the register of every name the setting gives the Shell-Bead People, their towns and their offices.

The Shuqash Language is unwritten, and the spellings on this page are the setting's one roman transcription of it. The tongue has no capital letters, so a word or a name stands here in lowercase; the prose of these pages capitalizes a name as it does any other.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in the form it never leaves. A word made of smaller parts names them, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly those parts set side by side, an ending added whole. A phrase of two words, such as _mak yal_, invisible, is cited as it is said.

### Fields

```sql
SELECT name AS "Field", holds AS "What it holds"
FROM fields
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

Its words are written in the setting's one guide, Pronouncing Thalorna.

### Word edges

A word opens on a vowel or a single consonant, _sh_, _ch_ and _ts_ each counting as one, and closes on a vowel or on one of the consonants below.

```sql
SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may open a word' WHEN 'closeConsonant' THEN 'Consonants that close a word' WHEN 'vowelPair' THEN 'Vowels that run as one' END AS "Edge", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Sounds"
FROM edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Clusters

Two consonants meet inside a word where a syllable closes on _sh_, _s_, _l_, _m_, _n_, _w_, _y_, _q_ or _k_ before the next. _sh_ is one sound written with two letters, so _hipalshuq_ holds three letters together and two sounds.

```sql
SELECT 'Most consonant letters together inside a word' AS "Rule", max AS "Count"
FROM clusters WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng'
```

## Building words {#building-words}

Two moves make every built word on this page. **Words set side by side** make a compound, the first marking the second: _hipal_, to cross, and _shuq_, a bead, give _hipalshuq_, a crossing strand. **A prefix or an ending on a word** makes a person, a people, a place, a noun or an ordinal of it.

### Suffixes

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Making names {#making-names}

**A person** carries two words set together, given by an astronomer-priest from the sky of the birth night: _ayulchim_ is _ayul_, the moon, and _chim_, to rise. **A place** names what it is or what is there: _-wil_ makes a place of a thing, so Kulaywil is _kulay_, a canoe, and _-wil_, Canoe Place, and two words side by side name a stretch of coast or country, as _shuq halaq_, the Bead Coast, and _tulish lumuq_, the Oak Valleys. **A body of people** takes _-ash_: _kulayash_ are the Canoe Kin.

**The people** are _shuqash_, the people of the bead, and the name these pages give them is the Shell-Bead People. The valleys call themselves _tulishash_, the oak people, and the coast calls them _kowiqash_, the acorn people. The south has no name for them.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of every note of the Shell-Bead People. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'cultures' THEN 'culture' WHEN 'parents' THEN 'parent' WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'cultures' THEN 0 WHEN 'parents' THEN 1 WHEN 'addresses' THEN 2 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives the Shell-Bead People, their towns, their places and their offices, with the note where it is met. A name built from this page shows how; a name in the reader's tongue, or in another people's, is tagged as such.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning || coalesce(CASE tag WHEN 'shuqash' THEN ', [[' || tongue_language || '|Shuqash]]' END, '') AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-shuqashlng' ORDER BY position
```
