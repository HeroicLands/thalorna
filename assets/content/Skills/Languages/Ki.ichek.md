Ki'ichek is a tongue of the Ki'ichek (independent isolate) family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Ki'ichek, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Ki'ichek is the ancient language of the K'ich'chik city-states, a civilization that predates human settlement of Ankaris by millennia. Spoken across an entire separate continent, Ki'ichek stands alone—no known linguistic relatives exist anywhere on Thalorna. The language is deeply connected to K'ich'chik cosmology, mathematics, and spiritual worldview. To the untrained ear, Ki'ichek sounds exotic and mysterious: a cascade of glottal stops and sharp consonants interspersed with clear vowel sounds, often accompanied by tonal glides that shift meaning.

Ki'ichek employs a complex hieroglyphic writing system that encodes both phonetic and logographic information. The language reflects the K'ich'chik obsession with order, hierarchy, astronomical cycles, and the sacred balance between the natural and divine worlds.

## How Ki'ichek sounds

Ki'ichek is clipped and glottal, built of short syllables with the catch of the ejective.

**The vowels.** _a_, _i_, _u_ and _e_, with _o_ rarer.

**The consonants.** _k'_, _ts'_, _ch'_, _p'_ and _q'_ (an apostrophe after the consonant), _sh_, _l_, _m_, _n_ and _h_.

**Clusters.** Few. Syllables are a consonant and a vowel, closing on _-l_, _-k_, _-n_ or _-m_.

**Endings.** Ki'ichek words end in _-al_, _-ik_, _-ul_, _-mah_ or _-bal_.

**What Ki'ichek leaves out.** It has no more than two apostrophes in a word, and only the straight apostrophe.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Ki'ichek phonology is dominated by hard stops and sibilants, creating a percussive, rhythmic quality:

- **Glottal stops (')**: Fundamental to the language; often serve as syllable delimiters
- **Hard consonants**: K, T, P, Q (often articulated with greater force than their Ankarian equivalents)
- **Sibilants**: X (like 'sh' in "shaman"), TZ (affricate), CH (palatalized)
- **Liquids**: L (rolled), allowing for some softness amid the hardness

### Vowels

Five clear vowels, each pronounced distinctly:

- **A** (father)—bright, open
- **E** (met)—mid, unrounded
- **I** (machine)—high, clear
- **O** (go)—mid, rounded
- **U** (flute)—high, rounded

Vowels rarely diphthongize; each vowel sound stands alone. This clarity aids comprehension when chanting sacred texts or astronomical calculations.

### Stress and Prosody

Stress typically falls on the second-to-last syllable unless marked otherwise. The language exhibits tonal variation tied to ritual context: ceremonial speech employs rising tones at phrase boundaries, while everyday speech is flatter.

### Distinctive Phonetic Patterns

Analyzing the name corpus reveals consistent patterns:

- Names frequently begin with hard consonants (K', B, T, H, N, Z)
- Glottal stops often mark syllable boundaries, preventing consonant clusters
- Vowel-final words are common, facilitating fluid speech
- Three-syllable names are the norm; longer names denote importance
- The sequences -ALAM, -IKAN, -TSUL, and -KUL appear frequently in compounds

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

Ki'ichek employs a **Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)** word order, fundamentally different from most Ankarian tongues:

- Example: _Ish'ik ha'nal ka-nal'un-ix_—"The lagoon (subj.) eats (verb) the river (obj.)"

This structure reflects K'ich'chik philosophical priorities: establishing who acts and what is acted upon before revealing the action itself.

### Word Order for Modifiers

Adjectives follow nouns; adverbs precede verbs. This allows the listener to ground themselves in the noun before applying descriptive qualities.

## Script and Literacy

Ki'ichek writing—[[skill-chlxplmscrpt|Ch'alish P'ilam]], "the glyphs of Ch'alish", after the goddess who composed the first codex—combines **logographic and phonetic elements**. Each glyph can represent either a whole word-concept or a syllabic sound, depending on context. The script is written in columns read from top to bottom, left to right.

Key script features:

- **Logographs**: Iconic or abstract symbols representing concepts (water, jaguar, maize, sky)
- **Phonetic complements**: Small glyphs indicating pronunciation to disambiguate homonyms
- **Diacritical marks**: Dots, lines, and flourishes modify meaning
- **Ceremonial variants**: More elaborate, ornate forms used in sacred contexts

Alongside the glyphs runs a second system entirely: the [[skill-ktzlcrdscrpt|K'otsel]], knotted cord records in colored thread, carrying tribute tallies, census and the day-counts, tied and read only by the priestly scribes.

Literacy is restricted among the K'ich'chik; scribes and priests hold tremendous social power. Common folk recognize a few dozen glyphs related to daily life (numbers, calendar markers, titles) but cannot read continuous text.

## Historical Development

Ki'ichek emerged in the earliest K'ich'chik settlements, predating the development of their complex calendar and astronomical mathematics. Over millennia, the language absorbed vocabulary related to celestial observation, architectural achievement, and ritual practice, but its core structure remained stable.

The language preserves archaic elements in formal speech and sacred texts—older verb forms and syllable patterns not used in everyday conversation. This linguistic conservatism reflects K'ich'chik reverence for ancestral knowledge.

## Regional Dialects

Geographically vast and politically fragmented, K'ich'chik city-states maintain distinct dialects:

- **Northern dialect**: Spoken in Ts'uma No'tun and P'enqal Ch'akal; emphasizes sharp consonant articulation
- **Central dialect**: The prestige form, centered on Shik'ul Ch'akal; balances clarity with flowing speech
- **Southern dialect**: Softer glottal stops, longer vowel sounds, influenced by older ceremonial forms

Inter-city trade and shared religious rites limit dialect divergence, but a K'ich'chik from the northern highlands will sound noticeably different from a southern coastal dweller.

## Sample Phrases

- _Ch'alish ish'bal ts'ik k'inish_—"Sacred jaguar rises above the sun" (blessing or salutation)
- _Ka-ts'akun-in kul'qat-um_—"We climb to the sacred peak" (call to pilgrimage)
- _Ha'nal ts'uma ka-ts'akun-um_—"The misty river flows" (poetic observation)
- _Na-nal'un-ix shak'nal shiq'il-ka_—"I ate the eagle feather" (ritual/mystical statement)
- _Ts'amal ishkan k'alish_—"From void comes thought, is being" (cosmological maxim)

## Related Languages

Ki'ichek exists in **complete isolation** on Thalorna. No other language shares its grammatical structure, phonological inventory, or root vocabulary. Scholars debate whether this reflects genuine historical separation (a separate diaspora) or merely the vast age of both the language and the K'ich'chik civilization.

Minimal borrowing occurs between Ki'ichek and other Thalorna languages. A few terms related to exotic K'ich'chik goods have entered maritime pidgins (e.g., _ch'ishal_—the bitter bean drink), but Ki'ichek speakers pride themselves on linguistic purity.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure and Convention

K'ich'chik names are **compound forms without gender distinction**—a person's name is equally valid regardless of sex. Names often function as brief poetic statements, encapsulating qualities, achievements, or circumstances of birth.

### Components

1. **Primary element**: A descriptor, animal, natural force, or deity name (e.g., K'ushi, Tsul, Ts'ultel, Ish'bal)
2. **Secondary element**: Modifies or completes the meaning (e.g., Ne'ok, Ts'alam, Ne')
3. **Tertiary element** (optional): For high-ranking individuals or complex biographical claims (e.g., Ch'akul Ts'ultel Ish'bal Tsul)

Names with three or more elements signal importance: nobility, priesthood, or exceptional deeds. Commoners typically carry two-element names.

### Philosophical Naming

Names encode philosophy. A child born during floods might be named _Ne' Ishkul_ ("water within the lake") to mark the auspicious conjunction. A warrior who survived wounds might be renamed _Tsul Ne'ok_ ("strength flows") to commemorate resilience.

### Titles and Honorifics

Titles are appended to names, not fused:

- _Q'emulmah_—"Overlord" (ruler of a city-state)
- _Ha'nalmah_—"Landholder" (chief of a district)
- _Ts'omalil_—"One Who Belongs" (full citizen)

### House Names

A name of three or more elements is too long for the household, and the K'ich'chik shorten it by taking its _first element whole_. The first element is the first name-word of the compound, spoken exactly as it stands in the full name, glottal stops included: it is never clipped to a syllable, never given an ending, and never replaced by a pet name, because each element is already a word with its own meaning. That short form is the _house name_.

- _Ch'akul Ts'ultel Ish'bal Tsul_ is _Ch'akul_ at home.
- _Ish'bal Ts'omalil K'inish_ is _Ish'bal_. A connector such as _Ts'omalil_ is never the house name, even where it stands second.
- _Shak'nal Mah'alil Shilan_ is _Shak'nal_. _Mah'alil_ is a rank word, and no title or rank word is ever the house name.
- _Ne' Ishkul_ and _Tsul Ne'ok_ have no house name. A two-element name is short already and is spoken whole by everyone.

The house name follows rank. Kin, the household and people of equal standing use it, and so does anyone of higher standing speaking to someone below. Someone of lower standing never does: a farmer addresses a lord by the full name, a priest of lesser grade addresses a hierarch by the full name, and a title is always appended to the full name and never to the house name. The full name is also the only name in the record. The K'otsel census cords and the Ch'alish P'ilam glyphs carry the full compound, and a house name is never knotted or written.

## Phonetic Rules

1.  **Syllables**: K’ich'chik words are typically 2-4 syllables, with occasional longer words for important concepts.

2.  **Consonants**: Common consonants include:
    - Glottal stops: `’`
    - Hard sounds: `k`, `t`, `p`, `q`
    - Soft sounds: `x`, `ch`, `tz`, `l`

3.  **Vowels**: The five vowels (a, e, i, o, u) are pronounced clearly.

4.  **Stress**: Stress generally falls on the second-to-last syllable unless otherwise specified.

## Naming Rules

1.  **Structure**:
    - Names often follow a descriptive phrase format: _noun + verb + descriptor_, e.g., “Jaguar prowls beneath the moon.”

2.  **Gendered Themes**:
    - **Male Names**: Predatory animals, quick motion, violence, great things (e.g., mountains, storms).
    - **Female Names**: Prey animals, flowers, stillness, peace, gentle natural features.

3.  **House Names**:
    - A formal name of three or more elements is shortened for informal use to its first element, whole, as set out under House Names above; a two-element name is not shortened.

4.  **Titles**:
    - Titles are appended to names to denote rank or role, e.g., _Q’emulmah_ (Overlord), _Ha’nalmah_ (Landholder).

5.  **Connector Words**:
    - **Member of Tribe**: _Ts’omalil_ (“One Who Belongs”).
    - **Leader of Tribe**: _Mah’alil_ (“Lord of the Tribe”).
    - **Tq'an**
      - **Meaning**: _Edge, boundary, or connection_
      - **Usage**: Often used to describe a transitional or liminal state, such as the edge of a natural feature (e.g., horizon, riverbank, or dawn) or the point of connection between two elements. It can imply a sense of joining or being on the verge of something.
    - **Ch'al**
      - **Meaning**: _Beneath, under, or within_
      - **Usage**: Commonly appears in phrases that describe something located under or within a larger feature, such as beneath the moonlight, under a tree, or within sacred waters. It suggests immersion, protection, or positioning relative to something overarching.
    - **Ts'ik**
      - **Usage**: Represents something elevated, external, or outside of an encompassing space.
      - **Examples**:
        - _Ts'ik k’inish_ ("Above the sunlight")
        - _Ts'ik ha'nal_ ("Outside the river")
    - **Shaq'al**
      - **Usage**: Indicates disconnection, separation, or being apart from something.
      - **Examples**:
        - _Shaq'al shik'tel_ ("Separate from the sacred grove")
        - _Shaq'al k'inish_ ("Disconnected from the sunlight")

## Reference Grammar

### Sentence Structure

1.  **Basic Word Order**: Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)
    - Example: _Ish’ik ha’nal ka-nal’un-ix_ (“The lagoon eats the river”)

2.  **Modifiers**: Adjectives follow nouns, adverbs precede verbs.
    - Example: _Ha’nal ts’uma ka-ts’akun-ix_ (“The misty river goes”)

### Structure of the K’ich'chik Numerical System

1. **Base Numbers (1–19)**: The numbers from one to ten are single words; eleven to nineteen are _olek’_ (ten) followed by the unit, as two words.
2. **Multiples of 20**: Numbers from 20 onward are combinations of multiples of 20, with separate terms for higher powers of 20:
   - K’aqil: 1 (20⁰)
   - Ts’ob: 20 (20¹)
   - Pel’ush: 400 (20²)
   - Q’umpel: 8,000 (20³)
   - Sholemash: 160,000 (20⁴) Higher powers follow similar patterns, though rarely used in daily life.
3. **Positional Representation**: Each place represents a power of 20, with the first position for units, the second for 20s, the third for 400s, and so on.

#### Combining Numbers

Numbers are expressed hierarchically, starting from the highest value and descending to the lowest:

1. Example: 1,325 1,325 = 3 × 400 + 6 × 20 + 5

- Lishe Pel’ush = 3 × 400 (three Pel’ushes)
- Ts’ishe Ts’ob = 6 × 20 (six Ts’obs)
- P’o = 5 Full Expression: _Lishe Pel’ush Ts’ishe Ts’ob P’o_

2. Example: 57,724 = 7 × 8,000 + 4 × 400 + 6 × 20 + 4

- Nuq’el Q’umpel = 7 × 8,000 (seven Q’umpels)
- Mushe Pel’ush = 4 × 400 (four Pel’ushes)
- Ts’ishe Ts’ob = 6 × 20 (six Ts’obs)
- Mushe = 4 Full Expression: _Nuq’el Q’umpel Mushe Pel’ush Ts’ishe Ts’ob Mushe_

#### Zero

The concept of zero, critical in K’ich'chik mathematics, is expressed as **Ts’amal** (“Shell” or “Empty”), reflecting its original representation.

## Ki'ichek Lexicon {#lexicon}

A scribe of [[affiliation-ixilankul|Ish'ilankul]] learns two things before a single glyph: the counts, and the endings. A word in [[skill-kicheklng|Ki'ichek]] names a thing, and an ending says who stands toward it: _p'ilam_ is a glyph, and the _p'ilambal_ is the one who serves the glyphs; _ts'en_ is the sun, the _ts'enmah_ is the lord who rules under it, and the _ts'enbal_ is the priest who keeps its count. The same handful of endings make every title in a K'ich'chik court, so a ruler's whole household can be read from its titles alone.

This page is the word-hoard of the [[place-kchchkcntnnt|K'ich'chik]] city-states: the words the language note uses, what each ending makes of them, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives to the continent's realms, gods, rites and scripts.

The sound of Ki'ichek is percussive. A stop may be said with a catch in the throat, and the catch alone is a glottal stop: _k'ayik_, _ne'_, _ish'bal_. Stress falls on the second-last syllable, and the five vowels never glide into one another.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited: a noun bare, a verb bare. A form that closes on a hyphen is a bound stem, which never stands alone. A word made of smaller parts names the pieces the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly what they make.

### Fields

```sql
SELECT name AS "Field", holds AS "What it holds"
FROM fields
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kicheklng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Building words {#building-words}

Two moves make every built word on this page. **A compound** sets two words side by side, the first naming what the second is about: _ne'_ water and _ts'ik_ above make _ne'ts'ik_, an island, what stands above the water. **An ending on a word** says what part the word plays, and the endings stand below. A title takes its ending last, so _ts'en_ with _-il_ and then _-bal_ is _ts'enilbal_, the prince, the one who serves what belongs to the sun.

### Suffixes

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Suffix", attaches AS "Stem", gloss AS "Makes", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(examples, e -> regexp_replace(e, '^(\S+) > ([^\s,;]+)', '_\1_ > _\2_')), ', '), ''), '—') AS "Example"
FROM affixes
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kicheklng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Words {#words}

### Sky, sun and the count of days

Four counts run at once, and each has its word: _ts'en_, the sun and its year; _ts'uqal_, the moon; _ish'ilan_, the stars; and _tq'an_, the count of 260 days that is also the word for an edge or a joining. A date is written as the meeting of several counts, and the fifty-two-year _K'ayik Kul'al_, the Living Flame, falls where _ts'en_ and _tq'an_ come back into phase. Zero, _ts'amal_, is a shell: the empty thing that holds a place.

### Water and land

Water is _ne'_, and the land is named by what water does to it: _ne'ts'ik_, an island, is what stands above the water, and _ne'ok_ is land or a river running with it. The sacred heights are _kul'qat_, the peak a pyramid is built to stand for, and every great road is an _ish'lan_.

### Living things

The great beasts give their names to peoples and realms: the jaguar, _ish'bal_; the eagle, _shak'nal_; the bird, _shelem_; the serpent, _ch'akun_. Flowers carry the light they bloom in, so the sun blossom is _pakum_ and the moon bloom _ish'mel_.

### Rank, temple and court

A title is a word with an ending: _-mah_ makes the lord of a thing and _-bal_ the one who serves it. So the _ts'enmah_ is the Sun Lord and the _ts'enbal_ the priest who keeps his count; the _p'ilambal_ serves the glyphs, the _ch'alishbal_ serves the sacred, and the _ch'umbal_ carries a lord's spoken word along the roads. A people's members are _ts'omalil_, those who belong by the _ts'om_, the bond.

```sql
SELECT form AS "Form", class AS "Class", gloss AS "Gloss", built_text AS "Built from", coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(attested, a -> '[[' || a || '|]]'), ', '), '—') AS "Attested"
FROM words
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kicheklng' AND field = 'Rank, temple and court' AND split_part(class, ' ', 1) IN ('name', 'stem', 'pref')
ORDER BY position
```

### Grammar words

Three short words say where a thing stands: _ch'al_ beneath it, _ts'ik_ above it, _shaq'al_ apart from it. The numbers count by twenties. One to ten are single words; eleven to nineteen are _olek'_, ten, followed by the unit as a second word, so _olek' lishe_ is thirteen. Above them stand the powers of twenty: _ts'ob_, _pel'ush_, _q'umpel_ and _sholemash_.

## Making names {#making-names}

**A K'ich'chik person** carries a compound of words, each element a word with its meaning, and the name reads as a short statement: _Ne' Ishkul_, water within the lake; _Tsul Ne'ok_, strength flows. A commoner carries two elements; three or more mark a noble, a priest or a person of great deeds, and such a name is shortened at home to its first element, never to a title or a connector. Titles are appended after the full name and never fused to it.

**A realm or a city** is named as a person is, by words set side by side: _Shelem_, the land of birds; _P'enqal Ch'akal_, the great grassland; _Ish'ilankul_, the seat of the stars, with _-kul_, the seat, closing the name of a city that rules.

**A god** is named by two words, a thing and its quality, and the Itsani faiths carry those names whole: _K'ayik Ch'alish_, the sacred flame; _Ts'en'ul Tq'an_, the sun at the world's edge.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of every note of the K'ich'chik lands and their gods. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'paths' THEN 'path' WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-kicheklng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'paths' THEN 0 WHEN 'addresses' THEN 1 END
```
