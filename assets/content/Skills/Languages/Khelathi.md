Khelathi is a tongue of the Khelathi (isolate) family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Khelathi, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Khelathi is the ancient and sacred tongue of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]], the monumental empire spanning the southern reaches of the [[place-xerathia|Zerathia]] continent. For over three millennia, Khelathi has served as the language of state, ritual, and sacred knowledge—deeply intertwined with the religious and magical traditions that form the bedrock of Khelathi civilization. Even as vernacular speech has evolved, the written and ritual languages remain largely frozen in their classical forms, creating a diglossia between the formal religious register and the spoken dialects of common life.

Khelathi is spoken by an estimated 8-12 million people across the Khelathi Empire and its trading colonies. It is also the liturgical tongue of several mystery cults and esoteric orders, giving it prestige and mystical authority far beyond its geographic boundaries.

## How Khelathi sounds

Khelathi is the old river-speech of the temples. It is heavy with _q_ and _z_ and the catch of the apostrophe.

**The vowels.** _a_, _e_ and _u_ lead. _au_ opens sacred names, as in _Au'Khelathu_.

**The consonants.** _q_, _z_, _l_, _kh_, _ch_, _g_, _t_, _n_, _m_ and _w_. The apostrophe marks the catch in the throat, at the seam of a compound and before a vowel. _ch_ is one sound, as in "church", and opens a word as freely as _kh_ does.

**Clusters.** Few. Syllables are mostly a consonant and a vowel, with _lq_ and _zn_ inside a word.

**Endings.** Khelathi words end in _-u_, _-et_, _-at_, _-aru_ or _-ir_. Titles stand before the name.

**What Khelathi leaves out.** It has no more than two apostrophes in a word and no word of more than four syllables. A compound written with apostrophes or dashes counts each part on its own, so every part has four syllables or fewer.

## Phonology

### Consonant System

Khelathi possesses a distinctive consonantal inventory with several features that create its characteristic timbre:

**Stops and Affricates:**

Unvoiced
: p, t, k

Voiced
: b, d, g

Uvular
: q (a k made far back in the throat)

Affricate
: ch (as in "church")

**Fricatives:**

Unvoiced
: f, th (as in "thin"), s, sh (palatal), kh (velar), h

Voiced
: z

**Nasals:** m, n

**Liquids & Semi-vowels:** l, r, w, y

**Distinctive Glottal:** the catch, a glottal stop that is phonemically significant. The spelling writes it with an apostrophe. Khelathi-zethu writes it as a sign of its own. Qalezu writes a catch before a consonant as three reed dabs in a triangle over that consonant, and keeps a catch sign of its own only where no consonant follows, as at the end of _Uqa'a_ or the start of _Au'Khelathu_.

**Gemination:** Double consonants appear frequently and carry grammatical weight (particularly in verbal and nominal derivations).

### Vowel System

Khelathi distinguishes three cardinal vowels, each with short and long variants:

**Vowels:** a, i and u, each short or long

**Secondary Vowel Reduction:** In rapid speech, unstressed vowels may flatten toward the "a" of "about", though this is not conventionally written in formal texts.

**Diphthongs:** ay, aw, iy occur but are often treated as vowel + glide sequences rather than true diphthongs.

**Vowels in Writing:** The Khelathi scripts record consonants and leave the vowels to the reader, so this inventory describes the language as it is spoken.

### Phonotactic Patterns

**A word ends in a vowel or in `n`, `t`, `s`, `r`, `l`, `z` or `th`.** Final vowels are frequent,
particularly in nominal and verbal inflections.

**The catch is written with an apostrophe, at the seam of a compound and before a
vowel.** It stands between a consonant and a vowel (`Weth'ur`, `Zu'Gezaru`),
between the parts of a compound (`Gar'Loru`) and between two vowels (`Qe'aret`),
and it gives a word a pause where a reader expects a consonant. That pause is what
a sacred or lineage name has and an ordinary one lacks—`Qe'aret` and `Saq'aru`
pause; `Mani` and `Dari` do not. A word that begins with a vowel carries no
apostrophe.

**Openings.** `An-`, `Am-`, `Re-`, `Wa-` and `Kh-` are the characteristic ones.
Initial clusters are otherwise rare and restricted to consonant + semi-vowel
(`y`, `w`) or fricative + stop: `Ps-` is the licensed instance, as in `Psaqa`.
`Ch-` is a single sound and opens a word freely, as in `Chelsur` and `Chaq'ur`.
Stop + stop is not Khelathi, so no word opens `Pt-`.

Syllable Structure
: CV or CVC; a word may begin with a vowel

Word Length
: Often moderate (two to three morphemes); compound names are less frequent than in [[skill-vedyarlng|Vedyari]], though hieratic epithets create lengthy formal designations

Stress Patterns
: Usually fall on the first or second syllable; later syllables receive weaker stress in polysyllabic words

## Grammar Notes

### Word Order

Khelathi follows a **Verb-Subject-Object (VSO)** order in narrative clauses, though marked deviations occur in dependent clauses and nominal sentences.

### Nominal System

- **Nouns** have number (singular/plural) and gender (masculine/feminine)
- **Definiteness:** The definite article (_en_, _et_) and demonstratives clearly mark specificity
- **Agreement:** Adjectives agree with nouns in gender and number

### Verbs

Verbs do not change. Each verb has one form, and the particle _agu_ and the order of the clause carry the rest.

### Agent nouns

A word takes _-ar_ to name the one who does it or handles it: _tegul_, divide, gives _tegular_, the butcher; _lagath_, guard, gives _lagathar_, the keeper; _mazel_, measure, gives _mazelar_, the surveyor; and _zugel_, harpoon, gives _zugelar_, the harpooner.

## Everyday speech

_Zuren nazu telu_ means “I see you”; _zuren telu nazu_ means “you see me”.
The verb comes first and keeps its form. _Wanel nazu zalet_ means “I am well”,
and _qamar nazu zamet_ means “I feel joy”. These ordinary utterances use the
speaker's own feeling; a sealed warrant or a paid account does not establish it.
_Nazu_ is I or me, _telu_ the person addressed, _saru_ he, _sari_ she, _qu_ it,
_naru_ they and _nazun_ we. _Telu_ leaves the addressee's number unstated.

_Qalret elu nazu_ is my cup, _qalret elu telu_ your cup and
_qalret elu nazun_ our cup. _Qamar nazu qalret_ means “I have a cup”;
_qamarun qalret ez nazu_ means “the cup belongs to me”. The kin expressions
_mathun elu talqar_ and _malen elu malen_ name a parent's mother and a child's
child. The named relative replaces the general kin word when the relationship
needs to be specified. _Meral_ names a romantic beloved; _merun_ names a spouse.

The short descriptions are given as whole expressions: _galmel thalen_ is a
long seat, _genar qarenar_ an adult man and _malen qarenar_ a boy or one's son.
The relation being discussed supplies the difference between a boy and a son.
_Genar mathenar_ is an adult woman, and _malen mathenar_ a girl or one's daughter.
_Mathun elu talqar_ can name a grandmother through either parent. A nephew is
_malen qarenar elu linetar_ through one's sister or
_malen qarenar elu linetun_ through one's brother; the corresponding female-child
expressions name nieces. An orphan's _malen khez talqar_ describes the loss of
living parents, rather than a judgement about birth or household rank.

_Khez zuren nazu telu_ means “I do not see you”. _Wanel nazu khez zuran_
describes blindness, while _wanel nazu khez khashel_ describes deafness.
The first is loss of sight and the second loss of hearing, rather than an
unseen object or an ignored speaker. _Zuret_ describes an object discernible
to an observer with sight; _khez zuret_ describes an object that observer
cannot discern. _Zurmur_ describes material one can see through, _qezmur_
material one cannot see through, and _zurmeth_ a visually distinct mark or outline.

_Khaleth nazu_ means “I inhale”, _khaluz nazu_ “I exhale” and
_khazuth nazu_ “I am suffocating”. Suffocation involves absent breathable air or
obstructed breathing. _Wanel nazu tamen_ means “I am full” after food or drink.
_Melqath sari_ means “she conceives”; _melath sari_ means “she gives birth”.
_Malzath sari malen_ means “she nurses a child at the breast”. _Qarnath saru_
means “he is growing older”. _Hazath saru nazu_ means “he hurts me”, while
_hazel nazu_ means “I hurt”. _Khez sheget_ describes loss of sensory awareness;
it says nothing by itself about a person's account or legal standing.

The heart expressions belong to felt life. _Halzi qemaz_ is contentment, a heart
at ease with what it has. _Hamret elu tamur qemaz_ is satisfaction at a desire
fulfilled. _Walet qarun ezu khazen_ names
courage through the image of retaining one's purpose while afraid.
_Walet nazu qarun ezu khazen_ means “I keep my purpose despite fear”. The
language also names resentment, envy and jealousy through their particular
objects; these expressions do not turn every account or obligation into anger.

_Amzel elu meral_ is romantic love, _amzel elu walun_ the affection of friends,
and _amzel elu talen_ family love. _Amzelu_ concerns everyone's good without
claiming repayment, advantage for one's house or credit after death. It differs
from _lamaz_, sexual appetite, and from the sacred giving of _nelgen_.
_Neralnet_ is companionship in shared company or activity, including company
without close friendship. _Qelmun_ names reliance, _qerun_ felt confidence and
_qenat_ truth; confidence can be misplaced.

_Nelaz nazu qalret ez telu_ means “I give you a cup”, and
_tuqel nazu qalret ez telu_ “I bring you a cup”. _Qalath nazu qalret az galar_
means “I put the cup at the house”. _Yarath nazu nyar galar_ means “I leave the
house”; _yalath nazu ez galar_ means “I arrive at the house”. _Yarqel nazu nyar
qaznar_ means “I flee from an enemy”. _Qelzath nazu galmun_ means “I lock the
door”. The lock is _qelzu_ and its key _qelzir_.

_Qaril galar_ means near the house, _qeril galar_ far from it, _qarmel galar_
beside it and _qarmath galar ta qedgar_ between the house and the mountain.
_Ezu galar_ is inside the house, _nazuqar galar_ outside it and _zagar galar_
beyond it. _Zar ez galar_ directs someone upward toward the house;
_nizar ez galar_ directs them downward toward it. _Zamar ez galar_ and
_nazar ez galar_ give forward and backward movement toward the named destination.
_Nyar zun_ means away from here. The place or reference object is named again
when “here” would leave the direction unclear.

_Ezu garel elu salet_ means in position seven; _ezu garel elu waqen_ in position
eight. A stated position number supplies further ordinals. _Gerun khul_ is the
listed count twenty. _Lazen elu khul_ selects either of a pair and
_khezan elu khul_ excludes both. _Qaril gerun_ is about ten,
_qaril nizar gerun_ almost ten, and _qen gerun_ exactly ten. The near-below
expression stays below the stated amount; it does not mean “a short time ago”.

_Wanel mazret elu qalret tazir shar mazret elu galmen_ means “the count of cups
is greater than the count of chairs”. _Wanel mazun elu qalu tazil shar mazun elu
qalmu_ means “the amount of water is less than the amount of drink”. _Tazir shar
temun zamat_ compares an item with every other item in the stated set to identify
the most; _tazil shar temun zamat_ identifies the least. With _leqet_ and
_laqet_, the same explicitly named set supplies best and worst. The comparison
can name people, counted objects or measured amounts as appropriate.
_Tazir shar mazun qemet_ is too much for the stated need; _khez qemet_ is too
little. _Wanel temun elu qenmet qezan_ means “the thing is of zero worth”.

_Az kalet lazen_ is once, _az kalet khul_ twice and _az kalet tazun_ repeatedly.
_Yalret_ marks recurrence rather than a count. _Ezu kalun elu shelun_ means during
a performance; _ezu kalun elu melnet_ during a birth. _Ezu kalun elu tamnet elu
nazu_ means while I eat, literally during the time of my eating. _Tamnet_ names
that activity as a noun. _Qethel melnet_ means until the birth and _narel melnet_
since the birth. Replace the named event with the actual starting or ending event.
_Telath nazu tamel_ means “I have already done it”, _thalal nazu tamel_ “I am
still doing it”, and _khez telath nazu tamel_ “I have not done it yet”. These
citations use finishing or continuing the named act; _agu_ keeps its force of an
undertaking. _Tharen mar tharen elu kalzu_ is yesterday and
_tharen mer tharen elu kalzu_ tomorrow.

_Zuren nazu qezunet_ means “I see myself”; _zuren sari qezunet_ “she sees
herself”. The reflexive object refers to the subject of its own clause.
_Yaler shel_ asks “who comes”; _zuren telu sher_ “what do you see”.
_Qalret shal_ selects “which cup” from a known set. _Alqar galar az sher garun_
asks where a house exists, _yaler telu az sher kalun_ when the addressee comes,
and _yarul telu ez qaran elu sher_ why they go. _Muzan telu galar qer elu sher_
asks how they build the house. _Wanel mazret elu qalret sher_ asks the count of
cups; _wanel mazun elu qalu sher_ the amount of water. Replace the counted object
or measured substance to ask about another quantity.

_Wanel qalret elu shel_ asks whose the cup is. _Nelaz telu qalret ez shel_
asks to whom the addressee gives a cup; _yaler telu nyar sher garun_ asks from
where they come. _Muzan telu galar ez sher qarun_ asks for what purpose they
build the house. _Qe zuren telu eqar_ asks “do you see this”. _Razal nazu sher
razel telu_ requests clarification of what the addressee says.

_Qenul_ affirms reality or sincerity. _Qerun zamat elu nazu_ avows the speaker's
complete assurance. _Zurnet elu nazu_ qualifies a claim by its observed appearance.
_Razel naru yaler saru_ means “they say he comes”: the coming stays inside the
report and need not be endorsed by the speaker. _Lan yaler saru_ means “perhaps
he comes”; _lan qerir yaler saru_ “he probably comes”; _lan qeril yaler saru_
“he is unlikely to come”. _Razqet_ is an intentionally deceptive statement;
_kezar khez qenet_ can be a mistaken claim without deception.

_Yaler telu ta yarul nazu_ means “you come and I go”; replacing _ta_ by _lu_
offers the alternatives, and by _thal_ contrasts them. _Khez yaler saru ta khez
yaler sari_ denies both arrivals: “he does not come, nor does she”.
_Qamaru yaler telu, tharun yarul nazu_ means “if you come, then I go”.
_Qamaru khez yaler telu, tharun yarul nazu_ means “unless you come, I go”.
The negation covers the complete condition. _Qamaru khez eqar, tharun yarul
nazu_ means “otherwise I go”, with _eqar_ referring to the preceding proposition.
_Thalamun wanel nazu zazet, yarul nazu_ means “although I am ill, I go”.
_Thar zazun_ names the obstacle “despite illness”. _Thal tharun yarul nazu_
means “nevertheless I go”. _Qalmu az garel elu temat_ names a drink instead of
food. _Yarul nazu ez qarun elu nelmat_ means “I go for the purpose of a gift”.

_Lu yaler telu lu yarul nazu_ means “either you come or I go”; _khez yaler telu
ta khez yarul nazu_ “neither you come nor I go”; _ta yaler telu ta yarul nazu_
“both you come and I go”. _Rethas nazu qe yaler telu lu khez yaler telu_ means
“I consider whether you come or do not come”. Both alternatives keep the same
subject. _Muzan telu galar qel nazu_ compares the addressee's building with the
speaker's: “you build a house as I do”. _Nyar zamat khez telu_ means except you;
_laz telu_ only you; _thaqel telu_ even you; _tan telu_ you also.

_Dunel nazu tarul_ means “I can walk” and _khez dunel nazu tarul_ “I cannot
walk”. _Lemel telu yarul_ gives permission to go. _Qelan nazu yarul_ states an
obligation to go; _qelan nazu khez yarul_ an obligation not to go.
_Khez qelan nazu yarul_ denies the obligation. _Leqamul telu shamel_ advises
sleep. The named action follows the person in these citations. _Lemath nazu
telu_ permits the addressee to do the act already under discussion;
_khelmath nazu telu_ prohibits it. _Lemet khez qelamet_ is both permitted and
not required; _khelmet_ is forbidden. _Lan yaler saru_ states a possible event
rather than granting someone leave to act.

The attested whole greeting _Khelathu elu anlagh_ remains in use. _Lagret ez
telu_ wishes safety on parting. A host's _wanel telu galmer_ receives someone
as a guest. _Qamar nazu remaz_ expresses thanks; _wazlat khez zeqat_ replies
by releasing any debt of thanks. _Qamar nazu tarmaz_ expresses regret;
_wazal telu nazu_ asks forgiveness. These are conventional spoken requests and
responses with the listed invariant words. _Lamethel_ makes a request courteous:
_lamethel khashath telu_, please listen. _Lamethel wathar_ asks room to pass or
forbearance for an intrusion. _Lamethel razel telu yalret_ asks that something be
said again. _Yalmet telu_, _yalem telu zun_, _yarul telu nyar zun_,
_lagel telu nazu_ and _thalet telu_ ask someone to wait, come here, go away,
help and stop. None depends on a new imperative ending.

_Rethulun nazu_ acknowledges understanding; _khez rethulun nazu_ acknowledges its
absence. _Razal nazu sher qaruth telu_ asks the intended meaning.
_Razal nazu qer elu sher razel telu eqar_ asks how to say this; the expression
being asked about replaces _eqar_. _Wanel rethan elu eqar sher_ asks the name of
this. _Qe qenul_ requests confirmation with conversational surprise.

_Thazit qemath_ is very small, _thazit qezathil_ tiny and _thazet qezathil_ huge.
_Thazit zamin_ is somewhat small and _thazit zammin_ slightly small. The tree
expression _malqat elu lagarun_ names wood as material, including branches and
fallen wood; _lagun_ keeps its ordinary use for timber.

The everyday list uses Qalezu. Existing sacred writings in Zethu keep their
recorded signs; an ordinary word's inclusion here does not assign it a new
hieroglyph.

## Script & Literacy

Khelathi is written in two hands, and the division between them is one of trade rather than of style. The sacred hand belongs to the temple and the tomb; Qalezu belongs to the counting-house, the tax roll and the contract. A scribe is trained into one of them, and training in one does not confer the other, so a priest who follows a mortuary text without effort may be unable to read a harbor manifest, and the clerk who wrote the manifest cannot read the wall behind him. A temple record and a tax record are therefore two separate errands, each needing a reader of its own, and whether a Khelathi is literate is the wrong question to ask of him: the question is which hand he was taught.

**Sacred Script ([[skill-khelthzscrpt|Khelathi-zethu Script]]):** The original writing system is hieroglyphic: about 470 signs, each standing for a concept, each drawn in two or three variants, about 1,100 drawn forms in all. Some signs also carry the consonants of the word they name and so spell words and names, and a sign set beside a word can show which word is meant. Certain signs carry a god's sense beside that god's name or in that god's temple, so a reading depends on context. This system is reserved for temple walls, royal monuments, and sacred texts; the difficulty of reading it in context keeps literacy the province of a trained priesthood.

**Everyday Script ([[skill-qalzscrscrpt|Qalezu Script]]):** An alphabet of 24 signs, reduced directly from the hieroglyphs about 800 years ago for administrative use. Each consonant sign comes from a hieroglyph whose word begins with that consonant, and the signs are written apart with a reed on papyrus. Qalezu is significantly faster and is learned in a season by scribes and tax officials, and by factors and traders who deal with them.

**Vowels and Foreign Spellings:** Neither hand records vowels. The phonetic signs carry consonants, and a reader supplies the vowels out of the word he already knows. The spellings other tongues give to Khelathi words are transliterations written in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], and the vowels a foreigner reads in one of them are a convenience his own scholars supply where the Khelathi scripts write nothing at all. A Khelathi says his name in the three vowels the language has; a foreigner writes it in the letters his own alphabet gives him, and the two need not agree.

**Literacy:** Formal training in sacred Khelathi script is restricted to the priesthood, royal scribes, and members of the esoteric orders. Among the nobility, perhaps one in five can read the Qalezu script, and a smaller number the sacred form. Professional scribes handle all written communication for the rest of the population; even guildsmen and merchants rely entirely on scribes for contracts and records. The gulf between the literate few and the non-literate majority is steep and has become a marker of social prestige and magical authority.

## Historical Development

### Proto-Khelathi

Khelathi is an isolate language with no widely-accepted external relatives. Its ancient origins are lost in myth and speculation. Some scholars propose that it represents a linguistic refuge—a language family that once spread widely but was supplanted by the expansion of other tongues, leaving Khelathi as its sole surviving member. Certain lexical and structural features show vague similarities to northern [[skill-okharclng|Okharik]], but these connections are tenuous and disputed.

### Classical Period (Age of Monuments)

The standardization of Classical Khelathi occurred during the early dynasties of the Khelathi Empire, roughly 2,300 years before present, under [Zab'Uzner]{.name data-type="being/npc"}, the fifth Gar-Au of the First House ([[lore-sacredhand|the Fixing of the Sacred Hand]]). This period saw the composition of the great temple inscriptions, the codification of ritual languages, and the establishment of scribal schools that have persisted to the present day. The language of this period—frozen in written form—remains the prestige register.

### Development of Qalezu

Around 800 years ago, the widening gap between the laborious sacred script and the practical needs of administration led to the development of Qalezu Khelathi, sanctioned by the queen [Gith'el'Retha I]{.name data-type="being/npc"} of [Amqelu'Gatau]{.name data-type="affiliation/lineage"} ([[lore-qalezusanction|the Sanction of Qalezu]]). While structurally identical to Classical Khelathi, Qalezu represents an orthographic reform rather than a linguistic one. However, its rapid adoption by the merchant class and administrative bureaucracies has gradually introduced vernacular features into what is written, accelerating the divergence between formal and colloquial speech.

### Modern Divergence

The spoken vernaculars of contemporary Khelathi show considerable divergence from Classical Khelathi:

Vowel Lengthening and Shortening
: Classical long/short distinctions are becoming phonetically eroded

Lexical Borrowing
: Trade languages, particularly maritime pidgins, have introduced numerous loanwords

Yet religious conservatism has resulted in Classical Khelathi being actively reinforced through education and ritual. The priesthood has successfully resisted reforms that would modernize the written system, maintaining the sanctity and untranslatability of the sacred texts.

## Regional Dialects

### Khelathi Proper (Core Regions)

- Closest to Classical Khelathi; the catch is kept at every seam
- Stress patterns adhere to classical norms
- Extensive ritualistic vocabulary related to temple and court

### Delta Dialect (Riverine Communities)

- Moderate divergence from Classical Khelathi
- The catch softens at the seams of compounds
- Specialized maritime and agricultural vocabulary

### Frontier Dialect (Border Regions & Trading Posts)

- Extreme simplification of its sounds
- Extensive code-switching with neighboring languages (particularly with Okharik and trade pidgins)
- Loss of gender
- Represents an early stage of creolization

## Learned Vocabulary

Two Khelathi words are the learned world's names for the undead, taken from the funerary texts of the [[affiliation-empireakhlth|Empire of Au'Khelathu]] by every scholarly tradition that copied them; see [[lore-undead|Undead]] for their use.

terebu
: root _t-r-b_, to wear, to cloak: _the cloaked one_, the sentient undead ([[being-tereb|tereb]]). Invariant in number: one _terebu_, many _terebu_.

dumatu
: root _d-m-t_, to drive, to goad: _the driven one_, the mindless undead ([[being-damut|damut]]). Invariant in number: one _dumatu_, many _dumatu_.

The temple form is the word as Khelathi speak and write it. _Tereb_ and _damut_ are what the rest of the world made of it: the international plural, _terebu_ and _damutu_, is the temple word as foreign ears set it down, and the international singular, _tereb_ ("TEH-reb") and _damut_ ("dah-MOOT"), is a back-formation from that plural. A Khelathi priest says _terebu_ for one and for many; a Vylarian physician says _tereb_ and _terebu_.

## Related Languages

Khelathi remains an isolate, yet areal contacts have created zones of lexical and structural influence:

Okharik
: Some mutual intelligibility in trade contexts; possible ancient contact is evidenced by certain cognates in ritual vocabulary

Vedyari
: Possible borrowings in domains of royal administration and diplomatic terminology, though the directionality and depth of contact remain unclear

Maritime Pidgins
: Khelathi has been a major substrate in the development of trade linguas along the Zerathian coast

## The Lexicon

This is what a Khelathi word is made of. An author coining a name works from
these tables and the rules above them, and a form built from anything else is not
Khelathi.

### Name elements

Eighteen elements build every given name and every house name. An element does not
stand alone as a word: it opens a compound or sits inside one, and the name it
builds is what a reader meets. Seven carry a longer form beside the short one, and
**the longer form is the one a house name takes**, since a house name ends in `-u`.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Element", coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(variants, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Longer form", sense AS "Sense"
FROM elements WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khelathlng' ORDER BY position
```

The openings `An-`, `Am-`, `Re-`, `Wa-` and `Kh-` carry much of the language's
character. They are phonology rather than lexicon, and they arrive through the
high-frequency elements—`anlagh`, `amqel`, `reth`, `waz`, `wal`, `anleth`—
rather than being chosen name by name.

**`Re-` and `Re-` carry `reth`, the name.** The sun-lord's house-form is `Uqa`, so
`Reqesehu`, `Rethur` and `Renute` all mean what they say, and a house ending
`'Relu` is named for a name and not for the sun.

### The hunt and its offices

The sacred hunt of _Zeghet'Nelgu_ has its own words. The beasts are _zagul_ the crocodile, _igelzut_ the hippopotamus and _igelqat_ the river cat; the gear is _zugel_ a harpoon, _shenulet_ a net, _tulaz_ a bow and _shebigel_ a reed boat. _Nelgat_ is an offering and _nelgu_ a mighty one; _zeghetar_ is a hunter.

An office is an agent in _-ar_ and, where it holds something, _elu_ and what it holds, as every construct does. The harpooner is _zugelar_ and the bearer is _tuqal_. The butcher of the offering is _tegular elu nelgat_, the one who divides the take. The keeper of the charter is _lagathar elu qathur_, and the scout of the marshes is _mazelar elu shebazet_, the same _mazelar_ that is a surveyor. The boat-steerer is the _igelar_, the boatman.

Two more words belong to the counting-house and the tomb: _qemul_, a cubit, and _zelzin_, a coffin, the place of the soul.

## Naming Traditions

### Cultural Significance

In Khelathi tradition, names are sacred utterances that encapsulate divine principles, royal genealogy, and magical potency. The bestowing of a name is a ritualistic act, often overseen by priests. Personal names are believed to carry the essence of the person and to establish sympathetic connections to the divine realm. Secret names, known only to the individual and the priesthood, are thought to confer protection and magical authority.

### Register

**The pause is the register.** A god, a throne, a great institution or a house
carries a glottal hiatus between two vowels, or a seam before a vowel. An
ordinary personal name carries neither, and a reader hears the difference without
being told: `Qe'aret` and `Reth'el'Leru` pause, `Aguri` and `Khelemur` do not.

So a name states a standing before it states anything else, and the standing
decides what follows the given name.

| Standing                                                    | How a person is named                     | Example                              |
| ----------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| `Iru'palu`, `Zabu`, `Zemelu`, priestly and scribal lineages | given + house                             | `Rethute Reth'el'Leru`               |
| `Zelar`                                                     | given + `ez` + place, village or district | `Aguri ez Zileti`, `Khelemur ez Iku` |
| Bondsman                                                    | given + `ez` + the estate that holds him  | `Gezebari ez Lut-Psaqa`              |
| Outcast, `Name Struck`                                      | given alone                               | `Ahmuze`, and nothing after it       |

**A commoner's byname never carries `-u`.** The collective is what makes a house
name a house name, and wearing one without the land, the shrine or the ancestor
behind it is a false claim rather than an affectation. That is why rank 0 in a
Khelathi body is `Name Struck`: a name carries legal weight here, so removing it
is a sentence.

A given name carries no seam and no hiatus whatever the standing. What rises with
standing is what comes after it.

### The Near Name

**A given name of four syllables or more has a near name**: the given name broken
off after its second vowel, with that vowel held long. A syllable is a vowel or a
run of vowels, so `Gezehutyu` has four and `Lersais` has two. Nothing is added and
nothing replaced; the near name is the opening of the given name, and the long
vowel is the voice holding the place where the rest of it would be. A vowel already
long stays as it is, so `Imhugepu` is `Imhu`.

```sql
SELECT '`' || near || '`' AS "Near name", '`' || given || '`' AS "Given name"
FROM near_names WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khelathlng' ORDER BY position
```

Names that open alike share a near name: `Amqelina`, `Amqelimun` and
`Amqelifu` are each `Amqe` to their own people. A name of three syllables or
fewer has no near name, because breaking it off at the second vowel leaves almost
all of it.

**The near name is never written.** Neither hand records vowels, and a reader
supplies them out of the word he already knows. A name broken off short is no word
he knows, so its consonants read back as some other name, or as none. A scribe
enters the given name, the temple account is kept under it, and
[[lore-readingweigh|the Reading at the Weighing]] reads it out. A contract made out
to a near name names nobody the archive holds.

**It belongs to the people a tie binds**—kin, the household, neighbors, a master
and the apprentice who lives under his roof. They are the people whose promises go
unentered, and the near name goes unentered for the same reason. Between strangers
it claims a tie that does not exist. A title always takes the given name:
`Thaz'Lekhau Anlaghafu`, never the near name after the title.

### Naming Patterns

**Given Names (Male):**

- Often incorporate theophoric elements (divine names prefixed or suffixed): _Ahmuze_ (devotion-blessed), _Imhugepu_ (he who arrives in stillness)
- Many reference royal titles, divine attributes, or favorable circumstances
- Frequently compound with elements meaning "true," "beloved," "strength," or "protection"
- Consonant-heavy phonology
- Written as a single word, however many elements they are built from, and never with the collective _-u_, which belongs to the house
- Examples: _Thulmes_ (moon-born), _Zinlegir_ (fair of soul)

**Given Names (Female):**

- Similarly theophoric; often invoke goddesses of motherhood, fertility, and magic
- Many end in long vowels (_-e, -a, -i_) which grammatically mark feminine gender
- Frequently incorporate feminine diminutive suffixes, creating terms of endearment
- Examples: _Anlaghesna_ (life-bearer), _Linya_ (fortunate), _Thiya_ (gift)

**Clan or House Names:**

- Typically elaborate compounds referencing mythological ancestors, founders, or place names, the seam between elements marked by the glottal and the following element capitalized
- Often include hieratic epithets or divine titles
- May reference the location of the family shrine or primary temple affiliation
- Carry the collective _-u_: a house is named as the people of its founder, its shrine or its land, so the plural is what a reader hears at the end of every one
- Examples: _Qar'kuzu_ (house of the balance, on `Qar`), _Zu'Zekenu_ (land of the ancestral realm), _Gar'Anlaghau_ (house of the living)

### Titles and Epithets

Formal address involves extensive titulature; individuals of rank may have five to ten titles reflecting their position, accomplishments, and piety. These are often written before the personal name and may be abbreviated in daily speech but elaborated in formal or religious contexts.

### Throne Names

A Gar-Au is crowned on [[place-yathtelgu|Yath-Telgu]] under a throne name, and the throne name dates
every contract, tax roll and temple record of the reign. **A throne name joins a
name element to a god's house-form across the seam**, and the seam is what marks
it: a given name never carries one, and a throne name always does. Before a
house-form that opens on a vowel the seam stands alone; before one that opens on a
consonant it takes the linking `el`, as a house name does.

| Throne name      | Built from               | Sense                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------ | ----------------------- |
| `Amqel'Uqa`      | `amqel` + `Uqa`          | beloved of the sun-lord |
| `Zab'Uzner`      | `zab` + `Uzner`          | lord of the healer      |
| `Anlagh'Uqa`     | `anlagh` + `Uqa`         | life of the sun-lord    |
| `Gez'el'Qar`     | `gez` + `el` + `Qar`     | enduring in order       |
| `Legir'el'Retha` | `legir` + `el` + `Retha` | fair in knowledge       |
| `Kuz'el'Psaqa`   | `quz` + `el` + `Psaqa`   | strong in the making    |

**A throne name is used again, and the king-lists number its bearers across the
whole list rather than within one house.** `Zab'Uzner` II reigned nearly two
thousand years after the first `Zab'Uzner`, in a house that had no tie to his.

A founder may keep his own given name as his throne name, seamless as it was, and
the house after him keeps it in his honor. Every Gar-Au of the reigning house has
been crowned `Meqes`, which is why the reigning Gar-Au is [[being-garaumeqesxv|Meqes XVI]].

### Institutional Names

A body of people is named from three parts: the **head-word** saying what kind
of body it is, the **work** it does or the **name** it is founded on, and whose
it is.

```
Lin'Zethu elu Au'Khelathu     the scribes' guild of the Empire
Lin'Melnu elu Galezkara       the smiths' guild of Galezkara
```

The head-word is the part a listener parses first, and it is the part that says
whether the body is a trade, a household, a priesthood or a court.

| Head-word | What it names                           | Example                         |
| --------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| _Gar-_    | a house: a family, a lineage, an office | _Gar-Sa'Akutu_, _Gar-Meglay_    |
| _Lin'_    | a sworn body of a trade                 | _Lin'Githar_, the weavers       |
| _Lut-_    | a mansion: a god's temple, or an estate | _Lut-Uzn'era_, _Lut-Mulu_       |
| _Lem'_    | an order, the servants of a god         | _Lem'Thuba'i_                   |
| _Genzet'_ | a court or council                      | _Genzet'Uzn'era_                |
| _Zab'_    | the lords of a thing                    | _Zab elu Au'Khelathu_           |
| _Zeghet'_ | a company that goes out                 | _Zeghet'Nelgu_, the sacred hunt |

**The linking word is _elu_.** It is the same element that links the halves of a
clan name—_Uqa'el'Leru_, the Uqa of Leru—standing free between a body and
whatever holds it. What follows _elu_ is a realm, a city, a god or a house:
_elu Au'Khelathu_ for a body chartered across the Empire, _elu Galezkara_ for
one that answers to a single city, _elu Reth'Sa'ar_ for one attached to a
temple.

**The same trade in two places is two bodies, not one.** _Lin'Melnu elu
Au'Khelathu_ is the imperial guild of metalworkers, chartered by the Gar-Au and
holding across every selat; _Lin'Melnu elu Galezkara_ is the smiths of the
capital, who answer to their own masters and to the city. A smith may belong to
both, to one, or to neither, and which he names when asked says a great deal
about him.

**A god's establishments share his name and differ by head-word.** The temple of
Uzn'era is _Lut-Uzn'era_; the priesthood serving in it is _Lem'Uzn'era_; the
council that governs its holdings is _Genzet'Uzn'era_. A speaker who says the
wrong one has said something else entirely, and the distinction matters in any
dispute over property, since the three hold different things.

**A house is _Gar-_ whether the tie is blood or office.** _Gar-Sa'Akutu_ is a
family; _Gar-Meglay_ is the frontier command; _Gar-Gezanu_ is the service that
collects the Gar-Au's dues. What they share is that each has a head who answers
for it, which is what the head-word asserts.

#### The Work-Words

These name what a body does, and stand between the head-word and _elu_.

| Word      | Trade                     | Word      | Trade                        |
| --------- | ------------------------- | --------- | ---------------------------- |
| _zethu_   | the written hand, scribes | _zuwaret_ | trade, merchants             |
| _zemnu_   | craft, artisans           | _melnu_   | the forge, metalworkers      |
| _githar_  | the loom, weavers         | _lagun_   | timber                       |
| _qenuwa_  | gold                      | _qelzu_   | the lock, locksmiths         |
| _zaglu_   | the made figure, toys     | _zamlu_   | music, minstrels             |
| _shelun_  | performance, players      | _legharu_ | the herb, apothecaries       |
| _zeghet_  | the hunt                  | _qeztu_   | war, mercenaries             |
| _igelu_   | the river, mariners       | _zegaru_  | the field, farmers           |
| _lutgar_  | the inn, innkeepers       | _genzet_  | the court, litigants         |
| _gezan_   | a weight of metal, debt   | _mazgaru_ | bulk, volume                 |
| _rethu_   | lore, scholars            | _lemu_    | service, servants            |
| _lemzabu_ | a great house's steward   | _qethar_  | the old way, traditionalists |

Several are the ordinary word doing double duty: _gezan_ is a weight of metal
before it is a debt, so _Lin'Gezan_ is heard as "the guild of the weighing" and
only then as the collectors; _mazgar_ is a measure of grain, so the merchants who
deal only in volume are _Mazgaru_ whatever they think of the name. _zaglu_ is the
funerary figure, which is why the toymakers carry it and why the trade is
thought slightly unlucky.

**The temple keeps an older word for some things the market also names.** A
tenth of a _gezan_ is a _qelu_ in the street and a _qezelet_ on an attestation,
and the two are the same weight of metal; which one a document uses says who
drew it up. The formal register is not a separate vocabulary but a handful of
such doublets, kept because the temples attest the weights and write their
records in the sacred hand.

**Foreigners keep their own word and put it after.** A Vylarian factor writes
_Lin'Zethu elu Au'Khelathu_ in a contract and says "the Scribes' Guild" in the
tavern, and both are understood. A Khelathi doing business abroad does the same
in reverse, which is how the trade tongues along the coast came by the words.
