Sinalë is a tongue of the Elder family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Sinalë, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Sinalë is the language of the **Sinalë** (known in human legends as "elves"), the eldest of the Elder Races to settle on Thalorna. The language predates all human tongues by millennia, surviving as living speech in scattered Sinalë communities hidden in deep forests and remote valleys. The Sinalë are vanishingly rare—fewer than one in ten thousand inhabitants of Thalorna—and their language is nearly as rare, known to outsiders only through legend and fragmentary encounter.

To the human ear, Sinalë is hauntingly beautiful: melodic and vowel-heavy, with frequent diphthongs and soft consonants that seem to flow like music. There are no harsh stops, no guttural sounds. Instead, the language glides between vowels, employing aspirated sounds and gentle fricatives. A Sinalë speaker seems to be singing rather than speaking, each phrase a cascade of cascades.

Sinalë grammar is complex despite its apparent simplicity. The language encodes subtle distinctions of mood, aspect, and philosophical stance through vowel gradation and consonantal softening rather than through inflectional suffixes. What appears to outsiders as beautiful poetry is often precise philosophical or temporal specification.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Sinalë consonantal inventory favors soft, flowing sounds:

- **Soft fricatives**: TH /d/ (voiced dental fricative), L, R (both rolled and soft)
- **Nasals**: M, N
- **Soft stops**: P, T, K (all unaspirated, gentle articulation)
- **Glides**: W, Y
- **Glottal stop** ('): Rare and marked, used for emphasis or at word boundaries in formal speech

**Consonant clusters are absent** from Sinalë. No word may contain two consonants in sequence. This contributes to the language's flowing, melodic character—every consonant is surrounded by vowels or glides.

### Vowels

Sinalë distinguishes **five basic vowels**, each with long and short forms that carry semantic weight:

- **A** (short, ah) / **Â** (long, ahhhh)—warm, open
- **E** (short, eh) / **Ê** (long, ehhhh)—gentle, refined
- **I** (short, ih) / **Î** (long, ihhhh)—bright, sharp
- **O** (short, oh) / **Ô** (long, ohhhh)—deep, resonant
- **U** (short, uh) / **Û** (long, uhhhh)—rounded, distant

**Diphthongs** are frequent and intentional: AE, AI, AU, EI, OU, etc. These are true two-vowel combinations, and shortening or lengthening either component changes meaning.

Example: _Aelindë_ (ah-eh-lin-deh) differs from _Âelindë_ (ahhhh-eh-lin-deh)—the first suggests movement or emergence; the second suggests eternal or infinite nature.

### Stress and Prosody

Stress is **unpredictable** in Sinalë—the language does not employ consistent stress patterns. Instead, meaning is encoded through **pitch, length, and vowel quality variation**. A word's semantic nuance is often conveyed by how softly or musically it is pronounced. Formal or ceremonial speech employs exaggerated pitch variation that approaches singing.

The language employs **tonal melody**: rising and falling pitches mark questions, emphasis, or transitions between ideas. To speak Sinalë poorly is to speak in monotone—the language requires musicality for full intelligibility.

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

Sinalë employs **Verb-Subject-Object (VSO)** word order, common among poetic languages:

- _Aelanthen Lúthion caladvae_—"Sings Lúthion of valleys" (Lúthion sings the valleys)

This word order places the action first, emphasizing what is done over who does it. The structure reflects Sinalë philosophical priorities: the action and its spiritual significance precede the actor.

### Verbs

Sinalë verbs do **not conjugate for tense**. Instead, they are inflected for **aspect (completed/ongoing/habitual) and mood (indicative/subjunctive/imperative)** through vowel gradation:

- **Ongoing action**: Root vowel remains short (e.g., _aelanthen_—"is singing")
- **Completed action**: Root vowel lengthens (e.g., _âelanthen_—"has sung")
- **Habitual action**: Root vowel diphthongizes (e.g., _aelanthen_—"sings habitually" when context is clear)

**Subjunctive mood** (wishes, possibilities) employs a soft fricative prefix:

- _Shaelanthen_—"might sing" / "would sing"

**Imperative** (commands) employs a glottal stop prefix:

- _'Aelanthen!_—"Sing!"

Temporal reference comes entirely from context or optional time words. A Sinalë poem about ancient events might contain no explicit temporal markers; the listener infers timing from narrative context.

### Nouns

Nouns do **not decline** for case, number, or gender. Plurality is optional and context-dependent:

- _Aelindër_ = "daughter" or "daughters" (singular and plural are identical)

**Gender** is implicit in the meaning rather than marked grammatically. The name _Arwenil_ is inherently feminine; _Eledhir_ is inherently masculine—not through suffix but through semantic association with the roots.

### Adjectives and Modifiers

Adjectives precede nouns in formal speech, though they may follow in poetry:

- _Míriel arathen_ = "Bright daughter" (formal order)
- _Arathen míriel_ = "Daughter bright" (poetic order)

Adverbs typically precede verbs:

- _Thîren aelanthen_ = "Softly sings" / "sings softly"

### Possessive Constructions

Possession is marked through word order and optional particles:

- _Lúthion caladwen_ = "Lúthion's sword" (genitive relationship, juxtaposition)
- _Caladwen Lúthion-ae_ = "Sword of Lúthion" (explicit possessive particle _-ae_)

## Script and Literacy

Sinalë is written in the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Calathindë]], a **flowing script** that resembles calligraphy. Glyphs are organic, curved, and highly stylized—each letter is an artwork. The script is written left-to-right, top-to-bottom, but the flowing nature of the letters creates a visual impression of music.

Key features:

- **Letter forms**: Curved, interconnected, resembling vines or flowing water
- **Diacritical marks**: Accents and dots indicate vowel length, stress variation, or poetic emphasis
- **Ligatures**: Frequent connections between letters create compound characters
- **Ornate variants**: Sacred texts employ extremely elaborate, decorated letter forms

Literacy in Sinalë is near-universal among the Sinalë themselves—the language and written tradition are central to their culture. However, human literacy in Sinalë is **extremely rare**. Only scholars, elves, and highly educated humans can read or write the language.

## Historical Development

Sinalë is the **eldest language on Thalorna**, older than even Kalihári or Ki'ichek. It has changed remarkably little in structure over millennia, though vocabulary has evolved with Sinalë experience and philosophy.

Scholars identify layers in Sinalë historical development:

- **Primordial layer** (oldest, rarely used in modern speech): Archaic verb forms, ancient color and elemental terminology
- **Classical layer** (main body of literature and ceremony): The form preserved in epic poems and sacred texts
- **Modern layer** (contemporary Sinalë speech): Philosophical and artistic vocabulary reflecting Sinalë concerns with meaning, beauty, and eternity

The language has **absorbed almost no borrowings** from other tongues. Sinalë pride in linguistic purity is legendary. When Sinalë encounter foreign concepts, they prefer to construct new words from Sinalë roots rather than adopt foreign terms.

## Regional Dialects

Sinalë is fragmented geographically, with scattered communities speaking distinct—though mutually intelligible—dialects:

- **Lothlandor dialect** (deep forests): The most conservative and archaic form, closest to ancient Sinalë
- **Highland dialect** (mountain valleys): Slightly more rapid speech, some pitch flattening, subtly different vowel qualities
- **Coastal variants** (rare, ancient settlements): Influenced by long isolation; some unique vocabulary and archaic constructions

A Sinalë from deep forests will sound archaic and formal to Sinalë from mountain settlements. The differences are subtle but noticeable to native speakers.

## Sample Phrases

- _Míriel aelanthen calathwen_—"Bright one sings of beautiful stones" (poetic blessing or greeting)
- _Shaelanthen caladwen lirendir_—"Might our swords sing starlight" (hope or prayer)
- _Arathen îalathen thêlindor_—"Daughters dance starlight falling" (joy or celebration)
- _Velastáron aelanthen finelë_—"From ancient times sings the white flame" (reference to timeless things)
- _Thairon caladeth shaelanthen_—"In valleys we would dwell singing" (longing or homesickness)

## Related Languages

Sinalë is one of the **two surviving Elder Tongues**, the other being Khazári. Both are descended from a **common Elder language** spoken by the Elder Races before they diverged into distinct peoples. This common ancestor is extinct; only the daughter languages survive.

The two Elder Tongues share a basic grammatical structure (VSO word order, inflection through vowel gradation) but diverged sharply in phonology:

- **Sinalë**: Vowel-heavy, flowing, melodic
- **Khazári**: Consonant-heavy, percussive, compact

To human ears, the languages sound completely unrelated. To speaker of Elder tongues, the kinship is obvious—a difference more of aesthetic emphasis than fundamental structure.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure and Philosophy

Sinalë names are **poetic compounds**, typically consisting of two or three syllables (sometimes more). Names are chosen for their beauty and symbolic resonance. Sinalë believe that a name carries spiritual weight—it shapes the bearer's destiny and soul.

### Given Names

Given names often reference:

- **Natural phenomena**: Light, stars, water, wind, stone, forests
- **Qualities or virtues**: Courage, wisdom, grace, beauty, steadfastness
- **Mythological or ancestral references**: Names of legendary figures or beloved ancestors

Examples:

- _Eledhir_—"star-wisdom" (one who carries starlight in thought)
- _Arwenil_—"noble grace" (grace born of noble lineage)
- _Lúthion_—"shadow-song" (song emerging from darkness, transformative)

### Surnames (Lineage Names)

Sinalë surnames are **poetic compounds** inherited through the **maternal line**. Surnames often reference:

- **Natural features**: Trees, stones, celestial bodies, weather phenomena
- **Qualities of light**: Brightness, dawn, starlight, moonlight
- **Abstract qualities**: Wisdom, music, song, dance

Examples:

- _Êlwethil_—family of starlight speakers ("star-whisper")
- _Ithilnén_—family connected to sacred waters under moonlight ("moon-water")
- _Celethloth_—family of grace and movement ("silver-leaf")
- _Lúthaelon_—family of hidden or nocturnal grace ("night-bloom")
- _Calenír_—family of joyful expression and revelation ("bright-song")

Surnames are inherited, not chosen, though a Sinalë who achieves great deeds may earn a new surname through legend. This is rare and deeply significant—it marks fundamental transformation.

### Gender Distinctions

Sinalë given names are **not strictly gendered**, though conventions exist:

- **Masculine-leaning**: Names ending in hard vowels (I, -on) or emphasizing action and strength
- **Feminine-leaning**: Names ending in soft vowels (-ie, -en, -eth) or emphasizing grace and subtlety

However, exceptions are common and celebrated. A female warrior might bear a traditionally masculine name; a male poet might bear a soft, traditionally feminine name.
