Sinale is a tongue of the Elder family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Sinale, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Sinale is the language of the **[[lore-flksinale|Sinale]]** (known in human legends as "elves"; one of them is a **Sinalo**), the eldest of the Elder Races to settle on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]]. The language predates all human tongues by millennia, surviving as living speech in scattered Sinale communities hidden in deep forests and remote valleys. The Sinale are vanishingly rare—fewer than one in ten thousand inhabitants of Thalorna—and their language is nearly as rare, known to outsiders only through legend and fragmentary encounter.

To the human ear, Sinale is hauntingly beautiful: melodic and vowel-heavy, with open syllables and consonants that seem to flow like water. Its consonants are clean and often long—_t_, _s_, _k_ and _n_ above all, with _l_ and _r_ running between them—and its vowels are the open _a_, _e_, _i_ and _o_, often held long. No word opens on a voiced stop and there are no guttural sounds. Nearly every syllable ends in a vowel, and the language slides from one to the next without ever stacking consonants at the front of a word. A Sinale speaker seems to be singing rather than speaking, and the song is a slow one: the language has the sound of a long memory, of starlight and twilight, and of things a long time lost.

What a human listener never notices is where the grammar sits. The work of saying how one word bears on another is done at the **front** of a word: a word that leans on another **wears down** at its head, and the worn consonant is the grammar. A Sinale hears the relation before the word is finished. A human hears a pretty variation and misses it entirely.

## How Sinale sounds

Sinale is the eldest tongue, sung as often as spoken. Its words are long and open, full of vowels, and its consonants are clean: the stops _t_, _k_ and _p_ beside _s_, _n_, _l_ and _r_, and nothing harsh.

**The vowels.** _a_ most often, then _i_, _e_, _u_ and _o_. A long vowel is written doubled (_nuuva_, _aamas_), and _ai_, _ei_, _oi_, _au_, _ou_, _ie_ and _uo_ are frequent (_huoma_, _toimu_, _eilava_). _y_ is a front vowel, said _i_ (_rivy_, _nelyve_). _ë_ marks a vowel sounded apart (_leësy_).

**The consonants.** _t_, _s_, _k_ and _n_ lead, then _l_, _r_, _p_, _m_, _v_ and _h_. A consonant is often long and written doubled: _tt_, _kk_, _ll_, _nn_, _ss_, _mm_ (_mollu_, _hekky_, _Kalpitti_).

**Clusters.** Two consonants meet only inside a word: a long consonant, a nasal and its stop (_nt_, _nk_, _mp_), or a liquid and a stop (_lt_, _lk_, _lp_, _rt_, _rk_). No word opens on two.

**Endings.** Most words end in a vowel. The rest end in _-n_, _-s_, _-l_ or _-r_.

**What Sinale leaves out.** It has no _th_, _rh_ or _w_, no _b_, _g_, _f_, _z_, _sh_ or _ch_, and no _d_ except after _l_, _n_ or _r_. _dh_ is rare and stands only between vowels.

## Phonology

### Consonants

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

_Dh_ is the voiced sound between the teeth, rare, and only between vowels. _D_ is the one voiced stop, and it stands only after a liquid or a nasal, where the tongue is already raised to it. There are no affricates and no back scrape—nothing a human would call harsh. _P_, _t_, _k_, _s_, _m_, _n_, _l_ and _r_ also have a **long** form, and the length is meaningful: _mollu_ is a deep pool and _molu_ is the colour of it.

### Vowels

Sinale has **seven vowels**, and each has a long form, so that length distinguishes words throughout. The vowels fall into three sets, and the sets govern the shape of every word:

```sql
SELECT '_' || pp.vowel || '_' AS "Vowel",
       CASE WHEN list_contains(harmony_back, pp.vowel) THEN 'back'
            WHEN list_contains(harmony_neutral, pp.vowel) THEN 'neutral' ELSE 'front' END AS "Set",
       '_' || pp.partner || '_' AS "In a suffix's front form"
FROM (SELECT unnest(harmony_partners) AS pp, harmony_back, harmony_neutral FROM syllables WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng')
ORDER BY list_position(['a', 'e', 'i', 'o', 'u', 'y', 'ë'], pp.vowel)
```

**A word never holds a back vowel and a front vowel together.** Neutral vowels stand in either kind, and a word built only of neutral vowels counts as back.

The front vowels are the language's rarer colour. Most words are back, neutral or a mixture of the two, so a suffix most often takes its back form, and a word that runs on _y_ and _ë_ stands out in a line as a bright word among dark ones.

**Suffixes are listed with their back and front forms**—_-ho_ beside _-he_, _-tos_ beside _-tes_—and the stem chooses the form. Use the listed forms rather than inventing an ending from the vowel table.

### Diphthongs

Two different vowels side by side are a diphthong and are said in one beat. Only these occur, and any other meeting of two vowels is not Sinale:

```sql
SELECT 'back' AS "Set", array_to_string(list_transform(list_filter(vowelPairs, p -> list_contains(harmony_back, p)), x -> '_' || x || '_'), ', ') AS "Diphthongs" FROM syllables WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng'
UNION ALL
SELECT 'neutral' AS "Set", array_to_string(list_transform(list_filter(vowelPairs, p -> list_contains(harmony_neutral, p)), x -> '_' || x || '_'), ', ') AS "Diphthongs" FROM syllables WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng'
UNION ALL
SELECT 'front' AS "Set", array_to_string(list_transform(list_filter(vowelPairs, p -> list_contains(harmony_front, p)), x -> '_' || x || '_'), ', ') AS "Diphthongs" FROM syllables WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng'
```

_uo_ and its front partner _ye_ open wide and close narrow, the reverse of every other pair. _ia_ and _eo_ open narrow and widen, and belong to the back set because their second vowel does.

### Medial clusters

**No word begins with a cluster.** Inside a word two consonants may stand together, never more than two, and only in these pairings. A digraph—_dh_ or _ng_—is one consonant:

```sql
SELECT what AS "Kind", array_to_string(list_transform(clusters, x -> '_' || x || '_'), ', ') AS "Clusters", use AS "Use"
FROM cluster_groups WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' ORDER BY position
```

**_d_ stands only after _n_, _l_ or _r_**, so it never begins a word, never ends one and never stands alone between vowels. _Dh_ stands only between vowels.

_nk_ is said with the back nasal.

The common pairings are the body of the language: a word holds one of them, or none, and its other syllables are open. The sparing ones are true Sinale and stand in old words, hard words and words for sharp things, but a line that holds more than one of them sounds clipped, and a poet avoids it.

### Onsets and finals

```sql
SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'openConsonant' THEN 'beginning a word' ELSE 'ending a word' END AS "Edge",
       'any vowel, or one of ' || array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '_' || x || '_'), ', ') AS "Sounds"
FROM edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' ORDER BY position
```

The back nasal _ng_ stands only between vowels, and no word opens on it.

### Stress

**Stress falls on the first syllable of every word, without exception.** So a Sinale word of any length can be said correctly on sight: _NUU-va_, _OL-tes-sa_, _SEL-kin-te_. Pitch does the work stress does in human tongues—rising for a question, falling to close a thought, held level through a list—and formal speech exaggerates it until it approaches singing. To speak Sinale in a monotone is not to speak it badly but to say something else.

### Wearing

The device that carries Sinale's grammar is **wearing**, _oltenne_—the softening of the consonant a word begins with when that word leans on another. A stop wears to the fricative made in the same place, and nothing else moves:

```sql
SELECT '_' || sound || '_' AS "Radical", '_' || becomes || '_' AS "Worn", example AS "Example"
FROM mutations WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' AND mutation = 'wearing' ORDER BY position
```

Every other beginning—_v_, _s_, _h_, _m_, _n_, _l_, _r_ and the vowels—is worn already and does not change. A name never wears. There is one grade of wearing and no other: a worn consonant never wears further.

### What wears

A word wears in these places, and it does not wear when it is the thing being spoken about:

| Where                                           | Example                                             |
| ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| a modifier standing before what it modifies     | _kalpi_ before _lonku_ gives _halpi lonku_          |
| a possessor, which is a modifier like any other | _kalpi_ gives _halpi eilava_ "sky of the pale wood" |
| the first stem of a compound                    | _kalpi_ and _lonku_ give _halpilonku_               |
| a noun in direct address                        | _kalpi_, called, is _halpi_                         |
| a verb after the negative particle              | _tupe_ "holds" gives _ahi supe_ "does not hold"     |
| a number multiplying the one after it           | _kevu_ "three" gives _hevu elki_ "sixty"            |

The consequence a reader can check by eye: **no worn form begins with _p_, _t_ or _k_.** A verb after _ahi_ never does.

A verb's object does not wear. A count is another exception: it follows the noun and stays unworn, as in _mollu kevu_ "three deep pools". Compare the worn multiplier in _hevu elki_ "sixty".

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

Sinale is **Verb-Subject-Object**:

_Yinse ehi pelvai_
: "I see the low cloud": _yinse_ is the act, _ehi_ the actor, and _pelvai_ the object. The object keeps its _p_.

The act comes first. Sinale rhetoric holds that naming the actor before the act is a way of taking credit, and formal speech avoids it.

### Verbs

Verbs do not mark person or tense. Time comes from context or from a word that says so.

_Serdattota ehii_ means "we remember" and can frame the following telling as memory. A poem about events ten thousand years past may carry no tense marker; the listener takes the time from that frame or from words such as _roselho_ "in the morning" and _unturoho_ "in winter".

Use each verb's stated meaning and make the actor and object clear:

| Clause                        | Reading                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _Korpe Kinsatti leësy vennas_ | "Kinsatti turns many paths": Kinsatti acts on the paths.                                              |
| _Paval kelkasi_               | "The cold spring opens": the spring is what opens.                                                    |
| _Lirta torvel_                | "The oak grows": the oak is what grows.                                                               |
| _Lirta ehii salpu vennas_     | "We grow many trees": we act on the trees.                                                            |
| _Raakke onsa melke kaltenho_  | "He takes water in a cup": _onsa_ is the actor and _melke_ the object; _kaltenho_ gives the location. |

In _Toimunu ilte onsa sirne sennave_, "his name's time is one year", _onsa sirne sennave_ is the time belonging to his name. The year measures time under that name, not the child's age.

### Number

Sinale counts the world the way it meets it. A noun for something met in quantity—leaves, stars, rain, grass, birds—is **collective** in its plain form, and a suffix picks out one of them: _nuuva_ is starlight and _nuuvanu_ a single star. A noun for a thing met singly—a person, a particular tree, a stone—is singular in its plain form, and the word _vennas_ "many" standing after it gives the plural: _mollu vennas_ "many deep pools". No suffix makes a plural: _leësy vennas_ means "many paths".

### Derivational suffixes

A closed set of suffixes makes new words from old ones. Each takes the back or front form its vowels call for, so a front word such as _rivy_ "the turning leaf" takes _-he_ where a back word takes _-ho_:

```sql
SELECT gloss AS "Makes", '_' || written || '_' AS "Back", '_' || coalesce(front, written) || '_' AS "Front", examples[1] AS "Example"
FROM affixes WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' AND kind IN ('derivation') ORDER BY position
```

### Compounds

Two stems make one word: the first is the modifier and wears, the second is the head. _Kalpi_ "pale wood" and _lonku_ "the deep wood" give _halpilonku_, a birch stand within the forest. Each stem keeps its own harmony, so a compound may join a back stem to a front one, and the suffix follows the last.

A **place name** is a compound of this kind, or a single stem with the place suffix. An enclave's own name is Sinale; the names human neighbours give enclaves are words in the neighbours' tongues.

### Adjectives and Modifiers

A modifier stands before what it modifies and **wears**:

_kalpi_ "pale wood" and _lonku_ "the deep wood" give _halpi lonku_
: "the pale-wood deep-wood", a stand of birch within the forest

_toimu_ "the turning year" and _liseri_ "still water" give _soimu liseri_
: "the year-turning water", a pool that freezes

A possessor also precedes its head: _onsa sirne_ is "his name". _Ehii ernatti Porkila Ilevanto_ means "Porkila Ilevanto is our kin": _ehii ernatti_ is the predicate, and the full name is the subject. _Ehii_ alone means "we"; it becomes a possessor through its relation to _ernatti_.

_Melke kalten_ is a "water cup", with water describing the cup; _melke kaltenho_ is "water in a cup", with _-ho_ expressing the location. The unchanging initial _m_ does not show the difference by itself.

Poetry may invert the order, and wearing goes with the word rather than the position. Many initials do not visibly change, so context and line grouping must still make the relation clear. Judge alliteration from the actual worn forms: _pelvai_ begins with _p_, but its modifying form _velvai_ begins with _v_.

### Address

A noun spoken to someone wears, so that a call can never be mistaken for a statement about the thing: _kalpi_ is the wood spoken of, _halpi_ the wood called. A name does not wear; the pitch of the call, falling at its end, tells it from talk about its bearer.

### Pronouns

The pronouns have three persons, each singular and collective, and no gender. A pronoun stands where a noun would. The collective lengthens the last vowel:

| Person | One    | More than one |
| ------ | ------ | ------------- |
| first  | _ehi_  | _ehii_        |
| second | _ovu_  | _ovuu_        |
| third  | _onsa_ | _onsaa_       |

### Being

There is no "is" in the present. Two words side by side, the one said of the other standing first as a verb would, make a statement: _lonku ehi_ "I am of the deep wood". The verb _eho_ says that a thing exists—_eho mollu_ "there is a deep pool".

### Negation

The particle _ahi_ stands before the verb, and the verb wears: _tupe_ "holds" gives _ahi supe_ "does not hold". A negated verb therefore never begins with _p_, _t_ or _k_: _ahi supe ehi kalten_ means "I do not hold a cup", with _tupe_ worn to _supe_ and the object _kalten_ unchanged.

### Questions

A question rises in pitch. A written question opens with the particle _uvi_, even when its pitch is annotated: _uvi tupe onsa_ "does it hold?".

### Joining words

_Evi_ "and" joins words and clauses alike. _Oso_ opens a relative clause and stands at its head, whatever the clause says of its noun: _mollu oso tupe_ "the pool that holds".

### Demonstratives

Three words point, by distance:

| Word  | Points to                      |
| ----- | ------------------------------ |
| _iha_ | this, here by me               |
| _eso_ | that, there by you             |
| _olu_ | that yonder, away from us both |

A demonstrative is a modifier and stands before its noun. All three open on a vowel, so none of them ever shows a worn consonant.

### Numbers

Sinale counts in twenties, with ten and fifteen as resting points along the way:

| Value | Word    |
| ----- | ------- |
| 1     | _ilte_  |
| 2     | _sipes_ |
| 3     | _kevu_  |
| 4     | _pemi_  |
| 5     | _letta_ |
| 6     | _louti_ |
| 7     | _ilpe_  |
| 8     | _lerre_ |
| 9     | _tumi_  |
| 10    | _tova_  |
| 15    | _ilto_  |
| 20    | _elki_  |

Eleven to fourteen are a unit on ten, set on ten with _-ho_: _ilte tovaho_ "one on ten" is eleven. Sixteen to nineteen are a unit on fifteen, _ilte iltoho_, except eighteen, which is _sipes tumi_ "two nines". Twenties are counted by a multiplier standing before _elki_ and wearing as any modifier does: _sipes elki_ is forty, _hevu elki_ sixty, and _tova elkiho_ "ten on twenty" is thirty. A count follows the thing counted and does not wear: _mollu kevu_ "three deep pools", _nuuvanu sipes_ "two stars".

### Comparison

The ending _-tos_ compares: what something exceeds is the place it stands out from. _Vautti kalpitos_ is "taller than the birch", tall from the pale wood.

## Script and Literacy

Sinale is written in the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]], a **flowing script** that resembles calligraphy. Glyphs are organic, curved, and highly stylized—each letter is an artwork. The script is written right to left, line below line, and the flowing nature of the letters creates a visual impression of music.

Key features:

Letter forms
: Curved, interconnected, resembling vines or flowing water

Vowel marks
: Vowels are not letters but marks set above the consonant they follow, so a word's consonants make its visible line and its vowels ride on top

Ligatures
: Frequent connections between letters create compound characters

Ornate variants
: Sacred texts employ extremely elaborate, decorated letter forms

The Veldikko is the Sinale's own featural script, made for this language and for no other, and the fit shows: each of the three places of articulation has its own family of letters, and within a family the stop, the fricative and the nasal are the same shape three times over, so a worn word looks worn. The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendi]] took the script from the Sinale, being the humans closest to them; the rest of [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia Region]] writes in [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron Script]], the Vylarian alphabet, and always has.

Literacy in Sinale is near-universal among the Sinale themselves—the language and written tradition are central to their culture. However, human literacy in Sinale is **extremely rare**. Only scholars, elves, and highly educated humans can read or write the language.

### Setting a romanized word back in the Veldikko

Every Sinale word on these pages is a romanization, written in the romanization for readers who do not have the hand. Setting one back into the Veldikko is not a matter of taste: the mapping below is fixed, so that two scribes working from the same romanized spelling produce the same page. What may differ between one page and another is the cut of the letterforms—an everyday hand, the elaborated sacred one, a carved inscription—and never which sign is which.

**The consonants are the visible line.** One sign to each consonant of the inventory, arranged in three families by place of articulation—the lips, the tongue-tip and the back of the mouth—so that the stop, the fricative and the nasal of one family are the same shape at three grades. The liquids and the soft sounds—_l_, _r_, _dh_ and _d_—stand outside the families and have signs of their own. Because wearing only ever moves a stop to the fricative of its own family, a worn word is the same shape one grade along—which is where the romanization is at its clumsiest, writing _t_ and _s_ as two unrelated letters for what the hand shows as one letter lowered. The back nasal has a sign like the rest, but no word opens on it and so it is never the first thing on a line.

**A doubled consonant is one sign and a bar.** The romanization writes a long consonant twice—_mollu_, and the _tt_, _ss_ and _rr_ of the phonology—while the Veldikko writes the sign once and sets a bar beneath it. _Mollu_ and _molu_ therefore differ in the hand by a single stroke, and a damaged page that has lost its bars has lost the difference between a deep pool and the colour of it.

**The vowels ride above.** Seven marks, one each for _a_, _e_, _i_, _o_, _u_, _y_ and _ë_, set over the consonant they follow. A word that begins with a vowel carries its mark on a bare carrier stroke, the one sign in the script that stands for no sound of its own. A doubled vowel in the romanization—_aa_, _eë_—is a single mark drawn long rather than two marks; a diphthong is two marks over one carrier, in the order they are said.

**Writing and performance.** Stress stays on the first syllable. Unmarked romanization omits the pitch that Veldikko may record; an unmarked line still has a tune when spoken. Optional caron, circumflex and acute notation records falling pitch, rising pitch and emphasis as set out in [[doc-prnncthlrn#sinale|Pronouncing Thalorna]]. A called name falls without wearing or shortening. The automatic Veldikko spelling rows preserve these performance annotations on their selected vowel nucleus, including long vowels and diphthongs. The distinct vowel _ë_ keeps its identity. An annotated surface form is registered explicitly in the lexicon, and its rendered reading is proofed in the native font. Ordinary Veldikko may leave pitch unwritten. The automatic map has no sentence-punctuation rows; an unmarked native passage uses language and line grouping, with editorial gaps outside the native font.

**The check that catches most copyists.** A word never holds a back vowel and a front vowel together, so every vowel mark in a word belongs to one set. A word carrying both is an error before it is anything else, and it is the first thing a Sinale reader sees.

**[[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]] has its own rows.** The Áelendan write the same letters in an older, plainer hand, unchanged in centuries, and their _g_ and aspirates take letters of their own, so a separate mapping holds them.

## Historical Development

Sinale is the **eldest language on Thalorna**, older than even [[skill-kalihrlng|Kalihari Language]] or [[skill-kiichek|Ki'i'chek Language]]. It has changed remarkably little in structure over millennia, though vocabulary has evolved with Sinale experience and philosophy.

Scholars identify layers in Sinale historical development:

- **Primordial layer** (oldest, rarely used in modern speech): Archaic verb forms, ancient colour and elemental terminology
- **Classical layer** (main body of literature and ceremony): The form preserved in epic poems and sacred texts
- **Modern layer** (contemporary Sinale speech): Philosophical and artistic vocabulary reflecting Sinale concerns with meaning, beauty, and eternity

The language has **absorbed almost no borrowings** from other tongues, and the reason is mechanical as much as cultural: a foreign word cannot be worn, cannot take an ending that agrees with it, and cannot be made to keep harmony with the rest of the sentence. It sits in a Sinale line like a stone in a stream. Sinale who need a word for a foreign thing build one from Sinale parts, and the coinage is heard as the language working properly rather than as an affectation.

### Words older than the rules

A handful of the oldest words keep shapes the regular language no longer makes: the people's name for itself, which holds a back vowel and a front one together, and a few names as old, which close on the hearth ending of the Primordial layer, _-nna_, or on its front form _-nne_, which that layer set after a stem of neutral vowels, and one woman's name as old as they are, which takes no ending of any kind. They are spoken as they have always been spoken, and nothing new is made on their pattern:

```sql
SELECT '_' || form || '_' AS "Form", coalesce(gloss, '—') AS "Gloss", coalesce(layer, '—') AS "Layer"
FROM older WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' ORDER BY position
```

The singular, _Sinalo_ "one of the Sinale", is not among them. It holds back and neutral vowels only and is a regular word of the language.

## Regional Dialects

Sinale is fragmented geographically, with scattered communities speaking distinct—though mutually intelligible—dialects, each named for the country it is spoken in:

- **Lonku** (the deep wood): The most conservative and archaic form, closest to ancient Sinale, and the slowest
- **Eilava** (the wide sky, the high valleys): Slightly more rapid speech, some flattening of pitch, subtly different vowel lengths
- **Lova** (the far bank, the old coastal settlements): Long isolated; some unique vocabulary and archaic constructions

Deep-wood speech sounds archaic and formal beside the speech of the high valleys. The differences are subtle but noticeable to native speakers.

## Writing a passage

Compose from the declared lexicon and grammar, then have the passage independently translated back into plain language. Check actors, objects, possessors and locations before judging the sound. A remembered event and the age of a name need their intended context, even when every word is valid.

Check the exact surface spellings used in the passage against the wordbook. It does not automatically derive Sinale's general wearing and suffix forms; the required variants must be stored explicitly. Register full foreign names both here and in their source language, as with _Hlusmil Svartrunar_ in Sinale and Nordmal. A suffixed place name also needs its used form; _Haulonnaho_ is already registered.

Finally inspect the actual rendered passage in its intended script and font, including line grouping. Lexical lookup cannot establish the meaning of a clause or the quality of its performance. The [authoring guide](https://git.pupluppy.org/HeroicLands/thalorna/src/branch/main/docs/writing-in-the-tongues.md) covers the lookup and rendering workflow.

## Sample Phrases

_Luuro nuuva liseriho_
: "Starlight sings on still water" (a greeting between friends, and the commonest one)

_Setty lenti talveho_
: "The tall grass stands in the low fog" (patience under a difficulty that will pass)

_Tupe tilvai hysse_
: "The seam of light holds the breath" (said when something long awaited has arrived)

_Pehe ovu huoma, pehe ovu nylki_
: "You give the first warmth, you give the deep root" (a blessing on a new house)

_Luuro ileva, luuro rivy_
: "The clear place sings, the turning leaf sings" (of a season and a place agreeing, and by extension of a thing done at the right moment)

## Related Languages

Sinale is one of the **two surviving Elder Tongues**, the other being [[skill-khazarlng|Khazari]]. Both descend from a **common Elder language** spoken before the Elder Races diverged. That ancestor is extinct and no text preserves it.

The kinship is not audible. Sinale runs on open syllables, long vowels and a clean inventory; Khazari runs on clusters, short vowels and back fricatives. Nothing in the sound of either suggests the other.

What the two share is a habit no human tongue on Thalorna has: **both make a new word by altering the body of an old one rather than by hanging a piece on its end.** Sinale changes the consonant a word begins with according to the work the word is doing; Khazari pours a different vowel frame through a fixed skeleton of consonants. Suffixes are the shallow layer in both—the deep one is inside the word.

Beyond that instinct the two agree on **a closed inventory of derivational shapes** rather than an open one, so neither language borrows readily.

Scholars debate whether the Elder Races deliberately drove their languages apart or whether geography and craft pulled them.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure and Philosophy

A Sinale carries a **given name** and a **lineage name**, and their endings separate them, so the two can never be confused:

1. A **given name** is a stem in its **radical** form with a naming ending. **A given name runs two to four syllables**, stressed on the first.
2. A **lineage name** is a stem in its **radical** form with the hearth ending. It means "the hearth of" the stem.

**The ending that would repeat the stem's own last consonant is not used**, so a stem ending in _-va_ takes _-tti_ rather than _-vo_, and **a stem whose last consonant is _n_ takes no hearth ending** and cannot found a lineage. Sinale find this last fact funny and outsiders find it arbitrary.

Sinale hold that a name carries spiritual weight and shapes what the bearer becomes, so a name is chosen slowly, sometimes over years, and a child goes unnamed in the meantime without anyone thinking it strange.


### Given Names

A stem is drawn from the world rather than from virtue: light, water, wood, weather, stone, the hours and the seasons. _Nuuva_ is starlight, _liseri_ still water, _rivy_ the turning leaf, _hysse_ the held breath, _tilvai_ the seam of light where a cloud parts. The name is that thing, with an ending that says who carries it:

- _Nuuvatti_ "starlight" (a man), _Nuuvala_ and _Nuuvatta_ (women)
- _Rivytti_, _Rivyle_, _Rivytte_—"the turning leaf"
- _Liseritti_, _Liserivo_, _Liserila_—"still water"

Two endings are available to each, and the choice is the namer's ear rather than a rule, so one household names a daughter _Selkitte_ and another names a daughter _Selkile_ from the same stem.

### Lineage Names

A lineage is a **hearth**, and its name is the thing the hearth keeps, closed with _-nto_ or _-nte_. _Kalpi_ "pale wood" gives **Kalpinto**; _tirvali_ "the long road" gives **Tirvalinto**; _pelvai_ "the low cloud" gives **Pelvainto**.

Lineage names descend through the **maternal line**. They are not chosen and not changed. A Sinale whose deeds no existing hearth can account for may found one, which happens perhaps once in an age and is the most consequential thing a Sinale can do.

A lineage name is glossed for outsiders and never translated into a foreign compound. What a hearth keeps is a Sinale thing, and the Sinale word for it is the only word that holds the whole of it.

### Gender Distinctions

The stems are not gendered and are freely shared: a brother and a sister are commonly named from the same stem, with different endings, and read as obviously a pair. Only the ending distinguishes them, and a Sinale who prefers the other set takes it without remark.

Lineage names have no gendered form.

### A Name Is Never Shortened

A Sinale is called by the whole given name, all the syllables of it, by a mother and by a stranger alike. The language has no shorter form to call by. Take the ending away and what is left is the stem, and the stem is the thing itself: _kalpi_ is the pale wood of the birch, so a Sinale who says _Kalpi_ is talking about trees, and _Kalpitti_ has not been called. Cut further and the syllable that remains names nothing at all. A name chosen slowly, sometimes over years, and held to shape what its bearer becomes is not trimmed for convenience, and four syllables stressed always on the first are no burden to a people who live as long as the Sinale do.

Closeness is heard in the call instead. A name spoken to its bearer takes the pitch of a call, falling at its end as a closed thought does, so every call already sounds different from talk about a person, and the pitch the speaker lays over the name carries the warmth, the summons or the reproach.

A Sinale known for something more carries it as an epithet, which stands after the full name and adds to it without taking its place. Among the Sinale a name grows by addition and never by subtraction.

Every word here obeys the rules the language page sets out—its sounds, its harmony and its wearing—so a word made from these by those rules is a Sinale word.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited: a noun in its plain form, a verb in its bare form. A word made of smaller parts names the words and suffixes the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form follows the language page's rules. Fixed lexical forms such as _kilpete_, _ansorun_ and _iltorin_ are retained as entries, not models for productive derivation.

## Making a word {#making-a-word}

Three moves make regular built words in this lexicon. Fixed lexical forms are learned as entries; their shapes do not create additional suffixes. Attested titles containing _-ren_ are also retained as whole titles, not examples of a productive genitive.

**A suffix.** A word takes one of the derivational suffixes the language page lists, in the form its vowels call for. _Lorrami_ "woven cloth" holds back and neutral vowels and takes _-nno_ to give _lorraminno_ "a weaver"; _symme_ "the art" holds front vowels and takes _-nne_ to give _symmenne_ "one who works the art".

**A compound.** Two words make one: the first describes the second and wears, the second keeps its own shape. _Kalpi_ "pale wood" and _lonku_ "the deep wood" give _halpilonku_ "a birch stand within the forest", the _k_ of the first worn to _h_. _Nuuva_ "starlight" and _talkaro_ "an age" give _nuuvatalkaro_ "the Age of Stars"; _nuuva_ opens on _n_, which is worn already, and does not change.

**A name.** A given name is a word in its plain form with a naming ending; a lineage name is the same word with the hearth ending. _Tirvali_ "the long road" gives the given names _Tirvalitti_ and _Tirvalitta_ and the lineage name _Tirvalinto_, "the hearth of the long road". The ending _-la_ is not used on _tirvali_, which already ends on _l_.

**No short name.** A given name has no short form. Taking the ending off leaves only the word for the thing—_Iltoritti_ without _-tti_ is _iltori_, a vision and not a person—so a Sinale is called by the whole name, on the pitch of a call, and the language page's naming section sets out why.

A new root word—one made from nothing in this lexicon—is a larger thing than a built one. It must keep every rule of sound and harmony, and it belongs in the table of its field with `—` in its Built from column.

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives a Sinale person, place, rite or thing, and every name another people gives to something the Sinale hold, with the note where it is met. A name built from this lexicon shows how; a name the Sinale use that is not yet built from it shows `—`.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Note",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + ') || CASE WHEN gloss = 'worn' THEN ', worn' ELSE '' END, '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-sinalelng' ORDER BY position
```
