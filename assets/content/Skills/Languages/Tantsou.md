Tantsou is a tonal tongue of single-syllable words. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Tantsou, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

The language is known natively as **Tantsou**, the name of the people (_tan_, celestial, and _tsou_, people) and of the country they hold. It is the common tongue of the [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou empire]]; [[skill-jurthatlng|Jurthati]] is its island daughter. This document gives the sounds, the grammar, the way names are built and the compound words of the court, the calendar and the [[lore-celestlrdr|Celestial Order]]. [[skill-tantsoulng#lexicon|The lexicon]] holds the full word-hoard.

## How Tantsou sounds

Tantsou is built of single syllables set side by side. Each syllable is short and complete.

**The vowels.** Syllables close on _-ou_, _-ei_, _-ai_, _-uo_ or _-ao_, or on _-ng_ or _-n_.

**The consonants.** _w_, _zh_, _ch_, _j_, _ts_, _sh_, _d_, _t_, _h_ and _l_ open syllables.

**Clusters.** None inside a syllable. A compound is two or three syllables written together.

**Endings.** Every syllable ends on a vowel, _-n_ or _-ng_.

**What Tantsou leaves out.** It has no tone marks: tone is not written, and a mark on a Tantsou word does not mark stress.

## Pronunciation Guide

The sound of Tantsou is sung. A syllable is an **initial**, a **final** and a **tone**. It opens on a consonant, rests on an open vowel or a gliding pair of vowels, and may close on a soft _-n_ or _-ng_. No hard stop ends a syllable, so speech runs on in a flowing line, and pitch carries meaning as much as the letters do.

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

Tone is contrastive: the same syllable in a different tone is a different word. Each syllable has one of four tones: high and level, rising from low to high, falling from high to low, or low and falling a little.

Each syllable keeps its own tone inside a compound, so _Zhazho_ (life-dragon) is said low, then falling.

## Grammar Notes

### Word Order

Tantsou is a **head-final, modifier-first** language. Adjectives, numbers and possessors precede nouns. The basic sentence order is **Subject–Object–Verb**, and in compound names and titles the pattern is **modifier + head noun**.

Sample phrases and sentences:

- _Rin zho_ = red (_rin_) + dragon (_zho_) > "a red dragon"
- _Jen chai_ = nine (_jen_) + mountain (_chai_) > "nine mountains"
- _Rong lei_ = myriad (_rong_) + spirit (_lei_) > "countless spirits"
- _You cheng._ = moon (_you_) + rises (_cheng_) > "The moon rises."
- _Zho teng cheng._ = dragon (_zho_) + sky (_teng_) + ascends (_cheng_) > "The dragon ascends the sky."
- _Lei wa._ = spirit (_lei_) + answers (_wa_) > "The spirit answers."
- _Shou jong._ = water (_shou_) + falls (_jong_) > "The water falls."
- _Dao chen tsou wa._ = great (_dao_) + emperor (_chen_) + people (_tsou_) + answers (_wa_) > "The people answer the great emperor."

### Compounding

The primary word-formation process is **compounding**: two or more single-syllable words set side by side, the first modifying the second. Nothing joins or alters them, and each keeps its tone.

- _ho_ (fire) + _sei_ (bureau) > _Ho Sei_ (Fire Bureau)
- _sing_ (death) + _wou_ (court) > _Sing Wou_ (Court of the Dead)
- _tui_ (iron) + _ja_ (blade) > _Tuija_ (Iron Blade, a personal name)

A compound that is a personal or place name, a god or a caste is written as a single word: _Tanzho_, _Wéizhuo_, _Raogui_. A descriptive title, an office or an institution keeps its words apart: _Teng Gui_ (Celestial Minister), _Lei Sei_ (Spirit Bureau).

### Suffixes

A small number of words close compounds so often that they act as suffixes:

| Suffix | Meaning               | Example                                                |
| ------ | --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| -tsou  | tribe, group, people  | _Noutsou_ (slaves), _Ha Tsou_ > _Hatsou_ (shadow folk) |

| -gui   | official, minister    | _Raogui_ (Thunder Lord), _Yaigui_ (Practitioner)       |
| -gen   | master, teacher       | _Leigen_ (Spirit Master), _Chougen_ (Lore Master)      |
| -zhuo  | lord, sovereign       | _Yozhuo_ (Demon Lord), _Wozhuo_ (War Lord)             |
| -léi   | spirit                | _Nuoléi_ (Decay Spirit), _Faléi_ (Fortune Spirit)      |
| -you   | month                 | _Hoyou_ (Fire Month), _Touyou_ (Earth Month)           |
| -jui   | festival              | _Hou Lei Jui_ (Harmony Festival)                       |

### Plurals

Tantsou does not mark plurality on nouns. Context says whether a word means one or many. The word _rong_ (ten thousand, a myriad) set before a noun gives the sense of "countless": _Rong Lei_ = "Ten Thousand Spirits."

### Honorifics and Titles

Titles follow the name: _Tanzho Wozhuo_ = Tanzho + "Sacred Lord" (a title). In formal address the word _dao_ (great, supreme) may be prefixed: _Daonéi_ (Great Sovereign), _Daoyai_ (Grand Physician).

## Script & Literacy

Tantsou employs a **logographic writing system** in which each character represents a morpheme. Characters are organized by semantic radicals and may be combined into compound characters mirroring the language's compounding grammar. Because a character stands for a meaning, the same character serves several words that differ only in tone. The system is extraordinarily rich but demands years of intensive study.

**Formal Script ([[skill-tengchouscript|Teng Chou]], "Celestial Record"):** The standard script used in imperial documents, religious texts, and classical literature. Characters are drawn with precise, angular strokes. Approximately 3,000-4,000 characters are required for general educated reading; scholars may master 6,000 or more.

**Cursive Script ([[skill-tengchouscript|Dou Chou]], "Flowing Record"):** A more rapid, connected hand used for administrative and mercantile purposes. Simplified character forms reduce the stroke count while preserving legibility for trained scribes.

**Literacy:** True literacy—the ability to read and compose continuous text—is confined to the nobility (30-50%), the priesthood, the imperial bureaucracy, and professional scribes. Formal education in classical characters requires 10-15 years of study. Even in major urban centers, most merchants and guildsmen possess only functional recognition of common characters—enough for trade marks, numerals, and familiar signs—and rely on scribes for contracts and correspondence. The vast majority of the population is non-literate.

## Historical Development

Tantsou is a language isolate with no widely-accepted external relatives. Older forms of the language, preserved in bronze inscriptions and classical texts, show considerable similarity to the modern tongue, a testament to the language's structural conservatism and the continuous scholarly tradition that preserves its forms.

**Classical Period (Age of Dynasties, ~2,300 years ago):** The standardization of the classical language occurred during the early dynasties of the empire. The language of this period is preserved in classical texts and bronze inscriptions, and remains largely comprehensible to educated modern speakers.

**Literary Standardization (~1,200 years ago):** A major literary standardization fixed the characters and their meanings in the _[Dao Chou]{.name data-type="lore"}_ ("Great Record"). This standardization has proven remarkably stable; scholars can read texts 2,000 years old with only minor difficulty.

**Modern Tantsou:** The spoken language has undergone some phonological change—drifting tones, minor vowel shifts—but the conservatism of the writing system and the prestige of classical education have kept the language extraordinarily stable.

## Regional Dialects

**Imperial Dialect:** The prestige standard, spoken in the capital and taught in formal education. Keeps all four tones and every classical grammatical form.

**Northern Tantsou:** The speech of the northern marches along the [[place-grkrhlmrgn|Grukarholm]] frontier, moderately divergent. Some vowel mergers and simplified compound forms. More borrowing from the neighboring steppe languages.

**Southern Tantsou:** Spoken in the agricultural and mercantile south. Tones lie closer together, speech rhythms run faster, and trade languages lend more words.

**Coastal Trade Dialect:** Extreme simplification for port use. Heavy code-switching with maritime pidgins. Approaching creolization in some trading communities.

## Naming Traditions

In Tantsou tradition, names are auspicious utterances invoking favorable qualities, celestial principles, or virtuous ancestors. The bestowing of a name is an occasion of considerable importance, often conducted by a learned scholar or priest who consults astrological and classical texts.

Names follow a **family-name-first pattern**. The family name (_zhao_) precedes the given name, creating names of two to four compound elements. Given names are constructed from the vocabulary through compounding—a name like _Tuija_ ("Iron Blade") or _Léngluo_ ("Bright Lotus") is simultaneously a name and a meaningful phrase. A name is written as one word, and every syllable carries its tone.

**Male given names** often incorporate elements associated with strength, virtue, or celestial forces: _dao_ (great), _wuo_ (warrior), _zho_ (dragon), _jing_ (gold), _chei_ (scholar), _rao_ (thunder).

**Female given names** often reference beauty, nature, or grace: _luo_ (lotus), _you_ (moon), _zhi_ (star), _wei_ (jade), _leng_ (radiant), _mei_ (dream).

**Family names** are typically monosyllabic and extremely stable across generations, derived from place names, occupations, or ancient clan affiliations.

## Tantsou Lexicon {#lexicon}

In local tradition, the Celestial Order of [[affiliation-tantsouempr|Tantsou]] calls itself the _Téngwou Rong Lei_, the celestial court of a myriad spirits, and every piece of that name is a word a farmer uses: _teng_ is the sky over his field, _wou_ the magistrate's hall, _rong_ the count past counting, _lei_ the breath in his chest. Tantsou builds its names by setting such words side by side, and the empire's castes, gods, bureaus, months and generals are all made this way. This page is the word-hoard of the tongue: the words, the rules a new word is built by, and the register of every name the setting gives in Tantsou, and in the Celestial Order.

A Tantsou word is a single syllable, and a compound reads modifier first: _zha_ is life and _zho_ is the dragon, so _Zhazho_, life-dragon, is the dragon-born caste of the Emperor's family. A name or a caste is written as one word; a title, an office or an institution is written as separate words, as in _Teng Gui_, the celestial minister.

The sound of Tantsou is sung. Every syllable opens on a consonant, rests on an open vowel or a gliding pair of vowels, and may close on a soft _-n_ or _-ng_; no hard stop ends a word. Pitch carries meaning: each syllable has one of four tones, and two words that differ only in tone are different words, as _wen_, the centre, and _wuen_, five.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited. A word made of smaller parts names the words the form is made of, joined by `+`, and the form is exactly those words set side by side.

### Fields

```sql
SELECT name AS "Field", holds AS "What it holds"
FROM fields
WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng'
ORDER BY position
```

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

### Word edges

A word opens on a single consonant and closes on a vowel or on _n_ or _ng_. A compound sets the close of one syllable against the opening of the next, so two consonants may meet inside a word.

```sql
SELECT CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may open a word' WHEN 'closeConsonant' THEN 'Consonants that close a word' WHEN 'closeCluster' THEN 'Pairs that may close a word' END AS "Edge", coalesce(nullif(array_to_string(list_transform(sounds, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), ''), CASE edge WHEN 'openCluster' THEN '—' WHEN 'closeCluster' THEN '—' ELSE '—' END) AS "Sounds"
FROM edges WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng' ORDER BY position
```

### Clusters

```sql
SELECT 'Most consonants together inside a word' AS "Rule", max AS "Count"
FROM clusters WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng'
```

## Building words {#building-words}

One move makes nearly every built word on this page: **two words set side by side, the modifier first.** _Ho_ is fire and _you_ is the month, so _Hoyou_ is the fire month. A handful of words close compounds so often that this note lists them as suffixes: _-tsou_ for a people or group, _-wo_ and _-léi_ for a spirit, _-gui_ for an official, _-gen_ for a master, _-zhuo_ for a lord, _-you_ for a month, _-jui_ for a festival, _-chai_ for a mountain or place, _-wou_ for a court and _-séi_ for a bureau. Each is the plain word, and each is set on its first word unchanged.

Nothing joins or alters the two words, each syllable keeps its own tone, and the tongue marks no plural: _rong_, ten thousand, set before a noun, gives the sense of countless.

## Making names {#making-names}

**A person** of Tantsou carries the house name first and the given name after it. The house name is a single word, older than any record: this note lists _Tan_, _Wei_, _Jing_, _Shou_ and their like. Most people never hold a personal name; one granted for service is a compound of two to four words, a man's drawn from strength and the sky, a woman's from flowers, the moon and the stars. [[lore-personlnms|Personal Names]] gives the grant.

**A god, a caste or a place** is a compound written as one word: _Tanzho_, the celestial dragon; _Wohou_, the warrior-nobles; _Jenchai_, the nine mountains. **A title, an office or an institution** keeps its words apart: _Ho Sei_, the fire bureau; _Teng Gui_, the celestial minister.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of every note of Tantsou and the Celestial Order. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'folders' THEN 'folder' WHEN 'cultures' THEN 'culture' WHEN 'lore' THEN 'lore' WHEN 'parents' THEN 'parent' WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'folders' THEN 0 WHEN 'cultures' THEN 1 WHEN 'lore' THEN 2 WHEN 'parents' THEN 3 WHEN 'addresses' THEN 4 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives a person, place, polity, god, caste, office or thing of Tantsou and the Celestial Order, with the note where it is met. A name built from this page shows how; a name the tongue uses that is not built from it shows `—`, and a name in the reader's tongue is tagged as such.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning || coalesce(CASE tag WHEN 'tanvuri' THEN ', [[' || tongue_language || '|Tantsou]]' END, '') AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + '), '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng' ORDER BY position
```

## Retired names {#retired-names}

A retired name is one the setting does not write: a word or a name of an Earth language, or a spelling the syllable rules of this page refuse. Each stands below with the name written in its place, and no page writes it, however accented. A `terran_analog` comment names the Earth source on purpose and is not read.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-tantsoulng' ORDER BY position
```
