Khazari is a tongue of the Elder family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Khazari, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Khazari is the language of the **[[lore-flkkhazar|Khazari]]** (known in human legends as "dwarves"), the second of the Elder Races and one of the oldest peoples on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]]. The Khazari are vanishingly rare—fewer than one in ten thousand inhabitants of Thalorna—and their language is nearly as rare, spoken only in isolated mountain strongholds and passed down within closely-guarded family traditions.

To the human ear, Khazari sounds hard and percussive: short, consonant-heavy words that crack and resound like hammer-blows on stone. There are few long vowels and few flowing transitions. The language runs on sharp stops, rolled liquids and back fricatives that carry along a passage. A Khazari speaker sounds strong and commanding even when speaking softly.

What makes the language strange to a human ear is not its sound but its build. A Khazari word is not a stem with endings glued on. It is a **skeleton of three consonants**, carrying the meaning, into which a **frame of vowels** is poured, carrying the grammar. The same three consonants yield the stone, the mason, the quarrying and the quarry, and a word never heard before is still readable from its skeleton. Where a human tongue makes a new word by hanging a piece on the end of an old one, Khazari makes it by pouring a different frame through the same skeleton.

## How Khazari sounds

Khazari is short and hard, a stone-cutter's tongue. Most of its words have two syllables, many have one, and few have three; nearly every word ends on a consonant.

**The vowels.** _a_ most often, then _u_ and _i_. _e_ and _o_ are rare: they stand in a few short grammar words (_ves_, _vog_, _tob_, _bep_), in the gender and plural endings _-ev_, _-od_ and _-ez_, and in the two rarer given-name charges, and never in a frame. One acute marks a stressed final syllable, as in _dulák_.

**The consonants.** The plain stops lead: _b_, _d_, _g_ and _k_ most, then _t_ and _p_. _l_, _r_, _m_, _n_, _z_ and _s_ are common. _th_, _dh_, _gh_ and _kh_ give the colour and are used less, _th_ most often of them; _v_ and _f_ are rarer still. There is no _sh_.

**Clusters.** Two consonants close a syllable, as _lg_, _mg_, _mk_, _lk_, _lb_, _rg_, _nd_ and _ld_ do, and less often _lth_, _nth_, _rv_ and _nv_. No word opens on two.

**Endings.** A word ends on a consonant: on its skeleton's last consonant (_dalk_, _dulák_, _Ramak_), on _-ir_ or _-ath_, or on a man's _-an_, _-in_ or _-un_.

**Built names.** A name of two skeletons is written as two parts joined by a dash, the thing first and the work second: _Dalk-thumár_, _Magar-thubád_. Each part is one or two syllables, and only the second carries the acute.

**What Khazari leaves out.** No native word ends on a vowel except the particle _zi_, no part of a word has more than three syllables, and no frame has _e_ or _o_.

**Said aloud.** In speech the short words come forward: the pronouns, the particles and the bare nouns are single blows, and the longer words fall between them with the stress where the acute stands. The gate-cry is three such blows and a built name, _Dalk. Bant. Tanv-guráz._ (DALK! BANT! TANV-gu-RÁZ!), and the toast at a finished work is two words that share their skeleton, _Bamur bamír._ (BAMUR ba-MÍR!), "the work is well made". More are in the [[skill-khazarlng#phrasebook|phrasebook]].

## Phonology

### Consonants

Its words are written in the setting's one guide; see [[doc-prnncthlrn|Pronouncing Thalorna]].

The four digraphs—_th_, _dh_, _kh_, _gh_—are single sounds and count as single consonants everywhere in the grammar below. There is no `sh`: the hushing sound does not exist in Khazari, and a Khazari saying a foreign name that has one says `s`. A Khazari speaker drops a foreign name's opening vowel, since no Khazari word begins with a vowel, so _Árdavon_ is said _Rdavon_. **Consonant clusters are common**, which is unusual on Thalorna: _thamur_, _bant_, _zamd_ and _maln_ are ordinary words, and the density is the point. More meaning travels per syllable than any human tongue manages.

### Vowels

Khazari has **five vowels**, and strongly prefers them short. _e_ and _o_ are rare and stand in no frame. A long vowel is written with an acute, is uncommon, and never occurs by accident: a long vowel is part of a grammatical frame and is always doing work. No word carries more than one, and no other mark is written.

Khazari has no diphthongs. Two vowels never stand together, because a native word ends on a consonant and a suffix that begins with a vowel follows it directly.

### Stress

**Stress follows the spelling guide: a word of two syllables is stressed on the first, a longer word on the second-to-last, and an acute marks the last syllable when the rule would not choose it.** There are no exceptions and no secondary stresses, so any Khazari word can be said correctly on sight: _dalk_ (DALK), _Khalvan_ (KHAL-van), _Kavalan_ (ka-VA-lan), _dulák_ (du-LÁK). The two parts of a dashed name are each said by the rule: _dalk-thumár_ (DALK-thu-MÁR).

### Clusters

No word opens on two consonants. A dash joins the two parts of a built name. Inside a word no more than two consonants stand together—a doubled consonant counts as one—and a word ends on at most two.

One rule governs every frame. **The second and third consonants of a skeleton stand together only when the second is one of five; otherwise a short _a_ stands between them.** No frame can override it. A bare or _hu-_ frame whose skeleton has _m_, _n_ or _s_ second and _r_, _m_ or _n_ third takes a short _u_ between them (_thamur_).

```sql
SELECT CASE WHEN second = ['any'] THEN 'any other' ELSE array_to_string(list_transform(second, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' ') END AS "Second consonant of the skeleton",
       CASE WHEN "between" IS NULL THEN 'nothing: _dalk_, _ramk_' ELSE '`' || "between" || '`' || ': _kaval_' END AS "Between the second and third"
FROM skeleton WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Skeletons and Frames

### Skeletons

Meaning in Khazari lives in a **skeleton**—a _marg_, literally a bone—of exactly three consonants, written with hyphens between them, always in a code span, so a dashed word in italics, such as _dalk-thumár_, is a built name of two parts and never a skeleton. A skeleton is never spoken on its own. To say it, a speaker pours a **frame**—a _vald_, a binding—of vowels through it, and the frame says what kind of word comes out. The skeletons the examples on this page are built from:

```sql
SELECT '`' || root || '`' AS "Skeleton", sense AS "Sense"
FROM roots WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' AND list_contains(['r-m-k', 'd-l-k', 'th-m-r', 'kh-l-v', 'k-v-l', 'v-l-d', 'v-r-n', 'd-n-k', 'z-m-d', 's-m-d', 'b-n-t', 'm-r-g', 'm-l-n', 'g-l-d', 'h-l-b', 'l-k-m', 't-n-v', 'g-r-z', 'n-l-p', 'z-v-k', 's-m-g', 'kh-s-p', 'l-m-v'], root)
ORDER BY list_position(['r-m-k', 'd-l-k', 'th-m-r', 'kh-l-v', 'k-v-l', 'v-l-d', 'v-r-n', 'd-n-k', 'z-m-d', 's-m-d', 'b-n-t', 'm-r-g', 'm-l-n', 'g-l-d', 'h-l-b', 'l-k-m', 't-n-v', 'g-r-z', 'n-l-p', 'z-v-k', 's-m-g', 'kh-s-p', 'l-m-v'], root)
```

Every skeleton the language is known to use, with the words its frames make, is set out in the [[skill-khazarlng#lexicon|Khazari Lexicon]].

### Frames

The frames are taught on the model skeleton **r-m-k**, "to lay a course of stone". In the shape column, `K1`, `K2` and `K3` stand for the skeleton's three consonants, and `V` for the vowel a given name chooses:

```sql
SELECT '**' || frame || '**' AS "Frame", '`' || replace(shape, ' + ', '` + `') || '`' AS "Shape",
       array_to_string(class, ', ') AS "Class", makes AS "What it makes",
       coalesce('_' || list_filter(examples, e -> e.root = 'r-m-k')[1].form || '_', '—') AS "On r-m-k",
       coalesce('_' || list_filter(examples, e -> e.root = 'd-l-k')[1].form || '_', '—') AS "On d-l-k",
       coalesce('_' || list_filter(examples, e -> e.root = 'th-m-r')[1].form || '_', '—') AS "On th-m-r"
FROM frames WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```

So from **d-l-k** alone: _dalk_ "stone", _dalkir_ "mason", _dulák_ "the cutting of stone", _hudalk_ "quarry", _dilkath_ "a dressed block". A Khazari hearing _dalkir_ for the first time does not learn a word; he recognises a skeleton in a frame he already knows.

The adjective frame makes a word distinct from the bare noun on the same skeleton, so _khalv_ is "the unlit", a thing, and _khalív_ is "unlit", a quality. The mastery frame doubles the middle consonant: _thummár_ is to strike as a master strikes, and the craft-records use it of work no apprentice could have done. The reflexive frame sets a _t_ after the first consonant, parted from it by _a_ because no word opens on two consonants: _thatumár_ is to strike oneself, or to strike one another.

The frames are a closed set. A speaker who needs a word Khazari has not got does not borrow one—he takes the nearest skeleton and pours a frame through it, and every other speaker understands the result at once. This is why the language has absorbed so little from its neighbours, and why the Khazari regard borrowing as an admission of poverty rather than a courtesy.

### Doubling

A doubled digraph is written by doubling its first letter:

```sql
SELECT '`' || sound || '`' AS "Consonant", '`' || becomes || '`' AS "Doubled"
FROM mutations WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' AND mutation = 'doubling' ORDER BY position
```

### Words older than the rules

A few words are older than the frames and keep shapes the frames no longer make. They belong to the Primordial layer of the language, and no Khazari would recast them:

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Form", coalesce(gloss, '—') AS "Gloss", coalesce(layer, '—') AS "Layer"
FROM older WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Grammar Notes

### Sentence Structure

Khazari is **Subject-Verb-Object**, and the order does not move:

_Dalkir thumár ramk_
: "The mason strikes the course"

The actor, the act and the thing acted upon, in that sequence. Khazari rhetoric treats any other order as evasion. Inscriptions are the one exception: an inscription may set the verb last, an archaic order kept for carved stone and never spoken.

### Verbs

A verb is a skeleton in the deed, mastery or reflexive frame. Person is carried by a separate pronoun and never by the verb.

#### Being

Khazari says "is" by saying nothing: _Vog dalkir_ is "He is a mason". The verb _zuvák_ is "to be, to stand".

### Nouns

A noun carries up to two suffixes, in a fixed order: gender, then number. Neither takes a hyphen.

#### Gender

Gender is grammatical and not semantic—every noun is masculine, feminine or neuter, and the gender belongs to the noun, not to its skeleton. The masculine is unmarked:

```sql
SELECT gloss AS "Gender", CASE WHEN zero THEN '—' ELSE '`' || form || '`' END AS "Suffix",
       split_part(examples[1], ', ', 1) AS "Example", substring(examples[1], length(split_part(examples[1], ', ', 1)) + 3) AS "Sense"
FROM affixes WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' AND kind IS NULL AND gloss IN ('Masculine', 'Feminine', 'Neuter') ORDER BY position
```

#### Number

A noun is singular unless it carries the plural suffix. Khazari never makes a plural by changing the vowels of the word:

```sql
SELECT gloss AS "Number", CASE WHEN zero THEN '—' ELSE '`' || form || '`' END AS "Suffix",
       split_part(examples[1], ', ', 1) AS "Example", substring(examples[1], length(split_part(examples[1], ', ', 1)) + 3) AS "Sense"
FROM affixes WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' AND kind IS NULL AND gloss IN ('Singular', 'Plural') ORDER BY position
```

So _lakamevez_ is "lamps".

### Adjectives

An adjective is a skeleton in the adjective frame. It stands before its noun and agrees with it in gender and number, taking the same suffixes in the same order:

_khalív dalk_
: "unlit stone"

_khalivev lakamev_
: "an unlit lamp"

_khalivez dalkez_
: "unlit stones"

Poetic inversion is permitted and is immediately recognisable as poetry.

### Numbers

Khazari counts in sixteens. Sixteen halves cleanly to eight, four, two and one, so ore, stores, gate-shares and lengths of course divide among houses by halving with no fraction left over, and a share is always a whole number of parts. ### Compounds

Khazari compounds two skeletons by setting the first in the bare frame, the second in the deed frame, and joining them with a dash. Neither half carries a suffix of gender:

- _dalk_ and _thumár_ give _dalk-thumár_, "the striking of stone"
- _gald_ and _vulád_ give _gald-vulád_, "the binding of fire"—the tempering of a blade
- _tanv_ and _guráz_ give _tanv-guráz_, "the guarding of the gate"

The compound is two parts joined by a dash, the second carrying the acute, and it means exactly what its two skeletons mean in that order. The same device makes house names, which is why a Khazari house name is a sentence about a craft and not a description of a person.

## Script and Literacy

Khazari is written in [[skill-drthrkscrpt|Pirzath]], which has **two forms**. The carved form resembles runes cut into stone—or rather, the runes of the [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar peoples]] resemble it, being in all likelihood a reduced work-row taught to the Proto-Pelwar tribes when those tribes were Khazari subjects, and simplified twice over since. The hand form is the everyday writing. Both read left to right and top to bottom.

Key features of **carved Pirzath**:

Letter forms
: Short angled strokes cut across the grain of the stone, never along it, because along the grain the stone splits; each letter is angular, and the same letter is cut differently in slate and in granite

The line of text
: Follows a seam, a bedding line or a natural edge of the stone, never a ruled baseline, so no inscription runs straight; reading carved Pirzath well means reading the stone

Inscription tradition
: Used for what must last—oaths, laws, tombs and the founding of a hold—cut into stone, metal, or wood

Diacritical marks
: A drilled dot over a vowel marks it long, and a short cut beneath a consonant marks it doubled. A dot divides words, a dash joins the two skeletons of a built name, a single cut along the grain ends a sentence, two such cuts, set before the words they govern, mark a command, and a short slanted cut marks a pause

Formality variants
: More elaborate, decorative versions for monuments or sacred texts

Key features of **the hand form of Pirzath**:

Letter forms
: Flowing and curved, and joined within a word

Media
: Ink or a stylus on slate and chalk (teaching, tallies, notes), wax tablets (drafts and accounts), hide or parchment (letters and books), and thin sheets of lead or copper cut with a stylus (records meant to last without being monumental)

Carved Pirzath writes the three consonants of a skeleton larger than the vowels of the frame, so a carved word shows its own grammar: the bone is cut deep and the binding is scored between. A Khazari reader takes in the skeleton first and the frame second, which is how a worn inscription can still be read when half the vowel-scoring has weathered away. The hand form marks the skeleton with a heavier stroke.

The runic rows of the [[place-nrdlndsrgn|Nordlands]], where anyone there writes at all, are near enough to Pirzath that a Khazari can pick out most of the staves. What they do not share is that depth of cut in the carved form. [[lore-nordheimnclt|A Nordman's staves]] all stand equal, because [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] has no skeleton to pick out; Khazari grades its own, and a stroke that carries grammar in one hand carries nothing in the other. Khazari who have compared the two rows say the northern one looks like a tool being held by the wrong end.

Literacy in Khazari is **nearly universal among the Khazari**—writing is fundamental to their culture of craftsmanship, record-keeping, and genealogy. However, human literacy in Khazari is **very rare**. Only dedicated scholars or those with Khazari kinship learn to read or write the language.

## Historical Development

Khazari is one of the **two surviving Elder Tongues**, sharing a common ancestor with [[skill-sinalelng|Sinale]] but diverging sharply in development. The language emerged among a people who live in the faces of mountains and talk along passages in the rock, and it reflects that origin: compact, resonant, built to carry along a passage.

Historical layers in Khazari:

- **Primordial layer** (oldest, archaic): Rare frames, ancient craft vocabulary, astronomical and geological terminology, and the few words older than the frames themselves
- **Classical layer** (the main body of Khazari tradition): The form preserved in genealogies, craft records, and ancestral epics
- **Modern layer** (contemporary Khazari): Philosophical vocabulary reflecting Khazari concerns with craft, honor, and legacy

The language has **absorbed minimal borrowings** from other tongues, for the reason the frames make plain: a Khazari who meets a foreign thing coins a skeleton for it rather than taking the foreign word, and the coinage is transparent to everyone at once. Khazari pride in linguistic purity is legendary, and it is cheaper for them than for anybody else.

## Regional Dialects

Khazari is fragmented geographically, with scattered strongholds and communities:

- **Northern stronghold dialect** (high mountains): The prestige form, slowest and most formal
- **Southern hall dialects** (foothills and lower regions): Slightly faster, some vowel shifts, minor vocabulary variations
- **Far-distant isolate dialects** (remote holds): Archaic frames, unique skeletons, extremely different from the modern standard

Inter-stronghold communication is maintained through formal written records and periodic gatherings, limiting dialect divergence. However, a Khazari from the northern peaks will sound notably different from one of the southern halls.

## Phrasebook {#phrasebook}

These are the words a visitor needs at a hold's gate and in its courts. Each phrase is given with its sound, the stressed syllables in capitals and the unstressed short words in lower case, then a gloss word by word, then its meaning.

Two habits run through them. Greeting and parting are a pair of stone-sayings: the one who speaks first names the stone, the one who answers names the course. And a request or an invitation is a question closed by _zef_, because the language has no form for a command.

**Greeting a stranger, and the answer**

_Dalk tasím._
: DALK ta-SÍM — stone · sound — "The stone is sound." (Good day.)

_Ramk tasím._
: RAMK ta-SÍM — course · sound — "The course is sound." (The answer.)

**An invitation to share a meal**

_Ves mulágh nuv gazun zef._
: ves mu-LÁGH nuv gazun zef? — you · feast · with · us · (question) — "Will you share our meal?"

**"Are you thirsty?", and an answer**

_Ves taríg zef._
: ves ta-RÍG zef? — you · thirsty · (question) — "Are you thirsty?"

_Zuvák, gaz taríg._
: zu-VÁK, gaz ta-RÍG — it-stands · I · thirsty — "Yes, I am thirsty."

**An invitation to sit and talk**

_Ves durám bep gunádh nuv gaz zef._
: ves du-RÁM bep gu-NÁDH nuv gaz zef? — you · sit · and · speak · with · me · (question) — "Will you sit and talk with me?"

**Thank you, and the answer**

_Gaz huváz._
: gaz hu-VÁZ — I · owe — "Thank you." (I owe you.)

_Ves thuf huváz._
: ves THUF hu-VÁZ — you · not · owe — "You're welcome." (You owe nothing.)

**Farewell, and the answer**

_Dalk durákh._
: DALK du-RÁKH — stone · holds-fast — "The stone holds." (Farewell.)

_Ramk durákh._
: RAMK du-RÁKH — course · holds-fast — "The course holds." (The answer.)

**Yes, no and please**

_Zuvák._
: zu-VÁK — it-stands — "Yes."

_Thuf._
: THUF — not — "No."

_Halz, thubágh._
: HALZ, thu-BÁGH — ale · an-asking — "Ale, please." _Thubágh_ follows the thing asked for.

**"My name is…", and "What is your name?"**

_Gaz Guruz, Tanv-guráz._
: gaz GU-ruz, TANV-gu-RÁZ — I · Guruz · Tanv-guráz — "My name is Guruz, of Tanv-guráz." A Khazár gives the house with the name.

_Thiz ves._
: THIZ ves? — who · you — "What is your name?" (Who are you?)

**"Where is…?"**

_Thuz tanv._
: THUZ TANV? — where · gate — "Where is the gate?" Any noun takes the place of _tanv_.

**Excuse me**

_Gaz tunáp._
: gaz tu-NÁP — I · squeeze-through — "Excuse me," to get past someone in a passage.

_Thubágh._
: thu-BÁGH — an-asking — "Excuse me," to catch someone's attention.

**I'm sorry**

_Gaz thurágh, gaz munád._
: gaz thu-RÁGH; gaz mu-NÁD — I · do-wrong · I · mend — "I'm sorry." (I did wrong; I will mend it.)

**Really? and Truly**

_Varn zef._
: VARN zef? — true-line · (question) — "Really?" Surprise or doubt at what someone has said.

_Varn._
: VARN — true-line — "Truly; honestly; indeed." Said after one's own words, to vouch for them.

One word serves both of the last two uses: _varn_, the true line the rock runs. Asked with _zef_ it doubts; stated, it vouches.

## Related Languages

Khazari is one of the **two surviving Elder Tongues**, descended from a **common Elder ancestor** shared with Sinale. The kinship is not audible. Sinale runs on open syllables, long vowels and a clean inventory; Khazari runs on clusters, short vowels and back fricatives, and nothing in the sound of either suggests the other.

What the two share is a habit no human tongue on Thalorna has: **both make a new word by altering the body of an old one rather than by hanging a piece on its end.** Khazari pours a different vowel frame through a fixed skeleton; Sinale changes the consonant a word begins with according to the work the word is doing. Suffixes are the shallow layer in both—the deep one is inside the word.

Beyond that instinct the two agree on **a closed inventory of derivational shapes** rather than an open one, so neither language borrows readily.

Scholars debate whether the Elder Races deliberately drove their languages apart or whether geography and craft pulled them.

## Naming Traditions

### Structure and Philosophy

A Khazari carries a **given name**, a **house name** and belongs to a **clan**, and the three are built by different frames, so no Khazari has ever mistaken one for another:

1. A **given name** is a single skeleton in a **given-name frame**. It is short—two or three syllables—it never carries an acute, it has no dash, and it always ends on a consonant.
2. A **house name** is **two skeletons compounded**, exactly as any other Khazari compound is built. It is two parts joined by a dash, and it always carries the acute of the deed frame on its second part.

3. A **clan name** is **one skeleton in the mastery frame**, the frame of the doing of a craft as a master does it. It is two syllables, it carries the acute, it has no dash, and each of the seven names the master craft its clan holds.

The dash and the acute therefore separate the three on sight, which matters in a culture that carves them onto the same lintel: a given name has no dash and no acute, a house name has a dash, and a clan name has an acute and no dash. The acute alone parts a clan name from a given name, and the dash alone parts it from a house name.

Khazari hold that a name is not a label but a **charge**: it names work the bearer is expected to do, and a name given carelessly is an insult to the child. Naming is performed by the elders of the house, who choose the skeleton and are held to account for the choice.

### Clans and Houses

The Khazari are seven **clans**, the seven who fled to Thalorna together with [Puthakun]{.name data-type="being/npc"} (see [[lore-khazararrv|the Coming of the Khazari]]), whom their tradition holds to be the agent of [[lore-goddreams|the God of Dreams]], called Lubát. A clan holds one **master craft**, and each of its **houses** holds a craft within it: a house name states that craft as an act, and the craft says which of the seven the house descends from. In [Khazartúrn]{.name data-type="place/settlement"} each clan held one of the seven towers.

| Clan     | Skeleton | Master craft                                                                |
| -------- | -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Summág` | `s-m-g`  | listening to stone: the finders, who choose where a hold is cut             |
| `Dullák` | `d-l-k`  | cutting stone                                                               |
| `Rummák` | `r-m-k`  | laying courses: halls, stairs and made roads                                |
| `Gullád` | `g-l-d`  | fire: the forge and smithing                                                |
| `Vurrán` | `v-r-n`  | the true line: following the rock's run, the work of miners and prospectors |
| `Gurráz` | `g-r-z`  | guarding: the gates and the approaches                                      |
| `Lummáv` | `l-m-v`  | the record: the archive and the account                                     |

Each name is its skeleton in the mastery frame, so _Dullák_ is the same word as _dullák_ "to cut stone as a master does", and _Summág_ is the listening of a master. The clan is named for the craft, and the craft is the whole of what the name says.

### Given Names

The given-name frame takes any of the five vowels, and the choice is meaningful rather than decorative. The **frame vowel is repeated**: in a man's name it stands again in the ending, _-an_, _-en_, _-in_, _-on_ or _-un_, and in a woman's name it stands on both sides of the second consonant. From **d-l-k** "stone": _Dalkan_ and _Dalak_, _Delken_ and _Delek_, _Dilkin_ and _Dilik_, _Dolkon_ and _Dolok_, _Dulkun_ and _Duluk_.

The five vowels carry the five senses in which a charge can be laid:

a
: the work as it stands. The commonest, and the plainest.

e
: the work hoped for, laid on a child born to a house that needs it.

i
: the work of a named forebear, taken up again.

o
: the work as an inheritance, used where a line would otherwise end.

u
: the work endured, given to a child born in a bad season or a bad year.

_e_ and _o_ are the rare charges, and most names carry _a_, _i_ or _u_. A Khazari therefore reads a stranger's given name twice: once for the skeleton, which says what he is for, and once for the vowel, which says why.

### House Names

A house name compounds two skeletons and states a craft as an act: _dalk_ "stone" and _thumár_ "striking" give **Dalk-thumár**, the house of the striking of stone. The name is not a description of anybody. It is the work the house holds, and the house is answerable for it.

House names descend from father to child and do not change. A Khazari who accomplishes something no existing house can account for may be granted a **new** house name, compounded for the occasion by the elders of several holds together; this happens perhaps twice in a century, and it founds a line.

Because both halves are Khazari skeletons, a house name means the same thing to every Khazari and nothing at all to anyone else. Written out for outsiders it is glossed, never translated into a foreign compound, and Khazari are notably short with human scholars who try.

### Place Names

A place takes its name from the place frame—_hudalk_, "the quarry"—or, for a hold, from a compound built as a house name is, stating the work the hold was cut for. Every hold carries a name of one of these two shapes.

### Gender and Naming

The skeletons are not gendered: a woman and a man may be named from the same skeleton and frequently are, within a house and within a generation. Only the ending distinguishes them.

House names have no gendered form at all. A woman of Dalk-thumár is of Dalk-thumár, and the convention some human chroniclers report—that certain houses are "women's houses"—is a misreading of holds where the senior craft happened to descend through sisters.

## How a word is made {#how-a-word-is-made}

An author needs three moves, and the language allows no others.

**A frame on a skeleton.** Take a skeleton and pour a frame through it. From **z-t-g** "steel": the bare
frame gives _zatag_ "steel", the worker frame _zatagir_ "a steelwright", the deed frame _zutág_ "to make
steel", and the mastery frame _zuttág_ "to make steel as a master does". The cluster rule sets a short
_a_ between the second and third consonants, because _t_ is not one of the five that stand together.

**A gender on a noun.** A noun that is feminine or neuter adds its suffix to the frame. From **d-v-g** "a
parent": _davag_ "a father", _davagev_ "a mother". The skeleton is the same, and only the noun's gender
differs.

**Two skeletons compounded.** Set the first skeleton in the bare frame and the second in the deed frame,
joined by a dash. From **p-n-d** "a tomb" and **b-g-n** "to seal": _pand-bugán_ "the sealing of
the tomb". This is how a house name or a hold's name is built, and it can be read the same way: the first
half is the thing, the second the work done to it.

A word no entry lists is still Khazari if it is a listed skeleton through a listed frame. A meaning no
skeleton carries needs a new skeleton, which belongs in this note before it is used anywhere else.

## Skeletons {#lexicon-skeletons}

```sql
SELECT '`' || root || '`' AS "Skeleton", sense AS "Sense", '`' || field || '`' AS "Field"
FROM roots WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives to a Khazari thing, and every name of a note about the Khazari, stands
here with the note it names and the language it is in. A Khazari name carries its skeletons and frame;
a name older than the rules is listed among the words older than the rules above.

```sql
SELECT name AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|]]'), ', '), '—') AS "Address",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Language",
       CASE tongue
         WHEN 'khazari' THEN '`' || array_to_string(built[1:len(built) - 1], '`' || ' + ' || '`') || '`' || ' ' || built[len(built)]
         WHEN 'older' THEN 'a word older than the rules'
         WHEN 'gloss' THEN CASE WHEN built IS NULL THEN '—'
              WHEN strip_accents(lower(name)) = strip_accents(lower(built[1])) THEN 'an unmarked spelling of `' || built[1] || '`'
              ELSE 'a glossed name on the word `' || built[1] || '`' END
         ELSE '—' END AS "Derivation"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Language tags

```sql
SELECT replace(tag, '<tongue>', '') AS "Tag", meaning AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-khazarlng' ORDER BY position
```
