Élavendri is a tongue of the [Pelwar Family]{.name data-type="lore"}. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Élavendri, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Élavendri is the lyrical tongue of the realm of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], a language steeped in magic, nature, and the influence of the fey-touched peoples who dwell in the borderlands between human and faerie realms. Where [[skill-vylarilng|Vylari]] is precise and administrative, Élavendri is fluid and suggestive—a language that reaches toward meanings beyond the grasp of ordinary words. It is the language of courts and verse: in western [[place-aureldirgn|Aureldia Region]] Élavendri is held to be the modern language of love, the courts and art, with [[skill-helonclng|Helonic]] its classical counterpart. The [[lore-vylarianclt|Vylarian]] people do not hold it so. Its grammar draws fine distinctions of rank, courtesy and feeling that the other western tongues leave unsaid, and nobles beyond Élavendre learn it as a mark of breeding. On [[place-aelwyth|Aelwyth]] the travelled and educated commonly speak it beside [[skill-aelwthlng|Aelwythi]], and the learned of Élavendre commonly speak Aelwythi in return; the two tongues have long borrowed words from each other.

The roman inventory includes `j` and `J` for the registered Élavendri name Jeldor; the repertoire records established names as well as ordinary word sounds.

## Overview

Élavendri speakers understand their language as more than communication; it is an art form and a path to understanding the deeper patterns of magic and nature. The language employs numerous words for subtle states of being, gradations of light, types of growth, and magical phenomena that have no equivalents in neighboring tongues.

The language is marked by a preference for alliteration, assonance, and poetic phrasing even in everyday speech. A simple greeting in Élavendri carries overtones of blessing and ritual. The language's connection to music is profound—many Élavendri linguistic features are better understood through melody than grammar.

The [[skill-elvndrlng#lexicon|Élavendri Lexicon]] gives the words with their sources, each traced to its [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] or imperial original.

## How Élavendri sounds

Élavendri is the soft lowland daughter of Áelendi. Its vowels break into pairs, its consonants loosen between vowels, and its words end on a murmured _-e_.

**The vowels.** Vowel pairs carry the language: _ie_, _ou_, _ae_ and _ei_, as in _kieron_ and _géilalon_. A final _-e_ closes many words.

**The consonants.** _l_, _v_, _r_, _n_, _s_, _m_ and _th_ do most of the work. _ny_ and _sh_ appear before a vowel.

**Clusters.** _lv_, _rd_, _nd_, _nth_ and _thr_ meet inside a word.

**Endings.** Élavendri words end in _-e_, _-el_, _-en_, _-and_, _-is_ or _-on_.

**What Élavendri leaves out.** It has lost _kh_, _gh_ and _dh_, which Áelendi keeps, and it has no apostrophe. It avoids words of more than four syllables.

## Phonology

Élavendri employs one of the richest vowel systems of any Pelwar language, with extensive use of diphthongs and subtle vocalic gradations. Consonants are relatively soft, with the language favoring liquids and sonorants over hard stops:

**Consonants:** The language reduces the number of stops (only t, d, k, g are common) and favors fricatives (th, s, sh, ch) and liquids (l, r). The combination of sounds creates natural palatalization—many consonants are pronounced with a slight y-glide in certain contexts. Aspiration (marking with h after a consonant) is phonemic and creates meaningful distinctions.

**Vowels:** Élavendri distinguishes at least seven vowel positions, many with both oral and nasal variants. The system employs systematic length distinctions and allows complex diphthongs that change meaning based on speed of articulation. Vowel harmony principles influence word formation—words tend to maintain vowel colors across morphemes.

**Prosody:** Élavendri employs melodic stress, with pitch patterns carrying as much information as stress accent. The language naturally divides into musical phrases, each with characteristic pitch contours. This has made Élavendri ideal for poetry and music, and difficult for non-native speakers to acquire.

**Distinctive Features:** The language makes heavy use of the "fey fricative" /th/ (voiced dental fricative /d/), which features prominently in words related to magic, fey beings, and abstraction. The combination th- at the beginning of words often indicates magical or abstract concepts. Nasal vowels occur in both oral and nasal variants, creating subtle distinctions.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Élavendri employs a relatively free word order. The basic order is VSO (Verb-Subject-Object) in formal contexts, but poetic reordering is common and expected.

**Gender:** Gender is somewhat less important than in Vylari or [[skill-tarvenlng|Tarveni]], with only two primary genders (animate/inanimate) in standard speech, though archaic texts preserve three-gender systems.

**Adjectives and Adverbs:** The language employs a complex system of derivational morphology to create adjectival and adverbial forms. Diminutive, augmentative, and affective suffixes are productive and frequently used. The language readily creates new descriptive forms on the fly.

**Article System:** Élavendri employs a well-developed article system distinguishing definite, indefinite, partitive, and demonstrative forms. The choice of article can carry significant meaning in poetic contexts, indicating levels of reality or abstraction.

### Ordinary expressions

_Dárkathen munde merlis_ means "I hold joy", a sudden gladness; _dárkathen munde merlon_ means "I hold happiness", a continuing gladness. _Dárkathen munde losnis_ names pleasure and _dárkathen munde tendaris_ names contentment. These are concrete expressions of the speaker's emotional state. _Dárkathen munde dáerison gierth tose_, "I hold trust toward you", says that the speaker relies on the other's keeping of their word, with the Pact at the heart of _dáerison_. _Tengis_ is confidence in ability or success, _tendris_ is conviction that a claim is true, and _thénnairsa_ is truth itself.

The loves remain distinct: _méldaris_ is romantic love, _ánsevirsa_ friendship, _kéinalis_ family love, and _ílthamon_ selfless love given to all. _Gérmisa_ is sexual lust. The other feeling expressions give their own pieces in their cited order: _gaer douris_, "overwhelming fear; terror", _grien gaerasa_, "sustained anger; resentment", _grien ilnis_, "sustained desire; longing", and _gaer rondis_, "overwhelming admiration; awe". They describe human feeling in ordinary life.

The physical tongue is _gaelis_; _óstila_ is speech or language. _Kenlon_ names a child whether named or unnamed, while _lyegon_ is the child before naming. _Hendron_ is the physical hearth, and _káerathon_ its kept fire. _Gyernis_ is a thigh and _gurnis_ a testicle. _Niemon_ is breath. _Níemathen munde rith_, "I breathe in", and _níemathen munde gounel_, "I breathe out", give the directions in these two bodily utterances. _Nond geindathen munde góldathen niemon_, "I cannot take breath", reports suffocation when breath is blocked or breathable air is absent. _Grunathen munde ruskis_, "I feel pain", names bodily sensation. _Rusnar gaelmar_, "feverish", names the heat of illness; _gaeri gaeri dielar_, "exhausted", uses the degree adverb _gaeri_ twice with tiredness.

The household words retain their different uses. _Ómbela_ is a chair, _mórvalis_ a table, _zúndela_ a lock and _kézra_ a key. _Gorth thodkis_ is a boot, the high foot covering. _Sira mirlis_ is water-soaked earth, mud. _En munde dielar_ means "I am tired", using the existing copula. _En munde gaeri gaeri dielar_ means "I am extremely tired". _Ryenathen munde rith soldar rundis_, "I speak in a loud voice", is a shout; _ryenathen munde rith niemon rundis_, "I speak in the breath voice", is a whisper, the unvoiced manner of speech.

The physical wood and weather words come through Áelendi. _Pelvai_ names any cloud here, and _nielke_ any plant root; the Áelendi parent words have those broad uses, beside their Sinale sources' low cloud and deep root. _Ravale_ is rain and _salve_ a tree. A weather report can put the existence verb first: _elnathen nonduk pelvai_, "no cloud exists; the weather is clear", or _elnathen gurnar pelvai_, "many clouds exist; it is cloudy". The separate river and lake words keep their scopes: _sirnon_ is a river above or below the tree line, _lántheve_ the river below it; _dulnon_ is a lake in any setting and _dulna_ the tarn in its corrie.

_Rith ordur taldis_ says "in position four; fourth". Another numeral names its own position in that expression: _rith reithik taldis_, "fifth", and _rith taehes taldis_, "tenth". _Ta taehes_, "two tens", is twenty; _diema diema_, "half of a half", is a quarter. _Uk threl thend_, "one from the two", selects either member of a pair, while _nonduk threl thend_, "none from the two", selects neither. _Rith kren miron_, _rith nonduk miron_ and _rith alnar miron_ say "in some place", "in no place" and "in all places".

The comparison expressions name the measure and its set. _Geirin geildar_ is "better", good to a greater degree, and _geirin geildar thinar ukal derar gaeron_ is "best", better than each other thing in the comparison. _Geirin thinar ukal derar gaeron_ is "most" and _thienen thinar ukal derar gaeron_ "least", more or less than each other thing. _Gaeri gaeri_ gives extreme degree, as in _gaeri gaeri lunnar_, "tiny; extremely small". _Goul antemar_ and _send antemar_ give too much and too little: a great or small quantity that fails the fitting measure.

These expressions name a time or an occasion. _Ti kieron_ is today, this day; _murnel óssenda_ is midnight, the middle of the night. _Rith kurnis kirdis_ means "during the feast", in the feast's time. _Kirdin_ is a countable occasion: _nal uk kirdin_, "once", _nal ta kirdin_, "twice", and _nal gurnar kirdin_, "repeatedly". _Nal treinen kirdin_ and _nal dáelamen kirdin_ put an event at the first and next occasion in sequence; _nal dyelon_ marks its final point. _Grend_ means again, a recurrence of the action in question. _Nond geinde_ says that an expected event has not happened yet.

_Gire_ links the object of a clause to that clause's subject: _nóurethen munde gire_, "I see myself", and _nóurethen gene gire_, "the person or thing sees itself". The lexical possessives have concrete uses in _mundri halthon_, "my house", _tosri genta_, "your spear", and _nusri omris_, "our sheep". _Kendar neli_ and _tendar neli_ refer to anyone and anything without restriction. _Nond kendar_ excludes every person; _uk derar_ names another, one other person or thing.

Questions use the words whose meanings they ask for: _gere miron_, "what place; where", _gere kirdis_, "what time; when", _gere deilnon_, "what number; how many", _gere menthis_, "what amount; how much", and _gere gouris_, "what reason; why". _Gese_ asks who and _geisri_ whose. _He en tose tis_, "are you here?", opens a yes or no question with _he_. _Ryenathen tose geisin_ asks how you say something, while _ryenathen tose gere_ asks what you say, a request to repeat or explain. _Dárkathen ti gere lenthon_, "what name does this have?", asks what it is called.

The modal utterances keep ability, permission, obligation and prohibition distinct. _Geindathen munde sénkathen_ means "I can walk"; _nond geindathen munde sénkathen_ means "I cannot walk". _Gaelgathen munde sénkathen_ gives permission to walk. _Targathen munde sénkathen_ states an obligation, and _norgathen munde sénkathen_ states a prohibition. In these concrete utterances the modal verb comes first, the subject follows it, and the action is named afterward. _Gaelgathen munde róugathen greile_, "may I pass through", is the courteous request used to pass or interrupt. _Eldathen ti herin_, "this might happen", states possibility with the event's truth left open.

For linked alternatives, _gose ... gose_ marks either ... or, _nond gose ... nond gose_ marks neither ... nor, and _reik ... reik_ affirms both items. Each marker stands before its item. _Gyese nond en tose tis, seis róugathen munde gourthe_ means "unless you are here, I go away": if your being here is false, my going away follows. The negation in _gyese nond_ applies to the whole following condition. _Gase gyese en munde dielar, róugathen munde_ means "although I am tired, I go"; tiredness is stated as a fact. _En munde dielar. Gase tenri róugathen munde_ affirms the same going across that contrast: "I am tired. Nevertheless, I go."

The conversational entries give whole utterances. _Laegnathen munde tose_, "I thank you", _sarnathen munde_, "I understand", and _nond sarnathen munde_, "I do not understand", put the verb before the named subject. The listed examples retain the cited verb form. _Máldathen munde tose tis_, "I give you your place here", keeps the host as the person offering the seat. _Dárkathen munde gournis_, "I hold regret; sorry", names an emotional state. _Kernathen munde_, "I ask", is a courteous preface. _Nond molthon_, "no debt of honor", answers thanks by releasing the recipient from repayment. A direct request names its addressee after the verb: _árnathen tose tis_, "come here", and _kenthathen tose munde_, "help me". _Shalor en kir a thel_ is the fixed formal greeting, "Welcome, friend of the light". _Reida rith tose_, "peace in you", takes leave.

Full questions keep their named roles clear. _En tose rith gere miron?_ asks "where are you?", literally whether you are in what place. _Eldathen to nal gere kirdis?_ asks "when does that happen?", at what time. _En al gouris gere?_ asks "what is the reason?", and _en al deirnis gere?_ "what is the purpose?". _En al deilnon gere?_ asks the number and _en al menthis gere?_ the amount. _En gene gese?_ asks who the person is; _en to geisri?_ asks whose the thing is. _Árnathen tose threl gere miron?_ asks from what place you come. _Méisathen tose ti leithon gierth gese?_ asks to whom you send this word.

Complete alternatives keep each marker with its own item. _Dalgathen munde gose sira gose korthon_ means "I choose either water or mead". _Ílnathen munde nond gose sira nond gose korthon_ means "I want neither water nor mead". _Dárkathen munde reik sira reik korthon_ means "I have both water and mead". _Gyese en tose tis, seis dárkathen munde sénthira_ gives a positive condition and its consequence: "if you are here, then I hold hope". _Róugathen munde gierth halthon seis dúrsathen munde_ gives a purpose: "I go toward home so that I sleep".

The explanatory markers also have concrete uses. _En ti tárneve; gere temar, uk tárnevine_ means "this is bread; namely, one loaf". _En salve mirnon; uk dírmalis, hersel_ gives an example: "a tree is a plant; for example, a pine". With water as the alternative in question, _dalgathen munde korthon dergin gerth_ chooses mead instead.

The period records a pause in both roman and Semaron readings, including a
pause within a continuing sentence. Where a translation has a comma,
semicolon, colon or dash for that pause, write a period in Élavendri. Questions
need their question word or the opening _he_; a period alone cannot make a
statement a question. A fragment that relies on intonation in translation can use the
existing clarification _ryenathen tose gere_, “what do you say”, to make the
asking explicit. Reporting words such as _ryenathen gene_, “the person says”,
and named speakers identify dialogue without quotation marks.

## Script & Literacy

Élavendri is written in the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], the same alphabet used in Vylari and neighboring regions, with diacritics added for pitch. The lowlands took it up readily, as they take up anything that serves. The Áelendan rune-row [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thurarth]] and the [[skill-clthndscrpt|Veldikko]] of the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]] belong to the highlanders, who carve in the one and write [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]] in the other, so two peoples who can hold a conversation cannot read a word of each other. Ancient texts in the region are Áelendi, and an Élavendri who reads one has learned a second tongue and a second script to do it.

A distinctive scribal tradition employs flowing, connected letterforms that reflect the language's musical nature. Decorative elements (flourishes, miniatures) are common in high-quality texts. True literacy is confined to the priesthood, professional scribes, those training for magical arts (for whom reading is an absolute necessity), and perhaps one noble in four. The common folk of Élavendre, like those elsewhere on [[place-worldthlrn|Thalorna]], are almost entirely non-literate and rely on scribes for any written communication.

## Historical Development

Élavendri developed from Proto-Pelwar stock in the northern forests and mountains of Élavendre. The language absorbed deep influence from the non-Pelwar populations of the region—people of fey heritage, indigenous forest dwellers, and magical practitioners—resulting in a tongue with features quite foreign to other Pelwar languages.

The most conservative member of the Pelwar family is [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]], spoken by the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] people in the mountainous interior of Élavendre, whose speech and writing have not changed in hundreds of years. The Élavendri are much less conservative. They have adapted to the new realities of their situation far more fluidly than the Áelendan, and Élavendri proper is the "modernized" variant that has absorbed more external influences and grammatical simplification, particularly the loss of one gender and some synthetic verb forms. The same readiness is part of why they took up the Semaron.

## Regional Dialects

**Coastal Élavendri:** Spoken in the port cities and plains, showing some influence from trading contacts with speakers of [[skill-provnzlng|Provenzal Language]] and Vylari. More straightforward phonetically and grammatically than inland variants.

**Sylvan Élavendri:** The dialect of the deep forests and rural areas, preserving more archaic features and fey vocabulary. More complex phonetics and more archaic grammar.

**Magical Register:** A specialized, highly formal variant used in magical rituals and high ceremony, employing archaic vocabulary and conservative phonetics. Knowledge of this register marks one as educated in magical arts.

For the ancestral Pelwar tongue of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]—the most conservative member of the family, spoken in the mountainous interior—see [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]].

## Related Languages

Élavendri stands somewhat apart from its Pelwar siblings, having developed under heavy influence from fey and pre-human populations. Its deep connection to Áelendi marks both as descendents of a northern branch of Proto-Pelwar, distinct from the southern branch that gave rise to Vylari, Tarveni, and the others.

Élavendri speakers find Provenzal somewhat intelligible, particularly in poetic contexts—Provenzal has absorbed substantial Élavendri vocabulary in the domains of art, emotion, and magic (words like _thel_, _sil_, _daren_, _thal-_, _ael-_), and Provenzal sílador poetry frequently employs Élavendri word order and the copula _en_. An Élavendri speaker hearing a Provenzal love song will catch familiar words amid the foreign grammar. Vylari and Tarveni speakers find Élavendri quite difficult. The northern languages [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal Language]] and [[skill-varokhlng|Varokhi Language]] share some phonetic features with Élavendri but remain largely mutually unintelligible.

## Naming Traditions

Élavendri names are often compound forms, combining roots related to nature, magic, and abstract qualities. A name might literally translate as "Bright-Stream" or "Shadow-Watcher" or "Keeper-of-Deep-Words." The meanings are taken seriously—a name is considered to shape character and destiny.

Many Élavendri individuals have both a "sun name" (used in formal and public contexts) and a "shadow name" (used only among intimates and at night). The shadow name is considered the true name, encoding the person's essential nature.

The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] of the interior name from a stock of their own, built on roots the lowlands no longer say and closed with endings the lowlands have lost; see [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]] for it. The two sets are not interchangeable, and an Élavendri hearing an Áelendi clan name knows the mountains are being spoken of before the speaker says so.

Élavendri names are typically melodic and employ frequent use of the fey fricative (th) and liquid consonants (l, r). Many names incorporate the morpheme _-ael_ (light), _-thal_ (shadow), or _-síl_ (song).

## Élavendri Lexicon {#lexicon}

A bard of a great house in [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]] is reciting the house's line at a feast, and he is doing it in two tongues at once without anyone at the table remarking on it. The names of the founders are highland names, said the way the highlands say them, with the clicks in; the words that join them are Élavendri, the same words with the clicks gone, the long vowels opened into diphthongs and the last vowel of each one let fall to a murmur. The house's claim to the old blood rests on the guests hearing the difference and believing the line runs across it.

This is the word-hoard of Élavendri, the tongue of the lowland kingdom. It is the daughter of Áelendi, the highland tongue that came over the eastern pass, made over by the lowlands in the centuries since: its clicked stops merged with the plain ones, it has lost _kh_, _gh_ and _dh_, its long vowels broke into the diphthongs it is admired for, and many of its final vowels softened to _-e_. Because it is Áelendi's daughter it carries Áelendi's great layer of [[skill-sinalelng|Sinale]] words, the trees, the light, the veil and the art, and several of them came back nearly to their Sinale shape on the way down. Because the kingdom owed the empire service for centuries, it carries a smaller layer of imperial words as well: the law, the coin, the letters and the University.

## How to read the dictionary {#how-to-read-the-dictionary}

An entry gives the word as it is cited: a noun in its basic form, an adjective in its one form, a verb in its infinitive. It names the Áelendi word it descends from, or the imperial Vylari word it was taken from. Its meaning is given in Élavendri. A word made of smaller parts names them.

### Classes

The highland masculine and feminine fell together as the animate gender, and the highland neuter became the inanimate. The names are grammar more than sense: a forge is animate because it was feminine in the highlands, and a learned word takes the animate when it names a person and the inanimate otherwise.

## Sounds and spelling {#sounds-and-spelling}

## The learned words {#the-learned-words}

The imperial words came with the clerks in the centuries of vassalage and with the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]] and the University after. A learned word keeps its imperial shape more than an inherited one does: it loses its masculine ending and its long vowels, keeps its last vowel, and takes the stress on its first syllable, but it keeps `b`, `f` and `z`, which no inherited word has. Several stand beside an inherited word of like sense: the learned _lórezand_ is a lecturer at the University, and the inherited _lornand_ is a reciter who keeps a house's records in his head.

## Words {#words}

### The house and the old blood

An Élavendri house is the whole descending line, the dead included, and its standing rests on one claim: that the line runs back to the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]. The words the houses use to make it are the highland words, worn down. A clan name in the highlands is the founder's name with _-ndi_; in the lowlands the old houses carry the same ending as _-nde_, and a house that can show _sóurethende_, kin of one blood, on a genealogy that goes back to the crossing outranks one that cannot, whatever its acreage.

An old line is _úrkenon_, and an earned name, _sílderon_, is the one a bard gives. Whether all the claims are genuine is another matter, and a claim kept up gracefully for four hundred years has earned a standing of its own.

### The land in keeping

Land is a duty and not a possession. A lord who lets his people go hungry has failed the one thing his rank is for, and the land words are the herders' words of the highlands turned to the lowland estate. _Mirla_ is the land a house keeps, _dólgalon_ the common store held against a bad winter, and _gerkand_ the steward who answers for it. A house is judged by the state of the land in its keeping as much as by its bards.

### The bard and the word

Poetry is how a house's history is held, how a prophecy is passed on and how a judgment is made memorable enough to bind, and the bard's words are the oldest in the tongue's care. _Lyeronno_, a singer, is the highland singer at the gathering grown into a court officer; _lornand_, a reciter, is the highland Tradition Keeper, and in the lowlands he keeps a house's records in the head. A satire composed by a bard of standing is a weapon the law recognizes, and it is made of these words.

Élavendri is written in the empire's [[skill-semrnscrpt|Semaron]], whose letter is _sémarzon_. The highlanders' rune-row, whose staves are _ugrin_, is cut for monuments, magic and old poetry, and its language is Áelendi.

### The art and the thin places

Magic is ordinary here, studied and discussed at table and expected of the well-bred, and its words came down through the highlands from the [[lore-flksinale|Sinale]]. That is where the tongue's _th-_ comes from: so many of the Sinale words open on it that _th-_ is the sound of the uncanny in the tongue. The art is _simme_, a thin place _sírnaste_, a spirit _áitheve_, and the fae, one at a time, _pénallane_. The lowland mage, _thórdimand_, and his working, _thórdima_, are the Arcane Order's words, learned from the empire; the highlands call him _símmenne_.

### The Pact, the oath and the judgment

The [Kindred Pact]{.name data-type="lore/law"} is the last thing an Élavendri owes, and he does not explain it to a foreigner, partly from courtesy and partly because he is not sure of the terms. Its words are the highland words: _géilathen_, to keep, _geiland_, a keeper, _nolgra_, a vow. In the lowlands they serve the courts as well as the groves. A judgment is _thargis_ and the council that gives it _mendis_.

### The hall and the guest

The lodge of the highlands is the lowland hall, and the courtesy of the hall is the highlands' own, refined. The kept fire is _káerathon_; the order of the seats, _máldalon_, is worked out for every feast; and the welcome, _éllevirsa_, is the Sinale word the highlands took and the lowlands kept.

### Light, sky and the turning year

The light words are the most Sinale of all, and the bards' favorites: _áulira_, light, _olthe_, a cast shadow, _írvale_, dawn. The years are counted from the crossing, _ganthis_, like the highlands' and unlike the empire's.

### The wood and its weather

The kingdom is a woodland kingdom, and every tree in it has a Sinale name brought down through the highlands: _hersel_, a pine, _sálvurne_, a grove. Several came back to their Sinale shape on the way, the lowland changes undoing what the highland mouth had done to them.

### Mountain, water and stone

The kingdom's interior is the Áelendan's mountains, and the lowland words for it are the highland ones softened: _neilis_, snow, _dulgis_, a valley, _sira_, water.

### The body and the dead

The dead are part of the house, and the words for them are many. The highland cairn, _groumon_, is a lowland tomb, and the Sinale words for death and grief, _sélvore_ and _nélthava_, are the bards' words for them.

### Arms and the border

The kingdom fights seldom, and its war words are the highland [[affiliation-alndnwrdns|Wardens]]' words with little added.

### Craft, coin and the market

The craft words are inherited, and the coin words came from the empire with its coins.

### The crown, the law and the University

For centuries the kingdom owed the empire service, and the words of that service came north with the imperial clerks and stayed: the law, _vornon_, the charter, _záktela_, the tax farmer, _sóhatand_. They are the only words in the tongue with `b`, `f` or `z`, and the three letters mark a clerk's word. The University and the [[affiliation-ordoarcanis|Thordimalon]] brought a second band, and the kingdom treats the Order as a rival to be balanced, licensed and argued with, in the Order's own words: a lecturer is _lórezand_, beside the highland _lornand_, a reciter.

### Doing, going and being

The verbs are cited by the infinitive, as the highlands cite them.

### Numbers and grammar words

The article _al_ is the highland _al_ with the lowland stress it takes like every word.

### Colour and the look of things

The colours are plain adjectives, each cited in its one form.
