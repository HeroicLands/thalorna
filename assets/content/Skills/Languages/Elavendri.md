Élavendri is a tongue of the Pelwar family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Élavendri, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

Élavendri is the lyrical tongue of the realm of [[place-elavendre|Élavendre]], a language steeped in magic, nature, and the influence of the fey-touched peoples who dwell in the borderlands between human and faerie realms. Where Vylari is precise and administrative, Élavendri is fluid and suggestive—a language that reaches toward meanings beyond the grasp of ordinary words.

## Overview

Élavendri speakers understand their language as more than communication; it is an art form and a path to understanding the deeper patterns of magic and nature. The language employs numerous words for subtle states of being, gradations of light, types of growth, and magical phenomena that have no equivalents in neighboring tongues.

The language is marked by a preference for alliteration, assonance, and poetic phrasing even in everyday speech. A simple greeting in Élavendri carries overtones of blessing and ritual. The language's connection to music is profound—many Élavendri linguistic features are better understood through melody than grammar.

## Phonology

Élavendri employs one of the richest vowel systems of any Pelwar language, with extensive use of diphthongs and subtle vocalic gradations. Consonants are relatively soft, with the language favoring liquids and sonorants over hard stops:

**Consonants:** The language reduces the number of stops (only t, d, k, g are common) and favors fricatives (th, dh, s, sh, ch) and liquids (l, r). The combination of sounds creates natural palatalization—many consonants are pronounced with a slight y-glide in certain contexts. Aspiration (marking with h after a consonant) is phonemic and creates meaningful distinctions.

**Vowels:** Élavendri distinguishes at least seven vowel positions, many with both oral and nasal variants. The system employs systematic length distinctions (marked with macron: ā, ē, etc.) and allows complex diphthongs that change meaning based on speed of articulation. Vowel harmony principles influence word formation—words tend to maintain vowel colors across morphemes.

**Prosody:** Élavendri employs melodic stress, with pitch patterns carrying as much information as stress accent. The language naturally divides into musical phrases, each with characteristic pitch contours. This has made Élavendri ideal for poetry and music, and difficult for non-native speakers to acquire.

**Distinctive Features:** The language makes heavy use of the "fey fricative" /th/ (voiced dental fricative /d/), which features prominently in words related to magic, fey beings, and abstraction. The combination th- at the beginning of words often indicates magical or abstract concepts. Nasal vowels occur in both oral and nasal variants, creating subtle distinctions.

## Grammar Notes

**Word Order:** Élavendri employs a relatively free word order, with grammatical relationships marked through inflectional endings rather than position. The basic order is VSO (Verb-Subject-Object) in formal contexts, but poetic reordering is common and expected.

**Nominal System:** Nouns are marked for case (nominative, accusative, genitive, dative) and number (singular, dual, plural). The dual number is well-developed, used for paired entities, couples, or balanced opposites. Gender is somewhat less important than in Vylari or Tarvéni, with only two primary genders (animate/inanimate) in standard speech, though archaic texts preserve three-gender systems.

**Verbal System:** Élavendri verbs conjugate according to person, number, tense (past/present/future), and aspect (perfective/imperfective). The conditional and subjunctive moods are extensively developed and frequently used in poetry. Many verbs are synthetic rather than analytical, meaning that complex meanings are embedded in single words.

**Adjectives and Adverbs:** The language employs a complex system of derivational morphology to create adjectival and adverbial forms. Diminutive, augmentative, and affective suffixes are productive and frequently used. The language readily creates new descriptive forms on the fly.

**Article System:** Élavendri employs a well-developed article system distinguishing definite, indefinite, partitive, and demonstrative forms. The choice of article can carry significant meaning in poetic contexts, indicating levels of reality or abstraction.

## Script & Literacy

Élavendri traditionally employs [[skill-thrvrdhscrpt|Thúravardh]], the old Áelendan rune-row, but this script has been largely supplemented by the [[skill-semrnscrpt|Sêmarion]]—the same alphabet used in Vylari and neighboring regions. Thúravardh persists in magical texts, poetry, and items of great antiquity.

A distinctive scribal tradition employs flowing, connected letterforms that reflect the language's musical nature. Decorative elements (flourishes, miniatures) are common in high-quality texts. True literacy is confined to the priesthood, professional scribes, those training for magical arts (for whom reading is an absolute necessity), and perhaps 20-35% of the nobility. The common folk of Élavendre, like those elsewhere on Thalorna, are almost entirely non-literate and rely on scribes for any written communication.

The language is sometimes written with vowel diacriticals to indicate stress and pitch patterns when clarity is important. This is particularly common in poetry and instructional texts related to magic.

## Historical Development

Élavendri developed from Proto-Pelwar stock but in isolation from the other branches, in the northern forests and mountains of Élavendre. The language absorbed deep influence from the non-Pelwar populations of the region—people of fey heritage, indigenous forest dwellers, and magical practitioners—resulting in a tongue with features quite foreign to other Pelwar languages.

The most conservative member of the Pelwar family is **[[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi]]**, spoken by the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] people in the mountainous interior of Élavendre. Élavendri proper represents a "modernized" variant that has absorbed more external influences and grammatical simplification, particularly the loss of one gender and some synthetic verb forms.

## Regional Dialects

**Coastal Élavendri:** Spoken in the port cities and plains, showing some influence from trading contacts with Provenzal and Vylari speakers. More straightforward phonetically and grammatically than inland variants.

**Sylvan Élavendri:** The dialect of the deep forests and rural areas, preserving more archaic features and fey vocabulary. More complex phonetics and more archaic grammar.

**Magical Register:** A specialized, highly formal variant used in magical rituals and high ceremony, employing archaic vocabulary and conservative phonetics. Knowledge of this register marks one as educated in magical arts.

For the ancestral Pelwar tongue of the [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]]—the most conservative member of the family, spoken in the mountainous interior—see [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]].

## Sample Phrases

- **"Thálira en sílen, élthar vé dâren."** – "The forest sings, the light remembers." (Poetic phrase about the presence of magic in nature.)
- **"Shâlor en kír a thél."** – "Welcome, friend of the light." (Formal greeting among Élavendri speakers.)
- **"Thalríen en míarân dú."** – "Our roots run deep in the earth." (Statement of connection and belonging.)
- **"Síath en thêrël - dúlir en síath."** – "Magic is life - life is magic." (Fundamental philosophical statement.)
- **"Ven'tálêth en síren a kîl, veth dû síath."** – "The fey walks between worlds, yet is part of magic." (About the nature of fey beings and reality.)

## Related Languages

Élavendri stands somewhat apart from its Pelwar siblings, having developed in relative isolation and under heavy influence from fey and pre-human populations. Its deep connection to Áelendi marks both as descendents of a northern branch of Proto-Pelwar, distinct from the southern branch that gave rise to Vylari, Tarvéni, and the others.

Élavendri speakers find Provenzal somewhat intelligible, particularly in poetic contexts—Provenzal has absorbed substantial Élavendri vocabulary in the domains of art, emotion, and magic (words like _thèl_, _síl_, _dàren_, _thal-_, _ael-_), and Provenzal síladòr poetry frequently employs Élavendri word order and the copula _en_. An Élavendri speaker hearing a Provenzal love song will catch familiar words amid the foreign grammar. Vylari and Tarvéni speakers find Élavendri quite difficult. The northern languages [[skill-nordmalng|Nordmal]] and [[skill-varokhlng|Varokhi]] share some phonetic features with Élavendri but remain largely mutually unintelligible.

## Naming Traditions

Élavendri names are often compound forms, combining roots related to nature, magic, and abstract qualities. A name might literally translate as "Bright-Stream" or "Shadow-Watcher" or "Keeper-of-Deep-Words." The meanings are taken seriously—a name is considered to shape character and destiny.

Many Élavendri individuals have both a "sun name" (used in formal and public contexts) and a "shadow name" (used only among intimates and at night). The shadow name is considered the true name, encoding the person's essential nature.

The [[lore-aelendnppl|Áelendan]] of the interior name from a stock of their own, built on roots the lowlands no longer say and closed with endings the lowlands have lost; see [[skill-aelendlng|Áelendi Language]] for it. The two sets are not interchangeable, and an Élavendri hearing an Áelendi clan name knows the mountains are being spoken of before the speaker says so.

Élavendri names are typically melodic and employ frequent use of the fey fricative (th) and liquid consonants (l, r). Many names incorporate the morpheme _-ael_ (light), _-thal_ (shadow), or _-síl_ (song).
