Grukar Language is a tongue of the Grukar family. Fluency measures the sophistication of expression in Grukar, from the halting phrases of a traveler to the nuanced and learned discourse of a native speaker. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Grukar is not a language in the way that scholars of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión Region]] understand the word, and "tongue" is the wrong metaphor for it entirely—the mouth of the [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar Folk]] is an organ for eating and biting, not for making sound. The majority of Grukar communication is **physical and demonstrative**—posture, gesture, bared teeth, raised hackles, eye contact, shoulder positioning, the angle of a weapon. This body language is the primary channel. Grukar acquire it instinctually, the way a wolf learns pack behavior. There are no teachers. Grukar young absorb it from the pack around them and begin producing it as naturally as breathing.

Audible signals exist, but they are secondary—an **emphasis layer** laid on top of the physical communication, and produced not from the mouth but from a complex system of internal chest- and throat-resonating structures unique to the Grukar form. These structures generate a much wider acoustic range than humans can perceive: percussive clicks at the upper edge of human hearing, ratcheting throat-rasps, low chest-rumbles that are felt rather than heard, and (most distinctively) sustained calls in registers above and below human auditory range. A Grukar warband coordinating in a forest produces, to a human ear, a sparse pattern of clicks and rasps; to another Grukar, the same exchange is dense with subsonic carrier-tones and ultrasonic signals that the human listener simply does not register. A Grukar asserting dominance does so primarily with its body; the resonant snarl that accompanies the display adds force and carries over distance, but the meaning is in the posture. Two Grukar can communicate entirely without audible sound if they can see each other. The vocal apparatus becomes essential only when physical display is insufficient: in darkness, across distance, or in the chaos of a fight where bodies are too entangled to read—and even then, much of what passes between them is in registers a human cannot hear at all.

No Grukar has ever reflected on the structure of their communication, because Grukar do not reflect.

The system—physical and vocal together—serves exactly four purposes: establishing dominance, coordinating fighting, claiming ownership, and marking territory. There are no gestures or sounds for gratitude, beauty, compassion, regret, or wonder. The concept of empathy does not exist in Grukar—not as a suppressed idea, but as a void. The signals simply aren't there because the need was never there.

Mídhalión scholars who have attempted to study Grukar speech have universally failed to produce a grammar. There is no grammar to produce. What exists is a set of physical displays supplemented by vocalizations that shift meaning based on volume, aggression, and bodily context. The same snarl that means "mine" when crouched over food means "attack" when bellowed in a charge—but the body is what disambiguates, not the sound.

## How Grukar sounds

Grukar is guttural and sparse. Its words are short, hard syllables thrown together.

**The vowels.** _a_, _u_ and _o_. _i_ and _e_ are rare.

**The consonants.** _g_, _k_, _r_, _z_, _sh_, _th_, _d_ and _v_.

**Clusters.** Words open on _gr-_, _kr-_, _vr-_, _dr-_ or _zr-_ and close on _-rk_, _-sk_ or _-rn_.

**Endings.** Grukar words end in _-ak_, _-ok_, _-ath_, _-urn_ or _-mor_.

**What Grukar leaves out.** It has no run of four consonants.

## Acoustic Repertoire

What audible signals the Grukar produce are generated by internal resonating structures of the chest and throat—not by the mouth, which throughout the encounter is reserved for its proper function of biting and eating. Mídhalión attempts to transcribe Grukar "speech" in human alphabets are doomed from the start: most of the actual signal lies outside human hearing, and the audible portion is only the upper-edge fragment that humans happen to be able to detect.

**The audible band** (what a human listener actually hears):

- Percussive clicks at the upper edge of human hearing, produced by sharp closures of the throat-resonator chambers
- Ratcheting throat-rasps—a sustained, low-pitched vibratory rasp that humans render imprecisely as "gr-, thr-, kr-, sk-, zr-, vr-"
- Heavy chest-rumbles felt as much as heard, often used to project dominance over distance
- Sharp closing-stops at the end of a signal, which humans transcribe as _-rk, -rg, -rn, -lk, -zg_

**The inaudible bands** (what a human listener misses entirely):

Subsonic chest-tones below roughly 20 Hz, used as sustained carrier signals during long-distance coordination
: Mídhalión scholars who have stood near a Grukar war-camp report a sense of vague unease and pressure on the chest, which is the only conscious access humans have to this register

Ultrasonic clicks and chirps above the upper limit of human hearing, used for fine-grained tactical coordination at close range
: dogs and certain forest creatures react visibly to these where humans do not

The overall effect on a human listener is harsh, percussive, and deeply unsettling—Mídhalión travelers describe it as the sound of rocks grinding together, punctuated by what feel like blows to the chest. The actual richness of the signal is largely invisible to them.

## What Passes for Meaning

Grukar communication—physical display with vocal emphasis—clusters around a narrow set of concerns:

**Dominance and submission:** The most nuanced part of the system. A Grukar can express fine gradations of threat through posture alone—the set of the shoulders, the lowering of the head, the positioning of hands relative to weapons. The accompanying vocalization adds intensity: a low rumble for "I could take you," a full-throated roar for "I will kill you now." Submission is communicated by making oneself small, averting the gaze, exposing the throat. Getting dominance signals wrong means death, which is why this domain has the most subtlety.

**Fighting and hunting:** Coordination for pack tactics. Direction indicated by body orientation, timing by rhythm of movement, target by gaze and gesture. Barked vocalizations supplement when visual contact breaks down in the chaos of combat. Grukar fight in coordinated groups with surprising effectiveness given the apparent crudeness of their communication—because the physical coordination is not crude at all.

**Ownership:** Crouching over a thing, covering it with the body, snarling when approached. "Mine." "Not yours." "Pack's." The concept of shared property does not exist; things belong to the strongest individual or to the pack as a collective extension of its leader's will.

**Territory:** The one domain where vocalizations take primary role. Sustained, bellowed calls that carry over long distances mark boundaries when the body cannot be seen. These are supplemented by scent marking and physical signs (scratched trees, piled stones, displayed kills).

There are no signals—physical or vocal—for abstract concepts. No way to indicate "tomorrow" distinct from "later." No way to discuss hypotheticals. No conditional constructions. Grukar live in an eternal aggressive present.

## The Grukar-sha Exception

The [[being-grkrsh|Grukar-Sha]] can learn what no [[being-grukaruk|Grukar-Uk]] or [[being-grkrhshk|Grukar-Hushk]] can carry home: an idea about the world beyond its tribe. It can plan and can use what another creature tells it. This makes a Sha useful to its spawner, but it gives no Sha authority to speak for another tribe or to pledge its own to an outsider.

A Sha is the only Grukar able to produce recognizable human speech. It blends mouth sounds with the chest and throat resonance used by the other castes, so the voice can seem out of step with the speaker's lips. A Sha that has heard a human tongue may repeat enough of it to deliver a threat, ask a question or learn a road. An outsider can tell a Sha something; an Uk or Hushk cannot take in the words as terms at all. Neither case amounts to a parley. The Sha listens for knowledge its tribe can use, and there is no promise that binds its spawner after the Sha leaves.

Among themselves, Sha add calls for waiting, another tribe and a shared immediate danger to the instinctive Grukar signals. The calls help them direct Uk and advise Hushk within one nest. They do not create a common council among nests: the [[being-grkrahk|Grukar-ahk]] do not cooperate, and a Sha's reach ends with its own tribe.

## No Script, No Literacy

There is no Grukar writing system, and even the Grukar-sha have not developed one. The concept is alien to them. Some Grukar scratch territorial marks into stone or trees, but these are symbols of ownership, not writing—no more linguistic than a dog marking a post. A mark on a tree can warn that a nest is near; it cannot record a bargain or a safe passage that the nest will honor.

## Names

Grukar names are acquired, not given. A young Grukar is called by whatever sound the pack uses to get its attention—often a bark or grunt that sticks. As the Grukar grows, the name may shift based on deeds, physical characteristics, or sheer repetition. There is no naming ceremony. There is no distinction between male and female names because there is no distinction in Grukar naming practice at all.

Some names are compound—two Grukar identified together by pack or deed (_K'dak Thrar_, _Momok Mur_, _Os'leot_). These are not family names in the human sense. They are identification tags, nothing more.

## Grukar Lexicon {#lexicon}

The Grukar have no words in the sense a scholar of [[place-midhalnrgn|Mídhalión]] gives the term. Grukar communication is posture first and sound second, and most of the sound lies above or below human hearing. What a human ear does catch is a narrow band of clicks, rasps and closing stops, and the neighbors of the Grukar write that band down in letters as best they can. This page is the word-hoard of that transcription: the calls the neighbors have learned to tell apart, the names they give the four castes, the rules the transcription keeps to, and how a Grukar name is made.

A call has no fixed meaning apart from the body that makes it. The snarl written _zruk_ is _mine_ when a Grukar crouches over food and _attack_ when it is bellowed in a charge, and the gloss below gives the sense a call carries in the display it most often goes with. A call takes no ending and is never inflected; the tongue has no grammar to inflect it with.

The sound of the transcription is harsh and percussive. Words open on a rasp, _gr-_, _thr-_, _kr-_, _sk-_, _zr-_ or _vr-_, and close hard, often on a pair: _-rk_, _-rg_, _-rn_, _-lk_ or _-zg_.

## Building words {#building-words}

**A call** is taken whole. Nothing is added to it and nothing is taken away, and two calls made together are two calls, not a compound.

**A caste name** is the kind's name and the caste's set together with a hyphen between them, as the joining table gives it.

**A name** is a call with a closing after it. A Grukar is called by whatever sound its pack uses to get its attention, and the sound that sticks is most often a call the bearer is known for, clipped short by the closing the pack puts on it. The closing carries no meaning of its own; it tells one bearer of a call from another.

## Making names {#making-names}

**A Grukar name** is acquired, not given, and the language note describes how one sticks and shifts. Many names in the accounts are a call with a closing: _Zrukak_ is the snarl of _mine_ closed on a bark, and _Vrulkath_ is the boundary bellow closed on a rasp through the teeth. A name of this kind says what the bearer is known for in its own pack, and two bearers of one call are told apart by the closing.

**A paired name** sets two names side by side, identifying two Grukar together by pack or deed. It is a tag, not a family name, and the language note gives examples.

**The caste names** are the neighbors' names for the four kinds of Grukar, written after the kind's own name with a hyphen: _Grukar-Uk_, _Grukar-Sha_, _Grukar-Hushk_ and _Grukar-ahk_. A page that has named the kind once writes the caste alone: an Uk, a Sha, a Hushk, an Ahk.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of the Grukar notes. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 0 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives the Grukar, their castes and the land they hold, with the note where it is met. A name built from this page shows how; a name the tongue uses that is not built from it shows `—`, and a name in the reader's tongue is tagged as such.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning || coalesce(CASE tag WHEN 'grukar' THEN ', [[' || tongue_language || '|Grukar]]' END, '') AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(built, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ' + '), '—') AS "Built from"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Retired names {#retired-names}

A retired name is one the setting does not write: a word or a name of an Earth language. Each stands below with the name written in its place, and no page writes it, however accented. A `terran_analog` comment names the Earth source on purpose and is not read.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```
