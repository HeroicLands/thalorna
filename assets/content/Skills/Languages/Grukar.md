Grukar is the way the [[lore-grukarfolk|Grukar]] converse with one another: by body language and by scent. It has no words, no grammar and no script. A Grukar has it from hatching. An outsider who learns it learns to read it and never to answer in it, because no human body or smell can make it. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

Two Grukar converse with the whole body and with the air around it. Posture, the set of the shoulders, the angle of the head, bared teeth, raised bristles, the gaze held or dropped, the hands moved toward a weapon or away from it: that is half of it. The other half is scent. A Grukar gives off pheromones that shift with what it means, and another Grukar takes in the shift as plainly as it sees the posture. The two together carry everything one Grukar has to tell another.

To a human the exchange looks like restlessness and smells like a strong stink. A band of Grukar standing together never stands still: they shift their weight, roll their shoulders, turn their heads and edge closer and away, and the air round them grows rank and changes as it grows. A human sees fidgeting and smells a reek. What passes between the Grukar in that time is a conversation.

The [[being-grkrahk|Grukar-ahk]]'s suppressing scent, which keeps its reserves barren within a chamber or two of it, is the strongest and steadiest of these signals, and the one every Grukar of a tribe lives inside.

Its empty `data.roman` inventory states that this language has no written words; a tagged roman passage cannot supply vocabulary for its displays.

## The sounds {#sounds}

The Grukar grunt, growl, bellow and scream, and none of it is a word. A sound is punctuation or emphasis laid over what the body and the scent already say: it gives a threat its weight, marks the moment a charge begins, or carries the force of a display past the reach of sight. The same snarl lends force to a claim over a carcass and to the start of a charge, and the body tells which. Sound matters most where the body cannot be seen: in darkness, across distance, and in a fight too close to read. Much of it comes from the chest and throat rather than the mouth, and the lowest of it a human feels as pressure against the ribs rather than hears.

The sounds the frontier peoples have learned to tell apart are below, with what each emphasises. None of them has a meaning apart from the display it goes with.

| Sound                                                | What it emphasises                                            |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A low rumble from the chest                          | A threat held back                                            |
| A full-throated roar                                 | A threat about to be carried out                              |
| A rasping growl, face to face                        | A challenge to a rival of the same caste                      |
| A thin whine, with the throat bared and gaze lowered | Yielding                                                      |
| A sharp bark                                         | The moment a charge begins                                    |
| A clipped grunt                                      | A halt, a stand where the body stops                          |
| A short cough as the body swings wide                | A turn to take the flank                                      |
| A double bark, the head thrust toward the quarry     | The quarry the body already points at                         |
| A low, falling grunt                                 | A quarry or an enemy down                                     |
| A snarl over food or a thing held                    | Possession                                                    |
| A long rumbling snarl                                | A thing of the tribe, and so of its spawner                   |
| A hiss through the teeth                             | A warning to back off                                         |
| A boundary bellow                                    | Ground already marked by scent and sign                       |
| A long call between bands out of sight of each other | Presence, carried farther than scent travels against the wind |
| A scream                                             | Pain or fury past bearing, and the moment of a kill           |

## What it carries

Grukar communication serves four ends: dominance, fighting, ownership and territory.

**Dominance and submission** are its finest part. A Grukar shows fine gradations of threat through posture and scent alone, and submission by making itself small, averting its gaze and baring its throat. A mistake in dominance means death, which is why this part has the most subtlety.

**Fighting and hunting** are coordinated by body orientation, by the rhythm of movement and by gaze. Grukar fight in coordinated groups with an effectiveness that surprises those who take them for crude, because the physical coordination is not crude at all.

**Ownership** is crouching over a thing, covering it with the body and snarling when it is approached. A thing belongs to the strongest Grukar or to the tribe as an extension of its spawner.

**Territory** is marked by scent, by signs and, where neither is enough, by the bellow. The signs are scratched trees, piled stones and displayed kills.

There is nothing in it for gratitude, beauty, compassion, regret or wonder, and nothing for the abstract: no tomorrow apart from later, no hypothetical, no condition. The Grukar converse in a present of appetite and threat.

## Grukar and human speech

Most Grukar cannot grasp verbal communication at all. Speech is sound, and to a [[being-grukaruk|Grukar-Uk]] or a [[being-grkrhshk|Grukar-Hushk]] sound is emphasis and nothing more, so speaking to one is worthless. It hears a threat or a challenge in the tone and takes nothing from the words.

The [[being-grkrsh|Grukar-Sha]] are the exception. A Sha almost always grasps the idea of a human language within a week of hearing one, and speaks it rudimentarily within a month, faster with a captive to learn from. Most Sha never pass basic fluency, though many know several human languages at a basic to modest level. A few especially intelligent Sha read and write simple human scripts. A Sha uses human speech with humans; with other Grukar it converses as every Grukar does.

## No script

The Grukar have no writing system of their own. A scratched tree, a pile of stones or a displayed kill marks a nest's ground the way scent does, and records nothing. A Sha that writes writes a human script in a human tongue.

## Names {#names}

The Grukar give no names. A Grukar's own "name", if it has one, is a shrug and a smell. A colony or a tribe may be named by the humans of its region, and that name is a human name in their tongue.

**The caste names** are an old convention among human peoples. The [[lore-pelwarpepl|Pelwar]] and the folk before them coined them for the four kinds of Grukar they see: Grukar-Uk, Grukar-Sha, Grukar-Hushk and Grukar-ahk. Nobody knows whether the Grukar recognise the distinction themselves. Each is written after the kind's name with a hyphen, and a page that has named the kind once writes the caste alone: an Uk, a Sha, a Hushk, an Ahk.

## Scope {#scope}

The register below holds every name of the Grukar notes. A note is in the register when one of these values names it:

```sql
SELECT CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 'address' END AS "Kind",
       string_agg('`' || CASE WHEN kind IN ('addresses', 'skip') THEN regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-note-', '') WHEN kind IN ('paths', 'types') THEN value ELSE regexp_replace(value, '^thalorna-(?:note-[a-z]+-|none-folder-)', '') END || '`', ' ') AS "Value"
FROM scope WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng'
GROUP BY kind ORDER BY CASE kind WHEN 'addresses' THEN 0 END
```

## Attested names {#attested-names}

Every name the setting gives the Grukar, their castes and the land they hold, with the note where it is met. All of them are names other peoples give, or names in the reader's tongue.

### Tongues

```sql
SELECT '`' || tag || '`' AS "Tongue", meaning AS "Meaning"
FROM tongues WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```

### Names

```sql
SELECT '**' || name || '**' AS "Name",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(notes, n -> '[[' || n || '|' || name || ']]'), ', '), '—') AS "Notes",
       '`' || tongue || '`' AS "Tongue"
FROM register WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```

## Retired names {#retired-names}

A retired name is one the setting does not write: a word or a name of an Earth language, or a pack name for a people that gives none. Each stands below with the name written in its place where there is one, and no page writes it, however accented. A `terran_analog` comment names the Earth source on purpose and is not read.

```sql
SELECT '`' || form || '`' AS "Retired",
       coalesce(array_to_string(list_transform(instead, x -> '`' || x || '`'), ', '), '—') AS "Written instead",
       why AS "Why"
FROM retired WHERE language = 'thalorna-note-skill-grukarlng' ORDER BY position
```
