Ekpelu is the tongue of the forest peoples of [[place-cntrlrnfrsts|the Central Rainforests]]. The River-keepers, the Canopy foragers, the Court-keepers and the Edge-people all speak it, and a band from one tradition can be understood by a band from another. Fluency measures how much of it a speaker commands, from the trade words a river-trader picks up to the full speech of a song-band at night. As with all specific languages, this skill inherits its mechanics from the general [[sohl-none-docskill-lang|Language]] skill.

## Overview

_Ekpelu_ is the people's own word for speech, and they use it for no other tongue: a stranger's speech is noise, and a stranger who learns Ekpelu is said to have been taken in. It is a small language, with few words and many of them concrete. It names the river, the fire, the band, the beast and the spirit exactly, and it leaves much else to song and gesture.

The four traditions speak it with different accents and use different words for their sacred things, but there are no branches of the language and no grammar that separates one from another. Outsiders do not hear the accents, and the words for the sacred are not spoken to outsiders.

Outsiders have never agreed on a name for it. [[skill-okharclng|Okháric]] river-traders call it the speech of the shadow, after the shade of the forest, [[skill-nyalbnlng|Nyáluban]] caravan-masters the quiet speech, and northern scholars, who have set down only a few hundred words of it, the common tongue of the forest.

## Phonology

### Consonant System

**Stops:** p, t, k, b, d, g

**Double-closure stops:** kp, gb

**Fricatives:** s, h

**Nasals:** m, n, ny

**Liquids & Approximants:** l, r, w, y

### Vowel System

**Vowels:** a, e, i, o, u

### Phonotactic Patterns

- **Consonant Clusters:** A nasal and a stop open many words (_mb_, _nd_, _ng_); otherwise no more than two consonants meet
- **Syllable Structure:** Open, a consonant and a vowel
- **Final Consonants:** Only _n_
- **Word Length:** Two or three syllables

### Tone

Ekpelu is a tonal tongue with two level tones, high and low. Tone tells words apart, so a word's tones are part of its form as much as its consonants and vowels. Because the pitch carries the meaning, the forest peoples can call to one another across a long distance in pitch alone, without shaping a single consonant. Names and prose are written here without tone marks, as the people's own speech is not written; the [[doc-ekpelulexcn|Ekpelu Lexicon]] shows the tones of every word.

## Grammar Notes

Word order is subject, object, verb. Nouns are not inflected for number. Four endings on a root say how a person stands toward it: the keeper of it, the child of it, the dweller beside it and the band of it. The [[doc-ekpelulexcn|Ekpelu Lexicon]] sets out the words and the endings.

## Script & Literacy

There is no script. What a band needs to remember it keeps in song and in the spirit-posts at its camps.

## Naming Traditions

A person carries one name, a root of the forest or a thing seen on the day of the birth, and in company is known by the band set after it. Bands are named for the river, tree or beast they follow, rivers and places for a head word and its quality, and spirit-courts for what they hold. The lexicon gives the rules.
